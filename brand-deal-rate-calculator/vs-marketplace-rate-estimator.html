This calculator vs. an influencer marketplace's rate estimator

Different jobs. An influencer marketing marketplace matches a listed creator to brands and usually estimates a rate only after you sign up or connect an account. This free calculator does the same kind of arithmetic from two numbers you already have — no account, no listing, nothing uploaded.

What each one is for

An influencer marketplace (the Intellifluence/Klear/ HypeAuditor-style category of tool) does something this calculator cannot: it puts a creator's profile in front of brands actively looking to book a sponsorship, and its own rate estimate is usually a feature bolted onto that matching product — something you see after connecting a social account or completing a listing. That listing and matching is real, ongoing work, and if getting inbound brand interest is the goal, a marketplace built around that is better placed to do it than a standalone calculator.

This calculator does the narrower part that comes before any of that: turning your own follower count, engagement rate, and platform into a price range for each common deliverable, using commonly cited creator-economy CPM-per-engaged-follower benchmarks. It needs no account, connects to nothing, and lists you nowhere — you type two numbers your own platform's native insights already show you, and get a number to defend the moment a brand reaches out on its own, through a marketplace, or anywhere else.

It also exists for creators a marketplace often has less to offer: accounts still below the size many marketplaces actively court, where a benchmark number would otherwise mean guessing from a rate-card template found online.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or sign up with an influencer marketplace?

Both can coexist, and they solve different problems. A marketplace connects a creator's account to brands actively looking to book a deal, and usually shows a rate estimate only after that account is connected or listed. This calculator answers the narrower question — what should I even ask for — from two numbers you already know, with no account, no listing, and nothing uploaded, so you have a number to defend before you ever talk to a brand or a marketplace.

Why do marketplace rate estimates require connecting an account first?

Because the estimate is a side effect of the marketplace's real product, which is matching a listed creator to a brand's campaign and taking a fee or commission on the deals it books. Reading its estimate usually means signing up, connecting a social account for it to analyze, or completing a creator profile brands can browse. This calculator has no matching product behind it, so it needs none of that — you type two numbers you already have from your own platform's native insights.

Are the price ranges the same between this calculator and a marketplace estimator?

Not necessarily, and neither one is the final word. Both are built on similar creator-economy CPM-per-engaged-follower logic, but a marketplace can weight its number by its own booked-deal history, niche, or audience-quality signals this calculator does not have access to. Treat either number as a starting point for a negotiation, not a guaranteed price — and if the two disagree, that gap itself is useful to know before you name a figure.

Does this calculator list me for brands to find, the way a marketplace does?

No. It is a static page that runs arithmetic in your browser on the two numbers you type in. Nothing is uploaded, no profile is created, and no brand can discover or contact you through it. If you want brands actively pitching you deals, that matching function is what a marketplace is for — this calculator only helps you price the deal once one exists.
