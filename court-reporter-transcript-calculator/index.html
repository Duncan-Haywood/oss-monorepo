    What this does and doesn't do

    Both calculators total the numbers you enter — page counts, rates, multipliers, fees, and hours. Neither one knows any court's official transcript-rate schedule, an agency's contracted rate, or which fees a specific bar or client will accept. Treat the output as the arithmetic behind your own invoice or acceptance decision, not a determination of what a client owes or what a contract requires.

    Common questions

    How do freelance court reporters price a deposition transcript?

    Most freelance reporters bill a flat appearance fee for showing up, plus a per-page rate for the transcript itself. The per-page rate is usually multiplied for faster turnaround (expedited, daily, or same-day), and certified copies, exhibits, a rough draft, realtime feed, and travel are billed as separate line items on top.

    How much more does expedited or daily turnaround cost?

    There is no single industry-wide multiplier -- it varies by reporter, agency, and jurisdiction. This calculator starts with illustrative defaults (expedited about 1.5x the ordinary rate, daily about 2x, same-day or hourly about 3x) that you can edit to match your own rate card before it reaches the invoice total.

    How do I check whether an agency's flat offer is worth taking?

    Divide the agency's total pay (page count times its offered rate, plus any flat appearance fee) by your total hours on the job -- travel both ways, time at the appearance, and production time. That effective hourly rate is what the assignment actually pays, which a per-page headline rate alone does not show. The agency offer check on this page does that division and compares it to a target hourly rate you set.

    Does this calculator know my state's official transcript-rate schedule?

    No. It only multiplies the rates, page counts, and multipliers you enter -- it does not know any court's official transcript-rate schedule, any agency's contracted rate, or which fees a specific bar or client will accept. Check your own state's rules and any governing contract before invoicing.
