This calculator vs. a flat day rate

A flat day rate — one round number for every booking of an asset — is the fastest way to quote a rental, and for bookings that rarely vary it can work fine. It stops working the moment delivery distance, cleaning effort, damage risk, or rental length changes from booking to booking, because none of that is priced separately in a single number.

What each one is for

A flat day rate is quick to quote and easy for a customer to compare against a competitor's number. What it does not do is separate the price into anything an operator can check against a specific booking — it does not scale with delivery distance or damage risk, it has no ownership-recovery logic built in, and it cannot tell you whether the rate actually covers the asset's per-day ownership cost or your monthly fixed overhead at your real booking volume.

This calculator takes one proposed booking — price, rental days, delivery, cleaning, and damage-reserve costs, plus the asset's purchase cost and recovery life — and works out the booking-specific costs, the per-day ownership cost, the minimum price that recovers ownership and your monthly fixed overhead, the price that hits a target contribution margin, and how many bookings like this one it takes each month to break even. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and nothing you type is uploaded.

Common questions

What is a flat day rate, and why do rental operators use it?

One round number charged for every booking of a given asset, regardless of how far it is delivered, how much cleaning or turnaround it needs, or how much damage-reserve risk it carries. It is fast to quote and easy for a customer to compare, which is why many small operators start there, especially early on when bookings are simple and similar.

What does a flat rate hide as bookings get more varied?

The price stays the same while the costs behind it do not. A flat rate sized for a short local booking under-prices one with a long delivery, heavy cleaning, or a higher damage risk, and over-prices a simple one — and it says nothing about whether the rate actually recovers the asset's ownership cost or your monthly fixed overhead at all.

Can a flat rate tell me how many bookings I need to break even?

No. A flat rate is a price, not a break-even calculation. Whether a given rate covers your monthly fixed overhead depends on the asset's ownership cost per day, the booking-specific costs on top, and how many bookings like it you run in a month — none of which a single number tracks. A rate can look reasonable and still lose money at your actual volume.

Does a flat rate separate ownership cost from booking-specific costs?

Only if the operator works it out by hand every time. A flat rate bundles delivery, cleaning, damage reserve, and the asset's per-day ownership cost into one number, so raising or lowering it does not say which piece is under-covered, or whether a longer or shorter rental should carry a different figure.
