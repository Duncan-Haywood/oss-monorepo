    Booking assumptions

    How to use the result

    The minimum booking price recovers the asset's allocated ownership cost plus booking-specific costs. Break-even bookings per month is how many of this booking it takes to cover monthly ownership at this contribution; the day-share row is those bookings times rental days, divided by available days. These are arithmetic scenarios, not a guarantee of profit, and they are not a recommended price.

    Common questions

    Why is the minimum price sometimes higher than the target-margin price, and sometimes lower?

    They are built from different pieces. The minimum price recovers booking-specific costs, this booking's ownership cost, and this booking's share of your monthly fixed overhead — on the defaults, $77 booking costs + $26.67 ownership + $7.50 of amortized monthly overhead = $111.17. The target-margin price applies your target margin to booking costs and ownership only, with no monthly-overhead add-on: $103.67 marked up to a 35% margin is $159.49. On the defaults the margin price wins, but a low margin target paired with a large per-booking share of monthly overhead can flip that. Always quote at least the higher of the two.

    How does the asset's purchase price turn into a per-day cost?

    Purchase cost divided by recovery life in rental days, then multiplied by the days this booking uses. On the defaults, a $2,400 asset with a 180-rental-day recovery life is $13.33 a day, so a 2-day booking carries $26.67 of ownership cost. A shorter recovery life — expecting to replace or overhaul the asset sooner — raises this number for every booking, not just long ones.

    What does “break-even bookings per month” actually count?

    How many bookings like this one, in a month, it takes for their contribution (booking price minus booking-specific costs and this booking's own ownership-cost share) to cover your monthly fixed overhead. On the defaults that is $75 fixed overhead ÷ $76.33 contribution ≈ 0.98 — just under one booking a month covers the fixed side. The day-share row turns that into a percentage of your available rental days (0.98 bookings × 2 days ÷ 20 available days ≈ 9.8%), so you can see how much of the month's capacity break-even actually consumes. A booking whose contribution is zero or negative never breaks even at any volume, however small fixed overhead is — see the next question.

    What does it mean if my contribution is negative?

    The booking price does not even cover its own booking-specific costs and ownership share before monthly fixed overhead is counted at all — every booking at that price loses money outright, and no amount of volume fixes it. Raise the price, cut booking-specific costs, or shorten the rental days before accepting bookings at that number.

    Can I use this for more than one type of booking or asset?

    Run each one separately. Rental days, delivery/cleaning/damage costs, and ownership recovery all differ by booking length and asset, so a single run only describes the scenario you typed in. Save a draft for each booking type you quote regularly rather than averaging them into one number.

    Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    No account and no network requests — everything runs in your browser. Saved drafts live in this browser's local storage, the downloaded quote snapshot is a plain-text file you keep yourself, and “Copy share link” encodes the same booking fields into the page's own URL fragment (never sent to a server) so you can hand a live, editable scenario to a partner or customer.
