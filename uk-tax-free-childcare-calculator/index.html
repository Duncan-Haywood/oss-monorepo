    Common questions

    How much does Tax-Free Childcare give me?

    The government adds 20p for every 80p you pay into your childcare account, up to £2,000 a year per child (£4,000 if the child is disabled). In practice that is £500 a quarter per child, so you pay in up to £8,000 a year to receive the full £2,000.

    Who qualifies for Tax-Free Childcare and the 30 hours?

    In England, each parent must expect to earn at least 16 hours a week at minimum wage over the next three months (about £2,540 a quarter in 2025/26) and neither may have adjusted net income over £100,000. Parents on statutory leave, some carers and those in the first 12 months of self-employment are exempt from the earnings test.

    What are the 30 hours of free childcare?

    In England, working parents get 30 hours a week of funded childcare for 38 weeks a year (1,140 hours) for children from 9 months until they start school. All 3 and 4 year olds get 15 hours whether or not their parents work. Some providers stretch the hours over more weeks of the year at fewer hours a week.

    Can I get Tax-Free Childcare and Universal Credit childcare help?

    Not for the same costs. Universal Credit repays up to 85% of childcare costs (capped monthly), which is often worth more than the 20% Tax-Free Childcare top-up. If you are on Universal Credit, compare both before choosing.

    What happens at £100,000?

    If either parent's adjusted net income goes over £100,000 the household loses the 30 hours and Tax-Free Childcare entirely, not just part of it. A pension contribution or Gift Aid donation can bring adjusted net income back under.

    Does this work in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland?

    Tax-Free Childcare applies across the UK, but free childcare hours differ by nation, so this calculator models England only.
