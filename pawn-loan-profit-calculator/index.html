    Questions pawnbrokers ask

    How much should a pawn shop lend on an item?

    Work back from what a forfeit really nets you. The largest loan is the one where the interest you collect, plus the gain on the forfeited items weighted by how often loans forfeit, less per-ticket cost and the cost of money, still leaves your target profit. This tool solves that for the item on your counter and shows it as a share of resale price.

    What redemption rate does a pawn shop need to break even?

    It depends on how much you lend against resale value. At a low loan-to-value, forfeits are profitable and you can survive a low redemption rate; at a high one you need most loans redeemed. The tool finds the break-even redemption rate for your figures and shows profit at redemption rates from 50% to 90%.

    What is a good loan-to-value for pawn loans?

    There is no single number. Many shops lend a third to a half of expected resale, but the right figure depends on your rate, how often loans forfeit, what forfeits sell for and your costs. The loan-to-value table shows profit per loan across a range so you can see where the margin turns.

    Why do small loans lose money?

    Every ticket costs about the same to write, store and report whether it is $20 or $200. The tool computes the smallest loan that pays at your loan-to-value, below which per-ticket cost outruns the interest and the resale gain.

    No. It is arithmetic on figures you type. Pawn charges, licences, holding periods and reporting rules are set by state and local law; check yours with a qualified professional.
