# Buying private data with a log-score market: a precision cap and an over-collecting first mover

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Decentralised training needs data and gradients from contributors who bear a privacy cost. The market framework for eliciting private data (Waggoner, Frongillo, Abernethy 2015) pays each contributor the improvement in a proper score of a public belief; we instantiate it in the cleanest solvable case. The market holds a Gaussian belief of precision `a` about a parameter; each contributor releases a statistic of noise variance `σ²` plus Gaussian-mechanism noise `u`, is paid `λ` times the log-score gain, and bears a privacy cost `c·Δ²/(2u)` (linear in zCDP). Ex ante the payment is exactly the information released, `(λ/2)ln(1+τ/a)` with `τ=1/(σ²+u)`, and payments telescope pathwise. The best noise is the larger root of `(1−κa)w² − (2σ²+κ)w + σ⁴ = 0` (`w=σ²+u`, `κ=cΔ²/λ`), and participation is possible **if and only if `κa < 1`**: the market's precision can never exceed the cap `1/κ = λ/(cΔ²)`, however many contributors arrive or how much data they hold in total, and it approaches the cap geometrically at rate `2σ²/(2σ²+κ)`. The whole market's payout is bounded by `(λ/2)ln(1/(κa₀))`. Because each agent is paid at its own arrival position but the last agents' presence is not internalised, early contributors add too little noise: against a planner with a common noise level the sequential market loses 1–22% of welfare and its first agent adds 1.5–5× too little noise (n = 2…100). With heterogeneous data sizes, admitting small holders first raises final precision from 28.0 to 34.2 (random orders 30.6 on average). Verified against grid search, Monte Carlo (10⁶ runs), and a pathwise telescoping identity. Stylised: Gaussian statistics, ex-ante noise commitment, zCDP-linear privacy cost.

## 1. Model
Market belief `θ ~ N(m, 1/a)`. Contributor `i` has a statistic `ȳ ~ N(θ, σ²)` (`σ²=s²/n` for `n` samples). It releases `y = ȳ + N(0,u)`; the market does the exact Bayes update `a ← a + τ`, `τ = 1/(σ²+u)`. After `θ` is revealed the contributor is paid `λ[ln p_new(θ) − ln p_old(θ)]` (a proper score, so it reports `y` honestly). With `n` samples clipped to `[−B,B]` the sensitivity is `Δ=2B/n` and the Gaussian mechanism has zCDP `ρ = Δ²/(2u)`; the contributor's privacy cost is `cρ`. The noise level is committed before the data is seen.

## 2. Payment equals information
The score gain is `½ln(a′/a) − a′(θ−m′)²/2 + a(θ−m)²/2`. Since `y|θ ~ N(θ,1/τ)` exactly, the market posterior is the true posterior: `E(θ−m′)² = 1/a′` and `E(θ−m)² = 1/a`, so the two quadratic terms cancel and `E[gain] = ½ln(1+τ/a)` = `I(θ;y)`. E1: Monte Carlo (10⁶ runs, prior mean ≠ 0) gives 0.54899±0.00082 vs 0.54927, 0.14372±0.00050 vs 0.14384, 0.07182±0.00037 vs 0.07259, 0.02406±0.00022 vs 0.02440. Payments telescope: over four sequential releases the pathwise discrepancy between the sum of gains and the final-minus-initial score is 1.8×10⁻¹⁵, so the market's total outlay is `(λ/2)ln(a_final/a₀)` whatever the order.

## 3. Best noise
Payoff/λ `= ½ln(1+1/(a(σ²+u))) − κ/(2u)`. The first-order condition `u² = κw(aw+1)` is the quadratic above; at `w=σ²` it is negative, so exactly one root exceeds `σ²`, the payoff rises up to it and falls after: `w* = [(2σ²+κ) + √((2σ²+κ)² − 4(1−κa)σ⁴)] / (2(1−κa))`. E2 checks five parameter sets against an 8000-point log grid (e.g. `a=0.5, σ²=1, κ=0.3`: 1.1616 vs 1.1601 within grid spacing, payoffs equal to 6 places).

## 4. The cliff and the cap
Since `½ln(1+x) ≤ x/2` and `w>u`, payoff `≤ 1/(2aw) − κ/(2u) < (1−κa)/(2au) ≤ 0` whenever `κa ≥ 1`: every noise level loses, so the agent stays out. For `κa<1` the large-`u` payoff is `≈(1−κa)/(2au) > 0`, so participation pays. Participation is possible **iff `κa<1`**, i.e. the market is alive only below `a = λ/(cΔ²)`. E3 (σ²=1, κ=0.4): best grid payoff `2.5×10⁻²` at `κa=0.5`, `4×10⁻⁶` at 0.99, `−2×10⁻¹⁹` at 1.00, negative beyond; the interior noise blows up (`3.3`, `22.6`, `239`) as the cliff nears. The contribution `τ` vanishes at the cliff, so precision creeps up to `1/κ`: near it `1−κa ≡ ε` obeys `ε′ ≈ ε·2σ²/(2σ²+κ)`, a geometric approach (measured ratio equals the formula to 4 places). E4 (a₀=0.2): κ=0.5 reaches 50/90/99% of the cap 2.0 after 3/10/20 agents and 2.0000 after 200; κ=0.1 needs 11/42/89 for a cap of 10; without privacy 200 agents give precision 200. A data buyer therefore cannot buy accuracy by recruiting: precision is bought with `λ` (the cap is linear in it) or by lowering `Δ` (more samples per contributor: `cap ∝ n²`).

## 5. The first mover over-collects
An agent at position `t` is paid the marginal log-gain at `a_t`, `1/(2(a_t+τ))`, but the planner's marginal value of its precision is `1/(2a_final)`, smaller. So early contributors add less noise than a planner who knows more will arrive. E5 (σ²=1, a₀=0.2, planner with a common noise level found by grid): welfare loss (information minus privacy cost) 1.0/2.5% at n=2 (κ=0.1/0.5), 4.1/9.3% at n=5, 11.5/18.6% at n=20, 18.2/21.9% at n=100; first-agent noise 0.43 vs planner 0.61 (n=2) up to 0.43 vs 11.2 (n=100, κ=0.1) and 1.29 vs 56.7 (κ=0.5). The market is a greedy planner; the fix is not to pay early agents the full marginal score, e.g. scale payments by `a_t/a_final`-style discounts that a public schedule can commit to (not analysed).

## 6. Order with heterogeneous data
Holders with `n` samples have `σ²=1/n`, `κ ∝ 1/n²`, so larger holders stay alive longer (threshold `∝ n²`). With `n ∈ {1,2,4,8,16,32}` and `a₀=0.05`, admitting small holders first gives final precision 34.15 with all six participating; large first gives 27.97 with two participants, because the large holders raise `a` past the small holders' cliff. 200 random orders average 30.56 (range 27.97–34.04); without privacy the precision would be 63.0. Ordering by ascending data size is the best of the orders we tried.

## 7. Limits and next steps
Gaussian statistics with a Gaussian mechanism (bounded-data clipping is only reflected in `Δ`), ex-ante commitment to noise (an interim choice would leak the data), privacy cost linear in zCDP and known to the market, no Sybil or collusion, single round. Open: the optimal payment schedule that closes the first-mover gap; heterogeneous unknown `c` (mechanism design over privacy types); the non-Gaussian, vector-valued case with Bregman scores.
