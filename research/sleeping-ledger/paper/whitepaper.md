# A self-financed ledger for sleeping modules

**Abstract.** Modular and continually learning systems keep many specialist modules that are relevant only in some regimes. A routing market that forces every module to report every round makes each specialist pay for rounds it should not play, and any forgetting mechanism (fixed share) taxes wealth earned in a regime that will return. I study the sleeping-experts version of a wealth market: only awake modules report, prices are wealth shares among the awake, and each awake module's wealth is multiplied by `1+η(ℓ̂−ℓ_i)`, where `ℓ̂` is the price-weighted market loss. Three exact facts follow. (i) The ledger is budget balanced round by round (total wealth is 1 to 1.6e−15 over 300 random instances), sleeping wealth never moves, so each regime keeps a frozen private ledger. (ii) For `η ≤ 1/2` every module's regret against the market on the rounds it is awake is at most `ln(1/π_i)/η + η·n_i`, with `n_i` its *own* awake time (the exponential variant gets `η T/8` with `T` the total rounds, and is not budget balanced). (iii) Admitting a new module with wealth `ε`, funded pro rata, costs each incumbent exactly `ln(1/(1−ε))/η` in its bound and gives the entrant `ln(1/ε)/η + ηn`. In a recurring-regime instance (K specialists asleep outside their own regime plus an always-awake generalist) the ledger matches the per-round oracle (0.1004 vs 0.1000 mean loss, regret about 2.7 total after 40 cycles regardless of K) while a forced-to-report Hedge sits at the generalist (0.30) and fixed share is worse (0.34–0.36).

## 1. Model
`N` modules with initial wealth `π_i` (sum 1). In round `t` an awake set `A_t` reports losses `ℓ_i ∈ [0,1]`. Price `p_i = w_i/W_A`, `W_A = Σ_{A_t} w_j`; the router plays the price-weighted mixture, whose loss is at most `ℓ̂ = Σ p_iℓ_i` by convexity. Update `w_i ← w_i(1+η(ℓ̂−ℓ_i))` for `i∈A_t`, nothing for sleepers. This is the sleeping-experts setting of Freund–Schapire–Singer–Warmuth and Blum with a self-financed wagering update (in the spirit of Kelly/wagering markets, Lambert–Pennock–Shoham and Frongillo et al.); I do not claim the regret rate is new, only the ledger reading and the specialist-local form of the bound below.

## 2. Exact properties
**Budget balance.** `Σ_A w_i(ℓ̂−ℓ_i) = W_A(ℓ̂−ℓ̂) = 0`, so the transfer is zero-sum among the awake and `Σ w = 1` forever; for `η ≤ 1` wealth stays positive. Sleepers are untouched, so a regime's wealth split is frozen while others run (test: module 0's wealth is bit-identical across the other 5 regimes; E4). Awake sets that never overlap are independent ledgers; only overlapping modules (the always-awake generalist) couple them.
**Specialist-local regret.** With `ln(1+x) ≥ x−x²` on `x ≥ −1/2` and `w_i(T) ≤ 1`: `Σ_{t:i∈A_t}(ℓ̂_t−ℓ_it) ≤ ln(1/π_i)/η + η Σ (ℓ̂_t−ℓ_it)² ≤ ln(1/π_i)/η + ηn_i`. Checked on 300 random instances (random wake rates, adversarial 0/1 losses): worst regret/bound 0.26, no violation (E1). The bound is loose in the second term: with module 0 at loss 0 and all others at 1 the regret saturates at 6.46 for `N=16` (`ln N/η = 5.55`) whether `n=20` or 200 (E2); in the regime runs the realised regret is 2.7 against a summed bound of 900–3700. The `ηn_i` term is a worst-case allowance, not what happened.
**Exponential variant.** `w_i ← w_i e^{η(ℓ̂−ℓ_i)}`: Hoeffding gives `ln(1/π_i)/η + ηT/8` with `T` counting asleep rounds too (a convex combination `(1−a)+ae^s ≤ e^s`); total wealth is non-decreasing (300/300 runs), so it is not a self-financed ledger.
**Entry.** Scale incumbents by `1−ε` and admit a module with `ε`: total wealth stays 1; `w_i(T) ≤ 1` gives each incumbent `ln(1/(π_i(1−ε)))/η + ηn_i`, an additive `ln(1/(1−ε))/η` (2.77 at `ε=.5`, 0.42 at .1, 0.004 at .001, `η=.25`), and the entrant `ln(1/ε)/η + ηn` from its entry. In E5 an entrant with loss 0.05 against incumbents at 0.4 takes half the market in 0, 25, 53, 80 rounds for `ε = .5, .1, .01, .001`, growing like `ln(1/ε)`: cheap to admit, a modest tax on the incumbents, and a logarithmic price for probing a doubtful module.

## 3. Regimes, forgetting, and forced reporting
K specialists, each awake only in its own 25-round regime (mean loss 0.1, cycled 40 times), plus a generalist always awake at 0.3. The ledger's mean loss is 0.1004 for K = 3, 6, 12 against an oracle at 0.0995–0.1002, and its total regret is 2.7–2.8, flat in K; the first cycle costs 0.1146 and later cycles 0.097–0.103 (E3, E4). A forced-to-report Hedge in which an off-regime specialist is charged 0.6 concentrates on the generalist (0.301), and fixed share at α=0.02 is worse (0.340, 0.356, 0.363), because it taxes exactly the wealth the returning regime needs. The comparison is stylised: the 0.6 charge stands for a specialist that must emit something when queried out of distribution, and a router with a hand-built gate would do as well as the ledger; what the ledger provides is that the gate is learned, priced, and auditable from public wealth alone.

## 4. Limits
Bounded losses and a mixture whose loss is at most the price-weighted loss (convex loss); wake sets are exogenous, which hides the incentive to declare oneself asleep (a module that sleeps on rounds it would lose keeps its wealth, so wake declarations need their own audit, e.g. a wake rate tied to a published domain or a skipped-round penalty); regret is against the *market* on the module's awake rounds, not against the best sequence of modules (ranking regret is a harder benchmark); entry is funded by proportional dilution and the entrant's quality is assumed learnable within the horizon; simulations are single seeds per configuration with noise ±0.05 around fixed means.
