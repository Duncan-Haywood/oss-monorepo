# A fixed-step twin of a bouncing ball gets restitution and settling time wrong by O(dt), and calibrating on the first bounce does not reliably fix it

## Question
Digital twins of hopping, juggling and grasping systems step contact with a fixed time step and a penetration test. The true rigid impact map with restitution `e` has infinitely many impacts in a finite (Zeno) time. What does a fixed-step twin report for the apparent restitution and the time to come to rest, how does the error scale with the step, and does fitting `e` on the first bounce (the usual calibration) repair it?

## Model
Truth: point mass dropped from `h0 = 1 m`, `g = 9.81`, Newton restitution `e` at each impact; closed form. Impact speeds `v_n = v1 e^{n-1}`, `v1 = sqrt(2 g h0) = 4.4294`, accumulation (Zeno) time `T = (v1/g)(1+e)/(1−e)`, and the last time above a height `δ` (here 1 cm) follows from the last bounce whose apex `h0 e^{2n}` exceeds `δ`. Twin: semi-implicit Euler (`v ← v − g dt; y ← y + v dt`) with a post-step penetration test, in three variants: *clamp* (set `y = 0`, `v ← −e v`), *mirror* (`y ← −y`, `v ← −e v`) and *locate* (linear interpolation of the impact instant in the step, reflect there, finish the step; a crude first-order version). Pure Python, step sweep `dt = 10⁻²` to `10⁻⁴`.

## Results
(all numbers from `experiments/results.txt`)
1. **Truth is finite-time and hypersensitive to `e`.** Zeno time 1.355, 4.064, 8.579, 17.61, 89.85 s at `e` = 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99; the relative error in it per unit error in `e` is `d ln T/de = 2/(1−e²)` = 2.67, 5.56, 10.5, 20.5, 100. Checked against the exact formula: a ±0.01 error in `e` changes `T` by ±2.7% at `e = 0.5` (exact −2.65/+2.68%), ±10.5% at 0.9 (−10.1/+11.1%) and ±20.5% at 0.95 (−18.8/+22.8%): first-order accurate, with the asymmetry growing with `e`.
2. **Apparent restitution is biased by the step, with scheme-dependent sign.** Apparent `e` from the first rebound apex (true 0.9): clamp 0.8859, mirror 0.8945, locate 0.8791 at `dt = 10⁻²`; at 10⁻⁴ all within 10⁻³ of 0.9. The apex is read off the step grid, so the dependence on `dt` is not monotone (mirror gives 0.9069 at 3·10⁻³, above the truth).
3. **Settling time error is first-order in `dt` and large at usual steps.** Time to last exceed 1 cm, relative error (twin − exact)/exact at `e = 0.9` (exact 7.660 s): clamp −19.6%, mirror +4.0%, locate −30.8% at `dt = 10⁻²`; −7.4%, +4.4%, −13.1% at 3·10⁻³; −1.7%, −0.3%, −4.4% at 10⁻³; −0.14%, +0.11%, −0.34% at 10⁻⁴. Log-log slopes of |error| against `dt` over the sweep: 1.11 (clamp), 1.12 (mirror), 1.00 (locate). At `e = 0.95` (exact 15.78 s) and `dt = 10⁻²` the errors are −34.8%, +46.3%, −47.1%. The *locate* variant, meant to be more careful, is the worst here (its interpolation is first-order in a semi-implicit step, so this is a statement about this crude implementation, not about event location in general).
4. **The restitution bias explains most, not all, of the settling error.** Clamp, `e = 0.9`, `dt = 10⁻²`: apparent `e` is 0.0141 low, which the sensitivity `2/(1−e²)` converts to a −14.9% change in Zeno time; the measured settling error is −19.6%.
5. **Calibrating `e` on the first bounce is not a reliable repair.** Bisection on the twin's `e` parameter until its apparent first-rebound restitution equals the true 0.9 (`e = 0.9`): clamp `dt = 10⁻²` parameter 0.9142, settling error −19.6% → −4.8%; at 3·10⁻³ and 10⁻³ the fitted parameter is 0.9004 and 0.9002 and the error is unchanged (−7.4%, −1.7%). Locate improves at every step (−30.8 → −18.0%, −13.1 → −7.8%, −4.4 → −2.8%). Mirror gets *worse* at `dt = 10⁻²` (+4.0 → +53.0%) and at 10⁻³ (−0.3 → −2.1%), and changes sign at 3·10⁻³ (+4.4 → −5.9%). The error does not come from the first bounce: later, low-speed bounces with flight time of a few steps dominate what is left.
6. **Impact counts are not a fidelity metric.** Exact bounces whose next flight exceeds `dt`: 43, 65, 87 at `dt` = 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴ (`e = 0.9`, clamp). The twin logs 238, 444, 688 impacts up to the exact Zeno time, of which only 20, 41, 62 are separated by more than two steps; the rest is floor chatter below the resolution limit, and the exact count is infinite anyway.
7. **Impact-time errors are irregular, not a clean drift.** Twin − exact impact time, in steps (clamp, `dt = 10⁻²`): −0.15, −1.43, −10.65, +6.98, −0.11 for impacts 1, 2, 5, 10, 20; at `dt = 10⁻³`: +0.48, +0.73, −3.55, −27.22, −1.05 for 1, 2, 5, 10, 20. The errors reach tens of steps mid-sequence and the sign varies; I did not derive the mechanism.

## Limitations
One-dimensional point mass, constant restitution, one gravity value, one drop height, one threshold (1 cm), three contact variants of one integrator. No compliant (penalty/spring-damper) contact, no velocity-dependent restitution, no friction, no spin, no noise; the "real" system is the closed form, not hardware. The non-monotone `dt` dependence comes partly from reading the apex on the step grid; bisection on a piecewise-constant apparent `e` can land on a jump (result 5, mirror). Result 7's mechanism is open, and the first-order slope in result 3 is measured on five step sizes, not proved.

## Next steps
Velocity-dependent restitution (viscoelastic `e(v) → 1` at low speed) where even the truth has no Zeno point; compliant contact as used in MuJoCo and PyBullet versus the rigid limit; event-detecting integrators with a proper second-order locate and their error constant; hopping and juggling robots (multi-contact Zeno); the data needed to identify `e` to the precision the rest time demands (`2/(1−e²)`); combining with `friction-twin`, `stiction-twin` and `timestep-twin` in this repository.
