# Hiding the sync behind compute does not pay: delayed outer updates in DiLoCo-style training

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
`outer-momentum` showed that `H` inner steps make the outer optimiser see curvatures `s = 1−(1−ηa)^H ∈ (0,1]`, so tuned outer momentum contracts at `≈1−2/√κ_H`, and that the best sync interval is `H* ≈ C` for a synchronisation costing `C` inner steps. Overlapped training tries to hide `C` behind compute by applying the outer update `τ` rounds late. On the same quadratic model a delayed outer step is `x_{t+1} = x_t − α v_t`, `v_t = βv_{t−1} + s x_{t−τ}` with characteristic polynomial `z^{τ+2} − (1+β)z^{τ+1} + βz^τ + αs z`. We find: (i) plain delayed descent is stable iff `αs < 2 sin(π/(4τ+2)) ≈ π/(2τ+1)` (matches the spectral radius to 10⁻⁶ for `τ ≤ 8`); (ii) the best plain step sits at 99.8% of that edge and contracts at `1 − 2 sin(π/(4τ+2))/κ_H`, exactly `κ_H/(κ_H+1)` for `τ = 1` (versus `(κ_H−1)/(κ_H+1)` with no delay); (iii) **momentum is useless once `τ ≥ 1`**: the best `β` is 0 on a 0.02 grid for every `τ ∈ {1,2,4}`, `κ_H ∈ {10,100}`, so delay forfeits the `√κ_H` acceleration (68.8 rounds at `τ=0`, `κ_H=100`, versus 1388 at `τ=1`); (iv) even against blocking *plain* averaging at the same `H`, a fully hidden sync gains at most `(τ+1) sin(π/(4τ+2)) ≤ 1` (equality only at `τ=0,1`, tending to `π/4`), and in an exact search over `(H, τ)` overlapped training is 1.5–4.9× slower than blocking plain averaging and 2.3–31× slower than blocking with tuned momentum (`κ ∈ {10³,10⁴}`, `C ∈ {10,…,10⁴}`); (v) delay stops mattering when the applied pseudo-gradient gives its *fresh* part weight `w > 1/2` (stable for all `τ`, rate equal to the undelayed plain step at `w=0.75`, `τ ≥ 4`), and at `w = 0.75, τ = 1` the stale term acts as negative momentum and beats the undelayed plain step by 2×. Stylised: noiseless quadratic, one Hessian shared by all workers.

## 1. Model
Work in the Hessian eigenbasis as in `outer-momentum`; a mode has outer curvature `s`. In overlapped training the all-reduce of round `t` finishes `τ` rounds later, so the pseudo-gradient applied at round `t` was computed from `x_{t−τ}`: `x_{t+1} = x_t − α v_t`, `v_t = βv_{t−1} + s x_{t−τ}`. Eliminating `v` gives `x_{t+1} − (1+β)x_t + βx_{t−1} + αs x_{t−τ} = 0`, i.e. the polynomial in the abstract; the per-round contraction of a mode is its largest root modulus (Aberth iteration; `τ = 0` closed form). A synchronisation costing `C` inner-step equivalents, hidden behind `τ` rounds of `H` steps, adds `max(0, C − τH)` to a round that takes `H` (so blocking is `H + C`).

## 2. Stability and the tuned rate (E1–E3)
For `β=0` the polynomial is `z^{τ+1} − z^τ + αs`; the classical delayed-gradient bound gives stability iff `αs < 2 sin(π/(4τ+2))`: 2, 1, 0.618, 0.347, 0.185, 0.095, 0.048 at `τ = 0,1,2,4,8,16,32` (E1, equal to the numerical threshold). For `τ=1` the tuned rate is exact: at `s_max = 1` the roots are complex with modulus `√α`, at `s_min = 1/κ_H` real with larger root `r` solving `r(1−r) = α/κ_H`; equating gives `r = κ_H/(κ_H+1)` and `α* = r²` (tested, `κ_H = 3 … 1000`). For larger `τ` the optimum is found numerically on the two endpoint modes (interior modes never exceed the endpoint rate: 41-point check) and is at 0.998 of the stability edge for `κ_H = 1000`, so `1 − rate ≈ 2 sin(π/(4τ+2))/κ_H` (0.9990, 0.6175, 0.3470, 0.1844, 0.0951 vs 1, 0.6180, 0.3473, 0.1845, 0.0952). Rounds to `10⁻⁶` at `κ_H = 100`: 691 (`τ=0`, plain) → 1388, 2249, 4003, 7534, 14610 at `τ = 1,2,4,8,16`, roughly linear in `2τ+1`. Literal simulation matches the exact rate to ≤0.2% (E4).

## 3. Delay kills acceleration (E2)
With `τ=0` heavy ball reaches 0.818 at `κ_H=100` (68.8 rounds, `β*=0.67`). With any `τ ≥ 1` a nested search over `β ∈ [0, 0.48]` (step 0.02) and `α` returns `β* = 0` and the plain-step rate to 5 digits. The acceleration of `outer-momentum` is therefore incompatible with overlap: the price of one round of hiding is not a factor `≈2` but a factor `≈√κ_H/…` (20× at `κ_H=100`). This is numerical, not proved.

## 4. Wallclock: overlap does not win (E5)
With the sync fully hidden (`C ≤ τH`) and `κ_H ≫ 1`, time per unit accuracy is `(H/θ_τ)`, `θ_τ = 2 sin(π/(4τ+2))`, versus `(H+C)/2` for blocking plain averaging (rate `1−2/κ_H`). Since `H + C ≤ (1+τ)H`, the gain is at most `(τ+1)θ_τ/2 = (τ+1) sin(π/(4τ+2))`: 1 at `τ=1`, 0.927, 0.868, 0.830, 0.809 at `τ=2,4,8,16`, → `π/4`. So delay can at best tie blocking averaging and never beats blocking with momentum. An exact search over `H ∈ {1,…,4096}`, `τ ∈ {1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16,32}` with tuned outer step agrees: overlapped/blocking-momentum is 2.3–31× and overlapped/blocking-plain 1.5–4.9× over `κ ∈ {10³,10⁴}`, `C ∈ {10, 100, 10³, 10⁴}`; the search picks `τ = 1` almost everywhere. The recommendation from `outer-momentum` (`H* ≈ C`, tuned momentum, blocking) stands.

## 5. What does work: fresh weight above one half (E6)
Eager schemes apply the local pseudo-gradient fresh and the others stale: `g = s(w x_t + (1−w)x_{t−τ})`, polynomial `z^{τ+1} − (1−αsw)z^τ + αs(1−w)`. If `w > 1/2` and `αs ≤ 1/w` then `|1−αsw| + αs(1−w) = 1 − αs(2w−1) < 1`, so every root has modulus below 1 for every `τ` (triangle inequality; tested). E6 (`κ_H=200`): at `w=0.75` the tuned plain step reaches 6.91 rounds per `κ_H` for `τ = 4, 16` (the undelayed value), while at `w = 0.25` the same delays cost 25 and 87, and at `w=0` 40 and 146. At `w = 0.75, τ = 1` the tuned step is 3.85 (above the undelayed limit 2) and needs 3.54 rounds per `κ_H`, 2× fewer than undelayed plain: the delayed term behaves like negative momentum. Uniform averaging over `M ≥ 2` workers gives the fresh share `w = 1/M ≤ 1/2`, i.e. the bad regime, so the design rule is to weight the fresh part above half (for instance via a fast local outer step before the slow cross-site step).

## 6. Limits and next steps
Quadratic, noiseless, shared Hessian, one delay `τ` for all workers; the mixed model assumes the fresh and stale parts are pseudo-gradients from the same `H`; weighting fresh above 1/2 changes the fixed point under heterogeneity (`local-sgd-bias`) and is not analysed; the `β* = 0` and edge-optimality claims for `τ ≥ 2` are numerical. Next: delay-compensated outer steps (extrapolating with the last pseudo-gradient), random delays (`straggler-backup`), and noise. Related: `outer-momentum`, `local-sgd-bias`, `straggler-backup`, `stale-rollouts`.
