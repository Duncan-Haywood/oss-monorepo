A twin without ground effect: where does a landing altitude loop go wrong?

research/groundeffect-twin

Rotorcraft altitude twin without ground effect vs real thrust gain 1/(1−(R/4z)²): hover sits 18 mm high at one rotor radius (twin: 0), and a delayed loop with 28% twin headroom goes unstable at 0.54 rotor radii. Stylised.

12 tests

What it found

With the constant-power ground-effect multiplier G(z) = 1/(1−(R/4z)2) a gravity-feedforward altitude hold sits above its target by 2.7, 17.7 and 30.2 mm at 3, 1 and 0.6 rotor radii (twin: 0), 0.9% to 50% of the height. A sampled loop with one-sample delay that the twin gives 27.6% gain headroom at every height loses it as the vehicle descends: the real margin is 1.19 at one radius and reaches the stability boundary at 0.54 radii, confirmed by a nonlinear simulation; the undelayed thrust-gradient stiffness matters, since scaling the gain alone misses the instability. Negligible above about two radii. Stylised: point mass, closed-form ground-effect model, no rotor lag.
