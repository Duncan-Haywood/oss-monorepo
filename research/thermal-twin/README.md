# Thermal twin: a constant-gain twin of a motor holding a load certifies every load, what does the real thermal feedback do?

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real: a motor holds a constant torque `τ`; winding heating `C θ' = R i² − θ/Rth` derates the torque constant `k(θ) = k0(1 − αθ)`, so the hold current `i = τ/k(θ)` rises and heats it further. Twin: constant `k0`, so the temperature is `θ = cτ²`, `c = R0·Rth/k0²`, for any load. With `φ = αθ`, `λ = αcτ²` and time in units of `Rth·C` the real loop is `φ' = λ/(1−φ)² − φ`. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`): (1) steady holds solve `φ(1−φ)² = λ` and exist iff `λ ≤ 4/27` (a fold at `φ = 1/3`), while the twin says `φ = λ`: at `λ = 0.10, 0.14` the real temperature rise is 1.33× and 1.76× the twin's and the current 1.15× and 1.33×; at the fold 2.25× and 1.5×; (2) above the fold there is no hold and `φ` runs away in finite time, which near the fold is `≈ 4π/(9√(λ − 4/27))` (measured ratio 0.925 at `λ − 4/27 = 0.0019`, 0.994 at 10⁻⁵; the approach is slow, `O(√ε)`); the RK4 run matches the quadrature to 10⁻³; (3) short tests cannot see it: at `λ = 0.14` a test of 0.1 `Rth·C` sees +1.4% current, and +5% needs 0.39 `Rth·C`; at `λ = 0.02` it never reaches +5%; (4) under a temperature limit `φ_m` the twin overstates the holdable torque by `1/(1−φ_m)` up to `φ_m = 1/3` and by `√(27φ_m/4)` beyond (1.11× at `φ_m = 0.1`, 1.64× at 0.4, 2.3× at 0.8); (5) refitting a constant gain on a hot low-load run under-predicts the temperature at higher loads (−0.06 to −0.10 at `λ = 0.14`) and still has no fold; (6) winding-resistance rise (`β/α = 1`) lowers the fold from 0.148 to 0.113. Stylised: lumped single-node thermal model, linear derating, constant load, noiseless; see `paper/whitepaper.md`.

```bash
cd research/thermal-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 8 tests, about 1 s

```

**Builds on.** The digital-twin simulation-fidelity and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>), HIRO Group (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>, manipulation workcells), and the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>) and RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>, UAV and field-robot simulation, where hover is a sustained holding load); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The fold and passage-time analysis is classical bifurcation theory (Strogatz 2015); machine thermal behaviour is textbook (Pyrhönen, Jokinen & Hrabovcová 2008). Companion to `windup-twin`, `saturation-twin`, `gravity-twin` and `uav-energy-twin` in this repository.
