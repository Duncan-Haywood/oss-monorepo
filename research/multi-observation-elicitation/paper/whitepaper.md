# Pricing the scale of drift: multi-observation elicitation for verifiable training

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and exact experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Verifying decentralised training needs a tolerance for benign floating-point drift, and setting it well needs the *scale* (variance) of the drift, not just a quantile. Single-observation scoring cannot elicit a variance: it is not a function of a convex-level-set property of one draw, which is why the companion `property-elicitation-verification` module prices quantiles and expectiles instead. Following the multi-observation elicitation framework (Casalaina-Martin, Frongillo, Morgan, Waggoner), a scorer that sees `m` independent draws can elicit any property with an unbiased `m`-draw kernel by squared loss. We make this concrete for verification and get four things. (i) The pair score `(r − (y₁−y₂)²/2)²` is strictly proper for the variance with excess loss exactly `(r−σ²)²`, checked by full enumeration in exact arithmetic, along with kernels for `mean²` (m=2), the third central moment (m=3) and `σ⁴` (m=4). (ii) The price of the property is payment noise `Var h = (μ₄+σ⁴)/2` per pair, so detecting a variance under-report of relative size ρ takes `N = ((z_α+z_β)/ρ)²(κ+1)/2` pairs, growing linearly in the kurtosis κ; in our Monte Carlo this formula gives power 0.70–0.79 against a nominal 0.8. (iii) A robust scale score on `|y₁−y₂|` beats the variance score once tails are heavier than Gaussian (relative sd of the σ estimate 0.882 vs 0.935 for Laplace, 0.86 vs 1.12–1.18 for t₅) and stays finite when the variance score's payment noise is not. (iv) Scoring all pairs of an audited pool instead of disjoint pairs cuts payment variance by `(κ+1)/(κ−1)` (2 at Gaussian) at no extra re-execution cost, while draws that are not independent (shared seed or batch) make the pair score elicit `σ²(1−ρ)`, letting a verifier under-report the variance by exactly the correlation.

## 1. Setup
A verifier reports a scale `r` of benign drift `y` (per-step deviation between replicas), and the scorer holds `m` independent draws obtained by extra re-executions. If `h` satisfies `E h(y₁..y_m) = Γ(F)` then `S(r;y) = (r − h(y))²` has expected value `(r−Γ)² + Var h`, so honest reporting is uniquely optimal and the honest expected payment is fixed by `Var h`. Since `Γ` is estimable with `m` draws exactly when it is a polynomial of degree ≤ `m` in `F` (Halmos 1946), the minimal `m` is the degree: mean 1, variance 2, third central moment 3, `σ⁴` 4. We verify `E h = Γ` by summing over every `m`-tuple of a 4-point distribution with rational probabilities (E1); we do *not* prove the converse (that no cheaper score exists), only cite the literature for it.

## 2. What a variance costs
The pair kernel has `Var h = (μ₄+σ⁴)/2` (equal to enumeration exactly on the test distribution). For a report `r` and a true scale that is a factor `1+ρ` larger, a one-sided test on the mean of `N` pair kernels at level `α` has power ≈ `Φ((ρ−z_α s)/((1+ρ)s))`, `s = √((κ+1)/(2N))`, so the sample size for power `1−β` (ignoring the sd inflation under the alternative) is `N = ((z_α+z_β)/ρ)²(κ+1)/2`. E3 measures the achieved power at that `N`: 0.70–0.79 for Gaussian, 0.71–0.75 for Laplace at ρ from 0.5 to 0.1, i.e. the formula is optimistic by up to ten points because it ignores the larger sd under the alternative; use it for scaling, then add margin. The scaling is the finding: Laplace drift (κ=6) needs 1.75× the pairs of Gaussian drift, t₅ needs 2.5×, and for κ=∞ the variance score has no finite payment variance at all. Drift from reduction-order differences is typically *not* Gaussian, so the audit cost of eliciting a variance is set by the tail, not the scale.

## 3. A robust alternative
Score `|y₁−y₂|` by squared loss: it elicits the Gini mean difference `E|y₁−y₂|`, proportional to σ within a scale family. Relative sd of the resulting σ estimate per pair: Gaussian 0.756 (variance score 0.707), Laplace 0.882 (0.935), t₅ 0.86 by Monte Carlo (variance score 1.12 exact, 1.18 measured), t₃ 1.06 (variance score infinite, the measured 3.2 is a finite-sample artefact). So the variance score is 7% better at Gaussian and worse in every heavier-tailed case tested; the choice of *which* scale to elicit is an audit-cost decision. The trade-off is that the Gini scale is proportional to σ only within a known family; across families the two disagree, and a tolerance built from it inherits the family assumption.

## 4. Pool the draws
Disjoint pairs from an audited pool of `M` draws have variance `≈ (κ+1)σ⁴/M`; the average of the kernel over *all* pairs is the unbiased sample variance with variance `(κ−1)σ⁴/M` for large `M` (exact: `μ₄/M − (M−3)σ⁴/(M(M−1))`, checked by enumeration for `M=2,3,4`). It is still a proper score (an average of proper scores with a common minimiser), so the verifier's incentives are unchanged while payment noise falls by `(κ+1)/(κ−1)`: 2 at Gaussian (measured 1.87 at `M=20`; the exact finite-`M` value is 1.9), 1.4 at Laplace (measured 1.37). Under a fixed re-execution budget, pool.

## 5. Independence is the assumption that carries the result
The score is proper for the kernel's expectation *under the sampling design*. If the two draws share a seed or a batch with correlation ρ the expectation is `σ²(1−ρ)` (Monte Carlo agrees to 0.01), so a verifier who under-reports the variance by exactly ρ is paid as if honest. Independence of the scorer's draws therefore has to be enforced by the protocol (fresh seeds, disjoint batches) and is the natural thing to commit to in advance, in the spirit of the committed traces of `reproducible-refereed-training`.

## 6. Relation to prior work
Multi-observation elicitation and its degree characterisation are due to Casalaina-Martin, Frongillo, Morgan and Waggoner; nothing here extends the theory. The contribution is the audit-cost translation (detection sample size in the kurtosis, robust scale, pooling, correlation bias) for verifiable training, complementing single-observation quantile pricing in `property-elicitation-verification` and the sequential tests of `sequential-slashing`, which could test the same pair stream anytime-validly.

## 7. Limitations
Scale-family assumptions (Gaussian, Laplace, Student-t) stand in for real drift, which we have not measured here; the honest-verifier model has no effort cost or collusion; power is checked by Monte Carlo (2,000 trials) so the ±0.02 sampling error is comparable to some gaps; the degree characterisation is cited, not proved; the result is about paying for a report, not about the tolerance rule that consumes it.
