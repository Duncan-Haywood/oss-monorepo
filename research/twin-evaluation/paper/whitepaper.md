# A biased digital twin is still a good control variate: what it saves when evaluating a robot policy, and why a noise-free twin can beat a faithful one

*Stylised: real return `R = μ + s·c + σ_e·e`, twin return `T = μ + b + g·s·c + τ·e'`, with scenario context `c ~ N(0,1)` observed and replayable, execution noise `e, e'` independent, `s=σ_e=1` unless stated. Pure Python; every number is from `experiments/results.txt` (seeded, ~5 min). Simulation repetitions are 600–6,000 per cell (variance estimates carry ≈2–6% sampling error), and "real" is itself a simulation.*

## Question
Real robot rollouts are expensive and a digital twin is cheap but biased. Two uses are common: trust the twin's score (biased) or discard it (wasteful). How much do real rollouts shrink if the twin is used only as a *control variate*, how does that depend on how faithful the twin is, when is it worth its compute, and does it change which of two policies is selected?

## Method
With `n` real episodes, each replayed in the twin, and `N ≥ n` twin episodes in total (drawn on fresh contexts from the scenario distribution), the estimate of `E[R]` is `R̄ₙ − λ(T̄ₙ − T̄_N)`. For any `λ` it is unbiased, because `E[T̄ₙ − T̄_N] = 0` regardless of the twin's mean. With `λ* = Cov(R,T)/Var(T)` its variance is exactly
`V = σ_R²((1−ρ²)/n + ρ²/N)`
(derivation: expand `Var(R̄ − λ(T̄ₙ−T̄_N))` using `Cov(R̄ₙ, T̄ₙ−T̄_N) = −c(1/n−1/N)`). Under a budget `C = n·c_R + N·c_T` the minimiser is `n ∝ √((1−ρ²)/c_R)`, `N ∝ √(ρ²/c_T)`, with `V* = σ_R²(√((1−ρ²)c_R) + √(ρ²c_T))²/C` (Peherstorfer et al. 2016); this beats real-only (`σ_R²c_R/C`) iff `c_T/c_R < (1−√(1−ρ²))²/ρ²`. In this model `ρ = g s²/(σ_R σ_T)`; for a faithful twin (`g=1, τ=σ_e`) `ρ = η = s²/(s²+σ_e²)`, and for a noise-free twin (`τ=0`) `ρ = √η`.

## Results
1. **Unbiased despite bias.** With twin bias `b=+0.7` (true mean 1.0, `n=30`, `N=3000`), twin-only has mean error +0.700 (sd 0.026: precisely wrong), real-only −0.005 (sd 0.255) and the control variate +0.001 (sd 0.231).
2. **The correlation is set by shared context, not by fidelity.** Variance relative to real-only at `n=50, N=5000`, `η=0.5`: 0.928/0.775/0.624/0.516 for twin noise `τ`=2/1/0.5/0 against the formula's 0.900/0.750/0.600/0.500 (the small excess is the estimated `λ` and finite `N`). Execution noise cannot be seeded in reality, so the twin's own execution noise only dilutes the correlation: the *noise-free* twin is the best control variate (`ρ=√η`) and the faithful one is worse (`ρ=η`). Equivalent real-sample multiplier `1/(1−ρ²)` at `N→∞`: `η`=0.2: 1.04× faithful vs 1.25× noise-free; `η`=0.5: 1.33× vs 2.00×; `η`=0.8: 2.78× vs 5.00×. This is a statement about estimating the *mean*; a noise-free twin is a poor simulator of the return distribution, so it is not a substitute for a faithful twin where tails or variances matter (`safety-twin`, `twin-certification`).
3. **Estimating `λ` costs little.** With a noise-free twin (`ρ=0.707`, `N=20n`), variance over the optimal fixed-`λ` variance is 1.326/1.113/1.067/1.027 at `n`=6/10/20/50 (the classical `(n−2)/(n−3)` is 1.333/1.143/1.059/1.021, used here only as a reference); a t-interval on the residual (`n−2` dof) covers 0.940/0.941/0.941/0.940 at nominal 0.95.
4. **Allocation and the worth-it threshold.** At `C=1000`, `c_R=1`, `σ_R²=2`: for the faithful twin (`ρ=0.5`) the twin pays only below `c_T/c_R=0.072`: variance is 0.778×/0.839×/0.956× real-only at `c_T/c_R`=0.001/0.01/0.05 and nothing is gained at 0.1. For the noise-free twin (threshold 0.172) it is 0.532×/0.605×/0.749×/0.866× at 0.001/0.01/0.05/0.1. Simulated variance at rounded allocations agrees within simulation error (e.g. 0.00172 vs 0.00168, 0.00155 vs 0.00150; the 0.001 faithful cell is 0.00145 vs 0.00156, 7% low with 600 repetitions, sampling sd ≈6%; the largest-`N` cell, noise-free at `c_T/c_R=0.001`, was not simulated). The gains are modest unless the twin is 100× cheaper *and* strongly correlated: the twin must be an order of magnitude cheaper than a real rollout just to break even at `ρ=0.5`.
5. **Ranking under a twin bias that favours the wrong policy.** True gap `A−B=+0.20`, twin biases 0 and 0.40 (twin-only gap −0.20: twin-only picks B, wrongly, with probability → 1 as twin runs grow). With `N=20n`, `P(wrong)` for real-only / control variate is 0.327/0.304 at `n=20`, 0.240/0.209 at 50, 0.159/0.126 at 100, 0.079/0.053 at 200 (faithful twin) and 0.327/0.269, 0.240/0.165, 0.159/0.084, 0.079/0.025 (noise-free); simulated values are within ≈0.01 of these (worst 0.011). Real episodes per policy for a 90% correct order: 164 real-only, 125 faithful, 86 noise-free. The twin never causes a wrong answer through its bias here; it only helps or fails to help.
6. **Binary success.** Real success 0.592, twin 0.500 (pessimistic), latent-difficulty coupling gives `ρ=0.412` (from 400,000 pairs), formula variance ratio 0.830; at `n=50`, `N=2000` the estimate is unbiased (0.594 vs 0.592) with variance 0.00410 vs formula 0.00403 and real-only 0.00483.

## Limitations
Gaussian returns linear in the context; the formula for `V` is exact for any joint distribution with fixed `λ` and finite variances, but the achievable `ρ` is model-specific. The twin is assumed to replay the real scenario exactly, i.e. the context is fully measured (in practice pose and friction are estimated, which lowers `η`), and to sample contexts from the true distribution (a mis-specified scenario distribution biases the estimate, unlike a bias in the twin's dynamics: the control variate corrects the second but not the first). Policy comparisons use independent policies. The interval treats `N` as large. Nothing here uses robot or twin data; costs `c_T/c_R` are chosen. Preliminary.

## Next steps
Estimate `η` from a small pilot to decide whether to build the twin at all; non-linear residual models (a learned `E[R|c]` in place of a linear `λ`, as in prediction-powered inference with a fitted predictor); anytime-valid versions for sequential policy comparison (cf. `forecast-duel`, `twin-audit`); imperfect context replay; combining this with the scoring of the twin's claims (`twin-elicitation`).
