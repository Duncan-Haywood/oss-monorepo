Can the log score be made payable?

Log score is ideal for rare faults but a confident wrong report costs unbounded stake. In a saturating contribution game v(S)=1−exp(−Σwi) every estimator variance is exact. Permutation sampling always pays out exactly v(N) but is noisy; size-stratified sampling has 6–8× lower variance (exactly zero for equal contributors) yet misses the pot by up to 7% in a run; reversing each permutation cuts RMSE 1.6× at no cost; Hoeffding sample counts are 4–5× larger than needed, and stratification finds the top-3 payees with about 4× fewer samples. Stylised, n≤10. Pure-Python, 9 tests.

Which proper score should verifiers face with K outcomes?

For K-ary reports the Brier, spherical and log scores have exact regrets and exact worst cases (1−2pmin+‖p‖² for Brier, ‖p‖−pmin for spherical). Per unit of payment range the spherical score gives exactly √K times Brier’s incentive at a uniform truth, but both bounded scores nearly ignore a dropped rare class: at a 1% class the loss is 1.3×10−4 (Brier) and 1.0×10−4 (spherical) against 0.15 for log. Multiclass follow-up to tangent-log. Pure-Python, 6 tests.

Can the multiclass log score be made payable?

Extending x ln x below a cutoff ε by its second-order Taylor polynomial gives a strictly proper K-class score that equals −ln ry whenever every rj≥ε and has payment range exactly 1−ln ε, the same for any K. Per unit of range it has 3× Brier’s rare-class incentive at a 5% class and 63× at 0.1%, the best cutoff is the rarest class’s mass, and a verifier that zeroes out a 1% class loses a closed-form 0.005 (Brier 1.3×10−4, log unbounded). Closes the gap left open in categorical-scores. Pure-Python, 7 tests.

What does it cost to pay for a ranking instead of probabilities?

A verifier who reports only the order of K classes can be paid with a score linear in the outcome indicator. It is strictly proper, and swapping two classes at adjacent ranks costs exactly (pi−pj)(wr−wr+1), linear in the gap. Per unit of payment range that beats a Brier-derived ranking whenever the gap is below 1/(K−1) (2.5× at gap 0.1, 250× at 0.001, K=5). Equal spacing maximises the smallest incentive, ranking everything costs K−1 times the mode, a top-k set costs the same as the mode, and the sample size to detect a swap is identical to Brier’s. Pure-Python, 7 tests.

Should severity reports be scored with the order in mind?

For ordered fault severities the ranked probability score is Brier on the cumulative distribution: strictly proper with exact regret Σ(Rk−Pk)², so moving mass ε a distance d costs exactly dε² where Brier charges 2ε² for every d. Pooling adjacent classes costs exactly half of Brier’s, and every threshold probability is within √regret of the truth. The price: per unit of payment range RPS gives Brier’s incentive only for a maximal-distance shift (1/(K−1) for adjacent ones). Extends categorical-scores. Pure-Python, 7 tests.

Can we elicit the mode of a mixture of honest and cheating drift?

When some workers cheat, drift is a mixture and the mean, which squared loss elicits, lands where almost no task lies (at a 30% cheater 6σ away, the mean’s window holds 0.15 of the mass versus 0.48 at the mode). The mode is not elicitable, but the centre of the width-2α window with the most mass is, with the bounded loss 1[|y−r|>α]. Regret is an exact window-mass difference; the best window is α=σ for normal drift and γ/√3 for Cauchy (no mean exists); an equal bimodal mixture has one α-mode iff α exceeds a closed-form root α*; and under 10–40% contamination the sample α-mode has ~0 bias while the mean is off by 5ε, at a 3.7× RMSE cost when clean. Pure-Python, 7 tests.

Can a verifier be paid for a tolerance interval, not a point?

The Winkler interval score is strictly proper for the pair of quantiles, with closed-form regret and an optimal expected score of exactly 4sφ(z)/α. Misreporting the width by the same factor costs more when narrow at 95% coverage but more when wide at 80%, so there is no universal rule that overconfidence is worse. Under Cauchy drift the scores have no mean, yet paired differences are bounded, so evaluate verifiers against a benchmark report.

Should drift tolerances be per layer or on the total?

Giving each of L layers its own tolerance lets a prover hide the sum of them: L·c(L,α)σ versus zα√Lσ for one test on the total, 6.6× more at L=16 and 23× at L=128. The sum test’s power depends only on the total shift; the exact (Šidák) max test’s depends on how it is spread, the prover’s best split is even (log Φ is concave), and the max test catches a 2.7σ one-layer shift half the time but lets 16σ through spread over all 16 layers. A union of both at α/2 caps the hidden budget at the sum test’s (7.8σ) while keeping one-layer sensitivity. Gaussian, independent layers, known σ. Pure-Python, 8 tests.

How do you pay a verifier whose report decides the job?

If a report of “fault” gets the job rejected and the outcome is seen only on acceptance, scoring observed jobs is improper: verifiers shade under the threshold to get scored, and every job is accepted (1.1–2.8× optimal decision loss at thresholds 0.7–0.2). A reserve payment for rejection makes truth optimal iff the score’s entropy minimum τs is at least the threshold τd, and then it is unique, 1+τd²−2τdτs. Plain Brier (τs=½) therefore fails above τd=½; recentring the score by a term linear in the outcome fixes every threshold, cheapest at τs=τd with rent τ3/3. Exploration with inverse-propensity payment is also proper but multiplies payment variance by about 1/ε. Stylised, single verifier. Pure-Python, 10 tests.

How should a tolerance interval be paid for?

The Winkler interval score is the sum of two pinball losses: proper for the equal-tailed quantile interval with exact regret (2/α)[PR(l,α/2)+PR(u,1−α/2)], finite even for Cauchy drift where the expected score does not exist, but its payment grows without bound in the miss distance. A width-plus-miss loss (u−l)+λ1[miss] is bounded by width+λ and elicits the density level set, the shortest interval for its coverage: 22–31% narrower than equal-tailed on lognormal(0,1) drift. The price is statistical: to detect a 20% understatement of a normal tolerance it needs 3–48× more tasks for α≥0.1 and about the same at α=0.01. Pure-Python, 9 tests.

How well does Freivalds' check work in floating point?

Checking a claimed matrix product by A(Br) ≈ Cr needs a tolerance t, so the error bound becomes size-dependent: a rank-one corruption of Frobenius norm F is missed by a Gaussian probe with probability exactly erf(t/(√2F)), rank one is the worst case for fixed F, and m probes give erfm (4 probes for 1% at 3t). Rademacher probes miss a two-entry corruption with probability ½ at every size. Emulated float32 noise grows ≈n0.9; a hashed probe with free retries lets a cheater grind in 1/erf tries (125 at 100t). Stylised, additive corruption. Pure-Python, 7 tests.

Who trades decides how liquid a verification market should be

A Glosten–Milgrom market maker facing an informed share μ with accuracy q moves its posterior log-odds by exactly 2·atanh(μ(2q−1)) per net buy, so its price path is an LMSR with b* = 1/(2 atanh m) (verified to 2×10−16). A wrong b costs log loss only in a transient whose peak is scale-free in m and 1.8–2.5× worse when overconfident; the LMSR subsidy tends to b* ln 2, exactly the spread the GM maker charges. How many honest runs does a slashing tolerance need?

A conformal tolerance (the k-th order statistic of n honest drift scores) has an exact, distribution-free false-slash rate (n+1−k)/(n+1) — but only 51–56% of calibration sets deliver a rate ≤ α. A PAC-safe tolerance exists iff n ≥ ln δ/ln(1−α) (459 runs at 1%/1%), and its margin is hiding room for a cheater: the threshold sits 1.26× above the true quantile at n=2000 on Pareto-3 drift. Gaussian plug-in tolerances over-slash skewed drift up to 17×; pooling calibration across mixed hardware slashes the noisy class 4.8× too often and lets the quiet class hide 2.9× more. Pure-Python, 7 tests.

What does a fee do to a prediction market’s price?

A proportional fee f on an LMSR verification market gives every trader an exact no-trade band [q/(1+f), (q+f)/(1+f)] of width f/(1+f), so an informed trader leaves the price at q/(1+f). At price ½ a signal of log-likelihood ratio λ moves the price iff f < tanh(λ/2) = 2a−1, above which the market is dead; fee revenue on a monotone path is exactly fb ln((1−p0)/(1−pT)). With ten 0.65-accurate traders, the break-even fee (≈0.11) raises Brier error from 0.115 to about 0.16 and revenue is hump-shaped in f. Naive traders, synthetic signals. Pure-Python, 8 tests.

How big should a random verifier committee be?

Drawing m verifiers from a pool of N with B Byzantine, majority capture is exactly hypergeometric: zero once m ≥ 2B+1 and up to 26× below the with-replacement binomial (N=100, B=30, m=41: 1.4×10−4 vs 3.6×10−3). For failure 2−40 the exact size is a steady ≈0.90 of ln(1/ε)/KL(½‖β) while Hoeffding over-sizes it up to 2.2×. With stake-weighted draws only the Byzantine stake share counts: 20 of 100 nodes at 4× stake capture a 41-member committee half the time. A threshold-t rule trades wrong acceptance (2.7×10−7 to 1.1×10−16) against stalls (2.7×10−7 to 3.3×10−2). Static adversary, exact computation. Pure-Python, 11 tests.

Can a calibration test certify a verifier?

No. A hedger with no information at all keeps its expected calibration error below √(K+1)(1/(2K)+1/√T) against any outcome sequence, even one that sees its forecast distribution: ECE 0.016 at T=2000 while its Brier score is 0.254 against 0 for a verifier who knows the rule. A lazy constant forecaster is slashed; the hedger passes every calibration threshold tried, but a Brier-gap test against an informed benchmark catches it in 100/100 runs.

When should a paid verifier stop checking?

A verifier who checks a claim one noisy check at a time and stops when it likes, paid a proper score, follows a symmetric threshold rule (confirmed against dynamic programming over all policies). Accuracy is pk = 1/(1+((1−a)/a)k) and expected checks are k(2pk−1)/(2a−1), exactly. Participation needs a score scale κ ≥ 4c/(2a−1)² under Brier (log needs about half), Brier’s threshold is ln(κ(2a−1)²/((1−a)c))/λ within one step, and adaptive stopping matches a fixed-sample verifier’s accuracy with 2–3× fewer checks. Binary state, known accuracy. Pure-Python, 8 tests.

Why should the first verifiers be sealed?

Verifiers who are paid for being right and see earlier verdicts herd: once two more revealed signals point one way than the other, everyone copies. With signal accuracy a the cascade is wrong with probability exactly (1−a)²/(a²+(1−a)²) (0.155 at a=0.7) and forms after 2/(a²+(1−a)²) revealed signals (3.4), so accuracy is capped at 0.845 however many verifiers join, versus ≈1 for an independent majority of 101. A sealed first batch of m ≥ 3 lifts the cap (0.875 at m=3, 0.949 at 11) and beats a majority of the same paid size. Recursion checked against fully Bayesian simulated agents. Stylised, symmetric signals. Pure-Python, 9 tests.

How many verifiers does deterrence-by-audit need?

Stake sets the audit rate a = G/(G+S); the audits then form a queue with offered load R = λas. When most verifiers miss a fault, who should be believed?

If a fault is flagged by fewer than half of the verifiers (0.45 detection, 0.05 false alarms), majority vote is wrong on faulty jobs with probability 0.84 at n=101 and 0.96 at n=301. The surprisingly-popular rule (verdict plus predicted verdict fraction) reduces for truthful Bayesian verifiers to an exact fraction threshold θ = v/(1−u+v) and errs 2.1×10−5 on faulty jobs at n=101, needing 47 votes for 10−3 total error against 36 for a Bayes rule that knows the detector. What is a seat in a prediction market worth?

In a sequential LMSR verification market the verifier in position k earns in expectation b·I(y; sk | s<k), a conditional mutual information, so seat rents telescope to the maker’s expected loss (at most b ln 2); an explicit cost-function simulation agrees to three decimals. Rents fall with position, the successive ratio approaching e−C (Chernoff information) from below; order only redistributes a fixed total (a 0.9-accurate verifier gets 0.368 of 0.407 first, 0.303 last). With entry cost c, accuracy improves roughly as 1/b: 11 verifiers at b=0.25 (92%), 41 at b=5 (99.6%). Myopic traders, symmetric signals, monotonicity numerical. Pure-Python, 8 tests.

How should a model be cut across devices of unequal speed?

For a fixed pipeline order, greedy filling is an exact test of a target stage time (agreeing with an independent dynamic program, with and without memory caps), and every order satisfies W/V ≤ T* < W/V + m wmax/V. A speed-blind equal split costs exactly v̄/vmin times the lower bound (2.40× at log-speed spread 0.5, 8.25× at 1.0) while speed-aware cutting stays within 1.4% at 240 layers. What does it cost to skip the slowest workers?

Waiting for the fastest k of n workers has exact round time s + (Hn−Hn−k)/μ for exponential tails, and for Pareto tails the mean is finite iff n−k+1 > 1/α, so with α ≤ 1 waiting for everyone has infinite expected round time. For SGD on a quadratic the time to accuracy has a minimum k and an interior optimum (34× faster than waiting for all at zero fixed cost, 1.12× at large fixed cost). If speed correlates with data, the fastest-k average is biased by Σ(πi/k−1/n)θi; Horvitz–Thompson reweighting removes it but only pays when the bias is large.

Does a bigger swarm learn more from shared rollouts?

Not without incentives. With diminishing returns and a peer-rollout discount θ, symmetric Nash play gives every agent exactly the learning level of a lone agent whatever the swarm size (total effort caps at (a/c−1)/θ), while the efficient level grows linearly in N: welfare per agent is 4.5× higher at N=256. At θ=1 a single agent generates everything. A Pigouvian matching subsidy covering θ(N−1)/(1+θ(N−1)) of cost implements the optimum exactly, and junk rollouts beat honesty iff c−ε > p(τ+F).

What does it cost to make workers race?

If a round pays the first k of n finishers a prize R and workers buy speed at cost c per unit rate, the win probability’s slope at the symmetric point telescopes to (1−k/n)(Hn−Hn−k), so equilibrium speed is R(n−k)(Hn−Hn−k)/(nc) and the round time is exactly nc/((n−k)R). Paying every finisher buys no speed at all; for a fixed budget, speed alone wants winner-take-all (0.10 at k=1 versus 3.2 at k=16 for n=32), and only the statistical floor on k justifies more winners. Recruiting more workers barely helps: total speed bought saturates at kR/c. Exponential races, linear cost, symmetric risk-neutral workers; equilibrium checked on a grid. Pure-Python, 12 tests.

Who drops out of a race when workers have unequal costs?

If only the first k of n finishers are paid, a slow worker entering at a vanishing rate wins with probability rate × E X(k) of the fast workers, which gives an exact exit rule: workers with cost ratio cs/cf ≥ nf/(nf−k) stay out entirely, and paying k ≥ nf never excludes anyone (checked by best-entry payoffs at thresholds 4/3, 2, 4). At cost ratio 4 with four fast workers, k ≤ 3 shuts the slow ones out and k=4..7 readmits them (share of prizes 0.21→0.46 against a fair 0.5). The note also proves the telescoping identity behind the symmetric race and shows that with convex speed cost, headcount does buy speed. Two cost types, exponential races, grid-checked equilibria.

How should a router treat modules that are usually asleep?

If only awake modules report and each awake module’s wealth is multiplied by 1+η(ℓ̂−ℓi), the ledger is exactly budget balanced, sleeping wealth never moves (each regime keeps a private ledger), and a module’s regret against the market on its awake rounds is at most ln(1/πi)/η + ηni in its own awake time (the exponential variant pays ηT/8 and is not budget balanced). Admitting a module with wealth ε costs each incumbent ln(1/(1−ε))/η in its bound. With recurring regimes the ledger matches the per-round oracle (0.1004 vs 0.1000, regret about 2.7 whatever the number of specialists) while a forced-to-report Hedge sits at the generalist (0.30) and fixed share is worse (0.34–0.36). How should a verifier's vector report be scored?

Can a module game a ledger by choosing when to sleep?

Yes, if it declares after seeing its loss. The wealth ledger pays each awake module exactly its leave-one-out saving, ηWA(1−pi)Si, but a module that sleeps whenever its own loss exceeds θ earns ηpBθ(1−θ)/2 per unit wealth against an equal-skill honest rival (best 1/8 at θ=1/2) and takes the market from a 0.1 share in about 130 rounds, while the router’s realised loss stays at 0.5. How many skipped training steps hide in the loss noise?

If a worker paid for T steps runs only (1−f)T, the verifier’s log-loss test (null sd σ′, level α) hides exactly the compute at which the loss curve, inverted, crosses the noise band: L((1−f*)T) = L(T)eσ′(zα+zP). How much does an appeal ladder of juries buy, and what must appeals cost?

A dispute goes to 3 jurors; the loser may pay a fee (proportional to panel size) to appeal to 11, then 27. If the fee lies in the window V(1−q) ≤ j n < V q, only the wrongly ruled truthful side appeals and the final error is exactly the product of the tiers’ error rates; below the window both sides appeal and only the last tier counts, above it nobody appeals. With jurors 70% accurate and prize 100 the smallest deterring panel is 11 (23 at prize 1000, 41 at 104); the 3→11→27 ladder errs 2.4×10−4 at 5.8 expected juror seats, against 12.6% for a single 7-seat panel, while a 9-juror middle tier leaks 0.78 wrong-side appeals per dispute. If jurors share a common bias with probability ρ, error cannot fall below ρ(1−a) however many tiers are added. Independent jurors, myopic appeals, no juror incentives or bribery; exact results agree with 200,000-trial simulation.
