## 2026-10-30 · Milestone 44: how far do the rungs displace the pool optimum, and is a rank assumption on it enough for zero regret? (exploratory; no predictions were written before the script ran)
- Why: milestone 43 said a pilot-side guarantee needs an assumption on the optimum's own excess. The simplest one is rank displacement: x* is cut at a cut keeping k only if at least k rivals rank at or ahead of it, so if rho_j (rivals at or ahead of x* under cut j's score) is below k_j at every cut, regret is exactly 0 (the kappa = 0 case of milestone 39). `experiments/theory/optimum_rank.py` (no flow run, about 5 s) measures rho_j / k_j on the 40 cached cells and checks the implication over 200 tie-breaks each.
- Outcome (`optimum_rank_results.txt`): the implication holds (0 of the 11 guaranteed cells shows any regret). Cells whose worst rho/k is at most 1, 2 and 4: 13, 21 and 29 of 40; median 1.97. Where rho/k > 1, only 0.167 of tie-breaks reach zero regret on average.
- Reading: the assumption "x* ranks inside the survivors at every rung" is a guarantee in 11 of 40 cells and nearly one in three at most; it does not hold generally, and failing it by a factor 2 already loses the zero-regret outcome in most draws. The optimum is typically within about 2 survivor-widths of the cut line, which is why halving sees non-zero regret in the other cells. A usable pilot-side assumption would bound rho/k from above by a constant and then bound the excess among those rivals, which is the quantity milestone 43 showed cannot be got from a quantile.
- Limits: one table and tool version; x* is the pool optimum, so a pilot cannot compute rho (it is not observed without the full table); no tests (script only).
- Next: model rho/k across pool sizes (does it grow with n?) or detail routing, which needs OpenROAD.

## 2026-10-30 · Milestone 43: no quantile of the per-candidate excess g bounds halving regret in general (a counterexample; theory, no flow run)
- Why: milestones 41 and 42 found the q-quantile of g covers empirically except in cells where the pool optimum itself has a large g. This asks whether any q < 1 can be made to work as a theorem. `experiments/theory/quantile_counterexample.py` builds the smallest instance: one cut to n/3 survivors, the optimum (y = 1) ranked last by the rung, every other candidate at y = c ranked by index. Every other candidate has only equal-y rivals ahead of it, so g = 0 for all but the optimum, whose g is log c.
- Outcome (`quantile_counterexample_results.txt`; `tests/test_bound.py::test_no_quantile_of_g_bounds_halving_regret`, n = 30, 90, 300): the optimum is cut at the first cut, so realised regret is log c (0.693 for c = 2, 2.303 for c = 10), while the 0.5, 0.9 and 0.95 quantiles of g are 0 for every n tried (30 to 3000); the 0.99 quantile is 0 once n >= 100. In general the q-quantile is 0 whenever q <= 1 - 1/n, so for any fixed q < 1 and large n the claim "regret <= sum of the cut quantiles of g" is false.
- Reading: this closes the branch from 41 and 42. The empirical coverage (97.6% of pilot draws at q = 0.95) is a property of this table, where the optimum is rarely an outlier in g; it cannot become a certificate by choosing q or the pilot size, because the failure instance puts all of the regret on one candidate. Only q = 1 (the maximum, milestone 37's bound) is valid, and the pilot cannot certify a maximum (milestones 38 and 40). A pilot-side regret guarantee needs an assumption that bounds the optimum's own excess, not a better statistic.
- Limits: a constructed instance, not a claim that real tables look like it (the real table has 2 failing cells of 40 at q = 0.9); one cut; it shows quantiles cannot be a theorem, not that they are useless as a diagnostic.
- Next: state an assumption on the optimum's excess (for example, bounded rank-displacement of the optimum under the rung) that a pilot can check, or detail routing, which needs OpenROAD.

## 2026-10-30 · Milestone 41: does a quantile of the per-candidate excess g_x^(k) bound halving regret? (predictions fixed before the script ran)
- Why: milestone 40 showed no fixed multiplier turns a 16-point pilot estimate of iota^(k) (a maximum over candidates) into a bound. The extreme-value alternative: replace the maximum by the q-quantile Q_q of g_x^(k) over candidates x (g_x^(k) = k-th largest of max(0, log y(z) - log y(x)) over rivals z ranked at or ahead of x). A pilot of m iid candidates sees a value at or above Q_q with probability 1 - q^m (0.81 for q = 0.9, m = 16), so a quantile is the thing a pilot could certify. `experiments/theory/quantile_g.py` (no flow run) uses the full table to ask whether sum_j Q_q(g^(k_j)) bounds the realised regret of the optimum-seeking halving, over the 40 group x start cells and 200 tie-breaks, for q in {0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 0.95}.
- **Predictions (fixed now):** (1) At q = 0.9 realised regret exceeds the quantile bound in more than 5% of (cell, tie-break) draws, because the pool optimum is selected, not sampled (the winner's curse of milestone 4 again), so a quantile of g over all candidates under-describes g at x*. (2) The share of cells with a violation falls with q but is still above 0 at q = 0.95. (3) The q = 0.9 bound is under the spread of y in more cells than the max bound (32). Each is refuted by the opposite.
- Limits known up front: idealised (rivals come from the full table, not from the pilot); one table and tool version; a quantile bound is probabilistic at best and is not a theorem about the argmin.

### Outcome (`experiments/theory/quantile_g.py`, about 3 s, `quantile_g_results.txt`; 40 cells x 200 tie-breaks)
- Share of draws with realised regret above the quantile bound: q = 0.5 0.511, 0.75 0.286, 0.9 0.0500, 0.95 0.0250. Cells with any violation: 21, 12, 2, 1 of 40. The q = 0.9 bound is under the spread of y in 38 cells (the max bound: 32).

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 39: an optimum-anchored halving bound (theorem plus check; exploratory, no predictions written before the script ran)
- Why: milestone 37's bound maximises over every candidate x, which is why it stays about 3 times the realised regret. Only the pool optimum x* matters for regret. If x* survives every cut regret is 0; if its first cut is j, the best survivor of that cut is within kappa^(k_j) (the k_j-th largest excess over x* among rivals ranked at or ahead of x*; 0 if fewer than k_j rivals, since then x* cannot be cut), and each later cut costs at most iota^(k_l). So regret <= max_j [kappa^(k_j) + sum_{l>j} iota^(k_l)] <= sum_j iota^(k_j), because kappa <= iota. `experiments/theory/anchored.py` (about 10 s, no flow run) checks it on the 40 cached cells with 200 tie-breaks each; `tests/test_bound.py` checks validity and the ordering on 400 random two-cut instances.
- Outcome (`anchored_results.txt`): 0 violations. New bound is below the milestone 37 bound in 23 of 37 cells with a positive old bound (equal in the rest), median new/old 0.89; non-vacuous (under the spread of y) in 33 cells against 32; median bound over the largest realised regret 2.77 against 3.02; 3 cells have bound 0.
- Reading: anchoring at the optimum is valid but buys little (about 11% at the median): the slack is mostly in the later cuts' iota^(k), which still maximise over all candidates. It also still needs the full table. A tighter certificate has to anchor those later cuts too (to the best survivor, which depends on the tie-break), or give up the worst case.
- Limits: one table and tool version; survivor counts n/3, n/9, n/27 only; nothing here is pilot-checkable.
- Next: put the theory (milestones 35 to 39) into the paper's section 4 as stated, or study anchoring the later cuts.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6: a regret bound for successive halving from the largest rung inversion (theory), planned before running
- Why: milestones 3 and 4 explain *that* plain halving from a biased rung loses and that per-point conformal coverage does not transfer to the argmin, but give no bound. The proposal's Method asks for a regret bound for halving as a function of the bias and the number of rungs, with the place where it is vacuous. This step states and proves one, then checks it against the cached table.
- Definition: for a rung r with score s_r and routed value y, the **inversion** iota_r = max over pairs (x, x') with s_r(x) <= s_r(x') of log y(x) - log y(x') (clipped at 0): the worst the rung can do by ranking x ahead of or level with x' when x is in fact worse by that factor. It needs no calibration and no model of the bias; it is invariant to monotone rescaling of the rung.
- **Proposition (to prove in `theory/BOUND.md`):** successive halving that keeps the top k of n entrants by s_r at each cut, and answers with the best routed value among the final survivors, returns y with log y - log y* <= sum of iota_r over the cut rungs (the final routed rung is exact). Proof is by induction: if the optimum of the entrants is cut, at least k entrants rank at or above it, and each of them is within iota_r of it. A property test on random instances checks the statement (it can only fail if the proof does).
- Experiment (`experiments/theory/bound.py`, no flow run): for each of the 20 design x clock groups of the cached table compute iota_1, iota_2, iota_3 over the pool; the realised log-regret of full-pool halving from R1 and from R2 (eta = 3, n0 = pool size, 200 random tie-breaks); the bound; and the ratio realised / bound. Also what a routed pilot of m = 10 random points sees of iota_r (its own max inversion over the pilot pairs, a lower bound by construction), 200 draws.
- **Predictions on record:** (1) the bound holds on every group (it is a theorem; a violation is a bug). (2) It is vacuous for R1 (iota_1 near the whole spread of routed values, since R1 ranks like noise: bound above 30% on most groups) and informative only for R2 at the relaxed clock (iota_2 under about 5%). (3) It is loose where it is informative: realised regret is typically a small fraction of the bound, because it charges the worst pair, not the pair that matters. (4) A 10-point pilot sees only a small part of iota (median under half), so iota is not pilot-checkable at the sizes that make the pilot cheap; the probabilistic version (a quantile of inversions instead of the maximum) is the open part and goes in the paper as such. If (4) is wrong and the pilot recovers most of iota, the "pilot-checkable bias" assumption of the proposal is realistic.


### Outcome (`experiments/theory/`: `BOUND.md` proof, `bound.py` about 10 s, `src/verified_dse/bound.py` with 4 new tests)
- **The bound is proved and holds in all 40 cells** (20 groups x halving from R1 and from R2, realised mean log-regret never above the sum of inversions; the property test in `tests/test_bound.py` checks 500 random instances including ties). Prediction (1) held, as it must for a theorem.
- **Prediction (2) half held.** The generic-gate rung is vacuous: iota_1 is above 0.3 (a 35% factor) on 8 of 10 groups per clock, median 0.36, so halving from R1 has a bound of 0.71 (tight) and 0.63 (relaxed) in log units. But I expected the mapped rungs to be informative at the relaxed clock (under 5%) and they are not: median iota_2 is 0.16 (tight) and 0.14 (relaxed), with only 3 of 10 groups below 0.05 at the relaxed clock, so the R2 -> R4 bound has median 0.35 / 0.23. A single bad pair in a pool of 50 sets iota.
- **Prediction (3) held strongly.** Realised regret of full-pool halving is 0.10 (tight) / 0.06 (relaxed) from R1, median 15% / 11% of its bound, and 0.000 median from R2 (it finds the routed optimum on most groups; it is not equal-budget, n0 is the whole pool). The worst-pair bound explains why plain R1 halving can lose and says nothing about how often.
- **Prediction (4) was wrong in the useful direction.** A 10-point random pilot sees a median 0.67 / 0.56 / 0.52 of iota_1 / iota_2 / iota_3 (over 19 to 20 groups, medians over 200 draws), above the half I predicted; per group it ranges from 0 to 1 (crc16 R3 relaxed: 0, because one extreme pair). So a pilot gives the order of magnitude of the inversion but never a certificate: its maximum is a lower bound by construction.
- Limits: one table (20 groups, pools of about 50, one corner); iota is measured on the pool that is searched, so on a new design it is an unknown, estimated only through a pilot; the bound ignores k_j and eta; no probabilistic (quantile) version yet, which is the part the paper needs for a guarantee with a stated probability; the proposition is simple enough that a bandit paper may state it already (not exhaustively checked).
- Next: the quantile version (replace the maximum with a conformal quantile of pairwise inversions, tie to the milestone-4 pilot and test coverage of the bound); then a larger or second-corner design set so the pool is not the limit; then milestone 7 (paper draft).


## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 5: is equivalence-proof success predictable from cheap features (H5), planned before running
- Why: H5 asks whether a classifier on cheap features predicts Yosys/ABC proof success or timeout, with AUC at least 0.8 on rewrites of held-out designs (refuted below 0.7). The "rewrite" is an ABC pass sequence from the milestone-1 space; the "proof" is a SAT miter of the rewritten gate netlist against the design's `synth -noabc` netlist (Yosys 0.33 `miter -equiv -make_assert` then `sat -prove-asserts`, per-proof timeout 10 s, wall time recorded).
- Data (`experiments/proof_success/run.py`): the 9 combinational designs of milestone 1 (the FIFO is sequential and is excluded; a one-step miter would prove nothing) x the same 60 sequences (seed 0) x 1 proof each, 4 worker processes. Deterministic flow; proof times are wall-clock and noisy, which is part of the question.
- Label fixed now: success = proof finished within 3 s (primary); 1 s and 10 s reported as sensitivity. Designs whose labels are all one class cannot be scored in leave-one-design-out and are reported as such. Designs are the unit: AUC computed per held-out design with both classes present, plus the pooled out-of-fold AUC.
- Features, all computed before the proof: sequence length, count of each of the 7 passes, rewritten netlist cells and depth (rung 1), reference cells, ratio of cells to reference, input bit-width. Models: logistic regression (standardised) and gradient boosting, leave-one-design-out. Baselines: sequence length alone, cell-count ratio alone, and the design-level base rate (what a model that only knows the design does).
- **Prediction on record:** most of the signal is which design it is (mult8 and mac4 are the only ones likely to time out), so the pooled AUC will pass 0.8 while the within-design AUC (the part a search could use to pick which rewrites to try) will be near 0.5 to 0.65; leave-one-design-out AUC with design-level features will be erratic with 9 designs. H5 is judged as worded (held-out designs): I expect it to be supported pooled, and I will report within-design AUC separately because that is the practical question. If the labels are almost all "success" at 3 s the test is vacuous and I will say so.


### Outcome (`experiments/proof_success/`: `proof_run.py` about 2.8 min + 5.7 min on 4 cores, `analyse.py` seconds; 752 proofs, 15 designs)
- **The first sweep could not test H5.** On the 9 combinational designs of milestone 1, 8 prove every one of their ~50 rewrites in under 0.1 s (median 0.01 to 0.05 s); only the 8-bit multiplier is hard (median 11 s; 17 of 50 proved within the 10 s timeout). No design had a counterexample (ABC's rewrites are sound, as expected). So at the 1 s and 3 s thresholds the held-out design either has all-success labels or, for mult8, all-failure: no held-out AUC exists. The prediction "labels almost all success, test vacuous" held.
- **5b (added after seeing this, so not predicted in the plan above):** the same 60-sequence draw on multipliers of width 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 (`mult_op`, parameter W; registered in `proof_run.py` so `flow.py` is untouched). Proof time is a steep function of width: median 0.01 s (W=4), 0.06 (5), 0.35 (6), 2.1 (7), 11 (8, 17/50 within 10 s), and 0/50 within 10 s at W=9 and 10. So "hard" is almost entirely a property of the design, not of the rewrite.
- **Pooled leave-one-design-out AUC passes 0.8 and means little.** Success within 3 s: logistic regression 0.989 (all features) and 0.994 (rewrite features only), gradient boosting 0.73; within 1 s 0.97 / 0.96; within 10 s 0.997 / 0.998 (gbt 0.83). The oracle predictor "the design's own success rate" scores 1.0 / 1.0 / 0.997, so the model is reading design size (reference cell count, and the cell count of the rewrite, which is nearly the same number) and nothing about the rewrite. This is the prediction on record (pooled passes, within-design weak), with the added observation that gradient boosting does much worse than logistic regression across held-out widths because trees cannot extrapolate beyond the sizes seen.
- **Within a design the picture is weak and uneven.** The only design with enough of both classes is mult8 at 10 s (17 of 50 proved). Leave-one-design-out AUC for held-out mult8: 0.70 (logreg, all features), 0.76 (rewrite features only), 0.65 (gbt); 5-fold CV inside mult8: 0.78 +/- 0.02 (logreg), 0.74 +/- 0.03 (gbt). The one-feature baseline "smaller netlist than the reference" (cells / ref_cells) alone gets 0.81 within mult8, higher than the models, and sequence length alone 0.60. So a rewrite that shrinks the netlist is the easier proof, which is a one-line rule, not a learned model.
- **Verdict on H5.** As worded (held-out designs, AUC at least 0.8, refuted below 0.7): *pooled, supported, vacuously*, since the AUC is the design-difficulty signal; *within a hard design, between the thresholds* (0.65 to 0.78 over the model/protocol variants, a one-feature baseline at 0.81), so neither supported nor refuted. I would not claim "proofs are predictable" in the paper; the claim that survives is "proof difficulty is set by the design's size and structure, and the rewrite's cell-count change carries a modest within-design signal". That gives the search no help in choosing which rewrites to verify.
- Limits: one proof engine (Yosys 0.33 `sat`, one solver, one timeout; ABC `cec`, `dsec` or a different solver would shift every threshold); wall-clock on a shared 4-core container is noisy, and the 10 s label for mult8 is censored; the only hard family is multipliers, whose hardness is well known to grow quickly with width, so "held-out design" is really "held-out width"; 60 sequences per design with 50 usable; no sequential designs; features are the cheap ones named in the plan (no AIG-structure similarity between the reference and the rewrite, which is the obvious next feature since SAT hardness for miters depends on shared internal equivalences).
- Next: add a structural-similarity feature (fraction of AIG nodes equal under simulation signatures, i.e. how many internal equivalences a SAT sweeper could use) and test whether it explains the mult8 within-design variation; add a second hard family with variation inside each design (wider MAC, a checksum tree) so there is more than one design to hold out on; then milestone 6 (theory).


## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4: conformal shortlist (H4), planned before running
- Why: H4 asks whether a conformal shortlist keeps nominal coverage of the routed optimum. Replayed on the cached rung_bias table (20 design x clock groups, about 50 points each), no flow re-run.
- Two protocols, `experiments/conformal/replay.py`. (A) leave-one-design-out rank shortlist: nonconformity of a group = the rank (fraction of the pool, ties broken at random) at which the rung ordering first reaches a point within epsilon of the routed minimum; the shortlist is the top q-hat fraction, q-hat the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))-th smallest score over the other designs' groups (both clocks). (B) per-design pilot: m random points get routed (R4) values, OLS of log R4 on log rung score, leave-one-out over-prediction residuals, one-sided conformal bound L = fit - q-hat; a point stays on the shortlist if L is at most the best pilot value. Success = the best of pilot plus shortlist is within epsilon of the pool minimum; cost = m + shortlist routed evaluations. alpha in {0.1, 0.2}, epsilon in {0, 0.02}, rungs R1 and R2, m in {10, 16}, 200 random pilots or tie-breaks per group. Compared with random search given the same number of routed evaluations.
- **Predictions on record:** (A) valid on average but near-useless for R1 (shortlist close to the whole pool, since tau is about 0), shorter for R2 only at the relaxed clock; coverage across designs may fall under nominal because designs are not exchangeable (n = 9 designs is small). (B) per-point coverage transfers poorly to the argmin (winner's curse: the optimum is selected for having a low residual), so coverage of the optimum falls more than 2 points below nominal in at least one setting, which would refute H4 as worded; the shortlist beats random search of equal cost only with R2 at the relaxed clock. If (B) holds nominal everywhere, the winner's-curse worry is unfounded here.
- Limits known up front: 20 groups, one corner, pool of about 50, residuals from 10 to 16 points are noisy, coverage is measured over groups of a single benchmark.

### Outcome (`experiments/conformal/`, 20 design x clock groups, 200 tie-breaks or pilots per group; `replay.py` runs in about 35 s, deterministic)
- **(A) Leave-one-design-out rank shortlist: coverage holds, usefulness depends on the rung.** Coverage over groups is at or above nominal in 6 of 8 cells and within the bootstrap noise in the rest (R1, alpha 0.2, eps 0.02: 0.807 against 0.80; intervals are wide, about +/-0.15, because only 9 designs calibrate). But the R1 shortlist must hold 89% of the pool at alpha 0.1 (63% at 0.2), i.e. no saving; the R2 shortlist holds 33% (18% at alpha 0.2) at coverage 0.90 (0.85).
- **(B) Per-design pilot with a conformal lower bound: H4 is refuted for R1 and over-satisfied for R2.** R1 coverage of the routed optimum is 0.651 against nominal 0.80 at m = 10, alpha 0.2, eps 0 (0.595 at m = 16): 15 to 20 points below, far outside the 2-point tolerance, and no better than random search of equal cost (0.606, 0.569). At alpha 0.1 it is 0.882 to 0.889 against 0.90 (inside 2 points) but again about the same as random search with the same evaluations (0.83). With R2 the shortlist covers 0.99 against 0.90 (alpha 0.1) and 0.90 to 0.92 against 0.80 (alpha 0.2) while routing 42 to 57% of the pool; random search with the same number of routed evaluations finds the optimum 0.53 to 0.65 of the time. So R2 gives a real saving (about half the routed runs, guaranteed above nominal) and R1 gives a guarantee that fails.
- Prediction check: winner's-curse undercoverage for R1 held (and is the programme's answer to why per-point conformal coverage does not transfer to the argmin); R2 beating equal-cost random search held, but at both clocks pooled, not only relaxed (not split by clock). The (A) prediction of R1 shortlists near the whole pool held (89%). I did not predict the R2 over-coverage of 0.99, so the bound is conservative here; I have not checked why (a guess: the leave-one-out residual of a 10-point fit overstates the error of the full fit, untested).
- Limits: 20 groups from 10 small designs on one corner; groups, not points, are the unit, so coverage differences of a few points are within noise; clock-split coverage not reported; the over-coverage of R2 means the shortlist is larger than necessary (a clock-aware or heteroscedastic residual would shrink it); pilot points are drawn from the pool being searched (their cost counts as routed evaluations, not as a separate calibration set); no theory yet for why the argmin undercovers (milestone 6); `replay.py` runs at import, so it has no unit tests (same as milestones 2 and 3).
- Next: split B by clock and by design; a heteroscedastic residual; milestone 5 (proof-success model, H5).

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3: successive halving across rungs, plain versus pilot-calibrated (H2, H3), planned before running
- Why: milestone 2 showed a one-shot screen loses to random search, which says nothing about successive halving (SH), which spends cheap rungs adaptively and keeps a widening survivor set. This replays SH on the same cached 1,200-run table, same cost model, same seeds, so it is paired with milestone 2's random and hill-climbing numbers.
- Strategies at equal wall-clock budget B (5, 10, 20, 30, 45, 60 s), 400 paired seeds per cell, regret = routed best found / pool minimum - 1: (a) `sh_r1`: draw n0 points from the pool, rank at R1, keep the top 1/3 for R2, again for R3, again for R4 (eta = 3); n0 is the largest whose total cost fits B (survivors pay only the incremental stage time, R2 pays its full Yosys + OpenROAD-to-netlist cost since it is a different flow from R1); (b) `sh_r2`: the same starting at R2; (c) `sh_cal`: spend 6 routed (R4) evaluations as a pilot (charged to B, and eligible as answers), compute each rung's Spearman rho against R4 on the pilot, start SH at the cheapest rung with rho >= 0.5 (R4 random search if none), the rest as (a). Baselines are milestone 2's random R4 search on the same seeds.
- **H2 (as stated in the proposal):** SH beats random R4 with a paired 95% interval excluding 0 on most (>= 6 of 10) designs at some budget below 60 s. **H3:** `sh_r1` loses more than 5% (mean regret) on at least one design at some budget that `sh_cal` brings within 5%.
- **Predictions on record:** H2 will mostly fail on this table (routed point costs only about 8x R1, the pool is about 50 points, so cheap rungs save little); `sh_r1` will be near random at the relaxed clock and worse at the tight clock because R1 ranks like noise and its eta = 3 cut discards the optimum two thirds of the time; `sh_r2` will beat `sh_r1` at the relaxed clock; the 6-point pilot is too small to separate tau 0.6 from 0.4 reliably, so `sh_cal` will sometimes start at R1 wrongly. If SH wins clearly the milestone-2 reading (cheap rungs do not pay here) is wrong.
- Limits known up front: pilot of 6 gives a noisy rho; pool of about 50 caps n0; costs are means over the table, not per point.

### Outcome (`experiments/halving/`, 10 designs, 400 paired seeds per cell, one core; `replay.py` runs in about 50 s)
- Mean routed regret over designs, % (tight clock; random R4 / SH from R1 / SH from R2 / calibrated SH): 10 s 6.3 / 11.4 / 8.9 / 5.9; 20 s 3.3 / 10.3 / 5.9 / 4.4; 30 s 2.1 / 10.1 / 4.5 / 3.6; 45 s 0.94 / 10.1 / 2.9 / 2.9. Relaxed clock, 45 s: 0.55 / 8.3 / 1.7 / 1.6. Full table in `experiments/halving/results.txt`.
- **H2 is not supported as a mean-regret result and only partly as a win count.** At the relaxed clock SH from R2 beats random R4 with a paired interval excluding 0 on 8 of 9 defined designs at 5 s and 6 to 7 of 10 at 20 to 45 s, which meets the proposal's literal "most designs" test, yet its mean regret is higher (1.7 vs 0.55 at 45 s) because one or two designs lose heavily (barrel16 about 12%, the ripple adder about 5%). At the tight clock it wins on 3 to 4 designs and loses on 4 to 6. The calibrated variant is the only one with a lower mean than random, and only at 10 s (5.9 vs 6.3, 3 wins, 0 losses tight; 6/0 relaxed). Mean regret is not the proposal's criterion, so I report both and do not call H2 refuted; the honest summary is "wins where R2 is faithful, loses badly where it is not, net no gain on this table".
- **Plain SH from R1 is the worst strategy at every budget of 10 s or more** (about 10% regret at the tight clock and 8% relaxed, flat once its n0 reaches the pool size: it never stops mis-ranking at R1). My prediction held. Why it is flat: with eta = 3 and an R1 ranking that is noise, the optimum survives the first cut with probability about 1/3 and the pool is too small to compensate.
- **H3 holds, with a caveat that matters.** Plain SH from R1 loses more than 5% on 8 of 10 designs at the tight clock (alu8 16.9%, fifo8x4 17.0%, barrel16 11.8% at 60 s) and the calibrated variant brings alu8 to 0.8%, fifo8x4 to 2.8% and barrel16 to 3.0%. But simply starting at R2 recovers alu8, crc16, mult8 and cmp_sat12 as well, so most of the recovery is "do not use R1", which a 6-point pilot only finds when it is lucky. The calibrated variant beats SH from R2 at 20 to 30 s tight (4.4 vs 5.9, 3.6 vs 4.5) and ties it from 45 s (2.85 vs 2.87), and it rarely beats random per design (0 to 1 of 10 from 45 s). Its worst design is the ripple adder (15.5%), whose routed scores take four distinct values, so a rank-based pilot cannot calibrate it.
- Prediction check: random-like SH R1 at relaxed clock held (worse, not equal); `sh_r2` beat `sh_r1` at relaxed (held); the small pilot sometimes starts at R1 wrongly (held: its tail at 5 s tight is identical to random because the pilot eats the whole budget).
- Limits: n0 is capped by the pool (about 50), so beyond about 30 s SH stops using the budget while random keeps drawing: the 45 s and 60 s columns favour random for that reason, the comparison is only fair up to about 30 s. eta fixed at 3 and a single pilot size; costs are table means; designs are small. The "(wins/losses)" counts cover only designs where the strategy was defined at that budget. No NSGA-II, Hyperband, BOHB or BO yet.
- Next: milestone 4 (conformal shortlist, H4), and a larger design set so the pool is not the limit; also SH with a per-design eta and a pilot-sized sweep.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 1: rung-bias benchmark (H1), planned before running
- Design set (10): adder (`+`, ripple, W=16), 8-bit multiplier, 4x4 MAC, 8-bit ALU, 16-bit popcount, 16-bit barrel shifter, CRC-16 byte update, 12-bit saturating add, 8x4 FIFO. Original small RTL, MIT; four are copied from `chip-pnr`/`chip-ppa-sky130mac`.

- Rungs: R1 generic-gate cells x depth (Yosys/ABC `-g`); R2 SKY130 HD mapped area x no-wire OpenSTA period; R3 post-detailed-placement area x period; R4 global route + `repair_timing` area x period (the reference, "routed"). Score = area x period (um^2 ns), as `chip-scriptsearch`. Flow is `chip-pnr`'s OpenROAD script, 40% utilisation.
- Clock target: the design's own baseline (empty-sequence) no-wire period ("tight", where wires force repair) and 1.5x that ("relaxed"), to see whether the answer depends on repair pressure.
- Analysis fixed now: Kendall tau-b of each rung against R4 per design and period, 95% bootstrap interval over sequences (2000 resamples); regret of the rung's pick (mean over ties) = R4(pick)/min R4 - 1; whether the rung's top 5 contains the R4 optimum. H1 holds if R1's tau is below 0.7 on at least 4 of 10 designs (at the tight period) and is refuted if tau is at least 0.7 on 9 or more. Also report R2 and R3, and rung wall times.
- Expectation I am on record with: R1 will do worst, R2 will already be close to R4 on most designs. If R1 is fine, H1 is refuted and the programme's premise (cheap rung is biased) needs rethinking for pass-sequence spaces.

### Outcome (same day, `experiments/rung_bias/`)
- 10 designs x 60 sequences x 2 clock targets = 1,200 runs; ABC aborted on 196 (all of them contain a `dch` followed later by `rewrite`/`refactor`, though 44 successful runs have that pattern too, so it is a necessary, not a sufficient, condition; 10 of the 60 sequences per design failed), leaving 50 usable sequences per design (52 for the ripple adder, whose 4 distinct routed scores make it nearly uninformative).

For the ripple adder R1 is constant, so tau is undefined (counted as uninformative). R1's pick costs a median 13.5% (tight) and 20.0% (relaxed) of routed area x delay.
- **My expectation was half wrong.** I expected R2 (liberty-mapped, no wires) to be close to routed on most designs. At the tight clock target it is not: tau below 0.7 on 6 of 10 designs, median 0.59, median pick regret 10.5%. At the relaxed target it is: tau below 0.7 on 3 of 10, median regret 0.0%. Repair pressure, which R2 cannot see, drives the bias. R3 (after placement) is no better than R2 at the tight target (7 of 10 below 0.7), because the timing repair that follows is what reorders designs.

The routed rung (0.46 s mapping + 0.80 s OpenROAD = 1.3 s) is only about 8x the generic one (0.15 s) here, so the savings multi-fidelity search can claim on this benchmark are small; the benchmark needs larger designs before H2 is testable.
- Caveats: one library corner and one utilisation (40%); "routed" is global-route-estimated with `repair_timing`, not detailed-routed; sequences are the same 60 for every design; the clock targets are tied to each design's own baseline, so targets differ across designs; bootstrap intervals resample sequences within a design, not designs; no multiple-comparison correction.
