# When is a cheap rung safe to trust? Rung bias and a pilot-checkable bound for multi-fidelity search over open-source chip flows

**Draft for MLCAD (6 pages when typeset); not submitted.** Programme `verified-dse`, milestone 7. Written with AI assistance
(Claude Code). Every number below is copied from a committed results file named beside it; `sh reproduce.sh` regenerates
them from the cached flow tables. No affiliation with any lab is claimed. Submission is an owner step.

## Abstract
Open RTL-to-GDS flows put a place-and-route run within reach of a laptop, but a search over ABC pass sequences needs
hundreds of runs, so practitioners rank candidates on a cheap rung (generic-gate count and depth, liberty-mapped
synthesis, placement) and trust it. We measure how far that trust is justified on SKY130: 10 small designs, 60 ABC pass
sequences each, two clock targets, four rungs, 1,200 runs, with routed area x delay as reference. The generic-gate rung is
uninformative to anti-correlated with the routed ranking (median Kendall tau-b -0.04 at the tight clock); mapped and
placed rungs mis-rank at a tight clock (median pick regret 9 to 10.5%) and are almost exact at a relaxed one. Replayed at
equal wall-clock, cheap-rung screening and plain successive halving **do not beat random or hill-climbing search** on this
table (about 10% against 0.9% mean routed regret at 45 s). We give a three-line worst-case bound (regret of halving is at
most the sum of the cut rungs' *inversions*), show it is vacuous for the generic rung, and test pilot-checkable
substitutes empirically: a statistic built from the pilot's best three points against the rest covers the realised
regret in all 40 group-start cells at a 16-point pilot (minimum 0.81) but is informative only where the rung is already
faithful. A conformal shortlist on the mapped rung retains the optimum 0.99 of the time at nominal 0.90 while routing
42 to 57% of the pool; on the generic rung it is no better than random. A proof-success predictor is not supported.

## 1 Introduction and related work
Multi-fidelity search is mature for hyperparameters: GP bandits with known maximum bias (Kandasamy et al., 2016,
arXiv:1603.06288), max-value entropy search over fidelities (Takeno et al., 2019, arXiv:1901.08275), successive halving
(Jamieson & Talwalkar, 2015, arXiv:1502.07943), Hyperband and BOHB (arXiv:1603.06560, arXiv:1807.01774), with BoTorch as
software (arXiv:1910.06403). Chip-flow search papers mostly use one fidelity: ABC pass-sequence learning
(Yu, Xiao & De Micheli, 2018, arXiv:1804.05714), surveyed in Huang et al. (2021, arXiv:2102.03357). Mirhoseini et al.
(2021, doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03544-w) and its reassessment (arXiv:2302.11014) are the reason we use equal-budget
baselines. Conformal prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021, arXiv:2107.07511) supplies the distribution-free tool for
shortlists. The related-work claims rest on titles and abstracts returned by `research/_shared/cite_check.py`, not on a
full reading of every paper (`PROPOSAL.md`). The inversion bound of Section 4 may be a special case of known
successive-halving analyses; we do not claim it as new.

## 2 Setup
Designs: 10 small combinational designs plus one FIFO (`designs/`). Space: 60 ABC sequences of length 0 to 3 over
`balance`, `rewrite`, `rewrite -z`, `refactor`, `refactor -z`, `dc2`, `dch`; 10 fail in ABC and are dropped, leaving pools
of 50 or 52. Rungs: R1 generic gates (cells x depth, 0.15 s per point), R2 liberty-mapped without wires (0.8 s), R3 after
placement (1.0 s), R4 routed with `repair_timing -setup` (1.3 s), all one core. Score: area x period; clock targets are each
design's own no-wire baseline (tight) and 1.5x that (relaxed). Flow: Yosys 0.33, OpenROAD 26Q2, SKY130 HD, one corner
(tt 25C 1.8 V), 40% utilisation. "Routed" is global-route-estimated, not detail-routed.

## 3 Results
**Rung bias** (`experiments/rung_bias/results.txt`). R1 tau-b against routed: -0.04 tight, -0.01 relaxed; the 95% bootstrap
interval lies below 0 for popcount16 and crc16 at the tight clock. R2: 0.59 / 0.73; R3: 0.50 / 0.76. Median pick regret
tight / relaxed: R1 13.5% / 20.0%, R2 10.5% / 0.0%, R3 9.4% / 0.0%. Timing repair, seen only by the later rungs, reorders
designs at the tight clock.

**Search at equal budget** (`experiments/search_baselines/results.txt`, `experiments/halving/results.txt`; 400 paired
seeds, replayed on the cached table). Mean routed regret at the tight clock: random R4 6.3 / 2.1 / 0.94% and hill climbing
7.4 / 1.7 / 0.61% at 10 / 30 / 45 s; screening all points at R1 then verifying the top 15.0 / 5.3 / 1.08%. Plain halving
from R1 is the worst strategy at every budget of 10 s or more. A 6-point routed pilot choosing the starting rung recovers
the designs plain halving loses (alu8 16.9% to 0.8%), but most of that gain is "skip R1". Net: no multi-fidelity gain at
equal wall-clock yet; routed runs here cost only about 8x the cheapest rung, so there is little to save, and the pool
caps halving beyond roughly 30 s.

**Conformal shortlist** (`experiments/conformal/results.txt`; 20 design-clock groups). Fit routed value from a rung on a
10 to 16 point routed pilot, keep points whose one-sided conformal bound could still beat the pilot's best. R2: optimum
retained 0.99 at nominal 0.90 (0.90 to 0.92 at 0.80) while routing 42 to 57% of the pool, against 0.53 to 0.65 for random
search with the same routed count. R1: 0.65 at nominal 0.80, no better than random, so the hypothesis fails for R1.

**Proof success** (`experiments/proof_success/results.txt`; 752 SAT-miter proofs, 15 designs, 10 s timeout). Rewrites never
failed a proof; time is set by the design (median 0.01 s at 4-bit multiplier, 11 s at 8, none of 100 proved at 9 and 10
bits). Pooled leave-one-design-out AUC 0.96 to 0.998 equals that of an oracle knowing only each design's success rate. Within
the one varying design (8-bit multiplier, 17 of 50 proved) AUC is 0.65 to 0.78; "the rewrite is smaller than the
reference" gets 0.81. We do not claim proofs are predictable from cheap features.

**Proof success under Yosys 0.70** (milestone 52, `experiments/proof_success/compare_y070.txt`; 900 launched, 751 usable, 751 shared with the Yosys 0.33 table). 0 counter-examples; 627 proved and 124 timed out at 10 s. So the reading that proof time is set by the design is not an artefact of the old solver. ## 4 A bound and what a pilot can check
Define the inversion of a rung, iota = max(0, max over pairs with s(x) <= s(x') of log y(x) - log y(x')). **Proposition.**
Successive halving with cut rungs r_1..r_m returns a value with log regret at most the sum of iota_{r_j}. Proof: if the
best survivor x of cut j-1 is cut, every survivor z has s(z) <= s(x), so log y(z) <= log y(x) + iota; chain over the cuts
(`experiments/theory/BOUND.md`; checked on 500 random instances and all 40 cells). It is vacuous for R1 (iota above 0.3 on
8 of 10 groups; bound 0.63 to 0.71) and loose for R2 (bound 0.23 to 0.35 where realised regret is about 0). A pilot's
maximum is a lower bound on iota, so a pilot can suggest but not certify it.

**A bound that uses the survivor count** (milestone 37, `experiments/theory/KTH.md`, `kth_results.txt`). Let iota^(k) be the largest,
over candidates x, of the k-th largest excess log y(z) - log y(x) among rivals z the rung ranks at or ahead of x. A cut keeping k
points loses at most iota^(k) (x is cut only if all k survivors are such rivals, and the best of any k of them is at most the k-th
largest), so regret is at most the sum of iota^(k_j); iota^(1) = iota. On the 40 cached group-rung cells the bound is never
violated (200 tie-breaks each), is below the old bound in 39 of 39 cells with a positive old bound (median ratio 0.54), and is
under the spread of y in 32 cells against 16. It remains about 3 times the largest realised regret and needs the full table. Anchoring the first cut at the pool optimum (milestone 39, `anchored_results.txt`) is also valid (0 violations) but lowers the bound by only about 11% at the median (lower in 23 of 37 cells).

**A rank assumption for zero regret** (milestones 44 and 45, `experiments/theory/optimum_rank_results.txt`, `optimum_rank_n_results.txt`; no flow run). Let rho_j be the number of rivals a cut's score ranks at or ahead of the pool optimum x*. A cut keeping k_j points can only drop x* if rho_j >= k_j, so rho_j < k_j at every cut makes regret exactly 0 (the kappa = 0 case of the bound above; no violation in 200 tie-breaks of each of the 11 guaranteed cells). The assumption holds in 11 of 40 cached cells (cells whose worst rho/k is at most 1, 2 and 4: 13, 21 and 29 of 40; median 1.97), and where it fails 0.167 of tie-breaks still reach zero regret on average. Subsampling the pool to n' = 20, 30, 40 and 50 candidates, the median worst rho/k rises 2.33, 2.60, 3.00, 3.00 and the share with rho/k at most 1 falls 0.168, 0.130, 0.086, 0.050; this is partly built in (the last cut keeps one point, so its ratio is the raw count rho_3), and nothing above 50 candidates was measured. It is a diagnostic on the full table, not a pilot-side certificate: a pilot cannot observe rho, and milestone 43 shows a quantile of the excess cannot supply the missing bound.

**Pilot-checkable statistics** (`topk_results.txt`, `sweep_results.txt`; 200 to 300 pilots x 10 to 20 tie-breaks per
cell, 20 groups, both halving starts). Max over random pilot pairs undercovers (under 0.8 in 6 of 40 cells, worst 0.28).
The top-3-vs-all statistic covers in all 40 cells at m = 16 (minimum 0.81) but is a median 4.6 times the realised regret
and under 0.1 only for halving from R2 at the relaxed clock (7 of 10 groups). Across pilot sizes the share of regret-bearing
cells covered is 0.855 / 0.915 / 0.969 / 0.988 / 0.995 at m = 6 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24, with median bound 0.21 to 0.33.
Coverage far exceeds the probability that the pilot holds one of the pool's best three (0.32 at m = 6, matching the
hypergeometric formula), so that event is not the mechanism. The coverage is empirical, not proved.

**A pilot gate** (milestone 16, `experiments/pilot_gate/results.txt`; all 20 designs, both clocks, budgets of 10 to 40 routed-point equivalents, 400 paired seeds). A five-point pilot's tau(R2,R4) separates the 12 of 40 cells where cutting on R2 rank misses by over 10% from the rest with AUC 0.85 (0.74 using R2 against R3), but a gate that picks halving with eta = 1.5 or the no-R2-cut arm from that statistic is within 0.1 points of the simple no-R2-cut arm at 25 and 40 equivalents (1.31 and 0.34 against 1.40 and 0.44; random 1.74 and 0.39) and worse at some budgets. On 20 designs eta = 1.5 loses to random at 25 and 40 equivalents (2.12, 1.38) because of barrel64; an oracle gate would reach 0.98 and 0.04. Six gates were compared without correction.

**Observable rank displacement** (milestone 84, `experiments/theory/pilot_rho.py`, `pilot_rho_results.txt`; no flow run). Rung scores are known for the whole pool, so the rank-displacement ratio of milestone 44 is exact for every routed pilot point. Let T be that ratio for the pilot's best routed point. Over the 40 cells T is at least the unrouted optimum's ratio in a median 0.89 / 0.97 / 0.99 of pilots at m = 8 / 16 / 24 (27, 27 and 28 cells at 0.8 or more); calling T < 1 a certificate marks 0.70 of pilots at m = 16 in the 11 cells where the optimum meets the zero-regret condition, and 0.039 of pilots in the 29 where it does not. Empirical on this table, not a theorem.

**A bound on false certificates** (milestone 90, `experiments/theory/pilot_rho_bound.py`, `pilot_rho_bound_results.txt`; no flow run). Let G be the candidates whose ratio is below 1. Since ratio < 1 forces rho_last < k_last, and at most k_last candidates have that (ties counted ahead), |G| &le; k_last (here 1 for the three-rung start and 5 for the R2 start). A false certificate needs ratio(x\*) &ge; 1, so x\* is outside G, and T < 1, so the pilot's best routed point is in G. Hence for a pilot of m distinct points drawn uniformly, P(false certificate) &le; 1 &minus; C(n&minus;g, m)/C(n, m) &le; mg/n with g = |G|, using only the rung scores and nothing about y. On the 29 cells where the optimum fails the condition the bound is 0 in 22 (G is empty, so no pilot can issue a false certificate) and the empirical false-certificate share of milestone 84 is at most the bound in all 29 at m = 8, 16 and 24 (largest empirical/bound 0.71, 0.63, 0.48). This explains the 4% figure above: it comes from 7 cells. It is a bound on how often T < 1 is wrong, not on regret when T &ge; 1, and it does not say how often a valid certificate is issued (0.49 to 0.85 of pilots in the 11 guaranteed cells). **Power** (milestone 92, `experiments/theory/pilot_rho_power.py`): a valid certificate needs the pilot's best point in G, so its probability is at most 1 &minus; C(n&minus;g, m)/C(n, m); in the 11 guaranteed cells this holds with no violation and is within a median 0.09 of the empirical share (0.49 / 0.72 / 0.88 at m = 8 / 16 / 24; median pilot size for a 0.9 hit probability, 18). **Exact power** (milestone 93, `experiments/theory/pilot_rho_exact.py`, `pilot_rho_exact_results.txt`): T < 1 holds exactly when the pilot's y-best point lies in G, so with y-tie groups g of size s_g (B_g points strictly better, G_g of them in G) P(T<1) = &sum;<sub>g</sub> (G_g/s_g)[C(n&minus;B_g, m) &minus; C(n&minus;B_g&minus;s_g, m)]/C(n, m); every guaranteed cell has duplicated y, so the tie-free rank formula is wrong in principle. Enumeration over all pilots of 60 small tied instances matches it, and in the 33 cell &times; m combinations none of the empirical shares (300 pilots) is beyond 3 binomial sigma of it (max |z| 2.32); the median exact power is 0.513 / 0.727 / 0.875 and the hit-probability bound exceeds it by a median 0.07 to 0.10. The formula needs y for the whole pool, so it chooses a pilot size in evaluation, not inside a live search.

**What a certificate says about regret** (milestone 95, `experiments/theory/pilot_cert_regret.py`, `pilot_cert_regret_results.txt`; no flow run, same pilots as milestone 84, 200 halving tie-breaks per cell, cells weighted equally). Read from the practitioner's side, a certified pilot (T < 1) comes from a cell where the optimum meets the zero-regret condition with probability 0.818 / 0.872 / 0.907 at m = 8 / 16 / 24, halving then has zero regret in 0.830 / 0.873 / 0.907 of draws (0.360 / 0.315 / 0.276 after an uncertified pilot) and a mean relative excess of 1.3% / 1.1% / 0.8% (6.6% / 7.0% / 7.4% uncertified). It is not a bound: tight/adder_op16 from the R2 start is not a guaranteed cell, yet 0.36 to 0.50 of its pilots are certified and its mean relative regret is 13.9%, so a certificate can sit on a cell with a large loss. Two of three predictions held (precision about 0.87; certified above uncertified); the 0.9 floor on P(zero regret | certified) at m = 16 and the 5% ceiling on any certified cell's mean regret were refuted.

**How a pilot ceiling depends on pilot size and ladder (milestones 97 to 104; all cached-table analyses, no flow run).** Any certified pilot point (ratio < 1), not only the pilot's best, gives a ceiling c = min y over certified pilot points that the returned cost stays under: 0 violations in about 145,000 to 253,000 draws at m = 8 to 24, a ceiling in 0.242 / 0.367 / 0.422 of pilots against 0.166 / 0.221 / 0.257 with T < 1, but a 3x looser one for the extra pilots (mean excess 10.2% against 3.0% at m = 16; milestone 97). The share of pilots with a ceiling flattens at about 0.45 (0.140 at m = 4, 0.448 at m = 40) while the ceiling's mean excess falls from 5.5% to 0.7% (milestone 99): a larger pilot tightens the guarantee but does not make it more likely. Milestone 100 counts why exactly: the plateau is the share of cells containing any certified point, 18 of 40; all 20 three-rung cells (r1 to r3) have none at the default keeps and 18 of the 20 two-rung cells have two to five. Milestones 101 to 103 show that default is a schedule effect, not a rung property (this corrects a reading in milestone 102): with keeps (n/3, n/9, K) cells with a certified point at K = 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 / 8 are 0 / 2 / 6 / 6 / 7 of 20; widening the middle keep adds one cell at most; the first keep is what binds, and with F = 8 / 16 / 25 / 35 / 50 of the 50-point pool kept through r1 (and keeps 16 then 8) 6 / 8 / 12 / 18 / 18 of 20 three-rung cells are certifiable, the two left being adder_ripple16 (ties). Milestone 104 prices that: the F = 35 three-rung schedule costs less wall-clock than the two-rung ladder in all 20 cells (median ratio 0.91) and certifies the same 18, but neither saves much over routing the whole pool of 50 (under half of its cost in 1 of 20 cells). So a certificate is available on this table, at a cost close to routing everything: the cheap rungs do not pay at this pool size. Limits: n = 50, one eta, mean per-cell stage times from one single-core run (the 9% is inside plausible timing noise), no regret measured for the wide schedules; detail-routed mult24/mult32 cells, where the routed rung is dearer, are the test of whether the saving grows.

**Choosing the keeps without peeking, and pool size (milestones 105 to 107; cached table, no flow run).** Milestone 105 measures regret, which the schedules above did not: the three-rung schedule (keeps 35, 16, 8) has mean regret 1.18% against 2.27% for the two-rung ladder (16, 5), and both beat random routing of the same count (5.38% and 7.86%); the three-rung one is at most as bad in 18 of 20 cells. A certificate is not the optimum: the two-rung ladder misses the routed optimum entirely in 5 cells and barrel16 is worse than random routing at both clocks, because its rung scores are anti-informative. Those schedules were chosen after seeing the table, so milestone 106 selects them leave-one-design-out over a grid (F in 16/25/35/50, M in 8/16, K in 3/5/8): all ten folds choose (50, 8, 8), "skip r1, score everything at r2, keep 8, route 8", with held-out mean regret 0.95% against 1.17% for (35, 16, 8) and 0.77% for the in-grid oracle; the edge is carried by mult8 and crc16. Milestone 107 shrinks the pool by random subsampling (N = 20 / 30 / 40): skip-r1 has regret 0.45 / 0.92 / 1.00% against 0.73 / 1.19 / 1.06% for the 105-style schedule and 3.39 / 4.41 / 4.77% for random routing of 8, better in only 5 to 6 of 10 designs. Regret falls as N shrinks (a prediction we got wrong) because routing 8 of 20 covers a larger share of the pool, so regret is not comparable across N. Limits: ten small designs, subsamples of the same 50-point pools, 20 subsets and 100 to 200 tie-break draws rather than a bootstrap over designs, cost not re-priced; the gain over the 105 schedule is a small mean shift within noise.

**A fresh, larger pool (milestone 108; flow run).** All of the above is on one 50-point table. Milestone 108 runs 120 new ABC pass sequences, disjoint from the original 60, through the four rungs on the same ten designs and clock targets (Yosys 0.70, OpenROAD 26Q3; 103 or 104 usable points per cell), with predictions fixed before the run. The held-out choice survives: skip-r1 (N, 8, 8) has mean regret 1.04% against 5.49% for (round(0.7N), 16, 8) and 8.00% for random routing of 8. The gap is concentrated: adder_ripple16 has four distinct generic-gate scores, 87 of 104 points share the lowest, and the routed optimum sits in the second group, so a first keep of 73 by r1 cannot contain it (34.3% and 39.6% regret, worse than random); barrel16 tight adds 12.7%. Excluding those two designs the schedules are equal (1.30% against 1.29%), and cell by cell skip-r1 wins 7, ties 9 and loses 4 (sign test p = 0.55). We therefore recommend skipping the generic-gate rung as protection against a catastrophic cut on tie-heavy cheap scores, not as an average gain. The mapped rung's median tau-b against routed is 0.55 / 0.65 (tight / relaxed) on the new pool against 0.59 / 0.73 on the original, and the generic-gate rung's stays near zero, under a tool-version change that confounds the comparison.

**Priced at equal wall-clock (milestone 109; no flow run).** The comparison above holds the routed count at 8; skip-r1 scores about 103 points at the mapped rung where (round(0.7N), 16, 8) scores 72 and pays for the generic-gate rung instead. With milestone 104's per-cell stage-time model on the fresh table, skip-r1 is dearer in 18 of 20 cells (median ratio 1.08) and costs a median 0.69 of routing the whole pool (0.64 for the other schedule). Random routing given the same wall-clock routes 35 to 79 points and reaches mean regret 1.26% against skip-r1's 1.04% (skip-r1 better in 14 of 20 cells, sign test p = 0.115) and 1.43% against the 105-style schedule's 5.48% (9 better, 11 worse). On designs this small, where the mapped rung is about 70% of a routed point, a rung ladder is therefore a robustness choice rather than a saving; the saving the programme asks about needs designs whose routed rung is far dearer than the mapped one.

**Held-out designs (milestone 110; no flow run).** Milestones 105 to 109 used only the ten small designs. Six cached tables hold 26 other designs (52 cells of 50 to 60 points, two clocks) that informed no schedule choice, among them the 16 to 40-bit multipliers where a routed point costs up to 22 times a mapped score. With the same schedules, regret and cost model, and predictions fixed before the replay, skip-r1 has mean regret 4.38% against 4.89% for (round(0.7N), 16, 8) and 5.95% for random routing of 8. At equal wall-clock random routing has the lower mean (2.31%), yet skip-r1 is better in 37 cells and worse in 13 (sign test p = 0.001; design-bootstrap 95% interval of the mean difference -1.3 to +6.9 points): the mean is carried by a few cells where the mapped rung misleads (bcastmix192 at 49.9% in both clocks, barrel64 at 26 to 29%, fanao96 at 11%). Split at a routed-to-mapped cost ratio of 3, fixed in advance, the 23 dear-routing cells give skip-r1 a median cost of 0.26 of routing everything and lower regret than equal-cost random routing (2.07% against 3.59%, 18 of 23 cells), while the 29 cheap-routing cells favour random routing (1.29% against 6.21%; these two means are post hoc). The multi-fidelity saving the programme asks about therefore exists on this benchmark, but only where routing is several times dearer than mapping, and it carries a tail risk from unfaithful mapped scores that a schedule alone does not remove.

**A switching rule (milestone 111; no flow run).** Milestone 110's split suggests a rule that needs no routed score: measure the routed-to-mapped cost ratio on one point, run skip-r1 above a threshold theta and equal-cost random routing below it. With theta chosen from {1.5, ..., 8} by leave-one-design-out on the 52 held-out cells, every fold picks 3 and the held-out mean regret is 1.66%, against 2.29% for random routing and 4.38% for skip-r1 at the same wall-clock, with the worst cell at 13.2% instead of 49.9% (theta = 3 was also milestone 110's pre-registered split, so the selection is not independent of it). A guard that routes a pilot of p = 3 to 6 random points and abandons the mapped ranking when tau-b on the pilot falls below tau0 does not help: the selected guard is the pure hedge (six random points plus the mapped best two), the tau-b test flags bcastmix192 in 94 to 100% of draws but its false alarms elsewhere cost more, and adding the hedge to the switch changes 12 of 52 cells, six each way. On the fresh pool, which chose nothing, the switch routes randomly in 18 of 20 cells and has 1.21% against 1.04% for skip-r1 and 1.18% for random (13 cells worse than skip-r1, 5 better, p = 0.10); the test set has almost no dear-routing cells, so the ladder branch of the rule is still untested out of sample.

**The switch's ladder branch out of sample (milestone 112; flow run).** Milestone 111's test pool had routed/r2 ratios of at most 3.4, so only the random branch was tested. We ran milestone 108's 120 fresh sequences (disjoint from the held-out tables' 60) through all four rungs on six held-out designs whose ratio exceeded 3 (mult16, mult24, xbar16, gf2mat48, barrel128, bcastadd64), both clocks, Yosys 0.70 and OpenROAD 26Q3: 1,440 runs, pools of 102 to 103 points. With every parameter fixed from milestone 111 and the predictions committed before the table was read, all 12 cells have ratios above 3 (3.6 to 19.5), so the switch runs skip-r1 everywhere: mean regret 1.05% against 3.16% for equal-cost random routing, better in 9 cells and worse in 3 (sign p = 0.15; design-bootstrap 95% interval of the difference -3.95 to -0.06 points). The losses are mult24 at the relaxed clock (6.46%, milestone 9's unfaithful family) and xbar16 at both clocks (3.18%, 2.43%). The six-point random hedge that milestone 111 selected by 0.07 points in-sample has 3.55% here, worse than random routing: in pools of about 103 points it leaves only two routed slots to the mapped ranking. The rule we recommend is therefore the switch alone. These designs informed theta, so this is a fresh-sample, not a fresh-design, test.

**Designs that chose nothing (milestone 113; flow run).** Eight designs no earlier table had run (mult18, mult22, a 20-bit squarer from a family never run, a 32-port crossbar, gf2mat64, barrel32, popcount32, adder_op32), milestone 108's 120 sequences, both clocks, Yosys 0.70 and OpenROAD 26Q3, 1,920 runs, predictions committed before the flow. With theta = 3 fixed, the switch has 0.91% mean regret against 2.64% for equal-cost random routing and 1.44% for always running skip-r1 (better than random in 9 cells, worse in 1, sign p = 0.021; design-bootstrap interval of the difference -3.54 to +0.05 points). Ten cells have ratios above 3 and there skip-r1 beats random (1.32% against 4.09%); at or below 3 random beats skip-r1 (0.22% against 1.63%). The oracle switch would have 0.26%; the 0.65-point gap is one cell, mult18 at the relaxed clock (10.48% against 1.00%), so the misleading mapped rung belongs to relaxed-clock multipliers as a family, not to one width (`experiments/fresh_designs/results.txt`).

**No guard from the mapped rung alone (milestone 114; no flow run).** Skip-r1 scores every point at the mapped rung anyway, so any statistic of that table is free. We tried two: the median mapped slack as a share of the clock period (the relaxed multipliers sit at +0.31 to +0.34) and the coefficient of variation of the mapped delay across sequences (0.31 to 0.38 there, against 0.21 and 0.22 for mult16 and mult20, whose relaxed cells the ladder handled; the mapped delay does not depend on the clock, so the spread is the same at both clocks). A guard flags a cell above the cost threshold and routes randomly instead. Chosen on the 52 held-out cells among 34 thresholds, the guard slack >= 0.15 and spread >= 0.3 flags exactly the five relaxed mult24 to mult40 cells and cuts held-out regret from 1.66% to 1.15% (1.25% leave-one-family-out). On the 28 dear-pool test cells of milestones 112 and 113 it misses both relaxed multipliers (mult24 0.26, mult18 0.19 spread) and raises false alarms in one cell, bcastadd64 relaxed, raising mean regret from 0.97% to 1.16% (design-bootstrap interval of the difference 0.00 to +0.57 points); four of the six predictions failed. Comparing the six designs both tables contain explains why: slack is a property of the design and clock (it moves by at most 0.10 between tables) but the spread is a property of the sequence pool (on the fresh sequences it fell by 11 to 35% in five designs and rose by 23% in gf2mat48). In hindsight the best of the 34 guards on the test cells (slack >= 0.3) reaches 0.89%, only 0.08 points better than no guard, flagging 7 cells to catch 2. The relaxed-multiplier failure is not visible in the mapped rung's slack or spread; a guard needs information from a later rung (`experiments/r2_guard/results.txt`).

**A mapped score clipped at the clock (milestone 115; flow run).** The guard was the wrong question. The ladder fails only at the relaxed clock, with the same top 8 that succeed at the tight one, because the routed flow repairs timing up to the period and stops. On every relaxed multiplier the median routed delay is 0.993 to 1.000 of the period, so the routed area x delay ranks by area alone, while the mapped score still rewards mapped speed. Scoring a point as mapped area x max(period, 1.25 x mapped delay) copies that saturation. alpha = 1.25 was chosen on the 52 held-out cells (1.66% to 0.77% in-sample, no gain leave-one-family-out, because the gain is in one family). We then ran seven new designs blind (mult14, mult26, sq16, sq24, xbar32x2, gf2mat40, gf2mat56; 1,680 runs; predictions committed first). The switch's mean regret falls from 2.73% to 0.83%, better in 5 of 14 cells and worse in none (design-bootstrap interval -3.63 to -0.41 points), and every relaxed cell on the ladder reaches 0.00% (mult26 from 9.58%, sq24 from 11.30%). The clip can hurt where the routed delay sits far below the period. On an earlier table seen during the exploration, barrel128 at the relaxed clock (routed delay 0.40 of the period) went from 0.00% to 19.2% (`experiments/clip_score/results.txt`).

## 5 Limitations and threats
Ten small designs, one FIFO, one corner, one tool version for the headline tables (milestone 48 re-measured the ten-design table under OpenROAD 26Q3 / Yosys 0.70: the qualitative conclusions reproduce, R4 scores correlate across versions at only tau 0.44 median and the tight clock target moved in 16 of 20 cells, `experiments/rung_bias_26q3/`), pools of about 50 so every strategy converges to 0 regret,
global-route estimates, one proof solver and timeout. Pilots are one third of the pool at m = 16. Coverage is over pilots
and tie-breaks within a fixed group, not over designs. Hyperband was run in its simplest form (one bracket per start rung, equal budget split; `experiments/hyperband/`): it does not beat random search at equal wall-clock on these pools but halves the worst-design regret of SH from R2 at the relaxed clock (4.6% vs 11.8% at 45 s). No BOHB baseline yet; the smallest model-based baseline we ran, a greedy ridge regression of routed cost on R2 features refitted as points are routed (`experiments/mf_surrogate/`, milestone 20), is worse than random R4 and than never cutting on R2 at budgets of 15, 25 and 40 routed-point equivalents (mean regret 4.8, 3.1, 1.7% against 3.1, 1.4, 0.4% for the latter), and stays at 19% on barrel64; adding an exploration term (a lower confidence bound or epsilon-greedy, milestone 21, `experiments/mf_explore/`, run once before it was logged) improves every variant over greedy, about halving barrel64's regret at 40 equivalents, but none beats never cutting on R2 or random R4 at any budget (mean regret 4.5, 2.8, 1.3% for the best lower-confidence-bound variant against 3.1, 1.4, 0.4% for the former). A GP Bayesian-optimisation baseline (exact GP with an RBF kernel on the R2 features, expected improvement or lower confidence bound, fixed untuned hyperparameters; milestone 22, `experiments/mf_gp/`, 100 seeds) closes barrel64 at 40 equivalents (0.13%) but still loses to never cutting on R2 at every budget (best mean regret 3.8, 1.9, 0.74% against 3.0, 1.5, 0.45%), losing on 23 of 40 cells at 25 and 40 equivalents. BOHB's model, a TPE density ratio over the same features inside the same single-pool search (milestone 23, `experiments/mf_tpe/`, 100 seeds; the bracket schedule is not reproduced), is weaker than the GP: best mean regret 4.3, 2.3, 1.1% against 3.0, 1.5, 0.45% for never cutting on R2, and 7% on barrel64 at 40 equivalents. NSGA-II likewise. Several
effects rest on a handful of designs; there is no multiple-comparison correction.

**Tool-version sensitivity of the fanout result** (milestones 73 to 76, `experiments/fanout_sweep/`). On the same 60 pass sequences, R2 pick regret on the width-parameterised one-hot mux is 16 / 19 / 27 / 109% (W = 24, 48, 96, 192) under Yosys 0.33 and 0 / 0 / 0 / 16% under Yosys 0.70, both with OpenROAD 26Q3: with the sample fixed, the tool version alone moves pick regret by up to 93 points. What survives both versions is the dose-response of the rank correlation of period (falling with W) and of the routed-to-mapped period ratio (rising). A trend of pick regret with fanout is therefore not claimed as a property of the flow. Two intermediate releases (0.47, 0.51) split the flow's `abc -g` gate list at the commas when it is passed with `-p`; with the commands in a script file (`-s`, which reproduces the 0.70 table exactly) they run and give identical results to each other: pick regret 1.4 / 0 / 0 / 0.9% and tau of period 0.33 / 0.52 / 0.03 / -0.04, so at W = 192 pick regret is 109, 16 and 1% for Yosys 0.33, 0.70 and 0.47/0.51 (milestone 77). The mapped period carries no rank information at W = 192 under any version (tau between -0.04 and 0.10).

## 6 What the paper still needs
Findings that used to be listed here now sit in Sections 3 and 4. What is open:
1. Full detail-routed cells for mult24 and mult32 (about 12 core-hours per 50-point cell; only 12 and 4 spread points so far) and an OpenROAD run of the larger designs at the ss corner (liberty-only so far).
2. A causal fanout result that does not depend on the Yosys version (Section 5).
3. BO/BOHB baselines on larger pools and with structurally unfaithful R2 beyond the multiplier family.
4. A pilot-side regret certificate for the top-vs-all coverage (milestone 96 proves the part that holds without assumptions: a pilot with T < 1 guarantees the returned cost is at most the pilot's best, 0 violations in 1.5 million draws, a ceiling about 2.7 times the realised regret at m = 16 \[`experiments/theory/pilot_cert_guarantee_results.txt`\]; milestone 97 shows the same ceiling holds for the lowest-y certified point of any pilot, not only a pilot whose best point is certified: the share of pilots that yield a guarantee rises from 0.22 to 0.37 at m = 16, with a ceiling about three times looser for the added pilots (10.2% against 3.0% mean excess over the pool minimum; 0 violations in 618,450 draws \[`experiments/theory/pilot_cert_anypoint_results.txt`\]); the optimum-relative part stays open; milestone 90 proves a false-certificate bound for the rank-displacement certificate only; a regret certificate from a pilot's observed excesses stays open and Section 4 says what is excluded).
5. Final typesetting to the MLCAD template. `paper/main.tex` (IEEEtran conference class, generated from this file by `paper/build.sh`; `paper/main.pdf`, 6 pages) is a working draft in that class; the official MLCAD style file and page limit still need checking against the call for papers.

Until (1) to (3) land, the honest headline is the negative one: on small designs, cheap rungs did not pay, and a pilot gate that detects when they will not has not been shown to pay for itself on the 30 designs it was replayed on (20 in milestone 16, five each in milestones 27 and 28).
