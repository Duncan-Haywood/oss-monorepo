# civ-patterns research log

## 2026-11-01 · Outcome: regimes age out of risk; big empires are memoryless; era beats age over a century

- Results: `experiments/regimes/results.txt`, `experiments/empires/results.txt`; figure `experiments/hazard_by_age.svg`.
- **H1 held** (Gates et al. 2006): Kaplan-Meier median 4.8 years for anocracies, 8.9 for democracies and 7.3 for autocracies; Weibull hazard ratio against democracy 1.89 (1.63 to 2.19) for anocracies and 1.45 (1.23 to 1.69) for autocracies.
- **H2 held for democracies and anocracies, not for autocracies**: k = 0.81 (0.74 to 0.88) and 0.86 (0.81 to 0.91), but 0.95 (0.88 to 1.02) for autocracies. The piecewise hazard shows why: democracies fall to 0.5% a year after age 40, while anocracies and autocracies plateau near 4.5 to 5.5%. The risk is not monotone either. It peaks in years 2 to 5 (12%), not in year 0 to 2 (9%).
- **H3 held**: Weibull+type beat the exponential by 0.015 Brier (95% bootstrap 0.007 to 0.022, n = 696, units repeat across cutoffs). Unplanned addition, logged here as such: a piecewise hazard by type was better still. Fitted on the 40 years before each cutoff, it beat Weibull+type by 0.015 (0.005 to 0.026) and was the best calibrated, but its log loss was worse (0.696 against 0.668) because empty cells give near-zero rates. All models under-forecast endings after 1960 by about 10 points.
- **H4 refuted for all polities, held for big empires**: k = 0.83 (0.80 to 0.87) overall; for the 224 that ever reached a million km², k = 0.91 (0.82 to 1.02), and the AIC barely prefers Weibull (0.8). Most of the overall ageing is early mortality: 1.1% a year before age 25, then about 0.6% a year to age 400. Big empires' mean observed life is 123 years against Arbesman's 220. That is a different sample and definition: Cliopatria splits a dynasty when its name changes.
- **H5 refuted**: the hazard ratio per tenfold initial area is 1.06 (1.00 to 1.13), and the largest-start quartile has the shortest median (69 years). Large starts are often modern colonial or wartime entities.
- **H6**: medians are 350 years (Bronze Age, n = 26, 100-year snapshots), 153 (1000 BCE to 0), 98 to 117 (0 to 1800) and 46 (polities begun after 1800, 433 of them).
- Backtest of 100-year state survival (cutoffs 1600 to 1900): base rates swing from 0.35 to 0.79, and age-based models score AUC 0.46 to 0.61 within a cutoff and lose to the constant hazard on pooled Brier (+0.006, 0.003 to 0.009). Over a century the era, not the age, is the information.
- Not done: covariates beyond type and age; scoring the 2018 forecasts against 2019 to 2024 events (V-Dem ERT); a calendar-time hazard for Cliopatria.

## 2026-11-01 · Plan: regime and polity lifespans on real data (written before the runs)

- Data: Polity5 regime spells, 1800 to 2018 (Marshall and Gurr 2020, `p5v2018d.xls`, downloaded at run time, not redistributed), and Seshat's Cliopatria v0.2.1 (Chalstrey et al. 2025, CC BY 4.0, 1,587 named polities from 3400 BCE to 2024 CE).
- Regimes. H1 (Gates et al. 2006): anocracies (Polity -5 to 5) have the shortest median duration; democracies and autocracies last longer. Refuted if the anocracy Kaplan-Meier median is not below both. H2: a regime's yearly risk of ending falls with its age (Weibull shape k < 1 with the 95% interval below 1). Refuted if the interval contains 1 or k > 1. H3: a Weibull model with regime type beats a constant-hazard baseline at forecasting which regimes end within 10 years, out of sample at cutoffs 1966, 1976, 1986, 1996 and 2006 (pooled Brier difference, bootstrap interval below 0). Refuted otherwise.
- Polities. H4 (Arbesman 2011, on Taagepera's 41 empires): lifespans are close to exponential, a constant hazard with no ageing. Refuted if the Weibull k interval excludes 1. H5: polities that start larger last longer (hazard ratio per tenfold initial area below 1, interval excluding 1). Initial area, not peak area, so a long life cannot cause a big number. H6 (exploratory, no direction): the median lifespan differs across the era a polity started in.
- Analysis: censoring at the end of each dataset; spells or polities already running at the start of a dataset (1800 for Polity, 3400 BCE for Cliopatria) are dropped because their age is unknown. Cliopatria's snapshots are coarse before about 1500 CE, so durations are measured to the snapshot spacing; I report the spacing by era and check the main fits on post-1500 polities. Nothing here is a forecast about a named country's politics beyond what a duration model fitted to its history says; the write-ups will say so.
