# Fees on a verification market: an exact no-trade band, a dead-market cliff, and the price of self-funding

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
A logarithmic market scoring rule (Hanson) subsidises its informed traders by at most `b ln 2`. Charging a proportional fee `f` on the cost of every purchase is the obvious way to recover that subsidy in a verification market (`market-routing`, `market-manipulation`, `order-flow-liquidity`), but it changes what the price can say. I derive (i) an exact no-trade band `[q/(1+f), (q+f)/(1+f)]` for a risk-neutral trader with belief `q`, of width `f/(1+f)` for every `q`, so a lone trader leaves the price at `q/(1+f)` and it lags belief by `qf/(1+f)`; (ii) the smallest signal that moves the price at price `p`, `logit(p(1+f)) − logit p`, which at `p=½` is exactly `2 atanh f`, so a signal of accuracy `a` moves a fair-coin market iff `f < 2a−1`; (iii) fee revenue on a monotone price path, exactly `f b ln((1−p₀)/(1−p_T))`, and the trader's exact profit net of fee; (iv) a simulation of ten sequential 0.65-accurate traders: the fee that brings the maker to break-even (≈0.11) raises Brier error of the final price from 0.115 to about 0.16 (+38%), the median gap to the full-information posterior is 0.20, revenue is hump-shaped in `f` (peak ≈0.17 near `f=0.1`) and is zero at the cliff `f = 0.30` where every signal is ignored. Fees buy solvency with information; the exact curve is the price.

## 1. Model
Binary LMSR with liquidity `b`, price `p`. Moving the price up to `p'` (buying yes) costs `b ln((1−p)/(1−p'))`, down (buying no) `b ln(p/p')`; the fee is `f` times that cost. A risk-neutral trader with belief `q` buys yes while `q > p(1+f)` and buys no while `1−q > (1−p)(1+f)`. Hence trading stops at the nearest edge of `[q/(1+f), (q+f)/(1+f)]`, whose width is `f/(1+f)`; `trade_target` and the profit-maximiser test in `tests/` confirm the edge is the exact optimum of the trader's profit `q x − (1+f) c`. Round trips pay the fee twice, and fees on gross purchase cost are not netted against held positions, a design choice that stiffens the band relative to fee-on-net-position.

## 2. Signal thresholds and the dead-market cliff
A trader who treats the price as sufficient (`q = σ(logit p + λ)`) trades iff `λ > logit(min(1,p(1+f))) − logit p` when buying yes, and the mirror image for no. The threshold is infinite once `p(1+f) ≥ 1`, so near the boundary a price cannot be corrected toward it (E2: `f=0.1`, `p=0.05`: yes-side 0.10 but no-side impossible; `p=0.95`: reverse). At `p=½` the threshold is `ln((1+f)/(1−f)) = 2 atanh f`, so a signal with LLR `λ = ln(a/(1−a))` moves the market iff `f < tanh(λ/2) = 2a−1` (checked at `a = 0.55, 0.65, 0.8`: cliffs 0.10, 0.30, 0.60). Above that a fair-coin market never leaves ½ and yields no fee revenue: raising fees past the signal's edge collects nothing.

## 3. What a fee raises and what it costs
For a monotone path revenue is exactly `f b ln((1−p₀)/(1−p_T))` (E4: `f b` × 0.22, 0.92, 2.30, 3.91 for `p_T` = 0.6, 0.8, 0.95, 0.99). For a lone trader `q=0.8` at `p=½`, `b=1` (E5): profit 0.193, 0.118, 0.050 at `f` = 0, 0.1, 0.25; the fee takes 9%, 34%, 62% of the trade's gross edge at `f` = 0.02, 0.1, 0.25 (E6). With ten sequential traders (accuracy 0.65, 4000 markets, `b=1`, E3), the maker's mean net P&L moves −0.327 (f=0) → −0.250 (0.02) → −0.147 (0.05) → −0.023 (0.1) → +0.070 (0.2) while Brier goes 0.115 → 0.119 → 0.132 → 0.159 → 0.208 and log loss 0.367 → 0.498 (f=0.1) → 0.609 (f=0.2). Mean fees are hump-shaped: 0.058, 0.118, 0.171, 0.155, 0.021, 0 at f = 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.29, 0.31. Median |p − full-information posterior| is 0.05, 0.11, 0.20, 0.33 at f = 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2. So covering the whole subsidy costs about 38% more Brier error, and beyond the revenue peak a higher fee both collects less and knows less.

## 4. Limits
Traders are risk-neutral, unconstrained in size, and naive (they read the price as a sufficient statistic, so lag compounds; a fully sophisticated trader who models the band would infer more from the price and lose less). The fee is on gross cost, single binary market, i.i.d. signals of equal accuracy, no adversary (see `market-sandwich` for fee thresholds against sandwiching, `market-manipulation` for manipulation cost). Fixed-`b`; interaction with time-varying liquidity (`anytime-liquidity`) and with the Kelly-market alternative (`kelly-markets`) is untreated. Related: Hanson (2003), Chen and Pennock (2007) on LMSR and cost-function markets, Abernethy–Chen–Vaughan (2013) on cost-function markets, Glosten and Milgrom (1985) whose spread is the natural fee benchmark, Frongillo's work on market design. Synthetic data.
