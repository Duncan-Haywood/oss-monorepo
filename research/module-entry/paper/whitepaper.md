# Admitting new modules to a routing market: a Kraft identity for entry shares

**Abstract.** A modular, continually learning system adds expert modules over time, and a Hedge/LMSR router must decide what share of the routing mass a newcomer gets on arrival. We show that the regret certificate of every expert against the mixture is `c_i/η + η·T_i/8` with `c_i = ln(1/π_i) + Σ_{j>i} ln(1/(1−π_j))` (own entry cost plus a tax from every later arrival), and that for *any* share sequence `Σ_i e^{−c_i} = 1`. Consequently `max_i c_i ≥ ln(n₀+K)`, with equality exactly for uniform entry `π_j = 1/(n₀+j)`, which is therefore the unique minimax entry rule and equalises all certificates at the static-Hedge value. Against a prior `P` over which module turns out best, the certificate-optimal shares are `π_j = P_j / Σ_{i≤j} P_i` with expected coefficient equal to the entropy of `P` (plus `P₀ ln n₀` for the initial pool). In a deterministic two-level game the price of entry is exactly `ln(1/π)/η` for a better arrival and `ln(1/(1−π))/η` for a worse one, independent of the size of the quality gap, and the catch-up time is `ln((1−π)/π)/(ηΔ)`. Simulation: uniform entry has max coefficient 2.77 versus 4.1–9.7 for constant-share rules, while under a recency prior the entropy-optimal shares cut expected regret from 3.11 to 2.70 nats. The results are elementary; the contribution is the exact bookkeeping and its checks. Losses in `[0,1]`, no delays.

## 1. Model
`n₀` initial experts have equal weight. After each arrival `j = 1..K` (at a known round) the router sets the newcomer's weight so that it holds posterior share `π_j` of the current mass, then runs Hedge with rate `η` on all experts (LMSR with liquidity `b = 1/η`). Regret against expert `i` is measured from its own arrival: `R_i = Σ_{t ≥ t_i} (ℓ_t − ℓ_{t,i})`.

## 2. Certificate and Kraft identity
Let `W_t` be total weight. Hoeffding gives `ℓ_t ≤ −(1/η) ln(W_t/W_{t−1}) + η/8` on ordinary rounds. An arrival at share `π` multiplies the total by `1/(1−π)`; that jump is not a learner loss, so it contributes `+ln(1/(1−π))/η` to the bound. Summing from expert `i`'s arrival (where it holds share `π_i`, and using `W ≥ w_i` afterwards):
`R_i ≤ c_i/η + η(T−t_i)/8`, `c_i = ln(1/π_i) + Σ_{j>i} ln(1/(1−π_j))`,
and for the initial pool `c = ln n₀ + Σ_j ln(1/(1−π_j))`. (`certificate`; the regret bound is checked against simulation on 30 random pools.)

**Kraft identity.** `e^{−c_i} = π_i Π_{j>i}(1−π_j)` is the probability that, running through the arrivals in order and accepting each with an independent coin of bias `π_j`, the *last* accepted arrival is `i` (an initial expert counts `1/n₀` of the event "no arrival accepted", probability `Π_j(1−π_j)`). These events are disjoint and exhaustive, so `Σ_i e^{−c_i} = 1` over all `n₀+K` experts. Tested exactly on 200 random share sequences.

**Corollary (minimax entry).** By convexity `max_i c_i ≥ ln(n₀+K)`, equality iff all `c_i` are equal, iff `π_j = 1/(n₀+j)`: adding an outcome as if it had been there from the start. The telescoping `Σ_{j>i} ln((n₀+j)/(n₀+j−1)) = ln((n₀+K)/(n₀+i))` shows each certificate equals `ln(n₀+K)` (tested on 100 random sizes; 2000 random alternatives never beat it). Any other rule favours some arrival position at the cost of another: constant `π = 1/2` has coefficients from 0.69 (last) to 9.70 (initial pool) at `n₀=4, K=12`; constant 0.2 from 1.61 to 4.06.

## 3. Priors on the champion
If the eventual best module is drawn from `P` (the initial pool as one block `P₀`, then each arrival), the expected coefficient `Σ P_i c_i` is minimised, by the Kraft identity and Gibbs' inequality, when `e^{−c_i} = P_i`, i.e. `π_j = P_j/S_j`, `S_j = Σ_{i≤j}P_i`: the conditional probability that arrival `j` is the champion given the champion is among the first `j`. The minimum is `H(P) + P₀ ln n₀`. Tested against 30 random alternatives for each of 50 random priors. A geometric recency prior `P_j ∝ ρ^{K−j}` gives shares that approach `1−ρ` for late arrivals; with `ρ = 0.8`, a constant `0.2` is nearly optimal (2.33 versus 2.30 expected coefficient).

## 4. The price of entry is exact
Take one incumbent with constant loss `½` and an arrival at share `π` whose loss differs by `Δ`. Incumbent share is `1/(1+k e^{ηΔt})`, `k = π/(1−π)` for a better arrival, so the summed regret to the arrival is `Δ Σ_t s_t → ln(1/π)/η` (integral `∫ ds/(1+ke^{cs}) = ln(1+1/k)/c`), plus an Euler–Maclaurin term `ηΔ s₀/2` in discrete time (tests assert both to 5·10⁻⁴). For a worse arrival the incumbents lose `ln(1/(1−π))/η`. **Neither depends on Δ**: entry is a tax fixed by `π` and `η`, so a tiny improvement and a large one both cost `ln(1/π)/η` before they are exploited. Table (η = 0.05, measured / theory): π=0.1 good 2.307/2.303, bad 0.106/0.105; π=0.02 good 3.917/3.912. The half-mass time is `ln((1−π)/π)/(ηΔ)` (tested within 1.5 rounds). Admission is worthwhile in expectation iff the arrival's remaining lifetime `Λ` exceeds `ln(1/π)/(ηΔ)`: with uniform entry into `n` identical modules (η=Δ=0.1) break-even is 161 rounds at `n=4` and 693 at `n=1024`, growing like `ln n`.

## 5. Simulation
`n₀=4, K=12`, an arrival every 100 rounds, Bernoulli losses with mean 0.5 and one champion at 0.3, `η=0.2`, 300 runs. Realised `η·R_i` versus the coefficient (uniform / half / constant 0.2 / recency-optimal): initial pool champion 3.42[2.77] / 11.51[9.70] / 5.02[4.06] / 6.84[5.62]; arrival 7: 3.25[2.77] / 4.93[4.16] / 3.30[2.73] / 3.24[2.67]; arrival 12: 2.68[2.77] / 0.80[0.69] / 1.72[1.61] / 1.66[1.55]. Realised regret sits 0.5–1.7 nats above the coefficient because `c` excludes the `ηT/8` sampling term. With the champion drawn from the ρ=0.8 prior (600 draws): uniform 3.11, half 3.53, constant 0.2 2.74, recency-optimal 2.70 against certificate expectations 2.77/2.95/2.33/2.30. Ordering agrees with the certificates.

## 6. Reading and limits
For verification the entry rule is a public schedule, so an auditor recomputes each certificate `c_i` from the published shares; uniform entry gives the same `ln N` guarantee as if all modules had been present. In market terms the same bookkeeping prices the maker's worst-case loss growth as outcomes are added (I have not proven a separate LMSR statement here). Limits: arrivals at known rounds with a fixed `η` (no anytime tuning, see `anytime-liquidity`); the bound is about regret from an expert's own arrival, not about switching regret between champions (fixed-share, see `market-routing`); losses are bounded and undelayed; no cost of training or integrating a module; the minimax result concerns the certificate, and the simulations show the certificate ranks rules correctly but is not an equality for realised regret.
