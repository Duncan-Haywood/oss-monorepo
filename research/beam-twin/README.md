# Beam twin: what approach speed does a lidar twin that returns every beam certify, when the real sensor's scans fade together?

Pure Python, no dependencies. A vehicle approaches a thin obstacle (0.5 m) that first becomes visible at `R0 = 30 m` (a pedestrian stepping out from behind a parked car), scanning at 10 Hz with 0.2° beam spacing, and must confirm it before its stopping distance `v·t_react + v²/2a`. With a uniformly random beam phase the number of beams on the obstacle is exactly `⌊λ⌋ + Bernoulli(frac λ)`, `λ = w/(rδ)`, so the miss probability is an exact product over scans, matched to a beam-pattern Monte Carlo (0.0156 vs 0.0158; 7·10⁻⁴ vs 8.5·10⁻⁴ at `m=2`). The real sensor returns each beam with `q = 0.9` but a scan *fades* (all beams lost together: wet or dark surface, glare, dust) with probability `s`; a twin calibrated on the mean return rate, `q_eff = (1−s)q`, with independent beams has no fades. A fade puts a floor of `s` under the per-scan miss probability, so the real miss probability is at least `sⁿ` for `n` scans while the twin's vanishes geometrically in the beam count. At certification level 10⁻³ the twin certifies 13.28 m/s against the real 12.00 at `s=0.3` and 12.94 against 10.87 at `s=0.5` (0.90×, 0.84×), and the real miss probability at the twin's speed is 0.09 and 0.125, i.e. 90× and 125× the level. Both miss probabilities are astronomically small at low speed (3·10⁻³⁵ real vs 6·10⁻³¹⁸ twin at 4 m/s) — the error in probability is huge, but the error in the *speed decision* is 10–30% because miss probability is a cliff in speed. It grows as the obstacle appears closer (0.79× at `R0=20`, 1.00× at 150 m). The error has the opposite sign when many beams are needed (`m=3`, 0.3 m obstacle): fades then help and the matched twin is too cautious (8.7 vs 10.7 m/s at `s=0.5`). Repair: estimating `s` from `n_cal = 20/50/200/1000` calibration scans leaves the real level violated with probability 0.42/0.22/0.07/0.00 (mean real miss 2.2·10⁻³/9.7·10⁻⁴/7.0·10⁻⁴/6.7·10⁻⁴). See `paper/whitepaper.md`.


**Builds on.** The sensor-simulation and sim-to-real perception direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>), the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>) and RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The stopping-distance model is textbook kinematics; dropout-aware lidar simulation is discussed in Zhao et al. (2020, survey) and Manivasagam et al. (2020, LiDARsim). Companions in this repository: `radar-clutter-twin` and `cfar-family-twin` (radar detection thresholds), `dropout-twin` and `autocorr-twin` (correlation the twin ignores), `terrain-twin` (same independent-vs-correlated structure for friction).

Stylised: one obstacle, one width, fade probability independent across scans, per-beam dropout independent and range-independent, constant speed, no motion blur, occlusion geometry reduced to a single first-visibility range `R0`, and the "real" sensor is itself simulated, so the Monte Carlo check tests the closed form, not the realism. No lidar logs. Miss probability is not strictly monotone in speed (integer scan counts), so the certified speed is a crossing found by bisection. MIT.
