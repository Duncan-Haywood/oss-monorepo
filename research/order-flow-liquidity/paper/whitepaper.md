# Order flow sets the liquidity: a Glosten–Milgrom view of LMSR verification markets

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Several projects in this repo price a verification claim with an LMSR of liquidity `b` and treat `b` as a free subsidy knob (`anytime-liquidity`, `market-manipulation`, `market-sandwich`). Here `b` is derived from who trades. In a Glosten–Milgrom (GM) market a fraction `μ` of traders are informed with signal accuracy `q` and the rest trade at random, so a trade is a buy with probability `(1±m)/2` in state 1/0, `m = μ(2q−1)`. Then (i) the Bayes posterior log-odds moves by exactly `k = ln((1+m)/(1−m)) = 2·atanh m` per net buy, so the competitive maker's price path *is* an LMSR price path with `b* = 1/k` (`b* = 4.98` at `μ=0.1`, `q=1`; 0.34 at `μ=0.9`), checked against a brute-force posterior to 2e-16; (ii) a wrong `b` costs log loss only in a transient, and the **peak-over-horizon** excess log loss is scale-free in `m` (constant to 2% for `m ≤ 0.1`) and asymmetric: `b*/2` (overconfident ×2) peaks at 0.079 nats, `b*·2` at 0.052; ×4 gives 0.40 vs 0.17 (overconfidence ≈1.5–2.4× worse); (iii) the two price paths coincide but the money does not: the GM maker earns exactly zero in expectation, while the LMSR at `b*` loses `→ b* ln 2` (1.12 at `m=0.3`, attained by n=320) because it prices at the path average with no spread, i.e. the LMSR subsidy is the value of the spread the GM maker charges; against pure noise flow the LMSR *earns* (5.2 at n=320); (iv) confidence takes `logit(target)/(k m)` trades (Wald; 147 at `m=0.1` vs 151 simulated), and (v) not knowing `m` costs at most ≈0.09 nats in a 49-point mixture, less than a wrong fixed `b` (0.08–0.23). Stylised, exact enumeration and simulation.

## 1. Model
State `s∈{0,1}`, prior `p₀`. Each round one trader buys or sells one unit. Informed (prob `μ`) buys iff its signal (correct w.p. `q`) says 1; noise buys w.p. ½. So `P(buy|s=1) = (1+m)/2`, `P(buy|s=0) = (1−m)/2`, with `m = μ(2q−1)`. Trade history matters only through the net flow `F = B − S`.

## 2. GM is an LMSR in disguise
The likelihood ratio of a buy is `(1+m)/(1−m)` and of a sell its inverse, so `logit P(s=1|h) = logit p₀ + kF`. LMSR with liquidity `b` has `logit p = logit p₀ + F/b`. Equating gives `b* = 1/k = 1/(2 atanh m)`, `≈ 1/(2m)` for small `m`; inversely, an operator who sets `b` is asserting an informed share `μ = tanh(1/2b)/(2q−1)` (`informed_fraction_for_b`). Test: the closed-form price equals the posterior computed from raw trade likelihoods for all histories tried (max error 2e-16). This is a price-path identity, not a payment identity — see §4.

## 3. What a wrong liquidity costs
Let `λ = b*/b`, so the market's log-odds slope is `λk`. `excess_log_loss(n, m, b)` is the exact expected log-loss gap to the GM price after `n` trades (enumeration over the binomial flow, state ~ prior). It vanishes at `λ=1` and at large `n` (all prices are certain and right) so the meaningful figure is the peak over the horizon (E2). For `m ∈ {0.05, 0.1}` the peaks agree to 2% (0.0789 vs 0.0791 at `λ=2`); they occur at `n ≈ c/m²` (`c≈0.65` at `λ=2`, `≈1.3` at `λ=½`). `m=0.3` differs by ≤5% and `m=0.6` by more (discreteness). Peaks (m=0.1): `λ=0.25, 0.5, 0.7, 1.4, 2, 4` → 0.169, 0.052, 0.015, 0.017, 0.079, 0.398. The scale-free constants are numerical; I have not derived them from the diffusion limit. Reading: a market that is too *liquid* (`b>b*`) is merely slow; one that is too *thin* is confidently wrong early. For a hand-set `b` it is safer to err high.

## 4. Money: the spread is the subsidy
GM maker quotes ask `P(s=1|h,buy)` and bid `P(s=1|h,sell)`; its expected profit is exactly zero (prior-weighted; with prior ½ zero in each state, tested to 1e-15), and with prior 0.2 it loses 1.13 when `s=1` and gains 0.28 when `s=0`. LMSR at `b*` has the same prices but charges `C(q+1)−C(q)`, which lies between bid and ask, so it earns no spread. Its expected loss (exact) is 0.31, 0.79, 1.11, 1.12 at `n = 5, 20, 80, 320` for `m=0.3`, approaching `b* ln 2 = 1.12`; a break-even per-trade fee is `≈ b* ln2 / n` (0.0035 at n=320). Against zero informed flow (`m=0`) LMSR earns (0.18 at n=5 to 5.2 at n=320, b=3.33), so the subsidy is insurance against informed order flow, priced by the worst case. This ties to `market-sandwich` (fees ≈ half-spread) and `anytime-liquidity`.

## 5. Speed and unknown informed share
Mean log-odds drift is `k m` per trade, so `≈ logit(target)/(k m)` trades reach `target` in the true state (E5: 146.7 vs 151.2 simulated at `m=0.1`, 15.9 vs 16.6 at 0.3, 3.5 vs 4.9 at 0.6 where overshoot matters). If `m` is unknown, the Bayes price mixes over a grid (`mixture_price`); flow enters only via `B, S`. With a uniform 49-point grid on `m ∈ (0, 1)` the regret against known-`m` GM is 0.093 (`m=0.1, n=10`), 0.035 (`m=0.3, n=40`), 0.044 (`m=0.6, n=10`) and decays to ~0 once the flow reveals `m`, but not monotonically (0.010 at n=160 vs 0.017 at n=640 for `m=0.1`). Fixed `b*` for a wrong `m` costs 0.083 (truth 0.3, b*(0.1)) and 0.232 (truth 0.1, b*(0.3)) at n=40.

## 6. Limits
Unit trades, no price impact of trade size, no strategic informed traders (Kyle/Glosten–Milgrom with strategic timing would change §2), exogenous `μ, q`, single claim, a static `m`; in a verification market `μ` is itself set by verification cost (`effort-elicitation`), and a manipulator appears as informed with wrong signal (`q<½` lowers `m`). Related: Glosten–Milgrom (1985), Kyle (1985), Abernethy–Chen–Vaughan (cost-function markets), Chen–Pennock. In this repo: `anytime-liquidity`, `market-manipulation`, `market-sandwich`, `kelly-markets`.
