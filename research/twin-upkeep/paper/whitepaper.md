# Twin upkeep: what it costs to keep a digital twin in sync with a drifting plant

*Stylised: scalar plant `x' = a x + b_t u + w`, `w ~ N(0,σ²)`, `a=0.9, q=1, r=0.1, σ²=1`, mean input gain `b=1`; the gain drifts as a stationary AR(1) `b_t−b = ρ(b_{t−1}−b)+η`, `η ~ N(0,q_d)` (or a random walk, `ρ=1`). The twin is a Kalman filter for `b_t` fed the closed-loop regressor `u_t = −k x_t + ν ξ_t` (`ν` is a probing, or dither, budget); the controller plays the certainty-equivalent gain `k*(b̂_t)`. "Mean-field" means the random regressor energy `u²` is replaced by its mean `v = E u²` in the Riccati recursion. Pure Python; see `tests/` and `experiments/results.txt`.*

## Question
`twin-transfer` and `randomized-twin` price a *fixed* twin against a fixed plant. Real plants drift: actuators wear, loads change, temperatures move. A digital twin that is kept alive by streaming data is a state estimator, so the question is quantitative: how much control performance does a *tracked* twin recover relative to a stale one, in closed form; and is it worth spending control effort on probing to keep it sharp?

## Exact results
1. **The tracking error has a closed form.** With per-step information energy `v = E u²`, the predictive variance `m` of the next gain (the quantity the controller acts on) is the positive root of `(v/σ²) m² + (1−ρ²−q_d v/σ²) m − q_d = 0`; for a random walk `m = q_d/2 + √(q_d²/4 + q_dσ²/v)`. At `ρ=0.99`, prior sd 0.1: formula `m = 0.00791`, closed-loop Kalman simulation `0.00790` (200k steps, 4 seeds); across six drift settings the formula is within 3% of simulation (`0.00232/0.00232 … 0.00681/0.00686`). Here `v₀ = 0.682` and `E u²` is the closed-loop value with no probing.
2. **Regret is a one-line function of `m`.** A certainty-equivalent gain from an estimate with variance `m` costs `½ J_kk (dk*/db)² m = 0.566 m` extra per step (`J_kk=2.362`, `dk*/db=−0.692`); checked against the exact Gaussian average of the regret to 3%. Against closed-loop simulation paired with a clairvoyant controller (same noise): `0.00447` predicted vs `0.00466` simulated at prior sd 0.1, `ρ=0.99`; the formula is 3–10% low across settings (the mean-field step ignores the randomness of `u²`, and the quadratic law ignores the third-order term). A drift random walk with `q_d=10⁻⁴` costs 0.6% of the clairvoyant cost `J*=1.074`; at `q_d=10⁻²` 6.6%.
3. **A tracked twin barely beats the population mean at fast drift.** At `ρ=0.99` a tracking twin removes only 7%, 21% and 33% of the prior variance at prior sd 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 (`0.928, 0.791, 0.667` of the prior variance); at slower drift `ρ=0.999` it removes 35–70%. One step of information is `v/σ² = 0.68`, so it takes on the order of the drift's memory (`1/(1−ρ)`) steps to beat a prior worth `1/prior-variance`. A twin calibrated to the posterior variance and then frozen has variance `ρ^{2t}P + q_d(1−ρ^{2t})/(1−ρ²)` and loses half its advantage over the prior mean after `ln 2/(2(1−ρ)) = 34.5` steps (`ρ=0.99`); regret goes `0.00445 → 0.00521` (50 steps) `→ 0.00566` (500, the prior-mean twin).
4. **Dedicated probing never pays for one drifting parameter.** Dither adds `(q+rk²)b²ν²/(1−c²) + rν²` to the stage cost (exact) and raises information energy by `(1+k²b²/(1−c²))ν²`, a shadow price `γ = 0.698` per unit of `E u²` at `a=0.9`. The marginal value of information `−dR/dv` at `ρ=0.99` is `0.0004 / 0.007 / 0.039` for `q_d = 10⁻⁴ / 10⁻³ / 10⁻²`: always below `γ`, so `ν* = 0`. Probing pays only above `q_d = 0.696` (`a=0.3`, where the price is 1.03 and `v₀=0.075`; a stationary drift sd of 5 for `ρ=0.99`), i.e. never in any regime where the small-error law holds; for `a=0.9, 1.2` there is no crossing below `q_d=1`. The natural closed-loop signal from the process noise already tracks the gain.
5. **Two parameters: the closed loop has a blind direction and the mean-field price of probing scales as `q^{1/6}`.** If both `a` and `b` are unknown and drift, a fixed gain `u=−kx` reveals only `a−bk`; the information matrix is `V[1,−k][1,−k]ᵀ + ν² e₂e₂ᵀ` with a null direction `(k,1)`. For random walks the covariance has no steady state at `ν=0` (regret grows without bound), and with dither the blind variance settles at `≈ √q_d σ/ν`. Balancing it against the `ν²` cost gives `ν* ∝ q^{1/6}` (measured log-log slope `0.167` across `q=10⁻¹⁰…10⁻⁴`, `ν*=0.0027…0.0267`) and excess cost `∝ q^{0.40–0.47}` (tending to `1/3`); at the optimum regret is 3.6–17 times the dither cost. When instead the drift mean-reverts, the blind direction saturates at its prior and the optimal probe is zero for all `ρ ∈ {0.99, 0.999, 0.9999}`, sd `∈ {0.1, 0.2}`.
6. **Certainty-equivalent control excites itself, so the mean-field law is an upper bound.** Simulating the two-parameter Kalman twin in closed loop (`q=2·10⁻⁷`, 100k steps, 6 seeds paired with a clairvoyant run) the excess cost is `0.00057` at `ν=0` where the mean-field regret is infinite, `0.00069, 0.00063, 0.00061, 0.00058, 0.00057` at `ν = 0.003, 0.0095 (=ν*), 0.03, 0.1, 0.3`: flat in `ν`, so any probing only adds its cost (total `0.00074` at `ν*`, `0.012` at 0.1). The mean-field prediction agrees when the fixed-gain approximation is good (`0.00056` vs `0.00058` at `ν=0.1`, `0.00061` vs `0.00061` at 0.03) and overpredicts at small `ν` (`0.00121` vs `0.00069` at 0.003), because the twin-driven gain changes `k̂_t` supply the missing regressor direction.

## Limitations
One or two parameters, Gaussian AR(1)/random-walk drift, scalar state, `a` known in the one-parameter case; the regret law is second order (small error, far from the stability cliff `b<2.31`) and a Gaussian estimate has a nonzero destabilisation probability, tabulated in `experiments/results.txt` (`9·10⁻⁴` per step at `q_d=10⁻²`), so expected regret under a Gaussian belief is formally infinite. The mean-field information energy is approximate. No unmodelled dynamics, no measurement of the state through a sensor twin, no cost for the computation of the twin itself.

## Relevance
For a slowly varying actuator gain, keep the twin on: continuous filtering from the data the controller already produces is enough, and dedicated probing signals waste control effort. A calibrate-and-freeze twin forfeits half of its advantage within `ln 2/(2(1−ρ))` steps, so its resync interval should be set from the drift's mean-reversion, not from a calendar. Probing needs a genuinely different justification than "the twin is drifting": it matters when a direction of the plant is structurally unobservable in closed loop *and* the controller is not adaptive; the certainty-equivalent controller's own jitter closes most of that gap.
