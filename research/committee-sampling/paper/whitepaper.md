# Sampling verifier committees: exact failure, sizes, stake weighting, and thresholds

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Decentralised training protocols (refereed verification, `replicated-execution`, `verification-game`) pick a random committee of verifiers per job. Their security is usually stated as a Chernoff or Hoeffding bound. I give exact numbers. (i) Drawing `m` distinct members from a pool of `N` with `B` Byzantine, the capture probability is hypergeometric, exactly zero once `m ≥ 2B+1`, and up to 26× below the binomial (`N=100`, `B=30`, `m=41`: 1.4×10⁻⁴ vs 3.6×10⁻³). (ii) For failure `2⁻⁴⁰` and Byzantine share β from 0.05 to 0.45 the exact odd committee is 0.89–0.93 of the first-order size `ln(1/ε)/KL(½‖β)` (e.g. 111 vs 124 at β=0.2) while the Hoeffding size is 1.1–2.2× the exact one (68 vs 31 at β=0.05). (iii) Under stake-weighted draws only the Byzantine share of stake matters, and node counts do not: 20 of 100 nodes with 4× stake get half the weight and capture a 41-member committee with probability 0.5, where a per-node uniform draw makes capture impossible. (iv) A threshold-`t` rule has exactly opposed failure modes: at β=0.2, `m=41`, raising `t` from 21 to 29 cuts the probability of accepting a wrong claim from 2.7×10⁻⁷ to 1.1×10⁻¹⁶ and raises the probability that the honest claim cannot be accepted from 2.7×10⁻⁷ to 3.3×10⁻². (v) A committee-size cost trade-off has an optimal size growing about logarithmically in the loss (15 → 73 members from 10³ to 10⁹).

## 1. Model
A pool of `N` nodes contains `B` Byzantine nodes that vote for a wrong claim; the rest vote correctly. A committee is `m` distinct nodes drawn uniformly (`hyper_pmf`, `hyper_tail`), or i.i.d. from a distribution in which the Byzantine draw probability is `s` (with replacement or by stake; `binom_tail`). A threshold-`t` rule accepts a claim with at least `t` votes. Safety fails if the wrong claim reaches `t` (Byzantine count ≥ `t`); liveness fails if the correct claim cannot (Byzantine count > `m−t`). Majority is `t = ⌊m/2⌋+1`. The adversary is static: corruption is fixed before the draw.

## 2. Exact capture and the finite-pool gain
E1 (`N=100`, `B=30`): at `m` = 5, 11, 21, 41 the hypergeometric capture is 0.158, 0.066, 0.014, 1.4×10⁻⁴ against binomial 0.163, 0.078, 0.026, 3.6×10⁻³ and Hoeffding 0.67, 0.41, 0.19, 0.038. For `m ≥ 31` capture is exactly 0 by pigeonhole. E3 (target 10⁻⁶): the smallest exact committee is 21, 31, 53, 101, 127 for `(N,B)` = (50,10), (100,20), (100,30), (500,150), (5000,1500) against 51, 51, 131, 131, 131 with replacement, so a small pool gains up to 2.5× in committee size and the gain vanishes as `N` grows. The corollary for protocol design is that with small pools sampling *without replacement* is worth implementing, since the with-replacement bound is a loose, safe overestimate.

## 3. Sizing
With replacement, capture is `P(Bin(m,β) ≥ ⌊m/2⌋+1) ≈ exp(−m·KL(½‖β))` up to a polynomial prefactor. For `ε=2⁻⁴⁰` (E2) the exact minimum odd `m` is 31, 49, 111, 285, 1217, 4943 at β = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.45, and the ratio to `ln(1/ε)/KL(½‖β)` is 0.928, 0.903, 0.893, 0.896, 0.896, 0.896: the prefactor buys a stable ≈10%. Hoeffding's `ln(1/ε)/(2(½−β)²)` gives 68, 87, 154, 347, 1386, 5545, over-sizing by 2.2× at β=0.05 (the KL exponent is much larger than the quadratic one when β is small) and only 1.1× near ½. The committee needed diverges as `1/(½−β)²`: at β=0.45 it is 4943.

## 4. Stake-weighted draws
If members are drawn with probability proportional to stake, capture depends only on the Byzantine share `s = n_adv κ / (n_adv κ + n_hon)` (E4: 20 of 100 nodes, κ = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 give `s` = 0.20, 0.33, 0.50, 0.67, 0.80 and capture of a 41-member committee 8×10⁻⁶, 0.014, 0.50, 0.99, 1.0). The same adversary under per-node uniform draws without replacement has capture exactly 0, since it holds only 20 nodes. So the security claim "fewer than β of the nodes are Byzantine" must be restated as a claim about stake share, and splitting or merging identities changes nothing when weight is linear in stake (compare `sybil-stake`).

## 5. Thresholds: safety against liveness
For β=0.2 (`N=200`, `B=40`), `m=41` (E5), threshold `t` = 21, 25, 29, 33, 37 gives wrong-acceptance 2.7×10⁻⁷, 2.2×10⁻¹¹, 1.1×10⁻¹⁶, 2.1×10⁻²³, 3.3×10⁻³² and failure of the honest claim 2.7×10⁻⁷, 2.9×10⁻⁴, 0.033, 0.44, 0.95. Weighting liveness failure by 1, 10², 10⁶ against safety puts the best threshold at 21, 20, 17: when a stall is cheap and a wrong acceptance is not, use a high threshold with a fallback (escalation, a larger committee, `escalation-bonds`).

## 6. Adaptive corruption and committee cost
If the adversary instead picks its members after the draw (bribing), it needs `⌊m/2⌋+1` members and pays that many prices: the cost is linear in `m` whatever the pool (`adaptive_cost`; `verifier-bribery` has the collusion-proof stake). With per-member cost 1 and static β=0.2, the size minimising `m + L·P(capture)` is `m*` = 15, 25, 43, 73 for loss `L` = 10³, 10⁴, 10⁶, 10⁹, capture 4×10⁻³ down to 5×10⁻⁹: `m*` grows by ≈28–30 per factor 10³ in `L` (from 10³), i.e. logarithmically.

## 7. Limits
Static adversary and fixed β; a single-round committee; all Byzantine votes coordinate on one wrong claim; honest members never err (fold their error into β, or see `noisy-referee`). Random beacons are assumed unbiased and ungrindable (see `fiat-shamir-grinding` for the failure of that assumption). Related: Chernoff–Hoeffding bounds, Algorand-style sortition and committee sampling in Byzantine agreement (Gilad et al. 2017), Bitcoin/Ethereum-style beacon literature, and Gensyn's refereed verification. Synthetic; the numbers are exact computations, not measurements.
