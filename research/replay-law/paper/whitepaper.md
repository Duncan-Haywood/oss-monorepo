# When to replay: an exact law for rehearsal schedules in sequential training

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`. Builds on `forgetting-law`.*

## Abstract
Continual learners in decentralised training rehearse old tasks to limit forgetting, but how often, and when? In the exactly solvable model of `forgetting-law` (tasks are Haar-random rank-`r` subspaces of `R^d` with a shared solution; training to convergence projects the error onto the task's null space), replaying a task jointly with each fresh one is a projection onto the sum of two subspaces. Tracking the expected loss `a` on the replayed task and the error `b` outside it gives a linear 2×2 recursion, so the expected loss curve under *any* schedule is an exact matrix product; it recovers the no-replay law and matches simulation at every step. Consequences: a single replay is best at the forgetting-peak lag (`t*` = 5, 11, 22, 89 against peak lags 5.2, 10.8, 22.0, 88.6 at `(d,r)` = (32,4), (128,8), (32,1), (128,1)) but removes only 25–42% of total forgetting; a replay period longer than the peak lag never lowers the peak; a fixed period beats random replay of the same rate only below roughly 1.2–3 peak lags, above which random replay wins; and halving total forgetting needs a per-step replay rate from 0.25 (`d=12, r=3`) to 0.015 (`d=128, r=1`). Stylised: realizable linear regression, exact convergence, one tracked task.

## 1. Model
Let `e = w − w*`. The tracked task has row space `U` (rank `r`); the loss on it is `a = ‖P_U e‖²`, and `b = ‖(I−P_U)e‖²`. Fresh tasks are independent Haar rank-`r` projectors `P`. By the moment identity of `forgetting-law`, a plain step gives `E[e'e'ᵀ] = λ eeᵀ + c₂‖e‖² I`, hence on `(a,b)`
`a' = (λ + c₂r)a + c₂r·b`, `b' = c₂(d−r)a + (λ + c₂(d−r))b` (matrix `M`).
A *replay step* trains jointly on the fresh task and the tracked one: projection onto `U + span(P)`. It sets `a' = 0`. Seen from the complement of `U`, `span(P)` is an `r`-dimensional subspace, uniform by invariance under `O(d−r)`, so `b' = κb` with `κ = 1 − r/(d−r)` (and `κ = 0` once `2r ≥ d`; matrix `R`). Both maps are linear, so the mean state after any schedule is a product of `M` and `R`, started at `s₀ = (0, 1−r/d)` (task just learned from a unit isotropic error). With no replay this reproduces `(r/d)ρ(ρᵏ−λᵏ)` to 12 digits (tests).

## 2. Totals in closed form
Write `Σ` for the loss summed over all steps (`Σ₀ = (r/d)ρ(d/r − 1/(1−λ))` with no replay). Bernoulli replay at rate `q` has mean matrix `A = (1−q)M + qR`, so `Σ_B(q) = e_aᵀ(I−A)⁻¹s₀`. Replay every `n` steps gives cycle matrix `C = R Mⁿ⁻¹` and `Σ_n = e_aᵀ(Σ_{j<n}Mʲ)(I−C)⁻¹s₀`. One replay at step `t` costs the no-replay prefix plus `e_aᵀ(I−M)⁻¹RMᵗ⁻¹s₀`. E1 (`d=12, r=3`, replay every 4th step, 20000 runs) matches step by step: exact/sim 0.04352/0.04321 at step 3, 0/0 at replay steps, 0.01056/0.01065 at step 6, 0.00256/0.00256 at step 10. E4 (Bernoulli, 3000 runs, 200 steps): 0.2323/0.2288, 0.1545/0.1523, 0.0764/0.0770 for `q` = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5.

## 3. When to replay
Replay restarts the hump: it zeroes today's loss but the error outside the task then forgets it again. Replaying too early wastes the reset, too late arrives after the loss has decayed anyway. E2: the best single replay step is 5, 11, 22, 89, 2, 2, 5 for `(d,r)` = (32,4), (128,8), (32,1), (128,1), (12,3), (32,8), (8,1), each within one step of the forgetting-peak lag (tested to <1.5), and it keeps 0.577–0.745 of the total. Replaying at step 1 keeps 0.667 (`r/d=1/4`), 0.857 (`r/d=1/8`) to 0.992 (`d=128, r=1`), i.e. immediate rehearsal buys almost nothing when tasks are small relative to the model; replaying at twice the peak lag keeps 0.69–0.81. E6 (`d=32, r=4`): the expected peak (0.0267 at step 5) is untouched by a period of 8 or 16 and falls to 0.0236 at period 4 and 0.0116 at period 2, so the period must not exceed the peak lag to shave the peak.

## 4. Periodic versus random replay
At equal rate, a deterministic period is not always better (E3). At `d=32, r=4` (peak lag 5.2) periodic wins through period 8 (0.575 vs 0.590 of `Σ₀`) and loses from 12 (0.745 vs 0.693); at `d=12, r=3` (peak 2.5) the crossover is between periods 3 and 4; at `d=128, r=8` (peak 10.8) between 16 and 32 (0.857 vs 0.779 at 32); at `d=32, r=1` (peak 22) it lies beyond 32. So the crossover sits at a period of about 1.2–3 peak lags. Reading: when replays are rare, the first replay of a periodic schedule lands after the hump has passed, whereas a random schedule sometimes replays early; when replays are frequent, regularity wins because random gaps waste replays. Replay budgets should therefore be scheduled regularly at rates above `1/(2·peak lag)` and randomised below it.

## 5. Budget sizing
E5 reports the Bernoulli rate needed to keep a target fraction of `Σ₀`: to halve forgetting `q = 0.250, 0.170, 0.102, 0.056, 0.015` for `(d,r)` = (12,3), (32,4), (128,8), (32,1), (128,1); to keep 10% needs 0.744, 0.647, 0.505, 0.346, 0.122. Small tasks in a wide model need proportionally far less rehearsal, because forgetting is small (`Σ₀ ≤ 0.47`) and arrives late. For `2r ≥ d` one replay is a full recovery (`Σ = 0` after replay at step 1, against 0.155 without at `d=6, r=3`).

## 6. Limits and next steps
One tracked task and unit-scale error; replaying several stored tasks each step is not covered exactly (replaying other tasks acts as a higher-rank fresh step correlated with the current error, and we did not derive it). Realizable regression, exact convergence, no SGD noise, no conflicting tasks (where forgetting has a floor, see `forgetting-law`), and replay is assumed to use the true old data. A verification hook: a verifier can audit a claimed rehearsal schedule against the exact curve, as `forgetting-audit` does for skipped training. Related: `forgetting-law`, `forgetting-audit`, `merge-law`.
