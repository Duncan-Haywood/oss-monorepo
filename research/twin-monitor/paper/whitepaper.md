# Twin monitor: anytime-valid monitoring of a deployed digital twin, the closed-loop blind line, and the price of dither

*Stylised: scalar linear-quadratic plant `x' = a x + b u + w`, Gaussian noise, a twin `(â, b̂)` that was trained once and is then compared with real transitions. Pure Python; every formula is checked against a literal simulation (`tests/`, `experiments/results.txt`). Plant used throughout: `a=0.9, b=1, q=1, r=0.1, σ²=1`, `α=0.05`, mixture prior `τ²=1`.*

## Question
`twin-transfer` showed that a twin can mistrain a controller while scoring perfectly on the wrong data. Once the controller is deployed, the operator keeps receiving real transitions. Can they be turned into a *statistically valid* alarm that the twin is stale, that may be checked after every step? How long does it take, when is the twin invisible, and what does it cost to make it visible?

## Model
Deployed policy `u = −k x + e`, `e ~ N(0, v)` (`v` = dither, deliberate exploration). The twin predicts `x' ~ N(â x + b̂ u, σ²)`. The residual `r = x' − â x − b̂ u = −(Δa x + Δb u) + w` has mean shift `θ·z`, `z = (x,u)`, `θ = −(Δa, Δb)`. Test: `H0`: twin exact (`θ=0`). The **mixture e-process** integrates the likelihood ratio over `θ ~ N(0, τ²I)`:
`log E_n = ½ mᵀA⁻¹m − ½ ln det(I + τ²G/σ²)`, `G = Σ z zᵀ`, `m = Σ z r/σ²`, `A = G/σ² + I/τ²`.
Because `z_t` is known before `x_{t+1}` is drawn, `E_n` is a nonnegative martingale under `H0` and Ville's inequality gives `P(∃n: E_n ≥ 1/α) ≤ α` however often it is checked (Ville 1939; Shafer–Vovk 2019; Howard et al. 2021; Ramdas et al. 2023). This is the same device as in `sequential-slashing`, `forecast-duel` and `inference-substitution`, applied to a regression residual rather than a score difference.

## Results
1. **Peeking breaks the naive test; the e-process does not.** Twin exact, dither `v=0.2`, 2000 steps, 1000 runs: refitting the residual regression after every step and alarming at the fixed-sample χ²(2) 5% value fires in **51.6%** of runs; the e-process fires in **2.3%** (bound 5%).
2. **Detection rate is the twin-transfer score gap.** Under the true plant the log-likelihood-ratio drifts at `[(Δa − kΔb)² E x² + Δb² v]/(2σ²)` per step, `E x² = (σ² + b²v)/(1−c²)`, `c = a − bk` — affine in the dither: `α₀ + βv`, with `α₀ = (Δa−kΔb)²/(2(1−c²))`, `β = [(Δa−kΔb)²b²/(1−c²) + Δb²]/(2σ²)` (matches the moment formula to 1e-12). The leading-order delay `ln(1/α)/rate` is **too small by 40–55%** because the mixture pays `½ ln det` for not knowing `θ`; a deterministic equivalent (replace `G, m` by their expectations, including the noise part `½ tr(A⁻¹G/σ²)`) is within 10–20% of the simulated median. Twin `b̂ = 1.4 b`, `k = k*(twin)`, regret 0.0567: predicted/simulated median delay 161/142 (`v=0`), 152/125 (0.05), 112/95 (0.2), 43/42 (1.0) steps; L/rate would say 92, 78, 54, 21. All 300 runs alarm within 6000 steps.
3. **A closed-loop blind line.** If the twin's own trained gain satisfies `â − a = k(b̂ − b)` the twin predicts the *deployed* closed loop exactly, so with no dither the rate is **exactly zero** and no monitor of any kind can distinguish it — the e-process alarms in 3/100 runs (≤ α) after 40,000 steps. With dither the rate is exactly `Δb² v/(2σ²)` (all three numbers agree to the printed digits) and delays match the mixture prediction: e.g. `â=0.5, b̂=0.070`: median delay 319 / 68 / 14 steps at `v = 0.05 / 0.2 / 1` (predicted 322 / 72 / 13). The blind line is a real family (`(0.7, 0.750)`, `(1.0, 1.120)`, `(0.5, 0.070)`, …), one twin per `â`.
4. **Most blind twins are harmless, one is not.** Along the line regret is second order: `R = 0.0006` at `(0.7, 0.750)`, `0.0001` at `(1.0, 1.120)`, `0.0013` at `(1.6, 1.818)` — but `R = 0.233` at `(0.5, 0.070)`, the twin that thinks the actuator is nearly dead (the underestimated-actuation cliff of `twin-transfer` again). Dither costs exactly `c_d v` per step, `c_d = (q+rk²)b²/(1−c²) + r ≈ 1.17`–`1.41`; that is large compared with the regret of the benign blind twins.
5. **When to dither, and how much.** Total expected extra cost over a horizon `H` with prior `π` that the twin is bad: `(1−π) H c_d v + π (R + c_d v) min(D(v), H)`, `D = ln(1/α)/rate(v)` at leading order. On the blind line (`rate = ρ v`, `ρ = Δb²/2σ²`) the interior optimum is `v* = √(π ln(1/α) R / (ρ (1−π) H c_d))`, worth taking only if it beats `v = 0` (`π H R`). For the benign blind twins the optimum is **no dither** (`v* = 0` at `π=0.1, H=10⁴` and `π=0.5, H=10⁵` for `(0.7, 0.750)`). For `(0.5, 0.070)` dither of variance `v ≈ 0.006` cuts the expected cost from **11,649 to roughly 800–1000** (`π=0.5, H=10⁵`; 801 by the mixture-delay formula, 959–1009 simulated), about 12×; simulated totals (60 runs per `v`) are 2799, 1009, 959, 2193, 7064 at `v = 0.001, 0.0034, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1`, so the minimum sits near `v≈0.006–0.01`. The leading-order delay puts the optimum at `0.0034` and under-predicts the minimum cost by 2× (481 vs 959); the mixture-delay formula matches simulation within 10% (925 vs 1009 at `v=0.0034`, 928 vs 959 at `0.01`) and puts `v* = 0.0058`.

## Limitations
Scalar plant, Gaussian noise known (`σ²` known), i.i.d. dither, a *single* alarm followed by recalibration, a two-point prior (good or bad), and a Gaussian mixture prior whose `τ²` was not tuned. The deterministic equivalent is not a bound on the delay. The blind-line analysis is for one twin at a time, deployed with its own certainty-equivalent gain; more general blind subspaces arise with state dimension `n>1`. Not tested on a real system.

## Relevance
(i) Monitor a deployed twin with an e-process, not repeated significance tests. (ii) Passive monitoring of a twin under the policy trained in it is blind to a whole line of twin errors; the ones with high regret are the ones where the twin thinks actuation is weak. (iii) Small, explicit dither buys visibility at a price that can be computed in advance and is usually negligible compared with the regret it prevents *only* when the twin's blind error is expensive. Companion to `twin-transfer`, `randomized-twin`, `sequential-slashing`, `forecast-duel`.
