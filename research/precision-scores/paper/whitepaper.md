# Scoring vector reports: the precision score and the price of an isotropic one

**Abstract.** A verifier in a decentralised training system often reports a *vector*: the expected drift of a worker's update from a trusted re-execution, one coordinate per layer, with noise scales that differ by orders of magnitude. Any proper score for a mean is a Bregman score, and the simplest family is quadratic, `S_A(r,y) = −(y−r)ᵀA(y−r)` for positive definite `A`; a misreport `D` costs exactly `DᵀAD`. Which `A`? I take the referee's job to be detection: compare the report with a trusted reference over `n` tasks. The per-task gap has mean `DᵀAD` and variance `4DᵀAΣAD`, exactly and for any noise law with covariance `Σ`, so the signal to noise ratio is `DᵀAD/(2√(DᵀAΣAD))`. Cauchy–Schwarz shows this is at most `½√(DᵀΣ⁻¹D)` for every `A` and every `D`, with equality for `A=Σ⁻¹`: the *precision score* is uniformly most detectable among quadratic scores, and needs `n = z²·4/(DᵀΣ⁻¹D)` tasks. An isotropic (Brier-type) score has efficiency `(DᵀD)²/((DᵀΣD)(DᵀΣ⁻¹D)) ≥ 4κ/(1+κ)²` (Kantorovich, `κ` = condition number of `Σ`), attained by a deviation split evenly between the extreme eigendirections, so it can need `(1+κ)²/(4κ)` times more tasks: 25× at κ=100, 250× at κ=1000. Checked by simulation (power 0.80 at 48 tasks for the precision score vs 0.29 for the isotropic score, which needs 261 for 0.80). With `Σ` estimated from `m` reference draws the efficiency is 0.61 at `m=2d`, 0.88 at `m=40`, 0.95 at `m=100` (d=6, κ=30), still above the isotropic 0.35; a small ridge helps only marginally.

## 1. Setting
The outcome `y ∈ ℝᵈ` has mean `μ` (what the verifier is asked to report) and covariance `Σ ≻ 0`. For `A ≻ 0`, `S_A(r,y) = −(y−r)ᵀA(y−r)` is strictly proper: `E S_A(μ+D, y) = −tr(AΣ) − DᵀAD`, so truth is the unique maximiser and a misreport costs exactly `DᵀAD` in expectation (test-verified to 1e-10). This is the Bregman score of `φ(x)=xᵀAx/2` up to scale; every proper score for the mean is a Bregman score (Savage; Banerjee–Guo–Wang; the elicitation-geometry view of Frongillo and coauthors), and `A` is its curvature. I restrict to constant curvature; state-dependent curvature (log-type scores) is a different trade-off and not studied here. The expected-drift statistic itself is elicitable (mean), unlike variance or expected shortfall alone.

## 2. Detection and the uniformly best `A`
Detection is against a trusted reference that reports `μ` (a gold re-execution or a duel as in the forecast-duel note). With `e = y−μ`, the per-task gap is `G = S_A(μ,y) − S_A(r,y) = DᵀAD − 2DᵀAe`. Only the linear term is random, so `E G = DᵀAD` and `Var G = 4DᵀAΣAD` **exactly, for any noise law with covariance Σ** (Monte Carlo, d=4, κ=30, 200 000 tasks: mean 3.52 and 3.45 against 3.60 with standard error 0.09, variance 1791 and 1766 against 1782, for Gaussian and Student-t(5) noise; E1). The signal to noise ratio per task is `snr_A(D) = DᵀAD/(2√(DᵀAΣAD))`; it is invariant to scaling `A`, so the payment scale is a separate choice.

**Theorem.** For all `A ≻ 0` and `D`: `snr_A(D) ≤ ½√(DᵀΣ⁻¹D) = snr_{Σ⁻¹}(D)`.
*Proof.* Put `u = AD`. Then `DᵀAD = Dᵀu = (Σ^{−1/2}D)ᵀ(Σ^{1/2}u) ≤ √(DᵀΣ⁻¹D)·√(uᵀΣu)` and `uᵀΣu = DᵀAΣAD`. ∎
So `A = Σ⁻¹` is best for every deviation simultaneously; there is no direction-dependent trade-off to tune. For a one-sided z-test with power ~0.8 at level 0.05 (`z=2.49`) the tasks needed are `n = z²/snr² = 4z²/(DᵀΣ⁻¹D)`: the Mahalanobis size of the deviation, with coordinates in units of their own noise. Simulated t-test power at the predicted `n=48` (Σ=diag(1,20,1), D on the extreme axes): 0.800 Gaussian, 0.811 Student-t(5) (E4).

## 3. The price of an isotropic score: Kantorovich
The efficiency of `A` in direction `D` is `eff = (snr_A/snr_{Σ⁻¹})² = (DᵀAD)²/((DᵀAΣAD)(DᵀΣ⁻¹D)) ∈ (0,1]`. For `A=I` it is `(DᵀD)²/((DᵀΣD)(DᵀΣ⁻¹D))`, and Kantorovich's inequality gives `eff ≥ 4κ/(1+κ)²`, with equality when `D` puts half its squared norm on the smallest and half on the largest eigendirection of `Σ`. Measured with that `D`: efficiency 0.640, 0.331, 0.0392, 0.0040 at κ = 4, 10, 100, 1000, matching the formula, so the task ratio is 1.6, 3.0, 25.5, 250 (E3). Minimum over 2000 random directions in d=6: identity 0.35, 0.044, 0.0045 at κ = 10, 100, 1000; a diagonal precision score (per-layer normalisation only) 0.41, 0.061, 0.0071, barely better, because the damage is in correlated directions, not per-coordinate scale; a random SPD `A` is no better than identity (E2). With unit variances and correlation `ρ` between two layers, `κ=(1+ρ)/(1−ρ)` and both identity and the diagonal score fall to 0.75, 0.19, 0.020 at ρ = 0.5, 0.9, 0.99 (E5; the diagonal score coincides with identity when variances are equal). Reading: tolerances that are rotation-invariant (a Euclidean ball) are the wrong geometry when layer noise is correlated or badly scaled; the tolerance ellipsoid is `{D: DᵀΣ⁻¹D ≤ c}`.

## 4. Estimated precision
In practice `Σ` is estimated from `m` reference draws, independent of the verifier's report and of the scored tasks; independence keeps the score proper (any fixed `A` is proper, so using `Σ̂⁻¹` is safe). Efficiency of `Σ̂⁻¹` in a fixed direction (d=6, κ=30, 300 draws each): mean 0.46 (10th percentile 0.18) at m=8, 0.61 (0.36) at m=12, 0.78 at m=20, 0.88 at m=40, 0.95 at m=100, 0.99 at m=400, against 0.35 for the isotropic and 0.39 for the diagonal score in this direction (E6). `1−d/m` is a rough guide for `m ≥ 20` but not a law (0.25 vs 0.46 at m=8). A ridge `Σ̂ + λI` with `λ = 0.05·tr(Σ̂)/d` moves the mean at m=12 from 0.62 to 0.65 and larger ridges hurt (0.53 at 0.5) (E7, single direction, differences of a few hundredths are inside noise): with `m ≥ 2d` the un-regularised precision estimate is already better than the isotropic score; below that estimate `Σ` more carefully (shrinkage) or accept a diagonal score.

## 5. Limits
Scope is quadratic, constant-curvature Bregman scores against a trusted reference that reports the truth; the referee compares verifiers or a verifier and gold, it does not learn `μ`. Detection uses the normal approximation for the mean of `n` per-task gaps and the exact moments above; heavy tails beyond finite `Σ` (infinite variance) break the moment formulas. `Σ` is treated as stationary and known or estimated from independent draws; nonstationary noise (loss spikes) needs a windowed or recursive estimate whose independence from the report must be enforced. A singular `Σ` (deterministic coordinates) makes `Σ⁻¹` infinite: those coordinates should be audited by exact checks, and the score applied to the rest. The efficiency figures are for the worst deviation direction; if deviations are known to lie along benign high-noise directions the isotropic loss is less costly. The estimation experiments are one covariance instance with 100–300 draws per cell.
