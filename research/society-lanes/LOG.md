## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 1: do people in a real bidirectional corridor keep to their right, and how lane-ordered is the flow? (predictions written before any trajectory was analysed)
- Why: every `society-*` result on robots and passing side so far (`society-robotnorms`, `society-plaza`, `society-twinfidelity`, `society-sidefit`) rests on a side bias that was assumed or read off a figure (17.5% left, Moussaïd et al. 2009, Fig. 4). Before any robot is added, the programme needs the human baseline measured from real trajectories: how strongly people hold their right, and how much lane order forms, in the setting the robot simulations will later be calibrated against.
- Data: the HERMES bidirectional corridor experiments (Düsseldorf, 2009; Forschungszentrum Jülich pedestrian data archive, DOIs 10.34735/ped.2009.12 free choice, .11 symmetric, .10 asymmetric), 24 runs, 50 to 309 participants, trajectories at 16 fps from overhead stereo cameras. The corridor runs along y (walls at x = 0 and x = 3.0 or 3.6 m, measured section y = -4 to 4 m). Data are fetched by script and not redistributed; only derived statistics are committed.
- Coordinates: x, y, z in cm, z is head height (positive, so z points up). Assuming the right-handed frame this implies, a walker heading +y has their right at +x and one heading -y at -x. If the frame were mirrored every side result below would flip sign; this is stated as an assumption and checked against nothing else in this step.
- Primary analysis (fixed before running): the five free-choice runs (`bo-360-*`, no exit assigned). Ordered runs (`bot-*`, `boa-*`) are secondary because participants were told to use the left or right exit, which can sort them laterally for reasons other than convention.
- Measures: per walker, direction from the sign of net displacement in y inside the section; mean signed lateral offset toward the walker's own right, as a fraction of the half-width; "on own right" if that mean is positive. Per run and pooled: share on own right with a 95% bootstrap interval resampling walkers (pooled: resampling runs, then walkers). Lane order: per frame, 30 cm lateral bins; per bin ((N+ - N-)/(N+ + N-))^2 weighted by occupancy, averaged over frames with at least 4 walkers of each direction in the section; compared with the same statistic after shuffling direction labels within each frame (200 shuffles), reported as excess over the shuffled mean.
- **Predictions:**
  - L1: pooled over the five free-choice runs, more than 50% of walkers are on their own right, and the 95% interval excludes 50%. Prior 70%.
  - L2: in the free-choice runs the pooled share on own right is at least 0.65. Prior 35%.
  - L3: lane order exceeds the shuffled-label null in every one of the 24 runs that has enough frames to measure it. Prior 85%.
  - L4: in the free-choice runs the share on own right does not fall as entrance width (and so density) rises: Spearman over the five runs is not negative. Prior 50%.
  - L5: free walking speed (median speed in `boa-300-frei`, 50 participants) lies between 1.1 and 1.5 m/s, as a sanity check on units and frame rate. Prior 85%.

### Outcome of milestone 1 (`experiments/m1_hermes_sides.py`, `experiments/m1_hermes_sides_results.txt`, `experiments/m1_hermes_sides_runs.csv`, figure `experiments/m1_hermes_sides.svg`; 12 s, stdlib; `lanes.py`, 5 tests)
- 24 runs, 4,752 walkers who crossed at least 2 m of the section. Archive hashes are recorded in the results file.
- **L1 held:** in the five free-choice runs, 1,016 of 1,084 walkers (**0.937**, two-stage bootstrap over runs and walkers [0.865, 0.993]) walked on their own right half. The mean signed offset was +0.32 to +0.50 half-widths in both directions in every run, so both streams picked the same relative side: a shared convention, not a geometric artefact of one entrance.
- **L2 held:** 0.937 is far above 0.65.
- **L3 held:** lane order beat the 97.5th percentile of the shuffled-label null in all 23 measurable runs (the 50-person free-speed run has too few frames with 4 walkers each way). Free-choice runs: 0.91 to 1.00 against null 0.25 to 0.52. Assigned-exit runs: 0.59 to 0.85 against null 0.20 to 0.58.
- **L4 refuted:** the right share fell at the widest entrance (0.822 [0.777, 0.864] at 1.6 m against 0.97 to 1.00 at 0.5 to 1.2 m; Spearman -0.60 over the five runs). At the highest density the convention weakened but held.
- **L5 refuted:** median walker speed in `boa-300-frei` was 1.73 m/s, above the 1.1 to 1.5 m/s range written from everyday walking speeds. Speeds in the other low-density runs were 1.42 to 1.51 m/s. Either the participants walked briskly in a hall experiment or the frame rate is not 16 fps for that run. The archive states 16 fps and nothing here can check it, so absolute speeds from this step are not used later until the fundamental diagram is compared with Zhang et al. (2012).
- Not registered, seen in the data and labelled so: in the 18 assigned-exit runs (each walker told to leave by the left or the right exit; Zhang et al. 2012 describe all 3.0 m symmetric runs as assigned, though their metadata leave the instruction blank), the right share collapses to **0.534** [0.503, 0.568] (13 symmetric runs) and **0.526** [0.487, 0.564] (5 asymmetric runs plus the 50-person free-speed run `boa-300-frei`, 0.50, pooled with them as the archive files it), while lanes persist at lower order. This matches the qualitative description in Zhang et al. (2012): stable separated lanes without instructions, dynamic multi-lanes with them. This step adds per-walker side shares and a permutation null to that description. Exit assignment is confounded with run order and corridor width (all 3.0 m runs had assigned exits), so this is descriptive until BaSiGo 2013 variants A and B (milestone 2).
- Caveat: "right" assumes the archive's frame is right-handed with z up. If it were mirrored, the people kept left. The finding that both streams chose the same relative side does not depend on the frame.
- Reading for the robot programme: people in an uninstructed corridor hold a near-unanimous side convention, and giving each person a lateral goal is enough to erase it. A robot fleet heading for side doors resembles the second condition more than the first, which is the held-out test any crowd twin will now have to pass (PROPOSAL.md, H4).
