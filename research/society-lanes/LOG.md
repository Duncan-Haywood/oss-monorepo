## 2026-11-15 · Milestone 4b: does a side bias that yields to a lateral goal fix the over-trusted convention, in three families? And a third family, JuPedSim's collision-free speed model (predictions written before the full run)
- Why this step: milestones 3 and 4a found that two families with no side of their own, given the keep-right convention as a steering bias fitted on free-choice runs, keep it under assigned exits where people dropped it (social force 0.78, AVM 0.996, people 0.54 pooled over the eight HERMES runs). Milestone 4a's log pointed at the bias rule rather than the operational model. This step tests that directly, and adds a third operational family that this session can run with industrial-grade open code: JuPedSim 1.4.2's CollisionFreeSpeedModel (CFSM; Tordeux, Chraibi and Seyfried 2016, *Traffic and Granular Flow '15*, 225-232). ORCA and the power law stay open (no maintained RVO2 wheel for this interpreter; the power law needs its own integrator).
- The rules (`sftwin.bias_weight`, no new parameter): **always** (milestones 3 and 4a); **yield**, the bias is off for a walker whose lateral goal lies on its left half; **scaled**, the bias is weighted by (1 + r) / 2, r the goal's offset toward the walker's right in half-widths (0.75 or 0.25 at exits 0.25 of the width from a wall). A walker without a lateral goal gets the full bias under every rule, so the free-choice runs, and the fitted beta, are untouched: social force keeps beta = 0.1 and the AVM 0.35, and nothing is refitted on the held-out runs.
- Disclosure: the rule is chosen after seeing the held-out failures of the "always" rule, so the held-out runs are not unseen for the choice of rule, only for its parameter (there is none to fit). Before writing these predictions three probe simulations ran once each (one HERMES-like corridor, N = 40, beta = 0.1, rule yield, one per family; shares 0.72 SF, 0.78 AVM, 0.60 CFSM) to check the code runs.
- Design (fixed now): **Part A**, social force (beta 0.1) and AVM (beta 0.35) at their earlier walker counts on the 14 held-out runs, exits at 0.25, rules yield and scaled, 20 seeds; their "always" numbers are read from the milestone 3 and 4a CSVs, not rerun. **Part B**, CFSM under milestone 3's protocol (N grid {20, 30, 40, 60, 80, 100, 130} calibrated to speed at beta = 0, 3 seeds; beta in {0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} fitted with 5 seeds; scored with 20), then the 14 held-out runs under all three rules.
- **Predictions:**
  - Y1: social force with yield puts the pooled bot-360 held-out share within 0.10 of the observed 0.541. Prior 55%.
  - Y2: the same for the AVM. Prior 30% (its lanes, once formed, hold).
  - Y3: yield lands closer to the observed pooled share than scaled in all three families. Prior 70%.
  - Y4: social force with yield puts b_03 (0.754) and b_08 (0.505) both within 0.10. Prior 25%.
  - Y5: social force with yield is below the observed share in at least 5 of the 14 runs (the error is no longer one-sided). Prior 50%.
  - C1: CFSM at beta = 0 gives a pooled free share in [0.40, 0.60]. Prior 70%.
  - C2 (H3, side): CFSM at its fitted beta puts the share inside the run's 95% interval in at least 6 of 9 free runs. Prior 35%.
  - C3 (H3, lanes): lane order within 0.10 in at least 6 of 9. Prior 35%.
  - C4 (H3, speed): b_03 and b_08 median speed within 0.15 m/s at the calibrated count. Prior 10%.
  - C5: CFSM with the "always" rule keeps the pooled bot-360 held-out share above 0.70 (the over-trust replicates in a third family). Prior 75%.

ORCA and the power law (Karamouzas et al. 2014) stay for milestone 4b.
- Source read before predicting (JuPedSim v1.4.2, `libsimulator/src/AnticipationVelocityModel.cpp`): the AVM's lateral influence points away from each neighbour's predicted position, with a random left/right only at an exact tie, so it has no side preference of its own. The same steering bias β as milestone 3 is added through the same direct-steering stage. AVM parameters are JuPedSim's defaults (radius 0.2 m, time gap 1.06 s, anticipation 1.0 s, reaction 0.3 s, neighbour repulsion 8.0 over 0.1 m); desired speeds as milestone 3.
- Disclosure: before writing these predictions a probe ran 16 AVM simulations (β 0 to 0.2, N 20 to 80, HERMES 3.6 m and BaSiGo 4.0 m geometry, one to three seeds). Seen: at β = 0 lanes form almost perfectly (lane order 0.94 to 1.00 below about 0.7 walkers/m²) and each run locks into one side as a whole (share 0.0 or 1.0 in the 3.6 m runs); in the 4.0 m corridor N = 100 deadlocks (median speed 0.002 m/s). Two code fixes followed, neither changing the social-force runs: an AVM entry refused for contact waits for room (a walker marked for removal is still present for one step), and a refused initial placement enters later.
- Design (fixed now): **Part A**, social force at the fitted β = 0.1 and the walker counts milestone 3 calibrated (`m3_sf_twin_runs.csv`), the 14 held-out runs with the exit goal at 0.10 (at the wall, after clipping to the body), 0.25 (milestone 3, rerun) and 0.40 of the width from a wall, 20 seeds. **Part B**, the AVM under milestone 3's protocol unchanged except the walker-count grid, made finer because the AVM jams: N in {20, 30, 40, 60, 80, 100, 130}; β in {0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}; calibration 3 seeds, fit 5, score 20; held out at all three exit positions and without goals. A run in which no walker crosses the measured section is counted as jammed and reported, not averaged.

## 2026-11-15 · Milestone 3: does a social-force twin, fitted on the free-choice runs, reproduce the keep-right convention, the lanes and the speed, and does it predict the assigned-exit collapse it never saw? (predictions written before any simulation ran)
- Data situation this session: the FZJ archive (ped.fz-juelich.de) refused the egress proxy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT), as did arxiv.org and crossref. Two things are reachable. (1) The per-run summaries milestones 1 and 2 committed (`experiments/m1_hermes_sides_runs.csv`, `experiments/m2_basigo_sides_runs.csv`): share on own right with its 95% interval, lane order and median speed for every run. H3 is scored on exactly these. (2) PedPy (github.com/PedestrianDynamics/PedPy, MIT; its `notebooks/demo-data/bi-directional/`) redistributes two raw BaSiGo runs, `bi_corr_400_b_03` and `bi_corr_400_b_08`, from the same archive (CC BY 4.0). They give a real inflow, density and speed for two runs, both assigned-exit (variant B), so they serve the speed check and the held-out test, not the fit.
- Model: the social-force model as implemented in JuPedSim 1.4.2 (PyPI; Forschungszentrum Jülich's open simulator), with its defaults, which are the parameters of Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek (2000): A = 2000 N, B = 0.08 m, body force 1.2e5 kg/s², friction 2.4e5 kg/(m s), reaction time 0.5 s, mass 80 kg, radius 0.3 m. JuPedSim's social force has no side preference, so the one added parameter is a steering bias β: every 0.1 s, a walker with an oncoming walker within 3 m ahead (heading within 45 degrees of opposite, lateral gap under 1 m) aims its desired direction at lateral slope β toward its own right; otherwise it aims straight ahead along the corridor. β = 0 is plain JuPedSim social force. Desired speed ~ N(1.34, 0.26) m/s clipped to [0.6, 2.0] (Weidmann 1993, as used by Helbing et al. 2000).
- Geometry: an open straight corridor of the run's width (3.6 m HERMES free choice, 4.0 m BaSiGo) and length (16 m for HERMES, assumed, since the archive geometry is out of reach; BaSiGo's `length_m`), entries at both ends across the run's entrance width centred on the axis (centring assumed), measured in the middle 8 m with the same statistics as milestones 1 and 2 (`lanes.py`: per-walker mean offset toward own right, lane order in 30 cm bins on frames with at least 4 walkers each way, median speed).
- Inflow: unknown for the nine free-choice runs (their durations are not in the committed summaries), so it is calibrated per run so that the simulated median speed matches the observed one (done at β = 0). Speed is then a calibration target in those runs, not a test. The speed test uses b_03 and b_08, whose real inflow is measured from the PedPy files.
- Fit set (fixed before running): the nine free-choice runs, HERMES `bo-360-*` (5) and BaSiGo a_02 to a_05 (4, the unguided ones, as milestone 2's primary). β on a grid {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}, 5 seeds per run per β; loss = sum over runs of the squared share miss divided by the run's interval half-width (floor 0.02), plus the squared lane-order miss over 0.05. Scored at the fitted β with 20 seeds per run.
- Held out (never used to fit): every assigned-exit run. Walkers get a lateral goal: the far end at a quarter of the width from the left or the right wall, 50/50 (exit positions assumed), with the fitted β unchanged.
- **Predictions:**
  - F1: with β = 0, the pooled simulated free-choice share lies in [0.40, 0.60]: plain social force carries no convention. Prior 90%.
  - F2 (H3, side): at the fitted β, the simulated share (mean over seeds) lies inside the run's 95% interval in at least 6 of the 9 free-choice runs. Prior 40%.
  - F3 (H3, lanes): simulated lane order lies within 0.10 of the observed in at least 6 of 9. Prior 50%.
  - F4 (H3, speed): with the inflow measured from the PedPy files, the simulated median speed of b_03 and b_08 lies within 0.15 m/s of the measured, in both runs. Prior 45%.
  - F5 (H4, held out): with lateral exit goals and the fitted β, the simulated share lies within 0.10 of the observed for b_03, b_08 (real inflow) and the pooled HERMES symmetric 3.6 m runs (`bot-360-*`, 0.5 to 0.65 measured). Prior 35%.
  - F6: across the nine matched free-choice runs the simulated share falls as calibrated inflow rises (Spearman negative), as the data did (milestone 1, L4). Prior 45%.
  - F7 (data check): the PedPy copies of b_03 and b_08 reproduce milestone 2's walker counts and shares for those runs exactly. Prior 95%.
- H3 for the social-force family passes if F2, F3 and F4 all hold.
- Design change, made after the F7 data check and before any simulation ran: the PedPy files show b_08 at 3.8 walkers/m² and 0.24 m/s with a *lower* inflow (1.64/s per direction) than b_03 (2.0/s, 1.0 walkers/m², 1.02 m/s). The congestion comes from the loop the participants walked and the exit geometry, not from the inflow, so an inflow-driven open corridor cannot reach it. The twin therefore holds the number of walkers fixed, as the experiment's loop did: a walker who leaves at the far end re-enters at its own entrance as soon as there is room. That number, not an inflow, is calibrated to the observed speed in the free runs. For F4 it is set from the measured mean section density (b_03 1.01/m², b_08 3.79/m²). The predictions above are unchanged.

Taken out of order; milestone 2 stays next once the archive answers.
- The contrast the proposal did not plan: Switzerland drives on the right and Cyprus on the left (Kincaid 1986, *The Rule of the Road*). If the corridor convention of milestone 1 is a local norm rather than a human universal, it should differ between the two cities.
- Measure (fixed before running): head-on encounters. Two pedestrians, not listed in the same group in `groups.txt`, both moving faster than 0.3 m/s, headings more than 135 degrees apart, approaching, with closest approach under 2.0 m inside their shared time window. At closest approach each walker sees the other on their left (the pair kept right), on their right (kept left), or the two disagree (dropped, counted). Share kept right per scene, 95% interval by a block bootstrap over 60 s time blocks. Sensitivity: closest approach 1.5 and 3.0 m, heading gap 150 degrees.
- Orientation: passing side depends on the handedness of the world frame. Each scene's `H.txt` maps image pixels to world metres; the image (u right, v down) is an unmirrored view of the ground from above, so the world frame is right-handed seen from above if and only if the image-to-world Jacobian has negative determinant. The projection is checked by drawing the world tracks back onto each scene's reference frame before any side is counted.
- Method check on lab data: the same encounter code on the HERMES 2D runs (OpenTraj mirror of the milestone 1 files).

## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 1: do people in a real bidirectional corridor keep to their right, and how lane-ordered is the flow? (predictions written before any trajectory was analysed)
- Why: every `society-*` result on robots and passing side so far (`society-robotnorms`, `society-plaza`, `society-twinfidelity`, `society-sidefit`) rests on a side bias that was assumed or read off a figure (17.5% left, Moussaïd et al. 2009, Fig. 4). Before any robot is added, the programme needs the human baseline measured from real trajectories: how strongly people hold their right, and how much lane order forms, in the setting the robot simulations will later be calibrated against.
- Data: the HERMES bidirectional corridor experiments (Düsseldorf, 2009; Forschungszentrum Jülich pedestrian data archive, DOIs 10.34735/ped.2009.12 free choice, .11 symmetric, .10 asymmetric), 24 runs, 50 to 309 participants, trajectories at 16 fps from overhead stereo cameras. The corridor runs along y (walls at x = 0 and x = 3.0 or 3.6 m, measured section y = -4 to 4 m). Data are fetched by script and not redistributed; only derived statistics are committed.
- Coordinates: x, y, z in cm, z is head height (positive, so z points up). Assuming the right-handed frame this implies, a walker heading +y has their right at +x and one heading -y at -x. If the frame were mirrored every side result below would flip sign; this is stated as an assumption and checked against nothing else in this step.
- Primary analysis (fixed before running): the five free-choice runs (`bo-360-*`, no exit assigned). Ordered runs (`bot-*`, `boa-*`) are secondary because participants were told to use the left or right exit, which can sort them laterally for reasons other than convention.
- Measures: per walker, direction from the sign of net displacement in y inside the section; mean signed lateral offset toward the walker's own right, as a fraction of the half-width; "on own right" if that mean is positive. Per run and pooled: share on own right with a 95% bootstrap interval resampling walkers (pooled: resampling runs, then walkers). Lane order: per frame, 30 cm lateral bins; per bin ((N+ - N-)/(N+ + N-))^2 weighted by occupancy, averaged over frames with at least 4 walkers of each direction in the section; compared with the same statistic after shuffling direction labels within each frame (200 shuffles), reported as excess over the shuffled mean.
- **Predictions:**
  - L1: pooled over the five free-choice runs, more than 50% of walkers are on their own right, and the 95% interval excludes 50%. Prior 70%.
  - L2: in the free-choice runs the pooled share on own right is at least 0.65. Prior 35%.
  - L3: lane order exceeds the shuffled-label null in every one of the 24 runs that has enough frames to measure it. Prior 85%.
  - L4: in the free-choice runs the share on own right does not fall as entrance width (and so density) rises: Spearman over the five runs is not negative. Prior 50%.
  - L5: free walking speed (median speed in `boa-300-frei`, 50 participants) lies between 1.1 and 1.5 m/s, as a sanity check on units and frame rate. Prior 85%.

### Outcome of milestone 1 (`experiments/m1_hermes_sides.py`, `experiments/m1_hermes_sides_results.txt`, `experiments/m1_hermes_sides_runs.csv`, figure `experiments/m1_hermes_sides.svg`; 12 s, stdlib; `lanes.py`, 5 tests)
- 24 runs, 4,752 walkers who crossed at least 2 m of the section. Archive hashes are recorded in the results file.
- **L1 held:** in the five free-choice runs, 1,016 of 1,084 walkers (**0.937**, two-stage bootstrap over runs and walkers [0.865, 0.993]) walked on their own right half. The mean signed offset was +0.32 to +0.50 half-widths in both directions in every run, so both streams picked the same relative side: a shared convention, not a geometric artefact of one entrance.
- **L2 held:** 0.937 is far above 0.65.
- **L3 held:** lane order beat the 97.5th percentile of the shuffled-label null in all 23 measurable runs (the 50-person free-speed run has too few frames with 4 walkers each way). Free-choice runs: 0.91 to 1.00 against null 0.25 to 0.52. Assigned-exit runs: 0.59 to 0.85 against null 0.20 to 0.58.
- **L4 refuted:** the right share fell at the widest entrance (0.822 [0.777, 0.864] at 1.6 m against 0.97 to 1.00 at 0.5 to 1.2 m; Spearman -0.60 over the five runs). At the highest density the convention weakened but held.
- **L5 refuted:** median walker speed in `boa-300-frei` was 1.73 m/s, above the 1.1 to 1.5 m/s range written from everyday walking speeds. Speeds in the other low-density runs were 1.42 to 1.51 m/s. Either the participants walked briskly in a hall experiment or the frame rate is not 16 fps for that run. The archive states 16 fps and nothing here can check it, so absolute speeds from this step are not used later until the fundamental diagram is compared with Zhang et al. (2012).
- Not registered, seen in the data and labelled so: in the 18 assigned-exit runs (each walker told to leave by the left or the right exit; Zhang et al. 2012 describe all 3.0 m symmetric runs as assigned, though their metadata leave the instruction blank), the right share collapses to **0.534** [0.503, 0.568] (13 symmetric runs) and **0.526** [0.487, 0.564] (5 asymmetric runs plus the 50-person free-speed run `boa-300-frei`, 0.50, pooled with them as the archive files it), while lanes persist at lower order. This matches the qualitative description in Zhang et al. (2012): stable separated lanes without instructions, dynamic multi-lanes with them. This step adds per-walker side shares and a permutation null to that description. Exit assignment is confounded with run order and corridor width (all 3.0 m runs had assigned exits), so this is descriptive until BaSiGo 2013 variants A and B (milestone 2).
- Caveat: "right" assumes the archive's frame is right-handed with z up. If it were mirrored, the people kept left. The finding that both streams chose the same relative side does not depend on the frame.
- Reading for the robot programme: people in an uninstructed corridor hold a near-unanimous side convention, and giving each person a lateral goal is enough to erase it. A robot fleet heading for side doors resembles the second condition more than the first, which is the held-out test any crowd twin will now have to pass (PROPOSAL.md, H4).
