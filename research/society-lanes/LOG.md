## 2026-11-16 · Milestone 6c: do crowd-side robots seed lanes in a weak-convention crowd, or do they only thin it? (predictions written before any run)
- Why this step: 6a read, after its run and unregistered, that in the half-bias social-force crowd crowd-side robots at 20% *cut* people's transit by 1.59 s and raised people's lane order from 0.756 to 0.873. Robots there replace people at a fixed agent count, so those cells also hold fewer people; the lead cannot tell a lane seed from a thinner crowd. If the robots do seed lanes, a fleet operator has a reason to deploy crowd-side robots beyond "they collide less" (6a). This also starts README Next steps 2 (sweep the human side bias toward the street value 0.65). Milestone 6b (SARL in each twin) is a sibling's open PR #16714; this step does not touch it.
- Tool: `experiments/m6c_lane_seeds.py` reuses `sftwin.simulate` and `m5_robots.py`'s constants unchanged. People-only measures (robots are excluded by `sftwin.measure`).
- Design (fixed now): social force only (JuPedSim 1.4.2, fitted &beta; 0.1), the one family in which halving the bias moved the crowd toward the street convention (6a, R7); bias multipliers 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0; the two HERMES 3.6 m runs with milestone 3's walker counts (N 30 and 60); four conditions: *none* (N people), *control* (N &minus; k people, no robots), *crowd* (N &minus; k people + k crowd-side robots), *opposite* (N &minus; k people + k opposite-side robots), k = 10% and 20% of N as in 5a; robots 1.2 m/s; 20 **fresh** seeds per cell (SEED0 20280000); 120 s runs, first 40 s warm-up. 56 cells, 1,120 runs. Seeding effect = crowd &minus; control; density effect = control &minus; none. Cell values are seed means, pooled over the two runs by their mean; 95% percentile bootstrap over seeds within each cell (B = 2,000).
- Disclosure: a 1-seed, 45 s run checked the script end to end; its numbers were not read before these predictions were written.
- **Predictions:**
  - S1 (the registered test): at 0.5 &times; and 20%, crowd-side robots shorten people's transit against the density-matched control (95% interval of crowd &minus; control below 0). Prior 40%.
  - S2: at 0.5 &times; and 20%, they raise people's lane order against the control (interval above 0). Prior 55%.
  - S3: at 0.5 &times; and 20%, the total change (crowd &minus; none) is a cut and the density effect is less than half of it. Prior 35% (fewer people in a 3.6 m corridor is the simpler explanation).
  - S4: the transit seeding effect is more negative at 0.25 &times; and 0.5 &times; than at the fitted bias (both the minimum and the mean over shares). Prior 55%.
  - S5: opposite-side robots lengthen people's transit against the control in all 8 multiplier-share cells (interval above 0). Prior 75%.
  - S6: at 0.25 &times; with no robots, people's share on own right falls below 0.75. Prior 50%.

## 2026-11-16 · Milestone 4d: does the over-trusted convention, and its yielding fix, carry over to ORCA, the velocity-obstacle family robots use? (predictions written before the full run)
- Why this step: ORCA is the last family milestone 4 names, and the one closest to the robot side of the programme (it is a standard multi-robot and crowd-simulation planner, and SARL-style crowd-navigation benchmarks simulate their crowds with it). It also tests 4c's unregistered lead on a fifth point: the yielding bias fixed the held-out share in the two families fitted with β &le; 0.2 and not in the two fitted with β = 0.35.
- Tool: `orca.py` is a numpy/numba port of RVO2's `Agent.cpp` (van den Berg, Guy, Lin and Manocha 2011; UNC, Apache-2.0) read in Inria UMANS's adaptation (`src/3rd-party/ORCA/ORCASolver.cpp`, `CostFunctions/ORCA.{h,cpp}`, `examples/policies/ORCA.xml`, MIT, same commit as 4c). Desired speed ~ N(1.34, 0.26) m/s clipped to [0.6, 2.0] (Weidmann 1993, as used by Helbing et al. 2000).
- Geometry: an open straight corridor of the run's width (3.6 m HERMES free choice, 4.0 m BaSiGo) and length (16 m for HERMES, assumed, since the archive geometry is out of reach; BaSiGo's `length_m`), entries at both ends across the run's entrance width centred on the axis (centring assumed), measured in the middle 8 m with the same statistics as milestones 1 and 2 (`lanes.py`: per-walker mean offset toward own right, lane order in 30 cm bins on frames with at least 4 walkers each way, median speed).
- Inflow: unknown for the nine free-choice runs (their durations are not in the committed summaries), so it is calibrated per run so that the simulated median speed matches the observed one (done at β = 0). Speed is then a calibration target in those runs, not a test. The speed test uses b_03 and b_08, whose real inflow is measured from the PedPy files.
- Fit set (fixed before running): the nine free-choice runs, HERMES `bo-360-*` (5) and BaSiGo a_02 to a_05 (4, the unguided ones, as milestone 2's primary). β on a grid {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}, 5 seeds per run per β; loss = sum over runs of the squared share miss divided by the run's interval half-width (floor 0.02), plus the squared lane-order miss over 0.05. Scored at the fitted β with 20 seeds per run.
- Held out (never used to fit): every assigned-exit run. Walkers get a lateral goal: the far end at a quarter of the width from the left or the right wall, 50/50 (exit positions assumed), with the fitted β unchanged.
- **Predictions:**
  - F1: with β = 0, the pooled simulated free-choice share lies in [0.40, 0.60]: plain social force carries no convention. Prior 90%.
  - F2 (H3, side): at the fitted β, the simulated share (mean over seeds) lies inside the run's 95% interval in at least 6 of the 9 free-choice runs. Prior 40%.
  - F3 (H3, lanes): simulated lane order lies within 0.10 of the observed in at least 6 of 9. Prior 50%.
  - F4 (H3, speed): with the inflow measured from the PedPy files, the simulated median speed of b_03 and b_08 lies within 0.15 m/s of the measured, in both runs. Prior 45%.
  - F5 (H4, held out): with lateral exit goals and the fitted β, the simulated share lies within 0.10 of the observed for b_03, b_08 (real inflow) and the pooled HERMES symmetric 3.6 m runs (`bot-360-*`, 0.5 to 0.65 measured). Prior 35%.

  - F7 (data check): the PedPy copies of b_03 and b_08 reproduce milestone 2's walker counts and shares for those runs exactly. Prior 95%.
- H3 for the social-force family passes if F2, F3 and F4 all hold.
- Design change, made after the F7 data check and before any simulation ran: the PedPy files show b_08 at 3.8 walkers/m² and 0.24 m/s with a *lower* inflow (1.64/s per direction) than b_03 (2.0/s, 1.0 walkers/m², 1.02 m/s). The congestion comes from the loop the participants walked and the exit geometry, not from the inflow, so an inflow-driven open corridor cannot reach it. The twin therefore holds the number of walkers fixed, as the experiment's loop did: a walker who leaves at the far end re-enters at its own entrance as soon as there is room. The predictions above are unchanged.
