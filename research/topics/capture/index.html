Autonomous multi-angle capture

Filming a travel creator from every angle with open, packable hardware and an automated edit: the pixel, perspective, battery and weight limits first, in simulation.

14 projects, each with its own page, code and tests. See them on the full research index.

How long a lens can a 360 camera fake by cropping into a reel, and what perspective can no crop fake?

research/capture-crop · 10 tests

How long a lens can a 360 camera fake by cropping into a reel? An 8K equirect is native only to ~24 mm-equivalent at 1080x1920 (85 mm is a 3.5x upscale), and perspective is set by distance: a stick at 0.8 m enlarges a 10 cm-nearer feature 14% vs 7.6% at an 85 mm framing distance, so portrait looks need a camera standing back. Pixel-count upper bound. First project of track 13.

Abstract

Pixel and perspective geometry for the simplest multi-angle rig, a 360 camera on a pole reframed in software. An 8K (7,680 px) equirect cropped into a 1080×1920 vertical reel is native only up to about 24 mm full-frame-equivalent; 50 mm is upscaled 2.1× and 85 mm 3.5×, and a native 85 mm crop would need a ~27,000 px equirect. Perspective is set by distance: from a stick at 0.8 m a feature 10 cm nearer the lens images 14% larger, against 7.6% from the 1.4 m an 85 mm head-and-shoulders shot is taken at, with the background 1.7× larger. So portrait and compressed-background shots need a camera standing back, whatever the resolution. Upper bounds from pixel counts (real cameras resolve less); framing distances and depths are stated assumptions.

What should a travel creator pack to film from many angles under a weight budget and the 100 Wh battery limit?

research/capture-kit · 10 tests

What should a travel creator pack to film from many angles within a weight budget and the 100 Wh battery limit? Exact search over 17,280 kits reaches all 18 band x viewpoint shots at 5 kg; a greedy packer stalls at 12 because lenses need their body (assumed device figures).

Abstract

Exact search over 17,280 kits of cameras, lenses, mounts and battery packs (11,520 legal), scoring distinct focal-band × viewpoint shots (18 at most) and recording hours against carried weight. With a 4 h recording floor, exact search reaches 15 shots at 4 kg and all 18 at 5 kg, while a greedy best-gain-per-gram packer stalls at 12 from 3 kg because a lens is worthless without its body. Without a floor, variety-first search returns kits that record only ~2.2 h. Every device figure is assumed (round class-typical numbers, not datasheets); only the 100 Wh limit is sourced.

How many cameras, and where, to film a person from every angle?

research/capture-rig · 8 tests

How many cameras, and where, to film a person from every angle? A 30° gap between neighbours needs 13 cameras on a ring (15° needs 25), but depth error is set by convergence angle, not count: a pair's error falls 11× from 5° to 90° (7.3 to 0.64 mm) while 3 vs 16 ring cameras only halves it. Idealised 2-D lower bounds.

Abstract

A 2-D geometric model of N cameras on a ring or a 90° front arc 3 m from a subject, scored on angle coverage and triangulation error. Cameras buy coverage, angle buys depth accuracy: a 30° gap between neighbours needs 13 cameras (15° needs 25), while centre depth error barely moves with count (0.52 mm for 3 on a ring, 0.23 mm for 16) but falls 11× as a pair’s convergence angle goes from 5° to 90° (7.3 mm to 0.64 mm). Four cameras on a front arc are within 11% of four on a ring. Idealised lower bounds with assumed noise (0.5 px) and no occlusion; novel-view quality is not modelled.

How far apart can cameras be before a reel editor can no longer synthesise the angle between them?

research/capture-novelview · 7 tests

How far apart can cameras be before a reel editor can no longer synthesise the angle between them? With exact depth a two-view warp beats cutting to the nearest camera only up to about 30° (24.8 vs 23.6 dB); beyond that holes dominate, and a bystander pulls the limit in to about 15° (ray-traced toy scene; ordering, not decibels).

Abstract

A ray-traced toy scene, two ring cameras, a depth-based warp to the midpoint, scored against the ray-traced truth. With exact depth, synthesis beats cutting to the nearest camera only up to a gap of about 30° (24.8 against 23.6 dB); at 45° it is worse (19.3 against 23.1 dB) because 8% of the target is a hole neither camera saw, though pixels it does fill stay near 29–30 dB. A bystander pulls the crossover in to about 15°, and depth error on the sources costs about 1.4 dB per percent at first (27.5 dB exact, 22.0 dB at 10%). Toy scene with smooth textures and oracle depth: read the ordering, not the decibels.

Is the depth a camera rig can actually measure good enough for the in-between angle?

research/capture-stereodepth · 7 tests

Is the depth a camera rig can actually measure good enough for the in-between angle? Block-matching stereo on rendered pairs feeds the two-view warp: mean depth error 14.9% at 3 cm baseline to 2.7% at 50 cm, coverage peaks at 91%, and the result never beats cutting to the nearest camera (22.3 vs 24.8 dB at a 15° gap; exact depth 27.3 dB).

Abstract

capture-novelview assumed independent depth noise of 2–5%. Here depth is measured instead: block-matching stereo on a rendered rectified pair, fed into the same two-view warp and scored against ray-traced truth at a 15° gap. Mean depth error falls from 14.9% at a 3 cm baseline to 2.7% at 50 cm, but coverage peaks at 91% near 20 cm (stereo cannot match occluded or flat pixels), and the synthesised view never beats cutting to the nearest camera: best 22.3 dB at 20 cm against 24.8 dB, with exact depth at 27.3 dB. Independent noise with the same mean error is optimistic by 1optimistic by 1–5 dBndash;7 dB, because real errors are correlated and missing depth leaves holes. Sensor noise of 3% already costs 3 dB. Toy scene, smooth textures, a classic matcher with no learned prior or hole filler.

Can the holes and correlated errors of measured stereo depth be repaired so the in-between view beats cutting to the nearest camera?

research/capture-hybrid · 6 tests

Can the holes and correlated errors of measured stereo depth be repaired so in-between synthesis beats cutting to the nearest camera? A hole filler and a no-ground-truth disagreement gate that falls back to the raw nearest camera lift a 15° gap from 22.3 dB to 29.2 dB against 24.8 dB for cutting (held-out seeds 29.2); with a bystander the gate loses its edge (26.1 vs 26.3 for plain filling). Toy scene.

Abstract

capture-stereodepth found that synthesis from block-matching stereo depth never beat cutting to the nearest camera (22.3 against 24.8 dB at a 15° gap). Here the same holey, correlated depth is repaired two ways: filling holes with the farther of the nearest valid row neighbours (27.4 dB), and a gate that needs no ground truth, where the two warped sources disagree or a hole remains, falls back to the raw nearest camera (29.2 dB, 4.4 dB above cutting; the same on three held-out noise seeds). Gains hold from 8° to 30° gaps and at 6–50 cm baselines, shrink to nothing at 6% sensor noise, and with a bystander the gate loses to plain hole filling (26.1 against 26.3 dB). A per-pixel oracle at 32.0 dB bounds what any confidence rule could reach. Stylised toy scene; read the ordering, not the decibels.

Which platform can follow a travel creator through a crowd and keep the shot?

research/capture-follow · 10 tests

Which platform can follow a creator through a crowd and keep the shot? A drone that ignores pedestrians frames 99.9% of the time but falls to 39% at 0.3 people/m² once it avoids them; a wheeled robot is stopped by step lines 42–47% of the walk, and a flat-floor one that ignores people passes within 0.5 m ~500 times an hour. 2-D simulation, assumed platform figures.

Abstract

A 2-D crowd simulation of a tripod, a wheeled robot, a wheel-legged robot and a drone holding a camera 2.5 m behind a walking creator among pedestrians who do not react to it. A drone that ignores pedestrians frames the creator 99.9% of the time at any density, but steering around people drops it to 69% at 0.1 people/m2 and 39% at 0.3; a ground robot on a plaza with curbs and one ramp is stopped 42–47% of the walk, and wheel-legs hold the frame 70% of the time in a thin crowd but 4.5% in a dense one. Avoidance and framing trade off: a flat-floor wheeled robot that ignores people frames 98% of the time while passing within 0.5 m of a pedestrian about 500 times an hour. All platform speeds, powers and masses are assumed; pedestrians do not avoid the platforms, and the drone is not limited by altitude, wind or regulation.

How much of a multi-camera shoot can an automatic edit recover, and what stops it?

research/capture-edit · 9 tests

How much of a multi-camera shoot can an automatic edit recover? At detector noise 0.3 a fixed 3 s rotation shows mean true quality 0.546, a cut-cost (Viterbi) edit 0.802 vs 0.815 on true scores, and best-score-every-frame 0.723 at 345 cuts a minute; clock sync fails first on short clips (5 s at noise 0.25: 25% over 40 ms). Synthetic quality model, no real footage.

Abstract

A synthetic model of four cameras whose true angle quality changes every 2–8 s, seen through a noisy detector. At noise 0.3 a fixed 3 s rotation shows mean true quality 0.546; an offline Viterbi edit that maximises detected quality minus a cost per cut reaches 0.802 against 0.815 for the same search on true scores, while picking the best score every frame gives 0.723 with 345 cuts a minute and every shot under 1.5 s. The cut cost matters more than detector accuracy (best near 1.0, about 14 cuts a minute). Clock sync from a shared loudness envelope fails first on short clips: a 5 s clip at noise 0.25 is off by over 40 ms in 25% of trials, 15 s in 5%. No real footage, reframing or film-look grade; quality is a made-up random process.

When does automatic blurring miss a bystander's face, and which filming platform is conspicuous?

research/capture-bystander · 11 tests

When does automatic blurring miss a bystander's face? On an assumed size-recall model a face detector leaves 5% of identifiable faces unblurred per frame, blurring the head of any detected body under 0.1%, and independent per-frame misses show a face in some frame of a 5 s shot 100% of the time; an offline whole-track blur leaves 0 to 5%. No real footage or detector.

Abstract

An assumed size-to-recall model of blurring strangers' faces while filming in public. A face detector that is 50% reliable at 20 px leaves a face-on face of at least 32 px unblurred 5% of the time per frame (8% at 5 m on an 8K 360 camera); blurring the head region of any detected body instead drops it under 0.1%, because a body is 11× taller than a face. A telephoto frame resolves about 10 identifiable people at once against 2.5 for an 8K 360 view. Per-frame blur is worse than it looks: with independent misses a person filmed for 5 s at the edge of range shows unblurred in some frame 100% of the time, 25% if misses last the whole shot, and an offline edit that blurs a whole track after one detection is left with 0–5%. Size alone does not separate the tall platforms. All detector curves, sizes and angles are assumptions; no real detector or footage; blurring is not legal compliance.

Does recording only when something happens save a travel creator's battery, and what does it cost in missed moments?

research/capture-trigger · 7 tests

Does recording only on a trigger save battery, and what does it miss? With 6 thirty-second moments an hour, standby with a 10 s pre-roll uses 19.4 Wh of an 80 Wh continuous day (76% less) and captures 90% of interesting seconds; deep sleep uses 7.7 Wh but captures 69%. Misses and wake latency, not the battery, set coverage. Assumed device figures, synthetic day.

Abstract

A synthetic 16 h day with assumed device figures. With 6 interesting 30 s moments an hour and a trigger that misses 10% and raises 2 false alarms an hour, a standby camera with a 10 s pre-roll buffer uses 19.4 Wh against 80 Wh continuous (76% less) and captures 90% of the interesting seconds; a deep-sleep camera uses 7.7 Wh but captures 69%, because it wakes too slowly to catch the start. Coverage is set by the trigger's miss rate and the wake latency, not the battery; energy decides only for a rig (four cameras: 320 Wh continuous, 78 Wh triggered). Stylised: every device figure is assumed and the trigger is a probability, not a detector.

When a camera rig records only on a trigger, should every camera have its own, or should one wake the rest?

research/capture-wake · 9 tests

When a camera rig records only on a trigger, should every camera have its own, or should one wake the rest? Four standby cameras with their own triggers use 80 Wh of 320 Wh continuous but get all four angles on only 68% of interesting seconds; one sentinel waking the others costs the same and gives 90.5%, and any-camera wake gives 100% for 92 Wh but its false-alarm energy grows with detector count. Assumed device figures.

Abstract

A synthetic 16 h day with assumed device figures and four standby cameras. With 6 thirty-second moments an hour and a 10% miss per detector, a trigger per camera uses 80 Wh (of 320 Wh continuous) but puts all four angles on only 68% of the interesting seconds, since independent misses differ by camera (≈0.94). One sentinel waking the rest costs the same 80 Wh and gives 90.5%; waking all on any camera's trigger gives 100% for 92 Wh but its false-alarm energy grows with the number of detectors (281 against 126 Wh at 40 false alarms an hour). Sleeping the other cameras halves the rig to 41.5 Wh at the cost of the first 7 s of each moment. If the misses are shared, extra detectors buy nothing. Stylised: every figure assumed, not measured.

Where in a camera ring should the sentinel go when the subject faces a favourite direction?

research/capture-sentinel · 10 tests

Where in a camera ring should the sentinel go when the subject faces a favourite direction? A sentinel facing the favourite direction misses 8.6% of moments against 34.4% for the one behind; spread cameras' misses are anti-correlated, so capture-wake's independence assumption overstates the all-miss rate by 2x on a ring of 4 and understates it 12x for a tight cluster. Assumed detector figures.

Abstract

A model with assumed detector figures (a miss rate of 3% when the subject faces the camera, 40% when facing away) and a subject whose facing at each moment is von Mises distributed around a favourite direction. In a four-camera ring with concentration κ = 2, the sentinel at the favourite direction misses 8.6% of moments, the one behind the subject 34.4%, and a rotating sentinel 21.5%; the closed form matches a 40,000-moment simulation to within 0.4 points. Misses across a spread ring are negatively correlated (a subject turned from one camera faces another), so assuming independent misses, as capture-wake did, overstates the chance that every detector misses by about 2× on a ring of four; for a tight cluster of cameras the correlation is positive and the independent figure understates it about 12×. Stylised: detector figures are assumed, not measured.

Does a soft confidence weight beat a hard switch for repairing stereo-depth view synthesis, and does matching cost help?

research/capture-softgate · 7 tests

Does a soft confidence weight beat a hard switch for repairing stereo-depth view synthesis, and does the matcher's cost help? A ramped weight on warp disagreement beats the hard gate by 0.1 to 0.5 dB in every condition (29.5 vs 29.3 dB held out at 15°) and leaves a quarter fewer seams, closing only a tenth of the oracle gap; matching cost is a worse signal than disagreement (level with hole filling, worse with a bystander). Toy scene.

Abstract

A ramped weight on how much the nearest raw camera replaces a stereo-depth synthesis, driven by disagreement between the two warps, beats the hard switch in every condition tried by 0.1–0.5 dB (29.5 against 29.3 dB at a 15° gap on held-out seeds) and leaves about a quarter fewer false edges, but closes only about a tenth of the 2.6 dB gap to a per-pixel oracle. The stereo matching cost is a worse confidence signal than warp disagreement: gating on it is level with plain hole filling and worse under a bystander or sensor noise. Stylised toy scene, parameters tuned on development seeds.

How much footage does a multi-camera travel kit produce, and what limits carrying and backing it up?

research/capture-storage · 9 tests

How much footage does a multi-camera travel kit produce, and what limits carrying and backing it up? Four cameras make ~860 GB a day; cards weigh ~8 g but the evening copy takes 2.7 h on one reader, so a safe plan needs 6 readers and ~450 g (assumed device figures).

Abstract

Data volume, card and SSD weight, nightly offload time and unbacked footage for a rig of 1 to 8 cameras filming 4 h a day for a week, at assumed 60 to 200 Mbit/s bitrates. Four cameras make about 860 GB a day (6 TB a week), yet their cards weigh about 8 g and a camera's battery is 440 to 980 times heavier per recording hour than its card. The binding limit is the evening: one reader copying one day to one SSD takes 2.7 h, so two SSD copies inside 1 h need 6 readers and about 450 g, half an hour is infeasible, and halving the bitrate halves the weight. All device figures are assumed, not datasheets.
