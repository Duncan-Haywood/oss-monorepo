What does an all-open RTL flow report for small designs, and can it prove them equivalent?

research/chip-toolchain · 8 tests

Reproducible open RTL flow (Yosys, Icarus, Verilator): gate count and depth for adders, a multiplier and a FIFO, SAT equivalence of two adders plus a broken-adder control. Gate counts, not silicon.

Abstract

Installs Yosys, Icarus Verilog and Verilator, records versions, and synthesises adders, a multiplier and a FIFO to a generic gate library: a ripple-carry adder is 5W−3 gates deep 2W−1, and the operator form + gets the same depth with a few more gates. A SAT miter proves the two adders equivalent and rejects a deliberately broken one. Stylised: gate counts, not silicon area; OpenROAD and a PDK are not yet part of the flow.

Which check catches an RTL bug: lint, random simulation or a formal equivalence proof?

research/chip-rtl-probe · 9 tests

What lint, random simulation and a Yosys equivalence proof each catch, on 12 small RTL tasks (companion to chip-rtl-loop); mutation probe shows 30 of 389 rejected bugs are caught only by the formal proof. Session-written attempts, not an LLM benchmark.

Abstract

A harness (a proposer writes Verilog; lint, random-vector simulation and a Yosys equivalence proof score it) with 12 small tasks. One attempt per task written by the session agent (not an LLM benchmark) passed every stage at round 0, so the loop never had a failure to use. Of 414 single-point mutants of those designs, lint rejects 56, simulation 303 and only the formal proof 30 (7.7% of the 389 rejected), while 25 pass everything. A no-LLM random-mutation repair baseline fixes 11/36 broken designs within 4 proposals and 22/36 within 16. Toy: tiny modules, bounded 10-cycle proof for sequential tasks, syntactic mutants.

How much does simulation and formal feedback help a proposer repair small Verilog designs?

research/chip-rtl-loop · 4 tests

Lint, simulation and formal-equivalence feedback for Verilog repair on nine small tasks; 6/9 pass at round 0 and 9/9 after one round, with the session as proposer (anecdotal, not an LLM benchmark).

Abstract

A checker (Verilator lint, Icarus lockstep simulation against a golden model, Yosys SAT proof for combinational tasks) scores nine small tasks. With the session itself as proposer, 6 of 9 pass from the spec alone and all 9 after one round of mismatch feedback; the three failures were a Gray encoder written as a decoder, a logical shift on a signed value and a combinational pulse that should be registered. Anecdotal: one attempt per round, one proposer who also wrote the tasks, no resampling baseline, simulation only for sequential tasks. Not an LLM benchmark.

What does a no-model baseline repair when a scripted RTL checker is the only feedback?

research/chip-repair-baseline · 7 tests

No-model mutation-repair baseline for a scripted Verilog checker (compile, lint, simulation, SAT miter) on ten small tasks: 9/20 one-fault cases repaired in 60 evaluations; the session agent passed all ten first try, so the feedback loop is unmeasured. Preliminary.

Abstract

A companion to chip-rtl-loop: a four-stage checker (compile, lint, simulation, SAT miter) over ten small Verilog tasks, with a random-mutation hill-climbing baseline that uses no model. Given 1, 2 or 3 injected faults and 60 checker evaluations, the baseline repairs 9/20, 4/20 and 4/20 cases. The session agent (Claude) passed all ten tasks on its first attempt, so the feedback rounds were never exercised and no pass-rate-versus-rounds curve is reported. Preliminary: tiny tasks, one attempt, mutation faults unlike a language model's mistakes, and the optional API proposer is untested.

Does a rewrite proposer beat a parameter sweep on area and depth when every rewrite must be proved equivalent?

research/chip-ppa-loop · 7 tests

Equivalence-gated area/depth rewrites of a 16-bit adder and 6x6 multiplier against a no-model parameter sweep; best adder 0.85x the reference cells×depth, tied by the sweep, no multiplier gain (stylised, gate counts).

Abstract

A loop in which rewrites of a 16-bit adder and a 6×6 multiplier must be proved equivalent to the reference by a Yosys miter before a generic-gate ABC script scores cells and depth. With nine attempts written by the session agent (not an LLM benchmark; two were seeded faults the gate rejected), the best adder is a 4-bit carry-select at 0.85× the reference cells×depth, the same circuit family as the best config of a no-model parameter sweep, so a random sweep of the same budget never beats it (ties in 63 of 200 seeds). No multiplier rewrite beat a * b. Stylised: gate counts, not layout; one tool version; two designs.

How does generic-gate area grow with bit width for adders, shifters, popcount and multipliers?

research/chip-scaling · 4 tests

How generic-gate cells and depth scale with bit width for seven arithmetic blocks: adder, comparator and mux are near-linear, the multiplier quadratic (exponent 2.19), and adder depth grows ~2&times;W (stylised, generic gates).

Abstract

Seven behavioural blocks synthesised by Yosys 0.33 to generic gates at widths 4–64. Cell counts for the adder, comparator, equality and 4:1 mux are close to linear in width (fitted exponent 1.00–1.13); the multiplier is quadratic (2.19 overall, 2.06 on the largest widths); the barrel shifter sits at 1.27–1.35. Logic depth scales differently from cells: the default-script adder's depth grows about 2×W (7 to 125) while the comparator's grows slowly (5 to 16). Preliminary: five widths, one tool version, generic gates rather than library area, no confidence intervals.

Does writing a fixed-weight multiplier as shift-and-add with canonical signed digits beat Yosys's x * K?

research/chip-constmul · 6 tests

Fixed-weight multipliers for all 255 8-bit constants: Yosys's x * K tracks binary shift-add (Pearson 0.97 with set bits), and a canonical-signed-digit form has 33% fewer generic-gate cells at similar depth (every form proved equivalent).

Abstract

For every 8-bit constant K (1–255), an 8-bit input is multiplied three ways — the * operator, binary shift-add, and canonical-signed-digit (CSD) shift-add/subtract — and scored by Yosys 0.33 in generic gates; every variant is proved equivalent to the operator form by a miter. The operator tracks binary shift-add (cells correlate 0.97 with the number of set bits of K, 0.65 with CSD digits). CSD uses 75.7 mean cells against 112.9 for the operator (33.0% fewer; fewer cells for 230 of 255 constants, worse for 14) at similar mean depth (20.0 vs 19.6); K=255 drops from 222 to 30 cells. Preliminary: one tool version, one ABC script, generic gates rather than library area, one input width, no shared sub-sums across constants.

Does sharing sub-sums across a bank of fixed-weight multipliers cut gates beyond per-constant shift-and-add?

research/chip-mcm · 6 tests

A bank of fixed-weight multipliers sharing sub-sums (multiple-constant multiplication): 9% fewer generic-gate cells than per-constant signed-digit shift-add at 2 constants and about 20% fewer at 8 and 16 (43% fewer than Yosys's *), every form proved equivalent; random constants, not trained weights.

Abstract

A follow-up to chip-constmul: one 8-bit input feeds a bank of M random 8-bit constants (M = 2, 4, 8, 16; 40 banks each), written three ways — the * operator, per-constant canonical-signed-digit (CSD) shift-add, and a greedy shared-sub-sum (MCM) form — scored by Yosys 0.33 in generic gates, every variant proved equivalent to the operator form. Sharing uses 9% fewer cells than per-constant CSD at M = 2 and about 20% fewer at M = 8 and 16 (690 vs 866 at M = 16; 43% fewer than the operator), fewer in 130 of 160 banks and more in 2. Adders removed in the source predict cells saved (Pearson 0.875, about 15 cells per adder). Sharing also recovers the depth CSD loses on large banks (28.2 vs 33.2 at M = 16). Preliminary: random rather than trained weights, one greedy heuristic, one tool version, generic gates rather than library area.

How many gates could an exact 4-input rewriter still remove from Yosys/ABC netlists?

research/chip-cutheadroom · 7 tests

How many gates an exact 4-input rewriter could still remove from real Yosys/ABC netlists: none in 28 of 30 runs (1 gate in two), even where the default script beats plain mapping by 25 to 42%, so the flow's gains are not local.

Abstract

chip-exact4 found Yosys/ABC about 1.2 gates above the proven minimum on isolated 4-input functions. Here every gate of six small netlists (adder, multiplier, comparator, popcount, shifter, priority encoder) under five ABC flows is checked against that exact minimum over all its 4-leaf cuts, counting only logic a local replacement could delete. The disjoint headroom is 0 gates in 28 of 30 runs and 1 gate in the other two, even though the default script cuts 25–42% of the gates of plain mapping on add16 and prio16: the flow’s gains are not local, so an exact small-cone rewrite proposer would find almost nothing. Stylised: six blocks, gate count with free inverters, one Yosys build; replacement circuits were not constructed.

How many gates above the proven minimum does Yosys/ABC use for 4-input logic?

research/chip-exact4 · 5 tests

How far above the SAT-proven minimum gate count Yosys/ABC lands on 4-input logic: the default flow is exactly optimal on only 34% of sampled functions (mean 1.19 gates above, max 4), and a heavier script is worse.

Abstract

The exact minimum 2-input gate count of every 4-input Boolean function (222 NPN classes, by SAT) against what Yosys/ABC returns on 884 sampled functions. The default flow is exactly optimal on only 34% and averages 1.19 gates above the minimum (max 4); the best of seven scripts per function reaches 38%. A heavier resyn2 script is worse (1.72), and the count changes with which negated or permuted form of the same function is synthesised. One Yosys version, generic gates, 4 inputs only. Preliminary.

Does the ABC synthesis script matter as much as the written RTL structure for adder and multiplier area and depth?

research/chip-abc-sweep · 4 tests

Sweeps five ABC scripts over five adder and eight multiplier structures (all proved equivalent): the script moves cells&times;depth by up to 1.75&times; for one adder, while all eight multiplier structures score identically (stylised, generic-gate).

Abstract

A follow-up to chip-ppa-loop: five ABC scripts are applied to five written 16-bit adder structures and eight 6×6 multiplier structures, each proved equivalent to its reference by a Yosys miter. For the adder, cells×depth moves by up to 1.75× with the script for one structure and 1.18–1.94× across structures for a fixed script; the best pair is 0.48× the default-script plain adder. All eight multiplier structures score identically under each script, yet the script alone moves them by 1.17×. Stylised: two designs, one Yosys version, generic-gate counts rather than layout.

Does the order or naming of equivalent Verilog change what Yosys and ABC synthesise?

research/chip-abc-order · 4 tests

Whether equivalent Verilog that differs only in wire names, statement order or the order of added operands synthesises to the same size: names and statement order never change cells&times;depth, operand order does for one carry-select adder (up to 1.43&times;, a 17% better result than the earlier sweep's best; stylised).

Abstract

Provably equivalent rewrites of generated adder and multiplier RTL (random wire names, shuffled statements, shuffled order of added operands; 864 scored variants, every text proved equivalent to its reference) are scored under three ABC scripts. Names and statement order never change cells×depth. Operand order changes one carry-select adder by up to 1.43× within a script, and its best variant under dch is 17% better than the best adder chip-abc-sweep reported, so that earlier best was one draw from a distribution the text controls. Five other structures are unaffected. Stylised: two designs, one Yosys version, 12 seeds, generic-gate counts, cause not yet traced.

Do the blocks of a carry-select adder share a + b independently when every chain gets its own operand order?

research/chip-abc-orderblocks · 10 tests

Tests whether carry-select blocks share a+b independently under mixed per-chain operand orders: the set of sharing blocks predicts Yosys's adder count in 400 of 400 random variants and fixes the cells x depth score (8 sets, one score each per ABC script); the cost is not additive across blocks (stylised, Yosys 0.33).

Abstract

A follow-up to chip-abc-orderpass, which only ran uniform orders. 400 random mixed variants of a 16-bit carry-select adder (each of the 7 a + b + const chains in its own random operand order; all proved equivalent to the reference) are scored under three ABC scripts. The set of blocks whose two chains both start with a, b predicts the Yosys adder count after opt in 400 of 400 variants and fixes the cells × depth: all 8 possible sets occurred, and each had exactly one score per script. The effect is not additive: sharing only block 1 costs 1.38× under dch_map, only block 3 costs 1.00×. Stylised: one adder, Yosys 0.33, a generic-gate library, no place and route.

Which Yosys pass makes the operand order of a + b + constant change the synthesised adder?

research/chip-abc-orderpass · 7 tests

Finds the Yosys pass behind chip-abc-order's operand-order effect: opt_merge shares a+b between the two carry-select sums only when both are written with a and b first (4 of 36 operand orders); removing it widens the spread (up to 3.2&times;), so it was hiding variation. One adder, stylised.

Abstract

A follow-up to chip-abc-order. All 36 operand orders of a 16-bit carry-select adder's a + b + const chains (each proved equivalent to the reference) are run through synth one pass at a time. The cell counts first diverge in opt_merge: when a block's two sums both start with a and b, one shared a + b is kept (7 adders instead of 10), which happens in 4 of 36 orders. Cells×depth then takes only two values per ABC script, and the shared netlist is 1.20–1.28× larger under dch_map and resyn2_map but 0.92× under Yosys's default script. Removing opt_merge does not remove the dependence: the spread over orders widens to 1.44–3.17×. Stylised: one adder, Yosys 0.33, generic-gate cells×depth, no place and route.

Does letting Yosys share a common a + b make adders smaller under every ABC script?

research/chip-sharesign · 3 tests

Tests whether letting Yosys share a common a + b shrinks adders: fewer cells in 54 of 55 design/script pairs but often deeper; stylised, Yosys 0.33.

Abstract

Eleven small adder-shaped designs whose outputs all contain a + b are written once so opt_merge shares the sum and once so it cannot (each pair proved equivalent). Scored under five ABC scripts, sharing gives fewer cells in 54 of 55 pairs (median ratio 0.76–0.85), but is deeper in 6–9 of 11 designs, so cells × depth favours it in 7–9 of 11. The sign flip seen on the 16-bit carry-select adder in chip-abc-orderpass does not appear. Stylised: generic gates, Yosys 0.33, no place and route.

Does writing the shared a + b sum of a carry-select adder explicitly change what Yosys and ABC synthesise?

research/chip-cse-style · 3 tests

Writing the shared a + b sum of a carry-select adder by hand (explicitly, or as an incrementer) gives the same netlist Yosys already finds; attaching the constant to b first defeats the sharing and is 17-22% smaller under ABC's optimising scripts (stylised, Yosys 0.33).

Abstract

Six equivalent 16-bit adder styles (each proved against a + b with a Yosys miter) are scored with three ABC scripts. Writing the shared sum by hand (p = a + b, y = p + 1) or as an incrementer (y = x + 1) gives exactly the netlist opt_merge finds for the plain a + b + 0 / a + b + 1 text (cells×depth 2090 / 1232 / 1665), so a hand common-subexpression rewrite is a no-op. Attaching the constant to b first, a + (b + k), defeats the sharing: 2268 / 1024 / 1300, 17–22% smaller under the two optimising scripts and 9% larger under yosys_default. One design, one tool version (0.33), generic gates. Stylised.

How far do synthesis-only area and delay sit from placed-and-routed numbers on SKY130?

Synthesis-only area and delay versus placed-and-routed OpenROAD numbers on SKY130 for 13 small designs (stylised, global-route estimates).

Abstract

Thirteen small designs (adders, multipliers, FIFOs) pushed through generic gate counting, Yosys/ABC mapping onto the SKY130 HD library, and an OpenROAD flow with placement, clock tree, global routing and timing repair. Re-synthesises the 16- and 32-lane MAC engines (same cycles) with three seeds and three Yosys flows: the sign of the cell-count gap depends on the flow, with swings of 1-10%, so chip-systolic-p32's 0.7% ranking is not reliable (artefact-sensitive, stylised, generic-gate counts).

Abstract

chip-systolic-p32 found the 32-lane MAC engine smaller than the 16-lane one although both take the same cycles. Re-synthesised with three model seeds and three Yosys/abc flows at F = 16 and 32, the sign depends on the flow: P = 32 is smaller in all 6 cases under two flows and larger in all 6 under a four-gate library, by 47 to 699 cells (1–10%). That swing is far above the 0.7% margin used to rank the best design earlier, so that ranking should be discarded. Three seeds, one tool version, generic-gate counts only; the mechanism is not traced.
