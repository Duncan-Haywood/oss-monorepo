Research topic

Sensing, imaging and 3D

Getting more from cheap sensors: fusing cheap IMUs and cameras, computational imaging, and 3D models from real captures, with the error floors that correlated errors set.

14 projects, each with its own page, code and tests. See them on the full research index.

How many cheap gyros, fused, match one better gyro at the same cost?

research/sensing-imuarray · 8 tests

How many cheap gyros, fused, match one better gyro at equal cost? With independent errors the price ratio must beat k² (100 for a 10× quieter unit); shared error above 1/k² means no array size ties; unequal white/bias improvement makes the answer depend on integration time. Stylised.

Abstract

Single-axis gyro model with angle random walk and rate random walk (Allan-variance terms) and a fraction ρ of each shared between units. With independent errors an array of n cheap units ties a unit k times quieter only when the price ratio exceeds k2 (9 for k = 3, 100 for k = 10); shared error above 1/k2 means no array size ties (k = 10, ρ ≥ 0.01). If the better unit improves white noise 10× but bias walk only 3×, the number of units needed falls from 100 at 1 s to 10 beyond 300 s, so the answer depends on integration time. Monte Carlo matches the closed form within 0.03. Stylised: illustrative grades, not datasheets; no real IMU data; no prices claimed.

Does a shared temperature coefficient put a floor under an array of cheap gyros?

research/sensing-imutemp · 9 tests

Does a shared temperature coefficient put a floor under an array of cheap gyros? The shared part of the tempco does not average out: in the illustrative case a 3× better gyro can no longer be matched at any array size above a 0.2 °C/min ramp over 100 s; compensation quality, not array size, decides.

Abstract

Follow-up to sensing-imuarray. Each gyro has a temperature coefficient ci = μ + s·ei; under a temperature ramp the shared mean μ leaves an angle-error floor μrT2/2 that no array size removes. In the illustrative case (100 s) a 3× better unit can be matched at 10–15 units up to 0.1 °C/min but at no size from 0.3 °C/min; a 10× better one at none from 0.1 °C/min. Compensating both grades equally brings the array size back near k2 only at about 2% residual tempco. Monte Carlo matches the closed form within 1%. Stylised: assumed tempcos, no real IMU data, no prices claimed.

How many frames from a small sensor, merged, match one frame from a bigger one?

research/sensing-burst · 12 tests

How many frames from a small sensor, merged, match one frame from a sensor with A× the pixel area? A shot-noise-limited burst needs A frames, a read-noise-limited one up to A² (formula and simulation agree); splitting the same total exposure never ties; registration blur and bracketed HDR are measured. Synthetic scenes only.

Abstract

Synthetic scenes with Poisson shot noise, Gaussian read noise and a full-well clip. A burst of N small-sensor frames (each at the large frame's exposure time) ties a sensor with A× the pixel area at N* = A²(sx+r²)/(Asx+r²): A frames when shot noise dominates, up to A² in the dark or at high read noise (simulation, A=4: 4.0 frames in bright light, 14.4 in dim light with 8 e− read noise). Splitting the same total exposure into frames never ties: the burst has more variance than the large frame at every N. With hand shake and Fourier-shift registration, shifts are recovered to 0.09–0.8 px RMS in good light but fail in the dimmest case tested (3.1 px), and the registration blur means A=4 needs about 21 frames in bright light. A bracketed burst recovers clipped highlights (42 dB against 22 dB for one frame). Stylised: synthetic images, global translation only, no real raw files.

What does a moving subject do to a burst merge, and what does robust merging cost?

research/sensing-ghost · 7 tests

What does a moving subject do to a burst merge? A plain mean ghosts at every speed and gets worse as frames are added (30.5 dB at 8 frames, 25.0 at 48), median and sigma-clip do no better for a slow subject, and a reference-guided merge keeps improving and ties a 4×-area frame at about 6 frames at 0.1 dB cost when nothing moves. Synthetic scenes only.

Abstract

Still camera, one disc moving across a synthetic burst (Poisson shot noise, Gaussian read noise, a large sensor with 4× the pixel area). A plain mean merge ghosts at every speed tested and gets worse as frames are added (30.5 dB at 8 frames, 25.0 dB at 48 for 0.25 px per frame, against 30.8 dB for one large-sensor frame); median and sigma-clip merges do no better because a slow subject covers each pixel in most frames. A reference-guided merge (add a frame only where it agrees with frame 0) keeps improving, to 37.6 dB at 48 frames, and ties the large frame at about 6 frames; with nothing moving it costs 0.1 dB against the mean, the median 1.3 dB. Stylised: synthetic images, one translating disc, a still camera, untuned thresholds, no real raw files.

Can aligning a moving subject, instead of rejecting it, rescue a burst merge?

research/sensing-ghostalign · 7 tests

Can aligning a moving subject rescue a burst merge instead of rejecting it? Inside the subject, yes (37.0 dB against 31.5 for reject-only and 32.8 for a 4×-area frame at 8 frames), but over the whole image rejecting is as good or better (31.7 against 31.8 dB), the aligner moves 10–31% of static pixels on noise, and the revealed-background ring stays 4 dB short of the large frame. Synthetic scenes only.

Abstract

Still camera, one disc moving across a synthetic burst (Poisson shot noise, Gaussian read noise, a large sensor with 4× the pixel area). A per-pixel sub-pixel alignment to the reference frame, followed by a rejection test, reaches 37.0 dB inside the disc at 8 frames and 1 px per frame, against 31.5 dB for reject-only merging and 32.8 dB for the large frame. Over the whole image it is no better than rejecting (31.7 against 31.8 dB at 8 frames, 34.4 against 35.6 at 24) because the aligner moves 10–31% of static pixels on noise, and in the ring where the background is covered and revealed no variant gets within 4 dB of the large frame. Stylised: synthetic scenes, one flat rigid disc, translation only.

Can a burst of hand-shaken low-resolution frames recover detail that merging and upsampling cannot?

research/sensing-superres · 10 tests

Can a burst of hand-shaken low-resolution frames recover detail that merging and upsampling cannot? At 2× in bright light a least-squares reconstruction reaches 29.5 dB at 16 frames against 24.0 for merge-and-upsample and 26.6 for merge-and-deblur, so 2.9 dB is sub-pixel information; it nearly vanishes in dim light and falls to nothing at about 0.27 px of shift error. Synthetic scenes only.

Abstract

Synthetic 32×32 frames from a box-averaging sensor (Poisson shot noise, 3 e− read noise), shifted by up to two pixels, reconstructed on a 2× grid by regularised least squares. In bright light it reaches 29.5 dB at 16 frames and 30.7 dB at 32, against 24.0 dB for register, average and bilinear upsample and 26.6 dB for register, average and deconvolve: 2.6 dB of the gain is deblurring the pixel box and 2.9 dB (4.0 at 32 frames) is sub-pixel information. In dim light that part is within ±0.3 dB; registration error of 0.27 LR pixel erases it. Against a sensor with 2× the linear resolution and the same total light the burst wins at 2 frames and loses from about 4; at 3× it ties at 16 frames and at 4× it wins. Synthetic scenes, translation only, no mosaic or lens blur; the reconstruction is plain Tikhonov, not a published method.

How many milliseconds of camera-to-gyro time offset double a fused attitude error, and can online calibration recover it?

research/sensing-timesync · 7 tests

How many milliseconds of camera-to-gyro time offset double a fused attitude error, and can an alignment fit recover it? About 12–14 ms at 30 deg/s motion; the fit recovers it to ~1 ms with motion and fails on a still platform. Synthetic, single axis.

Abstract

Single-axis simulation: a 200 Hz biased gyro and a 30 Hz camera whose timestamps are late by td, fused by a complementary filter. At 30 deg/s motion the error doubles at 12–14 ms; the doubling offset is 32–34 ms at 10 deg/s and 8–10 ms at 90 deg/s, and grows with camera noise. A gyro-integral alignment fit recovers the offset to about 1 ms rms and restores the zero-offset error, but fails on a nearly still platform. Stylised: one axis, constant offset, angle measurements only, synthetic noise.

How much motion does a camera-to-gyro offset fit need, and what does clock skew do to a one-off calibration?

research/sensing-syncskew · 6 tests

How much motion does a camera-to-gyro offset fit need, and what does clock skew do to a one-off calibration? A 20 ms offset needs about 16 deg/s rms motion to fit reliably; 1000 ppm skew leaves 0.254 deg error after a fixed-offset fit against 0.155 deg when skew is fitted too. Stylised, synthetic.

Abstract

Same single-axis gyro and camera world as sensing-timesync, with the camera stamp error growing as td = td0 + skew·t. An alignment fit recovers a 20 ms offset within 5 ms in 8 of 8 runs at 16 deg/s or more, but only 2 of 8 at 1 deg/s and 0 of 8 at 0.3 deg/s. A camera clock 1000 ppm fast raises attitude error from 0.152 to 0.514 deg uncorrected and 0.254 deg after a fixed-offset fit, while fitting offset and skew together gives 0.155 deg up to 2000 ppm; skew recovery needs a window of about 40 s. Stylised: one axis, linear skew, synthetic noise, skews exaggerated to show an effect in 40 s.

How accurate must a scan be to rebuild a part as parametric CAD?

research/sensing-scan2cad · 12 tests

How accurate must a scan be to rebuild a part as parametric CAD? On synthetic plate scans, RANSAC plane and circle fits give all five dimensions within 0.2 mm for noise up to 0.2 mm, but at 0.5 mm noise the error is a bias (thickness −0.20 mm) that more points do not remove, and a 0.5% scale error alone moves an 80 mm length by 0.40 mm. Synthetic, one shape.

Abstract

Synthetic point-cloud scans of an 80×40×10 mm plate with two 8 mm holes, fitted by RANSAC planes and circle fits. With scan noise of 0.2 mm or less every scan gives all five dimensions within 0.2 mm at 2 000 points or more (worst RMS 0.09 mm). At 0.5 mm noise only 0–50% of scans are usable at any point count because the error is a bias: thickness reads 0.20 mm low and the bias grows with the inlier band (−0.07 to −0.38 mm for 1.5σ to 4σ). A scale error of 0.5% from a reconstruction with no scale reference already moves an 80 mm length by 0.40 mm. Stylised: one shape, assumed Gaussian noise, pre-registered scan, generated CadQuery script not run.

How many photos does a usable 3-D reconstruction need?

research/sensing-recon · 11 tests

How many photos does a usable 3-D reconstruction need? On a synthetic object with known cameras, 12 views on a full orbit triangulate every feature, 8 views 89% and 4 or fewer at most 13%; per-point error then depends on pixel noise and pose error, not view count. Triangulation stage only (synthetic).

Abstract

Synthetic bumpy-ellipsoid object, 800 features, pinhole cameras on an orbit with known poses and linear (DLT) triangulation. On a full orbit 12 views reconstruct every feature, 8 views 89%, 6 views 59% and 4 or fewer at most 13%, because a point must be seen by two cameras. Once triangulated, error is about 0.0028 object radii per pixel of image noise, a 1° pose error does the damage of about 2 px, and a narrow baseline (5° span) raises error about 7× against a 40° span. Triangulation stage only: matches and poses are given, there is no occlusion, mesh or splat, and no real images.

Does a low-resolution thermal camera find people in darkness and smoke, and what does misalignment cost?

research/sensing-thermalfuse · 10 tests

Does a low-resolution thermal camera find people in darkness and smoke, and what does misalignment cost? On synthetic scenes RGB finds 36% in bright light and none in the dark or dense smoke, thermal alone 65% everywhere (capped by warm decoys), fusion 94% in bright light and the thermal level otherwise; a 4 px (one thermal pixel) displacement costs the fused detector 19 points and 6 px erases the gain. Synthetic only.

Abstract

Synthetic 64×64 scenes with people, cold clutter and warm non-person decoys, seen by a grey RGB camera (shot and read noise, smoke airlight) and a 16×16 thermal camera (0.3 K noise, 10% of the visible smoke extinction assumed). At 10% false alarms per image, RGB alone finds 36% of people in bright clear light, 13% at smoke transmittance 0.4 and none in the dark or dense smoke; thermal alone finds 65% in every condition, capped by the decoys; fusing them gives 94% in bright light and falls back to the thermal level otherwise. A thermal camera displaced by 2 px costs the fused detector 7 points, 4 px (one thermal pixel) 19, and 6 px leaves it below RGB alone (0.12 vs 0.37); training with displacement jitter keeps 0.54 at 6 px but loses 9 points when aligned. Stylised: synthetic scenes, logistic-regression detectors on local contrast, sideways shift only, no real data.

Which upgrade buys the most visual-inertial accuracy: a sharper camera or a better IMU?

research/sensing-vio · 8 tests

Which upgrade buys the most visual-inertial accuracy, a sharper camera or a better IMU? On a planar synthetic smoother only camera resolution moved drift (1280 px 1.2 % vs 1.7 % at 640 px); a 0.25–4× change in IMU noise did not, on 12 s paths. Stylised, not EuRoC.

Abstract

A planar synthetic visual-inertial smoother (bearing landmarks, 100 Hz gyro and accelerometer) on 12 s paths, 8 paired seeds. Camera resolution moved drift: 1.18 % of path rms at 1280 px against 1.65 % at 640 px and 8.9 % at 320 px. Scaling IMU noise 0.25× to 4× changed it by under 10 %, inside the noise. Limitations: the smoother has no marginalisation and diverged on 30 s paths, so the IMU result is for short runs only; planar, synthetic, not EuRoC.

Does modelling the sub-pixel coverage of a moving subject fix the edge ring that burst merging leaves behind?

research/sensing-ghostedge · 6 tests

Does modelling the sub-pixel coverage of a moving subject fix the edge ring? Yes, on a known shape: a layered disc-over-background model fitted from the burst lifts the ring from 27.1 to 33.1 dB at 8 frames and 2 px per frame (4×-area frame: 31.7 dB), less at slow speeds, and it is sensitive to a 0.5 px centre error. Synthetic flat disc only.

Abstract

Follow-up to sensing-ghostring, where no merge or mask fixed the edge ring. Here each frame is a layered mix, a·disc + (1−a)·background, with sub-pixel coverage a from a fitted disc, and the background solved by least squares over the frames. On the same synthetic bursts at 8 frames and 2 px per frame the ring goes from 27.1 dB (aligned plus reject) to 33.1 dB with the disc fitted from the burst itself (large sensor, 4× area: 31.7 dB); at 0.5–1 px per frame it gains 0.4–2.2 dB and stays below the large frame. It is sensitive to geometry: a 0.5 px centre error drops the ring to 25 dB. Stylised: the model assumes the exact shape of the synthetic subject (a flat disc), so this is an upper-bound-style result, not a general deghosting method.

Can a background estimate or a subject mask fix the edge ring that burst alignment leaves behind?

research/sensing-ghostring · 6 tests

Can a temporal-median background or a subject mask fix the edge ring that burst alignment leaves behind? No: four ring treatments leave it at 21.6–27.2 dB at 8 frames against 31.6–32.0 for a 4×-area frame, none beats plain aligned-plus-reject (27.0 dB), and the mask misses most of a slow subject. Synthetic scenes only.

Abstract

Follow-up to sensing-ghostalign, on the same synthetic bursts (one disc moving across a still scene, Poisson shot noise, Gaussian read noise, a large sensor with 4× the pixel area). Four explicit treatments of the edge ring — frame 0 alone, a per-pixel temporal median, the aligned merge, the reject-only merge, each gated by a subject mask estimated against the temporal median — all leave the ring at 21.6–27.2 dB at 8 frames against 31.6–32.0 dB for the large frame. None beats plain aligned-plus-reject (27.0 dB at 1 px per frame); the median is the worst (22.3 dB) unless the subject moves 4 px per frame, and the mask recalls only 44–64% of a slow subject. A negative result. Stylised: synthetic scenes, one flat rigid disc, translation only.
