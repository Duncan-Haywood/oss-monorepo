Research topic

Agentic co-design of low-cost robots

Designing a cheap robot and its controller together in simulation, with an agent, CMA-ES, Bayesian optimisation and random search at equal budget, for civilian domains.

12 projects, each with its own page, code and tests. See them on the full research index.

Which printable two-finger gripper holds eight different objects for the least plastic, and can an agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation at 40 simulations?

research/codesign-gripper · 9 tests

Which printable two-finger gripper holds eight objects for the least plastic, and can an agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation at 40 MuJoCo evaluations? Median best objective 0.991 (Bayesian opt) vs 0.979 (CMA-ES), 0.965 (random) and 0.963 (session agent, one run); the cheapest winners (0.5 g flat fingers) are also the most fragile when object offsets triple. Stylised, no learned policy.

Abstract

A parametric 3D-printable gripper finger (length, thickness, pad width, V angle) scored in MuJoCo on eight objects with a per-design grip force. At 40 evaluations the median best objective is 0.991 for Bayesian optimisation, 0.979 for CMA-ES, 0.965 for random search and 0.963 for a session agent’s hand-written proposals (one run; Bayesian optimisation matched or beat it in 10 of 10 seeds). The best designs are tiny flat fingers (0.5 g), but re-scored with object offsets tripled they hold 5 of 8 objects against 6 of 8 for the agent’s wide V. Rigid, stylised, no learned policy, nothing printed.

Which cheap wheel-legged rover covers the most stylised rubble under a 120 USD parts cap, and does a session agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation?

research/codesign-sar · 8 tests

Which cheap wheel-legged rover covers the most stylised rubble under a 120 USD cap, and can an agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation at 40 evaluations? Wheel radius at its bound plus about 0.5 N m torque reaches the ceiling (J 0.783, 69 USD); Bayesian opt and CMA-ES 0.782, session agent 0.783, random 0.716; stylised, falls never occurred.

Abstract

Five design variables (wheel radius, wheelbase, flipper length, drive torque, battery) are scored on twelve held-out random-rubble terrains with Gamma-patchy friction, each design with its own evolution-strategies speed-and-flipper policy (not PPO). Wheel radius at its 90 mm bound with torque of about 0.5 N m reaches the ceiling J = 0.783 (16 of 20 m) at 69 USD. Median best J at 40 evaluations: Bayesian optimisation 0.782, CMA-ES 0.782, session agent 0.783, random search 0.716; the gaps at the top are within policy-search noise. Falls were zero throughout, so the tip-over and recovery path is untested. Stylised statics, assumed unsourced prices, one agent run with a calibration-time prior.

Which three-finger pad design picks the most unbruised strawberry-like fruit under a 60 USD cap, and does a session agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation?

research/codesign-agri · 7 tests

Which three-finger pad design picks the most unbruised strawberry-like fruit under a 60 USD cap, and can an agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation at 40 evaluations? Bayesian optimisation did (median J 0.635 against the agent's 0.618); about 20% of fruit are lost to clutter collisions no pad setting changes. Stylised, assumed constants.

Abstract

Five design variables (pad width, pad length, pad modulus, grip force, finger count) are scored on 600 simulated fruit with ripeness-dependent bruise thresholds, wet and dry friction and placement error, using closed-form line-contact pressure, friction hold and a capture-and-clutter law. At 40 evaluations the median best objective is 0.635 for Bayesian optimisation, 0.618 for a session agent’s hand-written proposals (one run, by the model’s own author), 0.611 for CMA-ES and 0.574 for random search; all 20 Bayesian-optimisation seeds matched or beat the agent. The best design (25 mm pads, 0.19 MPa, 8 N, three fingers) picks 74% clean; about 20% of fruit are lost to clutter collisions that no pad setting can change. Stylised, with assumed constants and no learned policy.

Which suction gripper picks the most of a stylised mixed tote under an 80 USD cap, and does a session agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation?

research/codesign-logistics · 7 tests

Which suction gripper picks the most of a stylised mixed tote (boxes, polybags, bottles) under an 80 USD cap, and can an agent beat CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation at 40 evaluations? The agent's best J was 0.519 against 0.478 for Bayesian optimisation (median of 20 seeds); the best design is two 18.5 mm cups at 80 kPa and 60 L/min, and a catalogue-default single 30 mm cup scores 0.075. Stylised, assumed constants.

Abstract

Five design variables (cup diameter, cups in a row, pump vacuum, pump flow, cup compliance) are scored on 600 simulated items (boxes, leaky polybags, bottles) with a cup-fit law on each item’s flat patch, vacuum lost to leaks, holding force against 1.5× a 1 g-accelerated load, and a pick-time penalty. At 40 evaluations the median best objective is 0.478 for Bayesian optimisation, 0.361 for CMA-ES and 0.328 for random search; a session agent’s hand-written proposals (one run, by the model’s own author) reached 0.519, and 1 of 20 Bayesian-optimisation seeds matched it. The best design (two 18.5 mm cups, 80 kPa, 60 L/min) holds 77% of items quickly; a catalogue-default single 30 mm cup holds none quickly (J 0.075). Stylised, with assumed constants, no learned policy and no general-purpose arm modelled.

Should a quadrotor’s airframe and its learned attitude controller be sized together, or one after the other?

research/codesign-aero · 9 tests

Should a quadrotor's airframe and its learned roll controller be sized together? Yes in this stylised model: joint CMA-ES reaches 5.25° RMS roll error, equal to the grid optimum, against 7.06° for lightest-airframe-first; a controller reused across airframes is worse on 41% of them.

Abstract

A 15-minute inspection quadrotor with two airframe variables (arm length, prop diameter) and a three-gain roll policy found by CMA-ES, scored on held-out gusty step-tracking episodes. At 320 evaluations, joint CMA-ES reaches 5.25° RMS (the brute-force grid optimum is 5.25°), against 5.63° for joint random search, 5.52° for a frozen controller, 5.88° for bilevel search and 7.06° for design-first (lightest airframe, then tune). A controller tuned on one airframe and reused on others is 1° or more worse on 41% of cells. Gust level did not move the optimum. Stylised roll-axis model with assumed constants; the policy is linear, not deep RL; no flight software-in-the-loop.

Does the best quadrotor airframe stay best when motor strength, mass and motor lag are randomised?

research/codesign-aero-robust · 7 tests

Does the best quadrotor airframe stay best under widened motor-strength, mass and lag randomisation? Mostly: top five unchanged (Spearman 0.75), but retuning gains helps the median airframe 0.91° (stylised).

Abstract

Robustness follow-up to codesign-aero: 50 feasible airframes, each with roll gains tuned on the nominal, a narrow or a wide randomised plant (torque 0.7–1.0×, inertia 0.9–1.3×, motor lag 1–2×, assumed). With nominal-tuned gains the nominal and wide rankings have Spearman 0.75 and the same top five; the nominal optimum is 2nd of 50 on the wide plant, 0.17° behind the best. Tuning gains on the wide plant helps the median airframe by 0.91°. One tuning run per cell, so neighbouring ranks are within noise. Stylised, roll axis only, simulation only.

Should a quadrotor’s airframe and controller gains be searched jointly when the plant is uncertain?

research/codesign-aero-joint · 5 tests

Should airframe and gains be searched jointly under an uncertain plant? Yes in this stylised model: joint CMA-ES at 960 evaluations (5.75° median held-out, 8 seeds) beats random search and a 4800-evaluation grid-then-tune best cell (6.00°).

Abstract

Follow-up to codesign-aero-robust: joint CMA-ES over arm length, prop diameter and three roll gains, trained on the wide randomised plant, against uniform random search at equal budget and against grid-then-tune. On held-out episodes the median CMA-ES winner reaches 5.75° at 960 evaluations (8 seeds, 5.70–5.87°) and 5.74° at 4800, random search 6.17° and 5.96°, and the grid’s best cell (4800 evaluations, picked on the held-out set) 6.00°. Stylised: assumed constants and ranges, roll axis only, five parameters, coarse grid reference.

Is the quadrotor airframe ranking from one tuning run reproducible, or is it tuning noise?

research/codesign-aero-seeds · 6 tests

Is the airframe ranking from one tuning run reproducible? With wide-tuned gains yes (Spearman 0.99 over six replicates); with nominal-tuned gains it is noisier (0.84) and the earlier 0.17° regret was within noise (stylised).

Abstract

Seed-repeat follow-up to codesign-aero-robust: the 50-airframe grid is re-tuned and re-scored six times with independent CMA-ES, training and held-out seeds. With gains tuned on the wide randomised plant the ranking is highly reproducible (Spearman 0.99 between replicates, same top five every time); with nominal-tuned gains it is noisier (0.84, winner varies in arm length), so the earlier “rank 2, 0.17° regret” was inside the noise. Retuning for the wide plant helps the median airframe by 0.88° on average. Stylised (assumed constants, roll axis only, six replicates).

Does the ranking of printable soft-fruit gripper designs survive a shift in friction, fruit, placement error and print tolerance?

research/codesign-transfer · 6 tests

Does the ranking of soft-fruit gripper designs survive a shift in friction, fruit, placement error and print tolerance? Yes in this model (Spearman 0.993 over 600 designs), but the nominal optimum loses 13% of its J and a robust optimum gains only about 0.015 on average; sim-to-sim, assumed ranges.

Abstract

Takes the codesign-agri contact model unchanged and widens what it holds fixed: per world the fruit size, placement error, wetness, bruise threshold and stem pull are drawn, and the printed gripper differs from the design (pad modulus, force and width tolerance). Over 600 random feasible designs and 30 held-out worlds the Spearman rank correlation with the nominal world is 0.993, so a nominal winner is a good candidate. The level still falls: the nominal CMA-ES optimum loses 13% of its objective (0.653 to 0.567), and a robust optimum trained on 10 other worlds gains only 0.015 on average and 0.034 in the worst world, from single runs. Sim-to-sim in a stylised model with assumed ranges; no gripper was printed.

Which grasp scenes can no printable two-finger gripper hold, and what limits the ceiling?

research/codesign-ceiling · 7 tests

Which grasp scenes can no printable two-finger gripper hold? 10 of 140 (7%), all spheres with the hand more than about 2 mm above their centre height; lowering only the hand holds all 10, so the 0.85 plateau in codesign-diagnose is scene geometry, not friction or force (stylised, found after the fact, one intervention).

Abstract

Why every method in codesign-diagnose plateaued near 0.85. A MuJoCo sweep of 150 feasible gripper designs over 140 grasp scenes (cylinders, spheres, boxes of 150–1500 g) finds that 10 scenes (7%) are held by no design at any force, and all 10 are spheres where the simulated hand sits more than about 2 mm above the sphere’s centre height. Lowering only the hand height holds all 10 (hand at table level) or 8 of 10 (hand at sphere-centre height), so the ceiling comes from the scene geometry, not from friction, mass, force or finger strength; relaxing the finger strength limit lifts the best single design from 0.77 to 0.89 on fresh scenes. Stylised simulator; the 10-scene pattern was found after the fact and confirmed by one intervention.

Does diagnostic feedback (a failure summary, full rollout logs) help a design loop find better printable grippers than the score alone?

research/codesign-diagnose · 10 tests

Does a failure summary or full rollout logs help a gripper design loop beyond the score alone? No: three scripted readers end at 0.84 to 0.86 best test success, like random search, CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation (stylised, rule-based readers, not an LLM).

Abstract

Three scripted readers of a MuJoCo gripper co-design loop differ only in what they may read about each design: the score, a failure summary, or full rollout logs. At 40 designs and 8 seeds all end at 0.84–0.86 median best held-out grasp success, the same as random search, CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation (standard error about 0.05), and the richest reader proposes the worst designs on average. Negative and stylised: the readers are author-written rules, not an LLM.

How much of a best-of-40 gripper design's score is selection noise when every design gets its own trained grasp force?

research/codesign-forcefresh · 10 tests

Do agent-proposed gripper fingers beat random search, CMA-ES and Bayesian optimisation when each design gets its own trained grasp force? On 40 held-out scenes all methods' best seeds reach 0.93 to 0.95 and the ranking is within noise; on 100 fresh scenes the session agent's 0.95 falls to 0.81, level with the baseline median (stylised, preliminary, mostly negative).

Abstract

Companion to codesign-gripper with a different objective and a searched grasp force. Five parameters of a 3D-printable two-finger gripper (width, thickness, length, V-groove depth, TPU pad) are searched under a print-time cap; for each design a grasp force is tuned in MuJoCo and the design is scored on 40 held-out scenes (cylinders, spheres, boxes of 100–900 g). With 40 designs each, the best seeds of all three baselines reach 0.93–0.95 test success (medians 0.875, 0.887, 0.900 over 8 seeds) and one session-agent trajectory (n = 1, hand-written proposals) reaches 0.95 after 20 designs. On 100 fresh scenes every method’s best design drops 0.06–0.07 on average and the agent’s falls to 0.81, level with the baseline median: the task is nearly saturated and the ranking is within noise (standard error about 0.05). Stylised: assumed costs and strengths, rigid fingers, grid-plus-refine policy search rather than deep RL, nothing printed.
