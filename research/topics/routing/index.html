Markets as routers

Markets as routers: LMSR = Hedge, quadratic potential = sparsemax

research/market-routing · 11 tests

Cost-function markets as expert routers: LMSR = Hedge, quadratic potential = sparsemax; regret/loss bounds verified, continual-learning simulation.

Abstract

Cost-function markets as online learners for modular, continual-learning expert routing: verified equivalence and loss/regret bounds, and a switching-environment simulation where the quadratic market stays sparse and low-regret at liquidity levels where LMSR degrades. Small-scale; limitations stated.

Wagering mechanisms as routers for modular, continually learning experts

research/wagering-modular-experts · 13 tests

Self-financed wagering mechanisms as verifiable routers for modular, continually learning experts; shows plain WSWM locks in under recurring regimes and a fixed-share tax fixes it at the cost of subsidising free-riders.

Abstract

Weighted-score wagering (Lambert et al.) as a budget-balanced, replayable mixture-of-experts gate under recurring regime drift: plain wagering locks in, fixed-share wealth tax repairs it but subsidises free-riders.

How much should a router extremize?

research/expert-pooling · 6 tests

Pooling calibrated experts with shared training data: Bayes-optimal extremization a* = n/(1+(n−1)ρ) verified numerically, saturation at n_eff → 1/ρ, asymmetric robustness, and online learning of the exponent.

Abstract

Pooling calibrated experts with shared training data: the Bayes-optimal logit exponent a* = n/(1+(n−1)ρ) (matched to a numerical argmin), extra experts saturating at 1/ρ effective experts, naive independence pooling doing worse than chance at ρ=0.9, and an online learner that finds a*. Stylised.

What does late feedback cost a market router?

research/delayed-routing · 11 tests

Hedge/LMSR routing with delayed feedback: regret ≤ ln N/η + ηT/8 + ηD/2 (mean delay inflates horizon by 1+4d̄), 12× regret from ignoring delay against a reactive adversary, delay-tuned rate too cautious on oblivious data, LMSR subsidy ~20× at mean delay 5.

Abstract

Hedge/LMSR routing of modular experts when losses arrive after device-dependent delays: regret ≤ ln N/η + ηT/8 + ηD/2 with D the total outstanding rounds, so mean delay d̄ inflates the horizon by 1+4d̄; ignoring delay costs 12× regret against a reactive adversary at delay 100, the worst-case tuned rate is too cautious on oblivious data, an observable-count adaptive rate recovers most of it, and LMSR subsidy grows about 20× at mean delay 5. Stylised.

Is load balancing a congestion price?

research/balanced-routing · 10 tests

Capacity-constrained expert routing as a congestion-priced market: softmax with dual prices, verifier-computable duality-gap certificate from prices alone, sign-update balancing has a ≈2.5/√B sampling floor plus a step-size limit cycle, closed-form two-expert price of balance, Hoeffding load audit.

Abstract

Capacity-constrained expert routing is a softmax with a per-expert price, the Lagrange multiplier of its capacity, and the dual is smooth and concave. Any price vector gives a computable upper bound and, after repair toward the uniform assignment, a feasible lower bound, so a verifier certifies near-optimality from prices alone (gap below 10−4 at the optimum, always above the true suboptimality). Sign-update balancing has a sampling floor of about 2.5/√B in max relative overload that no step size removes, plus a step-proportional limit cycle on a fixed batch (22.6% at γ=0.2, 1.2% at 0.01). Two-expert Gaussian price of balance is closed form with price shift equal to the median gap; a Hoeffding audit of ln(2N/δ)/(2ε2) tokens covered 400 of 400 trials. Synthetic scores only. Stylised.

Is risk tolerance the learning rate of a wealth market?

research/kelly-markets · 11 tests

Fractional-Kelly wealth markets as expert routers: risk-tolerance-weighted price, ln(1/s)/λ regret bound, growth-optimal λ*=(π−p)/(q−p), manipulation cost V·χ² vs LMSR b·KL (7.5× stiffer at p=0.99), and a switching-truth trade-off where λ≈0.1–0.2 beats full Kelly 3–7×.

Abstract

When agents bet a fraction λ of wealth Kelly-style, the clearing price is the λ·wealth-weighted mean belief, total wealth is conserved, and λ=1 is exactly the Bayes mixture. Market log-loss exceeds expert i’s by at most ln(1/si)/λi on any sequence (loose on i.i.d. data), the growth-optimal fraction is (π−p)/(q−p), and moving the price against calibrated others costs Vχ²(p0‖p): equal to LMSR b·KL at b=2V for small shifts but 7.5× stiffer at p=0.99. On a truth that switches regimes, full Kelly is wrong about half of every segment (regret about 1630 at T=4000) while λ of 0.1–0.2 cuts it 3–7×. Binary events, static experts. Stylised.

How should a router pick k distinct experts?

research/topk-routing · 6 tests

Top-k MoE routing as a capped Hedge market: price min(1, λ w_i) with exact KL projection, regret vs the best fixed k-set held under k ln(N/k)/η+ηTk/2 in every run (41–58% of it), plain normalisation prices experts above 1 (282/300 rounds, loss understated 3.2×), and systematic sampling gives exactly k distinct experts with 8–14× lower variance.

Abstract

Sparse mixture-of-experts routing as a capped Hedge market: expert prices are min(1, λ wi) with a single shadow price λ and an exact KL projection. Regret against the best fixed k-set stayed within k ln(N/k)/η + ηTk/2 in every run (41–58% of it, not proved here); plain normalisation prices an expert above 1 in 282 of 300 rounds and understates loss 3.2×; systematic sampling realises exactly k distinct experts with 8–14× lower loss variance. Oblivious losses only.

How should a router treat modules that are usually asleep?

research/sleeping-ledger · 8 tests

A self-financed wealth ledger for specialist modules that sleep: total wealth conserved exactly, sleeping wealth frozen (one private ledger per regime), regret ≤ ln(1/π_i)/η + η·n_i in the module's *own* awake time, entry of a module with wealth ε costs incumbents ln(1/(1−ε))/η; matches the regime oracle (0.1004 vs 0.1000) where forced-to-report Hedge sits at 0.30 and fixed share is worse.

Abstract

If only awake modules report and each awake module’s wealth is multiplied by 1+η(ℓ̂−ℓi), the ledger is exactly budget balanced, sleeping wealth never moves (each regime keeps a private ledger), and a module’s regret against the market on its awake rounds is at most ln(1/πi)/η + ηni in its own awake time (the exponential variant pays ηT/8 and is not budget balanced). Admitting a module with wealth ε costs each incumbent ln(1/(1−ε))/η in its bound. With recurring regimes the ledger matches the per-round oracle (0.1004 vs 0.1000, regret about 2.7 whatever the number of specialists) while a forced-to-report Hedge sits at the generalist (0.30) and fixed share is worse (0.34–0.36). The bound is loose (measured regret 2.7 against a bound near 103); losses bounded, wake sets exogenous.

Can a module game a ledger by choosing when to sleep?

research/sleeping-incentives · 9 tests

When may a module choose to sleep? The ledger pays exactly leave-one-out savings ηW_A(1−p_i)S_i; sleeping *after* seeing losses gains wealth ηp_Bθ(1−θ)/2 (best 1/8) and takes the market in ~130 rounds with the router's loss unchanged; committed *before* serving it is a real saving; the deterring tax ηp_B/2 is a fixed share that halves honest specialists in 6 rounds.

Abstract

Yes, if it declares after seeing its loss. The wealth ledger pays each awake module exactly its leave-one-out saving, ηWA(1−pi)Si, but a module that sleeps whenever its own loss exceeds θ earns ηpBθ(1−θ)/2 per unit wealth against an equal-skill honest rival (best 1/8 at θ=1/2) and takes the market from a 0.1 share in about 130 rounds, while the router’s realised loss stays at 0.5. Declared before the router serves, the same wealth is a real saving that falls from 0.125 to 0.011 as the module’s signal about its own loss gets noisier. A sleeping tax that stops the exploit must be at least ηpB/2, a fixed-share rate that halves an honest specialist asleep 90% of the time in 6 rounds, so commitment, not taxation, is the fix. Two modules, uniform losses, threshold strategies; mean-field predictions 3–8% slow.

What does it cost to route between models with conditional markets, and what does a stake do to the answer?

research/decision-markets · 16 tests

Conditional markets that route between models: inverse-propensity Brier is proper even when the router reads the report, exploration costs exactly 1+(K−1)e^{ω²/2τ²} in payment variance (probit: infinite for τ ≤ ω), and a stake gap distorts the reported gap by a closed-form fixed point with second-order harm.

Abstract

Markets that forecast each model’s loss if chosen only ever see the chosen arm. An inverse-propensity Brier payment is exactly proper even when the router reads the report (without the weight a trader shades its forecast, or hides), and its price is exact: E[1/π] = 1+(K−1)eω2/2τ2, so payment variance blows up as the router cools (a probit router has infinite variance for τ ≤ ω; Monte Carlo at τ=0.3 sees 29 against the exact 195). Exploration regret is at most 0.2785τ. Equal owner stakes cancel in the decision, a stake gap shifts the reported gap by a closed-form fixed point, and the loss it causes is second order, ∝(ΔB/k)2. Gaussian, one-shot, stylised.

What share of the mass should a new module get?

research/module-entry · 6 tests

Admitting new modules to a Hedge/LMSR router: regret certificates obey a Kraft identity Σ e^{−c_i}=1, so uniform entry 1/(n+1) is the unique minimax rule; prior-optimal shares P_j/S_j cost H(P); the price of entry is exactly ln(1/π)/η independent of the quality gap.

Abstract

When a modular system adds an expert to a Hedge/LMSR router, the regret certificate to every expert is ci/η + ηT/8 with ci = ln(1/πi) + Σj>i ln(1/(1−πj)), and for any share sequence Σi e−ci = 1 (a Kraft identity). So uniform entry π = 1/(n+1) is the unique minimax rule (every ci = ln N, against 4.1–9.7 for constant shares at 4+12 modules), and against a prior on which module will be best the optimal shares are Pj/Sj with expected coefficient equal to the entropy of the prior. The price of entry is exactly ln(1/π)/η for a better arrival and ln(1/(1−π))/η for a worse one, independent of the quality gap; a recency prior cuts simulated regret from 3.11 to 2.70 nats. Known arrival rounds, fixed η. Pure-Python, 6 tests.
