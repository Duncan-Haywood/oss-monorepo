How far over its limit can a rate limiter be pushed in a true rolling window?

research/ratelimit-burst · 7 tests

How far over its limit can a rate limiter be pushed in a true rolling window? Adversarial search over fixed window, sliding log, sliding-window counter, token bucket and leaky bucket: only the sliding log holds the limit; the sliding counter admits up to about 1.5 L and the buckets nearly 2 L, and under Poisson overload at 1.5x load 39-47% of rolling windows exceed the limit for fixed window and buckets. Stylised.

Abstract

A stylised free-choice study of five limiters configured as L requests per window W, by adversarial search on an integer tick clock and a Poisson overload test. Only the sliding log never admits more than L in a rolling window. The sliding-window counter admits up to about 1.4–1.5L (1.40, 1.45, 1.48 for W = 20, 40, 100), and token and leaky buckets admit 1.90–1.98L; fixed window reaches 2L. At 1.5× offered Poisson load, 47% of rolling windows exceed L for fixed window, 39% for the buckets, 8.8% for the sliding counter and none for the sliding log. The searches give lower bounds, not proofs; one client, no distributed state.

What can one stubborn node do to gossip training?

research/gossip-consensus · 7 tests

Pairwise gossip for decentralised training: exact contraction (n−2)/(2(n−1))≈½ per matching round (~20 rounds at any n), noise floor (n−1)(n−2)s²/n, one stubborn node captures consensus at rate 1−1/(n(n−1)), trust-region clipping caps drift at τ/(n(n−1)) per step.

Abstract

Pairwise gossip averaging as a stand-in for all-reduce: disagreement contracts by exactly (n−2)/(2(n−1)) ≈ ½ per matching round (about 20 rounds at any n), the noise floor is (n−1)(n−2)s²/n (matched to 0.3%), a single stubborn node captures the honest mean at exact rate 1−1/(n(n−1)), and a trust-region clip turns capture into linear creep of at most τ/(n(n−1)) per step. Stylised.

What does infrequent averaging do to the answer?

research/local-sgd-bias · 8 tests

Local-SGD/DiLoCo-style averaging on heterogeneous quadratics: exact fixed point with saturating weights 1-(1-ηa_i)^H, non-monotone bias to the unweighted mean, exact a_i/w_i correction, step-count inflation moves the fixed point 0.50→0.91, wall-clock-optimal sync interval.

Abstract

Local-SGD / DiLoCo-style averaging of heterogeneous quadratic workers has a closed-form fixed point: a weighted mean of the workers' optima with weights 1−(1−ηai)H that saturate, so it is exactly unbiased at H=1, forgets curvature as H grows (converging to the unweighted mean), and is non-monotone in between. Weighting by ai/wi removes the bias exactly; if the server trusts claimed step counts, a low-curvature worker moves the fixed point from 0.50 to 0.91 by claiming 100 steps instead of 5, tying communication savings to verified work. Wall-clock-optimal H under a bias tolerance is 5.7× faster than H=1 at moderate comm cost, with a non-interval feasible set. Noise formulas match Monte Carlo. Stylised.

How often should preemptible workers checkpoint?

Checkpoint interval for preemptible decentralised training: exact Lambert-W optimum independent of restart cost, Young/Daly overshoot 5–25%, flat within 2×, synchronous efficiency wall (50% at n≈691), flaky workers cut throughput 5×.

Abstract

Exact checkpoint interval under Poisson churn: w* = (1+W0(−e−1−λC))/λ, independent of restart cost, with Young/Daly overshooting by 5–25%; intervals within 2× cost under 3.5%; a synchronous job on n workers restarts at rate nλ so efficiency falls to 50% near n≈700, and admitting flaky workers cut throughput five-fold. Stylised.

Should a pipeline replicate stages or skip them?

research/pipeline-replication · 11 tests

Replicate or skip? pipeline stages on unreliable workers: exact binomial drop law, replicas cannot beat the shared-domain floor (1−ρ)^L, a 4-stage skip budget halves workers for a 1e-3 drop rate (240 → 96), cost-optimal design shifts from skipping to replication as skips get costly.

Abstract

A microbatch crosses L stages held by workers that come and go. The drop probability is the exact binomial tail P(Bin(L,q)>s) with stage outage q = ρ + (1−ρ)(1−a)r, so replicas cannot lift success above the shared-domain floor (1−ρ)L: at L=48, ρ=10−3 no r reaches a 1% drop rate, but allowing one skipped stage makes r=3 enough. At a=0.9 a four-stage skip budget cuts the workers for a 10−3 drop rate from 240 to 96; under a per-skip quality loss κ the cost-optimal design is skip-heavy for cheap skips and moves to replication as κ grows (gain over no-skip 2.9× down to 1.08×). Independent outages, multiplicative skip penalty. Stylised.

How should a model be cut across devices of unequal speed?

research/hetero-pipeline · 8 tests

Cutting a model across heterogeneous devices: exact greedy bottleneck partition (matches DP, with memory caps), W/V ≤ T* < W/V + m·w_max/V, speed-blind splitting costs exactly v̄/v_min (2.4× at σ=0.5), device order is the residual decision (sorts no better than arbitrary, swap search within 1.1%), memory inverse to speed costs 1.7×.

Abstract

For a fixed pipeline order, greedy filling is an exact test of a target stage time (agreeing with an independent dynamic program, with and without memory caps), and every order satisfies W/V ≤ T* < W/V + m wmax/V. A speed-blind equal split costs exactly v̄/vmin times the lower bound (2.40× at log-speed spread 0.5, 8.25× at 1.0) while speed-aware cutting stays within 1.4% at 240 layers. What is left is the device order: at 14 layers and 6 devices the worst order is 1.34× the best, fast-first and slow-first sorts do no better than an arbitrary order (all about 1.16× optimal), and pairwise-swap search gets within 1.1%; the penalty fades to 1.03× at 224 layers. Memory that shrinks as speed grows inflates the bottleneck 1.7×. No communication cost, static speeds.

What does it cost to skip the slowest workers?

research/straggler-backup · 10 tests

Waiting for the fastest k of n workers: exact order-statistic round times (Pareto mean finite iff n−k+1 > 1/α), exact SGD time-to-accuracy with a minimum k and an interior optimum (34× over wait-for-all at s=0, 1.12× at s=32), joint step tuning, and the exact selection bias of the speed filter with Horvitz–Thompson correction.

Abstract

Waiting for the fastest k of n workers has exact round time s + (Hn−Hn−k)/μ for exponential tails, and for Pareto tails the mean is finite iff n−k+1 > 1/α, so with α ≤ 1 waiting for everyone has infinite expected round time. For SGD on a quadratic the time to accuracy has a minimum k and an interior optimum (34× faster than waiting for all at zero fixed cost, 1.12× at large fixed cost). If speed correlates with data, the fastest-k average is biased by Σ(πi/k−1/n)θi; Horvitz–Thompson reweighting removes it but only pays when the bias is large.

Does a bigger swarm learn more from shared rollouts?

research/rollout-commons · 7 tests

Rollout sharing in swarm RL as a public-goods game: Nash gives every agent a lone agent's learning level whatever N (total effort caps at (a/c−1)/θ) while efficient welfare is 4.5× at N=256; at θ=1 one agent generates; a Pigouvian matching subsidy (fraction θ(N−1)/(1+θ(N−1)) of cost) implements the optimum; junk beats honesty iff c−ε > p(τ+F).

Abstract

Not without incentives. With diminishing returns and a peer-rollout discount θ, symmetric Nash play gives every agent exactly the learning level of a lone agent whatever the swarm size (total effort caps at (a/c−1)/θ), while the efficient level grows linearly in N: welfare per agent is 4.5× higher at N=256. At θ=1 a single agent generates everything. A Pigouvian matching subsidy covering θ(N−1)/(1+θ(N−1)) of cost implements the optimum exactly, and junk rollouts beat honesty iff c−ε > p(τ+F).

How stale can a shared rollout be before it is worthless?

research/stale-rollouts · 7 tests

Stale off-policy rollouts in swarm RL: exact importance-weight moments (E w²=e^{δ²}, ESS fraction e^{−δ²}), closed-form truncation bias/variance verified by simulation, best cap cuts shift-estimation MSE 1.9× at δ=1 and ~750× at δ=3, and the staleness window √ln(1/ρ)/drift.

Abstract

With Gaussian policies whose mean has moved by δ standard deviations, the importance weight has exact moments E w = 1 and E w² = eδ², so the effective-sample fraction is e−δ² (0.37 at δ=1) and a learner needing fraction ρ accepts a lag of √ln(1/ρ)/drift policy versions. Truncating weights has closed-form lost mass, bias and variance (matched by simulation); the best cap cuts the mean-shift estimator’s MSE 1.9× at δ=1, 12.8× at δ=2 and about 750× at δ=3, and finite-sample ESS is optimistic once weights are heavy-tailed. Equal-variance Gaussians, one statistic.

How fast does a shared model forget a task, and does splitting it into modules help?

research/forgetting-law · 9 tests

Exact catastrophic-forgetting law for sequential training on random low-rank tasks: (r/d)(1−r/d)(ρᵏ−λᵏ) rises then vanishes, uniform module routing dilutes it but sharing wins on seen-plus-fresh loss, conflicting tasks leave a floor 2rτ²/d.

Abstract

Training to convergence on random rank-r tasks in Rd is a projection, and the expected loss on a task learned k tasks ago is exactly (r/d)(1−r/d)(ρk−λk): zero when learned, a peak (0.044 at d=12, r=3, lag 2.5) at a lag near 0.7d/r, then decay because the tasks agree; matched by simulation to the third decimal. Routing tasks to m modules dilutes both rates by m and cuts short-horizon forgetting (5.9× at T=4, m=8) but a single shared model minimises seen-plus-fresh loss at every horizon tried, and two modules forget more than one at T=12. With conflicting tasks forgetting has a floor 2rτ²/d that only task-specific modules remove. Stylised: linear regression, exact convergence, isotropic tasks.

How should independently trained modules be merged, and how much does parallel training buy?

research/merge-law · 7 tests

Merging independently trained modules on random low-rank tasks: exact residual 1−2amp+a²(mp+m(m−1)p²), optimal scale 1/(1+(m−1)p) (averaging stalls at (1−p)², unscaled sum diverges), harmonic 1/m decay, parallel speedup only logarithmic (8.9× at m=16, d/r=8).

Abstract

Averaging m modules trained to convergence on random rank-r tasks has an exact residual error 1−2amp+a2(mp+m(m−1)p2) with p=r/d, needing only isotropy. The optimal scale is 1/(1+(m−1)p) and leaves (1−p)/(1+(m−1)p): plain averaging stalls at (1−p)2 (0.78 vs 0.47 at m=8, d/r=8) and unscaled task arithmetic diverges (56 at m=64). The decay is harmonic, not geometric, so m parallel workers give a wall-clock speedup that grows only logarithmically (8.9× at m=16, 17× at m=64) and halves in efficiency near m≈d/r×2.5. Seen and fresh losses are closed forms matched by simulation. Stylised: linear regression, exact convergence, consistent isotropic tasks.

When should a continual learner replay an old task, and does a fixed schedule beat random replay?

research/replay-law · 8 tests

Exact expected forgetting under replay schedules in sequential training on random low-rank tasks: 2×2 matrix recursion matches simulation, one replay is best at the forgetting-peak lag yet removes only 25–42%, a fixed period beats random replay only below ~1.2–3 peak lags, halving forgetting needs rate 0.25 (d=12, r=3) to 0.015 (d=128, r=1).

Abstract

Replaying a task alongside each fresh one is a joint projection, so the expected loss on a tracked task is an exact product of 2×2 matrices for any schedule (matched to simulation at every step; recovers the no-replay law). One replay is best at the forgetting-peak lag — 5 at d=32, r=4; 89 at d=128, r=1 — yet removes only 25–42% of total forgetting (replaying at step 1 barely helps at small r/d). A period longer than the peak lag never touches the peak, and a fixed period beats random replay at the same rate only below roughly 1.2–3 peak lags (e.g. 1/8 at d=32, r=4, but random wins at 1/12). Halving forgetting needs a rate 0.25 at d=12, r=3 and 0.015 at d=128, r=1; tasks with 2r≥d are fully recovered by one replay. Stylised: realizable linear regression, exact convergence, one tracked task.

How much of an old task must a continual learner store, and is it better to replay a little often or a lot rarely?

research/replay-memory · 8 tests

Partial replay when only m of an old task's r examples are stored: exact 2×2 law interpolating no replay and full replay, best-step saving nearly linear in m/r, and at a fixed example budget a few large replays beat many small ones by only 0.1–6 points.

Abstract

If only m of an old task’s r examples are stored, replaying them with a fresh task is “project out the stored subspace, then take a plain step in dimension d−m”. That gives an exact 2×2 mean-state matrix interpolating no replay (m=0) and full replay (m=r, matched to replay-law to 12 digits), agreeing with simulation at every step. One replay at its best step recovers a fraction of the full saving that is nearly linear in m/r (0.23, 0.47, 0.72 at ¼, ½, ¾ for d=12, r=4; within 0.004 of m/r at d=128, r=8). At a fixed rate, half the memory buys 57–65% of the saving. At a fixed budget of examples per step, one large replay beats many small ones, but only by 0.1–2 points of total forgetting in wide models and up to 6 in a tight one (d=12, r=4), so buffer size matters more than schedule. Stylised: realizable linear regression, exact convergence, one tracked task.

When continual-learning tasks conflict, how much does a task-aware router of modules actually buy?

research/router-impurity · 8 tests

Task-aware routing when continual-learning tasks conflict: the long-run floor is (2r/d)[τ_w²+τ_b²(1−Purity)] with purity = 1 − the router's Gini impurity, random modules buy nothing, a perfect router removes (m−1)/(K−1) of the between-cluster floor, and a 10% routing error gives back 36%.

Abstract

With cluster-structured conflicts, the long-run loss on an old task is exactly (2r/d)[τw² + τb²(1 − Purity)], where Purity is one minus the router’s expected Gini impurity (matched to simulation within 0.3–2.3% across six routers). Random routing to any number of modules leaves the whole floor; a perfect router onto m modules removes exactly (m−1)/(K−1) of the between-cluster floor, so half needs 5 of 8 clusters’ worth of modules; and a routing error q costs 2q of impurity at first order (10% error returns 36% of the shared-model floor at K=8, 25% returns 59%). Purity is auditable with an unbiased pair-collision estimator. Stylised: realizable linear regression, exact convergence, Gaussian cluster centres, hard routing.

How should the outer optimiser and sync interval be set when workers run H local steps per round?

research/outer-momentum · 9 tests

DiLoCo-style outer momentum: H inner steps precondition the outer quadratic (κ_H ≈ κ/H), exact per-mode rates, optimal momentum falls 0.88 → 0.01 with H (fixed 0.7/0.9 Nesterov needs 4–8× more rounds), and the wallclock-optimal sync interval is H* ≈ C (sync cost in inner steps), worth ≈ √C/2.

Abstract

On a quadratic, H inner steps make the outer optimiser see curvatures 1 − (1 − ηa)H, which saturate at 1: the outer condition number shrinks from κ to about κ/H (1000 → 16.1 at H=64). Per-round rates are exact 2×2 spectral radii (matching literal simulation), the tuned heavy-ball outer step puts every mode at exactly √β*, and the best momentum falls from 0.88 at H=1 to 0.01 at H=1024, so a fixed Nesterov 0.7/0.9 needs 4–8× more rounds. With a sync costing C inner steps, the wallclock-optimal interval is H* ≈ C, worth about √C/2 over syncing every step (16× at C=1000), while plain averaging has no interior optimum. Stylised: noiseless quadratic with a shared Hessian.

How much gradient noise survives in DiLoCo-style local SGD, and how should the outer step, momentum, worker count and sync interval be set against it?

research/noisy-local-sgd · 9 tests

Noise floor of DiLoCo-style local SGD: exact stationary loss αV(1+β)/((1−β)s(2(1+β)−αs)), independent of H at α=1, momentum free at equal effective step, near-linear speedup in M, and a noise-limited sync interval that grows as the floor target falls.

Abstract

On a quadratic with M workers, H noisy inner steps and a heavy-ball outer step, the stationary variance per mode is exactly αV(1+β)/((1−β)s(2(1+β)−αs)) with V the averaged inner noise and s = 1 − (1 − ηa)H, matching literal simulation to 0.2%. At α = 1 the floor is independent of H; for small α more local steps shrink it by less than a factor of 2. Momentum costs no extra floor at equal effective step (and lowers it at large steps), workers give near-linear speedup (331× at M = 256, α still below 1), and unlike the noise-free case the best sync interval grows as the noise-floor target falls (H* = 5, 11, 38 at C = 10), up to 41× faster than syncing every step. Stylised: quadratic, Gaussian noise, shared Hessian, fixed α.

What happens to local SGD when workers drop in and out?

research/partial-participation · 11 tests

Local SGD when workers drop in and out: exact floors for participant averaging (E[1/K|K≥1]) and expected-count normalisation (stable iff αsc<2), at matched speed the fixed normaliser is quieter by E[K]E[1/K] at small steps, and reliable workers bias the fixed point unless inverse-propensity weighted.

Abstract

Each of N workers joins a round with probability p. Averaging the participants has exact stationary variance αVwm/(s(2−αs)) with m = E[1/K | K≥1]; dividing by the expected count instead is stable only for αs < 2/c, c = 1+(1−p)/(Np), and cannot contract faster than 1−1/c per round (0.22 at N=32, p=0.1). At matched speed the fixed normaliser is quieter at small steps by E[K|K≥1]·E[1/K|K≥1] (1.34×), and averaging wins only at fast rates. Reliable workers bias the fixed point (0.467 instead of 1.000 in our example) unless displacements are inverse-propensity weighted. Matched to simulation within 0.5%. Stylised.

What does compressing the pseudo-gradient cost local SGD, and is it better than syncing less often?

research/compressed-sync · 9 tests

Local SGD with compressed displacements: exact floor (1+ω) and stability limit αs(1+ω/N)<2 for rand-k (more workers restore the step, not the floor), closed form for norm-scaled quantisation, and at equal bytes syncing less often beats compressing.

Abstract

Unbiased compression of each worker's displacement (rand-k, relative variance ω) gives an exact stationary variance αVw(1+ω)/(N s((2−αs)−αsω/N)) and shrinks the stable outer step to 2/(s(1+ω/N)): more workers restore the step (0.47 → 2.44 for ω=99, N=16 → 256) but never the floor, which stays (1+ω)×. Norm-scaled (QSGD-style) quantisation has a closed form through one scalar T = E|d|2; at equal error energy its loss equals coordinate-wise at H=1 and is about 10% worse at H=16, with the stiff mode's variance doubling. For equal bytes, syncing 1/ρ times less often leaves the floor unchanged at α=1 while keeping a fraction ρ multiplies it by 1/ρ. Matched to simulation within 2%. Stylised: quadratic, shared curvature (no client drift), unbiased compressors only.

How much outer step does overlapping communication with compute cost, and how many rounds should be in flight?

research/delayed-outer · 11 tests

Overlapping communication in local SGD with τ-round-late outer updates: exact stable step 2 sin(π/(4τ+2)) (shrinks ~1/τ), double-root rate τ/(τ+1), exact noise floor matched to simulation, free at small steps, wall-clock-optimal delay ⌈L/T_c⌉ (1.9–7.5× faster than no overlap).

Abstract

Applying the averaged displacement τ rounds late obeys zτ+1 − zτ + c = 0 with c = αs, so the stable step is exactly c < 2 sin(π/(4τ+2)) ≈ π/(2τ+1): 2, 1, 0.62, 0.45 for τ = 0…3, shrinking like 1/τ. The fastest single-mode rate is the double root τ/(τ+1), and the stationary noise floor is an exact Yule–Walker solve (closed form (1+c)/((1−c)c(2+c)) at τ = 1) that matches literal delayed-DiLoCo simulation to 0.3%. Delay is free at small steps (+1% at α = 10−3, τ = 4) but multiplies the floor by 3.9× at c = 0.8. At an equal floor each delay round costs only about 6% more rounds, so the wall-clock-optimal delay is exactly ⌈latency / compute time⌉ (1.9–7.5× faster than no overlap). Stylised: constant delay, identical workers, quadratics, no momentum.

Does error feedback rescue the noise floor that compressed local SGD loses?

research/error-feedback-sync · 9 tests

Error feedback in compressed local SGD: exact stability limit 2(2−ρ)Nρ/(Nρ²+4(1−ρ)) and closed-form floor that returns to the uncompressed one at small steps (unbiased compression pays 1/ρ), dropping is biased to the send-rate-weighted mean and the residual removes it at a variance price.

Abstract

Workers keep the unsent part of their displacement and add it to the next message. On a quadratic with random sparsification the second moments close exactly: stability requires αs < 2(2−ρ)Nρ/(Nρ2+4(1−ρ)) — half the unbiased limit for a single worker, above the uncompressed limit for large cohorts — and the floor has a closed form that returns to the uncompressed one at small steps (1.08× at ρ=¼ where unbiased compression pays 4×) while the mean keeps its speed. What the residual buys is unbiasedness: dropping converges to the send-rate-weighted mean, feedback to the true mean, but at a variance that only pays off for very small steps. Top-k is simulation only; stylised quadratics.

How should a server weight workers that ran different numbers of inner steps?

research/heterogeneous-steps · 9 tests

Weighting workers that ran different numbers of inner steps: exact floor α²Σw²V/(r(2−r)), worker information I∞·tanh(λH/2) saturates, optimal weights span ≤2× so equal weights lose ≤9/8 (measured ≤1.09) while step-proportional weights lose up to 1.76× per mode and 10.5× on three modes.

Abstract

With worker i running Hi steps, weighted averaging has exact floor α2Σwi2Vi/(r(2−r)), r = αΣwisi. One worker's information is I∞tanh(λH/2): H=8 carries 20% of the ceiling and H=64 93%, so a fast device is worth far less than its step count. Information-optimal weights span at most 2×, hence equal weights lose at most 9/8 (measured ≤1.09), whereas step-proportional (FedAvg-style) weights lose up to 1.76× per mode and 10.5× on three modes when one worker in eight is 256× faster. Matched to simulation within 1%. Stylised: quadratic, shared optimum (no client drift), fixed Hi.

What does a median or trimmed-mean outer step cost local SGD, and what does it buy against Byzantine workers?

research/robust-outer · 11 tests

Median and trimmed-mean outer aggregation in local SGD: exact linear loop with floor and bias split, π/2 variance price with no attackers, bounded worst-case bias Φ(z)=1/(2(1−ε)) that more workers cannot average away but a longer inner loop shrinks.

Abstract

Because the median and trimmed mean commute with shifts, the outer loop stays exactly linear: floor αVwv/(s(2−αs)) plus a bias Vwm2/s2, with the same stability limit as averaging. With no attackers the median costs N·Var = 1.35 to 1.56 (→ π/2). With a fraction ε of attackers its bias is bounded (Φ(z) = 1/(2(1−ε)): 0.18 noise-sigmas at ε = 1/8) and beats averaging once the attack offset exceeds about 1.4 sigmas; more workers shrink only the variance part (total loss 0.103 → 0.102 from N=8 to 128), while a longer inner loop shrinks the bias (39× from H=1 to 256). Trimming exactly the attacker count is worse than the median. Matched to simulation within 1.3%. Stylised: quadratic, Gaussian noise, shared curvature, constant-offset attackers.
