How small can a keyword-spotting accelerator get, and what does each bit of precision cost?

research/accel-kws · 13 tests

Keyword-spotting accelerator: quantised MLP on Speech Commands, serial-MAC RTL bit-exact against an integer reference, accuracy vs cycles, cells and modelled energy over width and weight bits (stylised).

Abstract

A one-hidden-layer MLP on pooled MFCC features of Speech Commands v0.02 (10 keywords plus unknown), quantised to 2–8 bit weights, mapped to a serial-MAC RTL engine that matches an integer NumPy reference bit for bit on all 40 swept configurations. Test accuracy saturates near 75% (0.752 at 128 hidden units, 4 bits); 4-bit weights are within 0.4 points of 8-bit at 64 and 128 units, 2 bits collapses at small width, and a 64-unit 4-bit design (0.722) uses about 31% of the best design’s modelled energy. Modelled classifier energy is 0.0045–0.19 µW at 4 inferences per second, far below the 1.8 mW microphone floor in ambient-wearable. Stylised: assumed 45 nm energy constants not re-checked, generic-gate area without memories, no front end, a third-party dataset mirror, and a weak feature set.

Does a keyword-spotting accelerator fit in a Tiny Tapeout tile, and how accurate can it be?

research/accel-ttfit · 9 tests

A streaming keyword-spotting engine with no weight memory, exact against an integer reference and synthesised to SKY130 on 32 geometries (6,300 to 24,500 um2); 16 hidden units at 8-bit weights is the most accurate (0.616) that fits one assumed 1x1 Tiny Tapeout tile at 70% utilisation, 64 units at 4 bits (0.738) needs two. Stylised: synthesis only.

Abstract

A streaming serial-MAC engine with no weight memory (the host feeds one operand pair per clock over the 16 input pins; hidden activations sit in a rotating shift register), bit- and cycle-exact against an integer reference in Icarus on 32 geometries, synthesised to SKY130 HD with Yosys. Area is 6,300–24,500 µm2 (661–2,090 cells) for 8–64 hidden units and 2–8 bit weights; flops are 33–53% of it, and 8-bit weights cost about 3,000 µm2 more than 4-bit. Against an assumed 161×112 µm 1x1 tile (18,032 µm2), the best model by accel-kws test accuracy that fits one tile at 70% utilisation is 16 hidden units, 8-bit weights, 120 inputs (0.616); two tiles take 64 units at 4 bits (0.738), a 1.7× area step for 12 points. Stylised: synthesis only (no placement, routing or timing), random weights in the RTL check, accuracies carried over from accel-kws models that were not re-run through this engine, and the tile area is from a search-result snippet of a Tiny Tapeout guide, not the tile table.

Under a latency deadline, how much engine area does picking the most accurate model first cost, compared with choosing model and lane count together?

research/accel-codesign · 6 tests

Model-first vs joint choice of model and MAC lane count under a latency deadline, on the 40 measured accel-kws models; accepting 2 validation points less cuts engine area 31–42% (stylised, table-based, cycles for more than one lane analytic).

Abstract

Exhaustive joint search over the 40 measured accel-kws keyword-spotting models and six MAC lane counts (240 designs), against a model-first flow, at deadlines of 2,000–20,000 cycles. The most accurate model (validation 0.775, test 0.752) needs 16 lanes at 2,000 cycles and 7,667 generic cells; accepting 2 validation points less (test 0.738) cuts area by 31–42%, and 3 points by 35–56%. Stylised and only partly the milestone: models are a fixed measured table (the dataset was unreachable), cycles for more than one lane are analytic and not RTL-simulated, memory bandwidth is not counted, area is generic gates, and no NSGA-II or Bayesian search was run (the space is small enough to enumerate).

Does NSGA-II find a keyword-spotting accelerator's Pareto front faster than random search at equal budgets?

research/accel-nsga · 5 tests

NSGA-II against random search at equal budgets on a 3,840-design keyword-spotting accelerator space with exact ground truth; NSGA-II finds 54 to 89% of the exact front at 20.8% of the space against 19 to 21% for random search, though hypervolume is 0.97 to 1.00 for both. Stylised.

Abstract

NSGA-II against random sampling, 30 seeds each, on a 3,840-design space (40 measured accel-kws models × 6 lane counts × 4 memory-bank counts × 4 supply levels) under a latency deadline, scored against the exact front from exhaustive search. At 20.8% of the space NSGA-II finds 54% and 89% of the exact front's designs (50 and 200 µs deadlines) against 19% and 21% for random search, yet hypervolume is 0.97–1.00 for both, so random search is already good enough if only a near-front is needed. Stylised: bank and supply models are assumptions and no multi-lane RTL was simulated.

Was accel-nsga's NSGA-II win a tuning accident? Does population size, mutation rate or crossover change how much of the exact front it finds?

research/accel-nsgatune · 4 tests

Sweeps NSGA-II's population, mutation rate and crossover on the accel-nsga space; mutation 0.02 to 0.1 finds about 0.38 and 0.71 to 0.77 of the exact front at 400 designs, against 0.28 and 0.59 at accel-nsga's setting and 0.13 and 0.12 for random search (stylised).

Abstract

A sweep of NSGA-II population size (10–80), per-gene mutation rate (0.02–0.5) and crossover on the 3,840-design keyword-spotting accelerator space of accel-nsga, at 400 evaluated designs, 30 seeds, scored against the exact front. The earlier setting (population 20, mutation 0.25) was under-tuned: mutation 0.02–0.1 finds about 0.38 of the front at 50 µs (was 0.28) and 0.71–0.77 at 200 µs (was 0.59), against 0.13 and 0.12 for random search. Population 80 with mutation 0.5 is no better than random. Removing crossover changes little. Stylised: one budget, four genes, tuned on the score it is reported with.

Which dataflow should a small keyword-spotting accelerator use, and does the answer survive allowing an input buffer?

research/accel-dataflow · 4 tests

Weight-, output-, input-stationary and reduction-tree dataflows of a P-lane MAC array on the 40 accel-kws models, from a cycle-by-cycle schedule simulator that matches closed forms exactly and the P = 1 RTL cycles; output-stationary wins on SRAM energy at batch 4 until an input buffer is allowed, then input-mapped flows win at 32 lanes (stylised, assumed energy constants, no RTL for more than one lane).

Abstract

Four dataflows (output-, weight-, input-stationary and reduction-tree) of a P-lane MAC array, as schedules walked cycle by cycle on the 40 accel-kws models, with SRAM-access counts that match closed forms exactly and, at P = 1, the measured RTL cycles of accel-kws. At batch 1 output-stationary is best (input-stationary is a median 1.2× to 4.1× worse, falling with lanes); at batch 4 it wins every model while weight-stationary is a median 4.5–9.0× worse through partial-sum traffic. Allowing an activation buffer flips the batch-4 winner to the input-mapped flows at 32 lanes (1.37× better), so the ranking rests on a modelling choice. Stylised: assumed energy constants, a one-cycle register model, no RTL beyond one lane, Timeloop not tried.

How large must an activation buffer be before the best dataflow for a small keyword-spotting accelerator changes?

research/accel-dataflowbuf · 4 tests

How large a bounded LRU activation buffer must be before output-stationary stops being the best dataflow for the 40 accel-kws models at batch 4: 32, 64 and 128 words at 8, 16 and 32 lanes (for 10 to 30 of 40 models, input-stationary wins), and not at all at batch 1; buffer-read energy is assumed and no area is charged (stylised).

Abstract

A bounded LRU activation buffer of K 8-bit words added to the four accel-dataflow schedules and swept over the 40 accel-kws models at 8, 16 and 32 lanes. At batch 1 output-stationary wins every model at every K. At batch 4 input-stationary first ties or beats it at K = 32, 64 and 128 words (8, 16 and 32 lanes; 10 of 40 models at 8 and 16 lanes, 30 at 32), and no flip occurs for the 2-bit models. If a buffer read costs half an SRAM read instead of a tenth, the flips mostly disappear. Buffer-read energy is assumed and buffer area is not charged. Stylised.

Does a loop-nest cycle model of a P-lane MAC array match RTL at more than one lane, tails included?

P-lane output-stationary and reduction-tree matrix-vector engines in Verilog, bit-exact against NumPy at 1 to 32 lanes, with RTL cycles equal to accel-dataflow's schedule model plus a constant 2 or 3 at every point, tails included (stylised, generic-gate area, batch 1, no memories).

Abstract

Output-stationary and reduction-tree matrix-vector engines written in Verilog for the two accel-kws layer shapes (64×70 and 11×64), simulated in Icarus Verilog at 1 to 32 lanes. All 36 sweep points are bit-exact against NumPy, and RTL cycles equal the accel-dataflow schedule model plus a constant 2 (3 with a registered adder tree), including ragged last tiles: output-stationary needs 66 cycles against 46 for the reduction tree on the 11-output layer at 16 lanes, and 142 against 194 on the 70-input layer at 32 lanes. Yosys generic-gate area grows faster for the output-stationary engine (11,799 against 8,216 cells at 32 lanes), partly because of its registered output vector. Stylised: batch 1, generic gates not a library, no memories, no timing.

Does the weight-stationary advantage in the dataflow model survive RTL at batch above 1, and what does it cost in gates?

research/accel-planebatch · 5 tests

Output- and weight-stationary batched RTL engines at batch 1 to 16: bit-exact, cycles and operand-fetch counts equal accel-dataflow's schedule model, and weight-stationary's saved weight fetches cost up to 4x the generic gates (stylised, P = 8, no memories).

Abstract

One parameterised Verilog engine implements output- and weight-stationary batched matrix products (P = 8 lanes) for the two accel-kws layer shapes, simulated in Icarus Verilog at batch 1 to 16. All 20 points are bit-exact against NumPy, RTL cycles equal the accel-dataflow schedule model plus a constant 2, and RTL weight and activation fetch counts match the model exactly. Weight-stationary fetches the 4,480 weights of the 64×70 layer once instead of B times at identical cycles, but keeping its partial sums in registers grows the design from 3,024 to 12,284 generic cells (about 192 flops per extra batch element) against a flat ~3,000 for output-stationary. The model's ‘weight-stationary is worst’ ranking came from partial-sum SRAM traffic that this RTL trades for area. Stylised; generic gates, no memories, no energy figures.

How cheap must idle flops be, relative to a weight fetch, for register-file weight-stationary to beat output-stationary?

research/accel-fetchprice · 6 tests

Breakeven price ratios from accel-planebatch's RTL counts: register-file weight-stationary beats output-stationary only if an idle flop costs under ~1% of a weight fetch per cycle at batch 4 (falling ~1/B); no energy constant measured (stylised).

Abstract

Breakeven price ratios solved from the RTL-measured counts of accel-planebatch (P = 8, batch 1 to 16, two accel-kws layers). Register-file weight-stationary beats output-stationary on energy only if an extra flop costs under about 1% of a weight fetch per cycle at B = 4 (2.6×10−3 at B = 16), a bar that falls roughly as 1/B unless idle flops are clock-gated. Weight-stationary with partial sums in SRAM wins only if a 24-bit partial-sum access costs under 0.25 to 0.48 of a weight fetch, which looks implausible. Stylised: no energy constant is measured or sourced, so which side real technology falls on is left open.

At SKY130's own energy prices, does register-file weight-stationary scheduling beat output-stationary?

research/accel-pdkprice · 12 tests

Places accel-fetchprice's breakevens against SKY130 liberty energies (idle flop 46 fJ per cycle; OpenRAM macro 10 to 90 pJ per access): with weights packed into SRAM words register-file weight-stationary loses to output-stationary at nearly every batch; partial sums in SRAM lose everywhere. Library values, not measurements.

Abstract

Takes the breakeven price ratios of accel-fetchprice and prices them with the SKY130 HD liberty file (an idle dfxtp_1 flop costs 46 fJ per cycle, almost all clock-pin power) and the four OpenRAM SRAM macros shipped with OpenROAD-flow-scripts (10 to 90 pJ per access). If one 4-bit weight is fetched per access, weight-stationary wins at most batches; if weights are packed into the SRAM word (0.8 to 2.9 pJ per weight) output-stationary wins at nearly every batch, and weight-stationary with partial sums in SRAM loses in every case since a word holds fewer 24-bit sums than 4-bit weights. Library characterisation values, not measurements; the per-edge versus per-cycle reading of the OpenRAM figure is unresolved.

Does clock-gating the extra accumulators make register-file weight-stationary scheduling beat output-stationary?

research/accel-gatedprice · 9 tests

Prices clock-gated register-file weight-stationary scheduling from SKY130 and GF180 liberty files: an idle clock gate costs 0.86 of an idle flop (SKY130; 0.99 to 1.02 in GF180), so one gate per 24-bit accumulator cuts the idle cost about 28-fold and flips register-file WS from losing to winning against packed SRAM weight fetches in 35 of 64 cells, in all but one cell at 24 flops per gate. Library values, not measurements.

Abstract

Follows up accel-pdkprice, where idle flops made weight-stationary (WS) lose to output-stationary (OS) once weights are packed into SRAM words. Reads the idle clock energy of a flop and of an integrated clock gate (ICG) from the SKY130 HD and GF180MCU liberty files: an idle SKY130 dlclkp_1 costs 0.86 of an idle dfxtp_1 (39.7 against 46.4 fJ per cycle), and GF180 gates cost 0.99 to 1.02 of its flops at 3.3 and 5 V in both track heights. One gate shared by G accumulator flops therefore cuts the idle cost about G-fold. With one gate per 24-bit accumulator, WS needs a weight fetch of only 0.08 to 0.9 pJ (ungated: 2.2 to 26 pJ) and beats OS against every packed SKY130 macro in all 64 macro, layer and batch cells but one (an 80-bit macro on the 11×64 layer at B = 16 needs 27 flops per gate). Library characterisation values, a gate-per-group model with the enable logic, area and the register-file read mux unpriced; no RTL built or synthesised.
Stylised.

Does the clock-gated weight-stationary engine hold up once it is written in RTL and synthesised?

research/accel-gatedrtl · 6 tests

The clock-gated register-file weight-stationary engine in RTL and SKY130 synthesis: bit-exact, same accumulator writes as output-stationary, clock-pin energy 1.0 to 1.07x OS with a gate per set, but area only 7 to 18 % below ungated WS and 1.2 to 5.3x OS (stylised).

Abstract

Writes the register-file weight-stationary (WS) engine of accel-planebatch with clock gates (one per 24-bit accumulator, or one per set of eight) and synthesises it to SKY130 HD with Yosys. All 32 points are bit-exact against NumPy and write exactly as many accumulator bits as the output-stationary engine, confirming the assumption of accel-gatedprice. A gate per set brings the accumulator clock-pin energy to 1.00–1.07× output-stationary (ungated WS: B×), but area falls only 7–18 % against ungated WS, and gated WS is still 1.2× to 5.3× the output-stationary area at batch 2 to 16 because the extra accumulator sets are flops either way. Stylised: synthesis only, no place and route, clock tree or timing; energy is a liberty-based clock-pin model.

Does the gated weight-stationary engine's verdict depend on how many lanes it has?

research/accel-gatedlanes · 3 tests

The gated weight-stationary engine's lane count swept from 2 to 16 (32 ran out of synthesis memory): bit-exact, clock-pin energy 1.01 to 1.07x OS with a gate per set at every P, area 1.6 to 1.9x OS at B = 4 and flat from P = 4, so the verdict depends on the batch, not the lanes (stylised).

Abstract

Sweeps the lane count P (2 to 16, with a failed attempt at 32) of the four engine modes from accel-gatedrtl on the 64×70 keyword-spotting layer, in RTL simulation and Yosys synthesis to SKY130 HD. All 29 points are bit-exact and write the same accumulator bits in every mode. Accumulator clock-pin energy stays at B× OS for ungated WS and at 1.01 to 1.07× with a gate per set, independent of P; gated WS stays 1.6 to 1.9× OS area at B = 4 (2.9 to 3.0× at B = 8), flat from P = 4. So the earlier conclusion (cheap to clock, not small) is a property of the batch, not the lane count. Stylised: synthesis only, clock-pin energy only; Yosys exceeded 7 GB at P = 32, so wider engines are not covered.

Why did the gated weight-stationary engine need so much synthesis memory, and does fixing it change its area verdict?

research/accel-gatedmux · 4 tests

Why the gated weight-stationary engine needed so much synthesis memory: one variable-index read of the accumulator bus; an AND-OR read mux gives bit-exact, same-cycle engines 17 to 28 % smaller, gated WS 1.5x OS area at B = 4 and 2.2x at B = 8 (was 1.6 to 1.9x and 2.9 to 3.0x), and lets P = 64 synthesise (stylised).

Abstract

The accumulator read in the engine of accel-gatedrtl indexed the whole accumulator bus with a variable, which Yosys builds as one wide shifter per lane and which stopped accel-gatedlanes at 16 lanes. An AND-OR read mux over the batch sets gives the same cycles, fetches, accumulator writes and flop count, bit-exact against NumPy at all 48 points (P = 2–64, B = 4 and 8), but weight-stationary engines come out 17–28% smaller in SKY130 HD synthesis (output-stationary unchanged). Gated weight-stationary is 1.45–1.50× the output-stationary area at B = 4 and 2.1–2.2× at B = 8, flat in P, against 1.6–1.9× and 2.9–3.0× before; every point now synthesises under the old 7 GB cap. Stylised: synthesis only, one layer shape, random data, clock-pin-only energy; the cause is inferred from the before-and-after effect.

Does clock-gated weight-stationary scheduling still beat output-stationary on a second open process, with that process's own SRAM?

research/accel-ihpprice · 8 tests

Second-node verdict for clock-gated register-file weight-stationary scheduling from IHP SG13G2 liberty files (idle flop 34 fJ, clock gate 0.75 of a flop, ten SRAM macros with separate read energies): one gate per accumulator makes WS beat OS in all 120 cells, but ungated WS also wins 33 of 60 packed cells because IHP's SRAM costs 72 to 497 flop-cycles per weight against SKY130's 18 to 62. Abstract

Repeats the accel-gatedprice pricing with the IHP SG13G2 liberty files, which ship ten SRAM macros with separate read and write energies (GF180 has none). An idle IHP flop costs 34 fJ per cycle and an idle clock gate 0.75 of that (0.86 on SKY130, 0.95 on NanGate45); one gate per 24-bit accumulator makes register-file weight-stationary (WS) beat output-stationary (OS) in all 120 layer, batch, macro and packing cells, needing at most 6 flops per gate. Ungated WS also wins 33 of the 60 packed cells, where on SKY130 it lost nearly all of them: a packed IHP weight fetch costs 72 to 497 idle-flop-cycles against 18 to 62 for SKY130’s macros. A units trap (IHP declares 1 µA but its energies are pJ) is checked against C×V2. Library characterisation values, not measurements; clock gate priced for idle clock energy only, no RTL built for IHP.
Stylised.

Does the accumulator-read bug found in accel-gatedmux also inflate accel-planebatch's weight-stationary area?

research/accel-planemux · 8 tests

Does accel-gatedmux's accumulator-read bug also inflate accel-planebatch's WS area? No: the rewritten engine is bit-exact with identical counts and its area is within -3 % to +12 % with no trend (WS/OS 1.62 vs 1.66 at B = 4); the earlier verdict stands.

Abstract

accel-gatedmux found that a variable-index accumulator read made the weight-stationary (WS) engines of accel-gatedrtl 17 to 28 % too large. The batched engine of accel-planebatch has the same index. Rewriting its read as an AND-OR over constant indices and its write as decoded enables, and re-running all 40 points (two accel-kws layers, batch 1 to 16, P = 8), gives bit-exact outputs and identical cycles and fetch counts, with WS area between −3 % and +12 % of the original and no trend in batch (WS/OS cells at B = 4: 1.66 before, 1.62 after). Negative: the earlier verdict stands, because here the accumulators are a Verilog array that Yosys already muxes per entry. Generic-gate cells from one mapper run per point, which moves about 3 % by itself.

Does a quantised surrogate make a design search cheaper in finite-element solves than searching with the simulator alone?

At 150 FE evaluations a surrogate-assisted loop is 2.3 to 2.8 % from a best-known design against 6 to 8 % for an FE-only evolution strategy and 14 to 20 % for random search (float and 6-bit surrogates indistinguishable; stylised, 8 seeds).

Abstract

Minimum-mass design of the 2D Q6 cantilever of accel-surrogatelock under a tip-deflection limit, with the 64×8 FE solve as the expensive evaluator and 150 evaluations per run (8 seeds, two limits). Abstract

The 2D cantilever of accel-surrogate2d with a locking-free Q6 (incompatible-modes) element, which is within 0.6 % of Timoshenko theory on a 16×2 mesh where Q4 gives half the deflection. When the beam simulator is a 2D finite-element model, does a quantised surrogate (and an accelerator for it) pay?

research/accel-surrogate2d · 7 tests

When the beam simulator is a 2D plane-stress FE model, does a quantised MLP surrogate (and an accelerator) pay? The energy break-even is met (34x at 32x4 mesh, 319x at 64x8, assumed constants), and the surrogate's 2 % error against its FE is small beside the FE's own 9 % discretisation error; but the locking Q4 simulator is itself inaccurate. Stylised.

Abstract

The cantilever of accel-surrogate meshed as a 2D plane-stress continuum (bilinear quadrilaterals, 32×4 and 64×8 meshes). An 8-32-32-1 MLP imitates each solve and is quantised to 6 and 8 weight bits. With assumed energy constants the FE solve costs 34× (32×4) and 319× (64×8) the 8-bit MLP's energy, so the break-even that failed for the closed-form beam is met. Limitations: the locking Q4 elements make the simulator inaccurate, two meshes, three seeds, assumed energy model, no RTL. Stylised.

Can a quantised MLP surrogate of a finite-element beam beat the beam's own model-form error, and when would an accelerator for it pay?

No: float error is 1.7 % (18x the gap), 6-bit QAT stays within 0.3 points of float, and the surrogate only beats the solver above ~1,600 simulator flops. Stylised.

Abstract

A 8-32-32-1 MLP imitates an Euler-Bernoulli finite-element cantilever with eight section heights. Shear deformation (Timoshenko vs Euler-Bernoulli) moves the tip deflection by a median 0.09 %; the float surrogate's median error is 1.66 %, about 18× larger, and quantisation adds little at 6 bits with quantisation-aware training (1.95 %) but 21 points at 3 bits post-training. Stylised: one tiny network, assumed energy constants, no RTL.

Abstract

When does a task's accuracy tolerance let an accelerator drop its multipliers?

research/accel-sparse · 7 tests

Pruning, int4/int8, ternary and binary MLPs on scikit-learn digits (substitute task) with a modelled-energy engine: when does the accuracy slack let the hardware drop multipliers? Stylised; at 1–2 points tolerance, pruned int8 still beats multiplier-free.

Abstract

Pruning, int4/int8, ternary and binary variants of a 64-32-10 MLP on scikit-learn's 8×8 digits (a substitute task: Speech Commands was unreachable), with a modelled energy per inference for a zero-skipping serial engine, over 10 splits. Pruning 75% of int8 weights costs 0.2 points and cuts modelled energy to 36% of dense int8; ternary costs 1.4 points and binary 3.0. Within 1–2 points of float32 the cheapest design still has multipliers, because operand reads dominate in the assumed model; multiplier energy must be about 10× the assumed value before dropping it clearly pays. Stylised: assumed 45 nm constants, no RTL or synthesis, no lookup-table variant, toy task.

Does weight-sharing lookup-table inference beat a multiply-accumulate engine for a small accelerator?

research/accel-lut · 6 tests

Weight-sharing product-table inference against int8 MAC engines on the digits MLP: negative at this size, break-even only for wide layers with cheap table reads. Stylised, modelled energy.

Abstract

A k-means weight-sharing codebook with a per-input product table (no multiplier per weight) against int8 serial MAC engines, on the 64-32-10 digits MLP of accel-sparse, dense and 75% pruned, over 10 splits. Negative: within 2 points of float32 the table engine does not beat int8 (dense: 3.51 nJ for 8 shared values against 3.56 nJ for int8; pruned: 1.55 against 1.51 nJ), and it only looks better against a baseline that rereads activations per weight. An analytic sweep puts the break-even at layers of about 128 outputs or more, and then only if table reads cost a quarter of an array read. Stylised: assumed 45 nm constants, no retraining or RTL, substitute task.

Does a wider hidden layer make weight-sharing product-table inference beat an int8 MAC engine?

research/accel-lutwide · 6 tests

Product-table inference on trained wide digits MLPs (32, 128, 512 hidden): at best 14% under int8 energy, only with an assumed cheap table read. Stylised, modelled energy.

Abstract

Tests the break-even accel-lut predicted analytically, on trained 64-N-10 digits MLPs (N = 32, 128, 512, 10 splits), with the table engine on the wide layer and an int8 MAC on the 10-output layer. Mostly negative: the best table design is 14% below int8 modelled energy at width 512 only if a table read costs a quarter of an array read per bit (assumed), and 2× or worse at the array-cost read. Accuracy cost of sharing shrinks with width (K = 8: 1.6 points at 32, 0.4 at 512). Stylised, modelled energy, no synthesis.

Does fine-tuning the shared weight values rescue low-K lookup-table inference?

research/accel-lutft · 4 tests

Fine-tuning the shared values of accel-lut's product-table engine: rescues K=2 in the 75%-pruned digits MLP (14% under int8 energy only at an assumed cheap table read), not the dense network. Stylised, modelled energy.

Abstract

accel-lut left its shared values untrained. Here the codebook is kept in the loop for 60 epochs (straight-through, k-means re-fit each step) on the same 64-32-10 digits MLP, dense and 75% pruned, over 10 splits. Mixed: in the pruned network 2 shared values go from a 6.5-point to a 1.7-point accuracy drop, and with an assumed cheap table read (0.04 pJ/bit) that is 1.30 nJ against 1.51 nJ for int8; at 0.16 pJ/bit it loses. In the dense network fine-tuning does not help (8 shared values: 3.2 points down against 1.6 without it). Stylised: assumed energy constants, one fine-tuning recipe, no RTL, substitute task.

What does a product-table inference engine cost in RTL, against a serial MAC engine?

research/accel-lutrtl · 4 tests

The product-table engine and a serial MAC engine in Verilog, bit-exact against NumPy and synthesised with Yosys: negative, the table needs an accumulator per output (stylised)

Abstract

Both engines for a 64×N dense layer with 4-bit activations and int8 shared weights, written in Verilog, checked bit for bit against an integer NumPy reference, and synthesised with Yosys to generic gates (assumed gate-equivalent weights). Negative and stylised: the table engine keeps one accumulator per output, so its datapath is 2.4× (N=8) to 11.6× (N=128) the MAC engine's and it takes 1.1× to 3.0× the cycles, even though it stores half the weight bits. Random weights, area only, no energy measured.

Do trained keyword-spotting models fit a Tiny Tapeout tile's 16-bit bias and 20-bit accumulator?

research/accel-ttretrain · 8 tests

All 96 retrained keyword-spotting models fit the Tiny Tapeout engine's 16-bit bias and 20-bit accumulator limits (worst use 43% and 30%), accuracy through the tile contract equals the reference, and 32 trained models run bit-exact in RTL.

Abstract

All 96 retrained accel-kws models (70 and 120 inputs, hidden width 8–64, 2–8-bit weights, 3 seeds) on Speech Commands stay inside the streaming engine's limits: the largest layer-1 bias is 43% of the 16-bit limit and the largest accumulator 30% of the 20-bit limit, with no overflow on train or test clips. Accuracy through the tile's wrapping contract equals the integer reference on every model, and the 32 trained seed-0 models run bit-exact (class and cycles) through the Icarus RTL on 30 clips each. The accuracies accel-ttfit used are reproduced to within 0.010 for 3-, 4- and 8-bit weights. Stylised: one hidden layer, weak features, synthesis-only area.
