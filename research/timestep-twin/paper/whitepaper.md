# A controller tuned in a fixed-step twin inherits the integrator's damping: overshoot claims for explicit, semi-implicit and implicit Euler

*Stylised: a linear oscillator `x''=−k(x−1)−c x'` (`k=1`, time in `1/ω₀`), unit-step response. Twin: one fixed-step integrator with step `h`; "real": the exact flow. Pure Python; all numbers are from `experiments/results.txt` (deterministic, a few seconds). Sections 1, 2, 4 use exact formulas or exact matrix eigenvalues; section 3 uses a bisection tuner on the twin's sampled step response; the real overshoot is the closed form `exp(−πζ/√(1−ζ²))`, which matches an RK4 run at `dt=10⁻³` to five digits.*

## Question
Simulators advance a physical system with a fixed step. A designer tunes a gain until the twin's step response overshoots by no more than a target, then deploys. The twin's integrator adds or removes damping that the real plant does not have. How large is the resulting overshoot error, for which integrators does it have which sign, how fast does it shrink with the step, and what repairs it cheaply?

## Model
Closed loop `e''=−k e−c e'` with `e=x−1`, poles `λ=−σ±iω_d`, `σ=c/2`, `ζ=c/(2√k)`. One step of size `h` maps `(e,v)` by a 2×2 matrix `M`: explicit Euler `[[1,h],[−hk,1−hc]]`; semi-implicit Euler (velocity first, position with the new velocity) `[[1−h²k, h(1−hc)],[−hk, 1−hc]]`; backward Euler `(I−hA)^{-1}`. The exact flow has `det expm(hA)=e^{−hc}`. The determinants are `1−hc+h²k`, `1−hc`, and `1/(1+hc+h²k)` respectively. Mapping the twin's pole back with `λ_T=ln z/h` gives a decay rate `σ_T`. Expanding the logarithm gives `σ_T=c/2+(h/2)·b+O(h²)` with `b=c²/2−k` (explicit), `c²/2` (semi-implicit), `k−c²/2` (implicit). The designer's rule is "smallest `c` whose sampled twin step response peaks at most `target` above 1" (bisection; an unstable twin counts as infinite overshoot).

## Results
1. **The per-step laws are exact.** The closed forms equal the determinants of the step matrices to 1.1×10⁻¹⁶ over the grid tested. The first-order `σ_T` tracks the exact mapped rate closely at small `h` (e.g. `ζ=0.6, h=0.1`: exact `σ_T/σ`=0.971 / 1.065 / 1.018 for explicit / semi-implicit / implicit, first order 0.977 / 1.060 / 1.023) and loosens at `h=0.4` (`ζ=0.3` implicit: 1.402 vs 1.547). A forward-Euler twin also diverges when `c<hk` (`h=0.5, c=0.4`: spectral radius 1.025 for a real plant with `ζ=0.2`).
2. **The sign of the damping error flips at `ζ=1/√2` (to first order) for explicit and implicit Euler, not for semi-implicit.** At `h=0.1`, `σ_T−σ` is −0.044 / +0.009 / +0.038 at `ζ=0.3` and −0.017 / +0.039 / +0.011 at `ζ=0.6` (explicit / semi-implicit / implicit); at `ζ=0.9` it is +0.032 / +0.092 / −0.030. The exact sign change sits at `ζ`=0.742, 0.724, 0.716, 0.711 (explicit) and 0.675, 0.691, 0.699, 0.703 (implicit) for `h`=0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025, converging to `0.7071`. Semi-implicit Euler (`ρ²=1−hc`) adds damping at every `ζ` tested.
3. **Overshoot claims: implicit and semi-implicit twins under-report, forward Euler over-reports, at every target tested (1–20%), including above `ζ=1/√2`.** Tuned to a 10% target, the real overshoot is 0.105 / 0.111 / 0.121 / 0.142 (semi-implicit) and 0.110 / 0.120 / 0.142 / 0.192 (implicit) for `h`=0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4, against 0.091 / 0.081 / 0.065 / 0.036 (explicit). The ratio real/claim is larger at small targets: at a 1% target it is 1.32–4.90 (semi-implicit) and 1.14–2.40 (implicit) while forward Euler gives 0.87–0.20. The damping-bias sign flip in item 2 therefore does *not* carry over to overshoot: the overshoot depends on the twin's frequency warp and on sampling the peak as well as on its decay rate, and this project does not separate those contributions.
4. **The error is first order in `h`.** The excess of the real overshoot over the 10% claim halves on each halving of `h`: ratios 1.99–2.01 (semi-implicit), 2.17 → 2.01 (implicit), 1.80 → 1.99 (forward Euler, with opposite sign), from `h`=0.4 down to 0.0125 (e.g. implicit +0.092 → +0.0024).
5. **Cheap repairs.** At `h=0.2`, target 10%: the tuned gain gives real overshoot 0.121 (semi-implicit) / 0.142 (implicit). Re-running that gain in the twin at `h/8` predicts 0.119 / 0.136 and at `h/16` 0.120 / 0.139 (real 0.121 / 0.142). Richardson extrapolation `2·o(h/2)−o(h)` at fixed gain gives 0.1216 / 0.1371 from `h=0.2`, 0.1213 / 0.1409 from `h=0.1`, and 0.1213 / 0.1418 from `h=0.05`. Re-tuning with substeps until the real overshoot is within ±1 point of 10% needs ×4 twin cost for both integrators; within ±0.2 points it needs ×16 (semi-implicit) and ×32 (implicit).

## Limitations
One linear second-order loop with the controller evaluated continuously inside the twin's ODE, so the twin's error is the integrator's alone (no sensor delay, sampling of the controller, contact, constraints or nonlinearity); textbook integrators, not any particular simulator's scheme, and no claim is made about what a specific physics engine does by default; the "real" plant is the exact flow, not hardware; overshoot is read from samples of the step response, which mixes integrator damping with peak-sampling error; the overshoot sign result (item 3) is measured at `k=1` and the listed targets, not proved; no robot data. Preliminary.

## Next steps
Separate damping, frequency and sampling contributions to the overshoot bias analytically; second-order schemes (midpoint, Verlet, RK4) and adaptive steps; stiff joints and contact (a stiff spring at a step larger than its period); combining with `latency-twin` so delay and integrator damping interact; a proper-scoring check of the twin's overshoot claim (`twin-elicitation`); picking `h` from a target claim error via the first-order bias.
