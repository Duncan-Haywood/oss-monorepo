# Selection twin: how optimistic is the best of K controllers picked in a noisy digital twin?

Pure Python, no dependencies. A twin scores K candidate controllers by simulated cost and you deploy the argmin. With score noise `τ` (finite-rollout Monte Carlo error or candidate-specific model-form error) around true costs `μ_i ~ N(m, v²)`, the winner's claimed cost is exactly `m − √(v²+τ²)·c_K` and its real cost `m − v²c_K/√(v²+τ²)`, where `c_K = E[max of K std normals]`. The optimism is `τ²c_K/√(v²+τ²)`, so with fixed noise it grows like `√(2 ln K)` while the real gain from more candidates saturates (simulation matches the closed forms to 1–2% at K up to 1000). Under a **fixed simulation budget** noise grows with K (`τ² = σ²K/B`) and the winner's real cost is non-monotone in K: best K = 5 / 8 / 22 for `σ²/B` = 4 / 0.4 / 0.04 (`v=1`), and 5,000 candidates are worse than 5. In a concrete unstable-scalar LQ twin with an *exact* plant (K=50 gains, 10 rollouts each) the winner's claim is 0.0414 vs real 0.0450 (8% too low), below the 0.0444 that *any* gain can achieve; the Gaussian formulas only get the direction right there (the cost population is right-skewed; optimism measured 0.0036 vs 0.0017 predicted). Repairs at equal budget: fresh re-scoring of the winner removes the claim bias but not the choice; empirical-Bayes shrinkage barely helps here (bias 0.0031 vs 0.0036); screen-then-verify roughly cuts the claim bias by 4.5× (0.0008) and, in the Gaussian model, cuts regret 0.73 → 0.34 at K=100 with the best re-test size ~10. See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

cd research/selection-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 9 tests, ~2 s

**Builds on.** The sim-to-real and policy-validation direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>), HIRO (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>) and RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>), where many candidate controllers or planners are compared in simulation before hardware; no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The statistics are classical: the winner's curse and optimizer's curse (Capen, Clapp & Campbell 1971; Smith & Winkler 2006), empirical-Bayes shrinkage (Efron & Morris 1973; Efron 2011 on selection bias), and ranking and selection (Bechhofer, Santner & Goldsman 1995). Sim-to-real context: Zhao, Queralta & Westerlund 2020. Overlaps with `explanation-twin`, which derives the same `e_K = c_K` optimism for a planner's *quoted* cost with equal true costs; this project adds a spread `v` of true costs (so deployed regret and the `v²/√(v²+τ²)` shrinkage), the fixed-budget optimum K, and repairs compared at equal budget. Companion to `twin-transfer`, `randomized-twin` (there the twin's *model* is wrong and one design is trained; here the twin is noisy and many designs are compared) and `twin-certification` in this repository.

Stylised: i.i.d. Gaussian score noise for the closed forms, one scalar LQ plant, twin equal to the real plant in the concrete experiment (so only Monte Carlo noise, no model error), no hardware data. MIT.
