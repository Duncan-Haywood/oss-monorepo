# Jitter twin: a twin fitted to the delay histogram is blind to its persistence, what does bursty sensor delay do to the certified gain?

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real loop `x[n+1] = x[n] − k·x[n−d_n] + w[n]` with random measurement delay `d_n`; twin: constant delay equal to the mean (closed-form stable gain `k < 2 sin(π/(2(2d+1)))`: 2, 1, 0.618, 0.445 for d = 0…3). Mean-square stability is the spectral radius of the mode-wise second-moment operator of a Markov-jump linear system. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`): (1) independent jitter at the twin's mean delay moves the mean-square threshold little and in either direction: {0,2} gives exactly the twin's 1.000, {0,1,2} 0.952 (5% below), {0,1,2,3,4} 0.668 vs the twin's 0.618; for {0,2} with `P(d=2)=p` the threshold fits `kc(kc+1) = 1/p` (empirical) and at p = ½ the stationary variance equals the twin's; (2) *persistence* of the delay, invisible in the histogram, is the large effect: with the marginal {0,2} fixed, the threshold falls 1.000 → 0.875 → 0.672 → 0.624 at lag-1 autocorrelation ρ = 0, 0.5, 0.9, 0.99 (towards the worst-case constant delay's 0.618), a gain of 0.9 that the twin certifies is mean-square unstable from ρ = 0.435, and below the threshold the variance at k = 0.6 inflates from the twin's 2.56 to 6.26 (ρ = 0.9) and 15.2 (ρ = 0.99); (3) almost-sure and mean-square stability differ widely: for i.i.d. {0,2} the simulated almost-sure threshold is ≈1.84 against a mean-square threshold of 1.0, and it is nearly insensitive to ρ, so whether persistence matters depends on which stability the safety claim needs. Stylised: scalar plant, sticky-redraw delay process, simulated "real" system, Monte-Carlo a.s. thresholds (one seed); see `paper/whitepaper.md`.

**Builds on.** The digital-twin simulation-fidelity and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>), HIRO Group (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>, manipulation) and the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>) and RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>, UAV and field-robot simulation); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them.
