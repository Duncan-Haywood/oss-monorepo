# A twin fitted to the delay histogram is blind to its persistence: bursty sensor delay halves the mean-square gain margin

*Stylised: a scalar delayed-feedback loop, pure Python, every number is from `experiments/results.txt` (deterministic, about 90 s). The "real" system is itself simulated; no lab or field data. Negative and out-of-model results are marked.*

## Question
A digital twin of a sensor-feedback loop is usually given the delay it measures in logs, often as a single number (the mean) or a histogram. `latency-twin` showed that omitting delay altogether is dangerous. Here the twin does include the delay, correctly in law, and we ask what it cannot see: does a twin that reproduces the *marginal* distribution of the sensor delay certify gains that the real, randomly delayed loop cannot hold?

## Model
Real: `x[n+1] = x[n] − k·x[n−d_n] + w[n]`, `w ~ N(0,1)`, delay `d_n ∈ {0,1,2,…}` random. Twin: constant delay equal to the mean. For constant `d` the loop is stable iff `k < 2 sin(π/(2(2d+1)))` (2, 1, 0.618, 0.445, 0.347 for d = 0…4; the numerical second-moment threshold reproduces this, to 4·10⁻⁴ at d = 3, 4 where power iteration converges slowly). With random delay the state `s = (x[n],…,x[n−D])` follows `s[n+1] = A_{d_n}s[n] + b·w[n]`, a Markov-jump linear system. "Mean-square (MS) stable" means `E|s|²` decays: it is the spectral radius of the mode-wise second-moment operator `Q'_j = Σ_i P_ij A_i Q_i A_iᵀ` (power iteration) and gives the stationary variance of `x` by the same recursion plus noise. "Almost-sure (a.s.) stable" means the Lyapunov exponent is negative (Monte-Carlo, 10⁵ steps, fixed seed). Persistence: the delay chain stays put with probability `ρ` and otherwise redraws from the marginal law, `P = (1−ρ)·1πᵀ + ρI`; its eigenvalues are 1 and `ρ`, so the lag-1 autocorrelation of the delay is exactly `ρ` while the marginal is identical for every `ρ`. `ρ = 0` is i.i.d.

## Results
1. **Independent jitter at the twin's mean delay barely moves the MS threshold, and not always in the conservative direction.** Uniform on {0,2} (mean 1): 1.0000, equal to the twin's 1.0000; uniform on {0,1,2}: 0.9524 (5% *below* the twin's 1, equivalent to a constant delay of 1.082); uniform on {0,1,2,3,4} (mean 2): 0.668 against the twin's 0.618 (above, effective delay 1.805); {1,3}: 0.642 against 0.618. For {0,2} with `P(d=2) = p` the computed threshold satisfies `kc(kc+1) = 1/p` to the printed five decimals at p = 0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.65, 0.8, 0.95 (an empirical fit, not proved; it applies while it gives `kc ≤ 2`). For {0,2} with p = ½ the stationary variance equals the twin's to all printed digits at k = 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 0.95 (2.6915, 2.4000, 4.0179, 13.916), again unproved and not true for {0,1,2} (4.915 vs 4.018 at k = 0.8; 272.8 vs 13.9 at k = 0.95).
2. **Persistence is what the histogram hides.** Same marginal {0,2}: MS threshold 1.000, 0.942, 0.875, 0.780, 0.672, 0.645, 0.624 at ρ = 0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99, tending to the worst-case constant delay 2 (0.618); effective delay 1.00 → 1.98. A gain of 0.9 (90% of the twin's threshold) is MS-stable for i.i.d. jitter and unstable from ρ = 0.435; 0.8 from ρ = 0.662; 0.7 from 0.849; 0.65 from 0.940; 0.6 and below stay stable for every ρ < 0.999 (they are under the worst-case 0.618). Below the threshold the variance still inflates: at k = 0.6 the twin says Var(x) = 2.56, the real loop 2.83, 3.11, 3.72, 6.26, 8.85, 15.2 at ρ = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99 (×5.9 at 0.99); at k = 0.65 the variance is 2.74 (twin) vs 17.9 at ρ = 0.9.
3. **Mean square and almost sure disagree widely, and the gap is not bounded by the twin.** For i.i.d. {0,2} the a.s. exponent is negative up to k ≈ 1.8 (simulated crossing 1.84) while `E|s|²` grows from k = 1 (growth 1.067 per step at k = 1.1, 1.20 at 1.3). Between them typical trajectories decay while the second moment, and hence the variance a twin would estimate from many runs, does not converge. The simulated a.s. threshold is nearly insensitive to persistence (1.84, 1.98, 1.94, 1.85, 1.76, 1.75, 1.76 for the same ρ grid; the non-monotone wiggle at ρ = 0.3 is within what we expect from Monte-Carlo noise and the bisection's monotonicity assumption, which we did not check). If "stable" means a.s., the persistence effect in result 2 is not visible; if it means bounded variance, it is large. The choice is a property of the safety claim, not of the simulator.

## Limitations
Scalar integrator plant, a single delay law family, noise-free sensing except process noise, discrete time. Persistence is one-parameter (sticky redraw); real delay processes (queueing, bus arbitration, wireless retransmission) have richer structure that the lag-1 autocorrelation does not summarise. The a.s. thresholds are Monte-Carlo bisections with one seed and a monotonicity assumption; the MS thresholds for d ≥ 3 are good to about 4·10⁻⁴. The closed-form fit `kc(kc+1) = 1/p` and the variance coincidence for symmetric {0,2} are empirical. No claim about a particular robot, bus or network.
