## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 12: bf16 referee on the transformer (H1), planned before running
- Why: milestone 11 ran only the float32 reorder referee; the lower-precision referee is the realistic one (a verifier on different hardware). `margin_tf.py` already has the bf16 path (autocast forward, float32 loss); `experiments/transformer_bf16/run.py` uses it with the same 10 seeds, corpus and taus.
- Prediction: bf16 drift is far larger than float32 reorder drift (around 1e-4 to 1e-2 per step, from the fp16 numbers of milestone 8 scaled by bf16's coarser mantissa), so a referee tolerance must sit above it, and the honest false-dispute rate at tau = 1e-3 is high (above 10%). The skip shortcut at tau = 1e-2 (about the p99 drift) still skips 10 to 20% of steps at a cost below one seed std. Refutation of the narrow form of H1 here: a tau at or below 10x the max bf16 drift with degradation above one seed std and a CI excluding zero.
- Analysis: as milestone 11, plus drift quantiles and the share of honest steps whose bf16 drift exceeds 1e-3 and 1e-2.
- Result (`experiments/transformer_bf16/`, 10 seeds, same corpus and taus, torch 2.14.1 CPU, bf16 autocast referee): honest loss 2.7252 (std 0.0083). bf16 drift per step: median 8.5e-4, p99 2.4e-3, max 4.0e-3, against update norms of 0.0038 / 0.081 / 0.22 (10 / 50 / 90%). So a referee tolerance of 1e-3 falsely disputes 41% of honest steps, and 1e-2 disputes none. Skip shortcut judged by the bf16 referee: tau = 1e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2, 1e-1 skips 5.1%, 16%, 29%, 59% of steps for +0.00009, +0.0017 (CI 0.0011 to 0.0022, 0.2 std), +0.0085, +0.063 (all CIs exclude zero).
- Verdict: prediction held on drift scale (p99 2.4e-3 is inside 1e-4 to 1e-2) and on the false-dispute rate (41% at 1e-3, above 10%). The narrow form of H1 is not refuted: at tau = 1e-2, about 2.5x the max bf16 drift, the cheater skips 16% of steps and gives up 0.2 seed std. The cost numbers equal the float32 run to the third digit because the skip decision depends on the gradient norm, which bf16 barely changes; the referee matters for the false-dispute rate, not for what the cheater saves. Practical consequence: an honest-safe tolerance on bf16 hardware (>= 1e-2) already lets a cheater skip 16% of steps at no measurable quality cost.
- Limits: same as milestone 11 (30k parameters, 600 steps, fixed split, skip shortcut only); bf16 here is CPU autocast on the forward pass, not a different accelerator.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 11: the margin on a small transformer (H1), planned before running

- Prediction: the result carries over: drift is 4 to 6 orders below the update norms, so tau at drift size skips almost nothing, and degradation stays below one seed std for tau <= 1e-2 (about the 10 to 40% quantile of norms). It hurts at tau = 0.1 (above the median norm, so over half the steps skipped). Because the grid starts at 1e-3, the drift-sized test is the skipped share at 1e-3, which I expect to be near 10%, and the degradation there.
- Analysis: 10 seeds (init and batch order; the split is fixed, a stated limit), degradation = skip loss minus honest loss, paired by seed, 95% bootstrap CI, share of steps skipped.

- Result (`experiments/transformer/`, 10 seeds, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`, torch 2.14.1 CPU; about 5 minutes on 4 cores): honest held-out loss 2.7252 (seed std 0.0083); float32 reorder drift p99 9.1e-8, max 1.5e-7 against update norms of 0.0038 / 0.081 / 0.22 (10 / 50 / 90%), so drift is 4 to 6 orders below the updates, as predicted. Skip degradation (paired, 95% CI): tau = 1e-3 skips 5.1% of steps, +0.00009 (CI 0.00005 to 0.00013); tau = 1e-2 skips 16%, +0.0017 (0.0011 to 0.0022, 0.2 seed std); tau = 3e-2 skips 29%, +0.0085 (0.0077 to 0.0092, 1.0 std); tau = 0.1 skips 59%, +0.062 (0.057 to 0.068, 7.4 std). Skipping was better on 1 of 10 seeds at 1e-2 and on none elsewhere.
- Verdict: the narrow form of H1 is not refuted on a transformer: at any tau up to 10x the worst measured drift (about 1.5e-6) almost nothing is skipped (the smallest grid point, 1e-3, is already 6,700x the drift and skips 5%), and the loss given up stays below one seed std up to tau = 1e-2 (16% of steps saved). It reaches one std at tau = 3e-2 and clearly hurts at 0.1. Unlike breast cancer (milestones 8 and 9) skipping never helps here, so the cheater's saving is paid for in loss, as on digits. Two details of the prediction were off: the share skipped at 1e-3 is 5%, not about 10%, and the degradation at 1e-2 and 3e-2 is small but its CI excludes zero (measurable, not noise).
- Limits: a 30k-parameter character model on 87 kB of text, 600 plain-SGD steps (held-out loss 2.73 is far from converged, so the late-training regime where updates shrink is only partly reached), fixed split, 10 seeds, float32 reorder referee only (a bf16 referee path exists in `margin_tf.py` but is not run), skip shortcut only. It shows the pattern is not specific to MLPs; it does not show it at realistic scale.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 7a: H6, predicted against measured skip degradation (planned before running)
- Plan: two predictors that see only the 20 honest runs (recorded per-step update norms). A, first order: a skipped step forgoes the descent it would have bought, |d|^2/lr, summed over steps with |d| <= tau. B, fewer steps: the skipper has done (1 - f(tau)) T steps, so predict the loss of an honest worker trained that long on the same compressed cosine schedule. Measured: the milestone 3 skip degradation, reorder referee. H6 is refuted if the ratio predicted/measured leaves [0.5, 2] on a majority of settings. I expect A to overshoot (it ignores that later steps recover the loss) and B to overshoot at small tau (skipped steps are the low-value ones, not a random subset).
- Result (`experiments/h6/`, 9 taus): both predictors miss. Only rows with measured degradation above one seed std (0.0039) count as scored: tau = 0.1 and 0.3 (2 of 9). A is out of range at both (123x and 12x); B is 3.0x at tau = 0.1 and 1.00 at tau = 0.3, where every step is skipped and the prediction is just the untrained network, which is trivial. At tau = 1e-3 and 1e-2 (degradation 0.0002 and 0.0034, below noise) A overshoots 46x and 105x and B 18x and 4.6x. Verdict: H6 refuted for these two simple models (the ratio is in range in 1 of 9 settings, and that one is trivial). Both overshoot by an order of magnitude or more because skipped steps are exactly the small-update steps, whose loss is recovered by the remaining training.
- Caveats: zero-step case approximated by 1 step; ratios on below-noise rows are not meaningful and are reported only for completeness; one dataset, one MLP. A better predictor (second order, or a model of recovery) is open and would be the paper's constructive claim.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6: collusion on a shared trajectory (H4), planned before running
- Setting: m workers on one job, each submits honest (cost 1) or the shared cheap trajectory K (cost 1 - g). Outputs are compared; the strict-majority output is accepted and its submitters are paid r = 1 (individual rationality), submitters of any other output (or all, if there is no strict majority) get -S. One independent audit per job catches K with probability p, and then every K submitter gets -S. Brute force: for m = 1 to 7 and a grid of (g, p), find the least S at which all-honest is a strong equilibrium (no subset of workers has a joint deviation, over all 2^|subset| action profiles, that leaves every member strictly better off), by bisection over exact payoffs.
- Closed form to test (my derivation, not the proposal's 1/m): a minority deviation is punished by replication alone, and a majority coalition of size floor(m/2)+1 escapes replication with probability one and is deterred only by the audit: p (r + S) >= g, i.e. S* = max(0, g/p - 1) for every m >= 1, with no dependence on m.
- Predictions: (1) a coalition of floor(m/2)+1 workers escapes replication (trivially true); (2) S*(m) is flat in m, so the proposal's "falls as 1/m" is refuted for a per-job audit and belongs to the verifier-bribery setting, where a briber must pay each of m verifiers; (3) replication multiplies the pay bill by m and lowers the needed stake by nothing, so against a perfectly coordinating coalition it is dominated by m = 1 plus the audit. Refutation of my closed form: any (m, g, p) where brute force differs from it by more than the bisection tolerance.
- Also checked: the threshold is unchanged if the audit is drawn once per job rather than per worker (payoffs are linear in the caught indicator).
- Result (`experiments/collusion/`, 140 cells: m 1 to 7, g in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9}, p in {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1}; 14 tests pass with `tests/test_coalition.py`): brute-force strong-equilibrium search matches max(0, g/p - 1) to 7e-10 in every cell. The smallest coalition that escapes replication is floor(m/2)+1 (1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4 for m = 1 to 7). The stake is flat in m (g = 0.5, p = 0.1: 4.00 for every m), so the proposal's `1/m` fall (which would give 0.57 at m = 7) is refuted for a per-job audit; the pay bill grows as m.
- Verdict on H4: the first half (a coalition escapes replication with probability one) holds, with the coalition needing a strict majority. The second half, as worded, is refuted by construction in this model: a coordinating majority faces only the audit, so replication adds no deterrence beyond it; the `1/m` law belongs to verifier-bribery, where a briber pays each of m verifiers separately. Caveat on strength: the flat-in-m result follows from the payoff structure (it is a theorem here, proved in the docstring argument, and the search confirms the code implements it), so it is a check of the model rather than a discovery; what would make `1/m` appear is a different model (side payments, a split penalty budget, per-verifier bribes), not tested.
- Not done and limits: pure strategies and one shared cheat only (no mixed coalition equilibria, no partial copying kappa < 1, no benign drift delta, no side payments, no coalition that also bribes the auditor); majority rule only (no unanimity or reference-answer pay); the parameters are swept, not measured. H6 and the paper remain.
- Next: milestone 7, the paper skeleton in `paper/` (EC format), and H6 (predicted against measured degradation); a model variant with side payments to see when a `1/m` law does appear.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 5: peer agreement (H3) and a one-sided lower bound (H5), planned before running
- H3 setting: two workers, each picks honest (cost 1) or a cheat (cost 1 - g, audit catch probability p, one shared deterministic shortcut). Each is paid w(audit outcome, agree or disagree) >= -S, four numbers. Agreement probabilities: honest-honest 1 - delta (benign drift beyond the tolerance), honest-cheat alpha (the cheat's output stays within the agreement test), cheat-cheat kappa (kappa = 1 is the copying equilibrium). The cheapest contract is a linear programme in the four payments. Three versions: audit-only (w does not depend on agreement), peer contract that only requires honest-honest to be an equilibrium, and peer contract that also requires (cheat, cheat) not to be an equilibrium (strictly, margin 1e-6).
- Prediction: the first peer version never costs more (more signals cannot raise the cost: a refinement), and is strictly cheaper when alpha < 1 and kappa < 1, so the proposal's wording of H3 ("raises the minimum cost") holds only for the third version. In the third version at kappa = 1 I expect agreement to be worth nothing (cost equal to audit-only everywhere on a random sweep), because paying for agreement makes (cheat, cheat) more attractive. Refutation of H3 as stated: some instance with kappa = 1 where the third version is strictly cheaper than audit-only.
- H5 plan: prove a one-sided resolution bound (any contract implementing honest against a deviation with gain g, pay floor -S and one-sided resolution p, where p = 1 - min_s P_a(s)/P_h(s), costs at least max(C, g/p - S)), test LP >= L on random instances and on the measured skip actions, and report how loose it is where the score is Gaussian (I expect it to be vacuous there, since a Gaussian likelihood ratio reaches zero in the tail).
- Result H3 (`experiments/joint/h3.py`, 4,000 random instances, g, p, S, delta, alpha, kappa; alpha capped at 1 - delta, since a cheat should not agree with an honest peer more often than an honest worker does): version 2 (honest-honest only) is cheaper than audit-only in 1,191 instances and never dearer (as a refinement must be), up to 97% cheaper; version 3 (also exclude copying) is cheaper in 451 of 4,000 but in 0 of 1,349 instances with kappa = 1, where the contracts cost the same. Where cheats do not coordinate (kappa 0 or 0.5) agreement keeps helping (301 of 1,334 and 150 of 1,317). Excluding copying costs the peer contract more in 896 instances (403 of the 1,349 with kappa = 1) and the audit-only contract the same as before.
- Verdict on H3: supported in the narrow form that the proposal's own refutation clause names. Agreement lowers cost only when cheats cannot coordinate on a shared trajectory; once copying equilibria are excluded and cheats copy perfectly, an agreement term is worth nothing beyond the audit. The wording "raises the minimum cost" is wrong as stated: adding a signal cannot raise the cost of a fixed equilibrium, it raises the cost only of the unique-equilibrium requirement. A first sweep with alpha uniform on [0, 1] showed a spurious saving at kappa = 1 (403 instances, up to 80%) from instances where a cheat agrees with an honest peer more than honest workers do, so that a penalty on agreement separates them; the cap fixes it, and the margin was lowered from 1e-4 to 1e-6 after savings of 0.2% at small p turned out to be the margin divided by p.
- Result H5 (`experiments/joint/h5.py`): the bound max(C, g/p - S), with p the one-sided resolution, is proved (docstring) and is never violated by the LP in 2,755 random feasible instances; it equals the LP in 2,410 of them and is exact for two actions with a binary audit (error 1e-13). H5 as written (a function of (g, tau, S) met within a constant factor) is refuted in general: the largest LP/bound ratio is 2,093, with three or four actions. On the measured skip actions with a tau = 0 audit the bound is exact. For the Gaussian score the bound is vacuous (1.0 against an LP cost of 5.76), since a Gaussian likelihood ratio reaches zero in the tail; a bound for noisy scores needs the LP dual, which is open.
- Not done and limits: two workers, one shared cheat, independent audits, binary agreement; no mixed equilibria in the copying exclusion; the random sweep draws parameters from ranges rather than measuring alpha, kappa and delta on real runs (milestone 3 gives delta only for fp16 drift); H4 (collusion), H6 and the paper are untouched; `test_margin.py` needs scikit-learn, absent from this session's interpreter.
- Next: milestone 6 (H4, brute-force equilibrium search over small coalitions) and measure alpha and kappa on real training trajectories.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4: single-check frontiers and a first look at H2
- Plan, written before the first run: take the measured skip family of milestone 3 as the worker's action set (action a skips every step whose honest update has norm at most tau'; effort is one minus the skipped share; cost is effort times the honest compute cost C). Solve the hidden-action linear programme (`src/vtm/contract.py`) for audit-only, score-only and joint contracts, with pay bounded below by minus the stake S. An audit re-executes m random steps and catches a skipped step iff its update norm exceeds the referee's tolerance tau; the score is the held-out loss on n_test examples (Gaussian with measured mean, seed variance and per-example variance). Prediction: audit-only is cheapest at tau = 0 (one audited step deters, because the penalty probability grows with the gain), becomes infeasible at any tau above benign drift because a cheaper action with the same signal exists, score-only is expensive because near actions are indistinguishable, and the joint contract beats both only where the score is precise. Refutation of H2: joint cost equals the lower envelope of the two everywhere.
- Checks of the solver first: the LP equals the closed form `max(C, C/p - S)` of `audit.threshold` on a grid of detection probabilities, equals a 2,500x2,500 brute-force grid search on 12 random two-action instances (within 1%), and never costs more when a signal is refined (`tests/test_contract.py`).
- Result, honest target (all workers must do every step; `experiments/frontier/`, 20 seeds per action, 400 score bins, S = 1 x C): at tau = 0 audit-only costs 1.001 C (one audited step, since the proportional penalty always deters, and the replication endpoint m = T costs 2.0 C). At every tau >= 1e-4 audit-only is infeasible at any stake and audit size, because skipping the steps below tau passes every audit and the pay cannot tell it from honest work. Score-only then costs 5.76 C at n_test = 540, 12.4 C at 100 and 1.23 C at 5,000; the best contract linear in the score costs 1.2e4 C (a slope large enough to punish 0.0002 of loss, because the 9.5% of work saved at tau' = 1e-3 moves the loss that little).
- H2: the joint contract is strictly cheaper than the better single check in 4 of 45 cells (stake 0.25 or 1, n_test = 5,000, tau 1e-4 or 1e-3: ratios 0.72 and 0.81, one or two audited steps added to the score), and equal in the other 41. In all four the audit-only cost is infinite, so the comparison is joint against score alone, and where both single checks are finite (tau = 0) the joint contract equals audit-only. Verdict: supported only in that narrow form; the audit helps the score where the score alone is almost precise enough, and does nothing at n_test = 540 or 100.
- Unplanned, added after seeing the table above and flagged as post hoc: pricing the full-effort target is the wrong question for a requester who only cares about the model. Section C of `results.txt` sets the target to the action that skips every step below tau' and sets the referee tolerance to the same tau'; then any skipping beyond it is caught with probability equal to its gain per audited step, one audited step at S = 1 already deters, and the requester's total cost is the target's effort plus 1/T (e.g. 0.698 C at tau' = 1e-2, which gives up 0.0034 of loss, 0.88 seed std; 0.468 C at 3e-2, 3.3 seed std). In this model the tolerance is a menu of quality-for-cost trades, not a leak, as long as the requester accepts the margin it sanctions.
- Not done and limits: the action set is the skip family only (half-batch and float16 shortcuts of milestone 3 cost nothing measurable, so a requester that accepts them is already inside the margin; a mixed action set is open); one dataset, one MLP, plain SGD; the score is Gaussian and independent of the audit given the action; one worker (no replication, so H3 and H4 untouched); detection of a skipped step is deterministic given its norm; the audit cost is the real re-execution cost 1/T per step but no cost for the referee's coordination or dispute resolution. H5 (lower bound) and H6 (predicted vs measured) are untested.
- Next: milestone 5 properly (H3, and a lower bound for H5 on this LP), and a mixed action set including half-batch and float16 so the sanctioned margin is priced across all three shortcuts.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3: benign drift and the hidden margin on real training (planned before running)
- Setting: a 64-64-10 ReLU MLP trained by minibatch SGD in float32 on the real `sklearn` digits data (1,797 images, 70/30 split), 20 seeds (init and batch order), T = 1,500 steps. A refereed check re-executes one step from the worker's checkpoint and accepts if the L2 distance between the worker's next checkpoint and the referee's is at most tau.
- Benign drift: the referee recomputes the step with the batch rows in a different order (float32 reduction order changes), and, as a heterogeneous-hardware case, with float16-rounded inputs and weights. I expect float32 reorder drift near 1e-7 of the update norm and the float16 case near 1e-3.
- Shortcut family "cheat when cheap": on each step the worker computes the honest update and a cheaper alternative (skip the step; use half the batch; compute with float16-rounded inputs and weights) and uses the alternative whenever its distance from the referee's honest recomputation is at most tau, so a single-step check passes it by construction.
- H1 prediction: the skip shortcut degrades held-out loss by more than the seed-to-seed standard deviation only once tau reaches the late-training update norms (1e-3 to 1e-2); at tau within a few multiples of float32 reorder drift the margin is real but its cost is below noise, so H1 is probably refuted for float32-only tolerances and supported for tolerances sized for float16 hardware. Refutation rule is the proposal's: no tau at or above measured drift with degradation above one seed std.
- Analysis: paired by seed, degradation = loss(cheating) minus loss(honest); 95% bootstrap interval of the mean over 20 seeds; fraction of steps cheated; false-dispute rate of honest workers as a function of tau. H6 (predicted vs measured degradation) is not tested in this step.
- Result (`experiments/margin/`, 20 seeds, honest held-out loss 0.0916, seed std 0.0039, accuracy 97.6%): honest drift is far below my guess for float32 reordering (max 5.7e-8 in L2, zero on every step in the median case, because the BLAS reduction was nearly order-independent here), and the float16 referee gives median 3.3e-5, p99 9.2e-4, max 9.1e-3 against honest update norms of median 0.026. The skip shortcut passes 3% of steps at tau = 1e-4, 9.5% at 1e-3, 30% at 1e-2 and 89% at 1e-1, and gives up loss 0.0002, 0.0034 (CI 0.0026 to 0.0042, just under one seed std) and 0.118 (CI 0.113 to 0.124, 30 seed stds) at tau 1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1. Half-batch and float16-input shortcuts pass 95% (half-batch, tau = 0.1) and 89% (float16 inputs, tau = 1e-4) of steps and cost nothing measurable (|degradation| < 0.001, half-batch even slightly negative at tau >= 0.1).
- Verdict on H1: supported only in a narrow form. Cheating passes at every tau, so the margin exists, but for a tolerance sized to benign drift (tau up to about 10 times the worst fp16 drift, 1e-2) the quality it gives up is below one seed std; the shortcut that hurts (skipping) needs tau about 10 times the worst benign drift. The proposal's refutation rule is not met (tau = 0.1 degrades by 30 stds), but the cost to the requester at drift-sized tolerances is compute saved by the worker (up to 30% of steps), not a worse model. The model should price the margin as skipped work, with a quality cost that switches on at a threshold, not as a smooth loss.
- Disclosure: the float16-input shortcut computes exactly what the fp16 referee computes, so against that referee it passes every step by construction; the tables and the figure use the reorder referee for it (the skip and half-batch rows are the same under both referees to three digits).
- A bug found and fixed before the final run: the referee's row permutation shared a random stream with batch sampling, so cheaters and honest workers saw different batches and the seed pairing broke; `test_zero_tolerance_cheater_is_honest` caught it.
- Not done: H6 (predicted vs measured degradation) is untested; one dataset, one small MLP, plain SGD with a cosine schedule; tau is in absolute L2 units of one step. Next: milestone 4 (single-check frontiers), and repeat on a second model and a real transformer step where an installable framework allows.
