## 2026-10-29 · Milestone 48: how much harm fits under the per-tensor bound when the adversary plants k weights, not one? (H4/H3 link, planned before running)
- Why: milestone 47's "Next". A single-weight plant under K x m_t (K = 4) harmed 0 of 20 even when chosen by gradient or by a 20-candidate loss search, so any residual room is in plants the bound does not limit: many weights, each inside its own tensor's bound. Design (`experiments/bit_flips_multi/run.py`): the protocol of milestone 47 at K = 4 only; at a random step the adversary overwrites k = 1, 4, 16, 64, 256 weights (of about 14k; zero-init tensors excluded). Sites: `randk` (k uniform weights, random signs: control) and `topk` (the k weights with the largest first-order loss rise g_i (v_i - w_i), v_i = -sign(g_i) K m_t). n = 20 shared (seed, step) draws per cell. Harm = |dL_heldout| > 0.01 or non-finite, as before.
- **Predictions, before running:** (1) k = 1 reproduces milestone 47: topk harm at most 0.10. (2) topk harm rises with k and is at least 0.50 at k = 256 (first-order rises add, and a 256-weight push of size up to 4 x m_t is a large step in the gradient direction). (3) randk harm at k = 256 stays at most 0.25 (random signs do not add coherently). (4) topk exceeds randk by at least 0.25 at the largest k where randk is still at most 0.10 (the gap is the gain from choosing weights by gradient). Refuted if topk harm at k = 256 is under 0.20: then even a coordinated 256-weight plant fits under the per-tensor bound, and the open room is only in plants of thousands of weights.
- Limits known in advance: one tiny model and recipe; K = 4 only (the smallest honest-safe factor measured in milestone 46); first-order selection, no curvature, no search over values inside the bound; n = 20 gives about +-0.2 intervals; the verifier here checks per-tensor maxima only, not an aggregate such as the norm of the update.

## 2026-10-29 · Milestone 47: how much harm fits under the per-tensor bound when the adversary picks the site by gradient? (H4/H3 link, planned before running)
- Why: milestone 46's "Next". Its 3% harm at K = 4 came from a random plant site; a strategic cheater chooses the site. Design (`experiments/bit_flips_worst/run.py`): at a random step the adversary overwrites one weight with a value inside K x m_t of its own tensor (K = 2, 4; n = 20 shared (seed, step) draws per K). Sites: `random` (control), `grad` (argmax of the first-order loss rise g_i (v - w_i), v = -sign(g_i) K m_t, from the step's batch gradient), `eval` (top 20 by that score, each tried, the largest actual batch loss kept).
- **Predictions, before running:** (1) random-site harm at K = 4 is at most 0.15 (milestone 46: 0.03). (2) grad-site harm at K = 4 is higher than random by at least 0.10, i.e. a gradient-chosen site recovers a good part of the room. (3) eval-site harm is at least as high as grad-site (up to one draw). (4) At K = 2 grad-site harm stays at or under 0.20: refuted above that, which would mean even the tightest honest-unsafe bound leaves room.
- Limits known in advance: one weight, one tiny model, first-order score and a 20-candidate search only; n = 20 gives about +-0.2 intervals; a multi-weight plant, which the bound does not limit, is untested.

### Outcome of 47 (`experiments/bit_flips_worst/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`, `run.out`; first pass without the zero-tensor exclusion kept as `results_all.txt`, `run_all.out`)
- Share harmful, n = 20 per cell (same draws for the three sites): K = 2: random 1 (0.05), grad 1 (0.05), eval 1 (0.05). K = 4: random 0, grad 0, eval 0. No non-finite outcome; median |dL| at most 0.0003.
- First pass (zero-init tensors allowed, where K x m_t = 0 so the "plant" is a zeroing): the same, 0 harmful at K = 4 for all sites. There the gradient score picked bias tensors (median m_t 0), a weak attack, so I excluded zero-bound tensors (they need an absolute bound, as milestone 46 said) and reran; the exclusion changes the picked site (now the embedding, median m_t 3.9, so the plant is up to 15.6) and not the harm count.
- **(1) held** (random 0.00 at K = 4). **(2) refuted:** grad-site harm at K = 4 is 0.00, not 0.10 above random. **(3) held** (eval = grad = 0). **(4) held** (0.05 at K = 2).
- Reading: against the per-tensor bound at K = 4, a first-order gradient search and a 20-candidate loss search found no single-weight plant that harms in 20 draws each, even though the picked weights (embedding entries up to about 15) are far larger than any honest value of that weight. With 0 of 20 the 95% upper bound is about 0.17, so this does not show the room is zero; it shows a single-weight plant is a poor attack here, and milestone 46's 1 of 40 is the same size of effect. The residual room under the per-tensor bound, if any, is in multi-weight or coordinated plants, which the bound does not limit.
- Limits: as stated before the run, plus: the first-order score ignores curvature and the embedding's downstream LayerNorm may mask large values (as milestone 42 found for activations); one model, one recipe, K = 2 and 4 only; the zero-bound tensors are excluded, not bounded.
- Next: a multi-weight plant under the per-tensor bound (k weights chosen by gradient, k = 1 to 64), which is the adversary the bound does not obviously limit; biases under an absolute bound; paper H4 paragraph gains milestones 43 to 47.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 46: does a per-tensor magnitude bound leave an adversary less room than the global bound? (H4/H3 link, planned before running)
- Why: milestone 45's "Next". Its bound K x (global init max|w|) is loose because one tensor (the embedding, max about 4) sets the global maximum while most tensors have maxima near 0.1. A verifier who recomputes the init can hold one maximum per tensor. Design (`experiments/bit_flips_tensor/run.py`): the plant protocol of milestone 45 with paired draws: the same (seed, step, weight, sign) is set once to a x m0 (global) and once to a x m_t (the init max|w| of the tensor that weight sits in), a = 1, 2, 4, 8, 40 draws each. Both values pass a bound K = a of their own kind, so the harm rate vs a is what an adversary gains under each bound. Only the fp32 weight site, d = 32, 4 seeds. (A smoke run at scale 0.1, 4 draws per cell, ran before this entry and showed 2 of 4 global plants harmful at a = 8 and none per-tensor; it is not used as a result.)
- **Predictions, before the full run:** (1) most planted weights sit in tensors whose maximum is under half the global one (the smoke run: 69%), so a per-tensor plant is on average far smaller in absolute value. (2) The per-tensor harm rate at a = 8 is under 0.20 (global about 0.5 in milestone 45). (3) Harm is not zero under the per-tensor bound: a plant of a x m_t in a sensitive small-scale tensor (a LayerNorm gain, whose init is 1, is a tensor with m_t = 1) can still harm; per-tensor harm at a = 8 is at least 0.05. (4) Paired: a draw harmful under the per-tensor plant is almost always harmful under the global one (fewer than 3 draws per a harmful only under per-tensor), since the per-tensor value is smaller in absolute terms; refuted otherwise.
- Limits known in advance: one tiny model; the verifier must hold per-tensor maxima of the init (recomputable from the public seed); one planted weight at a random site and step, so a gradient-chosen worst site is not tested; n = 40 per cell gives about +-0.15 intervals; a pre-trained start changes the references.

### Outcome of 46 (`experiments/bit_flips_tensor/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`, `run.out`; `growth.py`, `growth.txt` for the honest-growth control)
- Planted weights: 88% sit in a tensor whose init maximum is under half the global one (median m_t / m0 = 0.05). Share harmful, global a x m0 vs own-tensor a x m_t (paired, n = 40): a = 1: 2 (0.05) vs 0; a = 2: 0 vs 0; a = 4: 11 (0.28) vs 1 (0.03); a = 8: 13 (0.33) vs 1 (0.03). No draw was harmful only under the per-tensor plant.
- **(1) held** (0.88). **(2) held** (0.03 at a = 8, ceiling 0.20). **(3) refuted:** per-tensor harm at a = 8 is 0.03 (1 of 40), under the 0.05 floor I set; I expected a LayerNorm gain (m_t = 1) to be sensitive, but a plant of 8 there harmed in only one draw. **(4) held** (0 draws harmful only under per-tensor).
- Control (`growth.py`, clean runs, 84 nonzero-init tensor x seed pairs): final / init max|w| per tensor has median 1.35, 90th percentile 2.84, **maximum 3.89**; 38% of tensors exceed 1.5, 20% exceed 2, none exceed 4. So a per-tensor bound needs K >= 4 to accept honest work here (K = 2 would reject one honest tensor in five). The 36 zero-init tensors (biases) have no relative bound at all; they grow to a median 0.06, max 0.47.
- Reading: at the smallest honest-safe factor K = 4, a per-tensor bound admits plants that harm 3% of jobs (1 of 40) where the global K = 4 bound admits 28% (11 of 40). So a verifier who holds one init maximum per tensor removes most of the room that milestone 45's global bound left, at no cost to honest runs here. It is still a filter, not a deterrent: an adversary plants just under K x m_t in the one tensor that is sensitive, and biases need an absolute bound.
- Limits: as stated before the run, plus: 3 planted draws landed in zero-init tensors, where a x m_t = 0 (a no-op plant, counted unharmful); honest growth was measured for one training recipe, and a longer or higher-lr run grows tensors more, so K = 4 is a measured value for this recipe, not a constant; n = 40 gives about +-0.15, so 0.03 vs 0.28 is a clear gap but 1 of 40 vs 0 of 40 is not; the sensitive site was random, not searched.
- Next: a gradient-chosen worst-case site under the per-tensor K = 4 bound; honest per-tensor growth at longer horizons; paper H4 paragraph gains milestones 43 to 46.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 45: does the magnitude-bound check survive a reference a verifier can actually hold, and how much harm fits under the bound? (H4/H3 link, planned before running)
- Why: milestone 44's "Next". Its bound used the true max|w| of the previous checkpoint, which a verifier without a trusted checkpoint lacks. Part A re-scores the same 240 fp32 flips (rng seed 43) against references a verifier can hold: max|w| at initialisation (recomputable from the public seed) and fixed absolute bounds B = 1 and 10, K = 1, 2, 4, 10, 100. Part B is the adversary: plant +-a x m0 (m0 = init max|w|) in one random weight at a random step, a = 1 to 128, 20 draws each, and read off the harm rate vs a, i.e. what a cheater gains by staying under a bound K x m0.
- **Predictions, before running:** (1) init max|w| is within a factor 2 of the clean final max|w| in every seed (milestone 44 saw max|w| nearly constant), so the init reference catches the same harmful flips as the true previous maximum at K = 4 and 10 (catch rate >= 0.95). (2) A fixed B = 10 catches every harmful flip at K = 1 (the same flips make weights above 1e3); B = 1 may false-alarm only on corrupted submissions, as before. (3) Planted values are harmless at a = 1 and 2 (harm rate <= 0.10) and harmful in most draws (>= 0.5) by a = 128, so there is a threshold a* between them: bounds with K < a* admit no harmful plant, K >= a* admit them. (4) a* is above 8: a weight of eight times the largest init weight is absorbed by LayerNorm and the rest of the network; refuted if the harm rate at a = 8 is above 0.25.
- Limits known in advance: one tiny model; the init scale of this model is a property of the init rule (a different init or a pre-trained start moves the reference); one planted weight, random site and step (a worst-case site would harm more); n = 20 per a gives about +-0.2 intervals; weights only; the clean-run false-positive side is trivial here because max|w| barely grows.

### Outcome of 45 (`experiments/bit_flips_ref/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`, `run.out`; Part A 120 fp32 flips, 15 harmful, same rng seed 43 as milestones 43/44; Part B 8 values of a x 20 plants)
- Clean runs: init max|w| 4.10 / 3.95 / 3.70 / 3.85 and final max|w| 4.09 / 3.95 / 3.72 / 3.85 for the four seeds, so the largest weight is set by the init and does not move in training.
- Part A: the init-scale reference, B = 1 and B = 10 each catch **15 of 15** harmful flips at every K in {1, 2, 4, 10, 100}, as the true previous maximum does. Flagged share of all flips 0.13 (0.17 for B = 1 at K = 1). The 15 harmful flips match milestone 44's fp32 row.
- Part B (plant +-a x m0, one random weight and step, n = 20): share harmful a = 1: 0.10, 2: 0.05, 4: 0.30, 8: 0.50, 16: 0.60, 32: 0.55, 64: 0.60, 128: 0.55; non-finite final loss in 0 or 1 of 20 per a.
- **(1) held** (init within 1% of final max; catch 1.00). **(2) held.** **(3) held** (0.10 and 0.05 at a = 1, 2; 0.55 at a = 128), with a plateau of about 0.55 to 0.6 from a = 8, not a rise. **(4) refuted:** harm at a = 8 is 0.50 and already 0.30 at a = 4, above the 0.25 ceiling; the threshold a* is between 2 and 4, not above 8.
- Reading: a bound K x (init max|w|) with K = 2 catches all 15 harmful random flips and leaves an adversary a plant of at most 2 x m0 with harm 0.05 (1 of 20); with K >= 4 a planted weight harms 30% to 60% of jobs. The check is cheap and needs only the public init, but it filters faults rather than deterring a strategic cheater, who plants just under the bound; K must sit close to honest growth (about 1 here).
- Limits: as stated before the run, plus: this model's global max|w| (about 4) is far above typical weights, so a global bound is loose; a per-tensor bound would be tighter and was not tested; the a = 1 harm of 0.10 (2 of 20) is within the +-0.2 noise of n = 20 but says a value equal to the init maximum can matter in a sensitive weight.
- Next: per-tensor bounds; a worst-case plant site chosen by gradient; the paper's H4 paragraph gains milestones 43 to 45.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 44: how many harmful silent bit flips does a magnitude-bound check on the weights catch? (H4, planned before running)
- Why: milestone 43's "Next". A finiteness check catches none of fp16's harmful flips, so test the check milestone 43 proposed: reject a submission if the flipped weight is non-finite or |w| > K x max|w| of the previous checkpoint (K = 1.5, 2, 4, 10, 100). Same flips as milestone 43 (same rng seed 43, same protocol, 3 formats, d = 32, 4 seeds). Also measure the false-positive side: in the 4 clean runs, the growth ratio of max|w| between checkpoints 25 steps apart (a bound K below this ratio would reject honest work).
- **Predictions, before running:** (1) at K = 10 the check catches at least 90% of harmful exp_hi flips in every format (a harmful flip makes a weight >= 1e3 or non-finite, against a clean max of order 1). (2) Clean growth of max|w| over 25 steps stays under 1.5 in every run, so K >= 1.5 has no false positives on honest training here. (3) The check flags (K = 10) no sign, exp_lo or man_hi flip in fp32/bf16 (they change a value by at most a factor 2 or a tiny amount). (4) Harmful flips the check misses at K = 10 are under 10% of all harmful flips; refuted above that.
- Limits known in advance: the reference is the true max of the previous checkpoint, which a verifier with only the submitted weights does not have (it would need a trusted reference scale, e.g. a replica or the init); one flip per job; weights only; tiny model; counts at n = 60 are small.

### Outcome of 44 (`experiments/bit_flips_bound/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`, `run.out`; same 360 flips as milestone 43)
- Harmful exp_hi flips (n = 60 per format): fp32 15, bf16 13, fp16 14. Catch rate of the harmful ones at K = 1.5, 2, 4, 10: **1.00 in every format**; at K = 100 fp32 and bf16 1.00, fp16 13 of 14 (0.93): one fp16 flip made a weight of about 10 to 100 times the clean maximum and still harmed. No sign, exp_lo or man_hi flip was harmful (0 of 60 per format) and none was flagged.
- Flagged share of all exp_hi flips at K = 10: fp32 0.267, bf16 0.250, fp16 0.317, against harmful shares of 0.250, 0.217, 0.233: the check also rejects 1 to 5 harmless flips per format (a big but benign value, e.g. in a weight the network barely uses), i.e. false alarms among corrupted submissions, not on honest ones.
- Clean growth of max|w| over 25 steps: max 1.001, 99th percentile 1.000 (n = 92 intervals). So **(2) held** trivially, but it says the largest weight in this model barely moves during training, so any K >= 1.5 is far from honest behaviour here.
- **(1) held** (100% at K = 10, all formats). **(3) held** (nothing flagged outside exp_hi). **(4) held** (0 missed at K = 10).
- Reading: against single flips, a magnitude bound relative to the previous checkpoint's maximum catches every harmful flip at K <= 10 in all three formats, so the residual silent-finite harm that milestone 40 priced is removable by a cheap check, and the replication placement of milestone 40 is an upper bound on what replication has to cover. This does not cover multi-bit or many-weight corruption, small harmful drifts (a flip that is finite and under K x max is untested because none harmed here), or an adversary: a strategic cheater stays under the bound.
- Limits: as stated before the run, plus: the reference scale is the true previous maximum, which a verifier lacks unless it holds a trusted checkpoint; the clean maximum is nearly constant for this tiny model so the false-positive side is not stressed; n = 60 per format gives intervals of about +-0.11 on each share, and "100%" of 13 to 15 harmful flips has a lower 95% bound of about 0.78.
- Next: a verifier-realistic reference (init scale or a replica) instead of the true previous maximum; a stronger adversary against the bound (H3/H4 link); paper H4 paragraph gains milestones 40 to 44.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 43: does a 16-bit stored format change the harm of one silent weight bit flip? (H4, planned before running)
- Why: milestone 42's "Next". Production weights are often stored in bf16 or fp16; the harm of a flip depends on how large a value one exponent bit can create. fp32 and bf16 share an 8-bit exponent (top flip gives about 1.7e38); fp16 has 5 (top flip of a value near 0.5 gives 32768, finite, and only an all-ones exponent gives inf/NaN).
- Design (`experiments/bit_flips_format/run.py`): protocol of milestone 42, weight site only; the flipped weight is round-tripped through fp32, bf16 or fp16 (one bit of that format's pattern flipped), training stays fp32. 60 high-exponent, 20 each low-exponent, sign, top-mantissa flips per format, 4 seeds.
- **Predictions, before running:** (1) fp32 reproduces milestone 42's weight row (exp_hi harm within 0.10 of 0.200). (2) bf16 matches fp32 within sampling error (same exponent width): |difference| in exp_hi harm below 0.15. (3) fp16 has a lower non-finite share than fp32 among exp_hi flips (under 0.07, since inf needs all five exponent bits set) but its total exp_hi harm is not lower than 0.10, because a finite 1e3 to 6e4 weight still wrecks the model. (4) sign, exp_lo and top-mantissa flips cause no harm at any format.
- Limits known in advance: storage-only model of a narrower format (no fp16 training, no loss scaling); one tiny model; n = 60 gives about +-0.11 intervals; q still unmeasured.

### Outcome of 43 (`experiments/bit_flips_format/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`, `run.out`; 3 formats, 120 flips each, 4 seeds, d = 32)
- Columns are non-finite / finite harm / any harm among exp_hi flips, n = 60 per format: fp32 0.150 / 0.100 / 0.250; bf16 0.117 / 0.100 / 0.217; fp16 0.000 / 0.233 / 0.233. exp_lo, sign and top-mantissa flips: 0 of 20 harmful in every format and class.
- **(1) held:** fp32 exp_hi harm 0.250 vs milestone 42's weight row 0.200 (band 0.10; different random draw of flips). **(2) held:** bf16 vs fp32 differ by 0.033 (band 0.15). **(3) held:** fp16 has no non-finite outcome (0 of 60, bound 0.07) but its exp_hi harm is 0.233, not lower than 0.10: a flip that creates a finite weight of 1e3 to 3e4 harms as often as one that creates inf. **(4) held.**
- Reading: a narrower exponent removes the inf/NaN failures but not the harm, so a finiteness check on submitted weights would catch none of fp16's harmful flips; a magnitude bound (e.g. |w| below a few times the clean maximum) is the check that carries over across formats. The check on the real clean-weight maximum was not run here.
- Limits: as stated before the run; at n = 60 the intervals are about +-0.11, so fp32, bf16 and fp16 totals (0.25, 0.22, 0.23) are indistinguishable; storage-only model, training stays fp32.
- Next: measure a magnitude-bound check's catch rate on these flips; fp16 training with loss scaling.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 42: do silent bit flips in gradients and activations harm training more than flips in weights? (H4, planned before running)
- Why: milestones 40 and 41 flipped one bit of one weight (P(harm | flip) 0.03 to 0.045, almost all high-exponent flips). A real silent data corruption can also hit the activations of the forward pass or the gradient before the update. Same protocol (d = 32, 600 steps of SGD, 4 seeds, final held-out loss vs the clean run of the same seed, harm = |dL| > 0.01 or non-finite), three sites: weight (re-run as control), gradient (one element of the flattened gradient at a random step), activation (one element of a random block's output at a random step; the flip then propagates through the loss, the gradient and the update). Bit classes exp_hi (27-30) 60, exp_lo (23-26) 20, sign 20, man_hi (16-22) 20 per site.
- **Predictions, before running:** (1) the weight control reproduces milestone 41 (overall harm over bits 16-31 within 0.02 to 0.07). (2) A gradient flip is less harmful than a weight flip per exp_hi flip: the weight flip persists in the model, a gradient flip is one bad update that later steps (and the shrinking cosine step size) can correct, unless it is non-finite or so large that it overwrites the weight (a 1e38 gradient times lr is itself a 1e37 weight): non-finite share of exp_hi gradient flips is at least as large as for weights (refuted if more than 10 points lower). (3) Activation flips are harmful in a larger share than weight flips (the corrupted value passes through a LayerNorm and the softmax and is multiplied into every downstream gradient): exp_hi harm at least 0.35; refuted if under 0.25. (4) exp_lo, sign and man_hi flips at any site cause finite harm in under 10% of cases.
- Limits known in advance: one tiny model (d = 32), plain SGD, fp32, a single flip per job, flips only in block outputs (not attention scores or the loss); 20 flips per low-bit class gives wide intervals; q is still not measured.
### Outcome (`experiments/bit_flips_site/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`, `run.out`; 3 sites, 120 faulty runs each, 4 seeds, d = 32, 32,801 parameters)
- Columns are non-finite / finite harm / any harm among exp_hi (27-30) flips, n = 60 per site: weight 0.133 / 0.067 / 0.200; gradient 0.150 / 0.100 / 0.250; activation 0.083 / 0.000 / 0.083. exp_lo, sign and man_hi: 0 of 60 harmful at every site. Overall P(harm) over bits 16-31 (mantissa 0-15 counted 0): weight 0.025, gradient 0.031, activation 0.010.
- **(1) held:** the weight control gives 0.025 (band 0.02 to 0.07), close to milestone 41's 0.027 to 0.040.
- **(2) the stated criterion held, the motivating claim did not:** gradient non-finite share 0.150 is not below the weight's 0.133. But I expected gradient flips to be less harmful overall, and they are not: 0.25 against 0.20 exp_hi harm, with an interval of about +-0.11 each, so no difference is detected. A bad gradient is not self-correcting on this run length; a 1e38 gradient times lr overwrites the weight, as predicted.
- **(3) refuted:** activation exp_hi harm is 0.083 (5 of 60, all non-finite), under the 0.25 floor; activation flips are the least harmful site, not the most. Reason, checked rather than assumed: about 80% of block-output values have |x| < 2, where flipping exponent bit 30 gives inf/NaN (checked directly: 1.49 -> NaN, loss NaN), while a large finite activation (bits 27-29) is absorbed by the next LayerNorm, so it leaves the loss unchanged. The residual stream's LayerNorm is a natural fault mask.
- **(4) held:** 0 of 60 finite harm in exp_lo, sign and man_hi at every site.
- Reading: P(harm | flip) is 1% to 3% over bits 16-31 whichever of the three sites is hit, and the part that matters is a high-exponent flip that makes a value inf/NaN; a finiteness check on the submitted weights (and on loss/gradient norms during training) would catch most of what harms. This does not change the f = q x P(harm) placement of milestone 40 (P(harm) 0.045 -> between 0.010 and 0.031 here). Not tested: bf16/fp16 training (smaller exponent range, so more flips overflow), optimiser state, attention scores, larger models.
- Limits: as stated before the run; intervals at n = 60 are about +-0.11, so the ordering gradient > weight > activation is suggestive only.
- Next: fp16/bf16 (does a narrower exponent change the non-finite share?); the paper's H4 paragraph gains milestones 40 to 42.
## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 41: does the harm of one silent bit flip fall as the model grows? (H4, planned before running)
- Why: milestone 40 measured P(harm | flip) = 0.045 on a 32,801-parameter transformer and flagged that a real flip lands on one of billions of weights, so the per-flip share may not carry over. Repeat the protocol at embedding width d = 16, 32, 64, 128 (about 9k to 400k parameters; 2 layers, 2 heads, same data, 600 steps of SGD, 4 seeds), flipping one bit of one random weight at a random step, bit classes exp_hi (27-30), exp_lo (23-26), sign, man_hi (16-22); mantissa 0-15 is omitted (0 of 100 harmful at d = 32). Harm = |final held-out loss change| > 0.01 or non-finite.
- **Predictions, before running:** (1) the non-finite share of exp_hi flips does not fall with width (a flip to about 1e38 in any weight that is used blows up the forward pass); its slope in log2(d) is within +-0.1 of zero. (2) The finite-harm share of exp_lo and sign flips (2% and 1% at d = 32) stays at or under 10% at every width, and is non-increasing from d = 32 to d = 128 up to sampling noise. (3) Overall harm over bits 16-31 stays between 0.02 and 0.08 at every width, so the 0.045 figure of milestone 40 is not an artefact of the small model; refuted outside that range at any width. (4) No width has a clean-run loss spread above 0.03 across seeds large enough to mask harm at the 0.01 tolerance (check, not a result).
- Limits known in advance: widths stop at 128 (about 400k parameters), far below production scale, so this is a trend over 8x in parameters, not a claim about billions; 60 exp_hi flips per width gives a binomial interval of about +-0.12 at 0.35; 4 seeds; weights only; one flip per job.

### Outcome (`experiments/bit_flips_size/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`, `run.out`; 4 widths, 120 faulty runs each, 4 seeds, 10,305 to 425,825 parameters)

## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 34: what does it cost to keep the auditor out of the coalition? (H4, planned before running)

## 2026-10-25 · Milestone 20: is the stale cheat expensive because reuse chains across steps? (H1), planned before running
- Why: milestone 19 found stale costs +0.0177 (2.1 seed std) at tau = 1e-2 and the mechanism was a guess. One candidate: an accepted stale gradient becomes the next step's "previous gradient", so reuse can chain over consecutive steps and drift further from the true gradient. `stale1` (margin_tf.py) allows reuse only of an honestly computed gradient, so a chain has length 1. Code: `experiments/transformer_stale_chain/run.py`; same setup as milestone 19 (600 steps, 10 seeds, fixed split, reorder referee, same taus).
- Prediction: stale chains are long at 1e-2 (mean chain above 1.5, max above 5) and `stale1` is accepted on fewer steps (under 8% at 1e-2, vs 13.6%) and costs under a third of stale: degradation at 1e-2 below +0.006 (stale: +0.0177) with `stale1` CI overlapping one seed std (0.0083) or less. Refutation of the chaining explanation: `stale1` degradation at 1e-2 above +0.012, or within 30% of stale's.
- Analysis: as milestone 19 plus mean and max accepted-chain length. Not tested: other models, a cap above 1, momentum.

### Outcome (`experiments/transformer_stale_chain/`: `run.py`, `results.txt`, `results.json`; 10 seeds, fixed split, honest loss 2.7252, seed std 0.0083)
- Stale reuse chains: mean accepted-chain length 25.3 / 43.5 / 13.0 / 5.4 steps at tau 1e-3 / 1e-2 / 3e-2 / 1e-1, longest 26 / 83 / 108 / 137 (the prediction of mean above 1.5 and max above 5 at 1e-2 was far too low). A chain is a run of consecutive accepted reuses, so the cheater skips every backward pass for dozens of steps.
- `stale1` (reuse only after an honest step), accepted share 2.2 / 6.9 / 12.1 / 22.9%; degradation (95% CI) +0.00001, **+0.00016 (+0.00004 to +0.00026)**, +0.00014, +0.00125 (+0.00071 to +0.00173). At tau = 1e-2 that is 1% of stale's +0.01765 and 0.02 seed std; at 1e-1 it is 0.15 std against stale's 187 std.
- Verdict: **prediction held** (accepted 6.9% < 8%; degradation < +0.006; refutation threshold +0.012 not reached). A rule that forbids reusing a submitted gradient (the prover must show a fresh forward pass, for instance) removes it in this setup.
- Limits: this shows the chain is what costs, not that the no-chain rule is enforceable (stale1 is an idealised cheater that obeys it, no enforcement mechanism is modelled); caps above 1 not tried; same 30k-parameter model, 600 steps, plain SGD.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6: collusion on a shared trajectory (H4), planned before running
- Setting: m workers on one job, each submits honest (cost 1) or the shared cheap trajectory K (cost 1 - g). Outputs are compared; the strict-majority output is accepted and its submitters are paid r = 1 (individual rationality), submitters of any other output (or all, if there is no strict majority) get -S. One independent audit per job catches K with probability p, and then every K submitter gets -S. Brute force: for m = 1 to 7 and a grid of (g, p), find the least S at which all-honest is a strong equilibrium (no subset of workers has a joint deviation, over all 2^|subset| action profiles, that leaves every member strictly better off), by bisection over exact payoffs.
- Closed form to test (my derivation, not the proposal's 1/m): a minority deviation is punished by replication alone, and a majority coalition of size floor(m/2)+1 escapes replication with probability one and is deterred only by the audit: p (r + S) >= g, i.e. S* = max(0, g/p - 1) for every m >= 1, with no dependence on m.
- Predictions: (1) a coalition of floor(m/2)+1 workers escapes replication (trivially true); (2) S*(m) is flat in m, so the proposal's "falls as 1/m" is refuted for a per-job audit and belongs to the verifier-bribery setting, where a briber must pay each of m verifiers; (3) replication multiplies the pay bill by m and lowers the needed stake by nothing, so against a perfectly coordinating coalition it is dominated by m = 1 plus the audit. Refutation of my closed form: any (m, g, p) where brute force differs from it by more than the bisection tolerance.
- Also checked: the threshold is unchanged if the audit is drawn once per job rather than per worker (payoffs are linear in the caught indicator).
- Result (`experiments/collusion/`, 140 cells: m 1 to 7, g in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9}, p in {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1}; 14 tests pass with `tests/test_coalition.py`): brute-force strong-equilibrium search matches max(0, g/p - 1) to 7e-10 in every cell. The smallest coalition that escapes replication is floor(m/2)+1 (1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4 for m = 1 to 7). The stake is flat in m (g = 0.5, p = 0.1: 4.00 for every m), so the proposal's `1/m` fall (which would give 0.57 at m = 7) is refuted for a per-job audit; the pay bill grows as m.
- Verdict on H4: the first half (a coalition escapes replication with probability one) holds, with the coalition needing a strict majority. The second half, as worded, is refuted by construction in this model: a coordinating majority faces only the audit, so replication adds no deterrence beyond it; the `1/m` law belongs to verifier-bribery, where a briber pays each of m verifiers separately. Caveat on strength: the flat-in-m result follows from the payoff structure (it is a theorem here, proved in the docstring argument, and the search confirms the code implements it), so it is a check of the model rather than a discovery; what would make `1/m` appear is a different model (side payments, a split penalty budget, per-verifier bribes), not tested.
- Not done and limits: pure strategies and one shared cheat only (no mixed coalition equilibria, no partial copying kappa < 1, no benign drift delta, no side payments, no coalition that also bribes the auditor); majority rule only (no unanimity or reference-answer pay); the parameters are swept, not measured. H6 and the paper remain.
- Next: milestone 7, the paper skeleton in `paper/` (EC format), and H6 (predicted against measured degradation); a model variant with side payments to see when a `1/m` law does appear.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 5: peer agreement (H3) and a one-sided lower bound (H5), planned before running
- H3 setting: two workers, each picks honest (cost 1) or a cheat (cost 1 - g, audit catch probability p, one shared deterministic shortcut). Each is paid w(audit outcome, agree or disagree) >= -S, four numbers. Agreement probabilities: honest-honest 1 - delta (benign drift beyond the tolerance), honest-cheat alpha (the cheat's output stays within the agreement test), cheat-cheat kappa (kappa = 1 is the copying equilibrium). The cheapest contract is a linear programme in the four payments. Three versions: audit-only (w does not depend on agreement), peer contract that only requires honest-honest to be an equilibrium, and peer contract that also requires (cheat, cheat) not to be an equilibrium (strictly, margin 1e-6).
- Prediction: the first peer version never costs more (more signals cannot raise the cost: a refinement), and is strictly cheaper when alpha < 1 and kappa < 1, so the proposal's wording of H3 ("raises the minimum cost") holds only for the third version. In the third version at kappa = 1 I expect agreement to be worth nothing (cost equal to audit-only everywhere on a random sweep), because paying for agreement makes (cheat, cheat) more attractive. Refutation of H3 as stated: some instance with kappa = 1 where the third version is strictly cheaper than audit-only.
- H5 plan: prove a one-sided resolution bound (any contract implementing honest against a deviation with gain g, pay floor -S and one-sided resolution p, where p = 1 - min_s P_a(s)/P_h(s), costs at least max(C, g/p - S)), test LP >= L on random instances and on the measured skip actions, and report how loose it is where the score is Gaussian (I expect it to be vacuous there, since a Gaussian likelihood ratio reaches zero in the tail).
- Result H3 (`experiments/joint/h3.py`, 4,000 random instances, g, p, S, delta, alpha, kappa; alpha capped at 1 - delta, since a cheat should not agree with an honest peer more often than an honest worker does): version 2 (honest-honest only) is cheaper than audit-only in 1,191 instances and never dearer (as a refinement must be), up to 97% cheaper; version 3 (also exclude copying) is cheaper in 451 of 4,000 but in 0 of 1,349 instances with kappa = 1, where the contracts cost the same. Where cheats do not coordinate (kappa 0 or 0.5) agreement keeps helping (301 of 1,334 and 150 of 1,317). Excluding copying costs the peer contract more in 896 instances (403 of the 1,349 with kappa = 1) and the audit-only contract the same as before.
- Verdict on H3: supported in the narrow form that the proposal's own refutation clause names. Agreement lowers cost only when cheats cannot coordinate on a shared trajectory; once copying equilibria are excluded and cheats copy perfectly, an agreement term is worth nothing beyond the audit. The wording "raises the minimum cost" is wrong as stated: adding a signal cannot raise the cost of a fixed equilibrium, it raises the cost only of the unique-equilibrium requirement. A first sweep with alpha uniform on [0, 1] showed a spurious saving at kappa = 1 (403 instances, up to 80%) from instances where a cheat agrees with an honest peer more than honest workers do, so that a penalty on agreement separates them; the cap fixes it, and the margin was lowered from 1e-4 to 1e-6 after savings of 0.2% at small p turned out to be the margin divided by p.
- Result H5 (`experiments/joint/h5.py`): the bound max(C, g/p - S), with p the one-sided resolution, is proved (docstring) and is never violated by the LP in 2,755 random feasible instances; it equals the LP in 2,410 of them and is exact for two actions with a binary audit (error 1e-13). H5 as written (a function of (g, tau, S) met within a constant factor) is refuted in general: the largest LP/bound ratio is 2,093, with three or four actions. On the measured skip actions with a tau = 0 audit the bound is exact. For the Gaussian score the bound is vacuous (1.0 against an LP cost of 5.76), since a Gaussian likelihood ratio reaches zero in the tail; a bound for noisy scores needs the LP dual, which is open.
- Not done and limits: two workers, one shared cheat, independent audits, binary agreement; no mixed equilibria in the copying exclusion; the random sweep draws parameters from ranges rather than measuring alpha, kappa and delta on real runs (milestone 3 gives delta only for fp16 drift); H4 (collusion), H6 and the paper are untouched; `test_margin.py` needs scikit-learn, absent from this session's interpreter.
- Next: milestone 6 (H4, brute-force equilibrium search over small coalitions) and measure alpha and kappa on real training trajectories.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4: single-check frontiers and a first look at H2

Prediction: audit-only is cheapest at tau = 0 (one audited step deters, because the penalty probability grows with the gain), becomes infeasible at any tau above benign drift because a cheaper action with the same signal exists, score-only is expensive because near actions are indistinguishable, and the joint contract beats both only where the score is precise. Score-only then costs 5.76 C at n_test = 540, 12.4 C at 100 and 1.23 C at 5,000; the best contract linear in the score costs 1.2e4 C (a slope large enough to punish 0.0002 of loss, because the 9.5% of work saved at tau' = 1e-3 moves the loss that little).

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3: benign drift and the hidden margin on real training (planned before running)
- Setting: a 64-64-10 ReLU MLP trained by minibatch SGD in float32 on the real `sklearn` digits data (1,797 images, 70/30 split), 20 seeds (init and batch order), T = 1,500 steps. A refereed check re-executes one step from the worker's checkpoint and accepts if the L2 distance between the worker's next checkpoint and the referee's is at most tau.
- Benign drift: the referee recomputes the step with the batch rows in a different order (float32 reduction order changes), and, as a heterogeneous-hardware case, with float16-rounded inputs and weights. I expect float32 reorder drift near 1e-7 of the update norm and the float16 case near 1e-3.
- Shortcut family "cheat when cheap": on each step the worker computes the honest update and a cheaper alternative (skip the step; use half the batch; compute with float16-rounded inputs and weights) and uses the alternative whenever its distance from the referee's honest recomputation is at most tau, so a single-step check passes it by construction.
- H1 prediction: the skip shortcut degrades held-out loss by more than the seed-to-seed standard deviation only once tau reaches the late-training update norms (1e-3 to 1e-2); at tau within a few multiples of float32 reorder drift the margin is real but its cost is below noise, so H1 is probably refuted for float32-only tolerances and supported for tolerances sized for float16 hardware. Refutation rule is the proposal's: no tau at or above measured drift with degradation above one seed std.
- Analysis: paired by seed, degradation = loss(cheating) minus loss(honest); 95% bootstrap interval of the mean over 20 seeds; fraction of steps cheated; false-dispute rate of honest workers as a function of tau. H6 (predicted vs measured degradation) is not tested in this step.
- Result (`experiments/margin/`, 20 seeds, honest held-out loss 0.0916, seed std 0.0039, accuracy 97.6%): honest drift is far below my guess for float32 reordering (max 5.7e-8 in L2, zero on every step in the median case, because the BLAS reduction was nearly order-independent here), and the float16 referee gives median 3.3e-5, p99 9.2e-4, max 9.1e-3 against honest update norms of median 0.026. The skip shortcut passes 3% of steps at tau = 1e-4, 9.5% at 1e-3, 30% at 1e-2 and 89% at 1e-1, and gives up loss 0.0002, 0.0034 (CI 0.0026 to 0.0042, just under one seed std) and 0.118 (CI 0.113 to 0.124, 30 seed stds) at tau 1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1. Half-batch and float16-input shortcuts pass 95% (half-batch, tau = 0.1) and 89% (float16 inputs, tau = 1e-4) of steps and cost nothing measurable (|degradation| < 0.001, half-batch even slightly negative at tau >= 0.1).
- Verdict on H1: supported only in a narrow form. Cheating passes at every tau, so the margin exists, but for a tolerance sized to benign drift (tau up to about 10 times the worst fp16 drift, 1e-2) the quality it gives up is below one seed std; the shortcut that hurts (skipping) needs tau about 10 times the worst benign drift. The proposal's refutation rule is not met (tau = 0.1 degrades by 30 stds), but the cost to the requester at drift-sized tolerances is compute saved by the worker (up to 30% of steps), not a worse model. The model should price the margin as skipped work, with a quality cost that switches on at a threshold, not as a smooth loss.
- Disclosure: the float16-input shortcut computes exactly what the fp16 referee computes, so against that referee it passes every step by construction; the tables and the figure use the reorder referee for it (the skip and half-batch rows are the same under both referees to three digits).
- A bug found and fixed before the final run: the referee's row permutation shared a random stream with batch sampling, so cheaters and honest workers saw different batches and the seed pairing broke; `test_zero_tolerance_cheater_is_honest` caught it.
- Not done: H6 (predicted vs measured degradation) is untested; one dataset, one small MLP, plain SGD with a cosine schedule; tau is in absolute L2 units of one step. Next: milestone 4 (single-check frontiers), and repeat on a second model and a real transformer step where an installable framework allows.
