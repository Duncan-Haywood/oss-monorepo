# When is a linear optimal power flow safe to dispatch from, and which cheap AC repair makes it safe?

**Live page:** https://marisstella.app/research/grid-linopf/

**Status: open research programme (opened 2026-11-12), track 21 (`infra`, grid first).** Markets and
grid operators dispatch generators with a linear (DC) optimal power flow. This programme measures, on
the PGLib-OPF benchmark library with its published thermal and angle limits, how often that dispatch
breaks a limit once the AC physics is applied, and how many AC power flows a repair needs to make it
feasible at the AC optimum's cost, with the repair's rules frozen on one set and scored on held-out
sets. Plan, related work and hypotheses: [`PROPOSAL.md`](PROPOSAL.md); log: [`LOG.md`](LOG.md).
Experiments run in [`research/infra-dcopf`](../infra-dcopf) (13 steps since 2026-10-21).

**Results so far (modelled on PGLib-OPF v23.07 with PYPOWER; AC optima within 0.005 % of the published values; voltages held at the AC optimum's schedule unless stated):**
- The plain DC dispatch overloads branches on **all 11** congested (API) cases with an AC optimum; derating or iterated loss and rating feedback repairs 1 of 11 ([`infra-dcopf`](../infra-dcopf/README.md)).
- A trust-region successive linearisation of the AC power flow (loss factors and flow sensitivities by finite differences, a QP, an elastic fallback) repairs **7 of 11** API cases at the AC optimum's cost; its rules were set on those cases.
- **Held-out (milestone 1, 2026-11-12):** on the 12 angle-limited SAD cases, the same rules plus two linearised angle rows per branch repair **7 of the 11** with an AC optimum, the tuning set's rate; the angle-blind version repairs 3, and its dispatches sit 6.5 to 17.6 % below the optimum's cost because they break up to 16 angle limits. The misses end within 0.01 % of a limit ([`results_sad.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_sad.txt)).
- **Without the oracle voltages (milestone 3, 2026-11-12):** at PGLib's own 1.0 pu generator setpoints the same repair clears **2 of 11** API and **2 of 11** SAD cases, and none of its dispatches meets voltage and Q limits; an AC OPF over voltages only, after it, makes 16 of 34 cases fully feasible but at a mean 0.8 to 4.3 % above the optimum's cost ([`results_vsched.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_vsched.txt)). The repair has to move voltages with P.
- **Voltages as decision variables (milestone 3b, 2026-11-12):** a repair that moves the generator voltage setpoints with P, with voltage and Q limits as linear rows, run from the 1.0 pu start, is fully feasible on only 5 of 34 cases as pre-registered. It ends within 0.1 % of every limit on 19 but rarely strictly inside. With a small margin inside every limit, a variant chosen after that result, it is fully feasible on **18 of 34**, and on **28 of 34** with the voltage-only stage after it, at a mean **0.17 to 0.34 %** above the AC optimum's cost (milestone 3's best: 16 at 0.8 to 4.3 %). This is tuned on the cases it is scored on; a held-out run is next ([`results_vlin_margin.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_vlin_margin.txt)).
- **Analytic sensitivities (milestone 2, 2026-11-13):** taking the repair's sensitivities from the power-flow Jacobian (one sparse solve per round, matching central differences to 1.3e-5) leaves the outcome counts where they were: **19 of 34** fully feasible (finite differences: 18), **29 of 34** with the voltage-only stage (28). It does not make the repair much faster (1.1x): the QP takes 98 to 99 % of the time. And the cost of a finished repair moves by up to 3.1 points with the derivative source, because the method stops at its first feasible round ([`results_vlin_margin_analytic.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_vlin_margin_analytic.txt)).

- **LPAC as a one-shot baseline (milestone 4a, 2026-11-13):** the linear model that carries voltage and reactive power (LPAC-C, Coffrin and Van Hentenryck 2014), solved once and run through one AC power flow, gives a fully feasible dispatch on **0 of 34** cases (the repair: 21). It has no solution at all on 9, seven of them angle-limited SAD cases, and where it solves, a third of its generator voltage setpoints sit exactly on a bound and the AC power flow breaks Q limits on 21 of 25. Used as the repair's starting point instead of the DC dispatch, it is about as good (17 against 15 on the 25 cases it solves) ([`results_lpac.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_lpac.txt)).
- **Load-perturbed copies (milestone 5, 2026-11-13):** on 244 copies of the 34 cases with every bus's load scaled by 0.9 to 1.1, the repair is fully feasible on **0.66** of copies (95 % cluster bootstrap over cases 0.52 to 0.79; one load point: 0.62) and **0.91** with the voltage-only stage (0.83 to 0.97; one load point: 0.91). The one-load-point verdict matches the copies' majority on 30 of 34 cases, and the repair beats the voltage-only stage on the DC dispatch on 29 cases with no losses (sign test P = 3.7e-9). Late finishers are the fragile ones (clean on 0.68 of copies against 0.97). Caveat: 101 of 360 copies, 71 of 120 congested ones, have no AC optimum and are excluded; the arms find a feasible point on only 4 of them ([`results_perturb.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_perturb.txt)).
- **Held-out larger cases (milestone 6, 2026-11-13):** on the 24 PGLib cases of 500 to 2,000 buses, which no rule was set on, the frozen repair is fully feasible within 10 rounds on **0 of 12** scored cases, so H2 as written is refuted; within the 20-round budget fixed beforehand **6 of 12** (all at round 14 or later) and **8 of 12** with the voltage stage, against 0 of 12 for the DC dispatch with the same stage (sign test P = 0.008), at a mean **+0.66 %** over the AC optimum. PIPS has no AC optimum on 11 of 24 (all SNEM and RTE cases), and on five RTE cases the AC power flow at the DC start does not converge, so the start, not the repair, limits reach there ([`results_heldout.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_heldout.txt)).
- **Round budget and convergence (milestone 7, empirical half, 2026-11-13):** traced round by round with 30 rounds instead of 10, the repair alone is fully feasible on **31 of 34** cases (by round 20; 10 rounds: 21), the late finishers at a mean **+0.41 %** over the AC optimum. Most misses are curvature: the step's second-order error exceeds the margin on thermal and reactive rows (168 of 224 misses in rounds 1 to 10). The programme's convergence bound (H4) as written is refuted: it is 10x or more off on 25 of 31 finishers, and case73 API stalls with a well-conditioned Jacobian at a point of local infeasibility ([`results_converge.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_converge.txt)).

- **A start with an AC power flow (milestone 6 Next (1), 2026-11-14):** on the six cases where the DC start has no AC power flow (five RTE, case300 TYP), a homotopy from the case file's dispatch fails at its first point, because PGLib's file dispatch is no operating point (its generation is 2.0 to 18.3 GW, 3 to 23 %, short of load). Started from DC power-flow angles, Newton solves all six networks at no load, but scaling load and the DC dispatch up together stalls at **48 to 85 %** of load as voltages sag to 0.72 to 0.79 pu, which looks like a loadability limit. The AC optimum's dispatch at the same setpoints reaches full load on case300. So no power-flow start rescues these cases; the start has to move the dispatch or the voltages ([`results_homotopy_dcangle.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_homotopy_dcangle.txt)).

- **Past the loading limit (milestone 6 Next (1) and (2), 2026-11-14):** that stall is a voltage-collapse fold on all six cases: the power-flow Jacobian's smallest singular value falls to 5 to 9 % of its no-load value where each continuation stops and fits the square-root nose of a saddle-node (R<sup>2</sup> &ge; 0.996), while the AC optimum's dispatch keeps 84 % at full load ([`fold_sigma.svg`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/fold_sigma.svg)). Repairing at the fold and walking up in load reaches full load and a fully feasible point on case300 TYP (+0.83 % over the AC optimum, with a re-solve fallback declared after the run) but on none of the five RTE cases, where the first linearisation at the fold has no power flow and the repaired points break hundreds of voltage limits ([`results_fold.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_fold.txt), [`results_fold_fallback.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_fold_fallback.txt)).

- **The slack, not the dispatch (milestone 6 Next, 2026-11-15):** raising voltages first fails as planned. A reactive stage at the fold with P pinned has no solution, because the slack must carry the AC losses: 1,544 MW against a 718 MW limit on case300. On the RTE networks PYPOWER's reference rule makes a 15 to 30 MW unit the slack, carrying 505 to 687 MW. A second reference solver, IPOPT under PYPOWER's unchanged AC OPF, solves **all 12** large cases PIPS cannot, each within **0.005 %** of PGLib's published objective. With the reference at the largest unit, the DC dispatch has an AC power flow at full load on all five RTE cases, and the frozen repair is **fully feasible on 3 of 5** at +0.02 to +0.62 % (pre-registered, held). That makes 4 of the 6 no-power-flow cases feasible ([`results_bigslack.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_bigslack.txt), [`results_ipopt_ref.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_ipopt_ref.txt), [`diag_slack_move.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/diag_slack_move.txt)).

- **Milestone 6 under one protocol (2026-11-15):** rerun on all 24 held-out cases with the slack at the largest live unit, a continuation to the full-load power flow, IPOPT for the reactive stage and PGLib's published objective as the cost reference, the frozen repair is fully feasible on **10 of 24** within 20 rounds and **12 of 24** with the reactive stage, against **0 of 24** for the DC dispatch. Five cases have no DC OPF solution at all (the three SNEM networks, case1951 API, case2000 API). Paired with the earlier protocol on the 19 cases it scored, 3 are gained and none lost, and on the 6 cases whose slack does not move the run is identical round by round. Clean points cost a mean **+0.50 %** over the optimum (+0.01 to +2.12 %), and H2 as written stays refuted (1 of 24 within 10 rounds) ([`results_heldout_slack.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_heldout_slack.txt)).

- **The five cases with "no DC solution" (2026-11-16):** on 4 of 5 the DC OPF has a solution that PYPOWER's solver (PIPS) does not find. The same DC model on a second solver (Clarabel) agrees with PIPS on all 89 of 144 problems both solve and solves **26 that PIPS reports as having none**. case2000 API is plainly feasible. The SNEM network has two zero-reactance bus ties that make MATPOWER's B matrix infinite. case1951 API is the one genuine case: 1.61 MW short on one rating row under MATPOWER's convention, feasible under PowerModels'. Started from the second solver's dispatch, case2000 API becomes **fully feasible at +0.19 %** with the voltage stage, so the held-out count with that stage rises from 12 to **13 of 24**. SNEM's AC power flow stalls at 75 to 84 % of load. The pre-registered 20-round tally stays at 10 of 24 ([`results_dc_infeasible.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_dc_infeasible.txt), [`results_dc_rescue.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_dc_rescue.txt)).

- **Why the repair finishes (milestone 7, theory half, 2026-11-17):** a convergence statement in terms of the step replaces H4, which was refuted ([`theory.md`](theory.md)). If the AC power flow is smooth along each step and every round's hard QP is feasible, a step shorter than a curvature radius r* always finishes, the trust region is halved at most log<sub>2</sub>(box size / r*) times, and the repair terminates whenever its accepted steps shrink. On the 31 recorded finishers the halving bound is 1 to 12 against 0 to 5 observed, and steps shrank before the finish on all of them, but most rounds (162 of 228 misses) are accepted cost-driven steps that nothing in the assumptions bounds, and repairs usually finish with steps longer than r* (27 of 31). On the traces alone the case73 API stall looked non-stationary, because its step keeps scaling with the box ([`results_theory.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_theory.txt)).
- **What stalls the repair (2026-11-17):** logging the elastic QP's own penalty settles it, and the two stalls differ. On **case73 API** the l1 voltage violation is within 1.6 &times; 10<sup>&minus;5</sup> of first-order stationary from round 22 at every box size tested (two buses up to 0.002 pu below V<sub>min</sub>, 14 of 33 setpoints on their bound): a local infeasibility of the repair's own controls, as Proposition 2 describes. On **case118 API** the steps lower the summed overload the QP minimises and are rejected by the merit, which scores the worst overload. Accepting elastic steps on the QP's penalty instead finishes neither case and loses none of the development finishers ([`results_elastic.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_elastic.txt)).
- **The repair's own margins (2026-11-17):** case73 API stalls because its target is empty. The repair aims inside every limit by a small margin (0.1 % of rating, 0.001 pu of voltage); with both pulled in, the AC OPF of case73 API has **no solution on PIPS or on IPOPT**, though each margin alone is solvable and IPOPT solves the unshifted problem. Its AC optimum has all 14 generator voltages at the ceiling and 10 branch ends at their rating. case118 API's shifted OPF solves (+0.41 %), so its stall is the acceptance test. Removing margins is no fix: it finishes neither stall (the repair stops at the check's tolerance) and loses 5 (voltage) to 21 (all) of the 31 finishers. The 0.001 pu voltage margin alone costs up to +6.9 % at the shifted optimum (case57 API) ([`results_margin.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_margin.txt)).

- **A fallback for an empty target (2026-11-17):** a rule fixed before it ran checks the margin-shifted AC OPF on two solvers before round 1 and, only if both find no solution, halves every margin until one does. On development cases it fires on two: **case73 API is fully feasible for the first time** (round 8, +0.96 % over the AC optimum) and case24 API finishes two rounds sooner at +0.17 % instead of +0.71 %; no finisher is lost, so the repair finishes **32 of 34**. On the 19 held-out cases that reach the repair it fires once (case588 API: same round, +0.66 % instead of +0.87 %) and changes no verdict. A single-solver check would have fired on two cases where only PIPS fails. The check costs up to 57 minutes on a 1,354-bus case, so it suits a stall, not every run ([`results_backoff.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_backoff.txt)).

- **What empties the hard QP (2026-11-19):** an independent LP solver (HiGHS) and an irreducible infeasible subset per round settle which constraints make the repair's hard QP infeasible on the two development stalls. On all **58 of 58** failed rounds the linearised constraint set is genuinely empty, and on **all 58 it becomes non-empty once the trust box is removed**; removing the safety margins instead fixes only 13. case73 API needs a box **1.08 to 1.11 times its first one** on every round from 3 to 25, while the rule "halve on rejection" shrinks it 18 times; case118 API needs 0.19 to 0.90 of it, and 22 of its 28 infeasible subsets tie 10 to 15 line ratings to the power balance and the full-output limits of 24 to 30 units. This corrects the 2026-11-17 reading of case73 API (that probe only shrank the box). The next step tests enlarging the box before the elastic fallback ([`results_iis.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_iis.txt)).
- **Enlarging the box before the elastic fallback (2026-11-19, rule E, not adopted):** when the hard QP is empty, one LP finds the smallest trust-box scale with a solution (capped at twice the first box) and the hard QP is re-solved there. It works as a fix for emptiness (the enlarged QP solves on all 94 development firings) and finishes case73 API for the first time under the full margins (round 12, +3.26 %). But it erases the repair's only memory: after a rejected enlarged step the base point is unchanged and the needed scale doubles as the box halves, so **68 of 94 firings rebuild the QP just rejected**. Development finishers go from 31 to 30 of 34 (case30 and case200 API lost to that cycle) and held-out from 10 to 8 of 19 (gained case500 SAD; lost case1888, case793 API and case588 TYP). The next step limits the enlargement to once per base point ([`results_enlarge.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_enlarge.txt)).
- **Enlarging at most once per base point (2026-11-19, rule E-once, not adopted):** the same widening, blocked after a widened step until the base point moves. The exact repeats are gone (0 of 43 development firings) and case73 API stays clean (round 12, +3.26 %). case200 API and held-out case588 TYP come back, and case500 SAD stays gained. But case30 API and held-out case793 API now cycle over two rounds: the widened step is rejected, then a tiny elastic step is accepted, which moves the base point and re-arms the rule. The widening's cap is fixed at twice the first box while every rejection halves the stored box, so it keeps proposing about the same large step. Development finishers **31 of 34** (as frozen, one gained and one lost), held-out **9 of 19** (frozen 10). The next step capped the widened box at twice the current stored box ([`results_enlarge_once.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_enlarge_once.txt)).
- **Capping the widening at twice the current box (2026-11-20, rule C, leading candidate):** rule E with its cap tied to the stored box, so every rejection also halves what widening can reach. All seven pre-registered predictions held: 0 repeats, development finishers **32 of 34** with none lost (case73 API clean at round 12, +3.26 %; case30 API and case200 API back, later and dearer than frozen), held-out **10 of 19** as frozen (case500 SAD gained, case1888 API lost, as under every widening rule). Round and cost changes are not significant. Next: diagnose case1888 API and confirm on an unused split before adopting ([`results_enlarge_rel.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_enlarge_rel.txt)).
- **Why widening loses case1888 API (2026-11-20, diagnosis):** a per-round trace shows a 20-round budget miss, not a stall: 12 of rule C's 15 trials after round 5 are accepted and the run ends at one branch end 0.03 % over, the merit the frozen repair had at round 11. Its widened steps cut voltage violations (35 to 7) at the cost of overloads (178 %, 211 %) that hard QPs repay slowly, because rows the linear model puts exactly on their margin land up to 8 points over in the AC check. Withholding any one of the three firings finishes the case (rounds 17 to 20, +0.23 to +0.54 % against the frozen +0.62 %) ([`diag_case1888.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/diag_case1888.txt)).
- **Widening only before the first halving (2026-11-20, rule P, leading candidate):** rule C, but once a rejected step has halved the box, a failed hard QP goes to the elastic chain. All seven pre-registered predictions held: development **32 of 34** with none lost (case200 and case57 API back near their frozen cost), held-out **11 of 19** with none lost against the frozen repair or rule C, 10 of 18 without case1888 API, where the rule came from. Round and cost changes are not significant. Because a held-out case suggested it, adoption waits for PGLib-OPF cases not used so far ([`results_enlarge_pre.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_enlarge_pre.txt)).

- **Confirmation on unused cases (2026-11-20 to 11-21, complete):** on the 33 PGLib-OPF cases of 2,001 to 2,999 buses, none used before, the frozen repair reaches 23 and finishes **9** within 20 rounds. With all 20 cases that meet a failed hard QP scored, rule C and rule P each finish **7**: they **lose 2 of the frozen repair's finishers** (case2848_rte TYP and case2868_rte TYP) and **gain none** (sign test P = 0.50), and P against C gains 0 and loses 0. On the 7 cases all three finish, rule P is 0.32 points cheaper on average (case2312_goc SAD +0.52 % against +2.33 %, case2848_rte SAD +0.23 % against +0.70 %), rule C 0.30. On 11 of the 20 cases the only failed hard QP is at round 1, so the widening fires there and nowhere else; that one firing turns 2 frozen finishers into misses (one voltage 0.003 to 0.005 pu out at round 20), speeds one (case2848_rte SAD) and leaves 8 unfinished either way. The adoption rule fixed beforehand keeps the frozen repair; over three splits the widening line nets +1, +1 and &minus;2 finishers, so it is closed as a finisher rule ([`results_confirm.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_confirm.txt)).
- **The 10 cases with no DC start (2026-11-21):** the protocol's DC OPF (PIPS) found no start on 10 of the 33 confirmation cases. The same DC OPF on Clarabel starts **9** of them and all 9 reach a full-load AC power flow; the frozen repair finishes **1** (case2742_goc API, round 15, +1.39 % over PGLib's AC optimum). With the second-solver start the split's tally is **10 of 32** reaching the repair (31 %, against 9 of 23 under the protocol), so the start rule was hiding mostly hard cases. Two misses (case2383wp_k TYP and SAD) are only reactive and tiny: no overload and 0.09 MVAr out across about 60 units ([`results_confirm_rescue.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_confirm_rescue.txt)).
- **The reactive stage on the confirmation split (2026-11-21, J+R):** of the 22 confirmation cases that reach the repair and do not finish, 10 fail only on reactive limits, under 0.1 MVAr in total. The IPOPT reactive stage at the repair's best point (P pinned, voltages and Q free) finishes **8 of those 10**, at **+0.008 to +0.212 %** over PGLib's AC optimum, and none of the cases with real overloads. Under the protocol's start, J or J+R finishes **15 of 23** (J alone 9; held-out 12 of 24); over all 33 cases, 18. A third DC solver, HiGHS, solves case2853_sdet API's DC OPF within 0.03 % of PGLib's published DC objective where PIPS and Clarabel failed, so all 33 confirmation cases reach the repair; that case then stalls with 76 overloads. All six pre-registered predictions on the reactive stage held; the one on HiGHS (an empty DC OPF) was refuted ([`results_confirm_reactive.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_confirm_reactive.txt), [`results_dc_highs.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_dc_highs.txt)).

- **A learned thermal margin, without round 1 (2026-11-22, rules K, K1, Kr; not adopted):** pulling each thermal row in by the AC remainder it showed (capped at 5 % of rating, decayed with the box) finishes development case118 API for the first time but loses case240 SAD, whose rows armed on the unboxed round 1. With round 1 excluded (K1) development is **32 of 34 with none lost**; held-out stays **10 of 19**, 8 of 10 common finishers sooner (P = 0.039) at **+0.65 cost points**, and no overload stall finishes, so the pre-registered adoption test fails ([`results_kfirst.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_kfirst.txt)).

- **A cost phase after the first clean point (2026-11-23, rule CP, adopted):** up to 10 hard QPs on the case's own costs in a small box, accepting only clean, cheaper steps. Fixed before it ran; 83 of 83 reruns reproduce their stored runs. It lowers the mean gap to PGLib's AC optimum on development from **+0.39 to +0.23 points** (frozen start; case14 SAD +3.85 to +0.73 %) and on held-out from **+0.58 to +0.51**, in a median 1.6 s against 5.8 s for the repair, and never makes a point worse. It does not make rule K1's early finishes cheap: held-out K1+CP is still +0.55 points dearer than frozen+CP (dearer on 9 of 10, P = 0.021). 27 of 83 finishers take no step, mostly because the margined QP is empty around a clean point inside a rating's margin band ([`results_costphase.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_costphase.txt)).
- **Slack-capped margins in the cost phase (2026-11-23, rule CS, not adopted):** capping each bound's margin at its own slack at the point cuts empty QPs from 185 to 10 of 803 rounds and gives a step on 76 of 83 finishers, but trials that leave the clean set rise from 88 to 275. Frozen-start mean gap: development +0.229 (CP) to +0.218, held-out +0.508 to +0.516, so rule CP stays ([`results_slackcap.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_slackcap.txt)).
- **The slack cap only on an empty QP (2026-11-23, rule CF, adopted):** the cost phase keeps the frozen margins and re-solves with each margin capped at its own slack only on a round whose margined QP is empty. Fixed before it ran, all eight predictions held: never dearer than rule CP on any of 83 finishers and cheaper on 28 (frozen-start development 10 of 31, P = 0.002), frozen-start mean gap development **+0.229 to +0.209** points and held-out **+0.508 to +0.494**, below rule CS on both. It replaces CP in the method of record ([`results_fallback.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_fallback.txt)).

- **The method of record on the confirmation split (2026-11-24):** on 33 PGLib cases of 2,001 to 2,999 buses that no rule was set on, the frozen repair and the IPOPT reactive stage finish 18; rule CF, fixed before it ran, then lowers the cost of all 18 and is never dearer than rule CP (cheaper on 10, sign test P = 0.002), taking the mean gap to PGLib's AC optimum from **+0.767 to +0.662** points (CP +0.689) and the median from +0.115 to **+0.089**. On the 8 reactive-stage points CP is stuck on 7 (every margined QP empty, since the point sits on its limits) and CF moves all 8 ([`results_confirm_cf.txt`](../infra-dcopf/experiments/results_confirm_cf.txt)).
**Not yet shown:** a DC solver that certifies infeasibility under the whole protocol (PIPS also missed angle-limited starts on three scored cases), the SNEM stall, the remaining near misses (1 to 4 overloads on case1354, one voltage on case1888 TYP and SAD), the strongest one-shot
linearisations (LPAC-W and an optimised linearisation) as baselines, intervals on the stressed (API) copies most of which have no AC optimum,
an a priori bound on the accepted rounds (a trust box tied to the violation), a box rule confirmed on cases not used to choose it (on the first unused split, complete, rules C and P lose 2 frozen finishers and gain none, so neither is adopted), and a cheaper empty-target check run only on a stall.
