## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 3a: a triangle finite-element tier, and does it pass the Cusick acceptance test? (predictions in `experiments/m3_fem_tier/run.py`'s docstring, committed before the first run)
- Why: milestone 3 is the critical path, and milestone 2a set the top tier's first acceptance test (the Cusick DC flat in EA and mesh), which the spring sheet fails (fabric A at EA 3200: 0.70 to 0.37 from 7.5 to 2.5 mm). ARCSim and C-IPC are C++ builds; ARCSim's element model (constant-strain triangles, dihedral hinges) fits in numpy, so this step builds that element without remeshing, with stretch EA and measured KES shear G as separate parameters, and tests whether the element alone removes the spring grid's dependence.
- Built before the run (`fem.py`; checked by hand, not results): gradient against finite differences; the hinge energy on an equilateral lattice needs a factor 1/2 against the textbook B theta^2 |e|^2 / (A1 + A2), because a piecewise-flat lattice on a cylinder also folds across the edges parallel to the curvature (by k a / (2 sqrt3)); with it, a cylinder's energy is B k^2 / 2 per area to 0.4% at five axis angles. The exact heavy elastica (`elastica.py`) gives the 41.5 degree chord at l = 2.000 c, the figure ASTM D1388 is built on, and Peirce's closed form gives 1.963 c.
- Plan: part 1, cantilever strips (L = 2 and 4 bending lengths, fabrics A and D, EA 150, 800, 3200, 8 to 32 elements) against the elastica; part 2, milestone 2a's Cusick test on lattices of side 7.5 to 2.5 mm, measured G, plus G = 0.1 EA for a like-for-like comparison with milestone 2b. 36 + 32 cases.
- Predictions: F1 32-element tip drop within 1% of l of the elastica in all 12 cases; F2 EA moves it under 0.5% of l; F3 at EA 3200 DC moves under 0.03 from 3.75 to 2.5 mm on both fabrics; F4 at 2.5 mm DC(3200) - DC(800) under 0.03 on both; F5 with tied shear the 5 to 2.5 mm change on A at EA 3200 is under half the spring sheet's 0.20; F6 A < D everywhere. Acceptance is F3 and F4.
- Two bugs before any prediction was evaluated: a pilot cantilever did not move, which was a sign error in the hinge gradient (the first finite-difference check used a random direction dominated by the membrane and missed it; the test now checks bending alone, to 1e-10). Fixing it also showed 3000 L-BFGS iterations stop 9% of l short on the cantilever, so both parts now use a damped Newton solver (`newton.py`, sparse Hessian from coloured central differences; forward differences gave spurious negative eigenvalues of order 1 N/m). One 16-element cantilever result was seen in the pilot; predictions unchanged. Added before its run: F7, milestone 2b's L-BFGS DC on the spring sheet is within 0.03 of a Newton solve (A and D, EA 3200, 5 and 3.75 mm).
- Outcome (`experiments/m3_fem_tier/results_cantilever.txt`, `results_spring.txt`, `results_cusick.txt`; 8 new tests pass; Cusick part 95 min on 4 cores): **F1 refuted:** at 32 elements the tip drop is 1.6 to 3.2% of l too large. The error halves per mesh doubling (8, 16, 32 elements: 9.0, 4.9, 2.6% on A at L = 2); post hoc (`posthoc_richardson.py`) the first-order extrapolation lands within 0.9% of l of the elastica in all 12 cases, and halving the strip width adds only 0.4%, so the error is along the length (clamp and curvature discretisation), not the free edges. **F2 held:** EA 150 to 3200 moves the drop at most 0.31% of l. **F7 refuted on A:** the Newton-converged spring sheet reads 0.622 against 2b's 0.567 at 5 mm (+0.055, 11 folds against 13, a different equilibrium) and +0.027 at 3.75 mm; D within 0.008. The spring sheet's mesh dependence on A survives (0.62 to 0.52). **F3 refuted:** on A, 3.75 to 2.5 mm at EA 3200 moves DC by -0.158; on D +0.019. **F4 refuted:** DC(3200) - DC(800) at 2.5 mm is +0.055 (A) and +0.037 (D), against the spring sheet's +0.141 and +0.061. **F5 refuted:** with tied shear at EA 3200, A's 2.5 mm case did not converge (residual 1e4 node weights, no folds, DC 0.93). **F6 refuted:** on the 7.5 mm lattice A drapes stiffer than D at EA 800 and 3200. Acceptance failed.
- Convergence: 9 of 32 Cusick cases hit the 400-step Newton cap, all at EA 800 or more, 8 of them on A; their residuals are 8 to 1e4 times a node weight, so those DC values are not equilibria (the table prints the residual). Every D case converged except EA 3200 at 2.5 mm.
- Reading: the triangle element calibrates and converges on a pure bend (the cantilever is EA-blind, as it should be), but the Cusick test, where the cloth must fold in two directions, still depends on mesh and stretch stiffness. On the suiting (D) with measured shear it converges at every EA up to 800 N/m, but DC still climbs about 0.016 per refinement step (0.45, 0.55, 0.57, 0.58 at EA 800) and EA 800 to 3200 still adds 0.034 at 3.75 mm, where both converged (0.037 at 2.5 mm, where the EA 3200 case did not), though the element cuts the spring sheet's EA dependence by 40%, and with this element the shear coupling hardly matters on D (2.5 mm, EA 800: 0.581 measured G against 0.578 tied), unlike the spring sheet. On the soft jersey (A) the element converges only at EA 150 (0.220, 0.184, 0.166, 0.161: changes shrinking to 0.006), which is near the tangent stiffness milestone 1d estimated for A at garment load (250 to 370 N/m); at 800 N/m and up it is mesh-dependent and Newton stalls. So constant-strain triangles do not remove membrane locking for cloth that is soft in bending and stiff in stretch, which matches the graphics literature's motivation for nonconforming elements; this element is not the top tier.
- Limits: one seed; lattice aligned with the warp; isotropic in-plane stiffness (EA in both directions); no contact with the clamp edge; Newton step cap 400; L-BFGS and Newton can land in different fold patterns, so per-case DC differences mix convergence with branch selection.
- Next: a nonconforming (edge-midpoint) membrane element or ARCSim/C-IPC proper for the top tier; for fabric A, run at its sourced tangent stiffness (about 250 to 370 N/m), where this element already converges; a continuation in EA (solve at 150, restart at 800, then 3200) to get the stiff cases to equilibrium.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 5: the decision-margin bound, and what paired designs can certify (predictions in `experiments/m5_margin_bound/run.py`'s docstring, committed before the first run)

Certificates: C1 margin_t > osc(e); C2 (cone) s_t(d) − s_t(d^) > L |d − d^| for all d, L the largest neighbour slope of e. Checked on 3,000 random instances.
- Plan: 50 instances, no new simulation: 2D chain → converged 3D sheet (28), 3000-iteration → converged (4), mesh change (4), guessed EA or the default sheet → KES-sourced EA and G (14).
- Predictions: M1 the inequalities hold everywhere; M2 median osc / (2 max|e|) ≤ 0.35 on 2D → 3D; M3 C1 certifies no 2D → 3D and at most 1 capped → converged; M4 C2 certifies more than C1 and at least one 2D → 3D; M5 the median 2D → 3D screening set keeps at least 4 of 7, EA 3200 → KES at most 2; M6 the 2D choice costs under 0.05 cm at EA ≥ 800; M7 L from three reference runs is at least half the true L in 75% of instances.

Milestone 1c's shallow-arm width shift is likely the same confound (its sheet ties shear to EA in the same way); that is the next thing to test, with the shear spring set from G and EA swept on its own.
- Limits: one seed at most grids; two fabrics; KES G is a slope at 0.5 to 5 degrees under 10 gf/cm pretension, with large hysteresis (2HG up to 3.5 gf/cm), and the model's shear is linear and elastic; fold counts were read but not compared with a measurement; still no measured drape coefficient.
- Next: rerun milestone 1c's shallow-arm width choice with k_s = measured G and EA swept separately; for fabric A, either a 1.5 to 2 mm grid on a smaller specimen or a nonconforming element (milestone 3).

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 1c: does 1b's tier disagreement survive the unmeasured stretch stiffness and the mesh? (predictions in `experiments/m1c_ea_mesh/run.py`'s docstring, committed before the first run)
- Why: milestone 2a showed the 3D spring sheet's drape moves with EA and the grid as much as with B (locking). The shallow-arm sheet of 1b is the same grid, so its 0.5 cm disagreement with the 2D chain might be an artefact.
- Plan: 1b's converged solve (chunks of 3000 L-BFGS iterations until the whole-sheet error moves under 0.002 cm, cap 60,000), wood-shaped profile on the 3 cm arm, fabrics A and D, offsets 0 to +3 cm in half-cm steps; EA 40, 150, 800 N/m at the base mesh (5 mm rows, 9 depth columns), and at EA 150 7.5 mm rows or 13 depth columns; the 2D chain at the same EA and row spacing. 70 cases.
- Predictions: R1 base best +1.5 on both fabrics (reproduces 1b); R2 EA 40 to 800 moves the 3D best by at most 0.5 cm; R3 each mesh change moves it by at most 0.5 cm; R4 3D best at least the 2D best in all 10 configuration-fabric pairs and strictly wider in 8; R5 the error at the best moves more than 0.05 cm across EA on some fabric.
- Outcome (`experiments/m1c_ea_mesh/results.txt`; all 70 cases met the stopping rule, 6,000 to 33,000 iterations, about 15 min on 4 cores; 3 new tests pass): **R1 held** (+1.5 on A and D, every base number identical to 1b). **R3 held:** 7.5 mm rows and 13 depth columns both keep +1.5 on both fabrics (errors shift by up to 0.17 cm, the choice does not). **R2 refuted, by three times the tolerance:** the 3D best is +2.0 (A) and +2.5 cm (D) at EA 40, +1.5 at 150, and +0.5 (A) and +1.0 cm (D) at 800, a 1.5 cm span on both fabrics. **R4 refuted:** 3D minus 2D best is +1.0 at EA 40, +0.5 at 150 and on both mesh changes, and -0.5 (A) and 0.0 (D) at 800; the 2D chain itself stays at +1.0 except D at EA 40 (+1.5). **R5 held:** the error at the best moves 0.22 (A) and 0.69 cm (D) across EA.
- Reading: the disagreement is not a mesh artefact at EA 150, but it is not a fixed offset either: its sign follows the stretch stiffness that no KES table in this programme gives. Softer cloth stretches under its own weight off the shallow arm, and a wider arm compensates; the 2D chain, whose fabric hangs in one plane, barely sees it. So on a shallow arm, B and W do not pick the width on the 3D tier: EA does, which is early evidence against H2 on this tier (tensile data changes the choice) and makes measured EA (KES-FB1) a precondition for milestone 4. The base decision also has a thin margin (0.004 to 0.006 cm between +1.0 and +1.5, against a last-chunk change of up to 0.0013 cm), so +1.5 against +1.0 at EA 150 is barely certified.
- Limits: EA swept, not sourced; two fabrics; mesh varied only at EA 150 (locking should be worst at high EA, not checked); the stopping rule bounds the change per chunk, not the distance to the minimum; one size, one profile, no friction; the spring sheet is not a validated solver.
- Next: source EA for the KES fabrics (KES-FB1 tensile, or a published woven tensile table) so the shallow-arm decision can be made at a measured value; milestone 3's top tier must reproduce this EA dependence (or show it is a spring-grid effect) before H1 is tested.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 2a: a simulated Cusick drape test on the 3D sheet, and can its drape coefficient calibrate bending rigidity? (predictions in `experiments/m2_cusick/run.py`'s docstring, written before the first run)
- Why: milestone 2 compares the sheet's Cusick drape coefficient (DC) with a published table of DC, B and W for the same fabrics. This session's egress allowed only GitHub: the hosts holding candidate tables (the Autex paper doi:10.2478/aut-2019-0034 with 63 woven fabrics, *Fashion and Textiles* s40691-023-00355-7, KTU *Materials Science*, CORE, White Rose) all refused, through both curl and the fetch tool. So this step builds the test and asks the question that decides whether a table is worth finding: does DC on this model move with B more than with everything else?
- Plan: specimen 30 cm on an 18 cm disc, square grid (5 mm) cut to the circle, `hanger-3d`'s spring sheet with bending made isotropic (diagonal bending lines; the grid-only energy is half as stiff at 45 degrees), shadow area rasterised at 1 mm, DC normalised by the flat mesh's own area. Fabrics A to D of `hanger-kes`; 5 seeds of fold-picking noise; B x 1/4 to x 4; B and W both x 4; EA 40 and 800 N/m; shear spring 0.03 and 0.3 of EA; 7.5 mm grid. Solve until DC moves under 0.001 per 2000 iterations (cap 40,000). 140 cases.
- Predictions: C1 DC orders A < B < C < D (bending length 0.81, 1.01, 1.29, 1.72 cm); C2 seed sd at most 0.02; C3 DC rises strictly with B and each doubling moves it at least 3 seed sd; C4 B and W both x 4 moves DC under 0.02; C5 EA 40 vs 800 under 0.03; C6 shear 0.03 vs 0.3 moves DC at least 0.03 on some fabric; C7 7.5 vs 5 mm grid under 0.03.
- Outcome (`experiments/m2_cusick/results.txt`; 140 cases, 4,000 to 20,000 iterations, every case met the 0.001 stopping rule, about 25 min on 4 cores; 5 new tests pass): **C1 held** (mean DC 0.194, 0.213, 0.321, 0.446). **C2 held** (seed sd 0.003 to 0.008; 8 to 14 folds). **C3 refuted** on fabric A only: DC rises with B on all four, but A's first doubling moves it 0.012 against 3 sd = 0.020. **C4 refuted:** B and W both x 4 lowers DC by 0.04 to 0.06. **C5 refuted, badly:** EA 40 against 800 N/m moves DC by 0.15 to 0.23. **C6 held:** shear 0.03 against 0.3 moves it 0.08 to 0.11. **C7 refuted** on A, B, D (0.044 to 0.047; C 0.005).
- Post hoc (`posthoc_ea.py`, not predicted; seeds 0 and 1): DC does not level off as EA grows. A: 0.19, 0.36, 0.57, 0.78 at EA 150, 800, 3200, 12,800 N/m; D: 0.44, 0.54, 0.63, 0.77. At EA 3200 the 7.5 mm grid reads 0.70 against 0.57 on A. Two fabrics with 19-fold different B drape alike at the stiff end: the square spring grid locks (folds are near-isometric, and a coarse grid cannot make them without stretching).
- Reading: on this tier, DC is set by the unmeasured stretch and shear stiffness and by the mesh as much as by B (on A, EA 40 to 800 spans more DC than B x 1/4 to x 4), so a measured DC cannot pin B here. Milestone 4 takes B from KES-FB for the cheap tiers and moves drape calibration to the top tier; the top tier's first acceptance test is this Cusick test, flat in EA and mesh. It also puts milestone 1b's 0.5 cm tier disagreement in doubt until it is rerun across EA and mesh, because the shallow-arm sheet is the same locking grid.
- Limits: no measured drape compared (hosts refused); EA and shear unsourced and swept; specimen isotropic (real wovens are not); shadow rasterised at 1 mm, DC normalised by the flat mesh area (the cut mesh is within 1% of the 30 cm circle); no contact with the clamp edge or friction.
- Next: milestone 3 (top-tier solver), with this Cusick test as its acceptance check; recheck 1b across EA and mesh.

## 2026-11-10 · Milestone 1b: does the 3 cm arm's width choice survive a converged solve? (predictions in `experiments/m1b_converged/run.py`'s docstring, written before the first run)
- Why: milestone 1's P3 held on 3000-iteration solves whose own error (0.05 to 0.11 cm between 3000 and 6000 iterations) is as large as the width margin (0.08 to 0.13 cm). A decision read off unconverged numbers is not a decision.
- Plan: wood-shaped profile on the 3 cm half-depth arm, fabrics A to D, offsets -1, 0, +0.5, +1, +1.5, +2, +3 cm; L-BFGS in 3000-iteration chunks, restarted from the last positions, until the whole-sheet error moves under 0.002 cm over a chunk (cap 60,000). The first chunk is milestone 1's solve and reproduces it (1.9212 cm on A at +1, checked before the run).
- Predictions: Q1 converged best integer offset +1 cm on all fabrics; Q2 converged margin more than twice the last-chunk change on all fabrics; Q3 the 3000-iteration error underestimates the converged one in every case, by at least 0.03 cm on average; Q4 converged best half-cm offset in [+0.5, +1.5].
- Outcome (`experiments/m1b_converged/results.txt`; 28 cases, 12,000 to 24,000 iterations each, about 8 min on 4 cores; every case met the 0.002 cm stopping rule, last-chunk change at most 0.0016 cm): **Q1 refuted** on fabric D: converged, +2 cm beats +1 cm by 0.066 cm (2.133 against 2.199). A to C keep +1 on the integer grid, but by 0.020 to 0.033 cm, against 0.11 to 0.13 cm at 3000 iterations. **Q2 held:** each margin is 8 to 25 times twice the last-chunk change. **Q3 refuted, and that is the finding:** the 3000-iteration error is not uniformly low. Its bias depends on the width: averaged over the fabrics, the converged error is 0.219 cm higher at -1 cm, 0.129 at +1, 0.026 at +2 and 0.065 cm *lower* at +3. Stopping early flatters narrow arms, so it pulls the chosen width down. **Q4 held:** the converged best on the half-cm grid is +1.5 cm on all four fabrics.
- Post hoc (`posthoc_2d.py`, not predicted): the 2D chain on the same grid picks +1.0 cm on all four fabrics, and sharply (fabric A: 0.18 cm at +1.0 against 0.51 at +1.5). So once it converges, the 3 cm arm disagrees with the 2D chain by 0.5 cm of arm width on every fabric. That is inside milestone 1's P3 tolerance of 1 cm, but it is a real tier disagreement where the 3D sheet adds physics (the front and back panels falling off a shallow arm). Milestone 1 hid it, because the iteration cap's bias towards narrow arms happened to land on the 2D answer.
- Reading: cheap-tier agreement can be produced by the solver budget alone. Decision-level fidelity needs convergence judged against the decision: a stopping rule on the change in error, checked against the margin (Q2's test), rather than a fixed iteration cap. The converged 3 cm curve is flat between +1 and +2 cm (A to C within 0.04 cm), so on a shallow arm the width is weakly identified, and a validated top tier (milestone 3) has a 0.5 cm disagreement to settle.
- Limits: the stopping rule bounds the change per chunk, not the distance to the true minimum (some cases still drift 0.001 cm per chunk; gradient max 1e-6 to 4.5e-4). Restarting L-BFGS per chunk differs slightly from one long run. The setup otherwise follows milestone 1: EA assumed, size M, one profile, no friction.
- Next: milestone 2 (simulated Cusick drape against a sourced table), and every later tier comparison uses the same convergence rule.

## 2026-11-10 · Milestone 1: does the 2D chain's choice of hanger survive in the 3D sheet on four fabrics with measured KES-FB bending rigidity and weight? (predictions in `experiments/m1_kes_fidelity/run.py`'s docstring, written before the first run)
- Why: every hanger design in track 18 so far comes from the 2D chain or the 3D sheet on assumed fabrics; `hanger-kes` sourced B and W for four fabrics but scored only the 2D chain. Before building a validated top tier (milestone 3), check whether the two cheap tiers even agree with each other on measured fabrics, on the decisions that matter: which stock hanger, which arm width.
- Plan: size M, EA 150 N/m, fabrics A to D of `hanger-kes`; three stock hangers and the wood-shaped profile at width offsets -1 to +3 cm; 2D chain at 5 mm rows, 3D slab arm (centre column), 3D 3 cm half-depth arm (whole sheet); a 3000-vs-6000-iteration convergence check on the softest and stiffest fabric.
- Predictions: P1 same stock ranking in all three models on all fabrics; P2 best offset identical in 2D and 3D slab; P3 3 cm arm's best offset within 1 cm of 2D; P4 |3D slab − 2D| under 0.10 cm for the wood-shaped hanger.
- Outcome (`experiments/m1_kes_fidelity/results.txt`, about 4 min on 4 cores; 4 tests pass): **P1 refuted, on a tie.** The wood-shaped hanger is best in every model on every fabric (0.38 to 0.47 cm on the slab, against 3.1 to 3.4 cm for wire and plastic); the one disagreement is second place on fabric D in the 2D chain, where wire and plastic both score 3.201 cm. **P2 held:** best width offset +1 cm in 2D and 3D slab on all four fabrics. **P3 held:** +1 cm on the 3 cm arm too. **P4 held:** the wood-shaped hanger's 3D slab and 2D errors differ by at most 0.004 cm.
- Convergence: on the slab arm, 3000 and 6000 L-BFGS iterations agree to 0.0004 cm; on the 3 cm arm they differ by 0.110 cm (fabric A) and 0.049 cm (D). The 3 cm arm's decision margins (best offset against the runner-up) are 0.126, 0.111, 0.115 and 0.077 cm, the same size as that solver error.
- Reading: on measured fabrics the two cheap tiers agree on every decision, but on a deep arm they agree to 0.004 cm because they are the same model in two coordinate systems, so their agreement is no evidence of fidelity; only the validated top tier (milestones 3 and 4) can test H1. Where the 3D sheet does add physics (a shallow arm), its numbers are not converged to the margin that decides the width, so `hanger-3d`'s shallow-arm figures (also run at 3000 iterations) carry about 0.05 to 0.1 cm of solver error. A decision-level check needs a convergence tolerance tied to the margin, which is the paired-difference idea of the proposal's Method.
- Limits: EA fixed at 150 N/m (not sourced), size M only, one hanger profile for the width sweep, the four fabrics are unfinished and from a trade-site PDF, no friction, no real garment.
- Next: milestone 2, a simulated Cusick drape test against a sourced table of drape coefficient with B and W for the same fabrics; and rerun the 3 cm arm cases to a gradient tolerance rather than an iteration cap.
