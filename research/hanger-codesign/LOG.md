## 2026-11-21 · Milestone 4e: the top tier on the hanger. All three tiers pick the same arm width on nine measured wovens, and calibrating bending never changes the top tier's pick (predictions in `experiments/m4e_hanger_tier/run.py`'s docstring, committed and pushed before any registered solve)
- Why: 4d's Next (d) and the proposal's milestone 4: put the calibrated tier on the hanger and test H1 (the cheap tiers' choice survives) and H3 (calibration changes a choice) on fabrics with measured mechanics, instead of chasing the drape bias further.
- Built (`hangerfem.py`): 3a's triangle element and 3d's local-Hessian Newton on cloth3d's shoulder-and-sleeve sheet (5 mm lattice, 3,321 nodes), with cloth3d's mass along s, swept-capsule contact (penalty smoothed over 10 µm; the unsmoothed kink stalled Newton in a pilot), neck pins (x held by a penalty off the arm), start and whole-sheet score; contact Hessian Gauss-Newton. 7 tests.
- Plan: Petrak's F1 to F9 as size M tees (mass × W/125.5 g/m², EA secant, G FAST on T3), bending c × FAST with c = 1 and 0.485 (4a's fit); wood-shaped arm, half-depth 3 cm, offsets −1 to +3 cm; T1 chain, T2 spring sheet and T3 each converged (T3 started from T2's state, 600-step cap). 90 cases.

- Predictions: E1 all converge; E2 (acceptance) T3 best = T2 best on ≥ 7 of 9 at both c; E3 T3 = T1 on ≥ 7 of 9; E4 (H3) calibration changes T3's best on ≥ 1; E5 |T3 − T2| at the best > T3's margin on ≥ 5; E6 T3 regret of T2's choice ≤ 0.02 cm on all 9; E7 paired differences carry over better than absolute scores on ≥ 7.
- Outcome (`experiments/m4e_hanger_tier/results.txt`, `results.json`, `run_log.txt`, `width_curves_t2_t3.svg`; 90 cases, about 9.8 core-hours): **E1 refuted:** T2 90 of 90, T3 81 of 90 (nine stopped at 600 steps, six of them on F1 or F2, none at a fabric's best offset except F4 +1 cm uncalibrated). **E2 held (acceptance passed):** 9 of 9 at c = 1, 8 of 9 at c = 0.485 (F2: T2 picks 0 cm, T3 +1 cm). **E3 held:** 9 of 9 and 9 of 9; every T1 and T3 choice is +1 cm. **E4 refuted:** calibration changes T3's choice on 0 of 9 (it changes T2's on F2). **E5 refuted:** 1 of 9. **E6 refuted:** 8 of 9; F2's regret is 0.050 cm. **E7 refuted:** 0 of 9.
- Post hoc (after the report): with c = 1 the top tier scores 0.148 cm worse than the spring sheet at +1 cm on average (−0.003 to +0.235); with c = 0.485 the gap is 0.045 (−0.011 to +0.102). The chain's absolute error is 0.21 to 0.25 cm against 1.7 to 2.0 cm on the 3D tiers (its front and back panels cannot fall off a narrow arm), yet its choice is the top tier's on 18 of 18. Kendall tau between T2 and T3 over the five widths is +0.60 to +1.00. The cheap tiers' margins are 0.46 to 0.53 cm (T1) and 0.008 to 0.14 cm (T2), the top tier's 0.003 (F6 uncalibrated) to 0.26 cm.
- Post hoc extension (nine capped cases rerun with a 2,000-step cap): running; results follow in this PR.
- Reading: on these nine wovens and this hanger family, H1 holds and H3 fails at the decision level. The 2D chain, which is wrong by 1.7 cm in absolute shape, picks the top tier's arm width every time, and the bending calibration that fixes the top tier's drape (4a) moves its scores by up to 0.1 cm but never its choice. The paired differences do not carry over better than absolute scores (E7): the tiers part most at the narrow end (−1 cm, where the cloth wraps the arm's end), which is far from the decision. So the decision is robust not because the tiers agree on differences everywhere, but because the best width sits in a basin all three see. The one split, F2 on the spring sheet at c = 0.485, has a T2 margin of 0.049 cm: the spring sheet is the tier whose choice is least safe, not the chain.
- Limits: one hanger profile and one arm depth; integer offsets (1b found +1.5 cm on its finer grid, which this grid cannot resolve); one start per case (T3 from T2), no seeds; 5 mm lattice only, where 4a's factor was fitted at 2.5 mm; nine unconverged T3 cases in the registered scoring; frictionless contact; simulated garments, no real hung shape.
- Next: (a) a finer width grid (0.25 cm) around +1 cm on T3, and a second arm depth, to find where the tiers' choices first part; (b) a 3.75 mm T3 check of the decisions; (c) milestone 5's margin bound applied to these tier pairs (can the chain's choice be certified from T2's paired errors?); (d) milestone 6, the multi-fidelity loop, now that the chain's choice is known to transfer on this family.

## 2026-11-20 · Milestone 4d: an edge-midpoint (Crouzeix-Raviart) membrane removes the locking at the fabrics' real stiffness and a fifth of the over-drape, but its stabilising penalty moves the drape by as much (predictions in `experiments/m4d_nonconforming/run.py`'s docstring, committed and pushed before any 5 or 3.75 mm solve)
- Why: 4c's Next (a). The conforming triangle locks; build the nonconforming element English and Bridson (2008) proposed for this, register a flat-in-EA drape coefficient (DC), and redo 4a's comparison.
- Built (`cr.py`): unknowns at the edge midpoints of 3a's lattice; 3a's Saint Venant-Kirchhoff membrane on each triangle; one discrete-shell hinge per interior edge between the two midpoint triangles sharing its midpoint, weight times kappa = 2.667 calibrated on a cylinder (within 1.8% over 0 to 90°); lumped midpoint mass; clamp where both ends of an edge are on the disc; DC and folds measured on vertices rebuilt from the midpoints (exact for affine fields). Without stabilisation the midpoint field has in-plane zero-energy modes at the free rim (30 on 15 mm, 39 on 12 mm) and Newton stalls; a penalty ks |P J|² on the tangential jump of the field across each edge (the in-plane part of Hansbo and Larson 2003) removes them. 7 tests.
- Pilots (disclosed in the docstring, F1 at 7.5 mm only, before the predictions): gamma 0 and ks = 1e-5 EA stall; ks ∝ EA converges but re-locks (DC 0.201 to 0.260 up the ladder); ks = gamma G is flat in EA to 0.009 but its level moves with gamma (0.251, 0.274, 0.318).
- Plan: FAST inputs, seed 0, 4a's ladder 150 N/m to the secant (×2 steps; ×4 for the sweep), 3a/3d Newton, lattices 5 and 3.75 mm. Part A gamma 0.1; part B gamma 0.01 and 1, and the conforming element on 5 mm.
- Predictions: D1 every stage converges; D2 (acceptance) p ≤ −0.8 on ≥ 7 of 9 at 3.75 mm; D3 (acceptance) |DC(secant) − DC(150)| < 0.01 on ≥ 8 of 9; D4 bias ≥ 0.044 below the conforming 3.75 mm bias and ≥ 0; D5 the gamma 1 − gamma 0.01 spread smaller on 3.75 than 5 mm on ≥ 7 of 9, mean ≤ 0.05; D6 Spearman ≥ 0.90; D7 RMSE ≤ 0.08; D8 |DC(3.75) − DC(5)| < 0.02 on ≥ 6 of 9.
- Changed after the first solves (disclosed): the first four 3.75 mm stages stopped at the cap with residuals 1e5 to 4e6 times the tolerance. The start drew 4a's 2 mm noise on the midpoints (1.9 mm apart), folding midpoint triangles over; it now draws 4a's start on the vertices with 4a's random numbers and averages to the midpoints (a conforming field with 4a's exact shape). Predictions unchanged; the failed attempt (1.2 core-hours) is not in the results.
- Outcome (`experiments/m4d_nonconforming/results.txt`, `results.json`, `run_log_A.txt`, `run_log_B.txt`, `run_log_attempt1_midpoint_start.txt` (the stalled first attempt), `dc_vs_ea_two_elements.svg`; 63 chains, 268 stages, about 7.3 core-hours plus the failed attempt): **D1 refuted:** 261 of 268; the seven misses are gamma 0.01 on 3.75 mm, F3 and F9 (every stage at the cap, residual 7e2 to 7e4 times the tolerance). **D2 refuted:** p −0.48 to −0.80 (1 of 9 at −0.8; F4 not fitted, its fold count changes). **D3 refuted:** rises +0.007 to +0.069 (1 of 9 under 0.01; conforming +0.022 to +0.069). **D4 refuted:** bias +0.102 against the conforming +0.133, a drop of 0.030. **D5 held, as scored:** spread smaller on 3.75 mm on 7 of 9, mean 0.059 → 0.048; on the seven converged fabrics alone 5 of 7, mean 0.062 → 0.050. **D6 held:** Spearman 0.967, Pearson 0.974. **D7 refuted:** RMSE 0.107. **D8 held:** 7 of 9.
- Post hoc (after part A's report): over the last ladder step the conforming element's stretch energy no longer falls (local exponent −0.10 to +0.30) while this one's does (−0.63 to −0.91), and the DC rise per doubling there is 0.0005 to 0.0067 against 0.0047 to 0.0111. Its DC increments shrink geometrically up the ladder (F9: 0.024, 0.018, 0.013, 0.009, 0.005), the conforming ones barely (0.021 to 0.0085).
- Reading: the edge-midpoint element is free of 4c's locking where it matters, at the wovens' measured stiffness (800 to 4,800 N/m): there EA has nearly stopped moving the DC. What remains of the rise from 150 N/m is the softer end of the ladder, where these fabrics are still stretching (strain near 1%). The fix is worth 0.030 of the +0.13 bias, less than 4c's 0.044 forecast. But the element needs a jump penalty to converge, and that numerical weight moves the DC by about 0.05 between 0.01 and 1, as much as locking did; the spread shrinks with the mesh only about as fast as h (0.059 → 0.048 for h 5 → 3.75 mm), and the weakest penalty fails to converge on two fabrics. So this tier still cannot quote an absolute DC to better than about ±0.025 at 3.75 mm, and the remaining +0.07 to +0.12 bias (by gamma) is not membrane locking.
- Limits: seed 0; two lattices, both coarser than 4a's main 2.5 mm; gamma has no physical value, so no setting is "right"; F3 and F9 at gamma 0.01 on 3.75 mm are unconverged; the penalty uses the mean tangent plane, one of several choices.
- Next: (a) a finer lattice (2.5 mm) at two gamma values to see whether the spread goes to zero, or a stabilisation that does not depend on a weight (a discontinuous Galerkin consistency term); (b) refit 4a's bending factor on this element (expected nearer 1 than 0.485); (c) a measured node count to separate bending from fold-state error; (d) milestone 1's hanger choice on the calibrated tier.

## 2026-11-18 · Milestone 4b: an orthotropic element (warp/weft bending and stretch kept apart) does not explain 4a's over-drape; it makes it slightly worse (predictions in `experiments/m4b_orthotropic/run.py`'s docstring, committed and pushed before any 2.5 mm solve)

- Post hoc (after the seed-0 rows): the calibrated lattice's B(φ), averaged over 0 to 90°, is 1.02 to 1.63 times the geometric mean (harmonic 1.02 to 1.29), so on this element orthotropy stiffens bending off the axes. On the four strongly anisotropic fabrics whose fold count did not jump (F1, F2, F4, F8) the DC rises as that predicts; across all nine the Spearman is +0.08, because fold-state changes (F7 +0.038 at ratio 1.2, F9 −0.086) are as large as the effect.
- Reading: the 0.485 factor is not the averaged-away anisotropy. The bias must come from something else: the mesh trend (4a M6, which grows with refinement), membrane locking at EA of 2,000 to 9,000 N/m, or the difference between FAST's cantilever bending and drape bending. A second finding is about method: one-seed comparisons of two models on this tier are not meaningful below about 0.01 DC, and fold-state jumps reach 0.1 (F9), so model comparisons need the pooled or interval rules of 3e to 3g.
- Limits: the hinge element's off-axis profile is fixed by the lattice and no measurement of bias bending was available to check it; seed 0 only for bend, stretch and the additivity test; one lattice orientation (warp along the rows); nine fabrics from one laboratory.
- Next: (a) the nonconforming (or locking-free) membrane, now the leading suspect for the bias, tested on the same nine at 3.75, 2.5 and 1.875 mm; (b) a second open DC table to test 4a's 0.485 factor out of sample (Europe PMC searched this session: no other open paper gives per-fabric FAST/KES and Cusick DC; the nylon CLO paper, PMC9773953, prints only regressions); (c) milestone 1's hanger choice on the calibrated tier.

## 2026-11-10 · Milestone 1b: does the 3 cm arm's width choice survive a converged solve? (predictions in `experiments/m1b_converged/run.py`'s docstring, written before the first run)
- Why: milestone 1's P3 held on 3000-iteration solves whose own error (0.05 to 0.11 cm between 3000 and 6000 iterations) is as large as the width margin (0.08 to 0.13 cm). A decision read off unconverged numbers is not a decision.
- Plan: wood-shaped profile on the 3 cm half-depth arm, fabrics A to D, offsets -1, 0, +0.5, +1, +1.5, +2, +3 cm; L-BFGS in 3000-iteration chunks, restarted from the last positions, until the whole-sheet error moves under 0.002 cm over a chunk (cap 60,000). The first chunk is milestone 1's solve and reproduces it (1.9212 cm on A at +1, checked before the run).
- Predictions: Q1 converged best integer offset +1 cm on all fabrics; Q2 converged margin more than twice the last-chunk change on all fabrics; Q3 the 3000-iteration error underestimates the converged one in every case, by at least 0.03 cm on average; Q4 converged best half-cm offset in [+0.5, +1.5].
- Outcome (`experiments/m1b_converged/results.txt`; 28 cases, 12,000 to 24,000 iterations each, about 8 min on 4 cores; every case met the 0.002 cm stopping rule, last-chunk change at most 0.0016 cm): **Q1 refuted** on fabric D: converged, +2 cm beats +1 cm by 0.066 cm (2.133 against 2.199). A to C keep +1 on the integer grid, but by 0.020 to 0.033 cm, against 0.11 to 0.13 cm at 3000 iterations. **Q2 held:** each margin is 8 to 25 times twice the last-chunk change. **Q3 refuted, and that is the finding:** the 3000-iteration error is not uniformly low. Its bias depends on the width: averaged over the fabrics, the converged error is 0.219 cm higher at -1 cm, 0.129 at +1, 0.026 at +2 and 0.065 cm *lower* at +3. Stopping early flatters narrow arms, so it pulls the chosen width down. **Q4 held:** the converged best on the half-cm grid is +1.5 cm on all four fabrics.
- Post hoc (`posthoc_2d.py`, not predicted): the 2D chain on the same grid picks +1.0 cm on all four fabrics, and sharply (fabric A: 0.18 cm at +1.0 against 0.51 at +1.5). So once it converges, the 3 cm arm disagrees with the 2D chain by 0.5 cm of arm width on every fabric. That is inside milestone 1's P3 tolerance of 1 cm, but it is a real tier disagreement where the 3D sheet adds physics (the front and back panels falling off a shallow arm). Milestone 1 hid it, because the iteration cap's bias towards narrow arms happened to land on the 2D answer.
- Reading: cheap-tier agreement can be produced by the solver budget alone. Decision-level fidelity needs convergence judged against the decision: a stopping rule on the change in error, checked against the margin (Q2's test), rather than a fixed iteration cap. The converged 3 cm curve is flat between +1 and +2 cm (A to C within 0.04 cm), so on a shallow arm the width is weakly identified, and a validated top tier (milestone 3) has a 0.5 cm disagreement to settle.
- Limits: the stopping rule bounds the change per chunk, not the distance to the true minimum (some cases still drift 0.001 cm per chunk; gradient max 1e-6 to 4.5e-4). Restarting L-BFGS per chunk differs slightly from one long run. The setup otherwise follows milestone 1: EA assumed, size M, one profile, no friction.
- Next: milestone 2 (simulated Cusick drape against a sourced table), and every later tier comparison uses the same convergence rule.

## 2026-11-10 · Milestone 1: does the 2D chain's choice of hanger survive in the 3D sheet on four fabrics with measured KES-FB bending rigidity and weight? (predictions in `experiments/m1_kes_fidelity/run.py`'s docstring, written before the first run)
- Why: every hanger design in track 18 so far comes from the 2D chain or the 3D sheet on assumed fabrics; `hanger-kes` sourced B and W for four fabrics but scored only the 2D chain. Before building a validated top tier (milestone 3), check whether the two cheap tiers even agree with each other on measured fabrics, on the decisions that matter: which stock hanger, which arm width.
- Plan: size M, EA 150 N/m, fabrics A to D of `hanger-kes`; three stock hangers and the wood-shaped profile at width offsets -1 to +3 cm; 2D chain at 5 mm rows, 3D slab arm (centre column), 3D 3 cm half-depth arm (whole sheet); a 3000-vs-6000-iteration convergence check on the softest and stiffest fabric.
- Predictions: P1 same stock ranking in all three models on all fabrics; P2 best offset identical in 2D and 3D slab; P3 3 cm arm's best offset within 1 cm of 2D; P4 |3D slab − 2D| under 0.10 cm for the wood-shaped hanger.
- Outcome (`experiments/m1_kes_fidelity/results.txt`, about 4 min on 4 cores; 4 tests pass): **P1 refuted, on a tie.** The wood-shaped hanger is best in every model on every fabric (0.38 to 0.47 cm on the slab, against 3.1 to 3.4 cm for wire and plastic); the one disagreement is second place on fabric D in the 2D chain, where wire and plastic both score 3.201 cm. **P2 held:** best width offset +1 cm in 2D and 3D slab on all four fabrics. **P3 held:** +1 cm on the 3 cm arm too. **P4 held:** the wood-shaped hanger's 3D slab and 2D errors differ by at most 0.004 cm.
- Convergence: on the slab arm, 3000 and 6000 L-BFGS iterations agree to 0.0004 cm; on the 3 cm arm they differ by 0.110 cm (fabric A) and 0.049 cm (D). The 3 cm arm's decision margins (best offset against the runner-up) are 0.126, 0.111, 0.115 and 0.077 cm, the same size as that solver error.
- Reading: on measured fabrics the two cheap tiers agree on every decision, but on a deep arm they agree to 0.004 cm because they are the same model in two coordinate systems, so their agreement is no evidence of fidelity; only the validated top tier (milestones 3 and 4) can test H1. Where the 3D sheet does add physics (a shallow arm), its numbers are not converged to the margin that decides the width, so `hanger-3d`'s shallow-arm figures (also run at 3000 iterations) carry about 0.05 to 0.1 cm of solver error. A decision-level check needs a convergence tolerance tied to the margin, which is the paired-difference idea of the proposal's Method.
- Limits: EA fixed at 150 N/m (not sourced), size M only, one hanger profile for the width sweep, the four fabrics are unfinished and from a trade-site PDF, no friction, no real garment.
- Next: milestone 2, a simulated Cusick drape test against a sourced table of drape coefficient with B and W for the same fabrics; and rerun the 3 cm arm cases to a gradient tolerance rather than an iteration cap.
