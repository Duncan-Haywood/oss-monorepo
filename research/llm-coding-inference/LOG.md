## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4d outcome: a label-free floor rule is valid in 8 of 9 categories and narrower than Wilson in 6 (prediction supported, in-sample)
- Ran `experiments/floor_rule.py` (500 draws per cell, ±1.9 points; results in `experiments/floor_rule.txt`; 22 s). Floor = clip(5 qbar, 0.02, 0.10) came out 0.020 for the five rarest categories, 0.047 to 0.059 for remorse, fear and disgust, 0.100 for sadness.
- Normal-IPW coverage: 94.8 to 96.0% for the five rarest, 92.4% remorse, 93.0% disgust, 94.4% fear, 95.0% sadness. So 8 of 9 reach 93% (remorse 92.4% is 0.6 under, inside one binomial sd of 1.0), against the refutation bar of three or more under.
- Width against uniform Wilson: 0.48 (grief), 0.54 (pride), 0.56 (relief), 0.65 (nervousness), 0.75 (remorse), 0.80 (embarrassment), 0.95 (fear), 1.00 (disgust), 1.13 (sadness). So six of nine are 20 to 52% narrower, and the prediction (three rarest at 0.90 or less) holds with room. Above ~1% prevalence the audit design buys nothing over uniform Wilson; it is merely valid.
- Limits: the multiplier 5 and the clip bounds were chosen after reading 4c on this same corpus, so this is an in-sample check of a one-parameter rule, not a test of it; one corpus, one weak non-LLM coder, 500 draws. The 0.02 floor leaves 49 to 59% of audits with no positive for the three rarest, where the interval still covers by design but a user would see a degenerate estimate.
- Next: milestone 5 (a real LLM coder; none available offline in this session, and an API-called one needs the owner's key); test the rule on a second corpus (NaijaSenti negative is not rare, so a rare-code corpus is needed) before it goes in the paper.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4d: choose the pi floor from the coder, no pilot labels (planned before running)
- Why: 4c left two regimes (floor 0.02 best below ~1% prevalence, 0.05 to 0.10 best above), and a rule must pick the floor without knowing the prevalence. The calibrated coder probability q is known for every corpus item with no human labels; its corpus mean qbar tracks prevalence (calibrated on the 30% train part). Rule fixed here, before running: floor = clip(5 * qbar, 0.02, 0.10). Same corpus, split, coder, calibration, n = 200 and normal IPW interval as 4c; 500 draws per cell (+/- 1.9 points). `experiments/floor_rule.py`.
- Prediction: the rule gives normal-IPW coverage of 93% or more in at least eight of nine categories, and a width at most 0.90 of uniform Wilson in at least the three rarest. Refuted if coverage is under 93% in three or more categories, or if no category is narrower than uniform Wilson by 10%. The rule is a guess made after seeing 4c on this corpus, so even a pass is in-sample for the floor range; I will say so.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4c outcome: a bigger pi floor helps the common-rare categories and hurts the rarest (prediction refuted)
- Ran `experiments/pi_floor.py` (500 draws per cell, ±1.9 points; same corpus, split, coder and calibration as milestone 4; results in `experiments/pi_floor.txt`). Planned before running: raising the floor on the inclusion weight w = max(floor, sqrt(q(1-q))) from 0.02 to 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 and 0.50 (0.50 is uniform sampling) puts audited positives in more audits and so brings the normal and Wilson IPW intervals to 93% or more in all nine categories. Refuted if any category stays under 93% at every floor.
- Refuted for the six rarest categories (0.10 to 0.89%): the floor 0.02 is the best of the five, and coverage falls as the floor rises (grief 96.0% at 0.02, 80.2% at 0.05, 59.4% at uniform; relief 94.8 / 86.2 / 69.2%; nervousness 95.0 / 98.0 / 80.0%). Share of audits holding no positive rises with the floor (grief 59% to 85%). The 0.02 floor concentrates the audit where the coder says "uncertain", which is where the few positives sit; more uniform sampling dilutes it, and a normal interval on an audit with zero positives is too narrow.
- Supported for the three commonest (1.05 to 1.96%): a floor of 0.05 to 0.10 gives normal-IPW coverage 93.0 to 95.6% (disgust 93.0 / 93.4%, fear 94.8 / 95.6%, sadness 93.4 / 95.0%), against 88.8 / 92.8 / 90.2% at 0.02. Width there is 0.98 to 1.13 times the uniform Wilson width, so the gain over the valid baseline is gone. Remorse (0.89%) covers 91.4 to 93.6% at every floor.
- The Kish-size Wilson interval is not a fix at any floor: it covers 55 to 96%, is erratic in width (up to 13 times the uniform Wilson width in some cells, from audits whose variance estimate is near zero), and is below the normal interval in most cells at floors above 0.02.
- So the two goals conflict: below about 1% prevalence only the 0.02 floor is near-valid and it is 44 to 52% narrower than uniform Wilson (grief, pride, relief; 0.48 to 0.56 of the width); from 1% up, a floor of 0.05 to 0.10 is valid but no narrower than uniform Wilson. A single audit rule would need the floor to depend on the category's (unknown) prevalence; the coder-positive rate or a pilot audit is the obvious proxy and is untested.
- Limits: one corpus, one weak non-LLM coder, one calibration rule, 500 draws per cell, floors on a coarse grid, the true prevalence is only known because the corpus is hand-coded. Still not tested: an exact conditional interval, and a real LLM coder (milestone 5).
- Next: milestone 5 with a real (local) LLM coder; choosing the floor from a pilot audit.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4b outcome: neither small-count interval fixes the weighted audit (refuted)

It fixes sadness (95.4% vs 87.5%) but is 1.3 to 1.6 times as wide as the normal IPW interval there, and undercovers where the audit holds almost no positives, because the effective size from a variance estimated on 0 to 3 positives is itself unreliable.
- Percentile bootstrap (`ipw_audit_bootstrap`) covers 93.8 and 93.9% for grief and pride but 88.6 to 92.6% for the middle categories and 77.5 to 89.8% for disgust, fear and sadness (worse than normal: resampling the audit cannot see the positives it lacks, and the heavy 1/pi terms make the percentile interval skewed).

The normal IPW interval remains the best of the three at the six rarest categories (93.2 to 96.1% before, 94.0 to 95.9% now) and fails at disgust and sadness; Wilson is the better choice only above ~1.5% prevalence. No interval tried is valid across the whole range; a design-based fix (a larger floor on pi, so every audit holds positives, or an exact conditional interval) is the open piece.
- Limits: one corpus and coder, 1,000 draws per cell (+/- 1.4 points), fixed corpus.
- Next: pi floor sweep and an exact (Clopper-Pearson-type) weighted interval; then milestone 5 with a real LLM coder.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4 outcome: H2 and H4 refuted as stated; the useful result is about validity
- Ran `experiments/rare_audit.py` (results in `experiments/rare_audit.txt`): GoEmotions parts 1 to 3, 54,619 examples after the filters, corpus 38,234, nine categories from 0.10% (grief) to 1.96% (sadness), n = 200, 1,000 draws per cell. Deviation from the plan: naive Bayes gave precision 0.00 to 0.03, so the stand-in coder became a class-weighted logistic regression on word presence (precision 0.04 to 0.24, recall 0.07 to 0.78); still weak, still not an LLM. Also the stratified and the plain-human stratified rows are identical by construction (the coder is constant inside a coder-label stratum), so "label-stratified PPI" is just post-stratified hand-coding; the coder's codes only define the strata.
- **Validity first.** The normal-approximation intervals break at low prevalence with n = 200: uniform human-only covers 16% (grief, 0.10%) to 61% (0.42%), 83 to 93% from 0.9%; PPI++ the same or worse; label-stratified 10 to 81%. Wilson on uniform human-only covers 91 to 99% in every category. The coder-uncertainty IPW audit (Q) covers 93.2 to 96.1% for the six categories at or under 0.9% and 94.4% for fear (1.05%), but only 85.6% for disgust (1.32%) and 89.0% for sadness (1.96%).
- **H2: refuted.** Uniform PPI++ is within 10% of human-only width in eight of nine categories (0.93 to 0.98 of it; remorse 0.87), so the first half holds, but it is irrelevant when both undercover. The stratified audit cut width by 25% or more in 2 of 9 (pride 26%, remorse 47%; fear 22% falls short) and by under 10% in 3 (grief 2%, relief 8%, nervousness 6%), with coverage 10 to 81%, so no valid gain. Refutation condition met (gain under 10% in several categories).
- **H4: refuted as stated.** Label stratification is not valid in normal approximation here, and it differs from uncertainty sampling by far more than 10%. What survives: against the only valid baseline at these prevalences (Wilson), uncertainty-IPW intervals are 45 to 52% narrower for grief, pride and relief (0.48 to 0.55 of the width), 22 to 34% narrower for embarrassment, nervousness and remorse, 5 to 13% for disgust and fear (disgust at 85.6% coverage), and wider for sadness (1.04), with coverage 93 to 96% except disgust and sadness.
- Limits: one corpus, a weak stand-in coder, truth = majority of 3+ raters (stricter than any single rater: prevalences are small), one uncertainty rule (pi proportional to sqrt(q(1-q)) from train deciles, floor 0.02), a fixed corpus, normal intervals for the IPW design (a small-count-safe interval for IPW audits is the next thing to build; its undercoverage at 1 to 2% is probably the same normal-approximation failure). Not tested: that a better coder moves the gain (milestone 5).
- Next: a small-count-safe interval for weighted and stratified audits (Wilson-type or bootstrap), then milestone 5 with a real LLM coder.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4: H2 and H4, rare-category and audit-design benchmark (planned before running)
- Data: GoEmotions part 1 (Demszky et al. 2020; Reddit comments, ~58k after the `qual-real` filters: at least 3 raters, not flagged unclear). Truth for a category = a majority of raters marked that emotion. Nine categories chosen for their prevalence, not their difficulty: grief, pride, relief, embarrassment, nervousness, remorse, disgust, fear, sadness (the true prevalence in the corpus is printed first and decides which fall at or under 2%). Seeded 30/70 split; the 70% test part is the corpus, so truth is known.
- Coder: a multinomial naive-Bayes log-odds score trained per category on the 30% part (a stand-in, not an LLM; milestone 5). Coder label = score above a threshold set on the train part so that coder-positives are about 3x the category's prevalence. Scores are binned into deciles on the train part to give a calibrated probability q for uncertainty sampling.
- Audit designs, n = 200 expected: (U) uniform, with human-only and PPI++; (L) coder-label stratified 60 positive / 140 negative, with stratified PPI and stratified human-only; (Q) uncertainty sampling, Poisson inclusion pi_i proportional to sqrt(q_i(1-q_i)) (floored at 0.02, scaled to 200 expected), `ipw_audit`. Also human-only with a Wilson interval, as the small-count-safe baseline. 1,000 draws per cell, fixed corpus, seed fixed.
- Prediction. H2: at prevalence <= 2%, PPI++ under U is within 10% of the width of human-only; stratified L cuts width by at least 25% at equal n. H4: L and Q have narrower intervals than U below 5% and are within 10% of each other, with coverage judged against 95% (+/- 1.4 points). I expect the normal-approximation intervals to undercover in the rarest categories (the simulated result says 57% at 1%), which would mean "narrower" is not "valid" there; I will report width only for cells that cover at least 93%, and say so where a gain exists only by undercovering.
- Refuted per the proposal: H2 if uniform PPI++ is 25% or more narrower than human-only at 2% or below, or L gains under 10%. H4 if either design loses to uniform, or they differ by more than 10%.
