## 2026-10-25 · Milestone 6i: does cross-fitting the PPI++ weight remove the coverage cost of tuning it on the audit? (planned before running)
- Why: 6h's next item. 6h chose lam per coefficient on the same audit that is then used for the estimate; coverage was 93.3 to 95.6% (up to about one point under human-only), which that run could not separate from noise or from the selection effect. Two-fold cross-fitting removes the selection effect by construction: lam for each half of the audit is chosen on the other half (`llmci.regression.ppi_ols_crossfit`), the two half estimates are averaged and the se is sqrt(se_A^2 + se_B^2)/2 (the half-audit se is sqrt(2) times the full one, so the average has about the full width; the cost is a lam estimated from n/2 = 200 rows). Same corpus, coders, n = 400, 1,000 draws and audit draws as 6g/6h (`experiments/h6i_crossfit.py`; the split permutation is drawn from the same generator after each audit, so the audits after the first differ from 6h's and the in-sample column below is a fresh replicate of 6h, not a copy).
- **Predictions (before running):** (1) cross-fit coverage is 93 to 96.5% in all 15 cells. (2) Mean over the 15 cells of coverage is within 0.3 points of 6h's in-sample figure (I expect no visible gain: 6h's loss was at most 1 point and the binomial sd per cell is 0.7). (3) Mean se over human-only is at most 1.03 in every cell. (4) nb-in-language keeps at least 3 of 5 coefficients below ratio 0.99 (the cost of estimating lam on 200 rows may erase part of 6h's 1 to 7% gain; I expect the gain to shrink, not vanish).
- Refutes: any cell under 92% coverage; any se ratio over 1.03; fewer than 3 nb-in-language coefficients under 0.99.
- Not tested: a real LLM coder (milestone 5), more folds, a cross-fitted lam that also uses the corpus (unlabelled rows), stratified audits.

### Outcome (`experiments/h6i_crossfit.py`, about 4 min; `experiments/h6i_crossfit.txt`; 1,000 draws per cell, numpy)
- **(1) held.** Cross-fit coverage is 94.4 to 95.9% in all 15 cells (human-only 95.0 to 95.8%).
- **(2) held, and the premise of the milestone weakens.** Mean coverage over the 15 cells: cross-fit 95.15%, in-sample tuned 95.15% (fresh replicate; cells 94.3 to 95.8%), human-only 95.30%. The one-point loss seen in 6h (lowest 93.3%) did not reproduce in the replicate, so it was within the noise of 1,000 draws (binomial sd 0.7 points); I cannot show that in-sample tuning costs coverage at this n, and cross-fitting therefore buys no measurable coverage here.
- **(3) held.** Cross-fit se over human-only is 0.943 to 1.021 (the worst cell is the lexicon-en intercept, 1.021; bar 1.03).

## 2026-10-25 · Milestone 6h: does power-tuned PPI++ for regression at least match human-only OLS? (planned before running)
- Why: 6g's last line. - Refutes: any cell with coverage under 92%; any se ratio above 1.03; no nb-in-language coefficient under 0.98.
- Not tested: a real LLM coder, cross-fitted lam, stratified audits, other covariate sets.
- **Correction found by the first run (disclosed).** My first construction kept the full-corpus matrix M in the estimating equation and only varied lam. ## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6a: H5, the plug-in width formula and the audit-size rule (planned before running)
