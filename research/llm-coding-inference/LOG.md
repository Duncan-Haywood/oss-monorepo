## 2026-10-25 · Milestone 6d: the sequential audit at a 1-point target (planned before running)
- Why: 6c's open item. At a 3-point target the audit is about 900 items and the p = 20 start (80 items) is a tenth of it; at 1 point the audit is about 9x larger, the sampling fraction reaches 20% in some strata (finite-population effects), and the stopping rule is exercised over ~70 rounds. Same corpus, split, coders, floor (0.02) and start (p = 20) as 6c; target 1.0 point; batches B in {100, 300}; cap 20,000; 300 draws per cell (+/- 2.5 points on coverage). Oracle n from the 6a Neyman rule at 1 point. `experiments/h6d_tight_target.py`.
- **H6d-i (coverage):** coverage at the stopping time is at least 92% in all 6 cells (300 draws, so the bar allows for binomial noise of about 1.3 points). Refuted if any cell is below 92%.
- **H6d-ii (price):** mean stopped n at B = 100 is within 10% of the oracle n for every coder. Refuted otherwise.
- **H6d-iii (target hit):** 100% of audits stop under the cap at half-width at most 1.0 points.
- **H6d-iv (relative spread shrinks):** the sd of stopped n divided by its mean at B = 100 is below that of 6c at B = 30 (0.038, 0.040, 0.052) for every coder.
- Not tested: a real LLM coder, a second corpus, a rarer category.

### Outcome (`experiments/h6d_tight_target.py`, about 17 min; `experiments/h6d_tight_target.txt`; 300 draws per cell)
- **H6d-i supported.** Coverage at the stopping time is 93.0 to 96.0% in all 6 cells (lowest: `lexicon-en`, B = 100, 93.0%; bar 92%).
- **H6d-ii refuted.** Mean stopped n at B = 100 is 6,937, 8,069 and 7,098 against the oracle 7,833, 9,240 and 8,039: 11.4%, 12.7% and 11.7% under, outside the 10% bar. At a 3-point target the gap was under 1%. The rule stops earlier than the plug-in Neyman size, yet coverage holds, so the oracle size is not what the stopped audit needs here. I did not test why; candidate causes are the sampling fraction (up to about a quarter of a stratum is audited, so the plug-in's constant variances and the realised without-replacement variances can differ) and stopping when the estimate happens to look narrow. Neither is checked.
- **H6d-iii supported.** 100% of audits stopped under the cap at half-width at most 1.0 points (mean 0.99 to 1.00).
- **H6d-iv supported.** sd/mean of stopped n at B = 100 is 0.011, 0.012, 0.015, against 0.038, 0.040, 0.052 for 6c at B = 30. B = 300 costs 75 to 144 more tweets than B = 100.
- Limits: one corpus, three stand-in coders, 300 draws (coverage +/- 2.5 points), no optional-stopping correction, no real LLM coder; the oracle-gap cause is open.
- Next: find why the stopped n sits under the oracle (compare realised half-width at fixed n against the plug-in); a rarer category; milestone 5 with a real coder.

## 2026-10-25 · Milestone 6c: a sequential audit that stops when the estimated width meets the target (planned before running)
- Why: 6b's open item. Planning from a pilot hits the +/-3 point target about half the time. The alternative is to audit in batches and stop the first time the estimated half-width (Z times the stratified-PPI standard error on the items audited so far) is at most 3.0 points. The width then meets the target by construction; what is unknown is the price in audit size and in coverage, because stopping on a noisy estimate of the width is optional stopping and tends to stop when the sample happens to look precise. Code: `experiments/h6c_sequential.py`; same corpus, split, three stand-in coders, seed scheme and floor (0.02) as `h6b_pilot.py`. Start with p = 20 per stratum (the 6b pilot), then add batches of B items split across strata by the Neyman shares from the current variance estimates (largest w_h / (n_h + 1) first, without replacement); B in {30, 90}; hard cap 2,500 items; 1,000 draws per cell.
- **H6c-i (coverage survives optional stopping):** coverage of the 95% interval at the stopping time is at least 93% in every one of the 6 cells (3 coders, 2 batch sizes). Refuted if any cell is below 93%.
- **H6c-ii (price):** mean stopped n at B = 30 is within 10% of the oracle n of 6a (902, 1,083, 927) for every coder. Refuted otherwise. My expectation is that it sits slightly under the oracle, since noisy estimates stop some audits early.
- **H6c-iii (target hit):** at least 99% of audits stop under the cap with half-width at most 3.0 points. Refuted otherwise (would show the cap binds).
- **H6c-iv (batch size trade):** mean n at B = 90 exceeds mean n at B = 30 for every coder (overshoot of up to a batch), by at most 90 items. Refuted otherwise.
- Reported without a bar: sd of stopped n, 5th and 95th percentiles, and the 6b p = 40 mean n and share under 3.0 for comparison.
- Not tested: a real LLM coder, a second corpus, a corrected (always-valid or alpha-spending) interval.

### Outcome (`experiments/h6c_sequential.py`, about 2.5 min; `experiments/h6c_sequential.txt`; 1,000 draws per cell)
- **H6c-i supported.** Coverage at the stopping time is 94.2 to 95.2% in all 6 cells (lowest: `nb-in-language`, B = 30, 94.2%). Stopping on the estimated width did not visibly cost coverage on this corpus.
- **H6c-ii supported.** Mean stopped n at B = 30 is 898, 1,074, 919 against the oracle 902, 1,083, 927 (0.4 to 0.9% under, as expected).
- **H6c-iii supported.** 100% of audits in every cell stopped under the cap at a half-width of at most 3.0 points (the rule meets the target by construction; the cap never bound).
- **H6c-iv supported.** B = 90 costs 29 to 30 more items on average than B = 30 (928, 1,103, 948), well inside one batch.
- Against 6b: the sequential rule hits the target in 100% of audits where a p = 20 pilot plan hit it in 44 to 52%, at essentially the oracle mean n. The sd of the audit size is 34 to 48 (B = 30), against 79 to 119 for the p = 40 pilot plan, because the stopping time absorbs the variance-estimation error instead of fixing n in advance. The price is operational: the audit has to be hand-coded in rounds (B = 30 means about 30 rounds) rather than in one pass.
- Limits: one corpus, three stand-in coders, one target (3 points), one start p = 20 and floor 0.02, no tuning of either; coverage is measured against the true share of this one corpus and the nominal normal interval was used with no optional-stopping correction, so the result says it is not needed here, not that it is never needed (a coder with heavier-tailed residuals or a much smaller target could differ); no real LLM coder.
- Next: a harder case for the stopping rule (rarer category or a 1-point target, where early variance estimates are poorer); milestone 5 with a real coder; a second corpus.

## 2026-10-25 · Milestone 6b: plan the audit size from a small pilot, not from oracle variances (planned before running)
- Why: 6a's size rule used the true per-stratum variances of r = y - f, which nobody has before auditing. The real use is two stage: audit p items per stratum as a pilot, estimate S_h^2 by the sample variance of r (floor 0.02 so a clean pilot cannot give zero), run the Neyman rule for a +/-3 point target to get the total n, top up each stratum to its Neyman share (never below p), and estimate with stratified PPI on all audited items (pilot included: within a stratum the pilot plus top-up is one simple random sample of size n_h). Code: `experiments/h6b_pilot.py`; same corpus, split, three stand-in coders, target and seed scheme as `h5_plugin.py`; p in {10, 20, 40} per stratum (pilot 40, 80, 160 items); 1,000 draws per cell.
- **H6b-i (mean width near target):** at p = 20 the mean simulated half-width is within 10% of 3 points (2.7 to 3.3) for all three coders. Refuted otherwise.
- **H6b-ii (a coin flip, not a guarantee):** at p = 20 the share of audits whose half-width is at most 3.0 points lies between 30% and 70% for every coder. The estimated variance is noisy and n is a function of it, so I expect no better than even odds of meeting the target; outside the range would mean the pilot estimate is biased.
- **H6b-iii (coverage):** coverage of the 95% interval is at least 93% in every one of the 9 cells (the plan depends on the pilot, which could hurt it). Refuted if any cell is below 93%.
- **H6b-iv (pilot size buys stability, not the mean):** the sd of the realised total n across draws at p = 10 is at least 1.5 times that at p = 40, for every coder.
- Reported without a bar: mean total n against the oracle n of 6a (902, 1,083, 927), the 95th percentile of the half-width, and the share of audits within 3.3 points.
- Not tested: a real LLM coder, a second corpus, a sequential rule that keeps auditing until the width is met.

### Outcome (`experiments/h6b_pilot.py`, about 30 s; `experiments/h6b_pilot.txt`; 1,000 draws per cell)
- **H6b-i supported.** At p = 20 the mean half-width is 3.03, 3.04 and 3.10 points (lexicon-en, nb-english, nb-in-language), inside 2.7 to 3.3.
- **H6b-ii supported.** Share of audits at or under 3.0 points at p = 20: 52.3%, 49.3%, 43.8%, as expected a coin flip. At p = 10 it is 42.7 to 43.7% and the 95th percentile of the half-width is 4.0 to 4.5 points; at p = 40 it is 50.1 to 54.7% with the 95th percentile at 3.2 to 3.4. Meeting a 10% looser bar (3.3 points) takes p = 40 (91.4 to 98.0%), not p = 20 (76.1 to 89.6%).
- **H6b-iii supported.** Coverage is 94.1 to 95.7% in all nine cells; the planning step does not hurt it.
- **H6b-iv supported.** The sd of the total n falls from 174, 240, 258 (p = 10) to 79, 109, 119 (p = 40), a ratio of 2.17 to 2.20 (bar 1.5).
- Mean total n at p = 40 (pilot 160 items included) is 892, 1,076, 917 against the oracle 902, 1,083, 927: the pilot costs nothing in expectation because the pilot items are reused in the final estimate, and the mean n is within 1.1% of the oracle at p = 40 and within 4% at p = 10. What the pilot changes is the spread: at p = 10 the audit size ranges widely (sd about 20% to 30% of n).
- Reading: planning from a pilot works on this corpus as a default (valid, unbiased in mean n), but it hits the target about half the time, so a user who needs "at most 3 points" should plan for a tighter target (about 10%, 3.3 vs 3.0) or use a pilot of 40 per stratum; a sequential rule that audits until the width is met is the clean alternative and is not tested here.
- Limits: same corpus, three stand-in coders, one target, one floor (0.02, not tuned: no other value tried); the pilot is a simple random sample per stratum, oracle strata sizes are known; no real LLM coder.
- Next: a sequential stopping rule (audit in batches until the estimated width is met) and its coverage; milestone 5 with a real coder; a second corpus.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6a: H5, the plug-in width formula and the audit-size rule (planned before running)
- Data and coders as milestone 3 (NaijaSenti, four language strata, three stand-in coders). Plug-in half-width = 1.96 sqrt(sum_h W_h^2 (1 - n_h/N_h) S_h^2 / n_h) with S_h^2 the variance of r = y - f in stratum h, written from the stratum's prevalence, sensitivity and specificity (S_h^2 = [fn + fp - (fn - fp)^2] N_h/(N_h - 1), fn = pi(1 - sens), fp = (1 - pi)(1 - spec)). The inputs are the true finite-stratum values (an oracle), so this tests the formula, not the planning from a pilot. Designs: equal quota (B), 70% Hausa (C) and Neyman allocation at n = 400. `experiments/h5_plugin.py`.
- Prediction (H5): simulated mean half-width is within 10% of the plug-in in all 9 cells (ratio 0.90 to 1.10); in fact the formula is an identity for the mean of the variance estimator up to Jensen's gap, so I expect ratios of 0.98 to 1.00. Audit-size rule: Neyman total n for a 3-point half-width gives a simulated mean half-width within 5% of 3 points and coverage of 93% or more. Refuted if any ratio is outside 0.90 to 1.10.
- What this cannot show: the formula is closed-form and nearly tautological for the width of the variance estimate; it says nothing about planning when sens/spec must be estimated from a pilot, which is the harder claim and is left open.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4c outcome: a bigger pi floor helps the common-rare categories and hurts the rarest (prediction refuted)
- Ran `experiments/pi_floor.py` (500 draws per cell, ±1.9 points; same corpus, split, coder and calibration as milestone 4; results in `experiments/pi_floor.txt`). Planned before running: raising the floor on the inclusion weight w = max(floor, sqrt(q(1-q))) from 0.02 to 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 and 0.50 (0.50 is uniform sampling) puts audited positives in more audits and so brings the normal and Wilson IPW intervals to 93% or more in all nine categories. Refuted if any category stays under 93% at every floor.
- Refuted for the six rarest categories (0.10 to 0.89%): the floor 0.02 is the best of the five, and coverage falls as the floor rises (grief 96.0% at 0.02, 80.2% at 0.05, 59.4% at uniform; relief 94.8 / 86.2 / 69.2%; nervousness 95.0 / 98.0 / 80.0%). Share of audits holding no positive rises with the floor (grief 59% to 85%). The 0.02 floor concentrates the audit where the coder says "uncertain", which is where the few positives sit; more uniform sampling dilutes it, and a normal interval on an audit with zero positives is too narrow.
- Supported for the three commonest (1.05 to 1.96%): a floor of 0.05 to 0.10 gives normal-IPW coverage 93.0 to 95.6% (disgust 93.0 / 93.4%, fear 94.8 / 95.6%, sadness 93.4 / 95.0%), against 88.8 / 92.8 / 90.2% at 0.02. Width there is 0.98 to 1.13 times the uniform Wilson width, so the gain over the valid baseline is gone. Remorse (0.89%) covers 91.4 to 93.6% at every floor.
- The Kish-size Wilson interval is not a fix at any floor: it covers 55 to 96%, is erratic in width (up to 13 times the uniform Wilson width in some cells, from audits whose variance estimate is near zero), and is below the normal interval in most cells at floors above 0.02.
- So the two goals conflict: below about 1% prevalence only the 0.02 floor is near-valid and it is 44 to 52% narrower than uniform Wilson (grief, pride, relief; 0.48 to 0.56 of the width); from 1% up, a floor of 0.05 to 0.10 is valid but no narrower than uniform Wilson. A single audit rule would need the floor to depend on the category's (unknown) prevalence; the coder-positive rate or a pilot audit is the obvious proxy and is untested.
- Limits: one corpus, one weak non-LLM coder, one calibration rule, 500 draws per cell, floors on a coarse grid, the true prevalence is only known because the corpus is hand-coded. Still not tested: an exact conditional interval, and a real LLM coder (milestone 5).
- Next: milestone 5 with a real (local) LLM coder; choosing the floor from a pilot audit.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4 outcome: H2 and H4 refuted as stated; the useful result is about validity
- Ran `experiments/rare_audit.py` (results in `experiments/rare_audit.txt`): GoEmotions parts 1 to 3, 54,619 examples after the filters, corpus 38,234, nine categories from 0.10% (grief) to 1.96% (sadness), n = 200, 1,000 draws per cell. Deviation from the plan: naive Bayes gave precision 0.00 to 0.03, so the stand-in coder became a class-weighted logistic regression on word presence (precision 0.04 to 0.24, recall 0.07 to 0.78); still weak, still not an LLM. Also the stratified and the plain-human stratified rows are identical by construction (the coder is constant inside a coder-label stratum), so "label-stratified PPI" is just post-stratified hand-coding; the coder's codes only define the strata.
- **Validity first.** The normal-approximation intervals break at low prevalence with n = 200: uniform human-only covers 16% (grief, 0.10%) to 61% (0.42%), 83 to 93% from 0.9%; PPI++ the same or worse; label-stratified 10 to 81%. Wilson on uniform human-only covers 91 to 99% in every category. The coder-uncertainty IPW audit (Q) covers 93.2 to 96.1% for the six categories at or under 0.9% and 94.4% for fear (1.05%), but only 85.6% for disgust (1.32%) and 89.0% for sadness (1.96%).
- **H2: refuted.** Uniform PPI++ is within 10% of human-only width in eight of nine categories (0.93 to 0.98 of it; remorse 0.87), so the first half holds, but it is irrelevant when both undercover. The stratified audit cut width by 25% or more in 2 of 9 (pride 26%, remorse 47%; fear 22% falls short) and by under 10% in 3 (grief 2%, relief 8%, nervousness 6%), with coverage 10 to 81%, so no valid gain. Refutation condition met (gain under 10% in several categories).
- **H4: refuted as stated.** Label stratification is not valid in normal approximation here, and it differs from uncertainty sampling by far more than 10%. What survives: against the only valid baseline at these prevalences (Wilson), uncertainty-IPW intervals are 45 to 52% narrower for grief, pride and relief (0.48 to 0.55 of the width), 22 to 34% narrower for embarrassment, nervousness and remorse, 5 to 13% for disgust and fear (disgust at 85.6% coverage), and wider for sadness (1.04), with coverage 93 to 96% except disgust and sadness.
- Limits: one corpus, a weak stand-in coder, truth = majority of 3+ raters (stricter than any single rater: prevalences are small), one uncertainty rule (pi proportional to sqrt(q(1-q)) from train deciles, floor 0.02), a fixed corpus, normal intervals for the IPW design (a small-count-safe interval for IPW audits is the next thing to build; its undercoverage at 1 to 2% is probably the same normal-approximation failure). ## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 4: H2 and H4, rare-category and audit-design benchmark (planned before running)
