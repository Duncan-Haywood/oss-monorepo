# llm-coding-inference: how much hand-coding can an LLM coder replace, with a valid interval?

**Status: open research programme; estimator library built (`llmci/`); H1 supported on real data (milestone 3), replicated more weakly on a second corpus (3b); one preliminary simulated result below.** When an LLM codes qualitative text and you want a valid confidence interval for a prevalence, a group gap or a regression coefficient, how small can the hand-coded audit be, and how should it be sampled, when the model's error differs between groups and the category is rare? See [`PROPOSAL.md`](PROPOSAL.md) for the question, related work, gap, hypotheses H1 to H5, method, evaluation, milestones and target venue, and [`LOG.md`](LOG.md) for the running log.

**Live page:** https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/research/llm-coding-inference/

It consolidates the `qual-*` projects (track 19 in [`../PROJECTS.md`](../PROJECTS.md)), which found on GoEmotions with stand-in coders (no LLM) that naive counts are confidently wrong, that prediction-powered inference is valid but no narrower than hand-coding for weak coders, and that a random audit cannot estimate a rare group. Starting points: [`qual-correction`](../qual-correction), [`qual-real`](../qual-real), [`qual-dialect`](../qual-dialect), [`qual-rareerror`](../qual-rareerror).

Related labs: none claimed; this is methods work for computational social science. Written with AI assistance (Claude Code); no affiliation is implied with any author cited.

**Preliminary result (simulated, stand-in coder).** The normal-approximation interval of a coder-label-stratified audit covers 57% at 1% prevalence, 83% at 3%, 92% at 10% and 94% at 20% (nominal 95%; 200 audited items of 4,000; [`experiments/rare_coverage.txt`](experiments/rare_coverage.txt)). Audit-design comparisons for rare categories therefore need small-count-safe intervals first. Reproduce: `python3 experiments/rare_coverage.py`.

**Paper:** draft 1 (one small zero-shot LLM, regression and small-audit sections) is in [`paper/paper.md`](paper/paper.md) with [`references.bib`](paper/references.bib).

**Real-data result (H1, milestone 3).** On 44,485 NaijaSenti tweets in four languages with native-speaker labels, with the audit's language mix different from the corpus's, the pooled prediction-powered 95% interval covers as little as 67% (a 70%-Hausa audit of 400 tweets, English-trained coder) and 69% (in-language coder); PPI++ is 89%; even pooled hand-coding covers only 87% under an equal-quota audit. A language-stratified estimator with corpus-known language sizes covers 93.8 to 95.9% in every design (the planned bar was 94%; one cell is 0.2 points under). It is not narrower than stratified hand-coding, because these stand-in coders are weak. No LLM yet; one corpus; three audit mixes. Reproduce: `python3 experiments/group_shift.py` (needs the `qual-dialect` tweet cache); table in [`experiments/group_shift.txt`](experiments/group_shift.txt).

**Rare-category result (H2 and H4, milestone 4; both refuted as stated).** On GoEmotions (38,234 comments, nine emotions from 0.10% to 1.96% prevalence, audit of 200, 1,000 draws, a weak stand-in coder) the normal-approximation intervals of uniform human-only, PPI++ and coder-label-stratified audits cover as little as 10 to 61% below 0.5% prevalence, so a "narrower" interval there is not a valid one. Wilson intervals on a uniform audit cover 91 to 99%. An audit that samples by coder uncertainty with inverse-probability weights covers 93 to 96% for seven of the nine categories (those at or under 1.05%) and is 22 to 52% narrower than Wilson for six of them, but covers only 86% (disgust) and 89% (sadness) for the two most common ([`experiments/rare_audit.txt`](experiments/rare_audit.txt), [`LOG.md`](LOG.md)). Two small-count-safe intervals for that weighted audit (Wilson at a Kish-type effective size; a bootstrap) do not fix it: Wilson covers 82 to 84% for grief and pride, the bootstrap 78 to 90% for the three commonest, so no interval tried is valid over the whole 0.1 to 2% range (milestone 4b). Raising the floor on the audit's inclusion probability does not fix it either (milestone 4c, [`experiments/pi_floor.txt`](experiments/pi_floor.txt)): the 0.02 floor is the best below 0.9% prevalence (grief 96.0% coverage, 80.2% at floor 0.05, 59.4% for uniform sampling), while 0.05 to 0.10 brings the three commonest categories to 93 to 96% but then the interval is no narrower than uniform Wilson.

**Second corpus for H1 (milestone 3b).** On 6,683 GoEmotions comments grouped by subreddit type (sports, tv-dating, personal, video-cringe; target: a majority of raters marked a negative emotion; stand-in logistic-regression coders, audit of 400, 1,000 draws), the group-shift failure is much smaller than on NaijaSenti: pooled PPI covers 91.4 to 95.6% in all 18 cells, with the one clear miss (91.4%, bias +1.74 points) for a coder trained on sports only and tested on a 70%-sports audit. Language-stratified-style (here group-stratified) PPI covers 93.5 to 95.0% in every cell but is wider than stratified hand-coding. Reading: undercoverage grows with between-group differences in coder error, which are small in this corpus (agreement within 5 points). Reproduce: `python3 experiments/group_shift_reddit.py` (needs the GoEmotions cache); table in [`experiments/group_shift_reddit.txt`](experiments/group_shift_reddit.txt).

**Planning result (H5, milestone 6a; supported).** On the NaijaSenti corpus, the half-width of the language-stratified PPI interval follows the plug-in formula built from each stratum's prevalence, sensitivity, specificity and the audit allocation: simulated over predicted is 0.996 to 1.000 in all nine design-by-coder cells (1,000 draws each; the bar was 0.90 to 1.10), although the formula is close to an identity for the mean variance estimate, so the informative part is what it implies. A Neyman-allocation audit-size rule for a 3-point half-width gives 888, 1,063 and 912 hand-coded tweets for the three stand-in coders and hits 3.00 points on average (95th percentile 3.09 to 3.13; coverage 94.6 to 95.8%; an earlier version of the rule had a typo in the finite-population term, corrected in milestone 6f), but plain stratified hand-coding needs only 851, because these weak coders are worse than no coder in some strata (e.g. specificity 0.06 in Nigerian Pidgin for the in-language coder). The sensitivity and specificity are oracle values from the full corpus; planning from a pilot is tested in milestone 6b below. Reproduce: `python3 experiments/h5_plugin.py`; table in [`experiments/h5_plugin.txt`](experiments/h5_plugin.txt).

**Planning from a pilot (milestone 6b; all four hypotheses supported).** Audit 20 tweets per language as a pilot, estimate each stratum's residual variance, and apply the same size rule: the mean total audit is 876, 1,049 and 893 tweets against the oracle 888, 1,063 and 912 (pilot items are reused, so they cost nothing extra), mean half-width 3.06 to 3.10 points, coverage 94.9 to 95.9% in all nine cells (1,000 draws each; corrected-oracle rerun, milestone 6f). The catch: only 43 to 47% of audits reach the 3.0-point target, and the audit size varies a lot with a small pilot (sd 114 to 178 tweets at 20 per language, 79 to 118 at 40). Plan for a slightly tighter target or use a pilot of 40 per stratum (see `h6b_pilot.txt` for the share within 3.3 points). Same corpus and stand-in coders, no real LLM coder yet. Reproduce: `python3 experiments/h6b_pilot.py`; table in [`experiments/h6b_pilot.txt`](experiments/h6b_pilot.txt).

**Regression coefficients (milestone 6g).** Regressing the human negative-sentiment label on language dummies and log tweet length (NaijaSenti, audit of 400, 1,000 draws), the same regression run on a stand-in coder's codes covers the corpus's own coefficients in 0 of 15 cells, with biases up to +0.52 and sign flips between coders. Prediction-powered OLS covers 93.6 to 95.7% in all 15, but is 1.08 to 1.41 times wider than hand-coding alone, because these coders agree with the human label only 68 to 74%. **Milestone 6h:** choosing the weight per coefficient (PPI++, general estimating equation, `llmci.regression.ppi_ols_tuned`) removes that penalty: coverage 93.3 to 95.6% in all 15 cells, standard error 0.99 to 1.00 times hand-coding for the two weak coders and 0.935 to 0.988 times for the 74%-agreement one. Same corpus, no real LLM. Reproduce: `python3.13 experiments/h6g_regression.py` and `experiments/h6h_ppipp_regression.py` (need numpy); **Milestone 6i:** cross-fitting the weight (chosen on the other half of the audit, `ppi_ols_crossfit`) gives the same coverage (94.4 to 95.9%) and is 1 to 3% wider than in-sample tuning; the one-point coverage loss of 6h did not reproduce, so cross-fitting is not needed at n = 400 (a null result, table in [`experiments/h6i_crossfit.txt`](experiments/h6i_crossfit.txt)). **Milestone 6j** (4,000 paired draws, [`experiments/h6j_paired.txt`](experiments/h6j_paired.txt)) corrects that: tuning on the audit it is scored on loses up to about 0.9 points of coverage in two of 15 cells (pooled under 0.35 points), and cross-fitting removes part of it for 1 to 3% width. **Milestone 6k (small audits, n = 60, 100, 200; 3,000 paired draws):** human-only OLS with a normal interval already under-covers (mean 91.6, 92.9, 94.0%), tuned PPI++ loses a further 0.7, 0.5 and 0.35 points on average (up to 2.6 in one cell, and 1 to 1.4 points persist for the weakest-signal coder at n = 200), and cross-fitting does not repair it (table in [`experiments/h6k_small_audit.txt`](experiments/h6k_small_audit.txt)); the 6i null at n = 400 was a power failure, and a small-sample interval correction is the next step. Tables in [`experiments/h6g_regression.txt`](experiments/h6g_regression.txt) and [`experiments/h6h_ppipp_regression.txt`](experiments/h6h_ppipp_regression.txt).

**Rare-category audit with a real LLM coder (milestones 6ab to 6ae).** On GoEmotions fear (1.15% prevalence) and sadness (2.3%), a zero-shot Qwen2.5-1.5B coder with twice the informedness of the naive Bayes coder shrinks the oracle audit size by at most 12% (1.14x; H3's 3x bar is not met). Tuned per-stratum PPI undercovers at n = 400 (85.2 to 90.2%); a single pooled weight removes that extra loss, and a Wilson interval on the estimate's effective sample size lifts fear's n = 400 coverage from 88 to 92% while staying 3 to 8% narrower than a human-only Wilson interval (`experiments/h6ad_wilson_ppi.py`, 500 draws, coverage +/- 2 points; one corpus, one small model). Milestone 6ae then pooled the four audit strata: at 1 to 2% prevalence stratifying buys 1 to 3% of the half-width and costs coverage (fear human-only normal 85 to 88% with four strata, 95.8% pooled; the Wilson-on-effective-n interval covers 94.6 to 97.2% pooled), so for a rare category the pooled design is the one to use (`experiments/h6ae_merge_strata.py`). Milestone 6af repeated it on admiration (8%) and neutral (23%): four strata move the half-width by about 1% there too, and the coder's width gain over a human-only Wilson interval follows its Youden J (9% at J 0.65, 1% at J 0.20), so pooling the audit held from 1% to 23% prevalence for groups of similar prevalence (`experiments/h6af_common_category.py`).

**Audit strata by the coder's own label (milestone 6ag).** Corpus-group strata do not pay (6ae, 6af), but cells defined by the coder's label do: with 2 coder cells the Wilson-on-n_eff interval is 15 to 25% narrower in fear, sadness and admiration at n = 1000 and 0.4 to 0.7% narrower in neutral (an uninformative coder), at the price of coverage of 88 to 91% at n = 400 in the rare categories (`experiments/h6ag_coder_strata.py`). One corpus, one 1.5B coder, oracle allocation. **Negative result (milestone 6ah):** estimating that allocation from a 30% pilot looks 25 to 48% narrower at n = 400 but covers only 49 to 84% (fear, sadness, admiration) because the low-prevalence coder cell is starved; size-proportional allocation over the same cells is valid but gains nothing (`experiments/h6ah_pilot_cells.py`).

**Milestone 5d (how good must a coder be).** A synthetic coder that flips the real human label independently (`experiments/h5d_breakeven.py`) beats human-only on width at any accuracy above about 0.6 (ratio 0.97 at a = 0.60, 0.66 at 0.90, n = 400), and tuned PPI++ undercovers at n = 100 once accuracy reaches 0.90 (91.8%). The real Qwen coders at 0.66 agreement gained far less than that, so correlated error, not agreement, is what costs the gain. Preliminary: one corpus, independent flips only.

**Milestone 5e (does the shape of coder error matter at matched accuracy).** At agreement 0.80, a coder whose errors depend on group, length or label still gives a tuned PPI++ interval 10 to 23% narrower than human-only (width ratio 0.77 to 0.89 at n = 100 to 400; `experiments/h5e_correlated.py`), and the four error shapes differ by at most about 0.1. So these kinds of correlated error do not explain the real Qwen coders' null result at 0.66 to 0.70 agreement. Preliminary: illustrative weights, one corpus, one coder draw per cell.

**Milestone 5h (informedness, not agreement, explains the real null).** On this corpus (32.7% negative) a constant code agrees 0.673, so the real coders at agreement 0.66 to 0.69 carry little information: Youden J (sensitivity + specificity - 1) is 0.008 (0.5B), 0.061 (3B) and 0.208 (1.5B) at the zero-shot cut, and at best 0.21 with a cut chosen in-sample, against 0.40 for the synthetic "agreement 0.70" coders of 5d to 5f. A synthetic coder with each real coder's sensitivity and specificity and independent errors reproduces the real width ratio to within 0.006 at n = 400 (e.g. 3B 0.993 real, 0.987 matched; 1.5B 0.978 and 0.980), so error correlation beyond sens/spec costs nothing measurable, and a 20% gain needs J of about 0.5 (`experiments/h5h_informedness.py`, `h5h_informedness.txt`). Preliminary: one corpus and prompt, the best-J cut is in-sample, one coder draw per cell.

**Milestone 5f (the real coders' accuracy, and errors tied to a text feature).** At agreement 0.70 the tuned PPI++ interval is still 3.5 to 10% narrower than human-only for every synthetic error shape tested (width ratio 0.86 to 0.97, n = 100 to 400), and at 0.60 it is 0.92 to 0.99 (`experiments/h5f_text_error.py`). The real 1.5B and 3B coders (0.978 and 0.993 at n = 400) fall inside that range, but milestone 5h (below) shows that agreement was the wrong axis, so this is not the explanation of their null. Preliminary: illustrative weights, one corpus, one coder draw per cell.

**Milestone 5 (first real LLM coder).** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, zero-shot on 6,000 NaijaSenti tweets (`experiments/llm_code.py`), agrees with the human negative label 66.1% of the time, worse than an in-language naive Bayes (73.5%). With valid intervals (HC3 + t) tuned PPI++ is 2.2% narrower than hand-coding alone at an audit of 400 (3.4% for the naive Bayes, 0.4 to 0.6% for the weakest stand-ins) and 5.8% narrower at 60, with coverage 93.2 to 94.9% for n >= 100. A small zero-shot LLM does not move the efficiency gain far; larger models are untested. **Milestone 5b (model size):** Qwen2.5-0.5B and 3B-Instruct under the same zero-shot rule are no better (n = 400 width ratios 0.996 and 0.993 against 0.978 for 1.5B): they call only 2.5 and 3.1% of tweets negative (truth 32.7%), so the gain follows where the Yes/No score is cut, not parameter count; **Milestone 5c (calibrated cut):** choosing the cut on 100 human labels and charging them to the comparator makes the width ratio 1.11 to 1.44 (PPI++ with n audit rows against human-only with n + 100) for all three models, so the cut does not pay at audits of 100 to 400 (0.5B's score is uninformative; 1.5B and 3B reach sens + spec 1.18 to 1.19). Results: [`experiments/h3_*.txt`](experiments/), log: [`LOG.md`](LOG.md).

**Sequential audit at a 1-point target (milestone 6e; all four hypotheses supported after the 6f correction).** The 6c rule at a target nine times tighter stops at a mean of 6,937, 8,069 and 7,098 tweets, 0.6 to 0.8% above the corrected plug-in oracle size (6,881, 8,017, 7,045; the 11 to 13% gap first reported came from a typo in the oracle, milestone 6f, so the bar of within 10% is met), with coverage 93.0 to 96.0% (300 draws) and every audit meeting 1.0 point. The relative spread in audit size is about 1.2%, against 4 to 5% at 3 points. Reproduce: `python3 experiments/h6e_tight_target.py`; table in [`experiments/h6e_tight_target.txt`](experiments/h6e_tight_target.txt).

**Sizing on a second corpus (milestone 6o; three of four predictions held).** On GoEmotions comments grouped by subreddit kind (6,683 comments), the plug-in half-width of the stratified PPI interval matches the simulated one within 0.2% in nine coder x design cells, and the audit-size rule hits its 3- and 2-point targets (mean half-width 3.00 and 2.00, coverage 94.4 to 96.3%). Against the prediction, the stand-in coders (agreement 0.69 to 0.77) need 1.23 to 1.58 times the audit size of the human-only stratified plan, because the lambda = 1 control variate adds variance. See `LOG.md` and `experiments/h6o_reddit_sizing.txt`.

**A lambda-aware sizing rule (milestone 6p; ordering and coverage predictions held, savings smaller than predicted).** Giving each stratum its own control-variate weight (`stratified_ppi_tuned`, sized with `stratum_lambda_s2` and `neyman_total`) removes the 6o penalty: the plan needs 62 to 75% of plain PPI's audit and never more than human-only's, saving 7.7 to 8.6% (pooled stand-in coder) and 1.9 to 2.6% (the two weaker ones) at 3- and 2-point targets. The rule hits its targets (mean half-width 3.00 and 2.00, coverage 94.8 to 96.1%), and estimating lambda from the audit costs no width. I had predicted 15 to 35% and 3 to 15% savings; the coders are less informative than I assumed. See `LOG.md` and `experiments/h6p_lambda_sizing.txt`.
**The sizing rule with real coders and a pilot (milestone 6q; all four predictions held).** On the 6,000-tweet NaijaSenti subsample, strata = language, the zero-shot Qwen2.5 0.5B, 1.5B and 3B coders: the oracle-lambda plan is never larger than human-only and saves 0.1 to 4.7% (the 1.5B coder saves most, not the 3B), while plain PPI (lambda = 1) needs 3 to 47% more audit than human-only. A pilot of 400 tweets that estimates lambda and the variance per language plans an audit within about 1% of the oracle size; mean half-width 3.01 to 3.02 and 2.01 to 2.02 points, coverage 94.6 to 96.8%. One corpus, zero-shot cut, no 7B coder; see `LOG.md`.

**The pilot-estimated sizing rule at a rare category (milestone 6r; predictions partly refuted).** GoEmotions subreddit groups, fear (1.15%) and sadness (2.30%), tuned stratified PPI. The oracle plan hits its target (half-width within 0.04 point; coverage 93.6 to 94.2% for sadness, 90.8 / 93.8% for fear). A 400-item pilot sees too few positives: planned audits are 25 to 30% below the oracle size, intervals 40 to 80% too wide, coverage 73% (fear, 0.5 point) and 85 to 87% (sadness). A 1,500-item pilot fixes sadness (coverage 93%) but not fear (22% of audits over 1.25x target). Two categories, one corpus; see `LOG.md`.

**A Wilson-upper pilot S2 at a rare category (milestone 6s; two of four predictions refuted).** Replacing the plug-in pilot variance by a Wilson upper bound on each group's prevalence removes the 6r failure (no audit wider than 1.25x target; fear coverage 92.0 / 94.4% at 1.0 / 0.5 points with a 400-item pilot, against 70% plug-in) but over-plans: 2.5 to 3.8x the oracle audit at pilot 400 and 1.5 to 2.3x at pilot 1,500, where I predicted 1.0 to 1.6x and 1.0 to 1.25x. Shrinking towards the pooled pilot variance helps only a little (0.86x of oracle for fear, coverage 78 / 92%). One corpus, 500 draws; see `LOG.md` and `experiments/h6s_wilson_pilot.txt`.

**A sequential audit at a rare category (milestone 6t; (1) held, (2) to (4) refuted).** The 6c rule (stop when the plug-in half-width meets the target) covered 93 to 96% at a 45% prevalence. On GoEmotions fear (1.15%) and sadness (2.3%) it covers only 89.8 / 63.8% (fear, 1.0 / 0.5 point) and 86.2 / 72.0% (sadness), although the stopped audit size is near the oracle: most audits stop holding fewer than 20 human positives. Requiring 20 or 40 positives before stopping made fear worse (47% and 41% coverage at 1,700 to 3,450 items). A fixed-size audit with the same estimator covers 91 to 94% at n = 1,000 and above, so the loss is optional stopping, not the estimator. One corpus, 500 draws; see `LOG.md` and `experiments/h6t_rare_sequential.txt`.

**Sequential audit (milestone 6c; all four hypotheses supported).** Start from the same 20-per-language pilot, add batches of 30 (or 90) tweets split by the current Neyman shares, and stop when the estimated half-width reaches 3.0 points. Every audit meets the target (1,000 of 1,000 in each of six cells), at a mean of 898, 1,074 and 919 tweets against the corrected oracle 888, 1,063 and 912 (about 1% over), with coverage 94.2 to 95.2% (no optional-stopping correction used) and a smaller spread in audit size than the pilot plan (sd 34 to 48 against 79 to 119). Batches of 90 cost about 30 more tweets. The price is that the audit must be hand-coded in rounds. Same corpus and stand-in coders, no real LLM coder yet. Reproduce: `python3 experiments/h6c_sequential.py`; table in [`experiments/h6c_sequential.txt`](experiments/h6c_sequential.txt).

**Sequential audit at a 1-point target (milestone 6d; two of four hypotheses refuted as stated).** The 6c rule at a 1.0-point target needs about 7,000 to 8,200 hand-coded tweets (16 to 18% of the corpus), stops under the cap in 100% of 200 draws per cell, and covers 95.0 to 97.0% with a start of 5 or 20 per language; a start of 5 costs no coverage (0.0 to 0.5 points). The audit is 14 to 16% smaller than 9x the 3-point oracle because the variance carries the finite-population factor, which my prediction missed. 200 draws only. Reproduce: `python3 experiments/h6d_hard.py`; table in [`experiments/h6d_hard.txt`](experiments/h6d_hard.txt).

**A label-free floor rule (milestone 4d).** Setting the audit's inclusion-probability floor to clip(5 × q̄, 0.02, 0.10), where q̄ is the corpus mean of the calibrated coder probability (no human labels needed), gives normal-IPW coverage of 92.4 to 96.0% in all nine categories (93% or more in eight; remorse 92.4%, 500 draws, ±1.9 points) and intervals 0.48 to 0.80 of the uniform-Wilson width for the six categories under 1% prevalence. At 1% and above the design is valid but no narrower (0.95 to 1.13). The multiplier was chosen after seeing milestone 4c on this same corpus, so this is an in-sample check, not yet a test ([`experiments/floor_rule.txt`](experiments/floor_rule.txt), [`LOG.md`](LOG.md)).

**The floor rule on 18 unseen categories (milestone 4e).** Applied unchanged to the 18 other GoEmotions emotions (0.99 to 7.69% prevalence), the rule keeps normal-IPW coverage at 93.2 to 97.0% in all 18, but the interval is 1.02 to 1.49 times the uniform-Wilson width in 16 of them (0.89 for desire at 0.99% and 1.00 for surprise). So the rule is a safe default, and its width gain is confined to prevalence below about 1%, which no held-out category reaches; same corpus, same weak non-LLM coder, 500 draws ([`experiments/floor_rule_heldout.txt`](experiments/floor_rule_heldout.txt), [`LOG.md`](LOG.md)).

**Small audits (milestones 6k to 6m).** At audits of 60 to 200 hand-coded tweets (NaijaSenti regression, stand-in coders, 1,500 draws per cell) the normal-interval PPI++ estimators cover 90 to 94%, and the shortfall is mostly a missing leverage correction, not the choice of the tuning weight: dividing each audit row's influence by 1 - h (HC3 type) with a t quantile lifts tuned PPI++ by about 2 points and a two-fold cross-fit estimator to 94.1 to 95.8% at n = 60 to 200. Interval width against human-only was not compared in this step, so it does not yet show PPI++ is worth using below n = 400 ([`LOG.md`](LOG.md), 6m).

**Limitations.** The references in the proposal were not machine-checked (arXiv and Crossref returned 403 in the writing session); milestone 1 does that first.

## Code

`llmci/estimators.py` (stdlib only) holds the estimators the programme compares: human-only, naive,
PPI, PPI++, design-based IPW audit, stratified PPI, stratified human-only and binary label-shift correction.
`llmci/audit_design.py` holds the screened audit-design rule of milestones 6ak to 6am (`informedness`, `screen`, `cell_allocation`; a heuristic with no coverage theorem). Tests: `python3 -m pytest research/llm-coding-inference/test_llmci.py`. Checked against closed
forms and a 600-study simulation; not yet against a published dataset.

`audit_interval(plan, f_all, labels)` closes `plan_audit` with the interval of milestones 6ac and 6ad (pooled control-variate weight, stratified variance, Wilson interval on the effective sample size). Run through the library only (`experiments/h7c_end_to_end.py`, 500 draws), the half-width is within 2% of 6al's guarded screen in all 14 corpus/coder/n cells and coverage is 92.0 to 96.6% (12 of 14 within 2 points of 6al's; two cells differ by 2.6 and 2.8 points, about two sd of the difference).
