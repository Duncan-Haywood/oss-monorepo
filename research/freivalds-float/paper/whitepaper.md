# Freivalds in floating point: what a tolerance does to probabilistic matrix-product verification

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Freivalds' check verifies `C = AB` in `O(n²)` instead of `O(n³)`: draw a probe `r`, accept iff `A(Br) = Cr`. It is the natural primitive for spot-checking layers of a claimed training or inference step in a refereed-verification protocol. Over the reals or a finite field it has a clean 1/2 error bound; in floating point the comparison needs a tolerance `t`, and the guarantee becomes size-dependent. For a Gaussian probe and an additive corruption `E`, the residual is `‖Er‖`, so: (i) a rank-one corruption of Frobenius norm `F` is missed with probability exactly `erf(t/(√2F))` (0.68, 0.38, 0.26, 0.08 at `F/t`=1, 2, 3, 10); (ii) for fixed `F` rank one is the worst case (miss 0.26 vs 0.10, 0.02, 0.001 when the same `F=3t` is split over 2, 4, 8 singular values); (iii) `m` independent probes give `erf(·)^m`, so detecting every corruption above `F*` with error `β` needs `⌈ln β / ln erf(t/(√2F*))⌉` probes (4 for `F*=3t`, `β=1%`; 16 for `β=10⁻⁹`); (iv) Rademacher (±1) probes, the usual choice, miss a two-entry corruption with probability exactly ½ at *every* size, so they never beat Gaussian probes on large corruptions; (v) the honest float32 residual is measured (grows ≈ `n^0.9` for a Gaussian probe of norm `√n`), which fixes `t`, and a cheater can hide corruptions of 3–6× the noise at `t` = 2–4× the mean residual; (vi) if the probe is a hash of the claimed result and retries are free, a cheater needs only `1/erf(t/(√2F))` tries (125 at `F=100t`), so the probe must be committed after the result and be unpredictable to the prover. Elementary and stylised; the contribution is the exact finite-tolerance formulas and their numerical check.

## 1. Setup
Prover claims `C' = AB + E`. Verifier draws `r` (entries `N(0,1)`), computes `w = A(Br)` and `z = C'r` in `3n²` multiply-adds, and accepts iff `‖w − z‖ ≤ t`. In exact arithmetic `w − z = −Er`. `Er ~ N(0, EEᵀ)`, so `‖Er‖² = Σᵢ sᵢ² Zᵢ²` for the singular values `sᵢ` of `E`, `Zᵢ` iid `N(0,1)`. Floating-point noise is added on top; `t` must exceed it or honest provers are rejected.

## 2. Exact miss probability
If `E = uvᵀ` then `Er = u(v·r)` and `‖Er‖ = ‖u‖‖v‖|Z| = F|Z|`. The miss probability is `P(F|Z| ≤ t) = erf(t/(√2F))`, tested against 40,000 simulated probes (±0.01). A single probe therefore misses a corruption of size 1.483 t half the time, and the miss probability decays only like `t/F` — not exponentially in `F`. Repetition is what buys confidence: `m` independent probes give `erf(·)^m` (E1). Probes needed (E2): for `β=10⁻⁶`, 20 probes at `F*=1.5t`, 11 at `3t`, 6 at `10t`.

## 3. Rank one is the worst case
Given `F²=Σsᵢ²`, `‖Er‖²` concentrates near `F²` when the mass is spread over many singular values, so the chance of falling under `t² < F²` shrinks. Simulation (E3, `F=3t`, 200k trials): miss 0.262 for rank one (exact 0.261), 0.104 for two equal singular values, 0.022 for four, 0.001 for eight. So the rank-one formula is a *conservative* guarantee for dense corruptions (a tampered layer with noise-like changes everywhere is easier to catch). I checked this by simulation for equal splits only, not proved for arbitrary spectra.

## 4. Rademacher probes fail on large corruptions
Take `E = uvᵀ` with `v = (a, a, 0, …)`. With ±1 probes `v·r = a(r₁+r₂)` is 0 with probability exactly ½ and `±2a` otherwise; the residual is 0 half the time however large `a` is, and `√2F` otherwise. So the miss probability is ½ once `√2F > t` (E4: 0.50 at `F=3, 10, 100t`, against Gaussian 0.26, 0.08, 0.008). Finite-field Freivalds accepts this ½ because it is a per-probe constant and repetition fixes it; here the Gaussian probe is strictly better at any `F` beyond ≈1.5 t. Dense ±1 probes are cheaper to generate; this argues for paying for Gaussian ones.

## 5. Noise floor and the hidden budget
I emulate float32 (each product and each partial sum rounded) for honest `n×n` products with `N(0,1/n)` entries and Gaussian `r` (E5, 30 trials, 12 at `n=64`). The mean residual is 3.5e-7, 6.2e-7, 1.2e-6, 2.4e-6 at `n=8, 16, 32, 64`: a fitted exponent 0.92 in `n`, about 2–5 × `ε₃₂√n`. Since `‖r‖=√n`, that is ≈ `n^0.4` per unit-norm probe. Setting `t = k ×` mean residual at `n=32`: false-positive rate 3.3% at `k=1.5`, 0 of 30 at `k≥2`; the corruption missed half the time is 2.2×, 3.0×, 4.4×, 5.9× the mean noise for `k = 1.5, 2, 3, 4`. This is the same "cumulative hidden-deviation budget" that appears for tolerance thresholds in `reproducible-refereed-training` and `property-elicitation-verification`: whatever the tolerance admits, a rational prover can hide, here at a rate that decays like `t/F` per probe. Thirty trials give a rough tail only; the zero false-positive rates are not evidence of a 0 rate.

## 6. Grinding and cost
If the probe is a deterministic function of the claimed result (Fiat–Shamir) and the prover may retry with fresh corruptions, each try passes with probability `erf(t/(√2F))`, so the expected number of tries is its inverse: 2.6, 12.6, 125, 1253 at `F = 2, 10, 100, 1000 t` (E6), ≈ `1.25 F/t` for large `F`. This is the Freivalds analogue of `fiat-shamir-grinding`: corruption size is limited only by the prover's hashing budget. The remedy is a probe from a beacon or a verifier-chosen nonce committed after `C'`. Cost: 8 probes cost 19%, 2.3%, 0.29% of recomputing the product at `n=128, 1024, 8192`.

## 7. Limits
One product, additive corruption, Gaussian noise model measured only for sequential float32 accumulation (blocked/tensor-core reductions differ and are what `reproducible-refereed-training` addresses); the honest residual is treated as a scalar threshold, not a distribution; no low-precision formats (bf16/fp8), no adaptive prover that knows the probe distribution's tail; miss-probability monotonicity in spread is numerical for equal splits. Related: Freivalds (1977); fingerprinting for verifiable ML (e.g. Gensyn's Verde / RepOps for canonical reduction order). In this repo: `reproducible-refereed-training`, `spot-check-slashing`, `fiat-shamir-grinding`, `verification-game`.
