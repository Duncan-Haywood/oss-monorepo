## 1. Classic secular theories of religion's origin, and Evans-Pritchard's critique

### Takeaway
The nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century theories (Tylor's animism, Frazer's magic-religion-science sequence, Durkheim's sacred/society, Freud's Oedipal primal crime, Marx's "opium") were all theories of *origin* built on evolutionary stage assumptions. Evans-Pritchard, a Catholic convert, judged them in 1965 to be unverifiable "just-so stories" resting on an "if I were a horse" fallacy. That critique is still the standard demolition of the genre, and it cuts against theological origin theories (Schmidt) as much as secular ones.

### Cited Findings
- **Tylor (1871).** Verbatim: "It seems best to fall back at once on this essential source, and simply to claim, as a minimum definition of Religion, the belief in Spiritual Beings." His chapter summary reads "Doctrine of Spiritual Beings, here termed Animism—Animism treated as belonging to Natural Religion". Tylor also hedged: "It cannot be positively asserted that every existing tribe recognizes the belief in spiritual beings, for the native condition of a considerable number is obscure in this respect" — E. B. Tylor, *Primitive Culture*, vol. 1 (1871), ch. XI — [Project Gutenberg #70458](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70458/pg70458.txt)
- **Tylor conceded degeneration in some cases.** Andrew Lang quotes Tylor (vol. ii p. 336): "the degeneration theory, no doubt in some instances with justice, may claim such beliefs as mutilated and perverted remains of higher religion" — Lang, *The Making of Religion* (1898) — [Gutenberg #12353](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/12353/pg12353.txt)
- **Frazer, three stages.** Verbatim from the closing chapter of *The Golden Bough*: "we shall perhaps be disposed to conclude that the movement of the higher thought, so far as we can trace it, has on the whole been from magic through religion to science. In magic man depends on his own strength to meet the difficulties and dangers that beset him on every side." And: "If an Age of Religion has thus everywhere, as I venture to surmise, been preceded by an Age of Magic..." Note his own hedges ("perhaps", "as I venture to surmise") — J. G. Frazer, *The Golden Bough* (abridged ed., 1922) — [Gutenberg #3623](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3623/pg3623.txt)
- **Durkheim (1912).** Verbatim definition: "_A religion is a unified system of beliefs and practices relative to sacred things, that is to say, things set apart and forbidden--beliefs and practices which unite into one single moral community called a Church, all those who adhere to them._" He adds that this shows "the idea of religion is inseparable from that of the Church" — Durkheim, *The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life* (1912; Swain trans. 1915) — [Gutenberg #41360](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41360/pg41360.txt)
- **Freud (1913).** Verbatim: "I want to state the conclusion that the beginnings of religion, ethics, society, and art meet in the Oedipus complex. This is in entire accord with the findings of psychoanalysis, namely, that the nucleus of all neuroses as far as our present knowledge of them goes is the Oedipus complex." In the same text Freud noted that "primitive races are not young races but really are as old as the" civilised — *Totem and Taboo* (Brill trans.) — [Gutenberg #41214](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41214/pg41214.txt)
- **Marx (1843–44).** Verbatim: "Religious suffering is, at one and the same time, the expression of real suffering and a protest against real suffering. Religion is the sigh of the oppressed creature, the heart of a heartless world, and the soul of soulless conditions. It is the opium of the people. The abolition of religion as the illusory happiness of the people is the demand for their real happiness." — Marx, *A Contribution to the Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right*, Introduction — [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1843/critique-hpr/intro.htm). (The "protest" clause is often dropped when the line is quoted; it matters for a fair reading.)
- **Evans-Pritchard's verdict (1965).** All verbatim from the Monoskop scan (OCR is noisy; I quote only clean stretches). On the genre: "I think you will not regard my strictures as too severe when you see how inadequate, even ludicrous, is much of what has been written in explanation of religious phenomena." On Spencer and, by extension, Tylor: "The argument is a priori speculation, sprinkled with some illustrations, and is specious. It is a fine example of the [introspectionist] ... 'if I were a horse', fallacy." On Tylor's animism: "The theory has the quality of a just-so story like 'how the leopard got his spots'. The ideas of soul and spirit could have arisen in the way Tylor supposed, but there is no evidence that they did." On Durkheim: "Totemism could have arisen through gregariousness, but there is no evidence that it did" (OCR-garbled, reconstructed wording; check before quoting). On emotionalist theories: "guesswork of the, once again, 'if I were a horse' type" — E. E. Evans-Pritchard, *Theories of Primitive Religion* (Oxford: Clarendon, 1965), roughly pp. 5, 24–25, 43, 108 — [Monoskop PDF](https://www.monoskop.org/images/e/e1/Evans_Pritchard_E_E_Theories_of_Primitive_Religion_1965.pdf)
- **Evans-Pritchard on believer and non-believer (conclusion, ~pp. 120–121).** He writes that both believer and atheist can, as anthropologists, only take into account what they observe, but "if either attempts to go further than this, each must pursue a different path. The non-believer seeks for some theory—biological, psychological, or sociological—which will explain the illusion" (OCR reconstructed). He then sides explicitly with Wilhelm Schmidt against Renan: "On this point I find myself in agreement with Schmidt in his confutation of Renan", and quotes Schmidt that a non-believer "will talk of religion as a blind man might of colours, or one totally devoid of ear, of a beautiful musical composition." He ends: "We seem always to have come out by the same door as we went in." — [Monoskop PDF](https://www.monoskop.org/images/e/e1/Evans_Pritchard_E_E_Theories_of_Primitive_Religion_1965.pdf)
- **E-P was no friend of Schmidt's evolutionism.** He calls Schmidt "As intellectualist and evolutionist as others of the time" and borrows Schmidt's own classification ("intellectualist and emotionalist theories") to organise the book — same source.
- The book grew from the Sir D. Owen Evans Lectures at University College of Wales, Aberystwyth, spring 1962; a 2020 article calls it "the ultimate critique of evolutionist theories of religion" — [search summary of the book's preface and of e-rhizome 2020 2(1):1–18, DOI 10.5507/rh.2020.001](https://e-rhizome.upol.cz/artkey/erh-202001-0001_primitive-theories-of-religion-evolutionism-after-evans-pritchard.php)
- **Larsen, *The Slain God* (OUP 2014).** It has a chapter each on Tylor, Frazer, Evans-Pritchard, Mary Douglas, and Victor and Edith Turner. T. M. Luhrmann's review: Tylor and Frazer "do conform to pattern one" (hostile), "But the next four do not. They are all Catholics. Three of them converted after their fieldwork. In the case of the Turners, it is clear that they converted because of their fieldwork." — [Luhrmann, *New Directions* review](https://www.new-directions.sps.ed.ac.uk/the-slain-god-book-review/). Search summaries of other reviews say Evans-Pritchard converted in 1944 and that Douglas's symbolism work drew on Catholic theological categories — [First Things review](https://firstthings.com/the-slain-god); [Marginalia review](https://themarginaliareview.com/?p=8585) (summary-level only, not read in full).

### Inferences
- Every classic theory treats contemporary "primitives" as living fossils of the first humans. Freud's own aside ("primitive races are not young races") undercuts that premise, and Evans-Pritchard makes it central. For the series, this is the hinge: the equation of *simple* with *earliest* fails in both directions, against Tylor's animism-first and Schmidt's monotheism-first alike.
- Evans-Pritchard's point about believer and non-believer is methodological, not apologetic. It says the explanatory theories presuppose the illusion they set out to explain. That is the same structure as the later "debunking" debate (section 7).
- Durkheim and Marx are better read as theories of religion's *function* than of its *origin*. Durkheim's definition makes community ("Church") constitutive, which a Catholic ecclesiology would partly welcome even while rejecting the reduction.

## 2. Primordial monotheism: Lang, Schmidt and the Vienna school, and what survives

### Takeaway
Lang (1898) made a real empirical point that animism-first theory could not easily absorb: some "low races" held a moral, creator "High God" not obviously derived from ghosts. Schmidt turned this into the twelve-volume *Urmonotheismus* thesis inside a diffusionist culture-circle (Kulturkreis) framework. Anthropology rejected the framework and the degeneration narrative, and critics included some Catholic theologians. Modern phylogenetic work (Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016) reconstructs animism, not high gods, as the oldest trait. It finds that "high gods" are a "stand-alone" trait that can appear regardless of other features, and that *active, moralising* high gods were absent in early humans. Honest summary: Lang's datum (high gods exist in some foraging societies) survives; Schmidt's chronology (monotheism first, then degeneration) does not.

### Cited Findings
- **Lang's programme (1898).** Verbatim: "we shall collect and compare the accounts which we possess of the High Gods and creative beings worshipped or believed in, by the most backward races. We shall then ask whether these relatively Supreme Beings, so conceived of by men in very rudimentary social conditions, can be, as anthropology declares, mere developments from the belief in ghosts of the dead." His chapter titles include "X. HIGH GODS OF LOW RACES", "XI. SUPREME GODS NOT NECESSARILY DEVELOPED OUT OF 'SPIRITS'" and "XV. THE OLD DEGENERATION THEORY". He also proposed that "the savage theory of the soul may be based, at least in part, on experiences which cannot, at present, be made to fit into any purely materialistic system" — reflecting his psychical-research interests (appendices on the poltergeist and crystal-gazing). He wrote: "I do not pretend to know how the lowest savages evolved the theory of a God who reads the heart and 'makes for righteousness'" — Lang, *The Making of Religion* (1898) — [Gutenberg #12353](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/12353/pg12353.txt)
- **Schmidt's thesis.** Wilhelm Schmidt SVD (1868–1954) defended *Urmonotheismus* in *Der Ursprung der Gottesidee* (1912–1955). He held monotheism to be the "natural" form of theism, later "degraded" by polytheism as ancestors and personified natural forces became objects of worship — [Wikipedia, "Urmonotheismus"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmonotheismus) (secondary; cites Pettazzoni and others)
- **Evans-Pritchard on Schmidt and Lang.** Lang's ideas "made little impression in the climate of animism then prevailing, and had fallen into oblivion till resuscitated by Wilhelm Schmidt" — [Evans-Pritchard 1965, Monoskop scan, ~p. 31](https://www.monoskop.org/images/e/e1/Evans_Pritchard_E_E_Theories_of_Primitive_Religion_1965.pdf)
- **Reception and critics** (Encyclopedia of Religion entry via encyclopedia.com):
  - Diffusionism, the basis of Schmidt's method, "is not particularly popular" (citing Alan Barnard 2000).
  - Some Catholic theologians did not support his "degenerationist views or his ideas regarding monotheism and primordial revelation".
  - **Raffaele Pettazzoni** (1957) held "that true monotheism stems from an antipolytheistic revolution instigated by a religious reformer".
  - **Guy Swanson** (1960) argued "that monotheism was not the actual religion of the simplest and most archaic societies"; monotheism required at least three dominant ("sovereign") groups in a society.
  - Schmidt's articles were written in part to support "the struggle the Catholic Church was then waging against Modernism".
  - Source: ["Schmidt, Wilhelm", Encyclopedia of Religion via encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/schmidt-wilhelm)
- **Pettazzoni** also denied that belief in a Supreme Being implies monotheism — [Wikipedia, "Urmonotheismus"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmonotheismus)
- **Sympathetic revival.** Winfried Corduan's book (*In the Beginning God*, 2013, per the review) is "an effort to rehabilitate the reputation and theory of Schmidt" — [First Things, "Original Monotheism"](https://firstthings.com/original-monotheism/) (seen as a search summary; an apologetic-leaning venue)
- **Peoples, Duda & Marlowe (2016)**, "Hunter-Gatherers and the Origins of Religion", *Human Nature* 27:261–282, DOI 10.1007/s12110-016-9260-0, open access as PMC4958132. They reconstruct ancestral states for seven traits (animism, afterlife, shamanism, ancestor worship, high gods, active ancestor worship, active high gods) across 33 hunter-gatherer societies (28 from the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample plus 5) on a time-calibrated supertree. Abstract, verbatim:
  - "the oldest trait of religion, present in the most recent common ancestor of present-day hunter-gatherers, was animism"
  - "Belief in an afterlife emerged, followed by shamanism and ancestor worship. Ancestor spirits or high gods who are active in human affairs were absent in early humans"
  - "the trait 'high gods' stands apart, suggesting that belief in a single creator deity can emerge in a society regardless of other aspects of its religion"
  - Body text: "High gods were not the first supernatural entities to monitor morality" and "Belief in high gods appears to be a rather 'stand-alone' phenomenon"; hunter-gatherers are "least likely to adopt morally punishing active high gods, if any high gods at all"
  - Source: [Europe PMC full text, PMC4958132](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4958132/fullTextXML)
- **Tylor's own fieldwork caveat** (above) and Swanson's sociological finding together pre-empt both monotheism-first and animism-first as universal claims — [Tylor, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70458/pg70458.txt); [encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/schmidt-wilhelm)

### Inferences
- Where the lens fits: the *existence* of remote, creator or sky "high gods" among some foragers is a real datum. The "stand-alone" finding in Peoples et al. is mildly congenial to Lang, since high gods do not look like a late outgrowth of ancestor cults.
- Where it strains:
  - Reconstruction puts animism at the root, and *active, moralising* high gods late, tracking social complexity (Swanson; Peoples & Marlowe 2012).
  - Schmidt's degeneration chronology and his use of present-day foragers as "oldest" cultures commit the same simple = earliest error that Evans-Pritchard charged against Tylor.
  - Phylogenetic reconstruction reaches back only to the common ancestor of *present-day* hunter-gatherers sampled. It is not direct evidence about the Palaeolithic, and 33 societies is a small sample. Both caveats limit how strongly the result can be read against or for any origin thesis.
- A theologically careful position need not depend on Schmidt at all. Augustine, *Nostra Aetate* §2 and CCC §28 (sections 3–4 below) assert a universal "perception of that hidden power", not a datable primeval monotheism.

### Gaps
- I did not verify from the full text whether Peoples et al. reconstruct *non-active* "high gods" as absent at the root node. The abstract states only that *active* high gods were absent and that "high gods" stands apart. Check Fig. 3e before claiming "high gods are not ancestral".
- I did not access any of Schmidt's twelve volumes or *The Origin and Growth of Religion* (1931 English). Pettazzoni's critique was summarised, not read.
- I found no reliable source for later Vienna-school figures (Koppers, Gusinde) on the reception question.

## 3. Patristic frameworks for non-Christian religion and philosophy

### Takeaway
The Fathers did not have an "origin of religion" theory in the modern sense. They had theological frameworks for why truth appears outside Israel and the Church:
- Justin: the seminal Logos sown in every race.
- Clement: philosophy as the Greeks' "schoolmaster" to Christ.
- Eusebius: pagan thought as *praeparatio evangelica*.
- Augustine: the "true religion" existed from the beginning of the human race and only later received the name "Christian".

These frameworks were also selective. Justin pairs the Logos theme with demonic imitation of Christian rites, a line often dropped in modern irenic readings.

### Cited Findings
- **Justin, *1 Apology* 46** (verbatim, ANF trans.): "We have been taught that Christ is the first-born of God, and we have declared above that He is the Word of whom every race of men were partakers; and those who lived reasonably are Christians, even though they have been thought atheists; as, among the Greeks, Socrates and Heraclitus, and men like them; and among the barbarians, Abraham, and Ananias, and Azarias, and Misael, and Elias, and many others" — [newadvent.org, First Apology](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0126.htm)
- **Justin, *2 Apology* 10** (verbatim): "For whatever either lawgivers or philosophers uttered well, they elaborated by finding and contemplating some part of the Word. But since they did not know the whole of the Word, which is Christ..." — [newadvent.org, Second Apology](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0127.htm)
- ***2 Apology* 8** (verbatim): the Stoics and poets were admirable "on account of the seed of reason [the Logos] implanted in every race of men" — [newadvent.org](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0127.htm)
- ***2 Apology* 13** (verbatim): "For each man spoke well in proportion to the share he had of the spermatic word, seeing what was related to it... Whatever things were rightly said among all men, are the property of us Christians... For all the writers were able to see realities darkly through the sowing of the implanted word that was in them. For the seed and imitation impacted according to capacity is one thing, and quite another is the thing itself" — [newadvent.org](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0127.htm)
- **Demons as the other half of Justin's account** (2 Apol. 8, verbatim): "the devils have always effected, that all those who anyhow live a reasonable and earnest life, and shun vice, be hated" — [newadvent.org](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0127.htm)
- **Clement, *Stromata* 1.5** (verbatim): "philosophy was given to the Greeks directly and primarily, till the Lord should call the Greeks. For this was a schoolmaster to bring the Hellenic mind, as the law, the Hebrews, to Christ. Galatians 3:24 Philosophy, therefore, was a preparation, paving the way for him who is perfected in Christ... The way of truth is therefore one." — [newadvent.org, Stromata Book I](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02101.htm)
- **Eusebius, *Praeparatio Evangelica*** (Gifford trans. 1903). Book I's contents include "II. The charges usually brought against us by those who try to slander our doctrines" and "III. That we did not adopt the sentiments of the word of salvation without inquiry". The work is apologetic: a review of Greek, Phoenician and Egyptian theology to justify Christians' preference for Hebrew over Greek wisdom — [tertullian.org, PE Book 1](https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/eusebius_pe_01_book1.htm). The phrase "a preparation for the Gospel" then passes, via *Lumen Gentium* 16, into CCC §843 (below).
- **Augustine, *Retractationes* 1.13.3**, on his early *De vera religione*. Verbatim Latin (PL 32): "Nam res ipsa quae nunc christiana religio nuncupatur, erat apud antiquos, nec defuit ab initio generis humani, quousque ipse Christus veniret in carne, unde vera religio quae jam erat, coepit appellari christiana." He continues that he called it Christian "non quia prioribus temporibus non fuit, sed quia posterioribus hoc nomen accepit." — [Bibliothek der Kirchenväter, Retractationes I.13.3](https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-250/versions/retractationes/divisions/58)
  - My own translation (not a published one; label it as such): "For the very thing which is now called the Christian religion existed among the ancients, and was not absent from the beginning of the human race until Christ himself came in the flesh, from which time the true religion, which already existed, began to be called Christian... not because it did not exist in earlier times, but because it received this name in later ones."

### Inferences
- The patristic frameworks are *selective-inclusive*:
  - Truth outside the Church is Christ's (Justin: it is "the property of us Christians"); error and cult are often ascribed to demons.
  - The frameworks value philosophy (Socrates, Heraclitus, the Stoics) far more than cult.
  - This maps onto *Nostra Aetate*'s "ray of that Truth" language. It sits uneasily with the modern comparative study of religion as such, which treats ritual as central.
- Augustine's text is the strongest patristic precedent for something like a "primordial true religion" thesis. But it is a claim about continuity of the true worship of God (the patriarchs, the just), not an ethnographic claim about early humans. Schmidt is sometimes read as giving it empirical content, which the anthropology did not bear out (section 2).

- I did not extract Augustine's *City of God* passages (e.g. Book VIII on the Platonists, Book XVIII on the Sibyl) this session. They are on newadvent.org if needed.
- Eusebius' own programmatic statement of what "preparation" means (PE 1.1) was not extracted verbatim.

## 4. Catholic magisterium on natural knowledge of God, other religions, and evolution

### Takeaway
The magisterial line has four pieces:
1. **A universal capacity.** God can be known by natural reason (Vatican I), and humans are "religious beings" (CCC 28).
2. **A positive but subordinated reading of other religions.** They "often reflect a ray of that Truth" (NA 2) and their goodness is a "preparation for the Gospel" (LG 16; CCC 843), yet their "belief" is distinguished from "theological faith" and their followers are "objectively" in a "gravely deficient situation" (Dominus Iesus 7, 22).
3. **Openness on bodily evolution with the soul excepted.** Humani Generis allowed research on the body's evolution but excluded polygenism (§§36–37). John Paul II (1996) recognised evolution as "more than an hypothesis" but posited an "ontological leap" at man.
4. **Benedict XVI's "broadening of reason"**, centred on the Logos.

### Cited Findings
- **Vatican I, *Dei Filius*** (1870), canon 2.1 on revelation, verbatim: "If anyone says that the one, true God, our creator and lord, cannot be known with certainty from the things that have been made, by the natural light of human reason: let him be anathema." — [papalencyclicals.net, First Vatican Council](https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum20.htm)
- **CCC §28**, verbatim: "In many ways, throughout history down to the present day, men have given expression to their quest for God in their religious beliefs and behaviour: in their prayers, sacrifices, rituals, meditations, and so forth. These forms of religious expression, despite the ambiguities they often bring with them, are so universal that one may well call man a religious being" (followed by Acts 17:26–28) — [vatican.va CCC Part 1](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9.HTM)
- **CCC §843**, verbatim: "The Catholic Church recognizes in other religions that search, among shadows and images, for the God who is unknown yet near since he gives life and breath and all things and wants all men to be saved. Thus, the Church considers all goodness and truth found in these religions as 'a preparation for the Gospel and given by him who enlightens all men that they may at length have life.'" **§844** follows: "In their religious behavior, however, men also display the limits and errors that disfigure the image of God in them" — [vatican.va CCC](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P29.HTM)
- ***Nostra Aetate* §2** (28 Oct 1965), verbatim:
  - "From ancient times down to the present, there is found among various peoples a certain perception of that hidden power which hovers over the course of things and over the events of human history; at times some indeed have come to the recognition of a Supreme Being, or even of a Father."
  - "The Catholic Church rejects nothing that is true and holy in these religions. She regards with sincere reverence those ways of conduct and of life, those precepts and teachings which, though differing in many aspects from the ones she holds and sets forth, nonetheless often reflect a ray of that Truth which enlightens all men. Indeed, she proclaims, and ever must proclaim Christ 'the way, the truth, and the life' (John 14:6)"
  - On Buddhism: it "realizes the radical insufficiency of this changeable world"
  - §1 grounds this in the "community of all peoples, one their origin"
  - Source: [vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651028_nostra-aetate_en.html)
  - Note: the phrase "recognition of a Supreme Being, or even of a Father" echoes the high-god discussion. The council does not claim chronological priority for it.
- ***Lumen Gentium* §16** (1964), verbatim: "Those also can attain to salvation who through no fault of their own do not know the Gospel of Christ or His Church, yet sincerely seek God and moved by grace strive by their deeds to do His will as it is known to them through the dictates of conscience... Whatever good or truth is found amongst them is looked upon by the Church as a preparation for the Gospel. She knows that it is given by Him who enlightens all men... But often men, deceived by the Evil One, have become vain in their reasonings" — [vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html)
- ***Dominus Iesus*** (CDF, 6 Aug 2000), verbatim:
  - §7: "the distinction between theological faith and belief in the other religions, must be firmly held... belief, in the other religions, is that sum of experience and thought that constitutes the human treasury of wisdom and religious aspiration, which man in his search for truth has conceived and acted upon in his relationship to God and the Absolute."
  - §22: "If it is true that the followers of other religions can receive divine grace, it is also certain that objectively speaking they are in a gravely deficient situation in comparison with those who, in the Church, have the fullness of the means of salvation."
  - Source: [vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000806_dominus-iesus_en.html)
- ***Humani Generis*** (Pius XII, 12 Aug 1950), verbatim:
  - §36: "the Teaching Authority of the Church does not forbid that, in conformity with the present state of human sciences and sacred theology, research and discussions... take place with regard to the doctrine of evolution, in as far as it inquires into the origin of the human body as coming from pre-existent and living matter - for the Catholic faith obliges us to hold that souls are immediately created by God."
  - §37: "When, however, there is question of another conjectural opinion, namely polygenism, the children of the Church by no means enjoy such liberty... Now it is in no way apparent how such an opinion can be reconciled with that which the sources of revealed truth and the documents of the Teaching Authority of the Church propose with regard to original sin, which proceeds from a sin actually committed by an individual Adam"
  - Source: [vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_12081950_humani-generis.html)
  - Note: "it is in no way apparent how" is weaker than an outright doctrinal denial. Theologians have used that wording to keep the question open. That is an interpretive point to source separately.
- **John Paul II, Message to the Pontifical Academy of Sciences** (22 Oct 1996), verbatim:
  - "some new findings lead us toward the recognition of evolution as more than an hypothesis."
  - "the theories of evolution which, because of the philosophies which inspire them, regard the spirit either as emerging from the forces of living matter, or as a simple epiphenomenon of that matter, are incompatible with the truth about man"
  - "With man, we find ourselves facing a different ontological order—an ontological leap, we could say."
  - Translation caveat (from the same page): the first English edition rendered the key sentence as "the recognition of more than one hypothesis within the theory of evolution", and it was later amended to match the other language editions.
  - Source: [EWTN library text with editor's note](https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/message-to-pontifical-academy-of-sciences-on-evolution-8825)
- **Benedict XVI, Regensburg lecture** (12 Sept 2006), verbatim:
  - "not to act in accordance with reason is contrary to God's nature."
  - "John began the prologue of his Gospel with the words: 'In the beginning was the λόγος'. This is the very word used by the emperor: God acts, σὺν λόγω, with logos. Logos means both reason and word"
  - He frames modernity's "programme of dehellenization" in "three stages".
  - Aim: "broadening our concept of reason and its application... if reason and faith come together in a new way"
  - Source: [vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/speeches/2006/september/documents/hf_ben-xvi_spe_20060912_university-regensburg.html)

### Inferences
- The magisterium's account of religion's *origin* is anthropological-theological, not historical. Religion is the universal expression of a created capacity (CCC 28; Acts 17), shadowed by error and "the Evil One" (LG 16; CCC 844). This is compatible with any empirical sequence (animism first, high gods late) because it makes no chronological claim. Its strain point is monogenism/original sin (HG 37) against population genetics, not the history of religions.
- *Dominus Iesus* is the clearest statement of the "fulfilment with hierarchy" model, and it is what critics of Catholic inclusivism usually target.

### Gaps
- Ratzinger's *Truth and Tolerance* (2003/2004 English; Ignatius Press) was not accessed. I have no verified quotations from it.
- Benedict XVI's *Spe Salvi* and *Caritas in Veritate*, and the 2004 International Theological Commission document *Communion and Stewardship* (on evolution and polygenism), were not checked. ITC 2004 §§63–70 is the likely best source for the current Catholic position on evolution; verify there.

## 5. Catholic and allied thinkers on religion's history: Chesterton, Dawson, Girard, Eliade, Otto, de Lubac, Rahner, Pieper

### Takeaway
- **Chesterton and Dawson** offer narrative-historical apologetics: religion is the root of culture, and Christianity is unique within the history of myth and philosophy.
- **Otto and Eliade** offer phenomenologies of the holy and sacred that resist reduction.
- **Girard** offers the most ambitious explanatory theory by a Catholic: religion originates in the scapegoat murder and the Gospels unmask it. It is also the most criticised, as unfalsifiable and over-ambitious.
- **Rahner's "anonymous Christian"** is the key theological inclusivist category, and it is contested.

### Cited Findings
- **Chesterton, *The Everlasting Man* (1925).** Part I chapters include "THE MAN IN THE CAVE", "PROFESSORS AND PREHISTORIC MEN", "GOD AND COMPARATIVE RELIGION", "MAN AND MYTHOLOGIES" and "THE DEMONS AND THE PHILOSOPHERS". On bias, verbatim: "it is stark hypocrisy to pretend that nine-tenths of the higher critics and scientific evolutionists and professors of comparative religion are in the least impartial... I do profess to be a great deal more impartial than they are; in the sense that I can tell the story fairly, with some sort of imaginative justice to all sides; and they cannot." — [Gutenberg #65688](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65688/pg65688.txt)
- **Christopher Dawson.** *The Age of the Gods* (1928), *Progress and Religion: An Historical Inquiry* (1929), and *Religion and Culture* (1948) and *Religion and the Rise of Western Culture* (1950) as his Gifford Lectures 1947–49 — [Wikipedia, "Christopher Dawson"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Dawson) (bibliographic; texts not read)
- **René Girard** (1923–2015), per the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Mimetic desire leads to rivalry and violence, "partially solved by a scapegoat mechanism, but ultimately, Christianity is the best antidote to violence." Main criticisms listed by the IEP:
  - Apologetic bias: critics "argue that he has a tendency to twist interpretations of classical texts and myths in order to favor Christian doctrine."
  - Over-reach: "Mimetic Theory Claims Too Much". His mimetic desire is "derived, not from a careful study of subjects and the implementation of tests, but rather, from the reading of works of fiction", and claiming all culture derives "from an original murder, is perhaps too much."
  - "The Origins of Culture are Not Verifiable": "the number of rituals and myths that do not display violence is even greater". Because Girard treats non-violent myths as erased traces, "Girard's work is not falsifiable in Popper's sense"; this is "the same Popperian objection leveled against Freud".
  - Source: [IEP, "René Girard"](https://iep.utm.edu/girard/)
  - Works: *Things Hidden since the Foundation of the World* (1978) and *I See Satan Fall Like Lightning* (1999), bibliographic, not read.
- **Rudolf Otto (Lutheran), *Das Heilige* (1917)**, the "mysterium tremendum et fascinans" — [Wikipedia, "The Idea of the Holy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Idea_of_the_Holy) (1917 publication; links to the Internet Archive text; text not read)
- **Mircea Eliade.** His understanding of religion "centers on his concept of hierophany (manifestation of the Sacred)—a concept that includes, but is not limited to, the older and more restrictive concept of theophany." A hierophany is a "revelation of an absolute reality, opposed to the non-reality of th[e vast surrounding expanse]"; profane space gives "no pattern for his behavior." In 1927 Eliade approved "an ethnic nationalist state centered on the Orthodox Church". The article has a full section, "Controversy: antisemitism and links with the Iron Guard" — [Wikipedia, "Mircea Eliade"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Eliade). This is a necessary critical note for any blog post that leans on Eliade.
- **Karl Rahner's "anonymous Christian".** The Stanford Encyclopedia: "Rahner used the term 'anonymous Christian' for non-Christians who unknowingly appropriate the salvific work of Christ." For Rahner, those who die without knowledge of Christ are judged by "the extent to which they sincerely seek truth and respond to their inner moral conscience... through, or even apart from, a religion other than Christianity." — [SEP, "Religious Pluralism"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/religious-pluralism/). The Wikipedia article has a "Criticism" section — [Wikipedia, "Anonymous Christian"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_Christian)

### Inferences
- These thinkers divide into two kinds:
  - **Phenomenologists** (Otto, Eliade, partly Dawson) protect the irreducibility of religious experience. They are hard to falsify but also make few historical predictions.
  - **Explanatory theorists** (Girard) make strong origin claims and so inherit Evans-Pritchard's "just-so" objection in full.
  - For the series, Girard is the clearest case of a Catholic theory that strains against the evidence for the same reasons Freud's does. The IEP draws exactly that parallel.
- Chesterton's argument is a rhetorical challenge to the impartiality of the comparative-religion establishment, not an empirical theory. His point anticipates Evans-Pritchard's believer/non-believer remark by forty years.

### Gaps
- **Henri de Lubac** (*Catholicism*, 1938; *The Drama of Atheist Humanism*, 1944), **Josef Pieper** (*Leisure: the Basis of Culture*, 1948, on cult as the root of culture) and **Dawson**'s actual arguments were not read this session. Do not quote them without opening the texts.
- Named critics of Rahner (von Balthasar, *Cordula*, 1966; Ratzinger) and of Girard (e.g. anthropologists on sacrifice) need primary citations.
- Otto's own wording of "the numinous" was not verified verbatim.

## 6. Orthodox perspectives: Schmemann, Florovsky, Stăniloae, Rose, Hart, Ware, the 2016 Council

### Takeaway
Orthodox treatments emphasise anthropology over chronology:
- Schmemann's *homo adorans*: worship is the defining human act, and secularism is a "liturgical heresy".
- Hart's classical theism: God is "the fullness of Being itself", not a being; this reframes the debunking debate.
- Florovsky's "Christian Hellenism" (not verified this session).

On evolution, Orthodoxy has no single conciliar ruling and a wide range. Fr. Seraphim Rose is the prominent incompatibilist, and others are compatibilist. The 2016 Holy and Great Council endorsed "inter-religious understanding and co-operation... without this involving any religious syncretism", but did not issue a theology of religions comparable to *Nostra Aetate*.

### Cited Findings
- **Holy and Great Council (Crete, 2016), "The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World"**, verbatim: "The various local Orthodox Churches can contribute to inter-religious understanding and co-operation for the peaceful co-existence and harmonious living together in society, without this involving any religious syncretism. We are convinced that, as God's fellow workers (I Cor 3:9), we can advance to this common service together with all people of good will" — [holycouncil.org](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world)
- **The Council's Encyclical**, verbatim: it criticises a globalisation that "has planned the gradual neutralization of the impediments from opposing national, religious, ideological and other traditions". It says the "Orthodox understanding of man is opposed both to the arrogant apotheosis of the individual and his rights, and to the humiliating debasement of the human person" — [holycouncil.org, Encyclical](https://www.holycouncil.org/encyclical-holy-council)
- **The Council's six official documents** include "Relations of the Orthodox Church with the Rest of the Christian World" (on intra-Christian relations, not other religions) — [holycouncil.org document list](https://www.holycouncil.org/-/rest-of-christian-world)
- **Schmemann, *For the Life of the World*** (1963/1973). The *homo adorans* passage is usually cited to p. 15. A scholarly article reports that Schmemann defined secularism as "above all a negation of worship" — a "liturgical heresy" — [search summary citing *RESS* 2023, DOI 10.2478/ress-2023-0107](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/ress-2023-0107.pdf). I did not see the book's text; verify before quoting.
- **David Bentley Hart, *The Experience of God: Being, Consciousness, Bliss*** (2013). Its parts are "God, Gods, and the World", "Being, Consciousness, Bliss" and "The Reality of God". Hart holds that God is not "a being among other beings, not even the greatest possible of beings, but is instead the fullness of Being itself, the absolute plenitude of reality upon which all else depends". He calls the notion of God as "some magical invisible friend who lives beyond the clouds, or in some ghostly cosmic mechanic invoked to explain gaps" a strawman. He argues "consciousness cannot be satisfactorily explained in purely physical terms." — [Wikipedia, "The Experience of God"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Experience_of_God), quoting the book (secondary)
- **Seraphim Rose.** *Genesis, Creation and Early Man* is a posthumous (2000) "collection of Rose's commentaries, letters, notes, and lectures defending creationist views" — [Wikipedia, "Seraphim Rose"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seraphim_Rose). It "has received a varied response within the American Orthodox community". - **No official Orthodox position.** A blog asks why "the administrative Orthodox Church does not hold a position" on evolution, and a bibliography lists Metropolitan Kallistos Ware's talk "Orthodoxy and Evolution" — [Mystagogy (John Sanidopoulos)](https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2009/11/q-why-is-there-no-official-orthodox.html?m=1) (weak source; use only as an indicator)

### Inferences
- Schmemann's and Hart's moves are structurally like CCC 28 and *Dei Filius*: religion's universality expresses a created orientation. Hart adds a philosophical move that matters for the CSR debate. If "God" names the ground of being rather than an agent within the world, then explanations of why humans *detect agents* do not straightforwardly bear on the classical theist's God. Critics would answer that ordinary believers' God is the agent CSR describes. (Inference; source both sides before publishing.)
- The 2016 Council's statement on other religions is pastoral and civic, not doctrinal. There is no Orthodox counterpart to the NA/LG/DI sequence to set against the Catholic one, which is itself worth saying.

## 7. Scientists and philosophers in dialogue: CSR and the debunking debate (Barrett, Plantinga, Clark, Murray, Wilkins and Griffiths, Conway Morris)

### Takeaway
The cognitive science of religion (CSR) explains belief through mechanisms such as hypersensitive agency detection. Theist philosophers argue that this leaves the truth question open:
- Murray (2009), Plantinga (2011), Clark & Barrett (2011).
- Clark & Barrett frame it in Reidian terms: a natural, non-inferential belief-forming faculty is innocent until proven guilty.

Wilkins & Griffiths argue that evolution supports a truth-tracking "Milvian bridge" for commonsense and scientific beliefs, but not for religious ones. A recent objection to the Clark–Barrett line: the "natural" beliefs CSR finds are not the specific beliefs of the world religions. Conway Morris's evolutionary-convergence work is often invoked for directionality in evolution, but it is a biological thesis, not a theory of religion.

### Cited Findings
- **Barrett, *Born Believers: The Science of Children's Religious Belief*** (2012; Free Press per *Publishers Weekly*, 336 pp.). Per reviews, Barrett holds that "just as human beings are born walkers and talkers, they are born believers", calls the tendency "natural religion", and says it "in and of itself is insufficient to produce a mature theology". One reviewer notes some of the cited studies used only children from religious families — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9781439196540); [Christian Scholars' Review](https://christianscholars.com/born-believers-the-science-of-childrens-religious-belief/); [Books & Culture](https://booksandculture.com/articles/webexclusives/2012/september/born-believers-3.html) (summary level; book not read; I could not verify the phrase "intuitive theism")
- **Clark & Barrett**, "Reidian Religious Epistemology and the Cognitive Science of Religion", *Journal of the American Academy of Religion* 79(3), Sept 2011, pp. 639–675 — [catalogue record via search](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/asn,65092658). A critic argues the argument fails "because the natural beliefs discovered by cognitive scientists of religion are not the religious beliefs of the major world religions" — [PhilArchive, VANRBI](https://philarchive.org/rec/VANRBI) (seen as a search summary)
- **Murray**, "Scientific Explanations of Religion and the Justification of Religious Belief", ch. 8 in J. Schloss & M. Murray (eds.), *The Believing Primate: Scientific, Philosophical, and Theological Reflections on the Origin of Religion* (OUP, 2009). The same volume contains Plantinga's "Games Scientists Play" — [OUP book page](https://academic.oup.com/book/12844). A reviewer reports that Murray answers "no" to whether evolutionary theories undermine belief — [A. Campbell review](https://acampbell.org.uk/bookreviews/r/schloss-murray.html) (reader review; chapter not read)
- **Wilkins & Griffiths**, "Evolutionary Debunking Arguments in Three Domains: Fact, Value, and Religion", in *A New Science of Religion* (Routledge, 2013). Earlier versions: "When do evolutionary explanations of belief debunk belief?" (PhilSci-Archive preprint 2010) and "Crossing the Milvian Bridge" (2015). The Milvian-bridge link between truth and fitness payoff can be built for commonsense and scientific beliefs but not for moral and religious ones — [PhilSci-Archive 5314](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/5314); [Melbourne record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/737583-evolutionary-debunking-arguments-in-three-domains--fact--value--and-religion); a reply in *Religious Studies* (2020), "Did natural selection select for true religious beliefs?" — [Cambridge PDF](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/25C7BCB53C332C2E2BAE9FD8E46F4BB4/S0034412520000141a.pdf/did-natural-selection-select-for-true-religious-beliefs.pdf) (all summary level; PhilSci-Archive blocked by a TLS error through the proxy)
- **Plantinga, *Where the Conflict Really Lies: Science, Religion, and Naturalism*** (OUP 2011). Per study-guide summaries, he argues that naturalistic origin stories do not by themselves discredit belief. He credits the agency-detection proposal to Stewart Guthrie, and argues the real conflict is between science and *naturalism* (his evolutionary argument against naturalism) — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/where-the-conflict-really-lies/part-2-chapters-5-6-summary/) (low-grade secondary; book not read)
- **Simon Conway Morris** works on evolutionary convergence: *Life's Solution: Inevitable Humans in a Lonely Universe* (CUP 2003), his 2007 Gifford Lectures, and earlier *The Crucible of Creation* (OUP 1998). His interest was "in part spurred by Stephen Jay Gould's arguments for the importance of contingency" — [Wikipedia, "Simon Conway Morris"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Conway_Morris)
- **Precedent within the classical debate.** Evans-Pritchard's 1965 conclusion makes the same point as Murray and Plantinga: theories that "explain the illusion" presuppose it — [Monoskop PDF](https://www.monoskop.org/images/e/e1/Evans_Pritchard_E_E_Theories_of_Primitive_Religion_1965.pdf)

### Inferences
- The debate has converged on a narrow question: does a belief-forming process that would produce the belief *whether or not it is true* defeat the belief? Theists reply that this assumes the process is not truth-apt. Wilkins & Griffiths reply that no fitness-to-truth link exists for religious content. The "natural beliefs are not creedal beliefs" objection cuts both ways:
  - It weakens Reidian support for specific doctrines.
  - It also weakens debunking of them, since specific doctrines come from testimony and tradition, not HADD alone.
- Where the theological lens fits CSR well: CSR's finding that religiosity is natural and early-developing matches CCC 28 and Schmemann's *homo adorans* almost too neatly. Where it strains: CSR also explains the *specific* contents (agents, minds, ancestors) as cognitive by-products, which fits animism-first (Peoples et al.) better than primordial monotheism.
