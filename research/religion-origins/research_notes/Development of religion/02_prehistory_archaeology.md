# Prehistoric and archaeological evidence for the emergence of religion (Middle Palaeolithic to Neolithic)

Notes for a blog series on the development of religion. Scope: what the material record shows, and does not show, about the earliest religious behaviour. Interpretations are presented as interpretations, with the debate around them.

Citation conventions. Sources fetched or surfaced by search in this session are linked directly. A few standard references are cited from the researcher's prior knowledge by DOI; these are marked **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]** and should be spot-checked before publication. "ka" = thousand years ago; "cal BP / cal BC" = calibrated radiocarbon.

## 1. Burial: earliest intentional burials, and what counts as evidence of afterlife belief

### Takeaway
Deliberate burial of the dead by *Homo sapiens* is well attested from about 120-90 ka in the Levant (Skhul, Qafzeh) and about 78 ka in Africa (Panga ya Saidi). Most specialists also accept some Neanderthal burials (La Chapelle-aux-Saints, Shanidar), though a minority still dispute them. The *Homo naledi* claims (2023-2025) remain contested. Even a secure burial shows care for the dead and a deliberate treatment of the corpse, not belief in an afterlife as such. Grave goods (Qafzeh 11's antlers) are the strongest, though still ambiguous, sign of something more.

### Cited Findings

**Skhul and Qafzeh (Israel), early *H. sapiens***
- First radiometric dating of Skhul used ESR on bovid teeth from the burial levels. Ages average 81 ± 15 ka (early uranium uptake model) and 101 ± 12 ka (linear uptake), and the authors conclude Skhul and Qafzeh are of similar age. Stringer, Grün, Schwarcz & Goldberg 1989, "ESR dates for the hominid burial site of Es Skhul in Israel", *Nature* 338:756-758. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/338756a0)
- Vandermeersch & Bar-Yosef 2019, "The Paleolithic burials at Qafzeh Cave, Israel", *Paléo* 30. They give TL and ESR dates of roughly 120-90 ka for the Skhul and Qafzeh clusters, and note that the Qafzeh burial ensemble had never been fully published until then. [Paléo/OpenEdition](https://journals.openedition.org/paleo/4848)
- About ten individuals come from Skhul layer B, some apparently intentionally buried (summarised in a UEA PhD thesis, a secondary source). [UEA thesis](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/19898/2/2009AndersonHPhDVol2.pdf)
- Qafzeh 11 is an adolescent of about 12-13 years, buried with fallow deer (*Dama*) antlers by the chest, which is commonly read as a grave offering. The skull shows healed or perimortem head trauma. [Wikipedia, Qafzeh Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qafzeh_Cave) (tertiary; primary source is Vandermeersch & Bar-Yosef 2019 above). A blog claims the antlers were "saturated with imported red ochre". This is unverified, so it should not be repeated without the primary report. [thearchaeologist.org](https://www.thearchaeologist.org/blog/qafzeh-cave-israels-100000-year-old-burials)
- Ochre at Qafzeh: Hovers, Ilani, Bar-Yosef & Vandermeersch 2003, "An Early Case of Color Symbolism: Ochre Use by Modern Humans in Qafzeh Cave", *Current Anthropology* 44(4):491-522, doi:10.1086/375869. The authors argue that the roughly 92 ka ochre from the Qafzeh terrace was selected for colour and is spatially associated with burials and other non-utilitarian finds. In the CA reply they conceded that the ochre was more often yellow than red. [Current Anthropology](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/375869); [Harvard DASH copy](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/12211574)

**Panga ya Saidi, Kenya ("Mtoto"), about 78 ka**
- Martinón-Torres et al. 2021, "Earliest known human burial in Africa", *Nature* 593:95-100, doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03457-8 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**. The burial is of a child aged about 2.5-3 years, dated to **78.3 ± 4.1 ka** by luminescence dating of the burial sediments. Geochemical, granulometric and micromorphological evidence indicates a deliberately dug pit. The body was fresh when placed and rapidly covered, and lay flexed. The head is inferred to have rested on a perishable support ("pillow") and the upper body to have been wrapped (shroud). No grave goods or ochre were found. [ANU record](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/earliest-known-human-burial-in-africa/); [CNRS news](https://news.cnrs.fr/Zqq); [USF repository](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/dhhc_facpub/26)
- The claim is "earliest in Africa", not earliest overall: Levantine *H. sapiens* burials are older. [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/?p=83210)

**La Chapelle-aux-Saints (France), Neanderthal**
- Rendu et al. 2014, "Evidence supporting an intentional Neandertal burial at La Chapelle-aux-Saints", *PNAS* 111(1):81-86, doi:10.1073/pnas.1316780110. A 12-year re-excavation found the pit to be anthropogenic and the body to have been rapidly covered. The site turned out more complex than the 1908 dig suggested, with other individuals present in the bouffia Bonneval. [CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/308990); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/12/131216154328.htm)
- **Contested:** Dibble et al. 2015, "A critical look at evidence from La Chapelle-aux-Saints supporting an intentional Neandertal burial", *Journal of Archaeological Science* 53:649-657. They argue the evidence "either fails to meet these criteria or supports other interpretations equally well". [UOW repository](https://ro.uow.edu.au/smhpapers/2937)

**Shanidar (Iraqi Kurdistan), Neanderthal: the "flower burial"**
- The original claim: Ralph Solecki proposed that Shanidar 4 was buried with flowers, based on clumps of pollen in soil samples. Solecki 1975, "Shanidar IV, a Neanderthal flower burial in northern Iraq", *Science* 190:880-881, doi:10.1126/science.190.4217.880 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**. Critics proposed modern contamination, wind (Gargett 1989), or rodent burrowing (Chase & Dibble 1987). [Sommer 1999 re-evaluation, PDF copy](https://afanporsaber.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/The-Shanidar-IV-‘flower-burial’-a-re-evaluation-of-neanderthal-burial-ritual.pdf); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/science/science-news/did-neanderthals-bury-their-dead-flowers-iraq-cave-yields-new-n1138601)
- **Re-excavation, 2015 onward (Cambridge / Liverpool John Moores):** Pomeroy et al. 2020, "New Neanderthal remains associated with the 'flower burial' at Shanidar Cave", *Antiquity* 94(373):11-26, doi:10.15184/aqy.2019.207. They report the articulated upper body of an adult ("Shanidar Z"), the first articulated Neanderthal found in over 25 years, lying close to the flower-burial location and dated to about 70-60 ka. Stratigraphy suggests the body lay in a water-cut channel that had been deliberately deepened, and a rock may have served as a marker. [CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/321242); [LJMU open access](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/11568/); [Cambridge news](https://cam.ac.uk/node/211482)
- **Flower burial undermined (2023):** Hunt, Pomeroy, Reynolds, Tilby & Barker 2023, "Shanidar et ses fleurs? Reflections on the palynology of the Neanderthal 'Flower Burial' hypothesis". They conclude that at least some of the pollen clumps were probably emplaced by nesting solitary bees, whose burrows are visible in less-trampled parts of the cave and reach about 0.5 m deep. The pollen itself is ancient. Hunt stresses that this "does not negate the possibility" of other Neanderthal symbolic behaviour. [LJMU repository](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/21025/); [LJMU press release](https://www.ljmu.ac.uk/about-us/news/articles/2023/8/29/neanderthals); [Live Science](https://livescience.com/archaeology/famous-neanderthal-flower-burial-debunked-because-pollen-was-left-by-burrowing-bees). **Venue conflict:** search summaries attribute the paper to *Journal of Archaeological Science*, while the brief cited *L'Anthropologie*. I did not confirm which; check the LJMU record.
- Net position: the deliberate placement of Shanidar Z remains plausible and is argued for, but the flowers no longer have good evidential support.

***Homo naledi*, Rising Star cave system (South Africa)**
- *H. naledi* remains date to about 236-335 ka. Dirks et al. 2017, *eLife* 6:e24231, doi:10.7554/eLife.24231 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**.
- June 2023: Berger et al. posted eLife reviewed preprints claiming deliberate burial (Dinaledi Chamber "Feature 1", Hill Antechamber) and engravings on cave walls. [eLife RP 89106, reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106/reviews); companion preprint [eLife RP 89125, reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89125/reviews)
- First-round reviews were strongly negative. Nature's news report was headed that reviewers say there is "no evidence", with the scattered bones bearing little resemblance to articulated burials elsewhere. [Nature news d41586-023-02415-w (PDF copy)](https://sheseeksnonfiction.blog/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/d41586-023-02415-w.pdf). A *PaleoAnthropology* critique called the public reviews "almost unanimously negative" and the evidence "incomplete and inadequate". [PaleoAnthropology PDF](https://paleoanthropology.org/ojs/index.php/paleo/libraryFiles/downloadPublic/25)
- Martinón-Torres, Garate, Herries & Petraglia, "No scientific evidence that Homo naledi buried their dead and produced rock art", *Journal of Human Evolution* 195 (2024; online 2023). They hold that natural agents and post-depositional processes had not been excluded, and that the "engravings" could be natural weathering or claw marks. They also objected to the claims being promoted through a Netflix documentary before review. [Griffith repository](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/fe978e9b-8880-43a9-9929-8c22720457ae); [Live Science](https://livescience.com/archaeology/no-scientific-evidence-that-ancient-human-relative-buried-dead-and-carved-art-as-portrayed-in-netflix-documentary-researchers-argue)
- **2025 revision:** a revised burial paper (v2, 28 March 2025; a later Version of Record dated 1 September 2025) is listed as Berger et al. 2025, *eLife*, doi:10.7554/eLife.89106. Co-author John Hawks describes the revised assessment as "remarkably positive", saying all reviewers accept burial as "a strong explanation". [eLife v2 reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106v2/reviews); [Hawks blog](https://www.johnhawks.net/p/burials-by-homo-naledi). Even in round 2, Reviewer 1 says Robbins et al. 2021 on alternative, easier cave access is barely addressed, and calls for independent teams to be allowed to analyse the material. Reviewer 2 supports the conclusions but asks for fuller data tables. [eLife v2 reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106v2/reviews)
- Status: the burial claim has moved from "inadequate" toward a more favourable editorial assessment, but independent replication is lacking. I found no positive re-assessment of the **engraving** claim.

**General skepticism about Palaeolithic burial**
- Gargett 1989, "Grave Shortcomings: The Evidence for Neanderthal Burial", *Current Anthropology* 30(2):157-190. He argued there was no single convincing Neanderthal burial, and revisited Qafzeh, Saint-Césaire, Kebara, Amud and Dederiyeh in 1999. [CA issue TOC](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/toc/ca/1989/30/2)
- Pettitt finds Gargett's critique "largely unconvincing". Most scholars accept that some Neanderthals were deliberately buried, typically without grave goods or other visible elaboration. Pettitt 2002, "The Neanderthal dead", *World Archaeology*. [PDF](https://arheo.ffzg.unizg.hr/ska/tekstovi/neanderthal_dead.pdf)

### Inferences
- A defensible hierarchy for blog purposes:
  1. corpse caching or protection (possible in many species);
  2. deliberate pit burial (Skhul, Qafzeh, Panga ya Saidi, probably La Chapelle and Shanidar Z);
  3. burial with apparent offerings (Qafzeh 11 antlers, Qafzeh ochre);
  4. repeated, elaborated mortuary programmes (Upper Palaeolithic onward, e.g. Sungir, which was not researched here).
- Only levels 3-4 begin to suggest beliefs about the dead's continued existence, and even these are compatible with grief, status display or social memory.
- Middle Palaeolithic burials are rare and scattered over tens of thousands of years. This fits "occasional" rather than "normative" mortuary practice.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary details on Skhul V (boar mandible "grave good") or Tinshemet Cave (2024-25 Levantine burials).
- I did not establish whether the Panga ya Saidi child is morphologically *H. sapiens* beyond the authors' attribution.
- The final eLife Version of Record assessment wording (significance and strength terms) could not be retrieved verbatim.

## 2. Symbolic behaviour (ochre, engravings, beads, cave art, figurines) and its interpretations

### Takeaway
Clear symbolic or abstract behaviour appears in Africa and the Levant by roughly 100-75 ka: pierced shell beads, engraved ochre, and an ochre-crayon drawing at Blombos. Figurative art appears by at least 51.2 ka in Sulawesi and about 40 ka in Europe (the Lion Man, a part-human, part-animal figure). These are evidence of symbolic capacity. The step from them to religion is interpretive. The shamanistic/neuropsychological reading is influential but sharply criticised, and the older "hunting magic" and totemism readings are largely historiographic.

### Cited Findings

**Beads**
- Two perforated *Nassarius gibbosulus* shells from Skhul are dated about 100-135 ka; a bead from Oued Djebbana (Algeria) was found about 190 km from the contemporary sea. Vanhaeren, d'Errico, Stringer, James, Todd & Mienis 2006, "Middle Paleolithic Shell Beads in Israel and Algeria", *Science* 312:1785-1788, doi:10.1126/science.1128139 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**. Summary: [Wikipedia, Shell jewelry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_jewelry)
- Taforalt (Grotte des Pigeons, Morocco): perforated *Nassarius* beads from levels dated about **82 ka** by luminescence and U-series. Bouzouggar et al. 2007, *PNAS* 104(24):9964-9969, doi:10.1073/pnas.0703877104. Reports give the count as 12 or 13 shells. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1891266); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/06/070618091210.htm)

**Blombos Cave, South Africa**
- Engraved ochre pieces from Middle Stone Age layers about 77 ka. Henshilwood et al. 2002, "Emergence of modern human behavior: Middle Stone Age engravings from South Africa", *Science* 295:1278-1280, doi:10.1126/science.1067575 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session; the 77 ka figure was not re-verified]**.
- A cross-hatched pattern drawn with an ochre crayon on a silcrete flake comes from about 73 ka levels. It predates other known drawings by at least 30 ka. The same layers contain shell beads and engraved ochre. Henshilwood, d'Errico, van Niekerk, Dayet, Queffelec & Pollarolo 2018, "An abstract drawing from the 73,000-year-old levels at Blombos Cave, South Africa", *Nature* 562:115-118, doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0514-3. [CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/317318); [CNRS press release](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/discovery-earliest-drawing); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v562y2018i7725d10.1038_s41586-018-0514-3.html)

**Sulawesi (Indonesia) cave art**
- At Leang Karampuang (Maros-Pangkep), a scene of three human-like figures interacting with a wild pig is **at least 51.2 ka**, by laser-ablation U-series dating of calcite crusts overlying the paint (a minimum age). It is described as the oldest securely dated figurative art and the earliest narrative scene. Oktaviana et al. 2024, "Narrative cave art in Indonesia by 51,200 years ago", *Nature* 631:814-818, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07541-7 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**. [Griffith news](https://news.griffith.edu.au/2024/07/04/cave-painting-in-indonesia-is-the-oldest-known-picture-story/); [Tempo](https://en.tempo.co/read/1887351/brin-finds-51200-year-old-cave-paintings-the-oldest-in-indonesia)
- Earlier Sulawesi records: hand stencils at about 39.9 ka (Aubert et al. 2014, *Nature* 514:223-227, doi:10.1038/nature13422) and a pig painting at Leang Tedongnge at about 45.5 ka (Brumm et al. 2021, *Science Advances* 7:eabd4648, doi:10.1126/sciadv.abd4648) **[both DOIs from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**. An earlier Sulawesi hunting scene (Aubert et al. 2019) was read as showing therianthropes (part-human, part-animal figures). Press coverage applies "therianthrope" language to the new finds too. [artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/worlds-oldest-cave-art-2508051)

**European Upper Palaeolithic**
- Chauvet-Pont d'Arc (France): a Bayesian model of more than 250 radiocarbon dates gives two human phases, **about 37-33.5 ka cal BP** (most of the art) and **about 31-28 ka cal BP** (torch marks). Only black (charcoal) paintings can be dated directly. Quiles et al. 2016, "A high-precision chronological model for the decorated Upper Paleolithic cave of Chauvet-Pont d'Arc", *PNAS* 113(17):4670-4675. [USF repository](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/2440); [Popular Archaeology](https://popular-archaeology.com/article/reconstructing-the-history-of-chauvet-pont-darc-cave/)
- Hohlenstein-Stadel "Lion Man" (Löwenmensch), a mammoth-ivory figure with a lion's head and a human body. Renewed excavation (2008-2013) located the 1939 findspot in layer Au, the lowest Aurignacian layer, radiocarbon dated to **about 39-41 ka cal BP**. This places it among the oldest figurative artworks. Kind, Ebinger-Rist, Wolf, Beutelspacher & Wehrberger 2014, *Quartär* 61:129-145. [Quartär PDF](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/qu/article/view/78462/72446); [Archaeology magazine](https://archaeology.org/?p=1163). Earlier estimates were about 32-34 ka.
- Hohle Fels "Venus": a mammoth-ivory female figurine, at least about 35 ka (radiocarbon, basal Aurignacian). Conard 2009, "A female figurine from the basal Aurignacian of Hohle Fels Cave in southwestern Germany", *Nature* 459:248-252, doi:10.1038/nature07995 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**.

**Interpretive models**
- **Neuropsychological / shamanistic model:** Lewis-Williams & Dowson 1988, "The Signs of All Times: Entoptic Phenomena in Upper Palaeolithic Art", *Current Anthropology* 29(2):201-245. They propose a three-stage model of altered states of consciousness: geometric entoptics, then construal, then iconic imagery. Built from San and Coso ethnography, it was then applied to the Palaeolithic and popularised in Lewis-Williams 2002, *The Mind in the Cave*. [Rock Art Research debate](https://rockartresearch.com/index.php/rock/article/view/134)
- **Critiques:** Helvenston & Bahn (from 2002) argue that the staged progression is characteristic of specific hallucinogens (LSD, psilocybin, mescaline) that they say were unavailable to these peoples, and that the model is reductionist. Helvenston 2015 (*Rock Art Research*) restates the critique. [Rock Art Research](https://rockartresearch.com/index.php/rock/article/download/134/129)
- **Counter-critiques:** defenders say critics attack a "misconception" of a "Three Stages of Trance" model that was never proposed in strict form. Froese, Woodward & Ikegami offer a revised model, and Woodward says Helvenston partly attacks a straw man. A 2016 *Economic Botany* paper argues *Psilocybe* species were native to Africa and Europe, undercutting the "unavailable" argument. [Akers et al. 2016, Economic Botany](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12231-016-9342-2); [CAJ discussion PDF](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/EAECEF9095A87C13979B1D37306669EC/stamped-S095977430400006Xa.pdf/waking_the_trancefixed.pdf)
- **Hunting magic and totemism:** these early-20th-century readings (associated with Reinach and Breuil) are now mostly treated as historiography. I did not retrieve sources this session (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Beads and engraved ochre are about social signalling and shared conventions. They show the cognitive and social machinery that religion presupposes, not religion itself.
- Composite human-animal figures (the Lion Man, Sulawesi therianthropes) are the strongest Palaeolithic visual hint of beings beyond the ordinary, a "supernatural" category. They are also compatible with myth, narrative or totemic identity, which need not imply worship.
- The Sulawesi dates show that figurative and narrative art was not a European invention. Any "creative explosion in Ice Age Europe" framing is outdated.

### Gaps
- I retrieved no sources this session on Lascaux's dating (commonly given as about 17-19 ka) or on Leroi-Gourhan's structuralist reading. Attach citations before using either.
- I retrieved no sources on interpretations of the Venus figurines (fertility, mother goddess, self-representation, etc.) or on Breuil and hunting magic.
- Sulawesi U-series dates are minimum ages, and the dating method has its own critics. I did not survey these critiques for the 2024 paper specifically.

## 3. Did Neanderthals have religion?

### Takeaway
Neanderthals sometimes buried their dead, used pigments, and built enigmatic stalagmite structures deep in Bruniquel Cave about 176 ka. The headline claims of something more (flower burial, 64 ka cave art) have weakened under scrutiny. The majority position is "symbolic capacity probable, religion unknowable". Skeptics argue even the burials are not secure.

### Cited Findings
- Bruniquel Cave (France): ring-shaped structures of about 400 broken stalagmites, with traces of fire, about 330 m inside the cave. U-series dating of regrowth on the structures and of burnt bone gives **176.5 ± 2.1 ka**, revising an earlier radiocarbon date of about 47 ka. Neanderthal authorship is inferred because no other hominins were present in the region. The authors state that the "function of the structures remains conjectural". Jaubert et al. 2016, "Early Neanderthal constructions deep in Bruniquel Cave in southwestern France", *Nature* 534:111-114, doi:10.1038/nature18291. [CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/308217); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v534y2016i7605d10.1038_nature18291.html); [Archaeology magazine](https://archaeology.org/issues/240-1701/features/5114-france-neanderthal-structures)
- Iberian cave art: Hoffmann et al. 2018, "U-Th dating of carbonate crusts reveals Neandertal origin of Iberian cave art", *Science* 359:912-915 (doi:10.1126/science.aap7778 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**), claimed minimum ages of more than 64 ka at La Pasiega, Maltravieso and Ardales, predating modern humans in Europe. [CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/316011)
  - **Contested:** White et al. 2020, "Still no archaeological evidence that Neanderthals created Iberian cave art", *Journal of Human Evolution* (44 co-authors), argue that uranium leaching can inflate ages and that the samples are not closed systems. [USF repository](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/5193). Slimak et al. (Science comment) argue the data better support about 47 ka, and Aubert et al. question whether the Ardales red flowstone markings are art at all. [Comment](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/858)
- Burials: La Chapelle-aux-Saints (Rendu et al. 2014 vs Dibble et al. 2015) and Shanidar Z (Pomeroy et al. 2020), with the flower-burial evidence weakened (Hunt et al. 2023). See section 1.
- Gargett 1989/1999 represents the skeptical pole; Pettitt 2002 the mainstream ("some deliberate burial, without visible elaboration"). See section 1.

### Inferences
- Neanderthals plausibly had the capacities underlying religion: deliberate treatment of the dead, pigment use, and coordinated construction underground. No Neanderthal artefact demands a religious explanation.
- Bruniquel is the most intriguing case for a "ritual" reading, given that people built a structure in deep darkness with fire. Its excavators decline to assign it a function.
- A fair blog framing: absence of secure evidence is not evidence of absence. The record is too thin to answer the question either way.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve sources on Neanderthal eagle-talon ornaments (Krapina) or Cueva de los Aviones pigmented shells (Hoffmann et al. 2018, *Science Advances*). Both are relevant to symbolic capacity.
- No source this session offered a synthesis such as Pettitt 2011, *The Palaeolithic Origins of Human Burial*. Consider adding one.

## 4. Göbekli Tepe, Karahan Tepe and the Taş Tepeler: "first the temple, then the city"?

### Takeaway
Göbekli Tepe (southeastern Turkey) has monumental enclosures with T-shaped carved pillars, built from about the mid-10th millennium cal BC (Pre-Pottery Neolithic A) by people who did not yet farm domesticated crops. Klaus Schmidt interpreted it as a hunter-gatherer ritual centre or "temple" without houses, implying that religion drove sedentism and, ultimately, agriculture. Since about 2015-2020 the DAI team (Lee Clare and colleagues) and the Turkish Taş Tepeler programme (Necmi Karul) have revised this. They identify water-collecting rock-cut features and probable domestic buildings, and now describe a settlement with special buildings. The monuments are still real and early, but "temple without village" is no longer the consensus.

### Cited Findings
- **Schmidt's model:** Göbekli was seen as a ritual site because of (1) special buildings with T-pillars, (2) an apparent absence of domestic structures and (3) no water source. Hunter-gatherers would have congregated for pillar carving, feasting and probably beer drinking. Dietrich, Heun, Notroff, Schmidt & Zarnkow 2012, "The role of cult and feasting in the emergence of Neolithic communities. New evidence from Göbekli Tepe, south-eastern Turkey", *Antiquity* 86:674-695. [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A1AA4FB20657599F859860D94CCD090E/S0003598X00047840a.pdf/role_of_cult_and_feasting_in_the_emergence_of_neolithic_communities_new_evidence_from_gobekli_tepe_southeastern_turkey.pdfResearch); [DAI blog, "A sanctuary, or so fair a house?"](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2017/01/24/a-sanctuary-or-so-fair-a-house/)
- The slogan "First came the temple, then the city" is attributed to Schmidt in popular coverage, notably Curry 2008, *Smithsonian* ("Göbekli Tepe: The World's First Temple?"). **I did not re-fetch this attribution this session**, and a search for the German form failed. Cite carefully. [Smithsonian (not re-fetched)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/gobekli-tepe-the-worlds-first-temple-83613665/)
- **Dating:** wall plaster from Enclosure D gave 9984 ± 42 BP, about 9745-9314 cal BC (95.4%), i.e. PPNA (Dietrich & Schmidt 2010). Fill dates spread from about 9746-8818 cal BC down into the 9th millennium cal BC. Because the Layer III enclosures were deliberately backfilled, the dating is complicated, and the date of abandonment is not agreed. Dietrich, Köksal-Schmidt, Notroff & Schmidt 2013, *Neo-Lithics* 1/2013:36-47. ### Inferences
- What survives the revision: large, labour-intensive, iconographically elaborate communal buildings were made by pre-agricultural or proto-agricultural communities in Upper Mesopotamia. The ritual or communal dimension of these buildings is not in doubt.
- What does not survive: the claim that these were standalone pilgrimage temples visited by mobile foragers. They now look like special buildings within settlements, as at other PPN sites. The causal sequence "religion first, then sedentism, then farming" is therefore weakened. Ritual, sedentism and cultivation look more like co-evolving developments than a one-way chain.
- For a Catholic/Orthodox-sympathetic reader: the monuments still show that sacred or communal architecture was central to these early villages. The data fit "religion as integral to the Neolithic transition" better than "religion as cause".

## 5. Çatalhöyük, Jericho plastered skulls and Levantine Neolithic ancestor cults

### Takeaway
At Çatalhöyük (central Anatolia, about 7100-6000 cal BC), Hodder's Templeton-funded seminars produced two edited volumes. Their argument is that religion was entangled with daily domestic life, with "history houses", burial under floors and animal installations, rather than set apart in temples. Religious routinisation is proposed as a driver of social complexity. Plastered skulls from Jericho and other PPNB sites are classically read as ancestor veneration (Kenyon). Later osteology shows women and children were also treated this way, which complicates an "elder male ancestor" reading.

### Cited Findings
- Hodder (ed.) 2010, *Religion in the Emergence of Civilization: Çatalhöyük as a Case Study* (Cambridge University Press). It is an interdisciplinary volume with natural scientists, archaeologists, anthropologists, philosophers and theologians, and includes Hodder & Pels, "History houses: a new interpretation of architectural elaboration at Çatalhöyük". [Cambridge Core listing via search](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/books/religion-at-work-in-a-neolithic-society/249C9FCC00EA0E931C9AD5E73511DB06)
- Hodder (ed.) 2014, *Religion at Work in a Neolithic Society: Vital Matters* (CUP; online June 2014). It arose from a John Templeton Foundation seminar held at Çatalhöyük in 2009-2011, and covers materiality, animism and object vitality. [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/religion-at-work-in-a-neolithic-society/modes-of-religiosity-and-the-evolution-of-social-complexity-at-catalhoyuk/6328A5C9CA3D9ECC52770C6A20166E71); [Hodder's site](https://ian-hodder.com/books/category/edited)
  - Whitehouse, Mazzucato, Hodder & Atkinson, "Modes of religiosity and the evolution of social complexity at Çatalhöyük": "a major factor driving the emergence of complex society was religious routinization". [Cambridge Core chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/religion-at-work-in-a-neolithic-society/modes-of-religiosity-and-the-evolution-of-social-complexity-at-catalhoyuk/6328A5C9CA3D9ECC52770C6A20166E71)
  - Another chapter is titled "Religion as anthropomorphism at Çatalhöyük". [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/religion-at-work-in-a-neolithic-society/religion-as-anthropomorphism-at-catalhoyuk/7CF3D194985E864C691EBBFF99910E3D)
  - A third volume, *Religion, History and Place in the Origin of Settled Life* (Hodder ed., 2018), is known to exist **[from prior knowledge, not verified this session]**.
- Jericho: Kathleen Kenyon found the first plastered skulls in 1953 and read them as portraits of venerated ancestors, probably male elders. [UT Austin, 'Ain Ghazal project](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/ain-ghazal/the-plastered-skulls/); [Ashmolean Jericho skull](https://ashmolean.org/node/575061) (the museum hedges the reading with "if correct")
- Bonogofsky challenged the ancestor-cult consensus with radiographic study and Melanesian ethnographic parallels. At Köşk Höyük she found skulls of both sexes and all ages modelled alike. - Skull-modelling technology study (Jericho, Kfar HaHoresh, Beisamoun): the plaster was locally produced. [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-technology-of-skull-modelling-in-the-pre-pottery-neolithic-b-/)

### Inferences
- The Levantine and Anatolian Neolithic shows systematic secondary treatment of the dead: skull removal, plastering, curation and sub-floor burial. This is the first point at which "relationship with the dead" becomes a durable, repeated, community-wide practice. Whether it is "ancestor *worship*" or memorialisation is debated.
- The Çatalhöyük volumes are themselves part of the scholarly debate. They were Templeton-funded and included theologians, which some readers may see as a strength and others as a bias. Present this neutrally.

## 6. Phylogenetic reconstruction of ancestral hunter-gatherer religion (Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016)

### Takeaway
Using 33 hunter-gatherer societies mapped onto a time-calibrated supertree, Peoples et al. reconstruct **animism** as the oldest religious trait, present in the most recent common ancestor of the sample. Belief in an **afterlife** comes next, then **shamanism**, then **ancestor worship**. Active ancestral spirits and active high gods appear late and were absent in the ancestral state. High gods do not co-vary with the other traits.

### Cited Findings
- Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016, "Hunter-Gatherers and the Origins of Religion", *Human Nature* 27(3):261-282, doi:10.1007/s12110-016-9260-0 **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]**. Open access: [PMC4958132](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4958132/); [CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/322685)
  - The traits coded are animism, belief in an afterlife, shamanism, ancestor worship (and active ancestral spirits), high gods and active (moralising) high gods. They reconstruct ancestral states on a time-calibrated supertree built from phylogenetic and linguistic classifications. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4958132/)
  - Animism is oldest, "in agreement with long-standing beliefs about the fundamental role of this trait". The absence of active ancestor spirits and active high gods in early humans suggests "a deep history for the egalitarian nature of hunter-gatherer societies". Most traits correlate positively, but high gods are the exception. [CARTA abstract](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/322685)
  - HRAF's hypothesis summaries record animism-first as supported (Pagel's test, p < 0.05) and no support for high-gods coevolution. [HRAF](https://hraf.yale.edu/ehc/documents/1037); [HRAF hypothesis](https://hraf.yale.edu/ehc/hypotheses/3083)

### Inferences
- The method reconstructs the state of the common ancestor *of the sampled living societies*. Its time depth is bounded by that tree, not by the Palaeolithic, and the sample of recent foragers has been influenced by neighbouring farmers and missionaries. It cannot directly tell us about 100 ka religion.
- The result is relevant to the debate over "Urmonotheismus" (primordial monotheism; Wilhelm Schmidt's 20th-century Catholic thesis), which the blog's audience may know. The 2016 analysis does **not** reconstruct an ancestral high god. Present this fairly, alongside the method's limits: a small sample, recent ethnographies, and coarse trait definitions.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve published critiques or replications of Peoples et al. (e.g., on sample size and trait coding). The exact sample size of 33 was not verified this session; confirm it against the PMC text.

## 7. The limits of inference from material culture to belief

### Takeaway
Archaeology recovers behaviour (pits, pigments, images, buildings), not beliefs. Every step to "religion" rests on bridging arguments: ethnographic analogy, cognitive theory, or exclusion of mundane explanations. Recent controversies show how fragile those bridges are: Shanidar's flowers, *naledi*'s burials, the Neanderthal cave-art dates and Göbekli's "temple".

### Cited Findings
- Taphonomy can mimic ritual: bee burrows produced the Shanidar "flower" pollen ([Hunt et al. 2023, LJMU](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/21025/)), and natural agents may explain the Rising Star accumulations ([Martinón-Torres et al. 2024, Griffith](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/fe978e9b-8880-43a9-9929-8c22720457ae)).
- Dating methods can be overturned: Bruniquel moved from about 47 ka to 176 ka ([Jaubert et al. 2016](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/308217)), and the Iberian "Neanderthal art" dates are disputed on grounds of uranium leaching ([White et al. 2020](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/5193)).
- Function is often explicitly left open by excavators ("the function of the structures remains conjectural", Jaubert et al. 2016), and site-wide interpretations change with new excavation (Göbekli Tepe, [Clare 2020](https://publications.dainst.org/journals/efb/article/view/2596); [Hürriyet 2025-26](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/new-excavations-at-gobeklitepe-unearth-rectangular-structures-225641)).
- Ethnographic models do not transfer neatly: the San-derived shamanistic model is contested when applied to Ice Age Europe ([Rock Art Research debate](https://rockartresearch.com/index.php/rock/article/view/134)). The phylogenetic method rests on recent foragers ([Peoples et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4958132/)).
- Publication process matters: the *naledi* claims were publicised (including in a Netflix documentary) before peer assessment, which critics flagged ([Live Science](https://livescience.com/archaeology/no-scientific-evidence-that-ancient-human-relative-buried-dead-and-carved-art-as-portrayed-in-netflix-documentary-researchers-argue)).

### Inferences
- A useful ladder for the series, from least to most inferential:
  1. capacity (symbolic signalling, beads, ochre);
  2. care for the dead (burial);
  3. imagined beings (therianthropes such as the Lion Man);
  4. organised communal ritual (Göbekli and Karahan Tepe, Çatalhöyük);
  5. specific beliefs (afterlife, ancestor veneration, gods).

  The record gets firmer at levels 1-2 from about 100 ka, and at level 4 by about 9500 cal BC. Level 5 is never directly observable before writing.
- Both naturalistic and theistic readers should note that the material record is silent on the *truth* or *content* of early beliefs. It constrains *when* and *how* religious behaviour became visible.

### Gaps
- I retrieved no formal methodological literature (e.g., Hawkes's 1954 "ladder of inference", Insoll's *Archaeology, Ritual, Religion*, Renfrew's criteria for recognising ritual). The ladder above is the researcher's own framing, and the classic sources should be added with citations.
