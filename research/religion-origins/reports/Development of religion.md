# Evidence Traces Religion From Instinct to Institution

**Bottom line.** Religion did not appear all at once, and no single lens accounts for it. The best-supported picture runs as follows. Human minds find agents and purposes easy to imagine. Whether a given person believes depends mostly on what they see the people around them do, not on any one cognitive bias. Care for the dead and symbolic behaviour show up from about 120,000–75,000 years ago, and figurative images of part-human, part-animal beings by about 51,000–40,000 years ago. Organised communal ritual is visible in monumental form by about 9500 cal BC, but inside settlements, not as a "temple before the city". The first states turned gods into landowners and king-makers and ritual into administration. Exclusive and moral claims about the divine (Israel's "Yahweh alone", the Zhou Mandate of Heaven, Greek philosophical monotheism) emerged in the first millennium BCE, though not as one synchronised "Axial Age". Much of the most-cited quantitative science has been corrected. The analytic-thinking and religious-priming effects failed preregistered replications, and the 2019 *Nature* paper claiming that complex societies came before "Big Gods" was retracted in 2021. Whether moralising gods drove social complexity or followed it remains genuinely unresolved, because the answer depends on how missing data are coded. For early Christianity, the existence and crucifixion of Jesus are about as secure as ancient history gets. So is the very early worship of Jesus. The miracle claims and the later dogmatic and jurisdictional developments are where the historical-critical, Catholic and Orthodox lenses separate. In the modern period, secularisation holds well in rich democracies (the United States is no longer an exception) but not globally. The world remains about three-quarters religiously affiliated, and the Christian centre of gravity has moved south. The theological lenses fit the evidence best where they make claims about human nature and meaning rather than chronology (Catechism of the Catholic Church §28, *Nostra Aetate*, Schmemann's *homo adorans*). They strain where they made datable historical claims, above all Wilhelm Schmidt's "primordial monotheism".

## Minds make gods easy to imagine, but other people decide who believes

### The byproduct story survived as description, not as causal experiment

**[Cognitive/evolutionary]** The cognitive science of religion (CSR) began with a "standard model". Religious belief is a byproduct of mental tools that evolved for other jobs: detecting agents, reading other minds (mentalizing), remembering minimally counterintuitive (MCI) concepts, explaining things by purpose (teleology), and treating minds as separable from bodies (dualism). This byproduct view is described as the most popular position among CSR researchers ([Wikipedia, Cognitive science of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science_of_religion), secondary, citing Pyysiäinen & Hauser 2010). The founding books are Guthrie's *Faces in the Clouds* (1993), Boyer's *Religion Explained* (2001), Atran's *In Gods We Trust* (2002) and Barrett's *Why Would Anyone Believe in God?* (2004) (bibliographic, not fetched; listed at [CSCA](https://csca.ca/2015/11/16/csr/)).

A decade of replication work has sorted these claims into two piles. **Descriptive claims held up better than causal ones.**

**Analytic thinking.** The flagship experiment, Gervais & Norenzayan's 2012 *Science* finding that priming analytic thinking (including showing people Rodin's *The Thinker*) reduced belief, failed a preregistered direct replication. With N = 941 (952 after an erratum), the effect was **d = 0.07 in the wrong direction, 95% CI [−0.12, 0.25]** ([Sanchez et al. 2017, PMC5325262](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5325262/); [OSF erratum](https://osf.io/qc6rh/wiki/home)). The study also failed in the Social Sciences Replication Project, where **13 of 21** *Nature* and *Science* studies replicated and replication effects averaged about half the originals ([FLoRA](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1126/science.1215647/); [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/91063/)). One fair caveat: Sanchez et al. replicated only Study 3, the *Thinker* manipulation ([Mind & Culture](https://mindandculture.org/looking-back-on-social-foundations-hypothesis/)). The correlational version fared little better across cultures. In 13 countries (N = 3,461), the link between analytic thinking and disbelief "appear[ed] robustly only in aggregate analyses and in three individual countries" ([Gervais et al. 2018, *JDM*](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/18/18228/jdm18228.html)).

**Mentalizing.** The claim that weaker mentalizing (including autism) predicts less belief in a personal God ([Norenzayan, Gervais & Trzesniewski 2012, *PLoS ONE*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0036880)) rested on a clinical comparison of 12 versus 13 adolescents and modest effects. It then failed a five-study replication with **more than 67,000 participants**, co-authored by Gervais himself. In the Dutch sample of 65,561, autism-spectrum scores added nothing beyond demographics (**OR 0.96, Nagelkerke R² < .001**). Exposure to credibility-enhancing displays (CREDs) predicted belief strongly (**OR ≈ 1.5 per scale unit; R² ≈ .10 alone**) ([Maij et al. 2017, PMC5568287](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5568287/)). The notes flag a discrepancy worth repeating: van Elk's 2022 review describes Maij et al. as having "replicated" a small, robust mentalizing–belief link ([PMC9714272](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9714272/)). That contradicts the 2017 paper's own title and statistics, and the primary paper should govern.

**Agency detection.** The hypersensitive agency detection device (HADD), the idea that threat makes people over-detect hidden agents, has weak support. A 2024 virtual-reality study (N = 199) did not find that expecting danger increased false agent detection ([Szymanek et al. 2024 PDF](https://mcll.uj.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Szymanek-et-al_2024_j-psych-rel-spir.pdf); full citation unverified). Agency-attribution measures together explained only **R² = .03** of supernatural belief in a US sample of 797 ([PMC5568287](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5568287/)). The notes found the six-experiment threat study by Maij, van Schie & van Elk (2019) but did not read its results ([UvA repository](https://dare.uva.nl/id/aa4a7834-585c-47f6-b479-0d133bff8d6b)), so its outcome is not reported here.

**What survived.** Kelemen's work on teleology is the most robust finding. Under time pressure, even professional physical scientists from top US universities accepted unwarranted purpose-based explanations of nature ([Kelemen, Rottman & Seston 2013, PubMed 23067062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23067062/)). Kelemen herself told the press she does not regard the tendency as "innate or hardwired" ([BU news](https://www.bu.edu/cas/even-scientists-look-for-purpose-in-nature-bu-psychologist-concludes)).

**Path models and dualism.** The Willard & Norenzayan path model (mentalizing → dualism and teleology → belief) replicated only partly. In nationally representative Czech and Slovak samples (N = 2,022), anthropomorphism was unrelated to belief in God overall, and the link reversed among Christian-affiliated believers ([Willard, Cingl & Norenzayan 2020](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/17715)). "Intuitive dualism" also looks weaker than once claimed. A 24-country study of more than 10,000 people found that cessation was the modal intuition for both mental and physical states after death. That fits "intuitive materialism" better than universal dualism ([van Elk 2022, PMC9714272](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9714272/); the study was "submitted" at the time of that review).

**MCI concepts.** The concepts themselves remain empirically unsettled. Boyer & Ramble found better recall for violations of intuitive expectations in France, Gabon and Nepal ([WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/cognitive-templates-for-religious-concepts-cross-cultural-evidenc/)). Later reviewers describe the literature as "mired in empirical contradictions and methodological shortcomings" ([Rutgers dissertation](https://rucore.libraries.rutgers.edu/rutgers-lib/65324/)), and Purzycki & Willard mounted a sustained theoretical critique ([PDF](https://www.gwern.net/doc/philosophy/religion/2015-purzycki.pdf)).

### Adaptationist claims are strongest in the field, weakest in the lab

**[Cognitive/evolutionary]** The second family of theories treats religion, or parts of it, as adaptive.

**Field and historical evidence.** This side of the record is suggestive but small. Among 83 nineteenth-century US communes, costlier requirements predicted longer survival for religious communes but not secular ones ([Sosis & Bressler 2003, SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1069397103037002003)). The widely repeated figure that 39% of religious and 6% of secular communes lasted 20 years comes via Haidt and is **unverified** against the paper. At a Spanish fire-walk, heart rates synchronised between performers and *related* spectators (N = 38) ([Konvalinka et al. 2011, PMC3100954](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3100954)). At Thaipusam in Mauritius, performers and observers of the painful kavadi ritual gave more to the temple than participants in low-ordeal collective prayer. That was 86 men, assigned non-randomly ([Aarhus IMC news](https://interactingminds.au.dk/news/enkelt/artikel/imc-researchers-published-in-psychological-science), secondary).

**Religious priming.** The experimental flagship, that implicit religious primes increase generosity, has not survived. A 92-study meta-analysis gave an optimistic overall g = 0.40 and a bias-corrected prosocial estimate near g = 0.18 ([as reported in van Elk et al. 2015, PMC4569810](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569810/)). A sceptical reanalysis using PET-PEESE bias correction pushed the full-set estimate to **−0.002**, though a Bayesian correction still found an effect ([van Elk et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569810/)). The preregistered test then came back null: **N = 650, no significant effect of implicit primes** on Dictator Game giving ([Gomes & McCullough 2015 PDF](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~memccullough/Papers/Religious%20Priming_GomesMcCullough_FinalProof.pdf)). Later Bayesian work supported the null for implicit priming even among the religious. It found a small, reliable *explicit* priming effect, but only among religious participants ([Billingsley, Gomes & McCullough 2018, PMC6124061](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6124061/)).

**Group selection.** D. S. Wilson's *Darwin's Cathedral* (2002) treats religion as a group-level adaptation ([Christian Century review](https://www.christiancentury.org/node/28981)). It remains outside mainstream CSR and is sharply contested by Pinker ([Edge](https://edge.org/node/21475)) and Coyne ([Coyne blog](https://whyevolutionistrue.com/2014/12/07/my-tls-review-of-darwins-cathedral/)).

### Cultural transmission now does most of the explanatory work

**[Cognitive/evolutionary]** The field's centre of gravity has moved to cultural learning.

**CREDs.** Henrich's 2009 model proposed that learners guard against manipulation by adopting beliefs from people whose displays would be costly if insincere ([Henrich 2009, author PDF](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~henrich/pdfs/evolution%20of%20costly%20displays%20_henrich%202009.pdf)). CREDs now beat cognitive-bias measures in the same samples (R² ≈ .10 against ≤ .03). Van Elk calls parental CREDs the strongest predictor of supernatural belief ([PMC9714272](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9714272/)). A caveat: the CRED evidence is mostly retrospective self-report. CRED researchers themselves note "currently little causal evidence" that religious authorities' behaviour shifts existing belief ([Turpin, Andersen & Lanman, QUB](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/creds-cruds-and-catholic-scandals-experimentally-examining-the-ef/)).

**Dual inheritance.** Dual-inheritance theory dissolves the old byproduct-versus-adaptation dichotomy. Cognitive biases shape *what kinds* of religious ideas spread, and cultural selection shapes *which packages* win ([Atran & Henrich 2010, author PDF](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~henrich/pdfs/atran_henrich_evolutionofreligion.pdf); [Norenzayan et al. 2016, author PDF](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~ara/Manuscripts/Norenzayan%202016%20cultural%20evolution.pdf)).

**Twin studies.** These fit the same picture. In adult male twins, heritability was **12% for retrospective adolescent religiousness against 44% for current adult religiousness**. Shared environment fell from 56% to 18% ([Koenig et al. 2005, UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/genetic-and-environmental-influences-on-religiousness-findings-fo/)). Across seven religiosity dimensions, variance was mostly additive-genetic plus unique environment ([Vance, Maes & Kendler 2010 PDF](https://www.gwern.net/doc/genetics/heritable/correlation/2010-vance.pdf)). The family shapes adolescent religiosity, adults drift toward a temperament-linked set point, and none of this says *which* religion anyone holds.

**"Born believers" or "born receptive"?** Barrett and Kelemen argue that children's agency and purpose biases make them "intuitive theists" ([Kelemen 2004, BU PDF](https://www.bu.edu/cdl/files/2013/08/2004_Kelemen_IntuitiveTheist.pdf); Barrett, *Born Believers*, 2012, via [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9781439196540)). Banerjee & Bloom counter that spontaneous theism without cultural input is, "as best we know, non-existent" ([summary with quotes](https://mindblog.dericbownds.net/2013/03/would-tarzan-believe-in-god.html), secondary). Coyne notes that their article gives no citation for that claim ([Coyne](https://whyevolutionistrue.com/2013/03/19/is-religion-hardwired/)). Corriveau, Chen & Harris found that 5–6-year-olds with church or parochial-school exposure judged miracle-story protagonists real, while secular children judged them fictional ([BU PDF](https://www.bu.edu/learninglab/files/2015/10/Corriveau-Chen-Harris-2015.pdf)). A weak "born receptive" claim is widely accepted. A strong "born believer" claim is not.

### Does any of this bear on truth?

**[Philosophical]** Philosophers on both sides largely agree that a causal account of a belief does not by itself show the belief false. Read that way, debunking arguments "commit the genetic fallacy, conflating the context of discovery and the context of justification" ([Jong & Visala 2014, Coventry repository](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/evolutionary-debunking-arguments-against-theism-reconsidered/)). The live question is whether the specific mechanisms are unreliable for religious content. Clark & Barrett argue, in Reidian terms, that naturally formed beliefs are innocent until proven guilty ([JAAR 79(3), catalogue record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/asn,65092658); abstracts only). Wilkins & Griffiths argue that a truth-tracking "Milvian bridge" can be built for common-sense and scientific beliefs but not religious ones ([PhilSci-Archive 5314](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/5314); summary level). A critic of Clark & Barrett points out that the "natural" beliefs CSR finds are not the creedal beliefs of world religions ([PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/VANRBI), search summary). That objection cuts both ways: it weakens Reidian support for specific doctrines, and it weakens debunking of them.

**[Orthodox]** David Bentley Hart's classical theism holds that God is not a being among beings but the ground of being. On that view, explanations of why humans detect agents do not straightforwardly target the God of classical theology ([Wikipedia, *The Experience of God*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Experience_of_God), secondary). The obvious reply is that ordinary believers' God is much more like the agent CSR describes. The notes did not find a published source making that reply, so it is offered here as the natural counterpoint.

## The material record climbs a ladder from care for the dead to communal ritual

**[Archaeological]** Archaeology recovers behaviour, not belief. A useful ladder, which is the researchers' own framing rather than a published scheme, runs from least to most inferential:
1. symbolic capacity;
2. care for the dead;
3. imagined beings;
4. organised communal ritual;
5. specific beliefs.

Specific beliefs are never directly observable before writing.

### Burials appear by about 120,000 years ago, but burial is not afterlife belief

**Early *Homo sapiens*.** Deliberate burial is well attested at Skhul and Qafzeh in the Levant, with TL and ESR dates of roughly **120,000–90,000 years ago** ([Vandermeersch & Bar-Yosef 2019, *Paléo*](https://journals.openedition.org/paleo/4848); [Stringer et al. 1989, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/338756a0)). Qafzeh 11, an adolescent buried with fallow-deer antlers at the chest, is the strongest Middle Palaeolithic case for something like a grave offering ([Wikipedia, Qafzeh Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qafzeh_Cave), tertiary). A blog claim that the antlers were "saturated with imported red ochre" is unverified and should not be repeated. Ochre from the Qafzeh terrace, about 92,000 years old, appears selected for colour and spatially associated with burials, though the authors conceded it was more often yellow than red ([Hovers et al. 2003, *Current Anthropology*](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/375869)).

**Africa.** The earliest known burial in Africa is a child of about 2.5–3 years at Panga ya Saidi, Kenya, dated to **78,300 ± 4,100 years ago**. It lay in a deliberately dug pit, flexed, probably shrouded and with the head on a perishable support. There were no grave goods ([ANU record](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/earliest-known-human-burial-in-africa/); [CNRS news](https://news.cnrs.fr/Zqq)).

**Neanderthals.** At La Chapelle-aux-Saints, a 12-year re-excavation argued for an anthropogenic pit ([Rendu et al. 2014, CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/308990)). Dibble et al. replied that the evidence "supports other interpretations equally well" ([UOW repository](https://ro.uow.edu.au/smhpapers/2937)). At Shanidar, re-excavation found an articulated adult ("Shanidar Z"), about 70,000–60,000 years old, in a channel that may have been deliberately deepened ([Pomeroy et al. 2020, LJMU](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/11568/)). The famous "flower burial" has collapsed. At least some of the pollen clumps were probably brought in by nesting solitary bees ([Hunt et al. 2023, LJMU repository](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/21025/); [LJMU press release](https://www.ljmu.ac.uk/about-us/news/articles/2023/8/29/neanderthals)). The notes could not confirm whether that paper appeared in *L'Anthropologie* or the *Journal of Archaeological Science*. The mainstream position, against Gargett's sceptical pole, is that some Neanderthals were deliberately buried, usually without visible elaboration ([Pettitt 2002 PDF](https://arheo.ffzg.unizg.hr/ska/tekstovi/neanderthal_dead.pdf)).

***Homo naledi*.** The 2023 claims of burial and engraving drew strongly negative first-round reviews ([eLife RP 89106 reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106/reviews); [PaleoAnthropology critique](https://paleoanthropology.org/ojs/index.php/paleo/libraryFiles/downloadPublic/25)). A separate critique was titled "No scientific evidence that Homo naledi buried their dead and produced rock art" ([Martinón-Torres et al. 2024, Griffith](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/fe978e9b-8880-43a9-9929-8c22720457ae)). A 2025 revision drew a more favourable round. Co-author John Hawks reports that reviewers accept burial as a strong explanation ([Hawks](https://www.johnhawks.net/p/burials-by-homo-naledi), an interested party), but one reviewer still asks for independent teams to analyse the material ([eLife v2 reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106v2/reviews)). The burial claim is improving but unreplicated. No positive reassessment of the engravings was found.

**What burial shows.** The archaeological reading is cautious. A deliberate pit shows care and deliberate treatment of the corpse. Only grave goods and repeated, elaborated mortuary programmes begin to suggest beliefs about the dead's continued existence. Even those are compatible with grief, status display or social memory. Middle Palaeolithic burials are rare and scattered over tens of thousands of years, which fits occasional rather than normative practice.

### Symbols by about 75,000 years ago; imagined beings by about 51,000 years ago

**Symbols.** Pierced *Nassarius* shell beads appear at Taforalt, Morocco, about **82,000 years ago** ([Bouzouggar et al. 2007, PMC1891266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1891266)). Blombos Cave in South Africa has a cross-hatched ochre-crayon drawing about **73,000 years old** ([Henshilwood et al. 2018, CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/317318); [CNRS press](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/discovery-earliest-drawing)).

**Figurative art.** It is not a European invention. At Leang Karampuang, Sulawesi, a scene of three human-like figures with a wild pig has a minimum age of **51,200 years** ([Griffith news](https://news.griffith.edu.au/2024/07/04/cave-painting-in-indonesia-is-the-oldest-known-picture-story/); the paper is Oktaviana et al. 2024, *Nature*, DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched). The Hohlenstein-Stadel Lion Man, a lion-headed human figure in mammoth ivory, comes from a layer dated about **39,000–41,000 cal BP** ([Kind et al. 2014, *Quartär*](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/qu/article/view/78462/72446)). Chauvet's art falls mostly about 37,000–33,500 cal BP ([Quiles et al. 2016, USF repository](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/2440)).

**Interpretation.** Composite human-animal figures are the strongest Palaeolithic visual hint of beings beyond the ordinary. They are equally compatible with myth, narrative or totemic identity. The influential shamanistic/neuropsychological reading (Lewis-Williams & Dowson 1988) remains in open dispute ([Rock Art Research debate](https://rockartresearch.com/index.php/rock/article/view/134); [Akers et al. 2016, *Economic Botany*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12231-016-9342-2)).

**Neanderthal religion.** It is unknowable on present evidence. Rings of about 400 broken stalagmites, built with fire some 330 m inside Bruniquel Cave, date to **176,500 ± 2,100 years ago**. Their excavators say the "function of the structures remains conjectural" ([Jaubert et al. 2016, CARTA](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/308217)). The claim of 64,000-year-old Neanderthal cave art in Iberia is contested by a 44-author rebuttal on uranium-leaching grounds ([White et al. 2020, USF](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/5193)).

### Göbekli Tepe: special buildings inside a settlement, not "temple first"

**[Archaeological]** Göbekli Tepe's T-pillar enclosures in southeastern Turkey date from the Pre-Pottery Neolithic A. Wall plaster from Enclosure D gave about **9745–9314 cal BC** ([DAI blog, "How old is it?"](https://dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)). Klaus Schmidt's team read the site as a hunter-gatherer ritual centre without houses or water, built and used by congregants who carved pillars and feasted ([Dietrich et al. 2012, *Antiquity*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A1AA4FB20657599F859860D94CCD090E/S0003598X00047840a.pdf/role_of_cult_and_feasting_in_the_emergence_of_neolithic_communities_new_evidence_from_gobekli_tepe_southeastern_turkey.pdfResearch)). The slogan "first came the temple, then the city" is attributed to Schmidt in popular coverage, but the notes did not re-verify the attribution ([Smithsonian, not re-fetched](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/gobekli-tepe-the-worlds-first-temple-83613665/)).

Since 2015 the German Archaeological Institute (DAI) team has been revising all three pillars of that model, including the claim that the site had no water ([Clare 2020, DAI](https://publications.dainst.org/journals/efb/article/view/2596); full text returned an error, content from the abstract). Press reports from 2025–2026 describe rectangular structures "that could have served as dwellings". Necmi Karul now calls Göbekli Tepe "a settlement where different types of buildings were used at the same time, rather than simply a ritual center" ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/new-excavations-at-gobeklitepe-unearth-rectangular-structures-225641)). No peer-reviewed publication of the houses existed when the notes were compiled. At Karahan Tepe, Karul reports "public structures surrounded by houses" in the earliest phases ([Hürriyet](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/amp/new-findings-at-karahantepe-offer-clues-to-gobeklitepe-mystery-215066)).

**What survives the revision.** Large, labour-intensive, iconographically elaborate communal buildings were made by pre- or proto-agricultural communities. What does not survive is the standalone pilgrimage temple of mobile foragers, and with it a clean "religion, then sedentism, then farming" chain. **[Catholic]** For a sympathetic reader, the data still place sacred or communal architecture at the centre of the first villages. They fit "religion integral to the Neolithic transition" better than "religion as its cause".

**Çatalhöyük and the dead.** At Çatalhöyük, Hodder's Templeton-funded seminars (which included theologians) argued that religion was entangled with domestic life ("history houses", burial under floors) rather than set apart. A chapter by Whitehouse and colleagues proposed that "religious routinization" drove social complexity ([Cambridge Core chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/religion-at-work-in-a-neolithic-society/modes-of-religiosity-and-the-evolution-of-social-complexity-at-catalhoyuk/6328A5C9CA3D9ECC52770C6A20166E71)). Kenyon read Jericho's plastered skulls as venerated male ancestors ([UT Austin](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/ain-ghazal/the-plastered-skulls/)). Bonogofsky's osteology found women and children also modelled, which complicates the elder-male-ancestor reading ([Bonogofsky 2005 abstract](https://ocf.berkeley.edu/~kan/abstracts/bonogofsky.html)).

### Phylogenies put animism at the root, which bears on the "primordial monotheism" thesis

**[Cognitive/evolutionary]** Peoples, Duda & Marlowe mapped seven religious traits onto a time-calibrated supertree of **33 hunter-gatherer societies** (28 from the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample plus 5). In their words, "the oldest trait of religion, present in the most recent common ancestor of present-day hunter-gatherers, was animism". Belief in an afterlife followed, then shamanism and ancestor worship. "Ancestor spirits or high gods who are active in human affairs were absent in early humans." Yet "the trait 'high gods' stands apart, suggesting that belief in a single creator deity can emerge in a society regardless of other aspects of its religion" ([Europe PMC full text, PMC4958132](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4958132/fullTextXML)).

**Two limits matter.** First, the method reaches only to the common ancestor of the sampled *living* foragers, who have been influenced by farmers and missionaries. It is not a window on 100,000-year-old religion. Second, the notes did not verify from the figures whether *non-active* high gods are reconstructed as absent at the root. Only *active* high gods are stated to be absent.

## The first states made gods into landlords, kings and information systems

**[Historical-critical]** Across independent civilisations, state formation coincided with several shared features:
- temple institutions that were also economic centres;
- royal ideologies placing the ruler between cosmos and society;
- state divination;
- human sacrifice, episodic or institutional;
- canonical ritual texts.

Trigger's seven-civilisation comparison concluded that none of these societies separated "religion" as a distinct sphere (bibliographic, not fetched). Many details in this era rest on standard monographs the notes did not reopen, so the strongest claims below lean on web-verified items.

### Mesopotamia and Egypt

**Mesopotamia.** Writing emerged in Late Uruk Eanna largely as bookkeeping for institutional households. The commonly cited 85–90% share of administrative tablets is Englund's estimate, and the notes say it still needs checking (open data at the [Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative](https://cdli.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/)). The old "temple-state" model, in which temples owned all land, has been revised: the temple was one large household among several ([Foster 1981, *JESHO*](https://doi.org/10.1163/156852081X00031), bibliographic, not fetched). Only a few Mesopotamian kings were deified, notably Naram-Sin and Shulgi ([Brisch ed. 2008, open access](https://isac.uchicago.edu/research/publications/ois/ois-4-religion-and-power-divine-kingship-ancient-world-and-beyond), bibliographic). At Ur's Royal Cemetery (about 2600–2450 BCE), CT scans of two attendants' skulls showed perimortem cranial trauma, which contradicts Woolley's peaceful "poison cup" theory ([Baadsgaard et al. 2011, *Antiquity*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/E44F1731C922EF9FF43E0B5D2DF67AC1)). The notes flag an unresolved discrepancy over whether the trauma came from a sharp instrument or blunt force.

**Flood stories.** The Atrahasis and Gilgamesh XI floods parallel Genesis 6–9 closely enough that nearly all scholars posit literary dependence or a shared tradition ([Frymer-Kensky 1977](https://doi.org/10.2307/3209526), bibliographic). **[Historical-critical]** On the critical reading, Genesis reworks Mesopotamian tradition. **[Catholic]**/**[Orthodox]** On the confessional reading, Genesis deliberately "demythologises" it: one God acting for moral reasons replaces gods annoyed by human noise. The two readings are not mutually exclusive.

**Egypt.** Egypt shows the most integrated religion-state of the period, built around divine kingship and *ma'at* (order, truth, justice). First Dynasty royal tombs at Abydos are ringed by subsidiary graves, about 200 around Djer's. Many scholars read these as retainer sacrifice, though direct evidence of cause of death is limited ([ANE Today](https://anetoday.org/human-sacrifice-near-east/)). Akhenaten's exclusive Aten cult (about 1353–1336 BCE) was reversed after his death. Whether it was monotheism, henotheism or royal monolatry is debated. Jan Assmann's "Mosaic distinction" between true and false religion is explicitly "mnemohistory", a claim about memory, not historical dependence (all bibliographic, not fetched).

### China: oracle bones, mass sacrifice and the Mandate of Heaven

**Divination and sacrifice.** The late Shang state at Anyang governed partly through pyromantic divination addressed to royal ancestors and to Di ([Keightley 1978, *History of Religions*](https://doi.org/10.1086/462792), bibliographic). Christina Cheung estimates that **more than 13,000 people** were sacrificed at Yinxu over about 200 years, often Qiang captives. That figure is an extrapolated estimate ([Sapiens](https://www.sapiens.org/archaeology/chinese-oracle-bones-history/); [Archaeology magazine](https://www.archaeology.org/news/5664-170616-china-sacrificial-victims)). Isotopes from 68 victims show non-local childhood diets but local diets before death, which suggests captives were held for years. The authors note there is no direct evidence of forced labour ([Cheung et al. 2017, *JAA*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaa.2017.05.006)).

**The Mandate of Heaven.** The Zhou conquest (about 1046 BCE) brought *Tianming*, the Mandate of Heaven: Heaven grants rule to the virtuous and withdraws it from the corrupt (Shaughnessy 1999, bibliographic). This makes divine favour conditional on moral conduct, a useful comparison with Israel's prophetic critique of kings, with no historical connection implied. Puett cautions against a simple "magical Shang, ethical Zhou" story (bibliographic).

### India, the Americas and the sacrifice–stratification link

**The Indus.** The Indus case is a caution against over-reading. Marshall's 1931 "proto-Shiva" reading of the Pashupati seal held "for about two generations". Critics have since read the figure as female, as a buffalo-man, or as unconnected to anything Vedic, and Samuel notes that the competing readings "cannot all be right" ([Wikipedia, Pashupati seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupati_seal), secondary aggregator). Whether the Indus signs even encode language is disputed ([Rao et al. 2009, *Science*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1170391), bibliographic). Claims that "Shiva worship is 5,000 years old" are largely unsupported. The Rigveda's core books are usually dated about 1500–1200 BCE, anchored in part by the Mitanni treaty's invocation of Mitra, Varuṇa and Indra ([Narasimhan et al. 2019 on Steppe ancestry](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat7487), bibliographic; the link to language spread is itself an inference).

**The Americas.** New World evidence shows independent convergence on sacred kingship and state sacrifice, though the Aztec and Inca cases are 14th–16th century CE and "archaic" only by type. INAH's excavation of the Huei Tzompantli skull rack in Tenochtitlan had yielded **more than 600 skulls by 2020**, including women and children. That confirms large-scale sacrifice, though not the "tens of thousands" of Spanish accounts ([Smithsonian, 14 Dec 2020](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/new-find-brings-skulls-discovered-aztec-tower-over-600-180976543/)). Hair from the Llullaillaco children of the Inca *capacocha* shows coca and alcohol intake escalating in the months before death. The authors raise questions of consent and coercion rather than settling them ([Wilson et al. 2013, *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1305117110)).

**Sacrifice and stratification.** **[Cognitive/evolutionary]** Watts et al. analysed 93 Austronesian cultures. Ritual human sacrifice occurred in **67% of highly stratified, 37% of moderately stratified and 25% of egalitarian** societies, and phylogenetic models suggested sacrifice helped societies gain and keep stratification, the "social control hypothesis" ([Watts et al. 2016, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27042932/); [MPI-SHH press release](https://www.shh.mpg.de/166222/humansacrifices)). The statistician Christian Robert noted on his blog (not peer-reviewed) that the key Bayes factor of **3.78** is "not overwhelming" ([R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2016/05/reversible-chainsaw-massacre/amp/)). The notes found no published rebuttal.

### Canaan and the Israelite starting point

**[Historical-critical]** The Ugaritic tablets (14th–13th century BCE) give a West Semitic pantheon of El, Athirat/Asherah, Baal and Anat. Mark S. Smith's "convergence and differentiation" model holds that Yahweh absorbed El's and Baal's traits, while Baal and Asherah were later rejected (Smith 2002, bibliographic, not fetched). The key epigraphy is checked. Kuntillet ʿAjrud in Sinai (about 800 BCE) has inscriptions reading "I bless you by Yahweh of Samaria and by his asherah" and "Yahweh of Teman and his asherah" ([Wikipedia, Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuntillet_Ajrud_inscriptions), secondary aggregator). Whether "his asherah" is the goddess (Dever), a cult pole (Blum: "the ashera of Yahweh"), or a grammatical misreading is unresolved. The famous drawings are widely read as Bes figures that may be unrelated to the text (same source). A tomb inscription from Khirbet el-Qom offers a parallel. **[Catholic]**/**[Orthodox]** A traditional reader can read this epigraphy as the popular syncretism the prophets condemned, and frame Yahweh's taking of El's titles as the true God claiming attributes falsely ascribed to others. That is a theological framing, not a historical finding. Both readings fit the inscriptions.

## The Axial Age shrinks from a unique "age" to a recurring threshold

### The strong thesis fails its best test

**[Sociological]** Karl Jaspers (1949) proposed that between about 800 and 200 BCE China, India, Iran, Israel and Greece independently broke through to reflexive, transcendent, ethical-universal thought (bibliographic, not fetched). Eisenstadt, Bellah and Joas built a research programme on it (bibliographic). The strongest empirical test, using the Seshat Global History Databank, found that the proposed axial traits "appeared across Afro-Eurasia hundreds, and in some cases thousands, of years prior to the proposed Axial Age". That "undercut[s] the notion of a specific 'age' of axiality" ([Mullins et al. 2018, *ASR*](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122418772567); [Exeter ORE](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/36086)). The follow-up Seshat volume argues that axiality "emerged time and again whenever societies reached a certain threshold of scale". Co-editor Jenny Reddish: "We couldn't find any consistent Axial Age that was confined to those five societies" ([Complexity Science Hub](https://csh.ac.at/news/new-book-our-deep-past-seen-through-big-data-eyes/)).

**Two caveats.** Seshat codes proxies such as moralising gods and constraints on rulers. Bellah's core claim about "theoretic culture" (second-order thought) is harder to code, so defenders can say Seshat tested a caricature. The notes found no published rebuttal making this case, so it remains the obvious counterargument rather than a documented one. **[Catholic]** Iain Provan's *Convenient Myths* (2013) rejects the Axial Age from a Christian-scholarly angle: lumping the cases together erases Israel's distinctiveness (bibliographic, not fetched). Secular data science and a theologically minded critic thus reject the strong form for opposite reasons.

**Proposed causes.** None has decisive support. Baumard et al. argued that affluence, measured as energy capture, best predicts ascetic, moralising doctrines ([Yale Daily News summary](https://yaledailynews.com/blog/2015/01/20/with-increased-wealth-religions-take-a-moral-stand); [bibliographic entry](https://stafforini.com/works/baumard-2015-increased-affluence-explains/)). Turchin called its all-or-nothing coding a "Procrustean bed" and Morris's Iron Age energy estimates "much shakier than the paper suggests" ([Turchin blog](https://peterturchin.com/does-affluence-lead-to-asceticism-part-i/)). Affluence, empire, warfare, coinage and literacy all rise with scale, so they are hard to separate.

### Israel's monotheism emerged gradually, or was gradually enforced

**[Historical-critical]** The dominant critical model runs in three steps:
1. early Yahwism within a Canaanite divine world;
2. a "Yahweh-alone" monolatry promoted by prophetic and Deuteronomistic circles in the late 8th–7th century;
3. explicit denials of other gods in Second Isaiah, about the 540s BCE ("besides me there is no god", Isa 44:6; primary text).

Kuntillet ʿAjrud (above) and the 5th-century Elephantine papyri, which record a temple of YHW in Egypt alongside oaths involving other deities (Porten & Yardeni, bibliographic, not fetched; the notes did not verify the oath wording), show that *practised* Judaean religion was not uniformly monotheistic even after the exile.

**[Catholic]**/**[Orthodox]** The traditional view places covenantal exclusivism with Moses (Exod 20:2–3; Deut 6:4) and reads later polytheistic practice as apostasy, not as an earlier stage. Kitchen (2003) and Hoffmeier (1997) defend substantial historicity (bibliographic, not fetched). Catholic teaching (*Dei Verbum* 12; the Pontifical Biblical Commission's 1993 document) accepts historical-critical method and reads the canon as progressive revelation. In that framework, gradual historical emergence is theologically compatible with divine pedagogy (bibliographic, not fetched). The decisive point is that **the archaeology documents practice, not the earliest normative ideal**, so it cannot by itself settle whether polytheistic practice was the original state or a departure. Scholars such as MacDonald, Hayman, Hurtado and Bauckham argue that "monotheism" is itself an anachronistic Enlightenment category. They define the biblical idea by exclusive worship or divine identity rather than by counting gods ([Kok 2015, Bible & Interpretation](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/opeds/2015/07/kok398030); MacDonald and Hayman bibliographic). This is a defensible scholarly position, not merely apologetic.

**The archaeology of early Israel.** It attests Israel and its kings, not exclusive worship. The Merneptah Stele (about 1208 BCE) names "Israel" (bibliographic, standard). The Tel Dan stele almost certainly reads "House of David" ([Wikipedia, Tel Dan stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Dan_stele), secondary). Khirbet Qeiyafa's olive pits date to about 1000 BCE, but its attribution to David's kingdom is contested ([Garfinkel et al. 2015, HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/king-davids-city-at-khirbet-qeiyafa-results-of-the-second-radioca/)). The 2023 Mount Ebal "curse tablet", claimed to bear a Late Bronze Age "YHW", has been rejected in most published specialist responses. Maeir & Rollston say the images "fail to demonstrate any discernible letters". Mazar proposes a fishing-net sinker. The lead's Lavrion source "is inconclusive" for dating ([Times of Israel, 7 Dec 2023](https://www.timesofisrael.com/new-academic-articles-heap-fresh-doubt-on-mount-ebal-curse-tablet-interpretation/); [Stripling et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40494-023-00920-9); [Haughwout 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40494-023-01130-z)). Stripling maintains "there is writing on the tablet" (same Times of Israel report). An argument for early Mosaic Yahwism should not rest on it.

**Persian influence.** It is plausible but unprovable. Zarathustra's date ranges from about 1500–1000 BCE (on linguistic grounds) to the traditional 6th century ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/zoroaster), tertiary). Most Zoroastrian texts describing resurrection and final judgment in detail are Sasanian or later, so the direction of borrowing is disputed. Even Bart Ehrman reports becoming "less sure" of influence on resurrection belief ([Ehrman Blog](https://ehrmanblog.org/?p=16503)).

### India, China and Greece took different paths

**India.** The Buddha's death date has slipped later. Heinz Bechert wrote that the "general agreement among scholars that Buddha died within a few years of 480 B.C." "has become a thing of the past". Most contributors to his symposium favoured a considerably later date, without agreeing how much later ([LMU ePub](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/21122/1/21122.pdf)).

**China.** In China, Mozi's explicitly theistic, punishing Heaven is the clearest "Big God", yet Mohism died out (primary text, bibliographic).

**Greece.** Xenophanes' "one god, greatest among gods and men" has often been read as monotheism. The plural "gods" and Homeric usage suggest "one greatest god", and the remarks may be "best read as an expression of a traditional Greek piety" ([SEP, Xenophanes](https://plato.stanford.edu/ENTRIES/xenophanes/)). Plato coined *theologia* (*Republic* 379a; primary text).

**[Historical-critical]** The contrast is real. Israel moved from cultic exclusivism toward ontological statements. Greece moved toward philosophical monotheising while civic polytheism continued. The two streams meet in Philo and the Church Fathers.

## Big Gods: which came first depends on how you code what you don't know

### The thesis is co-evolutionary, not "gods first"

**[Cognitive/evolutionary]** Norenzayan and colleagues argue that a package of "increasingly potent, moralizing, supernatural agents, credible displays of faith, and other psychologically active elements" promoted large-scale cooperation with co-religionists and helped groups win in competition. They describe large-scale cooperation and prosocial religion as "importantly linked and mutually energizing" ([Norenzayan et al. 2016, *BBS*, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26785995)). A finding that complexity came first refutes the strong reading that moralising gods were a *prerequisite* for large societies. It does not refute the reciprocal thesis.

### Individual-level games favour watchful, punishing gods over moral ones

In eight field sites (n = 591), people who rated their god as more punitive and more knowing allotted more coins to distant co-religionist strangers in an anonymous, cheatable dice game ([Purzycki et al. 2016, *Nature*, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26863190)). The often-quoted factors of 4.8 and 5.3 come second-hand and need checking. So does the observation that allocations stayed below a fair split even at maximum punitiveness ([Coyne 2016](https://whyevolutionistrue.com/2016/06/08/belief-in-moralistic-gods-makes-people-generous-towards-coreligionists/), secondary). A 15-society extension (N = 2,228) found that monitoring, punishing gods predicted less local favouritism and more sharing with distant co-religionists ([Lang et al. 2019, *Proc R Soc B*](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1098/rspb.2019.0202)). A preregistered 2023 analysis then separated *moral* content from punishment. It found "gods' moral concerns do not play a direct, cross-culturally reliable role in motivating cooperative behaviour" ([Bendixen et al. 2023, *Evolutionary Human Sciences*](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37587943)). All these studies are within-site correlations, none experimental with respect to belief.

### Phylogenies and atlases point to broad supernatural punishment

**Austronesia.** In 96 Austronesian cultures, *broad* supernatural punishment from any agent tended to precede political complexity, while moralising high gods followed it ([Watts et al. 2015, PMC4375858](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4375858/)). Only **6 cultures** had moralising high gods. None of the directional Bayes factors exceeds about 5 against independence, and against the unconstrained dependent model they are all below 2 (same source).

**The Ethnographic Atlas.** Belief in moralising high gods was more likely in poorer environments and in politically complex, herding societies, and spatial proximity was the strongest predictor, a sign of diffusion. The "91% accuracy" is an out-of-sample AUC, not percent correctly classified ([Botero et al. 2014, PMC4250141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4250141/)). The notes' own tally of the Atlas "high gods" variable finds **198 of 775 coded societies (25.5%)** with an active, moralising high god ([D-PLACE EA data](https://github.com/D-PLACE/dplace-dataset-ea/blob/master/cldf/data.csv)).

**The coding critique.** Lightner, Bendixen & Purzycki's paper is titled **"Moralistic supernatural punishment is probably not associated with social complexity"** (*Evolution and Human Behavior* 44(6): 555–565; online 2022). It argues that the standard "high god" variable requires a creator or director of the universe. That requirement produces false negatives for moralistic gods in small-scale societies, more often at low complexity, which yields "a spurious positive association". It adds that "ethnographic evidence supports the prevalence of moralizing gods among small-scale societies" ([OpenAlex abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2022.10.006)).

### The Seshat dispute: claim, critique, retraction, retake

**The 2019 paper.** Whitehouse et al. claimed in *Nature* that moralising gods "follow—rather than precede—large increases in social complexity", appearing only after "megasocieties" of about a million people ([Whitehouse et al. 2019, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30894750)).

**The critique.** Beheim and 14 co-authors showed that "61% of Seshat data points on moralizing gods are missing values" that had been recoded as absences. Analysing only known data or imputing reversed the finding ([PsyArXiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/jwa2n)). Their code shows that 490 of the 502 zeros in the outcome were recoded missing values, and that only 1 of 30 regions had a known absence before a presence ([babeheim reanalysis repo](https://github.com/babeheim/moralizing-gods-reanalysis)). The critique ran in *Nature* as a Matters Arising titled "Treatment of missing data determined conclusions regarding moralizing gods" ([Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34234331)); the preprint's title had said "determines".

**The retraction.** The Letter was retracted on 7 July 2021 ([Retraction Note DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03656-3)). As quoted by Retraction Watch, the authors accepted "that we should have labelled moralizing gods as 'absent' or 'inferred absent' rather than 'unknown'", but held that "our original conclusions are still strongly supported" ([Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/07/07/critique-topples-nature-paper-on-belief-in-gods/), secondary; the note itself was not fetched). The two sides frame the error differently: a wrong label on true absences (the authors) or unknowns treated as absences (the critics).

**The retake.** The corrected analysis appeared **in *Religion, Brain & Behavior* 13(2): 124–166, online 29 June 2022 and in the 2023 volume, not in *Nature* or any cognitive-science journal**. It reports that "the largest increases in social complexity do indeed precede Big Gods" and that Big Gods "did not contribute to the evolution of sociopolitical complexity" ([OpenAlex abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/2153599x.2022.2074085)). A companion target article attributes both trends to "intergroup warfare, supported by resource availability" ([Turchin et al. 2022, RBB](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/2153599x.2022.2065345)). The commentators were not persuaded. Purzycki, Bendixen & Lightner argue that each step of the workflow "makes it impossible to test the hypothesis that its authors claim they are testing" ([Crossref listing](https://api.crossref.org/works?filter=issn:2153-599X)).

**The historians' critique.** Historians recoded 12 Middle Yellow River polities with expert data from the Database of Religious History. Seshat agreed with the experts in "only under a third of cases". At publication, expert vetting of both variable sets was credited in only 13% of polities ([Slingerland et al. 2020, LSE PDF](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/107612/1/Historians_Respond_to_Whitehouse_et_al_complete.pdf)).

**The net result.** The robust finding of this whole episode is methodological. With Seshat's coverage, "which came first" turns almost entirely on how missing data are treated. Moralising gods first appear in Seshat together with writing, so any such gods in non-literate societies are structurally invisible. That biases Seshat tests toward "complexity first". **Neither side's causal conclusion is strongly identified.**

## Early Christianity: secure history at the core, divided readings beyond it

### Jesus existed and was crucified; what followed is where lenses split

**[Historical-critical]** Nearly all specialists hold that Jesus of Nazareth existed, taught in Galilee and was crucified under Pontius Pilate. Tacitus (about 116 CE) records that "Christus... suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus" ([*Annals* 15.44, Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0078:book=15:chapter=44)). Josephus' *Testimonium Flavianum* is widely judged partly authentic, and "almost all scholars today accept that the Testimonium is at least partially a Christian interpolation" ([ProQuest abstract](https://www.proquest.com/docview/220270817); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus_on_Jesus), tertiary). Feldman's 1980 survey, a dated, second-hand data point, found opinion spread from 4 scholars who judged it entirely genuine to 13 who judged it a total interpolation ([Kirby](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/testimonium.html)). There are minority positions at both ends. T. C. Schmidt (Oxford UP, 2025) argues it is "substantially authentic" ([Pearse review](https://www.roger-pearse.com/weblog/2025/05/)). Some recent work argues that Josephus wrote none of it ([Hrčak](https://hrcak.srce.hr/347774)).

**Mythicism.** Bart Ehrman argues that virtually no credentialed specialist doubts Jesus existed, citing Paul's personal acquaintance with Peter and with Jesus' brother James ([The Tech, 2012](https://thetech.com/2012/04/06/ehrman-v132-n16)). Richard Carrier's Bayesian case for a celestial figure later historicised is the leading minority position ([Vridar](https://vridar.org/2012/04/20/richard-carriers-on-bart-ehrmans-did-jesus-exist/), partisan).

**[Historical-critical]** On the resurrection, most historians affirm only that some followers, and Paul, sincerely believed soon after the crucifixion that they had seen Jesus alive (1 Cor 15:3–8). The cause of that belief lies outside historical method (Ehrman 2014, bibliographic). **[Catholic]**/**[Orthodox]**-congenial: N. T. Wright argues that the empty tomb plus the appearances is the best historical explanation (bibliographic, not fetched). Dale Allison's 2021 study is rigorous and fair in the *Church Times* reviewer's judgement, and concludes that the evidence fits more than one interpretation ([Church Times review](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2021/23-april/books-arts/book-reviews/the-resurrection-of-jesus-apologetics-polemics-history-by-dale-c-allison-jr); [PTS summary](https://ptsem.edu/?p=1196)). The fair summary: the *belief* is historically secure and very early, and the inference to a miracle is a philosophical or theological step.

### Jesus was worshipped early; what that meant took three centuries to define

**[Historical-critical]** Many specialists now accept that Jesus received cultic devotion within the first two decades, in Jewish-Christian circles. Hurtado argues that worshipping Jesus alongside the God of Israel was a "mutation" in Jewish monotheism, and that no Jewish intermediary figure "became the object of a cult". Bauckham argues that early Christians placed Jesus within the unique divine identity (both via [Kok 2015](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/opeds/2015/07/kok398030)). That cuts against Bousset's model of a Gentile "Kyrios cult" (bibliographic). Ehrman's gradualist alternative moves from "exaltation" to "incarnation" Christology ([Kok 2015](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/opeds/2015/07/kok398030)). His critics reply that his question presupposes an evolutionary model ([Zondervan Academic excerpt](https://zondervanacademic.com/blog/how-god-became-jesus-bart-ehrman-high-christology-excerpt/)). Pliny's report (about 112 CE) that Christians sang "a hymn to Christ as to a god" is the earliest pagan corroboration of such worship (primary text, URL not fetched). **[Catholic]**/**[Orthodox]** On a traditional reading, Nicaea (325) *articulated* a devotional pattern already present in the 30s–50s. The cautious historical claim is narrower: the *practice* was early, while the *ontological* clarification (*homoousios*) took three centuries and was contested.

### A sociologist's arithmetic, and a theologian's reading of it

**[Sociological]** No ancient source counted Christians. Rodney Stark's model, about 40% growth per decade (about 3.4% a year) from roughly 1,000 Christians in 40 CE to about 6 million by 300, is plausible arithmetic that needs no miracle ([Psephizo](https://www.psephizo.com/life-ministry/how-fast-should-the-church-grow/); [Ehrman Blog](https://ehrmanblog.org/the-rate-of-christian-growth/)). Its implications are striking. Keith Hopkins drew from the same curve roughly 7,000 Christians around 100 CE and 200,000 around 200 CE ([Ehrman Blog](https://ehrmanblog.org/?p=11431)). The starting figure is a guess and growth was surely not steady. Constantine's conversion (312) came when Christians were a minority, about 10% on most models (Barnes 1981, bibliographic). **[Catholic]**/**[Orthodox]** A traditional reader can accept the arithmetic and still read steady growth under persecution as providential, in the spirit of Tertullian's "the blood of Christians is seed". These are two levels of explanation, not competing data.

### Development, corruption or living Tradition

**[Catholic]** Newman's *Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine* (1845/1878) gives seven "Notes" of genuine development, from Preservation of Type to Chronic Vigour. It holds that "a corruption is a development in that very stage in which it ceases to illustrate, and begins to disturb" earlier acquisitions ([Newman Reader, ch. 5](https://www.newmanreader.org/works/development/chapter5.html)). **[Orthodox]** Florovsky's "neo-patristic synthesis" (1936) seeks a creative re-reception of the Fathers rather than "development". It was never rigorously defined, and critics have called its legacy anti-modern ([Zizioulas Foundation glossary](https://www.zizioulas.org/library/glossary/neopatristic-synthesis); [*Ortodoksia*](https://journal.fi/ortodoksia/article/view/147725)). **[Historical-critical]** Harnack's "Hellenisation" thesis, that dogma was "a work of the Greek spirit on the soil of the Gospel", is now a minority view among patristic scholars in its strong form (Pelikan and Gavrilyuk, bibliographic, not fetched). Newman and Florovsky both reject Harnack. Their real disagreement concerns *criteria*, namely whether the Roman see guarantees development, and specific dogmas after 1054.

### Canon, bishops and the Roman question

**Canon.** The 27-book New Testament list first appears exactly in Athanasius' 39th Festal Letter (367). The same letter lists the Didache and the Shepherd as books "appointed by the Fathers to be read" but not canonical ([New Advent, NPNF²](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2806039.htm)). The Muratorian fragment's date is genuinely disputed. Ferguson and Metzger defend the late second century; Sundberg and Hahneman argue for the fourth ([Hill 1995, *WTJ*](https://gospelstudies.org.uk/earlychurch/pdf/fragment_hill.pdf); [Themelios review](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/review/the-muratorian-fragment-and-the-development-of-the-canon-oxford-theological-monographs/)).

**Bishops.** **[Historical-critical]** Ignatius (about 110) attests one bishop per city in Asia Minor. Rome probably had collegial presbyteral leadership until about the mid-second century. That is Lampe's view, and the Catholic historian Eamon Duffy is widely reported to accept it while defending the antiquity of Roman pre-eminence (both bibliographic, not fetched).

**Rome.** **[Orthodox]** Afanasiev's "eucharistic ecclesiology" reads Rome's early priority as "presiding in love", not jurisdiction ([OrthodoxWiki](https://en.orthodoxwiki.org/Nicholas_Afanasiev)). **[Catholic]** The Catholic case rests on Matthew 16:18, Irenaeus' *potentiorem principalitatem*, and Chalcedon's acclamation "Peter has spoken through Leo" (primary texts; Siecienski 2017 is the recommended even-handed survey, bibliographic). The historical record establishes Rome's early and unique prestige. Whether this was jurisdiction *by divine right* is a theological judgement the sources do not settle.

### 1054 was a symbol; 1204 made the schism popular

**[Historical-critical]** Historians now treat 1054 as a symbolic marker within a gradual estrangement (about 800–1204), whose strands included:
- the filioque;
- papal claims;
- unleavened bread;
- Carolingian-era political drift.

The Fourth Crusade's sack of Constantinople in 1204 is widely seen as the event that turned estrangement into popular schism (Chadwick 2003, Siecienski 2010 and Kolbaba 2000, bibliographic, not fetched). The 1439 union of Florence was rejected in the East. Paul VI and Athenagoras I committed the 1054 excommunications "to oblivion" in 1965, while stating that the gesture "is not sufficient to end both old and more recent differences" ([Vatican, Common Declaration](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/speeches/1965/documents/hf_p-vi_spe_19651207_common-declaration.html)). At the declaration's 60th anniversary, Cardinal Koch said it ushered in an "ecclesiology of sister Churches" ([Vatican News, January 2026](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/vatican-city/news/2026-01/lifting-anathemas-60-years-paul-vi-anathagoras-koch-getcha.html)). The 2007 Ravenna document records agreement that Rome held first place (*taxis*) among the patriarchal sees in the first millennium. It leaves open how that primacy should be exercised, and is "not an official declaration" of either Church ([Dicastery for Promoting Christian Unity PDF](https://www.christianunity.va/content/unitacristiani/en/dialoghi/sezione-orientale/chiese-ortodosse-di-tradizione-bizantina/commissione-mista-internazionale-per-il-dialogo-teologico-tra-la/documenti-di-dialogo/testo-in-inglese.pdf)).

**[Catholic]** The schism is a wound within one Church, and Florence shows that union on first-millennium terms is possible. **[Orthodox]** Rome departed from conciliar ecclesiology, and Florence was signed under Ottoman duress and rightly rejected. **[Historical-critical]** Politics mattered as much as dogma.

## Secularisation holds in rich democracies but not across the world

**[Sociological]** The classic secularisation thesis runs from Weber through the early Berger to Bruce. Its critics include the supply-side theorists (Stark and Iannaccone), the later Berger, who in 1999 called his earlier claim essentially mistaken while excepting Europe, and Casanova, who separates the differentiation of social spheres, the decline of belief and the privatisation of religion. All of these are bibliographic, not fetched, and Berger's exact wording should be checked. The strongest recent empirical work removes the United States as the main counterexample. Voas & Chaves show decades of American decline driven by cohort replacement, with each generation less religious than the last, rather than by individuals losing faith ([Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1122832); [LSE USAPP](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/usappblog/2016/09/05/religion-is-in-decline-in-the-west-and-america-is-no-exception)).

### Global data

Pew's 2025 report gives the global figures for 2010–2020 ([Pew 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/09/how-the-global-religious-landscape-changed-from-2010-to-2020/)):

| Measure | 2020 figure | Change since 2010 |
|---|---|---|
| Christians | **2.3 billion**, 28.8% | −1.8 points of share |
| Muslims | 25.6% | +1.8 points; +347 million, more than all other groups combined |
| Religiously unaffiliated | **1.9 billion**, 24.2% | up from 23.3% |
| People with any religion | **75.8%** of the world | down from 76.7% |
| Switching, adults 18–54 | about **3.2 leavers per joiner** | |

That pattern fits Norris & Inglehart's existential-security thesis: switching runs secular, while the religious share holds up through fertility in poorer, more religious populations. It is harder to square with a pure supply-side model. In 2020, 30.7% of the world's Christians lived in sub-Saharan Africa and 22.3% in Europe (same source).

Pew's own note warns that its 2015 projection of a *falling* unaffiliated share to 2050 used a 2010 baseline that has since been revised, from 16% to 23.3% after China was re-measured ([Pew 2015 page note](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2015/04/02/religious-projections-2010-2050)).

### Catholic and Orthodox trends

**Catholics.** There were **1,405,454,000** Catholics (17.8% of humanity) at mid-2023. Africa accounted for 8.3 million of the 15.9 million annual increase. Priests fell to 406,996 and major seminarians to 106,495, while permanent deacons rose ([Agenzia Fides 2025](https://www.fides.org/en/news/76919-VATICAN_Catholic_Church_Statistics_2025)). US Catholic weekly Mass attendance, on CARA's modelled estimate, was back to about **24%**, the pre-pandemic level ([CNA, Feb 2025](https://catholicnewsagency.com/amp/news/262131/cara-mass-attendance-ticks-back-up-nationally-after-pandemic-falter-data-suggests)).

**Orthodox.** There are nearly 260 million Orthodox Christians, a falling share of world Christianity (20% in 1910, 12% today). Russia shows high identity and low practice (**6%** weekly attendance), while Ethiopia shows the reverse (**78%**) ([Pew 2017](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/11/08/orthodox-christianity-in-the-21st-century/)). Moscow broke communion with Constantinople over Ukraine on 15 October 2018 ([AsiaNews](https://www.asianews.it/news-en/Moscow-breaks-communion-with-Constantinople-45217.html)).

### Revival claims need graded evidence

**The US plateau: strong evidence.** The US Christian share fell from 78% (2007) to 62% (2023–24), but has hovered between 60% and 64% since 2019. The youngest adults are about as Christian as people born in the 1990s, which is a cohort plateau, not a revival ([Pew RLS, N = 36,908](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/02/26/decline-of-christianity-in-the-us-has-slowed-may-have-leveled-off/)).

**The UK "Quiet Revival": discredited pending a rerun.** The Bible Society report claimed monthly churchgoing rose from 8% to 12%, and from 4% to 16% among 18–24-year-olds. Before any retraction, critics noted the opt-in panel's vulnerability to "bogus respondents" and conflicts with probability surveys ([Christian Today](https://www.christiantoday.com/news/the-quiet-non-revival); [Religion Media Centre](https://religionmediacentre.org.uk/news/quiet-revival-data-is-flawed-nonetheless-the-story-is-true/)). In **March 2026**, YouGov reported finding "a number of respondents who we can now identify as fraudulent", accepted "full responsibility" and apologised, and said the survey would be rerun. The Bible Society maintains that other surveys still show greater engagement among young adults ([Christian Post, 28 Mar 2026](http://www.christianpost.com/news/yougov-retracts-bible-societys-quiet-revival-study.html)).

**US conversions to Orthodoxy: plausible but weakly measured.** Pew data cited by the BBC show US Orthodox Christians are 64% male, up from 46% in 2007, but the subsamples are small and the true increase is "hard to gauge" ([Baylor Religion Watch](https://religionwatch.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/eastern-orthodoxy-religion-young-men)). Projections of 69–115% growth by 2040 come from researchers at an Orthodox college ([Religion Unplugged](https://religionunplugged.com/news/2025/11/26/on-religion-the-flood-of-converts-reshaping-american-orthodoxy-part-2)). Conversions were already common in 2008, when 51% of OCA members were adult converts ([Christian Century](https://www.christiancentury.org/article/2008-12/us-orthodox-churches-have-more-converts-expected-study-says)).

**Weber's work ethic.** Becker & Woessmann's study of 452 Prussian counties attributes most of the Protestant economic advantage to literacy from Bible reading, not to Weber's work ethic ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/oupqjecon/v_3a124_3ay_3a2009_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a531-596..htm)).

## The theological lenses deserve their own hearing, and their own audit

### The classic secular origin theories fell to a Catholic anthropologist's critique

**[Sociological]** The 19th- and early-20th-century theories were all theories of *origin* resting on evolutionary stages:
- Tylor's "minimum definition of Religion, the belief in Spiritual Beings";
- Frazer's movement "from magic through religion to science", hedged with "perhaps";
- Durkheim's sacred things uniting adherents "into one single moral community called a Church";
- Freud's claim that "the beginnings of religion, ethics, society, and art meet in the Oedipus complex";
- Marx's "opium of the people", whose usually dropped clause calls religious suffering "a protest against real suffering".

Sources: [Tylor](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70458/pg70458.txt); [Frazer](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3623/pg3623.txt); [Durkheim](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41360/pg41360.txt); [Freud](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41214/pg41214.txt); [Marx](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1843/critique-hpr/intro.htm).

E. E. Evans-Pritchard, a Catholic convert, dismantled the genre in 1965. Of Tylor's animism he wrote that it "has the quality of a just-so story like 'how the leopard got his spots'... there is no evidence that they did". He diagnosed an introspective "'if I were a horse', fallacy", and ended: "We seem always to have come out by the same door as we went in" ([Evans-Pritchard 1965, Monoskop scan](https://www.monoskop.org/images/e/e1/Evans_Pritchard_E_E_Theories_of_Primitive_Religion_1965.pdf); OCR is noisy and page numbers are approximate). His critique cuts both ways. He called Wilhelm Schmidt "as intellectualist and evolutionist as others of the time" (same source). Timothy Larsen's *The Slain God* (2014) traces the arc further. In Luhrmann's review, Tylor and Frazer were hostile to religion, while the next four anthropologists Larsen covers "are all Catholics" ([Luhrmann review](https://www.new-directions.sps.ed.ac.uk/the-slain-god-book-review/)).

### Primordial monotheism: Lang's datum survives, Schmidt's chronology does not

**[Catholic]** Andrew Lang (1898) made a real empirical point that animism-first theory struggled with. Some foraging peoples held moral creator "High Gods" not obviously derived from ghosts ([Lang, *The Making of Religion*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/12353/pg12353.txt)). Wilhelm Schmidt SVD turned this into the twelve-volume *Urmonotheismus* thesis: monotheism came first and was later degraded into polytheism ([Wikipedia, Urmonotheismus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmonotheismus), secondary). Anthropology rejected his diffusionist framework. Some Catholic theologians also rejected his "degenerationist views". Swanson argued that monotheism was not the religion of the simplest societies, and the *Encyclopedia of Religion* notes that Schmidt wrote partly in support of the Church's anti-Modernist campaign ([Encyclopedia of Religion via encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/schmidt-wilhelm)). The phylogenetic result reconstructs animism at the root and active high gods as absent ([PMC4958132](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4958132/fullTextXML)). **Where it fits:** the "stand-alone" status of high gods is mildly congenial to Lang, because high gods do not look like a late outgrowth of ancestor cults. **Where it strains:** Schmidt's chronology, and his use of present-day foragers as "oldest" cultures, which is the same simple-equals-earliest error Evans-Pritchard charged against Tylor.

### The Fathers had frameworks for truth outside the Church, not origin theories

**[Catholic]**/**[Orthodox]** The patristic frameworks were inclusive but selective.

**Justin.** Justin Martyr held that Christ is "the Word of whom every race of men were partakers; and those who lived reasonably are Christians... as, among the Greeks, Socrates and Heraclitus" ([*1 Apology* 46, New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0126.htm)). He also insisted that "the seed and imitation impacted according to capacity is one thing, and quite another is the thing itself", and he paired this with demonic imitation ([*2 Apology* 8, 13](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0127.htm)).

**Clement.** Clement of Alexandria called philosophy "a schoolmaster to bring the Hellenic mind... to Christ" ([*Stromata* 1.5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02101.htm)).

**Augustine.** He went furthest: "the very thing which is now called the Christian religion existed among the ancients, and was not absent from the beginning of the human race". That is the notes' own translation of *Retractationes* 1.13.3 ([Latin text, BKV](https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-250/versions/retractationes/divisions/58)). It is a claim about the continuity of true worship, not an ethnographic claim about early humans.

### The magisterium claims universality, not chronology, and that is why it fits

**[Catholic]** The Catholic Church's official statements make several distinct claims.

**Natural knowledge of God.** Vatican I anathematises the claim that God "cannot be known with certainty from the things that have been made, by the natural light of human reason" ([*Dei Filius*](https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum20.htm)).

**A universal religious capacity.** The Catechism finds religious expression "so universal that one may well call man a religious being" ([CCC §28](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9.HTM)). *Nostra Aetate* speaks of "a certain perception of that hidden power" found "from ancient times", and of other religions that "often reflect a ray of that Truth which enlightens all men" ([*Nostra Aetate* §2](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651028_nostra-aetate_en.html)).

**A hierarchy among religions.** *Dominus Iesus* holds that followers of other religions "objectively speaking... are in a gravely deficient situation" ([§22](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000806_dominus-iesus_en.html)). The Catechism adds that "men also display the limits and errors that disfigure the image of God in them" ([CCC §844](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P29.HTM)).

**Evolution.** *Humani Generis* permits research on bodily evolution, but says "it is in no way apparent how" polygenism can be reconciled with original sin ([§§36–37](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_12081950_humani-generis.html)). John Paul II recognised evolution as "more than an hypothesis" while positing "an ontological leap" at man ([EWTN text](https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/message-to-pontifical-academy-of-sciences-on-evolution-8825)).

**Fit and strain.** Because none of these documents dates the first religion, they are compatible with an animism-first sequence. Their real strain point is monogenism and population genetics, not the history of religions. CSR's finding that religiosity is natural and early-developing matches CCC §28 "almost too neatly", in the notes' phrase. CSR's further finding is that whether a person believes depends mainly on cultural transmission. That fits a Catholic emphasis on Tradition and the witnessing community at least as well as it fits debunking.

### Orthodox theology emphasises anthropology over chronology, without a conciliar theology of religions

**[Orthodox]** The 2016 Holy and Great Council in Crete committed the Orthodox Churches to "inter-religious understanding and co-operation... without this involving any religious syncretism" ([holycouncil.org](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world)). It issued no doctrinal theology of other religions comparable to the Catholic sequence of *Nostra Aetate*, *Lumen Gentium* and *Dominus Iesus*, and that absence is itself worth noting. Alexander Schmemann's picture of the human being as *homo adorans*, the worshipping creature, and his diagnosis of secularism as a negation of worship are central here. The notes have them only from a secondary summary, so they are not quoted ([RESS 2023 summary](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/ress-2023-0107.pdf)). Orthodoxy has no official ruling on evolution. Fr. Seraphim Rose is the prominent creationist voice, and his work "has received a varied response" ([Wikipedia, Seraphim Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seraphim_Rose)).

### Explanatory Catholic theories inherit the same objections as Freud

**[Catholic]** René Girard's scapegoat theory is the most ambitious Catholic explanatory account of religion's origin. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy records the main criticisms: it is "not falsifiable in Popper's sense", which is "the same Popperian objection leveled against Freud"; "the number of rituals and myths that do not display violence is even greater"; and critics charge a tendency "to twist interpretations... to favor Christian doctrine" ([IEP, René Girard](https://iep.utm.edu/girard/)). Eliade's phenomenology of hierophany is influential, but any use of it should acknowledge his documented links with the Iron Guard ([Wikipedia, Mircea Eliade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Eliade)). The pattern is consistent. Theological lenses fare well as interpretations of meaning and human nature. They fare badly when they become datable origin theories, exactly as Tylor and Freud did.

### Lens scorecard

| Lens | Fits the evidence well | Strains against the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive/evolutionary** | Descriptive biases (teleology); CREDs and cultural transmission; the dual-inheritance synthesis; twin heritability by age | Causal priming and analytic-thinking experiments; mentalizing and autism; threat-driven HADD; WEIRD samples; MCI theory |
| **Archaeological** | Dating burial, symbol and monument; correcting myths (the Shanidar flowers, the Göbekli "temple") | Inferring belief from behaviour; dating disputes (Iberian art, Sulawesi minimum ages); claims publicised before review (*naledi*, Mount Ebal) |
| **Historical-critical** | Gradual Israelite monotheism from the epigraphy; the existence of Jesus; early worship of Jesus; schism as a process | Depends on dating disputed texts; cannot adjudicate miracle claims or normative ideals; Seshat-style coding of history |
| **Sociological** | US secularisation via cohorts; the global security–fertility pattern; Stark's growth arithmetic | Projections revised with new baselines; opt-in revival surveys; supply-side theory against switching data |
| **Catholic** | Universal religious capacity (CCC 28, NA 2) compatible with any sequence; development of doctrine; Tradition and witness congruent with CRED findings | Schmidt's primordial monotheism; Girard's unfalsifiable origin; monogenism against population genetics; Roman jurisdiction in the first-millennium sources |
| **Orthodox** | Anthropology of worship; living Tradition; Hart's classical theism as a reply to agent-detection debunking; first-millennium conciliarity, which Ravenna partly accepted | No conciliar theology of religions; creationist strands; "Tradition without development" against the documented growth of doctrine before 1054 |

## How confident can we be

**High confidence.** These rest on direct dating, large probability samples or multiple independent sources:
- The timing of the earliest burials (about 120,000–78,000 years ago), beads and drawings (about 82,000–73,000 years ago), and figurative art (at least 51,200 years ago, a minimum age).
- The early date of the Göbekli Tepe enclosures.
- The reality of large-scale state sacrifice in Shang China and Tenochtitlan.
- The existence of Yahweh-and-asherah inscriptions around 800 BCE.
- The existence and crucifixion of Jesus.
- The 2010–2020 global religious composition.
- The US cohort decline and the post-2019 plateau.
- The failures of the analytic-thinking, mentalizing and implicit-priming effects.

**Moderate confidence.**
- Cultural transmission (CREDs) outweighs cognitive biases in explaining who believes, though the evidence is correlational and retrospective.
- Israelite monotheism hardened gradually between the 8th and 6th centuries.
- Jesus was worshipped within two decades.
- The Axial Age is better described as a recurring scale threshold.
- Göbekli Tepe was a settlement with special buildings. The houses rest so far on press reports, not peer-reviewed publication.

**Low confidence, or genuinely open.** These are questions where the evidence underdetermines the answer, and no lens should present its view as settled:
- What any Palaeolithic person believed.
- Whether Neanderthals or *Homo naledi* had religion.
- Whether moralising gods followed or drove social complexity.
- Whether the polytheistic practice of Israel was original or apostasy.
- Whether Zoroastrianism shaped Jewish eschatology.
- Whether the resurrection appearances had a supernatural cause.
- Whether first-millennium Roman primacy was jurisdiction by divine right.
- Whether a revival among young Western men is real.

**Limits of this synthesis.** It is a literature synthesis, not new analysis. Many standard monographs (Smith, Assmann, Keightley, Meier, Sanders, Wright, Chadwick, Siecienski, Berger, Taylor, Casanova) are cited from bibliographic knowledge, not re-read. Several key numbers come second-hand, and the notes flag them:
- the 4.8 and 5.3 factors in Purzycki et al.;
- the 39%/6% commune survival figures;
- the share of administrative tablets in early Uruk writing;
- the 13,000 Shang sacrifice estimate.

The Orthodox theological strand is thinner than the Catholic one. Florovsky, Stăniloae and Ware were not read in primary text, and Schmemann's and Hart's wording is known only secondhand. A reader who wants to quote any of these should open the source first.

## Conclusion

The striking pattern is that every lens has had to retreat from *origin* claims toward *process* claims, and has become more credible for it. CSR moved from "minds generate gods" to "minds make gods learnable and communities make them believed". Archaeology moved from "first the temple" to ritual woven into the first villages. The Big Gods programme moved from a headline about which came first to a methodological lesson about missing data. Historical criticism moved from a single Mosaic or Axial moment to centuries of contested practice. Catholic thought moved from Schmidt's primordial monotheism to the Catechism's universal "religious being", and Orthodox thought from chronology to the anthropology of worship. The surviving theological claims are the ones that never depended on a date, which is why the evidence now strains them least.

One finding is mildly unexpected and deserves more attention than it gets. The empirical shift toward credible witness and cultural transmission as the main driver of belief brings scientific and theological accounts closer, not further apart. It turns the debate from evolutionary debunking toward the epistemology of testimony, which is where Tradition-centred Catholic and Orthodox theology has always located faith. That is an inference rather than a cited result. It suggests the most productive next arguments will be about *whose witness is credible and why*, not about whether minds evolved to see agents. Several open empirical tests would sharpen it: a rerun of the Quiet Revival survey with a probability sample, peer-reviewed publication of Göbekli Tepe's houses, and Seshat analyses that model the literacy bias directly.
