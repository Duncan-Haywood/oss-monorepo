# Auditing continual-learning jobs from old-task losses: an exact law for skipped and partial training

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`. Extends `../../forgetting-law`.*

## Abstract
In a decentralised training swarm a payer cannot watch a trainer work; it can only evaluate the returned checkpoint on tasks it holds. `forgetting-law` gave the exact expected loss of an honest, converged trainer on a task learned `k` tasks ago. Here the trainer may cheat by skipping tasks (probability `s`, saving a fraction `s` of compute) or by taking only a fraction `a` of each converging step. For iid step fractions with moments `m₁=E a`, `m₂=E a²`, the expected final-checkpoint loss of the task at position `t` of `T` (lag `k=T−t`) is exactly `ρ^{t−1}[(1−2m₁+m₂)(r/d)λᵏ + (r/d)ρ(ρᵏ−λᵏ)]`, with `ρ=1−(2m₁−m₂)r/d` and `λ=1−2m₁r/d+m₂c₁`; a never-trained task has mean `(r/d)ρᵀ`. Honest training recovers the forgetting law. Two consequences. (i) A task the trainer skipped is at least `d/(d−r)` times worse than an honestly trained one at every lag (`1/(ρ(1−(λ/ρ)ᵏ))`, e.g. 9.6× at lag 1, 1.14× floor at `d=16, r=2`), so pinpointing a skipped task works only inside a window (ratio ≥ 2 for lags ≤ 6.7 at `d=16, r=2`, ≤ 45 at `d=256, r=4`). (ii) Detecting a *skipping trainer* is a different problem: skipping shifts the contraction `ρ`, so every task's loss rises, and the number of jobs an audit needs is nearly independent of which lag is audited (17–20 jobs for 20% skipping, any lag from 1 to 23) and scales as `N ≈ 0.7/s²`. Partial training is far harder to see: a step fraction `a=0.9` needs ≈ 9600 jobs, `a=0.5` needs 10. Verified by exact quadrature at `d=2`, Monte Carlo, and power simulation. Stylised: linear regression, Haar-random tasks, known `‖e_init‖`.

## 1. Model
As in `forgetting-law`: error `e=w−w*` with `‖e_init‖=1`, tasks are Haar-random rank-`r` projectors `Pⱼ` in `R^d`, task `j` loss `‖Pⱼe‖²`. A trainer with step fraction `aⱼ` updates `e←(I−aⱼPⱼ)e` (`a=1` is convergence, `a=0` skipping); `aⱼ` iid, independent of the tasks, with moments `m₁,m₂`. The verifier holds the final checkpoint and the task data, evaluates old or fresh tasks, and gets one loss sample per job.

## 2. The law
With `E[PAP]=c₁A+c₂tr(A)I` (`c₂=r(d−r)/(d(d+2)(d−1))`, `c₁=r/d−dc₂`) and `E[PA]=(r/d)A`: `E[(I−aP)A(I−aP)]=λA+m₂c₂tr(A)I` and `E tr=ρ tr`, with `ρ=1−(2m₁−m₂)r/d`, `λ=1−2m₁r/d+m₂c₁`, and `ρ−λ=m₂dc₂`. Solving the recursion from `A₀=e₀e₀ᵀ` gives `Aₖ=λᵏA₀+((ρᵏ−λᵏ)/d)‖e₀‖²I`. The audited task's own update gives `P e₀=(1−a)Pe`, so its contribution is `(1−a)²(r/d)λᵏ` plus the isotropic part `(r/d)ρ(ρᵏ−λᵏ)` (using `E‖e₀‖²=ρ`), and the `t−1` earlier tasks scale everything by `ρ^{t−1}`:
`L(t,T)=ρ^{t−1}[(1−2m₁+m₂)(r/d)λᵏ+(r/d)ρ(ρᵏ−λᵏ)]`, `k=T−t`.
Skipping: `m₁=m₂=1−s`. Fixed step: `m₁=a`, `m₂=a²`. E1 (`d=16, r=2, T=24`, 6000 jobs each) matches: honest lag 23/16/8/2/0: law 0.00479/0.00440/0.00322/0.00113/0 vs sim 0.00476/0.00442/0.00321/0.00110/0; 20% skipping 0.00913/0.00832/0.00639/0.00358/0.00222 vs 0.00894/0.00828/0.00638/0.00351/0.00225; `a=0.5` 0.00740/…/0.00325 vs 0.00743/…/0.00326. A `d=2` double quadrature (500² nodes) matches the law to 5 places.

## 3. A skipped task hides at long lags
Take one task the trainer skipped and one it trained, both followed by the same honest tasks. The skipped one is just a fresh task at lag `k`: `(r/d)ρᵏ`; the honest one is `(r/d)ρ(ρᵏ−λᵏ)`. Their ratio `1/(ρ(1−(λ/ρ)ᵏ))` decreases in `k` to the floor `1/ρ=d/(d−r)`: at `d=16, r=2` it is 9.6, 5.1, 2.9, 1.8, 1.32, 1.16, 1.14 at lags 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64. The lag where the ratio drops to `q>d/(d−r)` is `ln(1−1/(ρq))/ln(λ/ρ)`: 6.7, 11.4, 16.8 for `q=2, 1.5, 1.3` at `d=16, r=2`; 23 for `q=2` at `d=64, r=2`; 45 at `d=256, r=4`; but only 6.4 at `d=64, r=8` (large tasks are forgotten fast either way). So to tell a specific task was skipped from its loss, audit it while it is recent.

## 4. Catching a skipping trainer is lag-free
A skipper is caught in aggregate, not task by task. `ρ` rises from 0.875 to 0.900 at `s=0.2`, so the fresh-task mean `(r/d)ρᵀ` doubles by `T=24` (0.00507→0.00997) and every old task inherits the same factor `ρ^{t−1}`. The samples are noisy (a loss is `‖e‖²` times a Beta draw), so the jobs needed for a one-sided z-test at size 0.05 and power 0.8, `N=((z_α σ₀+z_β σ₁)/(μ₁−μ₀))²`, are what matter. E3 (`s=0.2`, 6000 jobs/pool): `N` = 20.4, 17.9, 19.0, 19.4, 20.2, 18.6, 17.1 at lags 23, 22, 20, 16, 12, 8, 4 and 19.9 for a fresh task — flat; only lags ≤ 2 gain (12.5, 12.4) and lag 0, an intermediate checkpoint right after training, costs 8.3 (honest loss is exactly 0 there). So the audit needs no checkpoint history: the final checkpoint and any task suffice.

## 5. Cheater economics
E4 (fresh-task audit, `T=24`, 8000 jobs/pool): `N` = 2480, 253, 76, 17.9, 4.3 for `s` = 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, i.e. `Ns²` = 0.99, 0.63, 0.76, 0.71, 0.69: the mean shift is linear in `s` and the noise does not shrink, so `N≈0.7/s²`. Resampled z-tests at those `N` reach power 0.78–0.85 with size 0.052–0.068 (the loss is right-skewed, so the normal critical value is slightly liberal at small `N`). E6: the fresh-loss ratio at `s=0.1` grows with the horizon (1.06, 1.12, 1.25, 1.57, 1.98 at `T`=4, 8, 16, 32, 48) and `N` falls 5050, 695, 164, 57, 30, because `ρ̄ᵀ` compounds: long jobs are the cheapest to audit. Read with `spot-check-slashing`: a saving of `s` risks detection with `≈0.7/s²` jobs, so a per-job fine `F` deters when `s·G` (the compute saved) is below the expected fine over the jobs the trainer expects to be audited on.

## 6. Partial training is nearly invisible
A trainer that takes a fraction `a` of each converging step (`m₁=a, m₂=a²`) has `ρ=1−(1−(1−a)²)r/d`. E5: fresh-loss ratio 1.01, 1.03, 1.15, 2.32 and `N` = 28 458, 9644, 382, 9.7 for `a` = 0.95, 0.9, 0.8, 0.5. A verifier that only sees old-task losses cannot police a 10% shortfall in step size. Caveat: for gradient descent the step fraction is `1−(1−ηs)^n`, not linear in compute, so `a=0.9` may save much more than 10% of steps on well-conditioned directions; the law prices detection, and the compute map is a separate model.

## 7. Limits and next steps
Linear regression with Haar-random rank-`r` tasks, realizable, iid step fractions independent of tasks, `‖e_init‖` known (in practice estimated from the initial-checkpoint loss, adding a control term). Sample sizes use empirical variances of single-job losses (means are exact); an exact variance and an anytime-valid version (cf. `sequential-slashing`, `inference-substitution`) are natural next steps, as is a trainer who skips adaptively (e.g. easy tasks first) rather than iid. Related: `forgetting-law`, `spot-check-slashing`, `sequential-slashing`, `reproducible-refereed-training`, `wagering-modular-experts`.
