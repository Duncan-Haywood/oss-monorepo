# Inner steps precondition the outer optimiser: momentum tuning and the sync interval in DiLoCo-style training

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
`local-sgd-bias` computed the fixed point of DiLoCo-style averaging on heterogeneous quadratics and left the outer optimiser open. On a quadratic with Hessian eigenvalues `a_i`, a round of `H` inner steps of size `η` followed by an outer step on the pseudo-gradient is *exactly* an outer optimiser run on a quadratic with curvatures `s_i = 1 − (1−ηa_i)^H`. These saturate at 1, so `H` inner steps compress the outer condition number from `κ = a_max/a_min` to `κ_H = s_max/s_min ≈ κ/H` (1000 → 250.4 at `H=4`, 16.1 at `H=64`, 1.02 at `H=4096`). Consequences: (i) per-round contraction of any outer optimiser is an exact 2×2 spectral radius per mode, matched by literal simulation to ≤0.4%; (ii) the optimal heavy-ball outer step is `α* = 4/(√s_max+√s_min)²`, `β* = ((√κ_H−1)/(√κ_H+1))²`, and *every* mode then contracts at exactly `√β*`; (iii) the momentum that is optimal falls from 0.88 at `H=1` to 0.01 at `H=1024` (`κ=1000`), so a fixed DiLoCo-style Nesterov setting (0.7, 0.9) needs 4–8× more rounds than the retuned optimiser at every `H` we tried; (iv) with wallclock `rounds × (H + C)` for a synchronisation costing `C` inner steps, the optimal interval is `H* ≈ C` (10 → 10, 100 → 100–104, 1000 → 1035 at `κ=10⁴`) and beats syncing every step by `≈ √C/2` (1.7×, 5.1×, 16×), while plain averaging (no outer momentum) has no interior optimum and wants `H` on the order of `κ`. Stylised: noiseless quadratic, shared Hessian across workers, tuned to the spectrum edges.

## 1. Reduction
Work in the Hessian eigenbasis, loss `½Σa_ix_i²`. Inner gradient descent for `H` steps from the global point multiplies mode `i` by `(1−ηa_i)^H`. The pseudo-gradient (start minus end) is `g_i = s_ix_i` with `s_i = 1−(1−ηa_i)^H ∈ (0,1]` for `0<ηa_i≤1`. The outer optimiser (PyTorch conventions `v←βv+g`; heavy ball `x←x−αv`, Nesterov `x←x−α(g+βv)`) therefore sees a quadratic with curvatures `s_i`. Saturation is the whole story: every mode with `ηa_i` large is already solved in one round (`s≈1`), and only the flat modes have `s_i ≈ Hηa_i` small, so `κ_H ≈ κ/H` for `H ≪ κ` (with `η=1/a_max`; exact `κ_H = 1/(1−(1−1/κ)^H)`) and `κ_H → 1` once `H ≳ κ`.

## 2. Exact rates
Each mode is the linear map `(x,v) ↦` `[[1−αs, −αβ],[s, β]]` (heavy ball) or `[[1−αs(1+β), −αβ²],[s, β]]` (Nesterov); its spectral radius from the trace and determinant is the mode's per-round contraction, and the quadratic's rate is the maximum over modes. Literal simulation of inner loops agrees (E2): e.g. Nesterov (0.7, 0.9) at `H=40`: 0.8863 exact, 0.8863 simulated; heavy (0.9, 0.6) at `H=7`: 0.9086 / 0.9086; the worst deviation is 0.4% (0.9372 vs 0.9411, transient still present).

## 3. Tuning
Classical heavy-ball tuning applies to `[s_min, s_max]`: `α* = 4/(√s_max+√s_min)²`, `β*=q²` with `q=(√κ_H−1)/(√κ_H+1)`. In that setting the discriminant is non-positive for every `s ∈ [s_min,s_max]`, so *all* modes have radius exactly `√β* = q` (tested at 101 values of `s`), and no `(α,β)` on a grid beats it (tested). Plain averaging with the best step `2/(s_min+s_max)` contracts at `(κ_H−1)/(κ_H+1)`, i.e. needs `~κ_H` rather than `~√κ_H` rounds.
**Retuning with `H`** (E1, `κ=1000`): optimal `β*` falls 0.881, 0.602, 0.361, 0.126, 0.012 at `H = 1, 16, 64, 256, 1024`, with `α*` 3.76 → 1.23 and rate 0.939 → 0.111. A fixed Nesterov (0.7, 0.9) — the setting popularised for DiLoCo — contracts at 0.993, 0.943, 0.928, 0.870, 0.704 at those `H`, i.e. 8.5×, 4.3×, 6.8×, 7.5×, 6.3× more rounds to 10⁻⁶ (E3). Large `H` with momentum tuned for small `H` wastes the compression the inner steps bought.

## 4. Optimal synchronisation interval
Suppose one synchronisation costs `C` inner-step equivalents and the target is `ε`. With `η=1/a_max` and tuned momentum, rounds `≈ (√κ_H/2)ln(1/ε)`, so for `H ≪ κ` time `≈ (√κ/2)ln(1/ε)·(H+C)/√H`, minimised at `H* = C`: **sync as often as a sync costs**, giving speedup `≈ (1+C)/(2√C)` over per-step syncing (which is itself accelerated). Exact search (E4, `ε=10⁻⁶`): `C=10, 100, 1000` gives `H* = 10, 100, 1035` at `κ=10⁴` (10, 104, 1697 at `κ=10³`, where `C` exceeds the saturation point `H≈κ`); speedups over `H=1`: 1.7×, 5.1×, 16.0×. Without outer momentum time `≈ κ(1+C/H)ln(1/ε)/2` decreases in `H` until saturation, so the optimum is at `H` of order `κ` (152–5791 across E4), never interior, and the wallclock is 1.7–5.7× worse than the momentum optimum at `κ=10⁴`.

## 5. Limits and next steps
Noise-free quadratic with one Hessian shared by all workers: heterogeneity moves the fixed point (`local-sgd-bias`) and could be added as a bias/rate trade; the tuning uses `s_min, s_max`, which a practitioner must estimate (e.g. from a few rounds' pseudo-gradient ratios); stochastic gradients add a variance term that favours smaller `α` and would raise the optimal `H` for noisy problems. The 2×2 analysis is agnostic to how many workers are averaged; a per-mode noise model would give the batch/`H` trade-off.
