# Prediction markets with partially informed traders as randomized subspace descent: exact rates, correlation slowdown, and the price of noise

## Question
A cost-function market (LMSR and friends) is used in this repo as a verifier or router. Frongillo and Reid showed that a market whose traders each move prices within a subspace is randomized subspace descent on a Bregman divergence. How fast does it converge when each trader understands only part of the securities, when securities are correlated, and when traders' beliefs are noisy? What does the subsidy buy?

## Model
Cost C(q) = ½q'Aq (A is the Hessian of a smooth cost; LMSR locally), price p = Aq, mean payoff Aq*. A trader who can trade only a bundle subspace span(U) and moves a fraction κ of the way to their belief updates the error e = q − q* by e' = (I − κΠ)e, where Π = U(U'AU)⁻¹U'A is the A-orthogonal projector onto span(U). With noisy beliefs the target is q* + ξ, ξ ~ N(0, S). Each round draws a subspace from a finite list.

## Results
1. **The subsidy telescopes, whoever trades (exact identity).** A trade from q to q' has expected profit ½(e'Ae − e''Ae') under the truth, so the maker's total expected loss is ½(e₀'Ae₀ − e_T'Ae_T) ≤ ½e₀'Ae₀ however many traders, subspaces, or harmful trades (verified to 1e-9 against explicit cost-function accounting). Accuracy is bought once; extra rounds are free to the maker.
2. **Guaranteed rate from any state.** With ρ = λ_min(E[A^{1/2}ΠA^{-1/2}]), E‖e'‖²_A ≤ (1−ρ)‖e‖²_A every round, tight for the worst state for one step (tested). The exact asymptotic rate is the spectral radius of the second-moment operator, always at most 1−ρ, and the exact E‖e_t‖²_A is computed by iterating that operator (no simulation; agrees with 20,000-run Monte Carlo to 0.5%).
3. **Correlation slows single-security traders, in closed form.** With d securities of unit variance and common correlation c, uniform one-security traders have ρ = (1−c)/d and exact rate the top eigenvalue of [[1−(1−c²)/d, c²(1−2/d)], [(1−c)²/d, (1−c)²(1−2/d)]] (from the recursion on Σe² and (Σe)²; equals the power-iteration rate to 4 places). At d = 5, rounds to reach 10⁻³ of the error: 31 (c=0), 46 (0.5), 97 (0.8), 183 (0.9), 356 (0.95). The guarantee 1−ρ is loose, e.g. 0.980 against exact 0.963 at c=0.9.
4. **Offering A-conjugate bundles removes the slowdown exactly.** If traders trade along A-conjugate directions (columns of A^{-1/2}V, V orthonormal), every round zeroes one whitened coordinate, so ρ = 1/d and the rate is exactly 1−1/d for any correlation (tested on random SPD A): 31 rounds at every c, against 356 for coordinate bundles at c=0.95. Market design lesson: list orthogonalised bundles, not raw correlated securities.
5. **Noisy beliefs make the step size (the wealth or liquidity share a trader is willing to move) an SGD step.** For A = I with one-coordinate traders the stationary error is dκσ²/(2−κ) and the contraction is 1 − κ(2−κ)/d (both verified exactly by the moment recursion). Full steps (κ=1) never beat the last trader's noise: error 5σ² at d=5 for ever. Smaller steps lower the floor and slow convergence: κ = 0.2 reaches 0.57 by T=100 but 4.9 at T=20; κ = 0.05 reaches 0.13 only after ~1000 rounds. The best constant κ falls with the horizon (0.365, 0.145, 0.020 at T = 30, 100, 1000) but a per-security running-mean step 1/(n+1) is better still and needs no horizon: at T=100 error 0.26 vs 0.47 for the best constant step and 5.0 for κ=1; at T=1000, 0.025 vs 0.058 (exact binomial expectation over visit counts, checked by simulation to 3%).
6. **Real LMSR agrees with the local prediction.** For 4 outcomes with θ = (.5, .25, .15, .10) and traders who exactly minimise KL(θ‖p) over their bundle, the late-time decay rate of mean KL measured over 3,000 runs is 0.897 for single-outcome traders (predicted 0.901, guarantee 0.947), 0.737 for pair bundles (predicted 0.747, guarantee 0.835), and 0.585 for conjugate bundles (predicted 0.667). The prediction upper-bounds all three and the ordering is right; the conjugate rate is faster than the local prediction and we did not isolate why (the same fit on the exact linear model gives 0.68, so it is not Monte Carlo noise alone).

All numbers are printed by `experiments/run.py` (`experiments/results.txt`).

## Limitations
The theory is for a quadratic cost; LMSR results are local plus one simulation, and traders in the simulation optimise KL exactly (no fees, budgets or risk aversion beyond the step κ, cf. `lmsr-fees`, `kelly-markets`, `risk-averse-scoring`). Subspaces are drawn i.i.d. from a finite list, independent of the state; adversarial or state-dependent trader arrival is out of scope (see `market-manipulation`). Noise is Gaussian, independent across rounds and traders, and unbiased; correlated noise would put a floor on the running-mean gain. The closed form in result 3 needs unit diagonal and a common correlation; the general case uses the numerical operator. Results 1–2 restate known subspace-descent and Bregman-market facts (Frongillo–Reid 2015; Abernethy et al. 2013); the closed forms in 3 and 4 and the step-size/floor tradeoff in 5 are our own computations and are not checked against a published statement.
