# Gate twin: a Gaussian-sensor twin says an innovation gate only throws data away; with real outliers it cuts the error 3×, and on a jump it can lock the filter out for thousands of steps

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: a state estimator whose sensor occasionally returns an outlier (a Gaussian scale mixture: `N(0, R)` w.p. 0.95, `N(0, 100R)` w.p. 0.05), with an innovation gate `|ν| ≤ z√(P+R)` that skips the update when the innovation is implausible. The twin's sensor is the datasheet Gaussian `N(0, R)`, so the gate is sized from it and, in the twin, rejects exactly `α` of good data. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`; the post-update MSE is exact, given the mixture component `ν ~ N(0, P+R_c)` and `e | ν ~ N(Pν/S_c, PR_c/S_c)`, and is checked against independent quadrature in the tests and against 1e6-draw Monte Carlo to 0.2%): (1) with `P = R = 1`, gain 0.5 and `α = 0.01` the twin says the gate *costs* 8.5% (MSE 0.500 → 0.542; 28% at `α = 0.05`), while on the real sensor it cuts MSE 1.738 → 0.580 (3.0×), so a twin-based decision to drop the gate would triple the error; the gate still lets through 28% of the outliers (kappa = 10); (2) the ungated MSE grows like `κ²` (1.25e6 at `κ = 10⁴`) while the gated MSE is bounded and peaks at a finite outlier scale `κ* = 3.8, 4.1, 4.6` for `α = 0.05, 0.01, 0.001` (0.669, 0.589, 0.575), so the worst outliers for a gated filter are the *plausible* ones just outside the nominal noise, not the wild ones; (3) the real-optimal gate is wider than the twin's `α = 0.01` (`z` 3.2 to 3.8, not 2.58) but tuning it gains only 0–6% of MSE (0.561 vs 0.580 at `κ = 10`), so the large effect is having a gate, not its exact width; (4) negative/cautionary: after a jump `D` in a random-walk state (`Q = 0.01, R = 1, z = 2.58`) the gate rejects the correct measurements: mean time to re-converge is 21, 546, 4009, 10918, 30512 steps for `D = 3, 5, 8, 12, 20` (`≈ 76 D²` for `D ≥ 12`) against 12 to 30 steps with no gate; a noise-free first-acceptance count has the same `D²` scaling but under-counts by about 5× at large `D` because an accepted step resets the gate.

```bash
cd research/gate-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 14 tests, about 1 s

```

**Builds on.** The robust-perception and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>) and the field-robot state-estimation setting of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from either group is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Innovation gating is classical (Bar-Shalom, Li & Kirubarajan 2001), as is the contaminated-Gaussian model (Huber 1964; Andrews & Mallows 1974). Companion to `filter-twin` (wrong noise levels in the twin; here the wrong noise *shape*).

Stylised: simulated "real" sensor, no recorded data; one scalar update with the prior variance `P` assumed correct and a fixed gain, a symmetric zero-mean outlier component, one operating point (`P = R = 1`, 5% outliers) in the tables; the lockout experiment is a scalar random walk with a simplified covariance rule (grow by `Q` per rejection, reset on acceptance) and 400 runs per row. The twin is a deliberately naive baseline: a twin that included outliers would reproduce these numbers by construction. MIT.
