# A snapshot-scan twin of a spinning lidar: motion-distortion bias in the map, what one scan reveals about ego-velocity, and what yaw and acceleration do to scan-to-scan odometry

## Question
Lidar simulators commonly return a whole revolution as if it were captured at one instant. A real spinning lidar fires each beam at a different time while the platform moves. A perception stack tuned or certified against the snapshot twin (map accuracy, wall-angle estimates, scan matching) is then tested on a sensor that behaves differently. How large is the error, is it identifiable from the data, and which errors accumulate?

## Model
Real sensor: 2-D, period `τ = 0.1 s`, `n = 3600` beams per revolution, beam `i` at `t_i = τ i/n` with scan azimuth `φ(t) = φ₀ + s·2πt/τ` (`s = ±1` the spin direction). The platform moves along x, `x(t) = v t + a t²/2`, and optionally yaws at `ψ = ω t`. Range is an exact ray cast into a rectangle (front wall `x = 10`, back `x = −8`, sides `y = ±5`), plus optional Gaussian range noise. Twin: the same beams all fired at `t = 0`. "Naive" points place each return at `r(cos φ, sin φ)` in the scan-start frame (what a snapshot twin assumes); "deskewed" points use the platform pose at `t_i`. The seam is placed on a side wall (`φ₀ = −π/2`), parallel to the motion, so it does not split a wall that matters. Pure Python, no dependencies.

## Results
(all numbers from `experiments/results.txt`)
1. **Placement and length bias.** A beam fired at `t` measures `r cos φ = d − x(t)`, so the naive wall sits at `d − v t`. At `v = 10 m/s` the front wall (fired at `τ/4`) is 0.25 m short and the back wall (`3τ/4`) 0.75 m short, so the apparent length is biased by exactly `s v τ/2 = 0.50 m` (simulated 0.5000). Walls parallel to the motion are exactly right (max error 0). Deskewing with the true pose recovers both walls to 1e-15 m. The bias depends only on the firing-time gap between the two walls, so with a different seam it changes; I report only the side-wall seam.
2. **Wall tilt and convergence.** The naive front wall satisfies `x̃ = d − v t(φ)`, `ỹ = x̃ tan φ`, so its tilt is `−s v τ cos²φ/(2π x̃)`, i.e. `−s v τ/(2π d)` to first order; the back wall tilts the other way. Total-least-squares fits over ±0.3 rad fans: front 0.92° (first order 0.91°) at 10 m/s, back 1.02° (first order 1.14°, an 11% overshoot because the back wall's range has grown by 0.75 m), so two parallel walls appear to converge by 1.94° (first order `s v τ/(2π)(1/d₁+1/d₂)` = 2.05°). Halving `τ` at fixed `v` is the same as halving `v`. Clockwise spin, or driving backward, flips the sign (backward gives a 5% different magnitude because of the range shift). A scan-matching or wall-angle estimator certified on the snapshot twin inherits a heading bias of this size.
3. **One scan identifies the speed.** Inverting the convergence angle with `d₁, d₂` taken from the scan recovers `v`: mean 0.995, 4.91, 9.83, 19.6 m/s for true 1, 5, 10, 20 (200 seeded trials each, 1 cm range noise), standard deviation about 0.13 m/s at every speed, bias −0.4% to −1.8% from the first-order inversion (not removed). At `v = 0` the estimate is +0.005 ± 0.13 m/s. It assumes opposite walls that are truly parallel and aligned with the motion, constant velocity and no yaw; none of those is identified by one scan, so this is a consistency check, not an odometer.
4. **Yaw during the sweep.** A landmark at world bearing `β` from the start heading is returned where `ψ(t)+φ(t) = β`, i.e. at `φ = β/(1+κ)`, `κ = ωτ/2π`. The naive bearing error is `−κβ/(1+κ)`, matched by bisection to 1e-12 (−1.41° at ω = 1, β = 90°; −2.82° at β = 180°; −5.55° at ω = 2). It grows linearly with bearing, so it is a scale error on azimuth, not a constant offset.
5. **Acceleration and odometry.** Front-wall scan-to-scan displacement from naive scans equals the sensor's displacement over windows shifted by the wall's firing time `t_f`, so the velocity estimate exceeds the true mean velocity over the scan by exactly `a t_f` (0.050, −0.075, 0.125 m/s for the three cases simulated, `t_f = τ/4`). At constant velocity the bias is zero. Summed over a leg the errors telescope to `(v_end − v_start) t_f`, bounded and non-accumulating; this last step is derived, not simulated.
6. **Speed limit.** A length tolerance `tol` requires `v ≤ 2 tol/τ`: 0.2, 0.4, 1.0 m/s for 1, 2, 5 cm at 10 Hz, and double at 20 Hz. Each was checked by simulating the scan at that speed (bias equals `tol` to five digits).

## Limitations
2-D, a rectangular room, and a single straight-line motion; real lidars are 3-D, have multiple returns, beam divergence, range-dependent noise, and scan patterns other than a single rotating plane. The platform motion is known exactly in the simulator; real deskewing relies on an IMU or odometry estimate whose error I did not model (the residual after deskewing with a wrong velocity scales with the velocity error, but I did not simulate that). The velocity-from-convergence estimator is a first-order formula with a measured −1.8% bias and strong assumptions (item 3). Item 5's odometry is a 1-D front-wall match, not ICP on a full cloud. The "real" sensor is itself a model, so no number transfers to hardware, and I did not compare to any dataset.
