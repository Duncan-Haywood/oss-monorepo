# Sweep twin: a snapshot-scan twin of a spinning lidar ignores that the platform moves during the sweep, so how wrong is the map it certifies, what does one scan reveal, and what do yaw and acceleration do to it?

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real sensor: 2-D lidar at 10 Hz, 3600 beams/rev, beam `i` fired at `t = τ i/n` while the platform moves along x (and optionally yaws); twin: every beam fired at `t = 0`. Room: front wall x = +10 m, back wall x = −8 m, side walls y = ±5 m. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`): (1) at v = 10 m/s the front and back walls are placed 0.25 and 0.75 m short (`v t` at their firing times), so the apparent room length is biased by exactly `v τ/2` = 0.50 m while the width (walls parallel to the motion) is exact; deskewing with the true motion removes it to 1e-15 m; (2) wall tilt is `∓ v τ/(2π d)` to first order (0.92° measured vs 0.91° at 10 m/s, back wall 11% off because its range shifts), so two parallel walls appear to converge by `≈ v τ/(2π)(1/d₁+1/d₂)` (1.94° measured vs 2.05° first order); spin direction and platform direction each flip the sign; (3) one scan reveals the speed: inverting the convergence angle gives v with 0.13 m/s standard deviation at 1 cm range noise (first-order inversion biased −1.8% at 5–20 m/s); (4) yaw ω during the sweep biases a landmark's bearing β by exactly `−κβ/(1+κ)`, `κ = ωτ/2π` (−2.82° at ω = 1 rad/s, β = 180°); (5) under constant acceleration a, scan-to-scan front-wall odometry is biased by exactly `a t_f` in velocity (`t_f` the firing time of the wall's beams) and the bias does not accumulate; (6) the speed keeping the length bias under a tolerance is `2 tol/τ` (0.4 m/s for 2 cm at 10 Hz), verified by simulation. Stylised: 2-D, a rectangular room, constant velocity or acceleration along one axis, no range-dependent noise or beam divergence; see `paper/whitepaper.md`.

```bash
cd research/sweep-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 12 tests, under 1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # a few seconds; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The sensor-simulation and sim-to-real-for-perception direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>; radar/lidar simulation and mapping) and the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>) and RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>; field and aerial vehicles moving at speed); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Motion distortion and continuous-time compensation in lidar odometry are established (Zhang & Singh 2014, LOAM; Bosse & Zlot 2009). Companion to `shutter-twin` (the rolling-shutter camera analogue), `odometry-twin` and `doppler-twin` in this repository.
