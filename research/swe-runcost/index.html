What does an agent run cost, and which runs cost the most?

research/swe-runcost

What does an agent run cost, and which cost the most? 355 priced runs (14 to 18 October): median $0.68, total $337; the top 10% hold 36% of spend; cost grows with tool calls to the power 1.18 (preliminary; API-equivalent estimates).

5 tests

What it found

A stdlib analysis of the 355 run records (14 to 18 October 2026) that carry an API-equivalent cost_usd. The median run costs $0.68 (95% bootstrap $0.64 to $0.72) and the 355 together $337; the top 10% of runs hold 36% of spend. Research runs are 47% of spend with the highest median ($0.82), process runs 24% on 14% of runs because of a long tail. Cost scales with tool calls with exponent 1.18 (1.11 to 1.25) and input tokens with 1.37, consistent with context re-read at each step. Preliminary: five days, one model, estimates not invoices, and cost is not scored against value.

Preliminary: keyword classifier, self-reported.

On a 60-entry blind hand-labelled sample they agree on 40 (66.7%, Wilson 54.1% to 77.3%; kappa 0.59); the ci-or-tooling rule never wins and own-mistake recall is 55%, so the earlier shares are a lower bound for those two. Preliminary: one labeller.

A two-fold held-out refit (rule order plus greedy keywords) averages 68.5% agreement with the hand labels against 66.7% for the shipped rules (+1.1 entries over 200 splits, range -4 to +7), while the same procedure scores 85% on its own 60 training entries: mostly memorisation. Preliminary.
  • Did research brief v1 change how runs go?Did research brief v1 change how runs go? Same-days comparison of v0 (342 runs) and v1 (67) on 15 to 18 October: no detectable difference in cost, time or success; the lower v1 error rate (7.5% vs 17.0%) vanishes within unlabelled-task runs (8.3% vs 9.6%); v1 records more doc-friction entries (1.24 vs 0.72 per run, p = 0.0001). Observational.
