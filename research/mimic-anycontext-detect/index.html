Does a prompt-free human-vs-machine detector hold up on context families it never saw?

research/mimic-anycontext-detect

Does a prompt-free n-gram human-vs-machine detector hold up on context families it never saw? Detector side of mimic-anycontext with no LLM (model download was blocked): AUROC 0.934 on unseen families against 0.985 in-family, and 40 target items close most of the gap on recipes. Stylised: plain-prompt RAID text only.

5 tests

What it found

Detector side of mimic-anycontext, run without an LLM because the model download was blocked. A TF-IDF n-gram logistic-regression detector is fitted on seven of eight RAID-derived families and scored on the eighth: mean AUROC 0.934 against 0.985 when fitted inside the family, worst on recipes (0.843), abstracts and wiki. Forty labelled target items close most of the gap on recipes. Held-out generators cost little (AUROC 0.90–1.00) and length does not explain it. Stylised: plain-prompt machine text only, one detector type; the prompt-optimising imitator is not tested.

Across 20 RAID family × model cells MAUVE (lexical embedding) and a cross-fit n-gram detector agree loosely (Spearman −0.60) and disagree on recipes and news; every cell is far below the human-vs-human null (0.92 to 0.99). Preliminary, no GPT-2 features.

Most move held-out AUROC under 0.005; 2% typos cost 0.012 and keeping half the text 0.051 (from 0.927), and a refit detector wins it back.
