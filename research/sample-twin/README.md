# Sample twin: a continuous-time twin certifies a PD loop at every gain, what does the real zero-order-hold loop do?

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real plant `x'' = u` (double integrator) under `u = −kp x − kd v` computed at sample instants and held for a period `T`; `kp = ωn²`, `kd = 2ζωn`. Twin: continuous-time closed loop, stable for every `ωn, ζ > 0`, overshoot `exp(−πζ/√(1−ζ²))` independent of `ωn`. The exact step matrix has trace `2 − kdT − kpT²/2` and determinant `1 − kdT + kpT²/2`, and Jury's conditions reduce to `kp T/2 < kd < 2/T`, i.e. `ωnT < min(4ζ, 1/ζ)`. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`, ωn = 1 unless stated): (1) the boundary matches bisection on the spectral radius to six digits (ζ = 0.25, 0.5, 0.7, 1, 2, 4 → 1, 2, 1.4286, 1, 0.5, 0.25) and an independent RK4 held-input simulation shrinks at 0.98× and diverges at 1.02×; ζ = 0.7 fails at `ωnT = 1.4286` (4.4 samples per natural period) while the twin promises stability; (2) far from the boundary the twin is fine (overshoot 0.0460 → 0.0463 at `ωnT` = 0.1, settling 6.41 vs 6.30) but the twin's pole modulus `e^{−ζωnT}` is optimistic: at `ωnT` = 1.2 the real overshoot is 0.234 (twin 0.046), at 1.3 settling is 14.3 and at 1.4 it is 72.8 against the twin's 6.41; (3) at fixed `T = 0.1` raising `ωn` (twin settling falls 6.41 → 0.32) gives real settling 6.3, 3.1, 1.4, 0.6, 0.4, 0.5 for `ωn` = 1, 2, 4, 8, 10, 12, then 5.2 (14), 10.2 (14.14), and instability from 14.3; (4) the discrete model gives closed-form design: gains for poles `ρe^{±iφ}` are `kp T² = 1+ρ²−2ρcosφ`, `kd T = (3−2ρcosφ−ρ²)/2`, deadbeat is `kpT² = 1, kdT = 3/2` (state exactly 0 after 2 samples, settling 2T, no overshoot), the real speed limit, not the twin's `→ 0`. Stylised: noiseless linear plant, no computation delay, no quantisation, fixed `T`; see `paper/whitepaper.md`.

**Builds on.** The digital-twin simulation-fidelity and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>), HIRO Group (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>, manipulation) and the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>) and RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>, UAV and field-robot simulation); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Sampled-data control and the Jury criterion are classical (Åström & Wittenmark 1997; Franklin, Powell & Workman 1998). Companion to `timestep-twin` (the twin's own integrator step), `latency-twin` (sensor delay), `lag-twin` and `control-twin` in this repository.
