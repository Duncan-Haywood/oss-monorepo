# How much battery to carry: what a constant-wind twin does to a UAV leg planned in simulation

Pure Python, no dependencies. A UAV flies one leg at constant airspeed `v` (units of `V0`, the zero-wind minimum-energy-per-distance speed) with power `P(v) = (v³ + 1/v)/2`; the headwind `w` is constant within a flight and uniform across flights, `w ~ U[μ−a, μ+a]`, so the energy per unit distance is `P(v)/(v−w)`. A constant-wind twin has `a = 0`. With the uniform law everything real is exact: expected energy `P(v)·(1/2a)·ln((v−μ+a)/(v−μ−a))` (infinite if `v ≤ μ+a`), a Jensen factor `artanh(x)/x` with `x = a/(v−μ)` on the twin's energy claim, and the depletion probability of a battery sized `(1+m)` times the twin's nominal energy is `½ − (v−μ)m/(2a(1+m))` (clipped to [0,1]), so the margin needed for depletion probability `ε` is `q/(1−q)` with `q = a(1−2ε)/(v−μ)`. The twin's speed is the root of the quintic `2v⁵ − 3μv⁴ − 2v + μ = 0`. Base case (chosen, not measured): `μ = 0.3`, `a = 0.25`. Results, all from `experiments/results.txt`: the twin's cruise speed is nearly right (1.100 vs real optimum 1.123 `V0`; regret 0.10% of the optimal energy), but its energy claim is 3.5% low (Jensen factor 1.035) and a battery sized to that claim runs out in 50% of real flights (42% with a 5% margin); 5%-depletion needs a 39% margin, 1% needs 44%. The speed error only matters at large wind spread: regret is 0.8% of `J*` at `a = 0.4`, 6.4% at 0.6, 18% at 0.7, and the twin speed stalls in real wind (infinite energy) once `a > v_twin − μ = 0.80`. A mean-wind error in the twin of ±0.05 costs 0.4% (−) and ~0 (+, where it happens to offset the missing spread); logging `n` real winds and refitting (moment fit) beats the constant-wind twin's speed in 84% of fits at `n = 20` and 97% at `n = 50`. See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

```bash
cd research/uav-energy-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 7 tests, ~1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # ~20 s; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The UAV and field-robot simulation direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>) and the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>); no specific paper from either is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The rotary/fixed-wing power model is textbook (parasitic + induced power; e.g. Stevens, Lewis & Johnson 2016, *Aircraft Control and Simulation*, 3rd ed., Wiley); domain randomisation follows Tobin et al. 2017 (*IROS*, arXiv:1703.06907) and Peng et al. 2018 (*ICRA*, arXiv:1710.06537). Companion to the twin projects `grasp-twin`, `latency-twin`, `workcell-twin`, `targeted-observation-twin`.

Stylised: one leg, constant airspeed, wind constant within a flight and uniform across flights (no gusts, no vertical wind, no headwind/tailwind asymmetry within a round trip), a two-term power law with no battery-discharge nonlinearity, no reactive replanning or turn-back, and chosen (not measured) parameters.
