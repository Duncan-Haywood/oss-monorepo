How much battery should a UAV carry when its twin has a constant wind?

research/uav-energy-twin

Battery reserve of a UAV leg planned in a constant-wind twin: cruise speed nearly right (regret 0.1%) but the energy claim is low by exactly artanh(x)/x (3.5%) and a battery sized to it depletes in 50% of real flights; 5% depletion needs a 39% margin, and the twin speed stalls once wind spread exceeds v−μ.

7 tests

What it found

A leg flown at airspeed v against a headwind that is uniform across flights costs P(v)/(v−w) in energy per distance; a constant-wind twin has no spread. Its cruise speed is nearly right (1.100 vs the real optimum 1.123, regret 0.10%), but its energy claim is low by exactly artanh(x)/x with x = a/(v−μ) (3.5% here), and a battery sized to that claim runs out in 50% of real flights (42% with a 5% margin). A 5% depletion target needs a 39% margin and 1% needs 44%, in closed form, and the real-optimal speed trims only 39.2% to 37.6%. The speed error itself grows to 6.4% of the optimum at wind half-width 0.6, and the twin speed meets a stalling wind (infinite energy) once the half-width exceeds v−μ = 0.80. Refitting the twin to n = 50 logged winds beats the constant-wind speed in 97% of fits. Stylised: one leg, constant airspeed, wind fixed within a flight, chosen parameters, no gusts or turn-back.

More in Digital twins & sim-to-real

  • What does a too-clean odometry twin do to a SLAM loop-closure gate?A SLAM loop-closure gate tuned in a too-clean odometry twin: exact chi-square gate costs and closed-form optimal gate, real noise 2× the twin's rejects 38% of true closures (claimed 2%) at 4.4× the claimed cost, pessimistic twins are not safe either, and ~59 ground-truthed closures repair it.
  • What does an odometry twin that is too clean do to a loop-closure gate?A chi-square loop-closure gate tuned in a too-clean odometry twin: exact real error 2.1× the claim at drift-sd ratio 2, rejected genuine closures compound into lock-out (9–16× the ungated error, growing with horizon), the gate halves aliasing error when the twin is right but is 8× worse than no gate when it is not, and ~30 measured drifts make the repair safe.
  • How often should a mapping team rendezvous when its twin's odometry drift has no bias?How often a mapping team should rendezvous when its twin's odometry drift has no bias: real error is exactly √(1+ρ) times the claim, cost-optimal interval solves a cubic, twin regret 0.19–34.6%, shared bias cancels in the pairwise frame (5.3%→0.10%), and repair needs a long-lag ground-truth fit.
  • What does a simulator tuned on one-step odometry noise get wrong about drift, relocalisation and loop closure?A twin tuned on one-step odometry noise cannot see drift: variance ratio exactly 1+(T−1)ρ, relocalisation interval 3.4× too long (real miss 51.6% vs 5%), twin-tuned loop-closure gate recall 0.64 at T=200, closed-loop runaway, aliasing limits calibrated gates.
  • How wrong is a safety margin certified in a Gaussian-disturbance simulator when real gusts are heavy-tailed?A corridor-keeping safety margin certified in a Gaussian-disturbance twin: same variance, heavy-tailed real gusts give 0.29 real failure at a claimed 0.01, needed margin 7.3× at δ=0.001 (ν=3), variance inflation misses by 24×, conformal needs n≥1/δ, a Hill tail fit from one-step data repairs it.
  • How wrong is the thinnest obstacle a lidar twin certifies as detectable when real beam dropout is clustered?A lidar twin with i.i.d. beam dropout certifies the thinnest detectable obstacle: real clustered dropout (same rate, lag-1 correlation 0.6) misses 41× more at the certified width and the range claim is 4× too long (exact Markov miss law, p·q^(m−1)); a fitted persistence recovers the median width but needs a margin.
