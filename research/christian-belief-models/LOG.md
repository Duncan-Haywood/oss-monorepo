## 2026-11-09 · Milestone 3 (part): does fitting on the nearest years only fix the held-out-year bias? (predictions written before the run; outcome below)

- Why: the period-shift check (entry below) left the best variant, `hgb_shift` (3.38 points), at parity with interpolation (3.43), and the level shift did not close the gap on fast-moving items, which suggests the relation between other answers and the item changed, not only its level. `experiments/m3_local_window/run.py` fits the year-free logit and hgb only on training years within 5 or 10 years of the held-out year (fallback to all other years when the window has under 600 respondents or one class). Same 13 items, 307 item-years, 200 bootstrap draws and baselines. The predictions were written after the job started but before I opened any output.
- Predictions: (1) the best window variant has a lower mean absolute year-level error than plain hgb (3.87) by at least 10%; (2) it does not beat interpolation (3.43) by more than 0.3 points; (3) the 5-year window is better than the 10-year one on the fast-moving items (same-sex marriage, cohabitation) and worse on the slow ones (attendance, premarital sex), where less data costs more than drift; (4) at least 78% of year intervals contain zero for the best variant. Refuted if the best window variant is no better than plain hgb on mean error.
- **Outcome** (`experiments/m3_local_window/results/local_window.csv`; 13 items, 307 item-years, 200 bootstrap draws, about 15 min; the window fell back to all other years in 11 item-years at W = 5 and 5 at W = 10). Mean absolute year-level error in points: logit_w5 4.84, logit_w10 4.38, hgb_w5 3.44, hgb_w10 3.48, against plain hgb 3.87, hgb_shift 3.38, decade mean 3.54, interpolation 3.43. (1) **Held**: hgb_w5 cuts plain hgb error by 11%. (2) **Held**: it matches interpolation (3.44 vs 3.43; closer on 56% of item-years) and does not beat it by 0.3. (3) **Refuted**: the 5-year window is not systematically worse on slow items (attendance 2.5 vs 2.8 for W = 10; premarital sex 2.9 vs 2.6 is the one case that is) and ties on same-sex marriage (16.7 both). (4) **Refuted narrowly**: 77.5% of hgb_w5 year intervals contain zero (78% predicted); 76% for hgb_w10. Under the stated rule the window helps (3.44 < 3.87), so the hypothesis that period-local fitting helps is not refuted, but it only reaches parity with interpolation and the shift, as the level shift did.
- Reading: three different ways to make a model period-aware (a linear year term, a neighbour-fitted level shift, a local window) all stop at roughly 3.4 to 3.7 points, the same as carrying the neighbouring ballots' observed shares. A boosted model on other answers adds nothing about a wholly unseen ballot beyond what its neighbours already say. The exception is same-sex marriage, 16.7 points for both windows against 5.8 for interpolation: the item moved so fast that even a 5-year window straddles the break, and the logit windows are worse than hgb everywhere (4.4 to 4.8). So milestone 3's remaining question is no longer 'which period fix' but whether MICE or mixture imputation does better on items that were never asked; the held-out-year test cannot separate them from interpolation. Caveats: one seed; the bootstrap ignores GSS clustering; windows that are small fall back to all years (11 item-years), which blurs the W = 5 result at the ends of runs; same-sex marriage dominates the spread.

## 2026-11-08 · Milestone 3 (part): does a period term fix the held-out-year bias? (predictions written before the run; outcome below)

- Why: the held-out-year check (entry below) left hgb no better than carrying neighbouring years' shares, worst where opinion moved fast, since with `year` removed the model cannot learn a trend. `experiments/m3_period_shift/run.py` repeats it with three fixes: `logit_year` (linear year term), and `logit_shift` / `hgb_shift`, a logit offset fitted so the year-free model reproduces the observed share in the nearest earlier and later asked years, interpolated in year. Same items, years, bootstrap (200 draws) and baselines. The hgb neighbour fit is in-sample, so its offsets are optimistic (disclosed).
- Predictions: (1) the shift variants cut mean absolute year-level error of the plain models (hgb 3.9 points) by at least a quarter; (2) `logit_shift` or `hgb_shift` matches or beats interpolation (3.4) on mean error, but does not beat it by more than 0.5 points, since both lean on the same two neighbours; (3) `logit_year` helps the fast-moving items (same-sex marriage, cohabitation) but is worse than the shift on end-of-run years; (4) the share of year intervals containing zero rises from 69% to at least 80% for the best variant. Refuted if the best shift variant is no better than plain hgb on mean error.
- **Outcome** (`experiments/m3_period_shift/results/period_shift.csv`; same 13 items and 307 item-years, 200 bootstrap draws, about 20 min). Mean absolute year-level error in points: plain logit 4.06, plain hgb 3.87, `logit_year` 3.70, `logit_shift` 3.84, `hgb_shift` 3.38, decade mean 3.54, interpolation 3.43. (1) **Refuted**: the best shift cuts hgb error by 13%, not a quarter. (2) **Held, barely**: `hgb_shift` matches interpolation (3.38 vs 3.43; lower on 56% of item-years) and does not beat it by 0.5. (3) **Refuted**: `logit_year` is no better on the fast items (same-sex marriage 17.9 vs 17.3 for plain hgb, cohabitation 7.8 vs 6.9) and about equal to `hgb_shift` at the ends of runs (6.25 vs 6.34 points). (4) **Refuted**: 76% of `hgb_shift` year intervals contain zero (logit_year 74%, plain hgb 69%). Under the stated rule the shift is better than plain hgb on mean error, so the programme's hypothesis that a level shift helps is not refuted, but the gain is small.
- Reading: a level shift fitted on the neighbouring ballots buys a model parity with interpolation and no more, so imputing the share of a wholly unseen ballot gains nothing over carrying the neighbours' observed shares. Where opinion moved fast the shift does not close the gap: same-sex marriage stays at 15.6 points (interpolation 5.8) and same-sex relations 4.4 (2.9), which means the relation between a respondent's other answers and the item itself changed, not only the level. The next step is a period-by-feature interaction (or a model fitted on the nearest years only), and the MICE and mixture baselines. Caveats: one seed; the hgb neighbour offsets are in-sample and so optimistic, yet hgb_shift is still only at parity; the bootstrap ignores GSS clustering; interpolation needs asked neighbours that an item never asked does not have. The `m3_year_holdout/run.py` import of the milestone 1 module was changed to a path-based load so both scripts can be imported together; its results are unchanged.

## 2026-11-08 · Milestone 3 (part): does the imputed share match a ballot the model never saw? (predictions written before the run; outcome below)

- Why: milestone 3 asks whether imputed shares match the shares from ballots that asked the item. The out-of-fold check of 2026-11-07 still let the model see the same survey year. `experiments/m3_year_holdout/run.py` holds out a whole survey year for each Catholic item (at least 150 respondents asked), fits on all other years without `year` as a feature, and compares the weighted mean prediction with that year's observed share (200 respondent-bootstrap draws). Baselines for the same share: the mean of the other years in the decade, and linear interpolation between the nearest earlier and later asked years. Models: hgb and L2 logit. The predictions were written after the job started but before I opened any output.
- Predictions: (1) the median absolute year-level bias of hgb is under 3 points; (2) at least 80% of year intervals contain zero (wider than the 94% of the out-of-fold check, because a year shifts the level); (3) hgb has a lower mean absolute error than the decade mean on most item-years, but not than interpolation on the slow-moving items (attendance, premarital sex), where interpolation is hard to beat; (4) hgb is worst in years at the ends of an item's run (first and last year), where interpolation has only one neighbour.
- **Outcome** (`experiments/m3_year_holdout/results/year_holdout.csv`; 13 items, 307 item-years, 200 bootstrap draws, about 15 min). (1) **Held**: median absolute year-level bias of hgb is 2.8 points (mean 3.9; logit median 2.6, mean 4.1). (2) **Refuted**: only 69% of year intervals contain zero (80% predicted). (3) **Refuted**: hgb has a lower error than the decade mean in only 47% of item-years and than interpolation in 50%; mean absolute error is 3.9 points for hgb, 3.5 for the decade mean and 3.4 for interpolation. (4) **Held**: errors at the first and last year of an item's run are larger for both (hgb 6.9, interpolation 5.4 points) than in the interior (3.6 and 3.2).
- Reading: for the share of a whole unseen ballot, a model fitted on other years' answers is no better than simply carrying the neighbouring years' observed shares. It wins on certainty about God (1.9 vs 3.0 points for interpolation), and loses badly where opinion moved fast: opposing same-sex marriage is off by 17.3 points for hgb against 5.8 for interpolation, and cohabitation and same-sex relations by 6.7 to 6.9 against 2.9. With `year` removed the model cannot learn a secular trend, so its level is stuck at the pooled average; adding a period term, or fitting a shift on neighbouring years, is the obvious next step. The out-of-fold check (2026-11-07) looked better because the model saw the same year. Caveats: one seed, a bootstrap that ignores GSS clustering, the two baselines use the held-out items' own neighbouring ballots (not available for an item never asked), and no MICE or mixture baseline yet.

## 2026-11-07 · Milestone 2 (part): is the imputed share right in each decade and generation? (predictions written before the run; outcome below)

- Why: milestone 3 asks whether imputed shares match the shares from ballots that asked the item. Out-of-fold predictions are the same test in a cleaner form: every respondent's answer is predicted from a model that never saw them. `experiments/m2_calibration/run.py` compares the weighted mean out-of-fold hgb prediction with the weighted observed share in each decade x immigrant-generation cell (at least 100 respondents), with 200 bootstrap draws, and computes a weighted expected calibration error (10 equal-count bins) per Catholic target.
- Predictions: (1) the median absolute cell bias is under 2 points; (2) at least 90% of cell intervals contain zero; (3) the largest biases are in the first-generation cells (small, shifted levels); (4) ECE is under 0.03 on every target.
- **Outcome** (`experiments/m2_calibration/results/cell_bias.csv`, `ece.csv`; 13 targets, 180 decade x generation cells, seed 0, about 1 minute). (1) **Held**: median absolute cell bias 1.2 points. (2) **Held**: 170 of 180 intervals (94%) contain zero; at a 95% level about 9 would miss by chance, and 10 did. (3) **Half held**: the median bias is largest for first-generation cells (1.7 points, vs 1.3 second and 1.0 third-plus), but the two largest single misses are second-generation 1970s divorce-harder (-8.4 points, interval excludes zero) and first-generation 1980s sex-before-marriage (-6.7). (4) **Refuted for one item**: ECE is under 0.03 for 12 of 13 targets (0.006 to 0.026); cohabitation is 0.041 (n = 1,489, the smallest ballot), and same-sex marriage is 0.026 on 1,869.
- Reading: out-of-fold imputed shares match the observed shares within about 1 point in a typical decade x generation cell, and the cells that miss are as often second-generation as first. The two small-ballot items (cohabitation, same-sex marriage) are the least calibrated. Caveats: one fold seed; the cells' bootstrap ignores GSS clustering and strata; these are out-of-fold checks on asked respondents, not yet a comparison against a ballot that asked the item while the model never saw that ballot (milestone 3).

## 2026-11-07 · Milestone 2 (part): Hispanic vs non-Hispanic first-generation Catholics (predictions written before the run; outcome below)

- Why: H3 found that a model fitted on third-plus-generation Catholics ranks foreign-born Catholics almost as well as the pooled model. Foreign-born Catholics are mostly Hispanic, so the pooled result may hide a split. `experiments/m2_hispanic/run.py` fits hgb on generation-3 Catholics and scores first-generation Hispanic and non-Hispanic Catholics separately against the pooled out-of-fold predictions (seed 0, 200 bootstrap draws).
- Predictions: (1) the AUC loss is within 0.03 for both groups on most items; (2) the loss is larger for Hispanic than non-Hispanic immigrants on most items (the generation-3 training set is mostly non-Hispanic white, so shifted conditionals bite where it differs most); (3) level gaps exceed 5 points on more items for Hispanic immigrants. Refuted if the Hispanic loss is no larger than the non-Hispanic loss on at least half the items.
- **Outcome:** `experiments/m2_hispanic/results/hispanic_transfer.csv` (first-generation Catholics: 663 to 1,174 Hispanic and 208 to 493 non-Hispanic per item on 11 items; two small items, same-sex marriage and cohabitation, are scored for Hispanic immigrants only). Loss = pooled out-of-fold AUC minus generation-3-model AUC on the same rows. (1) **Held**: the loss is within 0.03 on every Hispanic item and on 10 of 11 non-Hispanic items; the exception is ever-divorced (0.057, interval −0.002 to 0.127). (2) **Not supported**: Hispanic loss is larger on 6 of 11 paired items, no systematic difference, and every paired interval overlaps zero except divorce-law for non-Hispanic immigrants, where the generation-3 model is *better* (−0.065). Under the stated rule (refuted if no larger on at least half), 6 of 11 does not refute it, but it is not a result either. (3) **Weakly held**: level gaps exceed 5 points on 6 of 13 Hispanic items and 4 of 11 non-Hispanic items. The large level misses are for Hispanic immigrants on certainty about God (−13 points), afterlife (+12.5), ever-divorced (+13) and divorce law (+8).
- A new observation, not predicted: absolute AUC is lower for Hispanic immigrants on the practice and doctrine items (weekly Mass 0.77 vs 0.87, daily prayer 0.71 vs 0.80, certainty about God 0.76 vs 0.82), whichever model is used. The other answers rank non-Hispanic immigrants better, so imputation is less accurate for the largest immigrant group even before any transfer.
- Reading: the pooled H3 result does not hide a Hispanic-specific transfer failure in ranking. Levels do move more for Hispanic immigrants on the God, afterlife and divorce items. One fold seed, 200 bootstrap draws, and the two groups differ in size, so intervals are wide. Still not done: the check of weighted shares against NORC's Data Explorer, calibration plots and design-based variance.

## 2026-11-03 · Milestone 1 results: other answers predict unasked items well; associations transfer, levels do not

- Ran `experiments/m1_imputation/run.py` on the GSS 1972–2024 R3a (fit and test about 30 min on 2 cores). Tables are in `experiments/m1_imputation/results/summary.md`, and `summarise.py` rebuilds them from the committed CSVs.
- A first full run crashed in the temporal step. Some items were never on the same ballot before 2014, which left an all-missing feature column. Such columns are now dropped per training split, and the whole run was redone.
- **Headline.** Across 49 tradition × item cells, a model given a respondent's other answers beats the decade prior by a median 0.21 AUC. Its log loss is lower in 44 of the 49 (paired 95% interval below zero).
  - Among Catholics, boosting reaches 0.86 for weekly Mass attendance, 0.85 for "sex before marriage always wrong", 0.84 for same-sex relations, 0.88 for opposing same-sex marriage, 0.82 for cohabitation, 0.75 for abortion, 0.69 for divorce law and 0.71 for ever-divorced.
- **H1: mostly supported.** The four sexual-morality and marriage-belief items clear 0.75. Adultery (0.73) does not: almost everyone (77%) says it is always wrong, so there is little to rank. Divorce law stays under 0.70 as predicted. Ever-divorced reaches 0.71 with boosting (logistic 0.67), which breaks the "< 0.70" half of H1 for that model.
- **H2: supported.** The median boosting − logistic gap is +0.003 AUC. Only 4 of 49 cells have an interval wholly above +0.01, and 3 of those are ever-divorced, where age and exposure act nonlinearly. Logistic regression is never worse by more than 0.01.
- **H3: supported.** A model fitted only on third-plus-generation Catholics ranks foreign-born Catholics within 0.03 AUC of the pooled model on 12 of 13 items (ever-divorced −0.038). Its levels are wrong by more than 5 points on 5 of 13:
  - It under-predicts certainty about God by 11 points and opposition to same-sex marriage by 8.
  - It over-predicts belief in an afterlife by 10, support for harder divorce by 7 and ever-divorced by 8.
  - So the association structure largely travels to immigrants, but group levels do not. An imputation model must carry generation (and later country of origin) as a level term.
- **H4: refuted.** Scored on Protestants, a Catholic-fitted model loses a mean 0.011 AUC on Evangelicals, 0.005 on Mainline and 0.007 on Black Protestants. Black Protestants are not the worst. Again levels are the problem: on Evangelicals the Catholic model under-predicts "sex before marriage always wrong" by 17 points and averages 6.7 points low.
- **Descriptive** (Catholics, weighted share, by decade and generation; `shares_catholic_decade_generation.csv`):
  - Weekly Mass attendance fell from 41–48% in the 1970s to 14–19% in the 2020s, in every generation.
  - In the 2020s, foreign-born Catholics are the most likely to oppose abortion for any reason (62% vs 47% third-plus generation) and to hold papal infallibility (56% vs 37%).
- **Orthodox Christians:** only 27–205 GSS respondents per item, below the 300 floor, so no models were fitted. Global and Orthodox coverage waits on EVS/WVS/ISSP access (milestone 4).
- **Not yet checked against a published figure.** This is milestone 2's first task: compare the weighted shares with NORC's GSS Data Explorer and with `research/catholic-demography` once it lands. Still to do: fold seeds (one seed so far), calibration plots, design-based (PSU and strata) variance.

## 2026-11-03 · Session 1, plan for milestone 1 (written before the run)

- Data: GSS 1972–2024 cumulative cross-section, release 3a (NORC; `data/download.sh`, checksums in `data/SHA256SUMS` match the copy `research/marriage-divorce-evidence` records). 75,699 respondents; Catholic (relig 2), Orthodox (relig 10) and the three Protestant traditions of Steensland et al. (`reltrad` 1–3).
- Targets: 12 binary items coded as the answer consistent with Catholic teaching (Catechism paragraph in `src/gss.py`) or a practice level, plus ever-divorced among the ever-married and, for Catholics, papal infallibility.
- The GSS asks many items on only some ballots, so most "missing" answers are missing by design (at random). That makes it a fair test of imputation: predict an item a respondent was not asked from the ones they were.
- Planned analysis: 5-fold weighted cross-validation within each tradition. Models are the decade prior, L2 logistic regression and gradient boosting. Metrics are weighted AUC, log loss and Brier, with paired respondent bootstrap intervals (300 draws). Then a temporal hold-out (train ≤2014, test 2016–2024), an immigrant-generation transfer, a cross-tradition transfer and permutation importance.
- **H1.** Other answers predict the sexual-morality items well beyond the prior (AUC ≥ 0.75 among Catholics). Divorce law and ever-divorced are much harder (AUC < 0.70). *Refuted if* sexual-morality AUCs are below 0.75, or divorce-law AUC reaches them.
- **H2.** Gradient boosting beats logistic regression by less than 0.01 AUC on most items, as flexibility did not help in `marriage-divorce-evidence`. *Refuted if* the bootstrap interval for the boosting minus logistic AUC gap lies above 0.01 on most items.
- **H3.** A model fitted on third-plus-generation Catholics ranks first-generation (foreign-born) Catholics almost as well as the pooled model does (AUC loss ≤ 0.03), but its level is off: the mean prediction misses the first-generation share by more than 5 points on at least a third of items. *Refuted if* the AUC loss exceeds 0.03 on most items, or levels transfer within 5 points everywhere.
- **H4.** A Catholic-fitted model ranks Evangelical and Mainline Protestants within 0.03 AUC of their own models. It ranks Black Protestants worse, because party and ideology carry less information about sexual morality there. *Refuted if* the Black Protestant loss is no larger than the other two.
