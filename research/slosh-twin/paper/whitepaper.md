# A rigid-body twin of a cart carrying liquid predicts no slosh: residual-vibration error, the price of a slosh-safe move, and the cost of a stale frequency calibration

## Question
Transporting liquid (a chemistry-lab workcell, a mobile manipulator with an open container) is often simulated with the payload as a rigid mass. The twin then plans the fastest move and certifies it. What does the real system do after that move, how much slower is the fastest move that keeps slosh inside a tolerance, and if the twin's slosh frequency was calibrated on one fill level, what happens at another?

## Model
Real system: a cart of mass `M` driven by force `F`, carrying liquid modelled as one damped spring-mass mode (mass `m`, stiffness `k = κ m`, damping ratio `ζ`); `M = 80 kg`, `m_full = 20 kg`, `ω_full = π rad/s` (period 2 s), fill `f = m/m_full`. Twin: a rigid mass `M + m`. The centre of mass obeys `(M+m) x_c'' = F` exactly, so the twin is right about the gross motion. The relative slosh `z = x_m − x_cart` obeys `z'' + 2ζω z' + ω² z = −μ a(t)` with `a = F/(M+m)`, `μ = (M+m)/M` and `ω² = k(1/M + 1/m) = κ(1 + m/M)`; the frequency therefore depends on fill only through the mass ratio. The twin predicts `z ≡ 0`. Move: rest-to-rest bang-bang, accelerate at `a₀ = 4D/T²` for `T/2` and brake for `T/2`; `D = 3 m`, `|a| ≤ 0.5 m/s²`, tolerance 1 cm on the residual amplitude `√(z² + (z'/ω)²)`. For the undamped mode the residual is `4 μ a₀ sin²(ωT/4)/ω²`; it vanishes at `T = 4πn/ω`, i.e. `n` whole periods per half-move. Slosh is propagated exactly (closed-form step for piecewise-constant force) and cross-checked by RK4 of the full two-body system. Pure Python, no dependencies.

## Results
(all numbers from `experiments/results.txt`)
1. **The twin is exact for the centre of mass and blind to slosh.** The RK4 centre-of-mass position equals the twin's `D` to ~1e-14 m. The closed form, exact propagation and RK4 agree for `T = 3…8 s`: 0.33774, 0, 0.10679, 0.16887, 0.06203, 0 m at `T = 3, 4, 4.9, 6, 7, 8 s`.
2. **The twin-planned minimum-time move.** `T_min = 2√(D/a_max) = 4.90 s` (2.45 slosh periods) leaves 0.1066 m residual (10.7× tolerance), peak slosh during the move 0.196 m; with damping ratio 0.02 / 0.05 the residual is 0.093 / 0.077 m. The cart's own position error at `T` is at most `m/(M+m)` of the amplitude (3.4 mm at `ζ = 0` here, depending on phase).
3. **Price of a slosh-safe move.** Scanning up from `T_min`, the fastest move with residual ≤ 1 cm takes 7.60 s at `ζ = 0` (7.58 s, 7.65 s at `ζ = 0.02, 0.05`; 11.45 s at `ζ = 0.10`), 55–56% slower than the twin's move, and 134% slower at `ζ = 0.10`. The first null (4 s) needs 0.75 m/s², above the cap; the second (8 s) is reachable, with window ±0.42 s at its acceleration. Light damping changes the fastest safe time little (I did not derive a damped closed form; these are numerical scans).
4. **Stale calibration at fixed distance.** Calibrating `ω` at a full tank and planning `T_n = 4πn/ω_model` at fill `f`: frequency error `ε = ω/ω_model − 1` is −1.0%, −2.5%, −5.1%, −7.8%, −9.5% at `f = 0.9, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1`. The residual is `4 μ a₀(T_n) sin²(πnε)/ω²` (exact propagation confirms, e.g. 0.009538 m at `f = 0.5`, `n = 2`). The smallest safe order is 2 (T = 8 s) down to `f = 0.5` (residual 9.5 mm, barely within tolerance) but 7 (T = 28 s) at `f = 0.25` and 0.1, versus 8.68 s and 8.83 s with `ω` recalibrated, 3.2× slower. The tuning-free bound `T ≥ 4√(μD/tol)/ω` (envelope of `sin² ≤ 1`) gives 24.7 s at every fill, so below `f ≈ 0.5` ignoring the calibration and using the envelope beats the stale null.
5. **Higher orders are not more fragile at fixed distance.** The acceleration `4D/T_n²` falls as `1/n²`, which outweighs the growth of the phase error `πnε`: the residual at `n = 8` is smaller than at `n = 1`. At fixed acceleration (distance growing with `n`) the picture reverses: the first unsafe order is exactly `⌊asin(√ρ)/(π|ε|)⌋ + 1` with `ρ = tol·ω²/(4μa₀)`: 7, 3, 2, 1 for `f = 0.9, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25` at `a₀ = 0.5`. This is verified against the closed form, not against independent data.

## Limitations
One linear slosh mode; real slosh is nonlinear at large amplitude (swirl, wall impact, amplitude-dependent frequency) and multi-modal. Force is bang-bang with an ideal actuator; I did not optimise an input shaper (ZV/ZVD, Singer & Seering 1990) or a smooth profile, which would be faster than the nulls found here. Fill dependence assumes `k ∝ m`, a pendulum-like assumption; a real tank's frequency depends on depth and geometry and this is not modelled. The tolerance is on the modal amplitude, not on an actual spill criterion. The "real" tank is a model, not a measured one; no numbers here transfer to hardware.

## Next steps
