# Smooth twin: how much smoothing does a differentiable twin of a hard contact event need, and what does it cost?

Pure Python, no dependencies. A one-parameter contact task (approach pose `θ`): the real reward is `R·1{θ≥0} − c(θ+m)²` (a hard success edge at 0, a quadratic cost for leaving the nominal pose `−m`; normalised `R=c=1, m=0.5`, real optimum `θ=0`, value 0.75). The real gradient at the nominal pose is exactly zero, so ascent on the real plant never moves (regret 0.75). A differentiable twin replaces the edge by a logistic of width `τ`. Results: (i) the twin optimum overshoots the edge, `θ̂ ≈ τ·ln(R/(2cmτ))` for small `τ` (within 5% at `τ=0.02`, verified against a brute-force grid), giving real regret 0.08 at `τ=0.02`; (ii) there is a two-sided trainable window: below `τ_c=0.105` the twin objective has a second local maximum at the nominal pose and gradient ascent from it is trapped (regret 0.75 for `τ`=0.02/0.05/0.10), and above `τ_u=R/(8cm)=0.25` (closed form, checked) the twin optimum falls below the real edge and the real reward is never collected (regret 0.98 at `τ=0.26`); (iii) annealing `τ` from 0.2 with warm starts crosses the trap and reaches the small-`τ` optimum (regret 0.148/0.080/0.054 at `τ`=0.05/0.02/0.01), which fixed-`τ` ascent cannot; without a twin, exploring the real plant needs 161 or 3.5 million evaluations at perturbation std 0.2 or 0.1; (iv) when the real edge is uncertain around the twin's, `θ̂(τ)` acts as a safety margin: the best trainable `τ` matches the Bayes-best fixed pose within 0.0001–0.0014 expected regret for edge-offset std 0.02–0.2, but the ceiling on reachable margins is `max_τ θ̂ = 0.168`, and annealing all the way to `τ→0` discards the margin (edge std 0.1: 0.298 vs 0.229 at the optimum). See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

**Builds on.** The differentiable-simulation and sim-to-real-for-manipulation directions of the HIRO Group (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>) and the contact-rich learning-from-simulation setting of the CAIRO Lab (<https://cairo-lab.com/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The method is classical: logistic smoothing of a step as a relaxation (Bengio et al. 2009 on curriculum and continuation; Mordatch et al. 2012 on smoothed contact for optimisation; Suh et al. 2022 on the bias and variance of smoothed contact gradients), and elementary calculus for the exact thresholds. Companion to `grasp-twin` (twin friction model), `randomized-twin` (domain randomization) and `twin-transfer` (LQ sim-to-real regret) in this repository.

Stylised: one parameter, one edge, a logistic smoother, deterministic gradient ascent (no stochastic gradients, no learned policy), a simulated "real" plant, no robot data. MIT.
