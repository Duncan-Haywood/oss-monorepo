## 2026-11-14 · Step 3b plan (a noise-aware gate: Mondrian bins on a blind SNR estimate, and a threshold-theory score), written before the run
- **Why now.** Step 3 found that a Learn-then-Test gate calibrated on pooled conditions holds across rooms and phones but not when the noise level shifts (2 s, gamma 0.25, calibrated without -10 dB windows: 4.65% and 7.25% wrong among accepted against a 1% target). A deployed gate never sees the added-noise label, so the fix has to use something the window can measure. Two candidates: condition on a blind noise estimate (Mondrian calibration, with refusal outside the calibration's support), or replace PSR by a score that already models noise, the step 2a threshold theory evaluated blind.
- **Method, fixed in advance by `experiments/gate/noise.py` and `src/capture_sync/gate.py`.** Step 1's windows replayed exactly (same sessions, seeds, times, prior errors, added noise) for gamma 1 and 0.25. Per window: error, PSR, `p_blind` (Ianniello cells model from Welch spectra of the two windows aligned at the window's own estimate, span 2.93 s) and `snr_hat` (10 log10 of the 90th over 10th percentile of 32 ms frame energies, the smaller of the two windows). Gates, all LTT at alpha 1%, delta 0.1: *pooled* (step 3), *mondrian* (one PSR threshold per calibration quartile of `snr_hat`; windows below the calibration's 1st percentile of `snr_hat` refused), *theory* (accept when `p_blind` <= t, t on a geometric grid of 241 values from 0.5 to 1e-6). Shifts S1 to S4 as step 3, S1 with step 3's splits.
- **Seen before writing this.** Step 3's results; one 2-trial smoke run of one 200 ms session (240 windows, to time it and check the columns). Nothing else from this run.
- **Predictions (to be reported held or failed).** (a) Replay: per-cell raw failure shares for gamma 1 and 0.25 equal step 3's `summary.json` replay to the digit. (b) S4, 2 s, gamma 0.25: mondrian keeps failure among accepted at or under 2% in both rooms. (c) It does so mostly by refusing: mondrian keeps under half of the -10 dB windows in both rooms (the -10 dB windows fall below the support floor). (d) S4, 2 s, gamma 0.25: the theory gate stays at or under 2% in both rooms while keeping at least half of the -10 dB windows. (e) S2 200 to 800, 2 s, gamma 0.25, -10 dB test: mondrian and theory each at or under 2% (pooled was 3.38%). (f) In distribution (S1, 2 s, gamma 0.25), mondrian keeps at least 90% as many windows as pooled in both rooms. (g) Exploratory, a direction only: on the real phones `p_blind` flags failures better than PSR (AUROC) for PHAT at 2 s, as on speech in step 2a (0.85 against 0.74).

## 2026-11-14 · Step 3 outcome: the gate's guarantee survives room and phone shifts on average but not a noise shift (2 s, gamma 0.25: 4.7% and 7.3% wrong among accepted against a 1% target)
- **Ran as planned, with one fix to the gate after a first scoring pass.** 96,000 windows collected in 33 min on 4 cores. The first scoring pass abstained on every cell: walking from the strictest threshold, the first thresholds accept a handful of windows, where even zero failures cannot reject H0, so fixed-sequence testing stopped at once. Fixed by skipping thresholds that accept fewer than 230 windows (where zero failures first reject at alpha 1%, delta 0.1). The skip depends on scores only, not labels. The only outputs seen before the fix were "abstain everywhere" and the plug-in rule's numbers, which the fix does not touch. Tests added for both.
- **(a) Replay: held.** All 120 step 1 failure shares reproduced to the digit.
- **(b) S1 within-room splits: held**, with a caveat. Over windows and gates, 13.2% of splits had more than 1% wrong among accepted (bar 15%), against 33.1% for the plug-in rule. That is above the i.i.d. bound of 10% (gamma 0.25 alone: 19.8%), because windows cluster by session. No split went over 2%.
- **(c) S2 room shift, 2 s, gamma 0.25: held.** Pooled over test noise, 1.45% (200 to 800) and 0.57% (800 to 200); at -10 dB, 3.38% (200 to 800), over 2%.
- **(d) S4 noise shift, 2 s, gamma 0.25: held.** Calibrated without -10 dB windows: 4.65% (200 ms room) and 7.25% (800 ms room) wrong among accepted. The calibrated threshold sits at the grid floor, so the gate accepts every window, because on cleaner audio nearly all windows are right.
- **(e) S3 leave one phone out, 2 s, gamma 0.25: held**, 5/5 at or under 2% (worst Xiaomi 1.30%, the only one over 1%).
- **(f) Agree keeps more than PSR under S2 at 2 s: failed.** It keeps fewer (88% and 92% against 98% and 97%) and is wrong less often (0.70% and 0.53% against 1.45% and 0.57%).
- **Post hoc, not predicted:** at 2 s the agree gate's threshold is the grid floor in every split, so it is effectively "accept when PHAT and gamma 0.25 agree". Under the noise shift it cuts wrong-among-accepted from 5.0% to 1.43% (200 ms room) and from 7.25% to 2.57% (800 ms room) at 84% and 68% kept. At 1 s it does not help: 4.7% and 11.3%.
- **What it changes.** A gate calibrated on a pool of conditions controls the pool, not each condition. Rooms and phones are not where it breaks; the noise level, which a deployed gate does not observe, is. Next: calibrate per estimated window SNR (Mondrian, on a noise estimate the window can measure), and make the calibration session-level so S1 meets its own bound.

## 2026-11-14 · Step 3 plan (a Learn-then-Test gate and where its guarantee breaks), written before the run
- **Why now.** Milestone 3. Step 1's PSR gate used a plug-in threshold with no guarantee, and it broke from the 200 ms to the 800 ms room at -10 dB (3.7%). A conformal threshold gives a finite-sample bound when calibration and test are exchangeable; this step measures where that bound holds on the real phones and where it fails.
- **Method, fixed in advance by `experiments/gate/run.py` and `src/capture_sync/gate.py`.** Learn then Test (Angelopoulos et al. 2021): on a fixed geometric grid of 241 PSR thresholds (2 to 400), binomial p-values for H0 "failure among accepted > 1%", fixed-sequence from the strictest, delta 0.1. Data: step 1's windows replayed exactly (same seeds), with each window's error and PSR kept. Gates: PSR of PHAT, PSR of gamma 0.25, and "agree" (gamma 0.25's PSR when PHAT and gamma 0.25 land within a frame of each other, else refused). Shifts: S1 random 5/5 session splits within a room (50 per room); S2 room to room; S3 leave one phone out; S4 as recorded + 0 dB to -10 dB. Noise levels pooled in calibration except in S4. Step 1's plug-in rule on the same grid is reported beside it.
- **Seen before writing this.** Step 1's aggregate results only. Nothing from this run; the gate code was tested on synthetic data only.
- **Predictions (to be reported held or failed).** (a) Replay: the raw failure shares equal step 1's cells. (b) S1: averaged over windows and gates, the share of splits whose test failure among accepted exceeds 1% is at most 0.15 for LTT (bound 0.10 for i.i.d. windows; sessions are clusters), and higher for the plug-in rule. (c) S2, 2 s, gamma 0.25: pooled over test noise, failure among accepted stays at or under 2% in both directions; at -10 dB test noise it exceeds 2% in at least one direction. (d) S4, 2 s, gamma 0.25: calibrating without -10 dB windows and testing on them gives failure among accepted above 2% in both rooms. (e) S3, 2 s, gamma 0.25: at or under 2% for at least 4 of the 5 held-out phones. (f) Exploratory, a direction only: at 2 s the "agree" gate keeps more windows than PSR of gamma 0.25 alone under S2 in both directions.

- **(b) H4 on speech: held.** 137 of 171 scored cells (80%) agree under the cells model, 143/171 under Rice's. The prediction runs low (median 0.58 to 0.74). The misses sit at high SNR and short windows (0 dB, 0.5 s: 22 of 22 underpredicted by more than x2), while 82 of 83 cells at -15 dB agree. The expected room pattern **failed**: dry agreed least (29/43), the 0.8 s room most (55/65).
- **(c) Raw PHAT beyond x2 of the prediction in most cells at -10 dB or below: failed** (20 of 41 speech cells, 1 of 9 Gaussian). Raw-bin weighting does cost PHAT: its mean miss rate over the speech cells is 0.397 against 0.326 for smoothed PHAT, and 0.252 against 0.056 at -10 dB, 2 s, white noise, RT60 0.8 s. At gamma 0 raw and smooth are identical, as they should be.
- **(d) Exploratory:** for PHAT, the theory probability from spectra aligned at the window's own estimate beats PSR as a failure flag in 12/12 scored 2 s speech cells (median AUROC 0.85 against 0.74). For gamma 0.25 it does not (0.567 against 0.578).

Step 1's "partial whitening, not PHAT" is therefore a white-noise result. Speech-shaped noise needs whitening.
- **What it changes.** The gross-error rate is roughly predictable from a window's spectra once noise dominates, and the model gives PHAT a better confidence than PSR. The weighting default must depend on the noise spectrum (milestone 3's gate should see it). Step 2b, the LibriWASN run, is ready in `experiments/threshold/libriwasn.py`, pending a session that can reach zenodo.org.

## 2026-11-13 · Step 2a plan (threshold theory against Monte Carlo and real speech), written before the full run
- **Why now, and why not LibriWASN.** Milestone 2 is H4: does Ianniello's (1982) gross-error model, fed with each window's measured spectra, predict how often a window lands on a wrong lag? This session's egress proxy refuses zenodo.org (403 on CONNECT; step 1's session could reach it), so the real-device recordings are out of reach. What is reachable: github.com, so MS-SNSD (Reddy et al. 2019, MIT): 37 min of real read speech (`clean_test`, 190 files) and real recorded babble (`noise_test/Babble_*`). This step therefore checks the theory where the answer is known (Gaussian signals) and on real speech and real babble with simulated delays and statistical rooms; the LibriWASN check (H4 proper) is step 2b.
- **Model** (`src/capture_sync/threshold.py`). Correlator weight W = (Gxx Gyy)^(-gamma/2) from smoothed spectra; mean output at the true lag mu = max over +-1 ms of the integral of W Re(Gxy e^{j2 pi f tau}); spreads sigma1^2 = (1/2L) int W^2 Gxx Gyy and sigma0^2 = (1/2L) int W^2 (Gxx Gyy + Re Gxy^2) (Knapp and Carter 1976); largest wrong-lag output over 2.93 s of lag from M = 2 B_eff T independent cells (Ianniello) or from Rice's upcrossing rate. Spectra: Welch, 512-sample non-overlapping Hann segments, of the two windows aligned at the true lag. Estimators: the smooth-weight GCC the model describes (`gcc_fixed`) and step 1's GCC, which divides each bin by its own raw |X Y*|^gamma.
- **Grid.** Gaussian white source, dry, white noise at -10/-15/-20 dB; real speech in dry, RT60 0.2 s and 0.8 s statistical rooms (a fresh impulse response per device and trial, DRR 0 dB), white or real babble noise at 0/-10/-15 dB. Windows 0.5/1/2/4 s; gamma 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0; 250 trials per cell; prior +-1 s, search +-1.5 s, failure more than 33.3 ms off, as step 1. A cell is scored only if it has at least 10 observed failures; "agrees" means predicted/observed in [0.5, 2].
- **Seen before writing this.** One 60-trial probe per cell of Gaussian white, dry, gamma 0/0.5/1, L 0.5 to 2 s, -10 and -15 dB (the smooth-weight GCC failed 0.05 to 0.98; the theory said 0.16 to 0.81; raw PHAT failed 0.42 where the smooth one failed 0.08), and a 3-trial smoke run of the whole grid. Nothing else.
- **Predictions (to be reported held or failed).** (a) Gaussian, smooth-weight GCC, cells model: at least 90% of scored cells agree, for every gamma (the implementation check). (b) H4 on speech, smooth-weight GCC, cells model: at least 75% of scored speech cells agree overall; I expect the dry cells to agree more often than the RT60 0.8 s cells, where reflections give the wrong-lag process structure the Gaussian model lacks. (c) Step 1's raw-bin GCC at gamma 1 (PHAT) fails more than twice the prediction in most scored cells at -10 dB and below, i.e. the raw-bin normalisation, not the smoothed weighting, is what makes PHAT worst; at gamma 0 raw and smooth are the same estimator and agree. (d) Exploratory, no prediction: whether the theory's probability computed blind (spectra aligned at the window's own estimate) flags failures better than the PSR of step 1 (AUROC).

## 2026-11-13 · Step 1 plan (real-device benchmark on LibriWASN phones), written before the full run
- **Why.** Every earlier `capture-*sync*` result came from a simulated delay on one recording, with simulated drift and (but for `capture-reverbsync`) no room. LibriWASN has five phones recording the same playback on their own clocks in two real rooms (about 200 ms and 800 ms reverberation), so delay, drift and reverberation are all real.
- **Seen before writing this.** One probe of session 0 of the 200 ms room with 3 window times per pair, recordings as published (no added noise): the ten reference lines had all or nearly all 20 s windows within 5 ms of the line, triple closure was 0.6 ms median and 1.3 ms max, clock-rate offsets were -16 to +15 ppm, and the published files start within 10 ms of each other. On that probe no 1 s to 4 s window failed. Nothing else.
- **Design.** Reference per pair: 20 s windows every 10 s, PHAT, Theil-Sen line; usable if at least half the windows sit within 5 ms of the line. Short windows of 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 s at 40 seeded random times per pair and session; the other phone's stretch is cut around the reference delay plus a prior error uniform in +-1 s (the published files are pre-aligned, so the hand-start offset is simulated) and searched +-1.5 s. Noise: as recorded, and independent white noise added per phone at 0 dB and -10 dB window SNR (the reverberation and clocks are real; the noise is not). Weightings gamma 1 (PHAT), 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0. Failure: more than 33.3 ms from the reference. Intervals: bootstrap over the 20 sessions (10 per room).
- **Predictions (to be reported held or failed).** (a) H1: at -10 dB, 2 s windows fail more often in the 800 ms room than in the 200 ms room, for PHAT and for gamma 0.25. (b) H2: at 0 dB and -10 dB, gamma 0.25 fails less than PHAT for 2 s windows in both rooms. (c) H3: a PSR threshold set in one room (accepted windows fail at most 1%, noise levels pooled) keeps accepted failures at most 2% in the other, for 2 s windows and gamma 0.25. (d) As recorded, 2 s windows fail under 1% in both rooms for every gamma.
