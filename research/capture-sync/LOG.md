## 2026-11-13 · Step 2a outcome: the threshold model predicts real-speech miss rates within x2 in 80% of cells, underpredicting; raw-bin PHAT adds misses; in babble PHAT wins
- **Ran as planned**, with one change before any result was seen: the first full run was stopped after 22 min because `np.convolve` with 0.8 s impulse responses would have taken another hour. It was rerun with `scipy.signal.fftconvolve`, with the same seeds and the same maths. 84,000 windows, 4 gammas each, about 15 min on 4 cores.
- **(a) Gaussian implementation check: failed narrowly.** 8 of 9 scored cells agree for every gamma (89%, the bar was 90%). The one miss is the same cell for all four gammas (-10 dB, 0.5 s), where the prediction is 0.15 to 0.17 against 0.07 to 0.08 observed. Median predicted/observed is 0.84.
- **(b) H4 on speech: held.** 137 of 171 scored cells (80%) agree under the cells model, 143/171 under Rice's. The prediction runs low (median 0.58 to 0.74). The misses sit at high SNR and short windows (0 dB, 0.5 s: 22 of 22 underpredicted by more than x2), while 82 of 83 cells at -15 dB agree. The expected room pattern **failed**: dry agreed least (29/43), the 0.8 s room most (55/65).
- **(c) Raw PHAT beyond x2 of the prediction in most cells at -10 dB or below: failed** (20 of 41 speech cells, 1 of 9 Gaussian). Raw-bin weighting does cost PHAT: its mean miss rate over the speech cells is 0.397 against 0.326 for smoothed PHAT, and 0.252 against 0.056 at -10 dB, 2 s, white noise, RT60 0.8 s. At gamma 0 raw and smooth are identical, as they should be.
- **(d) Exploratory:** for PHAT, the theory probability from spectra aligned at the window's own estimate beats PSR as a failure flag in 12/12 scored 2 s speech cells (median AUROC 0.85 against 0.74). For gamma 0.25 it does not (0.567 against 0.578).
- **Post hoc, not predicted:** with real babble (independent per device) the weighting ranking flips. PHAT is best and plain correlation worst (RT60 0.8 s, -10 dB, 2 s: raw PHAT 0.50, gamma 0.25 0.70, plain 0.79, smoothed PHAT 0.31). Step 1's "partial whitening, not PHAT" is therefore a white-noise result. Speech-shaped noise needs whitening.
- **What it changes.** The gross-error rate is roughly predictable from a window's spectra once noise dominates, and the model gives PHAT a better confidence than PSR. The weighting default must depend on the noise spectrum (milestone 3's gate should see it). Step 2b, the LibriWASN run, is ready in `experiments/threshold/libriwasn.py`, pending a session that can reach zenodo.org.

## 2026-11-13 · Step 2a plan (threshold theory against Monte Carlo and real speech), written before the full run
- **Why now, and why not LibriWASN.** Milestone 2 is H4: does Ianniello's (1982) gross-error model, fed with each window's measured spectra, predict how often a window lands on a wrong lag? This session's egress proxy refuses zenodo.org (403 on CONNECT; step 1's session could reach it), so the real-device recordings are out of reach. What is reachable: github.com, so MS-SNSD (Reddy et al. 2019, MIT): 37 min of real read speech (`clean_test`, 190 files) and real recorded babble (`noise_test/Babble_*`). This step therefore checks the theory where the answer is known (Gaussian signals) and on real speech and real babble with simulated delays and statistical rooms; the LibriWASN check (H4 proper) is step 2b.
- **Model** (`src/capture_sync/threshold.py`). Correlator weight W = (Gxx Gyy)^(-gamma/2) from smoothed spectra; mean output at the true lag mu = max over +-1 ms of the integral of W Re(Gxy e^{j2 pi f tau}); spreads sigma1^2 = (1/2L) int W^2 Gxx Gyy and sigma0^2 = (1/2L) int W^2 (Gxx Gyy + Re Gxy^2) (Knapp and Carter 1976); largest wrong-lag output over 2.93 s of lag from M = 2 B_eff T independent cells (Ianniello) or from Rice's upcrossing rate. Spectra: Welch, 512-sample non-overlapping Hann segments, of the two windows aligned at the true lag. Estimators: the smooth-weight GCC the model describes (`gcc_fixed`) and step 1's GCC, which divides each bin by its own raw |X Y*|^gamma.
- **Grid.** Gaussian white source, dry, white noise at -10/-15/-20 dB; real speech in dry, RT60 0.2 s and 0.8 s statistical rooms (a fresh impulse response per device and trial, DRR 0 dB), white or real babble noise at 0/-10/-15 dB. Windows 0.5/1/2/4 s; gamma 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0; 250 trials per cell; prior +-1 s, search +-1.5 s, failure more than 33.3 ms off, as step 1. A cell is scored only if it has at least 10 observed failures; "agrees" means predicted/observed in [0.5, 2].
- **Seen before writing this.** One 60-trial probe per cell of Gaussian white, dry, gamma 0/0.5/1, L 0.5 to 2 s, -10 and -15 dB (the smooth-weight GCC failed 0.05 to 0.98; the theory said 0.16 to 0.81; raw PHAT failed 0.42 where the smooth one failed 0.08), and a 3-trial smoke run of the whole grid. Nothing else.
- **Predictions (to be reported held or failed).** (a) Gaussian, smooth-weight GCC, cells model: at least 90% of scored cells agree, for every gamma (the implementation check). (b) H4 on speech, smooth-weight GCC, cells model: at least 75% of scored speech cells agree overall; I expect the dry cells to agree more often than the RT60 0.8 s cells, where reflections give the wrong-lag process structure the Gaussian model lacks. (c) Step 1's raw-bin GCC at gamma 1 (PHAT) fails more than twice the prediction in most scored cells at -10 dB and below, i.e. the raw-bin normalisation, not the smoothed weighting, is what makes PHAT worst; at gamma 0 raw and smooth are the same estimator and agree. (d) Exploratory, no prediction: whether the theory's probability computed blind (spectra aligned at the window's own estimate) flags failures better than the PSR of step 1 (AUROC).

## 2026-11-13 · Step 1 outcome: PHAT is the worst weighting in real rooms with noise; phone clocks differ by up to 19 ppm; a PSR gate transfers between rooms on average but not at -10 dB
- **Ran as planned**, 20 sessions x 10 pairs x 40 times x 3 noise levels x 4 windows x 5 weightings = 480,000 estimates; no code changed after the run. All 200 references usable (at least 97% of 20 s windows within 5 ms of the line); triple closure median 0.5 ms (200 ms room) and 0.3 ms (800 ms room), max 1.8 ms.
- **Clocks.** |SRO| median 13.7 ppm, max 19.1 ppm; each pair's rate repeats within about 1 ppm across sessions and rooms. The Nexus and Xiaomi sit 14 to 18 ppm off the three Pixels.
- **Predictions.** (a) H1 at -10 dB, 2 s: **held** (PHAT 0.336 vs 0.169; gamma 0.25 0.072 vs 0.050, intervals touching). At 4 s the rooms are level. (b) H2: **held** in all four cells (e.g. -10 dB, 800 ms room: PHAT 0.336, gamma 0.25 0.072). (c) H3: **held pooled** over noise levels (1.6% and 0.6% among accepted), **failed at -10 dB** from the 200 ms to the 800 ms room (3.7%; PHAT 5.5%). (d) **Failed**: as recorded, PHAT fails 3.3% of 2 s windows in the 200 ms room.
- **Post-hoc, labelled as such.** I expected (d)'s failures to come from one pair; `bypair.py` (4 s, 200 windows per pair) shows them spread over all ten pairs (PHAT 1.5% to 5.5%) at scattered lags, and gamma 0.25 removes nearly all of them. Cause open.
- **What it changes.** For the programme: partial whitening, not PHAT, is the default from here; the gate needs a room-aware calibration (or a conformal bound with a stated exchangeability failure), which is milestone 3. For earlier projects: the &plusmn;40 ppm assumption of `capture-syncgate`, `capture-syncslope` and friends is about twice what these phones show.

## 2026-11-13 · Step 1 plan (real-device benchmark on LibriWASN phones), written before the full run
- **Why.** Every earlier `capture-*sync*` result came from a simulated delay on one recording, with simulated drift and (but for `capture-reverbsync`) no room. LibriWASN has five phones recording the same playback on their own clocks in two real rooms (about 200 ms and 800 ms reverberation), so delay, drift and reverberation are all real.
- **Seen before writing this.** One probe of session 0 of the 200 ms room with 3 window times per pair, recordings as published (no added noise): the ten reference lines had all or nearly all 20 s windows within 5 ms of the line, triple closure was 0.6 ms median and 1.3 ms max, clock-rate offsets were -16 to +15 ppm, and the published files start within 10 ms of each other. On that probe no 1 s to 4 s window failed. Nothing else.
- **Design.** Reference per pair: 20 s windows every 10 s, PHAT, Theil-Sen line; usable if at least half the windows sit within 5 ms of the line. Short windows of 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 s at 40 seeded random times per pair and session; the other phone's stretch is cut around the reference delay plus a prior error uniform in +-1 s (the published files are pre-aligned, so the hand-start offset is simulated) and searched +-1.5 s. Noise: as recorded, and independent white noise added per phone at 0 dB and -10 dB window SNR (the reverberation and clocks are real; the noise is not). Weightings gamma 1 (PHAT), 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0. Failure: more than 33.3 ms from the reference. Intervals: bootstrap over the 20 sessions (10 per room).
- **Predictions (to be reported held or failed).** (a) H1: at -10 dB, 2 s windows fail more often in the 800 ms room than in the 200 ms room, for PHAT and for gamma 0.25. (b) H2: at 0 dB and -10 dB, gamma 0.25 fails less than PHAT for 2 s windows in both rooms. (c) H3: a PSR threshold set in one room (accepted windows fail at most 1%, noise levels pooled) keeps accepted failures at most 2% in the other, for 2 s windows and gamma 0.25. (d) As recorded, 2 s windows fail under 1% in both rooms for every gamma.
