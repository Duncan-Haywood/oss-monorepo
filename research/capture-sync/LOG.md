# capture-sync research log

## 2026-11-13 · Step 1 outcome: PHAT is the worst weighting in real rooms with noise; phone clocks differ by up to 19 ppm; a PSR gate transfers between rooms on average but not at -10 dB
- **Ran as planned**, 20 sessions x 10 pairs x 40 times x 3 noise levels x 4 windows x 5 weightings = 480,000 estimates; no code changed after the run. All 200 references usable (at least 97% of 20 s windows within 5 ms of the line); triple closure median 0.5 ms (200 ms room) and 0.3 ms (800 ms room), max 1.8 ms.
- **Clocks.** |SRO| median 13.7 ppm, max 19.1 ppm; each pair's rate repeats within about 1 ppm across sessions and rooms. The Nexus and Xiaomi sit 14 to 18 ppm off the three Pixels.
- **Predictions.** (a) H1 at -10 dB, 2 s: **held** (PHAT 0.336 vs 0.169; gamma 0.25 0.072 vs 0.050, intervals touching). At 4 s the rooms are level. (b) H2: **held** in all four cells (e.g. -10 dB, 800 ms room: PHAT 0.336, gamma 0.25 0.072). (c) H3: **held pooled** over noise levels (1.6% and 0.6% among accepted), **failed at -10 dB** from the 200 ms to the 800 ms room (3.7%; PHAT 5.5%). (d) **Failed**: as recorded, PHAT fails 3.3% of 2 s windows in the 200 ms room.
- **Post-hoc, labelled as such.** I expected (d)'s failures to come from one pair; `bypair.py` (4 s, 200 windows per pair) shows them spread over all ten pairs (PHAT 1.5% to 5.5%) at scattered lags, and gamma 0.25 removes nearly all of them. Cause open.
- **What it changes.** For the programme: partial whitening, not PHAT, is the default from here; the gate needs a room-aware calibration (or a conformal bound with a stated exchangeability failure), which is milestone 3. For earlier projects: the &plusmn;40 ppm assumption of `capture-syncgate`, `capture-syncslope` and friends is about twice what these phones show.

## 2026-11-13 · Step 1 plan (real-device benchmark on LibriWASN phones), written before the full run
- **Why.** Every earlier `capture-*sync*` result came from a simulated delay on one recording, with simulated drift and (but for `capture-reverbsync`) no room. LibriWASN has five phones recording the same playback on their own clocks in two real rooms (about 200 ms and 800 ms reverberation), so delay, drift and reverberation are all real.
- **Seen before writing this.** One probe of session 0 of the 200 ms room with 3 window times per pair, recordings as published (no added noise): the ten reference lines had all or nearly all 20 s windows within 5 ms of the line, triple closure was 0.6 ms median and 1.3 ms max, clock-rate offsets were -16 to +15 ppm, and the published files start within 10 ms of each other. On that probe no 1 s to 4 s window failed. Nothing else.
- **Design.** Reference per pair: 20 s windows every 10 s, PHAT, Theil-Sen line; usable if at least half the windows sit within 5 ms of the line. Short windows of 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 s at 40 seeded random times per pair and session; the other phone's stretch is cut around the reference delay plus a prior error uniform in +-1 s (the published files are pre-aligned, so the hand-start offset is simulated) and searched +-1.5 s. Noise: as recorded, and independent white noise added per phone at 0 dB and -10 dB window SNR (the reverberation and clocks are real; the noise is not). Weightings gamma 1 (PHAT), 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0. Failure: more than 33.3 ms from the reference. Intervals: bootstrap over the 20 sessions (10 per room).
- **Predictions (to be reported held or failed).** (a) H1: at -10 dB, 2 s windows fail more often in the 800 ms room than in the 200 ms room, for PHAT and for gamma 0.25. (b) H2: at 0 dB and -10 dB, gamma 0.25 fails less than PHAT for 2 s windows in both rooms. (c) H3: a PSR threshold set in one room (accepted windows fail at most 1%, noise levels pooled) keeps accepted failures at most 2% in the other, for 2 s windows and gamma 0.25. (d) As recorded, 2 s windows fail under 1% in both rooms for every gamma.
