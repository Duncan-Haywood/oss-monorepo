# Glint twin: a point-target radar twin of two unresolved scatterers gets the median right and the mean wrong

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: ideal linear sum/difference monopulse radar looking at two unresolved point scatterers (strong, amplitude 1, at `+d/2`; weak, amplitude `r ≤ 1`, at `−d/2`) with a fresh uniform relative phase on every look. The angle estimate is `(d/2)·Y`, `Y = (1−r²)/(1+r²+2r cos φ)`. The twin treats the pair as one point target at the power centroid with Gaussian angle noise whose variance is matched to the real glint. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`; exact formulas checked against simulation and, in the tests, against a phase grid): (1) `E[Y] = 1` exactly, i.e. the mean estimate is *the strong scatterer*, while the median of `Y` is exactly the power centroid `(1−r²)/(1+r²)`; so the twin has the median right and the mean wrong by `2r²/(1+r²)` (in units of `d/2`: 0.40 at `r = 0.5`, 0.90 at `r = 0.9`); (2) `Var Y = 2r²/(1−r²)` exactly (0.667 at 0.5, 8.5 at 0.9) and the estimate lies beyond the strong scatterer, outside the target span, with probability exactly `arccos(r)/π` (0.333 at 0.5, 0.144 at 0.9); the variance-matched Gaussian twin says 0.312 and 0.380; (3) a tracker gate at the twin's centroid + 3σ is exceeded by 3.6% of real looks at `r = 0.9` (twin: 0.14%, 27× too few) and 1.6% at `r = 0.97` (12×), while at `r = 0.5` the gate lies beyond the largest possible estimate; (4) averaging `N` looks does not converge to the centroid: the RMS error of the mean against the centroid floors at the bias (0.401 at `N = 1024`, `r = 0.5`; 0.899 at `r = 0.9`) where the twin predicts `√(var/N)` (0.026, 0.091), whereas the sample median converges to the centroid (0.024, 0.005) and the mean converges to the strong scatterer; (5) the twin's "the mean is π/2 more efficient than the median" fails for `r ≥ 0.477`, where the median has the lower variance as well as no bias (variance ratio 0.003 at `r = 0.9`).

cd research/glint-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 17 tests, about 2 s

**Builds on.** The radar sensor-simulation and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>) and the digital-twin simulation-fidelity direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Angle glint of unresolved targets and the complex indicated angle are classical (Sherman 1971; Sherman & Barton 2011; Skolnik 2008). Companion to `multipath-twin`, `radar-clutter-twin`, `doppler-twin` and `alias-twin` (what a simplified radar twin gets wrong about the statistics, not just the mean).

Stylised: simulated "real" system, no radar data; noise-free ideal monopulse (no receiver noise, no beam-pattern nonlinearity, so only valid for `d` well inside the beamwidth), two scatterers, phase uniform and independent across looks, no target motion. The noise-free model is singular at `r = 1` (`Y ≡ 0`, yet the variance diverges as `r → 1`), so results near equal amplitudes should not be read as a prediction for a noisy receiver. MIT.
