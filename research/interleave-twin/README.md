# Interleave twin: a link twin with independent packet loss says send repeats back to back; on a bursty link they are nearly wasted unless spaced, and spacing costs latency

Pure Python, no dependencies. A multi-robot team shares a map update by sending `r` copies `g` slots apart (latency `(r−1)g`; the update is lost only if every copy is). The twin loses slots independently with `p = 0.2`; the "real" link is a stationary two-state chain with the same marginal `p` and lag-1 correlation `λ` (a Gilbert model: bursts of loss). Spacing `g` leaves the copies correlated at `λ^g`, and the chain sampled every `g`-th slot is again a chain, so the exact residual is `p·q_g^(r−1)` with `q_g = p + λ^g(1−p)`, versus the twin's `p^r` (checked against enumeration of all loss patterns and 200,000-chain Monte Carlo; tests assert both). Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`): (1) the twin certifies `r = 5` back-to-back copies for residual ≤ 10⁻³ (latency 4, claimed 3.2×10⁻⁴); the real residual of that design is 0.0075 / 0.026 / 0.067 / 0.100 / 0.143 (23× / 81× / 208× / 311× / 448×) at `λ` = 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.8 / 0.9, and back-to-back copies need 8 / 12 / 21 / 32 / 65; (2) for two copies the real/twin ratio is exactly `1 + λ^g(1−p)/p`, which is 4.2 at `g = 1` and 1.28 at `g = 12` for `λ = 0.8`, and getting within 10% of the twin needs `g` = 6 / 17 / 36 at `λ` = 0.5 / 0.8 / 0.9; (3) under a copy budget the best design is a spaced one, but latency is far above the twin's 4: with at most 8 copies the minimum-latency design for ≤ 10⁻³ is 12 slots (7 copies, `g = 2`) at `λ = 0.5`, 35 (8, `g = 5`) at 0.8, 77 (8, `g = 11`) at 0.9 and 154 (8, `g = 22`) at 0.95, and with ≤ 4 copies it is infeasible at every `λ` tested; (4) spacing is not free of a counter-effect: at a fixed deadline with no copy budget, back-to-back copies still beat 2–4 spaced ones once the deadline is long (`λ = 0.8`, `D = 48`: 4.6×10⁻⁵ vs 2.2×10⁻³ for 4 copies), so "interleave" is advice for a bandwidth-limited link, not for every link.

```bash
cd research/interleave-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 10 tests, under 1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # about 1 s; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The multi-robot mapping and radio-link simulation direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>) and the UAV/field-robot digital-twin fidelity direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The two-state loss chain is the classical Gilbert (1960) / Elliott (1963) model; repetition and interleaving against burst errors are classical coding practice (e.g. Lin and Costello 2004). Companion to `dropout-twin` (same chain, there for lidar beam dropout and detection width) and `jitter-twin` (bursty delay): this project is about the design knob, spacing, that independence hides.

Stylised: simulated "real" link, no radio traces; loss is all-or-nothing in the bad state and zero in the good state (no soft-loss Gilbert–Elliott), one stationary chain with known `p` and `λ` (no estimation error, no non-stationary fades), copies cost bandwidth only through the budget `r` and do not load the link, no acknowledgements or retransmission, and map updates are treated as independent. The twin is a deliberately naive baseline. MIT.
