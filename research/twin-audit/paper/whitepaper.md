# Twin audit: how long a digital twin can be wrong before an anytime-valid audit catches it

*Stylised: scalar LQ plant `x' = a x + b u + w`, `a=0.9, q=1, r=0.1, σ²=1`, twin gain `b̂=1` (deployed Riccati gain `k*(b̂)=0.8233`), true gain `b=b̂+Δ`. The auditor sees `(x,u,x')` from the running closed loop and tests `H0: b=b̂` with a Gaussian-mixture e-process, `α=0.05`, prior `N(0,1)` on `Δ`. Pure Python; see `tests/` (7 tests) and `experiments/results.txt`. Numbers below are what `experiments/run.py` printed (seeded Monte Carlo, 150–1000 runs per cell); nothing is tuned.*

## Question
`twin-transfer` showed a twin can be perfect on passive logs and still mistrain a controller; `twin-upkeep` tracks a drifting twin with a filter. Neither answers the operator's simpler question: *I deployed a controller trained on a twin, and I am watching the real plant. When may I declare the twin wrong, without inflating false alarms because I look at every step, and how much does waiting cost?* This is the verification half of the sim-to-real pipeline, and the same anytime-valid machinery used in this repo for verifiable training (`sequential-slashing`, `inference-substitution`).

## Setup
Residual `r_t = x_{t+1} − a x_t − b̂ u_t = Δ u_t + w_t`, with `u_t = −k x_t + e_t` (dither `e_t ~ N(0,v)`) predictable. Under `H0` (`Δ=0`) each step is `N(0,1)`. Mixing the likelihood ratio over `Δ ~ N(0,τ²)` gives the closed form
`E_n = (1+τ²S_n)^{-1/2} exp( τ² R_n² / (2(1+τ²S_n)) )`, `S_n=Σu²`, `R_n=Σu r`,
a nonnegative martingale under `H0` for any predictable `u`, so `P(sup_n E_n ≥ 1/α) ≤ α` (Ville 1939; cf. Robbins 1970, Howard et al. 2021, Ramdas et al. 2023). Information per step is `U = E u² = k² Var(x) + v` with `Var(x) = (1+b²v)/(1−(a−bk)²)` in the *real* closed loop. Noiseless-drift prediction of the delay: the first `n` with `log E` evaluated at `S=nU, R=ΔnU` equal to `ln(1/α)`; for large `n` it is `≈ 2[ln(1/α) + ½ln(1+τ²nU)]/(Δ²U)`.

## Results
1. **Valid under peeking.** With an exact twin, 1000 audits of 3000 steps each: the e-process alarms in 2.4% (bound 5%; conservative because it is a mixture), while a fixed-`n` z-test at 1.96 checked after every step alarms in **58.1%**. Looking continuously is the operator's natural behaviour; only the e-process is safe under it.
2. **Delay matches the drift prediction to within a noise bias.** Mean detection step (predicted): `Δ=+0.1`: 1593 (1945); `+0.2`: 364 (429); `+0.3`: 162 (173); `−0.2`: 404 (407); `−0.3`: 156 (160). Simulation is 0–18% *earlier* than the noiseless prediction for upward gaps, because noise lets the statistic cross before the drift does (first passage is not the drift crossing); all 400 runs detected in every cell. `U` itself is 0.68–0.76 and depends on the *true* plant's closed loop (tested against a direct simulation of `E u²` to 3%).
3. **Regret paid before detection is nearly independent of the gap.** Per-step regret of the twin gain is exact (`0.00142, 0.00571, 0.0234, 0.0543` at `Δ=0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3`, a 38× spread) and delay shrinks ~`1/Δ²` (8646 → 173 predicted steps, 50×), so their product — the expected excess cost accumulated while the twin is wrong — is 12.3, 11.1, 10.0, 9.4 (predicted delay) or 10.2, 9.1, 8.4, 8.4 (simulated mean delay). It is *not exactly* constant: the logarithmic term in the delay and the nonlinearity of regret move it by ~20%, but it does not scale with how wrong the twin is. Small-gap form: `½J_kk k*'(b)²Δ² · 2[ln(1/α)+½ln(1+τ²nU)]/(Δ²U) = J_kk k*'(b)² [ln(1/α)+½ln(1+τ²nU)]/U`; the `ln(1/α)` part alone is 5.0, the log-mixture part supplies the rest. Consequence: a small gap is hard to detect but cheap to live with, a large gap is costly per step but detected fast; the bill is about the same, and is set by `α` and `U`, not by the twin's error. (True in this model; on a plant with a stability cliff close to the operating point the quadratic regret approximation fails.)
4. **A cautious operator hides twin error.** Same gap `Δ=0.2`, `r = 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10`: `k = 0.891, 0.823, 0.538, 0.194`, `U = 0.818, 0.683, 0.309, 0.068`, mean delay 325, 384, 778, 3730 steps. Expensive control shrinks actuation, so the twin's actuator gain is barely exercised (this is `twin-transfer`'s blind direction, made quantitative: information is `k²Var(x)`). Regret × delay falls 10.4 → 1.2 as well, because the cautious controller is also *insensitive* to `b̂` (regret per step 0.032 → 0.0003): when the twin is hard to audit it is also hard to be hurt by, for this parameter.
5. **Dither almost never pays for auditing.** Both regret and dither cost are per-step and information is linear in `v`, so total expected cost before detection `∝ (ρ+κ_c v)/(U₀+κ_u v)` is monotone in `v`: dither lowers it iff `ρ/U₀ > κ_c/κ_u`, with `κ_u = 1+k²b²/(1−c²)`, `κ_c = r + q b²/(1−c²)`, `c=a−bk`. With `b̂=1` this holds only for `Δ > +1.252` (true gain 2.25× the twin) or `Δ < −0.647` (0.35×): gross errors near the stability cliff. Simulation at `Δ=+0.2`: cost before detection 9.6 without dither, 90 / 160 / 202 at `v=0.25 / 1 / 4`. At `Δ=−0.8`, inside the rule's region: 7.8, 6.3, 5.9, 5.3 (a 32% saving at `v=4`). The rule's asymptotic premise fails at the other edge (`Δ=+1.3`, where detection takes ~1–2 steps): the rule says dither pays, simulation says it costs more (194 → 272 at `v=0.25`). We report this as a failure of the law outside its regime, not a confirmation.

## Limitations
One unknown scalar and known `a` (a multi-parameter twin would need a matrix mixture, and the blind direction of `twin-upkeep` then bites); Gaussian, known noise variance; fixed controller during the audit (no re-training on evidence); the prior `τ²=1` is arbitrary and affects the delay only through the log term; "excess cost before detection" is per-step expected regret × mean delay starting from the real closed loop's stationary law, not a realised-cost accounting; results are exact or numerical in the model and are not evidence about real robots or simulators.

## Relevance
Any pipeline that trains in a twin and deploys should audit the deployed system with a time-uniform test and report (a) how many steps of real operation it needs, which depends on the controller's own excitation `k²Var(x)`, and (b) that the accumulated regret of a wrong twin is governed by `α` and `U`, so the audit budget should be set from information rate, not guessed from an error tolerance. Probing the plant is justified only when the twin is already grossly wrong. The same certificate is natural for a *paid* twin provider (`twin-elicitation`): a principal can refuse or claw back payment when the e-process crosses `1/α`, without fixing the audit length in advance.
