# A retry budget certified in an independent-attempt twin: object-level difficulty turns a geometric failure law into a power law

*Stylised: a pick skill retried until success. Twin: every attempt succeeds independently with `p=0.6`. "Real": each object has a latent success probability `P~Beta(a,b)` with mean `0.6`, attempts i.i.d. given `P`. Pure Python; all numbers are from `experiments/results.txt` (seeded, ~7 s). Section 1, 2 and 4 numbers are exact formulas; Section 3 is a simulation check; Section 5 is simulated.*

## Question
Manipulation skills are scored in simulation by their per-attempt success rate, and a planner then chooses how many times to retry before escalating (to another grasp, a human, a different plan). If the twin draws attempts independently but real failures are object-specific (a mug that is hard to grip stays hard), the twin's single-attempt success rate can be exactly right while its retry certificate `P(fail after k)≤δ` is not. How wrong is it, what does the wrong budget cost, and what data repairs it?

## Model
Real: `P~Beta(a,b)`, `p=a/(a+b)`, attempt outcomes i.i.d. Bernoulli(`P`) given `P`; two attempts on one object have outcome correlation `ρ=1/(a+b+1)`. Failure after `k` attempts is `s_k=E[(1−P)^k]=B(a,b+k)/B(a,b)=∏_{j<k}(b+j)/(a+b+j)`, so `s_k~Γ(a+b)/Γ(b)·k^{−a}`. Twin: `(1−p)^k`. With a cap of `K` attempts the expected number of attempts is `Σ_{k<K}s_k=((a+b−1)−s_K(a+b+K−1))/(a−1)` (telescoping; checked against the sum), finite as `K→∞` iff `a>1`. With payoff `w` per success and cost `c` per attempt, attempt `k+1` after `k` failures has success probability `a/(a+b+k)` (Beta posterior), so the optimal cap is the first `k` with `w·a/(a+b+k)<c`, i.e. `K*=⌊wa/c−a−b⌋+1` (checked against brute force). The twin, with constant hazard `p`, never gives up if `pw≥c`.

## Results
1. **The twin's failure claim is geometric, the real one a power law.** At `k=8` the twin claims 0.00066; the real failure is 0.0031 / 0.0250 / 0.1275 for `ρ`=0.05 / 0.2 / 0.5 (5× / 38× / 195×). At `k=1` all four are 0.4, so single-attempt validation cannot distinguish them. The exact law matches a 200,000-object simulation at `k`=1…200 within about one standard error (`ρ=0.2`; e.g. `k=8`: 0.02502 vs 0.02442 ± 0.00035, 1.7 se; `k=20`: 0.00390 vs 0.00371 ± 0.00014).
2. **The budget needed grows like `(C/δ)^{1/a}`.** For `δ=0.01` the twin needs 6 attempts; the real system needs 7 / 13 / 571 (1.2× / 2.2× / 95×), for `δ=10^{−4}`: 16 / 101 / 1,230,303 against the twin's 11. At the twin's own budget the real failure is 2× / 10× / 37× the claim at `δ=0.01` and 15× / 322× / 2,522× at `δ=10^{−4}`. The power-law budget matches the exact one to a few percent once `a` is small (`ρ=0.2`, `δ=10^{−4}`: 103 vs 101) but is poor for `a=11.4` (28 vs 16), where the asymptotic regime has not been reached.
3. **With `a<1` retries may cost unboundedly many attempts.** For `ρ=0.5` (`a=0.6`) the expected attempts under a cap of 10 / 100 / 1,000 / 100,000 is 2.8 / 7.1 / 17.9 / 112.7 against the twin's 1.67 without a cap.
4. **The payoff loss is usually much smaller than the certificate error.** The closed-form give-up rule gives `K*`=2–552 depending on `w/c` and `ρ`. Using the twin's `δ=0.01` budget (6) instead loses 0.7–1.0% at `ρ=0.05`, 0–3.8% at `ρ=0.2` and 0–5.9% or 23.3% at `ρ=0.5` (23.3% at `w/c`=4, where the optimal cap is 2: real value 1.48 vs 1.135; 0% where 6 happens to be optimal). The twin also over-claims value: 2.32 vs a real 1.135 at `ρ=0.5`, `w/c`=4, and 8.30 vs 6.23 at `w/c`=10. The certificate is what fails badly; the economics fail mildly except when the real hazard falls fast and attempts are expensive.
5. **Repair needs repeated attempts on the same object.** One attempt per object is uninformative about `ρ`. With two forced attempts per object, the moment estimate `ρ̂=(E[x₁x₂]−p̂²)/(p̂(1−p̂))` (true 0.2) has median 0.19–0.20, but with 25 objects 20% of repeats estimate `ρ̂≤0` and revert to the independent twin, and the repaired budget for `δ=0.01` has a 10–90% range of 5–272 (true 13). At 400 / 1,600 / 6,400 objects the range is 9–21 / 11–17 / 12–15. Because the budget is aimed at `δ` without a safety margin, the real failure is ≤`δ` in only 51 / 52 / 54 / 61 / 79% of repeats (n=25…6,400) though the mean failure approaches `δ` (0.0214 → 0.0092): a budget at the target needs a margin on top of the estimate.

## Limitations
One latent difficulty with a Beta law; attempts are i.i.d. given the object (real retries change the grasp, so a failed attempt can also help by re-posing, the opposite sign of correlation); the twin's success rate is set equal to the real mean by construction; `a,b` are chosen, not measured; the repair uses forced attempts, which cost real trials and assume the Beta family; the method of moments is not the maximum-likelihood fit and no confidence interval is derived; no robot data. Preliminary.

## Next steps
Retries that change the grasp (negative correlation, or a mixture of hard and easy objects); fitting the Beta-binomial by maximum likelihood with a proper credible interval on the budget; scoring the twin's retry claim with proper scoring rules (`twin-elicitation`); combining with `twin-certification` for the number of real trials needed to verify a budget; a planner-level version where the escalation target (human, alternative plan) is itself stochastic (`handover-twin`).
