# How much of an old task must a continual learner store? An exact law for partial replay

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`. Builds on `forgetting-law` and `replay-law`.*

## Abstract
`replay-law` gives the exact expected forgetting of a task under replay schedules, but assumes the whole old task is available. In practice a decentralised learner keeps a small buffer. We solve the model of `forgetting-law` (rank-`r` Haar-random tasks in `R^d`, training to convergence projects the error onto the task's null space) when only `m` of the tracked task's `r` examples are replayed. Replaying them with a fresh task equals projecting out their subspace `V` and then taking a plain step in dimension `d−m`. Two cases differ. With a **fixed buffer** (`V` stored once and kept) the expected state `(error in V, error in the rest of the task, error outside it)` obeys an exact 3×3 law; with a **fresh subset** (the whole task is stored but each replay uses `m` newly drawn examples) the state collapses to an exact 2×2 law. Both reduce to the plain step at `m=0` and to `replay-law`'s full replay at `m=r` (tests to 12 digits), and each matches its own simulation at every step (E1, 20,000 runs: largest gap 0.0005 on losses of 0.003–0.05). Consequences: one replay at its best step recovers a fraction of the full-memory saving that is nearly linear in `m/r` (0.226/0.465/0.722 at `m/r` = ¼, ½, ¾ for `d=12, r=4`; within 0.004 of `m/r` at `d=128, r=8`), the same for both cases. Under repeated replay a fixed buffer's saving stays linear in `m/r` (half the buffer buys 50–51% of the saving at rate ¼), and at a fixed budget of examples per step, replaying the whole task rarely beats a small buffer replayed often by 2–18 points of total forgetting at ¼ example per step and 16–50 points at 1. Re-drawing the replayed examples from a fully stored task recovers most of that gap (within 0.1–6 points), so what matters is how much of the task is stored, not how it is scheduled. Stylised: realizable linear regression, exact convergence, one tracked task.

*Correction, 2026-11-14.* Earlier versions applied the 2×2 fresh-subset law to a fixed buffer, reported its agreement with a fixed-buffer simulation as within Monte-Carlo error (the largest gap, 0.0010 at step 6 for `m=2`, was about 5 standard errors and persisted at 300k runs), and drew the fixed-rate and fixed-budget conclusions of sections 3 and 4 from it ("half the memory buys 57–65% of the saving"; "a small buffer gives up 0.1–2 points"). Those numbers hold for a fresh subset only. Found by the repository's quality audit (`portfolio/quality/audits/2026-11-08-replay-memory.json`).

## 1. Model and the partial-replay step
As in `replay-law`, `e = w − w*`; the tracked task has row space `U` (rank `r`) and the initial error is uniform in `U^⊥`, of squared norm `1−r/d`. A partial replay trains on a fresh Haar rank-`r` task `P` jointly with `V ⊂ U`, `dim V = m` (the replayed examples). Then `V + span(P) = V ⊕ (I−P_V)span(P)`, and by invariance under rotations fixing `V`, `(I−P_V)span(P)` is a uniform `r`-subspace of `V^⊥` (dimension `D = d−m`). So the step projects out `V`, then applies a plain step of rank `r` in dimension `D`, with `forgetting-law` constants `λ' = λ(D,r)`, `c₂' = c₂(D,r)` (everything is zeroed if `r ≥ D`).

*Fixed buffer.* `V` is drawn once, uniformly in `U`, and kept. A Haar step keeps the second moment `E[ee^T]` invariant under rotations that fix both `V` and `U`, so the expected squared error in each of `V`, `U ⊖ V` and `U^⊥` (dimensions `m`, `r−m`, `d−r`) is a closed state `(v, u, o)`. A plain step sends each component `x_i ↦ λ x_i + c₂ dim_i (v+u+o)`; a replay sends `v ↦ 0` and `u, o ↦ λ' x_i + c₂' dim_i (u+o)`. The loss on the task is `v+u`.

*Fresh subset.* Each replay draws a new uniform `V ⊂ U`, independent of the past. Averaging over it (the traces `tr(P_U(I−P_V)) = r−m` and `tr((I−P_U)(I−P_V)) = d−r` are deterministic) closes the state `(a, b) = (‖P_U e‖², ‖(I−P_U)e‖²)`:
`a' = λ'(1−m/r)a + c₂'(r−m)[(1−m/r)a + b]`,  `b' = λ'b + c₂'(d−r)[(1−m/r)a + b]`.
This is the matrix earlier versions used for both cases. After a fixed buffer's first replay the error in `V` is zero while the error in `U ⊖ V` is not, so `a` alone no longer determines the next step.

Every schedule is a matrix product; Bernoulli, periodic and one-shot totals are closed forms as in `replay-law` (a partial replay leaves loss on the task, so the replay step itself is summed in the periodic cycle). With one replay the two cases agree. E1 (`d=12, r=4`, `m` = 1, 2, 3 examples every 3rd step, 20,000 runs per case) checks each law against its own simulation: largest gaps 0.0002, 0.0004, 0.0005 (fixed) and 0.0002, 0.0002, 0.0005 (fresh) for `m` = 1, 2, 3, the step-2 values (loss 0.052) carrying most of it. At step 6, `m=2`, the fixed law gives 0.0070 against 0.0071 simulated and the fresh law 0.0061 against 0.0061; the fresh law against the fixed simulation is 0.0061 vs 0.0071. A separate check of a total (`d=12, r=4`, one stored example replayed every step, 4,000 runs over 60 steps) gives 0.731 of no-replay forgetting simulated against 0.727 from the fixed law, and 0.589 against 0.581 for a fresh subset.

## 2. One replay: memory buys savings almost linearly
Let the *benefit* be the total-forgetting saving of the best single replay with `m` examples divided by that with all `r`. E2: `d=32, r=4`: 0.246, 0.495, 0.746 at `m/r` = ¼, ½, ¾; `d=128, r=8`: 0.125, 0.249, …, 0.875 for `m` = 1…7 (deviation from `m/r` under 0.004); `d=12, r=4`: 0.226, 0.465, 0.722, the most sublinear because the model is tight (`d/r = 3`). The best replay step is the same for all `m` in the wide models (5 and 11 at `(32,4)` and `(128,8)`, at the forgetting peak lags 5.2 and 10.8) and shifts by one step in tight ones (`(32,8)`: 3 for small `m`, 2 for `m ≥ 7`). Reading: near the forgetting peak, each stored direction removes roughly its own share of the task's loss, and the interaction with the fresh task's projection is second order at large `d/r`.

## 3. Fixed rate: a buffer's saving is linear in its size
E3 gives total forgetting relative to no replay under Bernoulli replay at rate `q`. For a fixed buffer at `d=128, r=16`, `q=0.25`: 0.842, 0.687, 0.534, 0.383 for `m` = 4, 8, 12, 16, nearly equal steps. E6 (`q=0.25`): half the buffer (`m=r/2`) yields 0.503, 0.508, 0.508 of the full saving for `(d,r)` = (32,8), (128,16), (32,4). Reading: every replay re-zeroes the same `m` directions, and the other `r−m` directions of the task are never protected, so the saving is about `m/r` of the full one at any rate. A fresh subset is concave instead (0.761, 0.598, 0.477 at the same point; half the subset yields 0.571, 0.652, 0.653 of the saving), because each replay protects different directions and the last examples overlap with what earlier replays and the fresh task already removed. Under periodic replay every 4 steps at `d=128, r=8` (E5, fixed buffer) the expected peak falls from 0.0146 (no replay, step 11) to 0.0122 at `m=2`, 0.0100 at `m=4` (step 7), and 0.0084 at `m ≥ 6`, where the peak is set by the pre-replay steps 1–3 and stops responding to memory.

## 4. Fixed budget: how much is stored matters, how it is scheduled much less
A learner that can afford `c` replayed examples per step on average can replay `m` of them with probability `c/m`. E4: the best size is always the whole task `m=r` in every configuration tried (`(32,8)`, `(128,16)`, `(12,4)`, `(64,4)`, `c` = 0.25–2), and for a fixed buffer the margin is large. At `c=1`, total forgetting relative to no replay is 0.704 (`m=8`) vs 0.869 (`m=1`) at `(32,8)`; 0.757 vs 0.937 at `(128,16)`; 0.536 vs 0.727 at `(12,4)`; 0.253 vs 0.749 at `(64,4)`. At `c=0.25` the gaps are 2.3, 3.1, 2.9 and 18 points. A single stored example replayed every step protects one direction of the task and nothing else.

The fresh-subset column answers a different question: with the whole task stored but only `c` examples of compute per step, does replaying a few random examples often lose much against the whole task rarely? Little: at `c=1`, 0.723 (`m=1`) vs 0.704 at `(32,8)`, 0.761 vs 0.757 at `(128,16)`, 0.581 vs 0.536 at `(12,4)`, 0.259 vs 0.253 at `(64,4)`, and at most 6 points anywhere tried (0.334 vs 0.274 at `(12,4)`, `c=2`, `m=2`). Practical reading: the memory budget (how much of the task is kept) sets the attainable saving almost linearly; given the memory, the replay schedule and the size of each replay are second order.

## 5. Limits and next steps
One tracked task with the stored subspace chosen uniformly inside it, either kept (fixed buffer) or re-drawn at each replay (fresh subset); a buffer refreshed partially, or a reservoir that changes slowly, lies between the two and is not treated (we did not treat an adversarial or gradient-selected subset, which can only do better than random); realizable regression, exact convergence, no SGD noise, no conflicting tasks. Replaying several stored tasks each step remains open: it is a higher-rank step correlated with the current error. A verification hook: the exact curve for a claimed buffer size lets a verifier bound the loss a replay claim should produce, as `forgetting-audit` does for skipped training. Related: `forgetting-law`, `replay-law`, `merge-law`, `forgetting-audit`.
