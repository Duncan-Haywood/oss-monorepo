# How much of an old task must a continual learner store? An exact law for partial replay

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`. Builds on `forgetting-law` and `replay-law`.*

## Abstract
`replay-law` gives the exact expected forgetting of a task under replay schedules, but assumes the whole old task is available. In practice a decentralised learner keeps a small buffer. We solve the model of `forgetting-law` (rank-`r` Haar-random tasks in `R^d`, training to convergence projects the error onto the task's null space) when only `m` of the tracked task's `r` examples are stored. Replaying them with a fresh task equals projecting out the stored subspace and then taking a plain step in dimension `d−m`, so the expected state `(loss on the task, error outside it)` obeys an exact 2×2 matrix `R_m` that reduces to the plain step at `m=0` and to `replay-law`'s full replay at `m=r` (tests to 12 digits), and matches simulation at every step. Consequences: one replay at its best step recovers a fraction of the full-memory saving that is nearly linear in `m/r` (0.226/0.465/0.722 at `m/r` = ¼, ½, ¾ for `d=12, r=4`; within 0.004 of `m/r` at `d=128, r=8`), and the best step barely depends on `m`; at a fixed rate, half the memory buys 57–65% of the saving; and at a fixed budget of examples per step, one large replay beats many small ones, but the gap is 0.1–2 points of total forgetting in wide models and up to 6 in a tight one (`d=12, r=4`, 2 examples per step). Storage size, not schedule, is the lever. Stylised: realizable linear regression, exact convergence, one tracked task.

## 1. Model and the partial-replay step
As in `replay-law`, `e = w − w*`; the tracked task has row space `U` (rank `r`), `a = ‖P_U e‖²`, `b = ‖(I−P_U)e‖²`, and the initial state is `(0, 1−r/d)`. A partial replay trains on a fresh Haar rank-`r` task `P` jointly with `V ⊂ U`, `dim V = m` (the stored examples). Then `V + span(P) = V ⊕ (I−P_V)span(P)`, and by invariance under rotations fixing `V`, `(I−P_V)span(P)` is a uniform `r`-subspace of `V^⊥` (dimension `D = d−m`). So the step projects out `V`, then applies a plain step of rank `r` in dimension `D`, with `forgetting-law` constants `λ' = λ(D,r)`, `c₂' = c₂(D,r)` (everything is zeroed if `r ≥ D`). Averaging over `V` (uniform in `U`, equivalent to fixed `V` by invariance; the traces `tr(P_U(I−P_V)) = r−m` and `tr((I−P_U)(I−P_V)) = d−r` are deterministic) gives
`a' = λ'(1−m/r)a + c₂'(r−m)[(1−m/r)a + b]`,  `b' = λ'b + c₂'(d−r)[(1−m/r)a + b]`.
Every schedule is a matrix product; Bernoulli, periodic and one-shot totals are closed forms as in `replay-law` (a partial replay leaves loss on the task, so the replay step itself is summed in the periodic cycle). E1 (`d=12, r=4`, `m` = 1, 2, 3 examples every 3rd step, 6000 runs) agrees with simulation at all 8 steps; e.g. exact/sim at step 6: 0.0110/0.0117, 0.0061/0.0071, 0.0025/0.0030 for `m` = 1, 2, 3. The largest gap is 0.0010 (step 6, `m=2`), within Monte-Carlo error at 6000 runs.

## 2. One replay: memory buys savings almost linearly
Let the *benefit* be the total-forgetting saving of the best single replay with `m` examples divided by that with all `r`. E2: `d=32, r=4`: 0.246, 0.495, 0.746 at `m/r` = ¼, ½, ¾; `d=128, r=8`: 0.125, 0.249, …, 0.875 for `m` = 1…7 (deviation from `m/r` under 0.004); `d=12, r=4`: 0.226, 0.465, 0.722, the most sublinear because the model is tight (`d/r = 3`). The best replay step is the same for all `m` in the wide models (5 and 11 at `(32,4)` and `(128,8)`, at the forgetting peak lags 5.2 and 10.8) and shifts by one step in tight ones (`(32,8)`: 3 for small `m`, 2 for `m ≥ 7`). Reading: near the forgetting peak, each stored direction removes roughly its own share of the task's loss, and the interaction with the fresh task's projection is second order at large `d/r`.

## 3. Fixed rate, fixed memory: concave returns
E3 gives total forgetting relative to no replay under Bernoulli replay at rate `q`. At `d=128, r=16`, `q=0.25`: 0.761, 0.598, 0.477, 0.383 for `m` = 4, 8, 12, 16. At a fixed rate the saving is concave in `m`: E6 (`q=0.25`) shows half the memory (`m=r/2`) yields 0.571, 0.652, 0.653 of the full saving for `(d,r)` = (32,8), (128,16), (32,4), and half the full saving needs `m/r` = 0.50, 0.38, 0.50. Overlap with the fresh task's step makes the last stored examples worth less. Under periodic replay every 4 steps at `d=128, r=8` (E5) the expected peak falls from 0.0146 (no replay, step 11) to 0.0117 at `m=2`, 0.0100 at `m=4`, and 0.0084 at `m ≥ 6`, where the peak is set by the pre-replay steps 1–3 and stops responding to memory.

## 4. Fixed budget: few large replays vs many small ones
A learner that can afford `c` replayed examples per step on average can replay `m` of them with probability `c/m`. Since the saving is concave in `m` at a fixed rate but the rate falls as `1/m`, the trade is not obvious. E4: the best size is always the whole task `m=r` in every configuration tried (`(32,8)`, `(128,16)`, `(12,4)`, `(64,4)`, `c` = 0.25–2), but the margin is small. At `c=1`: 0.704 (`m=8`) vs 0.723 (`m=1`) at `(32,8)`; 0.757 vs 0.761 at `(128,16)`; 0.536 vs 0.581 at `(12,4)`; 0.253 vs 0.259 at `(64,4)`. The gap is largest in tight models at high budget (0.274 vs 0.334 at `(12,4)`, `c=2`, `m=4` vs 2). Practical reading: store whole tasks when you can and replay them rarely, but a buffer of single examples replayed often gives up about 2 points of forgetting or less in wide models, so the buffer *size* matters far more than how it is scheduled.

## 5. Limits and next steps
One tracked task with the stored subspace chosen uniformly inside it (we did not treat an adversarial or gradient-selected subset, which can only do better than random); realizable regression, exact convergence, no SGD noise, no conflicting tasks. Replaying several stored tasks each step remains open: it is a higher-rank step correlated with the current error. A verification hook: the exact curve for a claimed buffer size lets a verifier bound the loss a replay claim should produce, as `forgetting-audit` does for skipped training. Related: `forgetting-law`, `replay-law`, `merge-law`, `forgetting-audit`.
