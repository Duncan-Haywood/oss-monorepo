# Flying-pixel twin: a single-ray lidar twin never returns a range between two surfaces, and a finite beam with merged pulses returns one across a band of 2.6 beam widths

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: a lidar beam with a Gaussian footprint crossing a depth edge (a near surface at `r1`, a far wall at `r1 + D`), where return pulses closer than the detector's resolution merge and the reported range is the received-energy centroid. The twin casts a single ray and returns the near or the far surface, never a range between. A return more than `ε` from both surfaces is a ghost (flying pixel). Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`; closed form checked against a direct scan of the returned range, a periodic-edge scan simulation and a pointing-jitter simulation): (1) with equal received energies the ghost band is exactly `2Φ⁻¹(1−ε/D)` beam standard deviations wide, 2.563 at `ε/D = 0.1`, 4.653 at 0.01, 0.507 at 0.4, and none once `ε ≥ D/2` (direct scan agrees to 1e-4); the width depends on the tolerance relative to the step, not on the range; (2) with unequal energies `κ` (far over near at half coverage) the band keeps the same closed form via `u(f) = Φ⁻¹(κ(1−f)/(f+κ(1−f)))`, moves toward the near side when the far wall is brighter (centre 1.178 at `κ = 10`) and narrows (2.225 at `κ = 10`, 1.671 at `κ = 100`); a strongest-return detector instead moves the apparent edge by `Φ⁻¹(κ/(1+κ))` (1.335 beam widths at `κ = 10`); (3) with `ε = 0.10 m` a 0.25 m step gives 0.5 beam widths of ghost band, 0.6 m gives 1.9 and 2 m gives 3.3; at a beam std of one scan step an edge crossing yields 0.51 to 3.3 ghost returns, and at two steps every edge with `D ≥ 0.25 m` yields at least one; (4) a scan with edges every ≈100 beams has a ghost fraction equal to the prediction `width·q/100` to within 0.3% on five settings (e.g. 2.56% at `q = 1`, 7.69% at `q = 3`); (5) repeated scans from a static robot do not average ghosts out: with 5 scans and pointing jitter of 0.05, 0.1, 0.3, 1 beam widths, 99.2%, 98.4%, 95.0%, 80.4% of ghost samples pass a 3-of-5 persistence filter.

**Builds on.** The lidar and radar sensor-simulation and sim-to-real perception direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>), and the field-robot and UAV sensing setting of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Mixed pixels at depth discontinuities are a known lidar artefact (e.g. Tuley, Vandapel and Hebert 2005; Sotoodeh 2006). Companion to `glint-twin` (unresolved scatterers in radar, there with a point-target twin) and `beam-twin` (a lidar twin that returns every beam).

Stylised: a hard merge threshold rather than a partial-resolution waveform model, a 1-D Gaussian footprint crossing a straight edge (no 2-D footprint, no surface slope), received energy proportional to covered fraction times a fixed `κ` (no incidence-angle or saturation effects), a centroid detector, and no range noise or real scan data. The twin is a deliberately naive baseline: a beam-aware twin reproduces these numbers by construction. MIT.
