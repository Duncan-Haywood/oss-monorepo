## 2026-11-07 · Milestone 2: CMA-ES against random search for the MPC weights

- Prediction written after launching `experiments/run_tune.py`, before reading any output. Question: does tuning the three sampling-MPC cost weights (log10 space, [-3, 3]) with CMA-ES at 90 evaluations beat uniform random search at the same budget, on held-out offsets (-100/0/+100 m) never seen in training (+-50 m)? **Prediction:** CMA-ES's mean held-out loss is lower than random search's (prior 60%), and both beat the untuned default weights (1, 0.02, 3) on mean held-out loss across sigma_v 0/0.3/0.6 (prior 70%); the gap between CMA-ES and random is under 0.02 loss (prior 65%) because the loss surface over three weights is flat once the shortfall term dominates. Refuted if the default beats the median tuned config, or random beats CMA-ES on the mean. Loss = 1 - share(safe and compliant) + 0.5 x mean detour / 1000 m.
- **Outcome (`experiments/results_tune.txt`, about 25 minutes on 4 cores; 3 CMA-ES and 3 random runs of 90 evaluations).** Prediction: two parts held, one refuted. (1) CMA-ES lower than random on mean held-out loss: **refuted**, a tie (0.1655 against 0.1651; the best random run, 0.1589, beats every CMA run, the worst, 0.1760, loses to all). (2) Both beat the default: **held**, 0.260 default against 0.159 to 0.176. (3) Gap under 0.02: **held** (0.0004). What tuning did: the port-turn penalty weight rose from 3 to roughly 40 to 870 and the deviation weight fell to its lower scale, lifting held-out compliance from 76.8-84.1% to 85.5-95.7% while safe share stayed at 84-90% (it is 87.0% at sigma 0 for every config but one). The gain is from compliance, not safety. Limits: the three weights are one lever (port penalty), train and test share geometry families (only the offsets differ), 1 seed in training, the loss mixes compliance and detour with a chosen 0.5/1000 m factor, kinematic model.
