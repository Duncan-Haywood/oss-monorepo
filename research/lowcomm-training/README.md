# Low-communication training: which of sync interval, compression, delay, momentum and robust aggregation sets the final loss?

**Status: open research programme, milestone 1 done.** The harness (`src/lowcomm/`, `tests/`, `experiments/m1_check.py`) reproduces the synchronous loop exactly at `M = 1, H = 1` and the `noisy-local-sgd` closed form on a quadratic (ratio 0.983, 95% CI containing it). No result about the research question yet. This directory is where the work
toward one publishable paper accumulates across sessions (see [`PROPOSAL.md`](PROPOSAL.md)).

**Live page:** https://marisstella.app/research/lowcomm-training/

**The question.** DiLoCo-style local SGD trains a language model across workers that synchronise only
every `H` steps. The sync interval, a compressor, a delayed or momentum-based outer update and a robust
aggregator each change the final loss. Can a model with a few measured constants per model size predict
the loss of a configuration never run, from the structure of the algorithm, as well as an empirical
scaling law refitted on the same runs?

**Why it is open.** Theory bounds local SGD on convex problems with constants nobody measures; empirical
scaling laws (Charles et al., 2025) fit size, workers and `H` but do not explain the cost of each axis.
About a dozen projects here derive the stationary loss and stability limit of each axis on a quadratic
(`noisy-local-sgd`, `compressed-outer`, `delayed-outer`, `outer-momentum`, `robust-outer` and others). Whether those
factors hold on a trained transformer, and whether they compose, is untested.

- First loss-against-`H` smoke sweep (numpy harness, 14k parameters, 3 seeds, outer step untuned): U-shaped, `H=16` 2.32, `H=4` 2.74, `H=160` diverges on one seed; synchronous 2.44. It does not test H2. See [`experiments/numpy_h_sweep/`](experiments/numpy_h_sweep) and the LOG. Run: `cd experiments/numpy_h_sweep && PYTHONPATH=. python3 h_sweep.py` (about 8 min; tests in `tests/` need numpy only).
- Same sweep with the outer optimiser tuned per `H` (grid of 9, tuned on one held-out half, reported on the other, 5 seeds): `H=1` 2.342, `H=4` 2.348, `H=16` 2.274, `H=64` 2.410, `H=160` 2.481, synchronous 2.383. Tuning removes most of the small-`H` penalty and the divergence, but `H=4` stays 0.075 above `H=16` (Welch t = 4.3), so the weak form of H2 still fails as stated; the lr grid tops out at 1.0, which binds for three of the five `H`. See the LOG, "Milestone 2a".
- Inner lr tuned per `H` (grid 1e-3 to 3e-2) with the outer step set to plain averaging, 5 seeds: synchronous 2.159, `H=1` 2.149, `H=4` 2.167, `H=16` 2.183, `H=64` 2.210, `H=160` 2.218. The U-shape and the "DiLoCo beats synchronous" readings of the earlier sweeps disappear: loss rises gently with `H`, and `H<=16` is within 2 sd of synchronous (`H=16` narrowly). `H<=16` picks sit at the grid edge. See the LOG, "Milestone 2e".
- Inner lr 0.1 added to the grid (it loses at every `H`, so the edge picks were real optima) and outer momentum put back on top of the tuned inner lr: it hurts. At `H=160`, plain averaging 2.218, outer (1, 0.5) 2.269, the DiLoCo-paper setting (0.7, 0.9) 2.486 ± 0.07, against synchronous 2.159. Tiny, under-trained model, three outer settings only; not a statement about scale. See the LOG, "Milestone 2f". Run: `cd experiments/numpy_h_sweep && OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 outer_momentum_sweep.py` (about 17 min on 4 cores).
- First result on the real transformer (413k-parameter GPT, Tiny Shakespeare, `M = 4`, 960 inner steps per worker, plain averaging, inner lr tuned per `H`): the gap to synchronous AdamW is already +0.08 nats at `H=4` and +0.14 at `H=16`, reaching +0.155 at `H=240` (sync 1.798). That is 2.5 times the numpy model's gap and rises earlier, then flattens. Three report seeds, one size and a short budget. See the LOG, "Milestone 3a". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_h_sweep && PYTHONPATH=../../src python3 h_sweep.py tune`, then `report` and `analyse.py` (about 75 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Longer budget (Milestone 3b): at 3,840 inner steps per worker the same sweep shrinks the gap to synchronous AdamW to +0.004 nats at `H=4`, +0.034 at `H=16` and +0.017 at `H=64` (sync 1.599), about 4 times smaller than at 960 steps, so most of 3a's gap was a short-budget effect. Three seeds, inner lr not re-tuned. See the LOG, "Milestone 3b". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_long_budget && PYTHONPATH=../../src python3 long_budget.py` (about 40 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Re-tuned lr (Milestone 3c): tuning the inner lr at 3,840 steps (grid 3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3; two tuning seeds) picks 1e-3 for both `H=16` and `H=64`. `H=16` then matches synchronous AdamW (gap -0.002 nats, three seeds, within noise) and beats `H=64` (+0.017), so 3b's inversion was a stale learning rate. Sync and `H=4` were not re-tuned. See the LOG, "Milestone 3c". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_retune && PYTHONPATH=../../src python3 retune.py tune && python3 retune.py report` (about 1 hour on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Fair re-tune of every method (Milestone 3d, after 3c): at 3,840 steps every `H` picks 2e-3. The gaps to synchronous AdamW (1.5897) become -0.007 at `H=4`, -0.014 at `H=16` and +0.005 at `H=64` nats, and `H=16` beats `H=64` on every seed, so 3c's finding holds with sync and `H=4` also re-tuned (sync gains too: 1.5994 to 1.5897) came from stale learning rates. 3 seeds, one shared 3-point grid: the sign of the `H=16` gain is suggestive only. Run: `cd experiments/gpt_retune_all && python3 retune.py tune && python3 retune.py report` (about 2 hours on 4 cores, needs torch).

Run: `cd experiments/gpt_compression && OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 compression.py run && python3 compression.py report` (about 45 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).

- Where error feedback breaks (Milestone 4c): EF top-k at keep 1/64 costs +0.043 nats and at 1/256 +0.121 (3 seeds, `H=16`, `M=4`), against -0.005 at 1/16 (4b); the pre-registered claim that 1/64 stays within 0.02 was refuted. Run: `cd experiments/gpt_topk_ef_low && python3 topk_ef_low.py run && python3 topk_ef_low.py report`.
- Where compression starts to cost (Milestones 6n to 6r, 915k-parameter GPT, `H = 64`, `M = 4`, 4 to 6 seeds per cell): rand-k compression costs nothing measurable up to keep 1/8 (omega 7), then the excess rises linearly, +0.017 at keep 1/12, +0.044 at 1/16 and +0.132 nats at 1/32. A hinge in omega (knee near 8, 0.0057 nats per unit of omega) fits all five keep levels (chi-square 3.2 on 3 dof); a power law and an omega-proportional rule do not. Its prediction for keep 1/32 was registered before the run (+0.152, band +0.108 to +0.205). One `H`, one width. See the LOG, "Milestone 6r". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 scale.py run keep32 && python3 keep32_6r.py` (about 18 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Does the knee move with the sync interval? (Milestone 6s, same model, `H = 16`, 4 to 6 seeds per cell): yes, it disappears. At `H = 16` rand-k costs +0.003, +0.022, +0.045 and +0.098 nats at keep 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, a straight line through the origin (0.0031 nats per unit of omega, chi-square 2.5 on 3 dof). Keep 1/8 is free at `H = 64` but not at `H = 16`; keep 1/32 costs a quarter less at `H = 16`. The curves cross near keep 1/16, which is why that one keep level looked H-independent. All three pre-registered hypotheses (an H-independent knee) were refuted. See the LOG, "Milestone 6s". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 scale.py run knee16 && python3 knee16_6s.py` (10 runs, about 55 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Does the shape change smoothly between `H = 16` and `H = 64`? (Milestone 6t, `H = 32`, keep 1/8 and 1/32, 4 seeds): consistent with it. Rand-k costs +0.011 (se 0.007) at keep 1/8 and +0.109 (se 0.008) at keep 1/32, between the `H = 16` and `H = 64` values and within 1 se of linear interpolation in log2 H (+0.010, +0.115). The fitted knee sits at omega 0.5, 4.25, 7.75 at H = 16, 32, 64, but the `H = 32` seeds are twice as noisy and its knee interval (0.75 to 7.0) overlaps both neighbours. All three pre-registered hypotheses held. See the LOG, "Milestone 6t". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 scale.py run knee32 && python3 knee32_6t.py` (8 runs, about 40 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Why does the knee move with the sync interval? (Milestone 6u, uncompressed runs at `H = 16, 32, 64`, 2 seeds, per-round delta norms): not because of the step's noise budget. The ratio of a worker delta's energy to the workers' disagreement, which sets how much noise rand-k adds per unit of omega relative to the noise the step already carries, is 1.07, 1.08 and 1.11, nearly flat and rising slightly with `H`, while the knee moves from omega 0.5 to 7.75. Rescaling omega by it does not collapse the 12 measured cells (chi-square 92.7 against 97.2, 10 dof). All three pre-registered hypotheses were refuted; the probe reproduced every baseline loss exactly. See the LOG, "Milestone 6u". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && python3 mechanism_6u.py run && python3 mechanism_6u.py report` (about 35 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Is it rand-k's sparsity? (Milestone 6v, omega 7, `H = 16` and `64`, 4 seeds each): no. Unbiased Gaussian noise of rand-k's energy with rand-k's per-coordinate variance and no coordinate dropped costs +0.014 at `H = 16` and -0.001 at `H = 64`, the same pattern as rand-k (+0.022, -0.002); noise spread evenly over each tensor costs +0.022 and +0.009. Both "same as rand-k" hypotheses held (the even-spread one by 1.9 se at `H = 64`); the registered H-gap for even-spread noise missed at 1.8 se. A build check reproduced a stored baseline to six decimals. See the LOG, "Milestone 6v". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && python3 noise_6v.py run && python3 noise_6v.py report` (about 90 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Do long inner loops wash out compression noise? (Milestone 6w, fork-and-inject at mid-training, omega 7, `H = 16` and `64`, 4 seeds each): no. One round of real rand-k noise injected into an otherwise identical run keeps all of its parameter-space energy after 256 inner steps at both `H` (it grows 6 to 7%), and the validation-loss bump it causes is larger at `H = 64` (+0.012 nats) than at `H = 16` (+0.006) and fades more slowly. Two of three pre-registered hypotheses were refuted and the third held only by a 0.6% margin. Since a whole run compressed at the same level is free at `H = 64`, single-round costs do not add up to the run's cost: the knee is a whole-trajectory effect. A control fork reproduced the reference exactly. See the LOG, "Milestone 6w". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && python3 contraction_6w.py run && python3 contraction_6w.py report` (9 runs, about 25 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- Why is compression free at `H = 64`? (Milestone 6x, fork at the last round, omega 7, `H = 16` and `64`, 4 seeds, 8 noise draws per fork): two effects with opposite signs. Each round of rand-k noise costs about the same when it is injected, at the end of training as at mid-training (+0.012 nats at `H = 64`, +0.006 at `H = 16`), and runs compressed throughout do not adapt to it (refuted at `H = 64`; 0.0008 less at `H = 16`). The trajectory before the last round, though, is worse than uncompressed at `H = 16` (+0.016) and **better** at `H = 64` (-0.014, se 0.0035, all four seeds). The visible break-even at `H = 64` is that gain cancelling the unrepaired last round, so a run that sends only its last sync uncompressed ends 0.014 nats below uncompressed training. Validation split and an untuned uncompressed baseline: a lead to check, not a recipe yet. All 16 trunks reproduce the stored runs exactly. See the LOG, "Milestone 6x". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && python3 lastround_6x.py run && python3 lastround_6x.py report` (16 runs, about 95 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).

Rand-k keep 1/8 on every sync but the last ends **0.014 nats below** plain averaging on the fresh batches (se 0.0063, 2.2 se, three of four seeds) and 0.020 below lr 0.7 (all four seeds). (Milestone 6z, the 6x recipe at omega 3, 15 and 31 and with Gaussian noise of rand-k's per-coordinate variance at omega 7, `H = 64`, 4 seeds, scored also on 6y's fresh batches): the gain is a property of outer-step noise, not of sparsity, and it is largest at the smallest compression tried. - Does the clean-last-sync recipe pay at a short sync interval? (Milestone 6ab, rand-k keep 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8 with a dense last sync, `H = 16`, 4 seeds, both batch sets): no gain, but free compression. Against uncompressed training on the fresh batches the recipe ends -0.004 (0.006) at keep 1/2, **-0.0004 (0.0035)** at keep 1/4 and **+0.014** (3.9 se) at keep 1/8, so the -0.018 H = 64 gain at keep 1/4 is absent at H = 16 (paired difference 4.4 se, exploratory), and keep 1/4 is break-even for a quarter of the bytes. The last sync's own cost is linear in omega at about half H = 64's slope (0.0007 to 0.0009 nats per unit). All 12 reproduction checks against the stored runs are bit-exact. See the LOG, "Milestone 6ab". Run: `cd experiments/gpt_scale && python3 recipe_6ab.py run && python3 recipe_6ab.py report` (16 runs, about 80 minutes on 4 cores, needs torch).
- And at H = 32? (Milestone 6ac, the same recipe at keep 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8 with a dense last sync, `H = 32`, 4 seeds, both batch sets): in between, and within noise of zero.
