# Which rungs of a ladder of digital twins are worth running? An exact multifidelity variance, a one-line rung condition, and a timestep-ladder check

*Stylised: Gaussian chains for the variance law; one plant (a saturated PD-controlled, mildly nonlinear mass-spring under random disturbance) for the ladder experiment, in which the "real" system is itself the finest-step simulation. Pure Python; every number is from `experiments/results.txt` (seeded, ~1 min). Simulated variances carry ≈2.6% sampling error (3,000–6,000 replications). No robot data. Preliminary.*

## Question
`twin-evaluation` shows that one biased twin, used as a control variate, saves real rollouts when its correlation `ρ` with the real return beats `ρ*(r)=2√r/(1+r)` for cost ratio `r`. Simulators come in ladders (timestep, mesh, sensor-model or contact-model fidelity), each rung cheaper and less faithful than the one above. Which rungs should a real-evaluation budget actually run, in what proportions, and does a rung that pays on its own still pay when a better twin is already in use?

## Method
Order the models by decreasing correlation with the real return: real (`ρ_0=1`, cost `c_0`), twins `1..k` with `ρ_i`, `c_i`, and `ρ_{k+1}=0`; put `a_i=ρ_i²−ρ_{i+1}²`. With nested samples `m_0≤m_1≤…≤m_k` (the estimator of Peherstorfer, Willcox & Gunzburger 2016, coefficients `α_i=ρ_iσ_0/σ_i`) the variance is `σ_0² Σ a_i/m_i`. It uses only second moments, so it is distribution-free. Under budget `C=Σ c_i m_i` the optimum is `m_i=C√(a_i/c_i)/S`, `S=Σ√(a_j c_j)`, variance `σ_0² S²/C`; it is attainable with nested samples iff `a_i/c_i` is nondecreasing (the "feasible" ladder). With one twin this is `(√(1−ρ²)+ρ√r)²`, the `twin-evaluation` law.

**Rung condition.** Appending a cheaper twin 2 below twin 1 replaces the term `ρ_1√c_1` by `√(c_1(ρ_1²−ρ_2²))+ρ_2√c_2`. Dividing by `ρ_1√c_1` with `x=ρ_2/ρ_1`, `r=c_2/c_1`, the ladder is better iff `√(1−x²)+x√r<1`, i.e.
`ρ_2/ρ_1 > ρ*(c_2/c_1) = 2√(c_2/c_1)/(1+c_2/c_1)`.
The rung is judged against its parent exactly as the first twin is judged against reality, with the *step* correlation `ρ_2/ρ_1` (for a Markov chain of fidelity levels, the correlation between adjacent levels). This is an algebraic identity of the variance formula; the substantive points are its form and the feasibility caveat below. Best ladders are found by brute force over subsets that are decreasing in `ρ` and feasible.

## Results
1. **Rung condition and feasibility.** On a grid of 1,505 (`ρ_1`, `x`, `r`) points the sign of the exact variance change equals the condition at every point (identity check). Of the 336 grid points where a rung pays below a twin that itself pays (`c_1=0.2c_0`), 10 give an infeasible ladder: twin 1 would be sampled less than the real system, so it is redundant given twin 2 and the extended-ladder variance is not attainable. `ρ*` for `r`=0.01/0.05/0.1/0.25/0.5/0.75 is 0.198/0.426/0.575/0.800/0.943/0.990: halving cost per rung demands a step correlation above 0.943; a 10× cost drop only 0.575. Example (`ρ_1=0.9`, `ρ_2=0.8`): appending changes variance by −30%/−9%/+9%/+23% at `r`=0.05/0.25/0.5/0.75.
2. **The nested estimator matches the variance law.** Gaussian chains with twin biases +0.7 and +1.5, 6,000 replications, optimal integer allocation: mean 1.0009/1.0001/0.9995 (se 0.001–0.0014, truth 1); simulated/exact variance 1.022/0.977/0.980 (allocations `m`=[51,154,666], [30,65,430], [51,116,608]); variance 0.0059/0.0064/0.0115 against real-only 0.0125/0.0167/0.0167.
3. **More rungs is not better.** For a geometric ladder with cost ratio `r` and step correlation `q` per rung, using all `K` rungs versus the best feasible subset (variance relative to real-only): `q=0.99, r=0.5`, `K=6`: 0.284 vs 0.196 (2 rungs); `q=0.95, r=0.5`: 0.913 vs 0.577; `q=0.98, r=0.25`, `K=6`: 0.159 vs 0.141 (3 rungs). With `q=0.9 < ρ*(0.5)` no single rung pays and all-rungs is worse than nothing (1.15→1.41 for `K`=1→6) while the best subset reaches 0.83 by skipping levels. When `q` is well above `ρ*` and costs fall fast (`q=0.8, r=0.1`) every rung is used and returns diminish (0.728→0.645 for `K`=1→6).
4. **Timestep ladder (E4).** Steps 1,2,4,8,16 × 1/160 s, costs 640/320/160/80/40 steps, budget of 200 real episodes (128,000 steps), 4,000-context pool, 3,000 resampled replications. With the disturbance fully replayed in the twins (`ρ`=0.9998/0.9990/0.9950/0.9763; step correlations 0.981–0.9998 all above `ρ*(½)=0.943`), the best single twin (step 8) has variance 0.204 of real-only (simulated 0.217, sim/exact 1.06), and the best ladder, levels 2 and 4 (skipping 1 and 3), 0.156 (simulated 0.156, sim/exact 1.005; allocation 23/215/1980 episodes); both are unbiased against the pool mean (se 0.003). When the twins replay only 90% of the disturbance's amplitude (`ρ≈0.87–0.89`, flat across levels) the best ladder is just the cheapest twin: 0.51 predicted, 0.52 simulated. At 70% (`ρ≈0.65–0.69`) 0.85 predicted, 0.89 simulated (within the ±2.6% error of the 0.995 measured for real-only) — a coarser ladder adds nothing when the ceiling is set by what the twin cannot replay, not by its step.
5. **A coarser rung can fail outright.** At step 0.2 s the same plant diverged in 2 of 20,000 contexts (floating overflow); one such episode in an early 600-context probe made the mean 5×10⁴⁸ and the correlation with the real return 0.09. Its moments do not exist for practical purposes, so it was excluded from the ladder: a rung needs a stable simulator before a correlation is meaningful.

## Limitations
The variance law and the rung condition are exact but take `ρ_i` and `c_i` as known; estimating them from pilots (`twin-pilot`) adds decision and allocation regret not modelled here, and with several correlations to estimate the plug-in cost is larger. Twins here share the real contexts (replay) or a fixed fraction of them; the cost of building each twin is ignored. The ladder experiment uses one plant, one return, and a pool bootstrap (the population is the pool, so the variance formula is exact for it but the pool's ρ's are estimates of the true ones); the "real" system is a fine-step simulation. Costs are step counts, not wall-clock. The 10/336 infeasible cases show that the closed-form condition is a statement about the unconstrained variance; feasibility must be checked. Preliminary.

## Next steps
