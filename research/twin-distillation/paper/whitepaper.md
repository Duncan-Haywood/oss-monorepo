# Twin distillation: what a too-clean digital twin does to a cloned student

*Stylised: scalar plant `x' = a x + b u + w`, `a=0.9, b=1, q=1, r=0.1`, `Var w = 1` (real). The twin has actuator gain `b̂` and process-noise variance `σ̂²`. The teacher sees `x` and applies the twin-optimal LQR gain `g = 0.8233` (at `b̂=1`). The student sees `z = x + ζ`, `Var ζ = s`, and is a linear memoryless policy `u = −k z`. "Regret" is real stage cost minus that of the best memoryless student for the real plant. Pure Python; see `tests/` (9 tests) and `experiments/results.txt`. Every number below is what `experiments/run.py` printed. No real robot data.*

## Question
The usual sim-to-real recipe trains a teacher with privileged state in simulation and distils it into a student that sees only onboard sensors (Chen et al. 2019; Lee et al. 2020; Kumar et al. 2021). `twin-transfer` asked what a wrong twin costs a controller trained by optimisation. Here the student is trained by *imitation*, so the twin enters twice: through the teacher, and through the state distribution on which the student's regression is fitted. What does a twin whose noise is wrong do to the second channel?

## Setup and closed forms
Regressing the teacher's action `−g x` on `z = x + ζ` over a loop with state variance `P` gives (no intercept, Gaussian)
`k_BC = g P / (P + s)` (attenuation), and `P` for a teacher loop in the twin is `σ̂²/(1 − (a − b̂ g)²)`. The student's own loop in a plant `(a, b, σ²)` has `P(k) = (σ² + b²k²s)/(1 − (a − bk)²)` and stage cost `q P + r k²(P + s)`. Consequently `k_BC` depends on the twin's noise only through `σ̂²/s` (tested exactly). On-policy imitation (the DAgger limit) is the root of `k = g P(k)/(P(k)+s)`; it exists in `(0, g)` by the intermediate value theorem and was unique on all 36 configurations checked. Monte-Carlo checks: cloned gain 0.2755 (theory) vs 0.2756, 0.2757 and 0.9715 (theory 0.9714) in three twins, 20×10⁵ steps each; cost 1.6520 vs 1.6504 and 2.0302 vs 2.0300.

## Results
1. **On-policy imitation of an exact teacher is nearly optimal.** With no twin error the DAgger fixed point is within 3.5% (relative cost regret) of the best memoryless student across `a∈{.5,.9,.99}`, `b∈{.5,1,2}`, four `(q,r)` pairs and `s∈[0.01,20]` (252 cells; worst case `s=20`, `a=0.99`). At `s=0.5` its regret is 2×10⁻⁵. Plain BC in an *exact* twin is already off: regret 0.0054 (s=0.5) and 0.098 (s=2), because the teacher's own loop is tighter than the student's (`P` is smaller, so the attenuation is stronger).
2. **A too-clean twin teaches a lazy student.** Exact actuator, twin process noise `σ̂²` from 0.10 to 4: BC gain at `s=0.5` runs 0.138 → 0.732 (real-optimal 0.597). At `σ̂²=0.25` BC regret is 0.357 (s=0.5) and 1.114 (s=2), against 0.128 and 0.309 for DAgger in the same twin (2.8× and 3.6×) and 0.119 and 0.280 for direct optimisation in the twin. A too-noisy twin over-drives the student (σ̂²=4: 0.040 and 0.125). So of the regret of imitation in a wrong-noise twin, most is a *distribution* effect that on-policy data in the same twin removes only partly (twin noise still enters `P(k)`), while direct optimisation in the twin does about as well as DAgger.
3. **Behaviour cloning's errors cancel, unreliably.** With a wrong actuator (`b̂` from 0.5 to 2) and correct noise, BC regret can be *below* the teacher's own floor (on-policy real imitation of the twin teacher): at `b̂=0.7`, s=2, BC 0.0037 vs floor 0.0791; at `b̂=1.0`, s=2, BC 0.098 vs 0.0003. The BC gain equals the real-optimal gain exactly at `b̂=0.906` for `s=0.5` and `b̂=0.642` for `s=2`: the attenuation offsets the teacher's over- or under-gain only at a point that moves with the sensor. With both errors present (`b̂=0.7, σ̂²=0.25`) BC regret is 0.155 (s=0.5) and 0.785 (s=2) against floors 0.083 and 0.079, and direct twin optimisation gets 0.012 and 0.081 (a cancellation in that case too). None of this is a robustness property; it is the sum of two errors.
4. **DAgger with pooled data is slow.** In the `σ̂²=0.25`, `s=0.5` twin the aggregated-data gain by round is 0.2755, 0.3456, 0.3609, … 0.3798 at round 10 against a fixed point 0.3859; regret 0.357 → 0.193 → … → 0.137 (limit in the twin: 0.128). Pooling weights old, off-distribution data equally, so convergence is `~1/n`.
5. **Match one scalar, not the twin.** Deploy the student, log `z`, estimate `P̂ = mean(z²) − s`, then choose injected teacher-action noise `ν = ((1−ĉ²)P̂ − σ̂²)/b̂²` (`ĉ = a − b̂ g`) so the twin's demonstration variance equals `P̂`; labels stay the clean teacher action (DART; Laskey et al. 2017). With exact real statistics the twin student's regret goes 0.357 (plain BC) → 0.0035 → 0.00003 in two rounds, reaching the real DAgger gain 0.5944 — without privileged real state, which DAgger would need. With finite real logs (one round, 300 repetitions) the mean regret is 0.0058, 0.0042, 0.0037, 0.0035 at n = 100, 300, 1000, 10⁴ steps (90th percentile 0.014, 0.0089, 0.0060, 0.0042), and no run had `P̂ ≤ 0` or was unstable.

## Limitations
One state, one gain, Gaussian noise, known sensor noise `s`, memoryless linear student (a student with memory would filter the sensor and shrink the whole effect); the teacher is exact-state LQR, so the "imitation is nearly optimal" result is specific to LQ teachers; the demonstration distribution is matched in one scalar, which suffices in a scalar model but would need a covariance in higher dimension; the twin's actuator error is untouched by DART (it stays in the teacher); the DAgger limit is the infinite-data one. No nonlinear, contact-rich or vision setting was run, so nothing here says how large the effect is for a real manipulator or vehicle; the analysis predicts the *mechanism* (regression attenuation tied to the demonstration state variance) and its scale in the linear case.

## Relevance
When a twin is used to distil a privileged teacher into a sensor-limited student, the twin's *state statistics* — not just its dynamics — become part of the student's parameters. Measure the real closed-loop state variance once, and match it in the twin by injecting noise into demonstrations, rather than hoping the twin's noise floor is right. Do not credit plain cloning with robustness when it does well: check whether the twin error and the attenuation happen to cancel.
