# Watchdog twin: an i.i.d.-loss heartbeat twin says a missed-beat watchdog almost never false-trips, and on a bursty link it trips every few seconds

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: a heartbeat link between a robot and its supervisor modelled as a two-state (Gilbert) channel, with the heartbeat of a tick lost iff the link is in its bad state (mean loss `p = 0.05`, mean loss-burst length `L`), and a watchdog that e-stops after `m` consecutive lost heartbeats. The twin drops each heartbeat independently at the same `p`. With the link statistically unchanged every trip is a false trip. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`; exact mean times to trip by exact rational linear recursion, checked against enumeration of all paths, a survival-sum identity and Monte Carlo): (1) at `m = 5` the twin's mean time to a false trip is 3.4e6 ticks (18.7 h at 50 Hz) and the real link's is 637, 235, 286, 453, 988 ticks for `L = 2, 5, 10, 20, 50`, i.e. 3.4e3 to 1.4e4 times sooner (4.7 to 20 s at 50 Hz); (2) it is not monotone in `L`: at fixed mean loss longer bursts are rarer, so the worst case is `L ≈ m` (argmin 3.10, 5.15, 8.30, 12.45, 20.75 for `m = 3, 5, 8, 12, 20`) where the mean time to trip is `≈ e·m/p` (6–20% below it, closer as `m` grows) however large the twin's value is (1e26 ticks at `m = 20`); (3) the large-`m` rate `(p/L)(1−1/L)^(m−1)` matches the exact value to 1.2% (`m = 10, L = 5`) and 0.15% (`m = 40, L = 10`), so `m ≈ 1 + ln(target·p/L)/ln(L/(L−1))`, which grows linearly in `L`: for a 1e6-tick mean time to trip the twin picks `m = 5` (0.10 s to detect a dead link), the real link needs `m = 16, 43, 82, 154` for `L = 2, 5, 10, 20` (0.32 to 3.08 s); (4) with `L` unknown, guaranteeing a mean time to trip of 1e4 ticks over all burst lengths needs `m = 192` (3.8 s) and 1e6 ticks would need `m ≥ 18394` (368 s), because the worst burst length adapts to `m`.

```bash
cd research/watchdog-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 12 tests, about 5 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # about 2 min; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The simulation-fidelity and sim-to-real direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>) and the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>) for UAV and field-robot safety supervision, and the multi-robot communication setting of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The two-state loss channel is classical (Gilbert 1960; Elliott 1963). Companion to `jitter-twin` (persistence of sensor delay, there on control, here on a supervisory timeout) and `stop-twin` (rare-event counting in a twin).

Stylised: simulated "real" system, no link traces; loss is exactly 1 in the bad state and 0 in the good one (a Gilbert, not a Gilbert–Elliott, channel), geometric sojourns, a stationary start, one fixed `p = 0.05` and a 50 Hz heartbeat in the tables, and the watchdog is a plain consecutive-miss counter (no windowed or adaptive rule, no recovery logic). The twin is a deliberately naive baseline: a twin that included burst structure would reproduce these numbers by construction. MIT.
