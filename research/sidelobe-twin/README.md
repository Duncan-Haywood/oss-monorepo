# Sidelobe twin: a point-response radar twin has no range sidelobes, so a strong target never ghosts in it and always does in the real filter

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: a pulse-compression (matched-filter) radar whose output around a point target is the window's response `ρ_w(x)` (x in resolution cells; rect: `sinc`, Hann and Hamming: closed-form sums of shifted sincs), with unit-power complex Gaussian noise per cell and a cell threshold `T² = −ln Pfa0` (Pfa0 = 1e-6). The twin treats a target as a single cell at its own range and nothing elsewhere. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`; exact Rician/Marcum exceedance checked against numerical integration and Monte Carlo): (1) peak sidelobes −13.26, −31.47, −42.68 dB for rect, Hann, Hamming, at the cost of a 1.63× / 1.47× wider 3 dB mainlobe and 1.76 / 1.34 dB of SNR; (2) the ghost false-alarm probability at the peak sidelobe goes from the twin's 1e-6 to 0.020 at S = 20 dB and to 1.0 at 30 dB for rect (Hann 0.020 at 40 dB, 1.0 at 50 dB; Hamming 0.010 at 50 dB, 1.0 at 60 dB); (3) the largest strong-target SNR with ghost Pfa ≤ 1e-4 is 12.9 dB for rect, 32.9 dB for Hann and 43.7 dB for Hamming, a gain over rect that is exactly the sidelobe-level difference plus the taper's ENBW loss (19.97 and 30.76 dB); (4) the zone around a strong target inside which a ghost exceeds Pfa 1e-4 has radius 3, 10, 33, 105, 331 cells for rect at S = 20…60 dB (growing `√10` per 10 dB), but for tapers it is not ordered by peak sidelobe: at S = 60 dB Hann's radius is 7 cells and Hamming's 42, because Hamming's far sidelobes decay like `1/x` and Hann's like `1/x³`; (5) a weak target 13 dB above noise next to a 40 dB target is "detected" with probability 1.0 at rect's first sidelobe and 0.78 at 5.5 cells, but the same cell without it already alarms with probability 1 and 0.999, so Pd − Pfa is 0 and −0.22 (the twin: 0.874).

```bash
cd research/sidelobe-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 17 tests, about 2 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # about 1 min; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The radar sensor-simulation and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>) and the digital-twin simulation-fidelity direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Pulse compression, window sidelobes and equivalent noise bandwidth are classical (Harris 1978; Richards 2014; Skolnik 2008). Companion to `glint-twin`, `multipath-twin`, `radar-clutter-twin` and `radar-detection-twin` (what a simplified radar twin gets wrong, here on false alarms rather than misses).

Stylised: simulated "real" system, no radar data; a single strong target and one weak one at a known cell, noise white in the output cell, perfect matched filter (no Doppler mismatch, no clutter, no ADC or compression-point limits, no CFAR adaptation, which would raise the threshold near the strong target and trade ghosts for masking), uniform relative phase, and windows only from the cosine family. The twin is a deliberately naive baseline; a twin that included the window response would reproduce the sidelobes by construction. MIT.
