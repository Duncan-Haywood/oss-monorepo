# An odometry twin fitted to one-step noise cannot see drift: relocalisation intervals too long, loop closures rejected exactly when they matter

*Stylised: 1-D along-track error. Each traversal has one persistent bias `b~N(0,σ_b²)` plus white step noise `σ_w²`; `σ_1²=σ_b²+σ_w²=0.02²` m² per step, persistent fraction `ρ=σ_b²/σ_1²`. The twin is fitted to one-step increment statistics and treats them as white. Pure Python; every number is from `experiments/results.txt`. Closed forms are checked against 30,000 simulated traversals; the closed-loop rows are Monte Carlo (200 runs × 400 segments, no standard errors computed).*

## Question
A simulator of a robot's sensors and motion is often tuned so that its odometry noise matches the real robot's step-to-step statistics. Real odometry error also contains slowly varying components (wheel-scale error, gyro bias, slip on a surface) that are nearly constant over a traversal. Both give the *same* one-step variance, so a twin fitted to it cannot tell them apart, yet the error after `T` steps is `T b+Σw`, with variance `σ_1²(ρT²+(1−ρ)T)` rather than the twin's `σ_1²T`. The ratio is exactly `1+(T−1)ρ`. What does a perception stack tuned in such a twin get wrong, and how much real data repairs it? This is the odometry analogue of the sensor-model mismatch studied in `radar-clutter-twin` and `occupancy-twin`; the drift-versus-white-noise distinction is classical (Allan 1966).

## Model
Two decisions use the twin's variance. (a) Relocalisation interval: the largest `T` with `P(|E_T|>tol)≤δ`. (b) Loop-closure gating: a closure offered after `T` steps has residual `r=E_T+v`, `v~N(0,ν²)`; it is accepted if `|r|≤g√S`, with `S` the model's variance plus `ν²` (a Mahalanobis gate; Bar-Shalom et al. 2001). Aliased closures (place-recognition mismatches; cf. Sünderhauf & Protzel 2012) add a fixed offset `d`. Acceptance probabilities are differences of normal CDFs and are exact. A closed-loop chain runs repeated segments with a filter belief `P` (grows by the model variance per segment, Kalman update on acceptance) and reports the share of segments left with `|x|>tol`.

## Results (`σ_1=0.02` m, `ν=0.05` m, `g=3`, `tol=0.5` m, `δ=0.05`)
1. **Variance ratio.** Real/twin sd ratio at `T=100`: 1.41/2.44/4.56 for `ρ`=0.01/0.05/0.2 (variance ratio 1.99/5.95/20.8); at `T=400` and `ρ=0.05` it is 20.95 (simulation 20.81). At `T=1` the two coincide, which is why one-step fitting cannot detect it.
2. **Relocalisation.** The twin allows `T=163` steps. Real intervals are 87/48/27/13 (`ρ`=0.01/0.05/0.2/1), so the twin's interval is 1.9×/3.4×/6.1×/12.8× too long and the real miss probability at it is 22.6%/51.6%/73.4%/87.8% against the claimed 5%.
3. **Loop-closure recall.** A true closure passes a 3σ gate with probability 0.9973 at every `T` if the gate is calibrated (or the twin is right). With the twin's gate at `ρ=0.05` it is 0.992/0.908/0.793/0.642/0.491/0.327/0.168 at `T`=10/50/100/200/400/1000/4000; at `ρ=0.2`, 0.961 at `T=10` and 0.502 at `T=100`. It decays like `2Φ(g/√(ρT))−1`: the loops that are long enough to need a closure are the ones the gate discards.
4. **Closed loop (`ρ=0.05`).** Without aliasing, the share of segments left above 0.5 m is 18.3%/72.3%/91.6% (twin) vs 0.3% (calibrated) at `T`=50/200/800, and RMS error is 0.76/8.8/42.3 m vs 0.07/0.17/0.57 m. Rejections make the filter belief grow only at the twin's rate, so once the error passes the gate it is rarely corrected: a runaway, not a bias. With aliasing (10% of closures, `d`=1 m) the calibrated gate is no longer safe: 7.5%/10.5%/10.2% exceedances, because its wide gate accepts 32%/98%/99.5% of aliased closures at `T`=50/200/800 (exact single-shot acceptance at `T=200`: 0.973). The twin's gate rejects aliases (14–21%) mostly by rejecting everything. When drift uncertainty exceeds `d`, no variance calibration can separate aliases from truth: this is a limit of gating, not a repair target.
5. **Repair by lag-`T` variance fitting.** Inflate the twin variance by `R̂`, the mean squared ground-truth error at lag `T` over the twin's, from `n` traversals (`T=200, ρ=0.05`, `R=10.95`). Mean recall: 0.805/0.905/0.970/0.986/0.993/0.996 for `n`=1/2/5/10/20/50 (twin 0.642, calibrated 0.9973). Mean alias acceptance rises correspondingly, 0.706→0.970 (calibrated 0.973). Ratios do not transfer across lags: a ratio fitted at `T=50` (3.45) and used at `T=200`/`1000` gives recall 0.909/0.565.

## Limitations
1-D, Gaussian, one bias per traversal (no bias random walk, no heading coupling, no loop geometry, no pose-graph optimisation); the twin is *assumed* to be fitted to one-step variance rather than a real simulator's identified noise model; gate constant and alias offset are chosen, not measured; closed-loop numbers are Monte Carlo without error bars; the fit uses ground-truth errors at the loop length, which needs a reference such as motion capture. No real odometry data. Preliminary and not evaluated on hardware.

## Next steps
Bias as a random walk (Allan-variance slope fit across lags); 2-D pose graphs with switchable or robust closures; domain-randomising the bias inside the twin (`randomized-twin`); scoring the twin's drift claim with a proper score (`twin-elicitation`, `twin-audit`); multi-robot map merging where inter-robot closures have both robots' drift.
