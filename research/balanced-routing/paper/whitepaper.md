# Balanced routing as a congestion-priced market: certificates, noise floors, and audits for capacity-constrained expert routing

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Sparse or soft routing of tokens to experts is usually balanced with an auxiliary loss or, more recently, with per-expert bias terms updated by the sign of the load error. We treat the balanced router as a cost-function market with capacity constraints (see `market-routing` for the unconstrained case). The entropic capacity-constrained assignment is a softmax with an added per-expert *congestion price* `p_i ≥ 0`, the Lagrange multipliers of the capacity constraints; the price vector solves a smooth concave dual whose value `D(p)` upper-bounds the optimum for *every* `p`. Pairing it with a feasibility-repaired primal gives a certificate a third party can compute from `(scores, prices)` alone; on `B=400` tokens over `N=8` experts the certified gap is below `10⁻⁴` at the optimum and always dominates the true dual suboptimality. The sign-update rule has two distinct error terms: a sampling floor of about `2.5/√B` in max relative overload that no step size removes (halving for every 4× batch), and a step-size-proportional limit cycle on a fixed batch (22.6% overload at γ=0.2, 4.5% at 0.05, 1.2% at 0.01). For two experts and Gaussian score gaps `d~N(μ,σ²)` the utility lost by forcing an even split is closed form with price shift `t = μ` (the median), checked to 3 decimals by Monte Carlo. Finally a load claim is audited by re-routing `⌈ln(2N/δ)/(2ε²)⌉` tokens: 7,211 tokens for `ε=0.02, δ=0.05, N=8` gave 0/400 violations. Everything is on synthetic Gaussian scores; we make no claim about trained MoE layers.

## 1. Model
`B` tokens, `N` experts, scores `s_ti`, temperature `τ`, capacity fractions `c_i` with `Σc_i ≥ 1`. The primal is `max_x Σ x_ti s_ti + τ Σ_t H(x_t)` subject to `Σ_i x_ti = 1` and `Σ_t x_ti ≤ c_i B`. Its dual is
`D(p) = τ Σ_t logsumexp_i((s_ti − p_i)/τ) + B Σ_i c_i p_i, p ≥ 0,`
with optimal assignment `x_ti ∝ exp((s_ti − p_i)/τ)`. So the router is an LMSR-style softmax whose only change is a per-expert tax, `∂D/∂p_i = Bc_i − load_i(p)` (finite-difference tested), and complementary slackness holds at the solution: `p_i > 0` only if expert `i` is exactly full (tested, and slack capacity gives `p = 0`). The Hessian is `(1/τ)Σ_t(diag x_t − x_t x_tᵀ)` with operator norm at most `B/(2τ)`, so projected gradient with step `2τ/B` converges; accelerated projected gradient reached residual `10⁻⁷` in 81, 135, 210, 447 iterations for `τ = 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1` (E1): iterations grow 5.5× for a 10× colder router.

## 2. Price of balance
At capacity `1.25/N` (E1) the soft objective falls 1.15%, 3.59%, 5.35%, 6.02% for `τ = 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1`, and the largest price rises 0.69→0.97: colder routers are more loaded at the popular experts and pay more to be balanced.
For the hard-routing two-expert case with gap `d = s₁−s₂ ~ N(μ,σ²)` and expert 1 limited to half the tokens, the constrained rule picks 1 iff `d > t` with `P(d>t) = ½`, so `t = μ`, and the utility loss is `E[d·1(d>0)] − E[d·1(d>μ)]`, with `E[d·1(d>t)] = μ(1−Φ(z)) + σφ(z)`, `z=(t−μ)/σ`. For `σ=1`: `μ = 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2` gives 0.0124, 0.0489, 0.1844, 0.6095 against Monte Carlo 0.0124, 0.0489, 0.1846, 0.6098 (E5), and exactly zero at `μ=0` where balance is free. The loss is close to quadratic in `μ` for small `μ` (0.0124→0.0489 is ×3.9 for ×2) and grows more slowly than quadratically after (0.1844→0.6095 is ×3.3 for ×2).

## 3. Certificates from prices alone
Weak duality gives `OPT ≤ D(p)` for every `p ≥ 0`. For a lower bound, take `x(p)`, mix it with the uniform assignment (loads `B/N`) by the smallest weight `λ` restoring all capacities, and evaluate the (concave) primal: a feasible primal value. This needs strict slack `c_i > 1/N` (Slater); the code asserts it, after a first version at `c=1/N` returned meaningless gaps. The difference is a certified gap. At the solution it is `≤ 6·10⁻⁵` (E1). For an under-converged price vector (E2, `τ=0.5, c=0.2`): after 1, 10, 100 dual steps the true suboptimality `D−D*` is 5.56, 0.22, ≈0 and the certified gaps are 112, 13.9, ≈0: always an upper bound on `D−D*` (checked in every row), but conservative by 10–20× early on because the repair mixes in the uniform assignment. So a verifier who receives only `p` can bound how far from optimal the claimed routing is at `O(BN)` cost, without re-solving.

## 4. Sign-update balancing
The bias update `p_i ← p_i + γ·sign(load_i − Bc_i)` on a fresh minibatch (E3, `N=8`, `τ=0.5`, `c=1/N`) has a floor in mean max relative overload over the last 600 of 1200 steps. At the smallest step γ=0.003: 31.8%, 15.9%, 7.6%, 4.0% for `B = 64, 256, 1024, 4096`, i.e. `≈ 2.5/√B` (2.5, 2.5, 2.4, 2.6 times `1/√B`): it halves per 4× batch and is a property of *sampling*, not of the update. Larger steps add to it (12.6% at γ=0.1, `B=4096`). On a *fixed* batch (E4, `B=400`) the sign rule cannot converge: it enters a limit cycle whose overload is 22.6% at γ=0.2 (never reaching 5%), 4.5% at 0.05 (15 steps to first reach 5%) and 1.2% at 0.01 (71 steps), sublinear in γ (overload ×5.0 for γ×4 from 0.05 to 0.2, and ×3.8 for γ×5 from 0.01 to 0.05). So a step size trades speed against a floor, and the batch size sets a floor that the certified-price approach of §3 (which is exactly solvable on the batch) does not have, at the cost of solving a per-batch dual.

## 5. Auditing a load claim
A provider claims prices `p` and that every expert's load fraction is within `c_i`. A verifier re-routes `m` uniformly sampled tokens from the committed scores and prices. By Hoeffding and a union bound `m = ⌈ln(2N/δ)/(2ε²)⌉` suffices for all `N` load fractions within `ε`. For `N=8, ε=0.02, δ=0.05`, `m = 7,211`; over 400 trials the worst deviation was 0.0103 with 0/400 violations (the bound is conservative by about 2×). A provider that shaves the price on the most congested expert by 0.3 moves its true load from 0.147 to 0.201 (cap 0.156), detectable with ≈5.8k samples. The audit needs the scores to be committed (see `reproducible-refereed-training`); it detects misreported *loads*, not wrong scores.

## 6. Limitations
(1) Synthetic i.i.d. Gaussian scores with fixed expert means; trained routers have correlated, drifting scores and token-order effects. (2) Soft (entropic) assignment; hard top-k is discontinuous and only the two-expert closed form covers it. (3) Static batch capacity, no dropped tokens, no per-device communication cost. (4) The certificate needs Slater slack and is loose early. (5) The comparison to aux-loss-free balancing is of an idealised sign rule on synthetic data, not an implementation of any specific system. Contributions: the price/dual view of balancing with a certificate a verifier can compute, separated sampling versus step-size error floors for sign-update balancing, a closed-form two-expert price of balance, and a sample-size rule for load audits.
