## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 4c: does a REMUS-100-class hotel load, read from its endurance figures, put the AUV above or below the glider's break-even? (planned before running)
- Why: 4b ended with "the break-even AUV hotel multiple (median 4.87 times the glider's) makes the measured power budgets decisive". The vendor's endurance figures give a sourced total power, from which the hotel load follows once the model's own propulsion power is subtracted. Sourced (all approximate, vendor marketing): REMUS 100 battery 1.5 kWh, endurance 12 h at 1.5 m/s ([UST magazine](https://www.ust-media.com/ust-magazine/UST008/files/assets/basic-html/page-86.html); [Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/hydroid-launches-next-gen-remus-100-auv/)); a 2022 datasheet ([REMUS 100B](https://bluezonegroup.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/REMUS-100B-3-31-22_web.pdf)) gives 8 h at 3 kn (1.54 m/s) with standard sensors, 38.6 kg, 1.85 m, 0.19 m diameter. Total power at 1.5 m/s is therefore 125 W (12 h) to 188 W (8 h). The 22 h / 1 kWh older-generation figure (45 W) was seen in a search snippet only and is not used for the headline.
- Plan (`experiments/run_remus.py`, `src/` unchanged): AUV geometry L = 1.85 m, D = 0.19 m, V = 38.6 kg / 1025 (neutral), wetted area pi D L (so c_s = S / sqrt(V L)), form factor k in {0.1, 0.2, 0.3}, drivetrain efficiency as the model's 0.55 x 0.85 and also 0.7 x 0.9 (swept, unsourced). Implied hotel H = P_total - P_prop(1.5 m/s) for P_total in {125, 150, 188} W; usable battery fraction is folded into P_total. Then the 4b glider cases (Slocum coefficients, same grid) against this AUV at H: fraction of cases with the glider's range greater, and the AUV's best range.
- **Predictions (before running):** (1) Implied H is above 60 W in every case (prior 80%: propulsion at 1.5 m/s for this hull should be tens of watts, not 100+). (2) The glider has the longer best range in over 90% of the 4b cases at the implied H (prior 85%; 4b gave 99.9% at x20 of a 0.7 to 5 W hotel, i.e. 14 to 100 W). Refuted: (1) any case under 60 W; (2) 90% or fewer.
- Limits known in advance: the vendor figures disagree and are not test data; the endurance may include battery reserve and payload sensors, so H is "everything but propulsion in the model", not a measured hotel load; the model's hull has no appendage drag (fins, thruster duct), which would lower the implied H; the glider side is unchanged from 4b (hotel, pump efficiency and dive energy still swept).

### Outcome of milestone 4c (`experiments/run_remus.py`, under 1 s, deterministic; `experiments/results_remus.txt`; 18 AUV cases x 810 glider cases)
- Model propulsion power at 1.5 m/s for the 38.6 kg, 1.85 m, 0.19 m hull is 13 to 21 W (form factor 0.1 to 0.3, drivetrain efficiency 0.47 or 0.63). Subtracting from the vendor total (125 to 188 W) leaves an implied non-propulsion load of 104 to 175 W (median 133 W).
- **(1) held:** the implied load is above 60 W in all 18 cases (minimum 104 W). It is 21 to 250 times the glider's swept 0.7 to 5 W (4b's break-even was 2.4 to 10.7 times).
- **(2) held, and at the limit:** the glider has the longer best range in 100% of the 810 cases against every one of the 18 AUV cases. AUV best energy per metre is 83 to 125 J/m (65 to 43 km on 1.5 kWh, close to the vendor's 50 to 72 km), against a glider median near 15 J/m at 5 W hotel and well under that at 0.7 W; median AUV/glider energy ratio 5.4.
- Reading: with a hotel load read from the vendor's own endurance, the 4b conclusion changes from "depends on the unsourced power budgets" to "the glider wins on energy per metre unless the AUV's non-propulsion load is below about 5 to 24 W", which is 4 to 20 times lower than the vendor figures imply. Energy per metre is not speed or time: the glider moves at 0.2 m/s against 1.5 m/s, so 500 km takes about 29 days. That is what the range comparison leaves out.
- Limits: the vendor endurance is marketing, bundles reserve and sensors, and cannot separate hotel from propulsion except through the model (no appendage drag, thruster duct, or free-flooded sections, all of which would lower the implied hotel and so the AUV's penalty); the AUV best speed is pinned at the grid's 1.5 m/s upper edge because hotel dominates, so the AUV range is if anything a lower bound above 1.5 m/s (a faster AUV is outside the vendor point used to infer the load); the glider side is 4b's, unchanged. The 22 h / 1 kWh older figure was not used.
- Next: a Slocum G2/G3 or Seaglider datasheet for the glider's hotel and pump (the glider side is still swept), time-to-complete as a second metric, then milestone 3 (Holtrop-Mennen).
## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 5 (hydrofoil rung): can a foilborne craft out-range the displacement monohull at 200 kg? (prediction stated before the sweep was run)
- Why: H5 rungs. Milestone 4c (REMUS hotel load, PR 10954) is claimed by another run, so this takes the hydrofoil rung. Plan (`experiments/run_foil.py`, `src/marine_hull/foil.py`): Prandtl lifting-line foil (induced drag CL^2/(pi e AR)) plus section drag CD0, two flat-plate struts with ITTC 1957 friction, a junction/spray multiplier; weight carried by the foil, so CL = W/(q S) must lie in [0.05, CL_max] (takeoff limit). Against milestone 2's 4 m Wigley monohull (k 0.25, Michell scale 1). Same 200 kg, 25% battery, 30 W hotel, efficiencies. Grid 3^7 x 2 = 2,916 cases: foil area 0.02 to 0.08 m^2, AR 6 to 14, e 0.8 to 0.95, CD0 0.008 to 0.02, strut draft 0.3 to 0.5 m, multiplier 1 to 1.4, CL_max 0.8 to 1.6, battery 150 to 250 Wh/kg; speeds 1 to 10 m/s.
- **Prediction:** the foil out-ranges the monohull in under 5% of cases (prior 90%): at 5+ m/s the thrust needed is W/(L/D), about 150 to 250 N, against a few newtons of displacement resistance at 1 m/s. Refuted if 5% or more.
- Unsourced, swept: every foil coefficient (CD0, e, CL_max) and the multiplier. Not modelled: ventilation, free-surface wave drag on the foil, foil control power, takeoff hump.

### Outcome (`experiments/results_foil.txt`, under 1 s, deterministic; 26 tests pass)
- **Prediction held:** the foil out-ranges the monohull in 0 of 2,916 cases. Foilborne flight is feasible in 1,620 (the rest need CL above CL_max at every speed to 10 m/s). Range ratio foil/mono among feasible cases: min 0.064, median 0.130, max 0.248; foil median 105 km against 824 km for the monohull. Best case (area 0.08 m^2, AR 14, CD0 0.008, no multiplier) reaches 204 km at 7.75 m/s, still a quarter of the monohull.
- Reading: for endurance-limited survey the foil is a speed option (5 to 10 m/s transit) bought with about 4 to 15 times the energy per metre, not a range option. In 369 feasible cases the best speed sits at the 10 m/s grid ceiling, so those foil ranges are lower bounds on the foil's own optimum only if drag keeps falling; speed is not the question here since the ratio stays below 0.25.
- Limits: all foil coefficients swept not sourced; no tank data checked; an idealised foil with no ventilation. The result is a bound from the induced-plus-friction floor, which the omitted effects only raise.
- Next: milestone 4c (PR 10954), then SWATH and planing rungs, battery from `energy-density`.

## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 4b: does the glider-vs-AUV verdict survive Slocum coefficients taken from a source? (planned before running)
- Why: milestone 4 swept every glider coefficient because the Merckelbach papers were unreachable (the hosts still 403 or fail DNS). The MIT-licensed `gliderflight` package (PyPI 1.2.1, Merckelbach's implementation of the Slocum flight model of Merckelbach et al. 2010 and 2019) ships the Slocum values, so they can be read from its source. Sourced there: wing area S = 0.1 m^2, aspect ratio 7, sweep 43 deg, Oswald e = 0.8, hull lift ah = 3.8 /rad, hull induced drag Cd1_hull = 9.7 /rad^2, wing lift slope from its own parameterisation aw = 2 pi AR / (2 + sqrt(AR^2 (1 + tan^2 Omega) + 4)) = 3.73 /rad, so lift slope a = aw + ah = 7.5 /rad and induced K = Cd1_hull + aw^2/(pi e AR) = 10.5 /rad^2 (the package's calibration examples use 10.5); parasite drag Cd0 is calibrated per deployment: its examples and docs use 0.15 to 0.16 and one calibration converges to 0.31. Milestone 4's grid (a 2 to 5, K 0.1 to 0.5) did not contain this point at all.
- Plan (`experiments/run_slocum.py`, `src/marine_hull/glider.py` unchanged): glider a, K, S fixed at the sourced values, Cd0 in {0.15, 0.23, 0.31}; swept because unsourced: buoyancy B (10 values, 1 to 10 N), pump efficiency {0.3, 0.5, 0.7}, dive energy {0, 5 kJ, 20 kJ}, hotel {0.7, 2, 5} W (0.7 W is the NOC average sensor figure quoted for gliders, a lower bound); AUV as in milestone 4 (52 kg, three hulls) with AUV hotel multiple x1, x5, x20. Outputs: glider best range against AUV best range at equal hotel; the AUV hotel multiple at which the two tie (break-even), per case; glider horizontal speed at the best-range point.
- **Predictions (before running):** (1) At equal hotel load the glider still has the longer best range in under 25% of cases (prior 65%; milestone 4 gave 5.9%, but a lift slope of 7.5 and L/D near 3 are better than the old box). (2) The break-even AUV hotel multiple has median between 2 and 10 (prior 60%). Refuted: (1) 25% or more; (2) median outside 2 to 10.
- Limits known in advance: the hotel loads and pump efficiency remain unsourced (swept); pump energy per dive is an order-of-magnitude check only (NOC: about 65 W for 2 min at 1000 m); steady glide, no current; the package is a derivative source for the numbers, not the original papers.

### Outcome of milestone 4b (`experiments/run_slocum.py`, under 1 s, deterministic; `experiments/results_slocum.txt`; 2,430 cases)
- Sourced lift slope 7.5 /rad and K 10.5 /rad^2 give glide angles 18 to 26 deg (median 22), horizontal speed median 0.21 m/s (10th to 90th 0.12 to 0.33), the right range for a Slocum.
- **(1) held, and more strongly:** at equal hotel load the glider has the longer best range in 0 of 2,430 cases (milestone 4's loose box: 5.9%). Median AUV/glider energy-per-metre ratio 0.36, i.e. the glider spends about 2.8 times the energy per metre: at 0.2 m/s even 0.7 W of hotel load costs 3.5 J/m, and the glider's L/D of about 3 means its pump and slow speed do not pay for themselves.
- **(2) held:** the break-even AUV hotel multiple has median 4.87 (10th to 90th 2.38 to 10.67), defined in every case. The glider wins in 51.6% of cases at AUV hotel x5 and 99.9% at x20. So the claim "the glider's edge is a hotel-load edge" survives sourced aerodynamics, and the break-even is about 5 times the glider's hotel load, which makes the measured AUV and glider power budgets the decisive inputs (still unsourced).
- Consequence for H4: "the glider out-ranges the AUV at 0.25 m/s but not above 1 m/s" is not the right shape. In this model the glider's horizontal speed never reaches 1 m/s (max 0.33 at the 90th percentile) and its range per energy is below the AUV's unless the AUV carries several times the hotel load. Whether a fielded AUV (REMUS-class, tens of watts of hotel) crosses that multiple needs its datasheet.
- Limits: Cd0 is a calibrated per-deployment value, swept 0.15 to 0.31; hotel loads, pump efficiency and dive energy swept; steady glide, no current; numbers read from the package's source and docs, not from the Merckelbach papers. Citation check of the papers and doi not run this step.
- Next: source a REMUS 100 (or similar) datasheet hotel and propulsion power and a Slocum G2/G3 hotel load, then re-run the break-even against them; milestone 3 Holtrop-Mennen.

## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 4: where does a buoyancy glider stop beating a propeller AUV? (planned before running)
- Why: H4. Plan (`experiments/run_glider.py`, `src/marine_hull/glider.py`): a 52 kg-class glider (steady glide, lift slope a, CD0, induced K, wing area, flown at best-glide incidence, pump efficiency, per-dive overhead, hotel load) against a same-mass, same-battery propeller AUV (ITTC 1957 body, `hull.py` constants), over a 3^8 grid, speeds 0.1 to 1.5 m/s. Crossover speed = lowest horizontal speed at which the AUV out-ranges the glider. The pump term is B tan(gamma)/(2 eta) per metre, independent of dive depth (derived in the module docstring, tested).
- Sourcing: the Merckelbach 2010 and Eriksen 2001 papers could not be fetched (nora.nerc.ac.uk, wikipedia, oceanrep.geomar.de return 403 through the proxy; web search gave no coefficients). So **no glider coefficient is sourced**; all are swept over ranges chosen from general hydrodynamics (wing CL slope 2 to 5 per rad, CD0 0.05 to 0.2 on wing area). H4 as written ("on published figures") therefore cannot be settled here; this step tests whether the crossover prediction survives across the plausible swept box.
- **Prediction (before running):** the crossover lies in 0.4 to 0.9 m/s in a majority of cases, and the glider wins at 0.25 m/s in most (prior 45%: the hotel load is shared, which hurts the AUV little at low speed, so the crossover may sit lower, near 0.3 to 0.5). Refuted if under half of cases have the crossover in 0.4 to 0.9 m/s.
- Limits: steady quasi-static flight, no ocean current, battery energy cancels from the crossover (both ranges scale with it), glider limited by pump capacity (10 N).

### Outcome of milestone 4 (`experiments/run_glider.py`, under 1 s; `experiments/results_glider.txt`)
- Pre-registered part (equal hotel load, 6,561 cases; reproduced as the x1 row): the glider has the longer best range in 5.9% of cases (median ratio 0.58), and the crossover prediction is **refuted**: the crossover sits in 0.4 to 0.9 m/s in 0.4% of cases. Glider horizontal speed is capped at 0.13 to 0.70 m/s (median 0.30) by the 10 N pump, so it never reaches 1 m/s and the "crossover" is mostly the glider's speed ceiling or the 0.1 m/s grid floor, not a range crossing. The metric was badly chosen; best-range at own best speed is the usable comparison.
- Exploratory (added after seeing this, not pre-registered): an AUV-hotel multiplier axis. Glider has the longer best range in 5.9% / 89.2% / 100% of cases at AUV hotel x1 / x5 / x20. Over all 19,683 cases the median ratio is 1.61 and the glider's best-range speed is 0.25 m/s (median). The glider's advantage in this model is hotel load, since pump work per metre B tan(gamma)/(2 eta) is small beside AUV propulsion only if hotel is not shared.
- Limits: no glider or AUV coefficient or hotel load is sourced; the multiplier range is a guess; glider steady flight only, no current. H4 stays open until a fielded glider's and AUV's measured power budgets are found (Eriksen 2001; Merckelbach 2010; REMUS or similar datasheet).
- Next: source hotel loads and a published glider range; milestone 3 Holtrop-Mennen.

## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 2: does a Michell wave term change the catamaran verdict? (planned before running)
- Why: milestone 1's wave term was a placeholder, so H2 ("no equal-length case favours the catamaran") rested on an unsourced input. `src/marine_hull/michell.py` computes Michell thin-ship wave resistance for Wigley hulls (closed-form x and z integrals, Simpson over theta) and Havelock's two-hull interference. Plan (`experiments/run_michell.py`): the milestone-1 box with the placeholder replaced by Michell, real Wigley wetted area instead of the c_s constant, demi-hull spacing s/L swept 0.15, 0.25, 0.40, a Michell scale 0.5, 1, 1.5 (thin-ship theory is known to err in both directions), and the same battery, hotel load and propulsive efficiencies.
- **Predictions (before running):** (1) the catamaran still wins in under 5% of cases and in none of the equal-length cases at Michell scale 1 (prior 65%). (2) Wave resistance is under 10% of viscous resistance at the monohull's best-range speed in over 90% of cases (prior 70%). Refuted if the catamaran wins 5% or more, or in any equal-length case at scale 1.
- Checks done first: closed-form J against Simpson sums (agree to 1e-9 relative at 3 angles); dependence on theta steps (800 against 3,200: no change at 3 decimals). Not done: comparison with a published Wigley tank curve (no numeric table found in a web search; only plots).
- Limits known in advance: thin-ship theory, no viscous-wave coupling, Wigley shape only, form factor and interference still swept.

### Outcome of milestone 2 (`experiments/run_michell.py`, 1.5 s, deterministic; `experiments/results_michell.txt`; 6,561 cases)
- **(1) held:** the catamaran out-ranges the monohull in 216 of 6,561 cases (3.3%), none of the 729 equal-length cases at any Michell scale (0.5, 1, 1.5). Milestone 1 with the placeholder: 0.4%. Median range ratio cat/mono 0.87 (0.70 to 1.06). Wins need a 3 m monohull against 4 to 5 m demi-hulls or a low catamaran form factor.
- **(2) refuted:** wave resistance is under 10% of viscous at the monohull's best-range speed in 81.5% of cases, not over 90% (median 0.069, 90th percentile 0.106, max 0.121). The best-range speed is 1.0 m/s (max 1.1), Fr about 0.16 for a 4 m hull, so wave making is a small but not negligible term.
- Scale 0 to 1.5 on a 4 m monohull moves best range 847 to 815 km (-4%); the catamaran moves 721 to 710 km. The verdict does not move with the wave term, nor with demi-hull spacing (3.3% at each of s/L 0.15, 0.25, 0.4: Havelock interference shifts range by under 1% at these speeds).
- The placeholder understated wave resistance: Michell/viscous at 4 m, Fr 0.24 is 0.34 against the placeholder's 0.088. It does not matter for range because the optimum sits below Fr 0.2.
- Limits: Michell thin-ship theory with no published Wigley tank curve checked against (no numeric table found; plots only), so the absolute wave level is unvalidated, which the 0.5 to 1.5 scale only brackets. Wigley shapes only; form factor and viscous interference are still swept, not sourced.
- Next: milestone 3, Holtrop-Mennen against tank data for small hulls; digitise a Wigley Cw curve (Lazauskas 2009 thesis, Univ. of Adelaide) to validate `michell.py`.

## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 1: does a catamaran out-range a monohull at survey speed? (planned before running)
- Why: H2. Plan (`experiments/run.py`): 200 kg craft, 25% battery, 30 W hotel load, 0.5 to 3 m/s; ITTC 1957 friction times form factor plus a placeholder wave term; 3^8 = 6,561 grid cases over hull constant, form factors, catamaran interference, both lengths, wave scale and battery specific energy (150 to 250 Wh/kg).
- **Prediction (before running):** the catamaran wins in under 5% of cases and in none at equal length (prior 75%). Refuted if it wins in 5% or more, or in any equal-length case.

### Outcome (`experiments/results.txt`)
- **Prediction held:** the catamaran wins 27 of 6,561 cases (0.4%), none of the 2,187 equal-length cases. Median range ratio cat/mono 0.85 (0.72 to 1.02). Wins need a shorter monohull (3 m) against 5 m demi-hulls or the reverse of the swept lengths with a low form factor, so they come from length, not the catamaran layout.
- Monohull best range 560 to 1,188 km (median 836 km) at a median 1.05 m/s: at 30 W hotel load the optimum is slow, which is the regime H1 describes. The range figures are an upper bound: no waves, current, turns or reserve, and the wave term is a placeholder.
- Not shown: anything about stability, deck area or payload mounting, which are why catamarans are chosen. Wave resistance, the main place a slender catamaran gains, is the unsourced part. The result says a friction-limited comparison at equal displacement does not favour the catamaran; it does not rank real boats.
- Citation checks: `cite_check.py` found two DOIs written from memory were wrong (Wynn et al. 2014 resolved to a seamount-observatory paper; the Rudnick 2004 DOI to a floats paper). Both were fixed or demoted in `PROPOSAL.md`.
- Next: milestone 2, a sourced wave-resistance curve and a published resistance curve to check against; Holtrop-Mennen for H3.
