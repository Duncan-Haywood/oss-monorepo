## 2026-10-31 · Milestone 7d: can a hull-specific fixed mission energy flip the mono/cat ranking? (not pre-registered)
- Why: 7c's "Next". Written and run in one go: I logged no predictions first, so treat the outcome as exploratory.
- Outcome (`experiments/results_h5_overhead.txt`): the catamaran out-ranges the monohull at equal overhead in only 9 of 2,187 cases (0.4%), so there is almost nothing to flip. Rank swaps: 0 at d = 0, 3 (0.1%) at d = 100 Wh, 0 at 250 Wh and above (past the window, the cat has already lost by 7.5 kWh). The closed form agrees with brute force in every row (new test).
- Reading: within this model H5 stays refuted for mono against catamaran, even with a hull-specific fixed energy; a swap needs a near-tie in km/Wh and an overhead gap tuned to the 7.5 to 12.5 kWh window. The only pairs with real flip potential are the ones with close km/Wh (the nine cat-ahead cases), which the sweeps of run.py can list.

### Outcome of milestone 6e (`experiments/run_solar_seastate_persist.py`, 10 seconds, seeded; `experiments/results_solar_seastate_persist.txt`; 20 cells)
- **(1) held:** at a 7-day spell persistence recovers 0.68 to 0.80 of the C-B gap in all 10 cells (median 0.75), above the 0.6 line, so my central guess of 0.3 to 0.5 was too low: E/B is +3.6 to +6.4% with the 10 kWh pack and +4.1 to +11.4% with the 1 kWh pack. My arithmetic for the hit rate was also wrong: with p = 0.5 and 7-day spells in both states the day-to-day flip chance is 1/7, so the hit rate is 0.85, not 0.71. Rough-spell persistence is what makes a sensor-only boat nearly as good as the oracle, and no forecast skill parameter is needed.
- **(2) refuted, and the cause is a model artifact:** at spell 1 day E equals C (share 1.00 in all 10 cells, E/B up to +5.5%) while the hit rate is 0.00. With p = 0.5 the chain `markov` builds for spell 1 is a deterministic alternation (stay = 0, enter = 1), so yesterday's state predicts today's perfectly inverted and the in-sample pair simply swaps the two speeds. ## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 4b: does the glider-vs-AUV verdict survive Slocum coefficients taken from a source? (planned before running)
- Why: milestone 4 swept every glider coefficient because the Merckelbach papers were unreachable (the hosts still 403 or fail DNS). The MIT-licensed `gliderflight` package (PyPI 1.2.1, Merckelbach's implementation of the Slocum flight model of Merckelbach et al. 2010 and 2019) ships the Slocum values, so they can be read from its source. Sourced there: wing area S = 0.1 m^2, aspect ratio 7, sweep 43 deg, Oswald e = 0.8, hull lift ah = 3.8 /rad, hull induced drag Cd1_hull = 9.7 /rad^2, wing lift slope from its own parameterisation aw = 2 pi AR / (2 + sqrt(AR^2 (1 + tan^2 Omega) + 4)) = 3.73 /rad, so lift slope a = aw + ah = 7.5 /rad and induced K = Cd1_hull + aw^2/(pi e AR) = 10.5 /rad^2 (the package's calibration examples use 10.5); parasite drag Cd0 is calibrated per deployment: its examples and docs use 0.15 to 0.16 and one calibration converges to 0.31. Milestone 4's grid (a 2 to 5, K 0.1 to 0.5) did not contain this point at all.
- Plan (`experiments/run_slocum.py`, `src/marine_hull/glider.py` unchanged): glider a, K, S fixed at the sourced values, Cd0 in {0.15, 0.23, 0.31}; swept because unsourced: buoyancy B (10 values, 1 to 10 N), pump efficiency {0.3, 0.5, 0.7}, dive energy {0, 5 kJ, 20 kJ}, hotel {0.7, 2, 5} W (0.7 W is the NOC average sensor figure quoted for gliders, a lower bound); AUV as in milestone 4 (52 kg, three hulls) with AUV hotel multiple x1, x5, x20. Outputs: glider best range against AUV best range at equal hotel; the AUV hotel multiple at which the two tie (break-even), per case; glider horizontal speed at the best-range point.
- **Predictions (before running):** (1) At equal hotel load the glider still has the longer best range in under 25% of cases (prior 65%; milestone 4 gave 5.9%, but a lift slope of 7.5 and L/D near 3 are better than the old box). (2) The break-even AUV hotel multiple has median between 2 and 10 (prior 60%). Refuted: (1) 25% or more; (2) median outside 2 to 10.
- Limits known in advance: the hotel loads and pump efficiency remain unsourced (swept); pump energy per dive is an order-of-magnitude check only (NOC: about 65 W for 2 min at 1000 m); steady glide, no current; the package is a derivative source for the numbers, not the original papers.

### Outcome of milestone 4b (`experiments/run_slocum.py`, under 1 s, deterministic; `experiments/results_slocum.txt`; 2,430 cases)
- Sourced lift slope 7.5 /rad and K 10.5 /rad^2 give glide angles 18 to 26 deg (median 22), horizontal speed median 0.21 m/s (10th to 90th 0.12 to 0.33), the right range for a Slocum.
- **(1) held, and more strongly:** at equal hotel load the glider has the longer best range in 0 of 2,430 cases (milestone 4's loose box: 5.9%). Median AUV/glider energy-per-metre ratio 0.36, i.e. the glider spends about 2.8 times the energy per metre: at 0.2 m/s even 0.7 W of hotel load costs 3.5 J/m, and the glider's L/D of about 3 means its pump and slow speed do not pay for themselves.
- **(2) held:** the break-even AUV hotel multiple has median 4.87 (10th to 90th 2.38 to 10.67), defined in every case. The glider wins in 51.6% of cases at AUV hotel x5 and 99.9% at x20. So the claim "the glider's edge is a hotel-load edge" survives sourced aerodynamics, and the break-even is about 5 times the glider's hotel load, which makes the measured AUV and glider power budgets the decisive inputs (still unsourced).
- Consequence for H4: "the glider out-ranges the AUV at 0.25 m/s but not above 1 m/s" is not the right shape. In this model the glider's horizontal speed never reaches 1 m/s (max 0.33 at the 90th percentile) and its range per energy is below the AUV's unless the AUV carries several times the hotel load. Whether a fielded AUV (REMUS-class, tens of watts of hotel) crosses that multiple needs its datasheet.
- Limits: Cd0 is a calibrated per-deployment value, swept 0.15 to 0.31; hotel loads, pump efficiency and dive energy swept; steady glide, no current; numbers read from the package's source and docs, not from the Merckelbach papers. Citation check of the papers and doi not run this step.
- Next: source a REMUS 100 (or similar) datasheet hotel and propulsion power and a Slocum G2/G3 hotel load, then re-run the break-even against them; milestone 3 Holtrop-Mennen.

## 2026-10-27 · Milestone 4: where does a buoyancy glider stop beating a propeller AUV? (planned before running)
- Why: H4. Plan (`experiments/run_glider.py`, `src/marine_hull/glider.py`): a 52 kg-class glider (steady glide, lift slope a, CD0, induced K, wing area, flown at best-glide incidence, pump efficiency, per-dive overhead, hotel load) against a same-mass, same-battery propeller AUV (ITTC 1957 body, `hull.py` constants), over a 3^8 grid, speeds 0.1 to 1.5 m/s. Crossover speed = lowest horizontal speed at which the AUV out-ranges the glider. The pump term is B tan(gamma)/(2 eta) per metre, independent of dive depth (derived in the module docstring, tested).
- Sourcing: the Merckelbach 2010 and Eriksen 2001 papers could not be fetched (nora.nerc.ac.uk, wikipedia, oceanrep.geomar.de return 403 through the proxy; web search gave no coefficients). So **no glider coefficient is sourced**; all are swept over ranges chosen from general hydrodynamics (wing CL slope 2 to 5 per rad, CD0 0.05 to 0.2 on wing area). H4 as written ("on published figures") therefore cannot be settled here; this step tests whether the crossover prediction survives across the plausible swept box.
- **Prediction (before running):** the crossover lies in 0.4 to 0.9 m/s in a majority of cases, and the glider wins at 0.25 m/s in most (prior 45%: the hotel load is shared, which hurts the AUV little at low speed, so the crossover may sit lower, near 0.3 to 0.5). Refuted if under half of cases have the crossover in 0.4 to 0.9 m/s.
- Limits: steady quasi-static flight, no ocean current, battery energy cancels from the crossover (both ranges scale with it), glider limited by pump capacity (10 N).

### Outcome of milestone 4 (`experiments/run_glider.py`, under 1 s; `experiments/results_glider.txt`)
- Pre-registered part (equal hotel load, 6,561 cases; reproduced as the x1 row): the glider has the longer best range in 5.9% of cases (median ratio 0.58), and the crossover prediction is **refuted**: the crossover sits in 0.4 to 0.9 m/s in 0.4% of cases. Glider horizontal speed is capped at 0.13 to 0.70 m/s (median 0.30) by the 10 N pump, so it never reaches 1 m/s and the "crossover" is mostly the glider's speed ceiling or the 0.1 m/s grid floor, not a range crossing. The metric was badly chosen; best-range at own best speed is the usable comparison.
- Exploratory (added after seeing this, not pre-registered): an AUV-hotel multiplier axis. Glider has the longer best range in 5.9% / 89.2% / 100% of cases at AUV hotel x1 / x5 / x20. Over all 19,683 cases the median ratio is 1.61 and the glider's best-range speed is 0.25 m/s (median). The glider's advantage in this model is hotel load, since pump work per metre B tan(gamma)/(2 eta) is small beside AUV propulsion only if hotel is not shared.
- Limits: no glider or AUV coefficient or hotel load is sourced; the multiplier range is a guess; glider steady flight only, no current. H4 stays open until a fielded glider's and AUV's measured power budgets are found (Eriksen 2001; Merckelbach 2010; REMUS or similar datasheet).
- Next: source hotel loads and a published glider range; milestone 3 Holtrop-Mennen.

- **(1) held:** the catamaran out-ranges the monohull in 216 of 6,561 cases (3.3%), none of the 729 equal-length cases at any Michell scale (0.5, 1, 1.5). Milestone 1 with the placeholder: 0.4%. - Limits: Michell thin-ship theory with no published Wigley tank curve checked against (no numeric table found; plots only), so the absolute wave level is unvalidated, which the 0.5 to 1.5 scale only brackets. Wigley shapes only; form factor and viscous interference are still swept, not sourced.
