### 2.1 Introduction (draft)

Small autonomous survey craft of 100 to 500 kg are usually chosen for deck area and stability, not
endurance. If the payload's fixed power draw dominates at survey speed, the hull matters little;
if not, it matters a great deal. Published comparisons fix one resistance method and one vehicle
(Manley 2008; Liu et al. 2016), and the regression methods in common use (Holtrop and Mennen 1982)
were fitted to ships of tens of metres. We put the hull types on one energy basis, a 200 kg craft
with a 25% battery mass fraction, and sweep every input we cannot source, so that the result is
the region where the ranking holds rather than one number. Contributions, stated narrowly:
(i) a common energy-per-kilometre comparison of seven body types with every unsourced input swept
and every sourced one named; (ii) the finding that the ranking is stable where absolute range is
not; (iii) a first contact with tank data, one Wigley point, showing the swept form-factor box is
conservative for slender hulls; (iv) a seeded, one-command-per-table artefact.

### 2.2 Model (draft)

Displacement hulls: ITTC 1957 friction line times a form factor (1 + k), plus Michell thin-ship
wave resistance for Wigley hulls, with Havelock two-hull interference for the catamaran. Planing:
Savitsky (1964) prismatic surface, no mean-bottom-speed correction. Hydrofoil: Prandtl lifting
line plus flat-plate struts, no ventilation. Glider: steady glide at best-glide incidence with
pump work B tan(gamma) / (2 eta) per metre, independent of dive depth; the AUV is a same-mass,
same-battery propeller body. Range is battery energy over (propulsive power / efficiency + hotel
load) times speed, maximised over speed. Deck solar adds a daily-mean harvest with NASA POWER
insolation at five sites. All coefficients that are not sourced are swept on a grid or drawn jointly
(milestone 5m).

Currents: weighted by OSCAR surface currents (6,377 ocean points, 13 dates of 2013), the median speed is 0.126 m/s and 30.1% of the ocean exceeds 0.2 m/s; with the whole current cross-track the glider cannot hold its track in 29.6% of the ocean and keeps lower energy per ground metre than the AUV in 69.0% of triples, at a random heading 16.5% and 82.5% (`results_current_oscar.txt`). Observed coastal currents (NOAA CO-OPS PORTS, 64 US stations, January 2024, 6-minute readings; `results_current_coops.txt`) are much stronger: median 0.303 m/s, 63.5% of readings above 0.2 m/s, and the glider cannot hold its track in 61.1% of readings in the worst case (41.6% at a random heading), keeping lower energy per ground metre than the AUV in 38.0% (57.4%) of triples. Split by each station's own median speed (`results_current_coops_regimes.txt`): in 18 calm stations (median at or below 0.2 m/s) the worst-case stall share is 31.6% and the glider wins 67.1%; in 30 moderate stations (0.2 to 0.5) 66.6% and 32.5%; in 16 strong stations (above 0.5) 84.0% and 15.6%. Only the calm group keeps a majority glider win in the worst case. The stations are ports, channels and bay entrances chosen for navigation, so they overstate a typical shelf survey; one winter month; the groups are formed and evaluated on the same data, thresholds not sensitivity-checked; stall persistence (how long a glider is held) is not measured because only percentile summaries are kept. Net for H4: the glider's range advantage is robust to every AUV hotel load found, but it is a persistence advantage that costs days and fails in strong currents, so for a survey with a deadline or a boundary-current site the AUV is the answer.

### 2.5 Wing-sail drone: what tack control and forecasts are worth (milestones 5a to 5g; numbers copied from `../LOG.md` and the files named)

A first-order wing-sail polar (`src/marine_hull/sail.py`, every drone parameter swept, no vendor polar) and tack controllers run on 24 h windows of NOAA CO-OPS observed 6-minute wind (Jan and Jul 2024, 15 coastal stations), with a +-1.5 km corridor to a mark dead to windward and a 30 s tack loss. Results are shares of the no-corridor, no-tack-cost bound; wind is uniform in space.

- **5a.** Corridor-edge tacking 83.7% (July), header-following 87.6% (+3.2 points, bootstrap 2.7 to 3.7, positive at all 12 stations). The 5a clairvoyant (99.7%) and 5b's finding that no forecast helps were both corrected by 5c.
- **5c.** The clairvoyant rode the corridor edge without tacking; with the same edge rule it is 92.7%. A full-day policy on 15-minute-smoothed wind, executed on the true wind, gives about +5 points over the header-follower (4.7 to 6.1); 60-minute smoothing keeps about 38%, 120-minute about 0 to 11%. `results_sail_policy.txt`.
- **5d.** With hourly replanning on a synthetic forecast whose error has e-folding time T, the policy keeps 16 to 20% of the +5 points at T = 30 min, 38 to 45% at 60, 51 to 58% at 120 and 62 to 68% at 240. Pure persistence loses to the header-follower (-0.2 Jan, -2.6 Jul) and tacks 150 to 250 times a day. `results_sail_lead.txt`.
- **5e to 5g (negative).** Neither a proportional switch margin (0.02 to 2 step-equivalents; margin 0 is best or within noise everywhere, T = 120: +3.4 Jan / +3.3 Jul, margin 1: -1.9 / -2.0) nor a minimum dwell between voluntary tacks (D = 10 to 80 min) rescues the low-skill rows. At T = 0 the best dwell cuts tacks per day by 55% (Jan, 166 to 74) and 39% (Jul, 248 to 152) but progress falls 1.8 to 3.9 points below the header-follower, and at T = 120 D = 10 already costs 1.6 to 1.8 points. Tack count is not the cost driver; below about 30 minutes of forecast skill the header-follower is the better rule. `results_sail_margin.txt`, `results_sail_margin_fine.txt`, `results_sail_dwell.txt`.

- **5h to 5j (real archived forecast, negative).** Replacing the synthetic error model with archived HRRR 10 m winds (3 km cell, newest cycle issued 3 h before the replan, 12 stations, every 4th day of Jan and Jul 2024): raw HRRR is 2.9 (Jan) and 6.5 (Jul) points below the header-follower and below persistence. A trailing-hour speed-ratio and direction correction recovers about 2 points (-0.5 / -3.0 against the header-follower); a 3 h window is worse (lag), fading the correction adds at most 0.6 over the 1 h version, and a station bias learned from the other windows is worse again (-1.0 / -4.5, with more tacks). The HRRR error against the stations is a site mismatch (mean absolute speed error 1.7 to 2.0 m/s, direction 32 to 41 degrees, no growth with lead), not forecast decay. Reading: the 5d requirement of 1 to 2 hours of skill at 15-minute scale is not met by a gridded 3 km model compared against pier anemometers; whether it is met over open water is untested (`LOG.md` 5h, 5i, 5j; `experiments/results_sail_hrrr*.txt`). **5k (open water).** The same test at 9 to 10 NDBC buoys (Jan, Jul 2024, 72 and 80 windows): raw HRRR is 0.8 (Jan) and 2.3 (Jul) points below the header-follower, the trailing-hour correction brings it to +0.0 and -0.9, and persistence is +0.0 and -0.2, against +2.1 and +2.4 for the 5d synthetic forecast; direction error falls to 24 to 30 degrees. Siting explained part of the pier loss, but over open water HRRR still only matches following the headers (`LOG.md` 5k; `experiments/results_sail_hrrr_buoy.txt`). **5l and 5m (nowcast and height, negative).** Letting the buoy supply the level and HRRR only the change since the replan (a trend nowcast) lifts it to level with persistence but not above it (trend minus persistence +0.2 Jan, -0.2 Jul; no variant beats the header-follower by more than 0.1 points). Scaling the buoy winds from 4 to 5 m up to 10 m with a neutral log law cuts the speed bias from +0.6 to +0.9 m/s to +0.1 to +0.4 but moves raw HRRR only from -0.8 / -2.3 to -0.8 to -1.0 / -1.7 to -1.9 points against the header-follower (heights swept, not sourced per buoy). Direction error and day-to-day shifts carry the gap, so archived 3 km HRRR adds nothing over following the headers at these buoys (`LOG.md` 5l, 5m; `experiments/results_sail_hrrr_trend.txt`, `results_sail_hrrr_buoy_z4.txt`, `_z5.txt`).

- **5n (latency, negative).** On every hourly HRRR cycle, replanning with a 1 h instead of 3 h delivery latency changes raw HRRR by +0.2 points in January and July (bootstrap intervals include 0.5 only in neither month); bias-corrected 1 h HRRR is +0.1 (Jan) and -0.6 (Jul) points against the header-follower. Forecast hours beyond 18 are held flat (hourly cycles stop there).

So the paper can say: *with a drone model that reproduces fielded survey and capability speeds, wind beats sun by 1.2 to 1.5 times in January and is level with or below it in July (0.72 to 0.93 times); the "2 to 3 times" of the unsourced model does not survive.* Removing every light-air (below 4 m/s) block's distance, a pessimistic floor, leaves January at 1.40 times solar (above it at 9 of 9 buoys) and takes July to 0.71 times (above it at 1 of 10), so the July figure is a ceiling for the wind side (6r). Limits: the error model is synthetic (no archived NWP winds yet), the tack penalty is the only tack cost, wind is spatially uniform, and heel, waves and currents are absent. A reader should take the +5 points as an upper bound that needs roughly 1 to 2 hours of forecast skill.

### 2.5.1 Reading of the wing-sail chain at a fielded tack price (milestones 6x to 6zb; numbers from `../LOG.md`)

One sentence the section can carry: *at a measured tack cost of about 240 s, the only wind information that pays is skill a 3 km archived forecast does not have at these buoys, and no tuning of the tack rule recovers it.* The chain behind it, July 2024 holdout, 10 NDBC buoys, every 4th window, shares of the no-tack-cost bound:

1. 6w measured the tack price (218 s, 127 to 301). 6x showed the header-follower's +3.2 point edge over corridor-edge tacking vanishes by 360 s (+2.3, +0.8, +0.0) while the clairvoyant stays 16 to 26 points above.
2. 6z: a synthetic perfect-smoothed forecast gains +7.5, +8.7, +11.0 points at 120, 240, 360 s, so forecast value rises with tack cost; no-skill persistence only ties the header (-1.1 to +0.2).
3. 6za: archived HRRR does not collect that gain. At 240 s bias-corrected HRRR (L = 1 h) is -2.4 points (95% -4.8 to -1.0) and raw -5.5 (-6.7 to -3.7), with more tacks than persistence (66 against 44 per day, raw L1).
4. 6zb: a switch margin sweep (0 to 8 step-equivalents, margin read off January) takes bias-corrected HRRR to -0.2 (-1.9 to +0.0) at margin 4. The best cell is +0.0, not a gain: the margin converges HRRR on the header's roughly 42 tacks per day, i.e. it switches the forecast off.

5. 6zc to 6zf close the cheapest forecast sources, all from `../LOG.md` and the `results_wind_*` and `results_sail_ridge*` files. A buoy's own history adds -0.5% to +1% over persistence at 10 to 30 min and +2% to +4% at 60 to 120 min (6zc, 8 buoys; damped trend worse at every lead). At coastal CO-OPS stations a ridge forecast has 4 to 8% vector-RMSE skill (San Francisco +0.076 and Pensacola +0.055 at 30 min), and a neighbour 15 or 70 km away adds under 1 point at 30 min (6zd). Fed through `sim_replan_dead` at tau 240 s (11 stations, 88 July windows), the ridge forecast scores 0.8 points below the 60-minute trailing-mean persistence (95% -2.0 to -0.3, 6ze). Fitting it to the 18-minute-smoothed wind and shrinking it by alpha = 1 or 0.5 gives -1.2 (-2.2 to -0.4) and -0.3 (-1.0 to +0.3) points (6zf). So fitted single-site and pair forecasts with 4 to 8% RMSE skill do not beat a trailing mean for tack planning; the ceiling stays +10.9 points above it.

Limits to carry with it: one tack price per row (tau is pure dead time), 10 buoys with within-month windows not independent (intervals optimistic), buoy anemometer height not corrected, HRRR only (no ensemble or radar nowcast), and a margin grid not refined between 0.5 and 4. The open input is offshore upwind sensing or a radar nowcast, which need data not in `experiments/data`.

### 2.6 Wave-powered rung: a fielded Wave Glider against a quasi-steady foil model (milestone 6p; numbers from `../experiments/results_waveglider.txt`)

One sentence the section can carry: *a fielded Wave Glider's through-water speed responds to sea state far more weakly than a quasi-steady foil model predicts, and a slow 30% loss over the mission is as large as the whole sea-state effect.* Data: the Honey Badger Wave Glider (MAGI mission, Hawaii, May to Nov 2015, NOAA ERD ERDDAP), 1,245 hourly means within 300 km of an NDBC wave buoy. Pooled Spearman(speed, Hs) is 0.12; median speed at Hs at least 2.5 m is 1.05 times that at 1.5 m or less, against 1.93 for the fitted model (linear in Hs at fixed period). Held out on Aug to Oct, the model (MAE 0.165 m/s, bias +0.12) loses to a constant (0.138) and beats a two-parameter power law fitted on Jun to Jul (0.194); no foil constant in an 18-cell sweep brings it below the constant (best 0.153). Post hoc, monthly median speed falls from 0.60 to 0.64 m/s (Jun, Jul) to 0.41 to 0.47 (Aug to Oct) while monthly Hs rises from 1.45 to 2.07 m; within months speed does track Hs (Spearman +0.02 to +0.76, four of six months above +0.25), and month-normalised the height ratio is 1.18. At the programme's 2024 buoys the fitted model gives 50 km/day (January) and 37 (July) and the power law 43 and 33: about half the 86 km/day solar ceiling and below 6o's capability wind drone (126 and 80). Limits: one vehicle and mission, a buoy up to 300 km away, no record of thruster use or fouling, period from DPD only.

Milestone 6q (`../experiments/results_waveglider_drift.txt`) asks which input forecasts that speed, on the same Aug to Oct hours (761 targets scored by every model). The median speed of the trailing 24 h predicts speed at 6, 24 and 72 h with MAE 0.034, 0.048 and 0.054 m/s, against 0.135 for the training median (24 h ratio 0.354, block-bootstrap 95% interval 0.294 to 0.422). Multiplying by (Hs/trailing Hs)^b, with the observed buoy Hs as an oracle forecast and the within-month exponent b = 0.218 fitted on Jun to Jul, gives a ratio of 0.991 to persistence (0.860 to 1.163). A log-linear trend in days since launch fitted on May to Jul (+3.5% a day) diverges when extrapolated. So the forecastable part of speed is vehicle state, and the sea-state response, about Hs^0.2 at fixed state, is real but small next to it. Within 150 km of a buoy (281 targets) the constant is already good (0.065), and persistence's edge is gone by 72 h.

Milestone 6r (`../experiments/results_waveglider_site.txt`) carries that into the planning rows. With U = s Hs^0.218, s the median of u/Hs^0.218 in a 2015 mission month, the 2024 buoy-months give fleet medians of 52.4 (January, 9 buoys) and 48.8 km/day (July, 11) at the best month's state and 32.0 and 29.8 at the worst. The best buoy over the worst is 1.20 (January) and 1.34 (July), the best state over the worst 1.64 (1.41 for July to August alone), and 82.5% of the variance of log km/day over the buoy x month x state grid is between states. Changing the exponent over 0.188 to 0.248 moves the fleet medians by at most 2%. So the 6p rows should be read as a band set by vehicle state, about 30 to 52 km/day, with site a smaller correction.

Milestone 6s (`../experiments/results_waveglider_period.txt`) adds the wave period 6r left out. With month intercepts, log u = b2 log Hs + c log DPD on the 416 Jun to Jul hours gives b2 = 0.224 and c = -0.025 (1-day block bootstrap 95% -0.271 to 0.170), so the pooled period residual of 6p (-0.26) does not survive holding the vehicle state; the period ratio adds nothing to the oracle forecast (24 h MAE ratio 0.991, 0.979 to 1.001), and the 2024 buoy-months move by -0.5% to +1.2% (fleet medians 53.1 and 49.1 km/day at the best state). NDBC's binned DPD and the up-to-300 km buoy match both attenuate a period slope, so the claim is "no detectable effect at this resolution", not "none".

Milestone 6u (`../experiments/results_waveglider_onboard.txt`, pre-registered; `../experiments/results_waveglider_onboard_posthoc.txt`, exploratory) revises 6q to 6s. With the vehicle's own Datawell MOSE wave record in place of the buoy, the within-month exponent of speed on Hs is 0.354 (1-day block bootstrap 95% 0.299 to 0.415, 3,281 hours) against 0.22 with buoys. On the hours that have both records, the buoy's log Hs correlates 0.20 with the on-board one within a month. At a fixed vehicle state the MOSE average period enters strongly (c = -0.71, 95% -0.80 to -0.61, with b = 0.54), and a single heave-velocity term (Hs/T_avg)^0.53 fits 1,430 BIC better than Hs alone. Headwind has no measurable effect (-0.6% at 5 m/s). Post hoc, an oracle Hs-and-period term cuts the 24 h persistence error to 0.767 (0.674 to 0.874) and the 72 h error to 0.674. The sentence for this section therefore changes to: *a fielded Wave Glider's speed is a sub-linear function of heave velocity, roughly (Hs/T)^0.5, measurable only with on-board waves; a buoy 100 to 300 km away hides most of it.* The 7 August 2015 step remains mostly vehicle-side (sea state explains 30% of the week-scale log drop, none of the hour-scale one), and the June drop is mostly a calm spell (73%). 6r's km/day rows use the attenuated exponent without a period term and must be rerun before they are quoted.

Milestone 6v (`../experiments/results_waveglider_site_period.txt`, pre-registered) reruns them. With s_m the median of u/(Hs_ob/T_avg)^0.534 per 2015 month (June to October) and each 2024 buoy-month's own WVHT and APD, the fleet medians are 55.4 (January, 9 buoys) and 45.2 km/day (July, 11) at the best state and 33.0 and 27.0 at the worst; the state ratio is 1.68. The best buoy over the worst falls to 1.12 in January, because the Pacific's high seas come with long periods, and rises to 1.73 in July, where the calm, short Gulf and Nantucket seas plan 32 to 35 km/day. The between-state share of variance in log km/day falls from 0.83 to 0.66. The free (Hs^0.54, T^-0.71) pair agrees to 1.4%, and a sensor-to-buoy calibration within 150 km shifts every row by +5%. The July low end is an extrapolation: 88 to 95% of those buoys' hours lie below the mission's 5th percentile of Hs/T, and post hoc only 35 mission hours fall below 0.15 m/s. The sentence for this section: *with on-board waves a Wave Glider plans about 27 to 59 km/day at these sites; in winter the vehicle state decides it, in summer the site's heave velocity decides it about equally, and the calm-sea end is untested.*

Milestone 6w (`../experiments/results_waveglider_era5.txt`, pre-registered) asks whether a planner without the vehicle's sensor loses that law. ERA5 reanalysis waves (Hersbach et al. 2020) at the vehicle's nearest 0.5 degree grid point correlate 0.866 with the on-board log(Hs/T) within a month (ERA5 swh 0.96 of MOSE Hs; mwp 1.29 of MOSE T_avg). On the same 3,281 hours the exponent is 0.533 (95% 0.447 to 0.618) against 0.534, with no errors-in-variables attenuation, and 6v's monthly plan from ERA5 is within 0.4 km/day of the on-board plan. With ERA5's peak period the exponent falls to 0.329. As a forecast term with the ratio law, ERA5 gives MAE ratios to persistence of 1.004, 0.955 (0.812 to 1.135) and 0.845 (0.700 to 1.014) at 6, 24 and 72 h, against the on-board oracle's 1.011, 0.880 and 0.772. The sentence for this section: *a 0.5 degree reanalysis carries the Wave Glider's heave-velocity law and its monthly plan, so km/day can be planned from a wave model anywhere; short-range speed forecasts gain little from it, and a reanalysis is an upper bound on what a real forecast would give.*

### 2.7 History length: how many seasons of its own log a solar boat needs to correct its climatology (milestones 6zz to 6zzr; numbers from `../experiments/results_history_length_table.txt` and the files it parses)

The section's one-sentence claim: *on ordinary water, one season of the boat's own log, pooled within a latitude band, already beats the uncorrected climatology in every year we could draw; above about 50 degrees no one- or two-season rule tested is safe in every year; from three seasons a fitted yearly cosine, pooled within its band or not, never falls below the uncorrected rule in either pool, a season before the raw month-by-month ratios do.*

Setting. A boat carrying a 50 W payload plans its speed from a ten-year NASA POWER climatology (2015 to 2024) using the 90-day adaptive ratio rule AT_90 (6zw). It is scored on 2005 to 2014, split into two five-year halves, at 30 points with packs of 2.5 to 20 kWh. The climatology carries 20 swept bias shapes plus the real one, giving 168 cells. A rule wins a cell when it goes at least as far as the 7-day trailing-mean rule B with no more excess dark hours. The real seasonal error of the climatology has a median amplitude of 1.8% and exceeds 3% at nine points, all at or above 50 degrees or at P-30 (6zz). Those nine points form the high-amplitude pool (hi168). The learned correction scales the climatology by the month-m ratio of harvest to climatology from earlier years of the boat's own log, clamped to 0.85 to 1.15 (6zza). With five years of log it matches the per-day oracle on the swept cells (120 against 120 of 128) and wins 158 of 168, against AT_90's 139.

What the chain established, in order:

1. **One raw season adds more error than it removes** (6zzb, averaged over years in 6zzi). One year's monthly ratio carries about 4.4% of that year's weather, against a real seasonal error of 1.8% at most points. So on average the raw correction loses to no correction (121.8 against 139). It needs two seasons to pass AT_90 on average (140.8) and four never to fall below it (worst 143).
2. **Shrinkage buys a floor, not a mean** (6zzc to 6zzh, 11 rules). We tried shrinking the months toward the year mean or toward a fitted cosine, shrinking the cosine's amplitude toward zero or toward a latitude prior, and pooling the cosine within latitude bands. All of them narrow the year-to-year span, from 55 cells for LC to 13 for HR and HX. The moment and REML estimates of the band variance agree within 0.004 in 44 of 48 cases (6zzh), so the estimator is not the weak link.
3. **The year drawn mattered more than the rule** (6zzi, 6zzj). Every one-year result of 6zzb to 6zzh learned from the year next to the scored half, and that turned out to be LC's worst year (97, against a range of 97 to 152 over the five years). Averaged fairly, one season of band-pooled log (HR) beats no correction by 8 cells and is never below it in any year. Recency did not order the outcome (Spearman +0.40 over the five single years).
4. **A second season buys about 5 cells and a higher floor** (6zzk). All five harmonic rules reach 151 to 152 on average, and the band pools never fall below 145 over the ten pairs.
5. **A third season lets a fitted cosine cross both floors** (6zzr). Scored over every set of three learning years, HR, HX and LHF never fall below no correction in either pool (HR worst 150 and 31). The raw ratios need four seasons (worst 123 on all168 at three). On ordinary water the shrinkage rules keep a 6 to 7 cell lead over LC at three and four seasons; at the high-amplitude points LC keeps its lead at every k (95.7 against 56.7 for HR at three seasons, 105 against 73 at five).
6. **High latitude is a different regime.** At the nine high-amplitude points the real seasonal error (4.5 to 8.5%) is larger than one year's noise. So the rules that keep the boat's own fit win more on average (LC 81.0, HX 51.8 and HR 50.2, against AT_90's 25), and shrinkage throws signal away. The cost is variance: one learning year (lag 3, meaning 2007 for the late half and 2012 for the early half) takes LC down to 1 of 168.
7. **That bad year is real weather, and it fails through a summer level error** (6zzl to 6zzo). ERA5 reproduces NASA's month anomalies for the two years (r = +0.71) and the lag-3 errors themselves (r = +0.77). By the size of its departures, the year sits in the middle of the five learning years in both sources (6zzm). All 167 of its losing cells lose on distance. Replacing its three local-summer months with the scored years' own ratios recovers 0.87 of the gap (6zzl). A forced shift showed that a brighter learned summer makes the boat bolder, not more cautious (6zzn). Splitting the summer error into level and shape showed that the damage sits in the cells where the year learned a *darker* summer than the scored years saw: repairing the level alone recovers 0.54 of the gap, the shape alone 0.03 (6zzo).
8. **A deployable guard moves the trade-off but does not remove it** (6zzp). The guard DG never lets a one-year summer ratio fall below the climatology's. At the high-amplitude points it lifts lag 3 from 1 to 74 and has the best one-season mean on that pool (109.2), and in no year does it fall below AT_90 there (worst 74). Over all 30 points it costs about 16 cells on average (106.0 against 121.8), and every cell it fails there fails on dark hours: away from the high-amplitude points the guard makes the boat too bold.
9. **A guard gated on the boat's own replay repairs the cost but not the gain** (6zzs). Replaying the uncorrected plan against B over the learning year and applying DG only where it fell short on distance lifts the 30-point mean above the raw correction (127.2 against 121.8; with the mirror guard on dark-heavy years 131.6), but at the high-amplitude points 80% of learning years replay as fine, so the gated guard keeps little of DG's gain (85.8 against 109.2, lag 3 18 against 74). The high-latitude failure is a forecast error about other years that the one logged year cannot reveal.
10. **On ordinary water, less one-season correction is better in every direction tried** (6zzt). Falling back to no correction where the replay is not short (OA) gives 128.8 on the 30-point pool; on short replays the raw ratios beat DG (135.0), and shrinking DG toward 1 by the replay's km shortfall rises monotonically with the shrinkage scale (130.0 to 137.2), approaching AT_90's 139 from below. The fallback rules beat AT_90 on the per-point count (129.2 to 130.2 against 126) and lift the worst high-amplitude lag to 41.

11. **The high-latitude ordering survives ERA5; its size does not** (6zzu). With ERA5 sunlight in place of NASA POWER at the nine high-amplitude points (climatology, learning and scored years alike), every ordering above holds on hi168 (DG 75.6 > OG 68.2, OA 66.8 > LC 57.4 > AT_90 29; the lags rank identically, lag 3 worst at 16), but each rule's lead over no correction falls by a third to a half (LC +56 to +28, DG +84 to +47), and on the per-point real-shape count (72 cells) LC, DG and OG fall below AT_90 (36.4, 37.4, 37.0 against 38); only the fallback rules stay level (OA 38.8).
12. **A source mismatch is a level error the learned correction catches** (6zzv). Planning on the NASA POWER climatology while sailing, logging and being scored under ERA5 at the nine points sinks no correction to 3 of 168 on hi168 (all 165 failures on distance), while LC reaches 45.2 (lead +42.2, against +28.4 with ERA5 throughout) and DG 67.4; the orderings DG > OG, OA > LC > AT_90 hold and the lags rank as on ERA5 (Spearman 0.90). 23.7% of learned real-shape month ratios sit at the 1.15 clamp (6.7% on ERA5), mean 1.044. Per point on the real shape the lead is 0.4 of a cell (35.4 against 35 of 72).
13. **The gain is the season's shape, not the level** (6zzw). Splitting the learned correction at the nine points: a learned annual level alone leads no correction by +2.2 in the cross world (LM 5.2 against AT_90 3; widened to [0.70, 1.30], +23.2) and trails it in the NASA world (21.8 against 25), while the level-free month shape keeps all of LC's lead (LS 45.4 against LC 45.2). AT_90's running ratio absorbs level; one season supplies the seasonal part of the error. Widening the month clamp to [0.70, 1.30] lifts cross from 45.2 to 61.6 and moves nasa and era5 by +0.4 and +2.4. With the mismatch reversed (ERA5 climatology, NASA sea) LC reaches 83.8 against 18, and no correction's failures remain on distance (150 of 150).
14. **The clamp is a shrinkage knob** (6zzx). Clamping the one-season ratios to [1 - h, 1 + h] for h = 0.05 to 0.40: on all30 the five-lag mean falls with h (151.4 [138, 160] at 0.05, 134.4, 121.8 at 0.15, 117.8, then 117.6 flat; AT_90 139), with dark-hour failures falling as the clamp tightens; at the nine points in the NASA world it barely moves above 0.15 (81.0 to 81.4; 60.0 at 0.05); in the cross world it rises with h (22.2 to 62.4). No h is within 5 of the best in every world. C05 is the best of a fixed seven-value sweep (a selection), and per point it trails AT_90 (124.2 against 126 of 240).

15. **The boat can set the clamp from its own ratios** (6zzy). With h equal to the learned ratios' mean distance from 1 (AP, in [0.02, 0.40], fixed in advance) the worst gap to the best fixed h over all30 and the four hi168 worlds is -13.8 (best fixed width -23.8): all30 147.8, cross 52.8, reverse 70.8 (best fixed 84.6).
