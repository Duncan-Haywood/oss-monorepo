# Scoring verifiers whose report decides the job: a reserve payment and a recentred Brier score

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
In refereed or optimistic verification a verifier's report often *decides* whether a job is accepted, and the ground truth (did the model really fault?) is observed only if the job is accepted. Scoring such reports only when observed is not proper: a verifier who thinks a job is bad has every reason to shade its report under the threshold so as to be scored and paid. For a Brier-type reward and accept-iff-`r≤τ_d`, with rejection paying a reserve `c0`, I show: (i) a reserve making truthful reporting optimal for every truth exists **iff** the score's entropy minimum `τ_s` satisfies `τ_s ≥ τ_d`, and it is then unique, `c0 = 1+τ_d²−2τ_dτ_s`; (ii) plain Brier (`τ_s=½`) therefore works only for thresholds `τ_d≤½`; (iii) recentring the score by a linear term in the outcome, `S=1−(y−r)²−(2τ_s−1)y`, fixes every threshold; (iv) the cheapest recentring is `τ_s=τ_d`, with expected rent `τ³/3` for uniform truth, and rent rises with `τ_s`; (v) with no reserve every verifier is accepted (decision loss 1.10–2.8× optimal at `τ_d`=0.7–0.2, uniform truth); (vi) exploration (accept with probability ε anyway, inverse-propensity payment) is exactly proper for any `τ_s` but multiplies payment variance by about `1/ε` and adds decision loss `ε(1−τ)²/2`. Elementary calculations in the decision-scoring literature's setting; not claimed as new theory.

## 1. Setup
Truth `q=P(fault)`. A risk-neutral verifier reports `r∈[0,1]`; the job is accepted iff `r≤τ_d`, and the outcome `y` is then observed and paid `S(r,y)=1−(y−r)²−(2τ_s−1)y`, while a rejected job pays a constant `c0`. Adding a term linear in `y` to a proper score keeps it proper, and `τ_s=½` is plain (reward-form) Brier. In expectation `E_q S = 1−r²+2q(r−τ_s)`, so the best accepted report for truth `q` is `min(q,τ_d)` and pays `G(q)=1+q²−2τ_sq` for `q≤τ_d`, or the tangent value `1−τ_d²+2q(τ_d−τ_s)` for `q>τ_d`. The decision-maker's cost is `q` per accepted job and `τ_d` per rejected one (in fault units), so the ideal loss for uniform `q` is `τ−τ²/2`.

## 2. Feasibility theorem
Truthful reporting is optimal for all `q` iff `c0` ≥ every accepted deviation payoff for `q>τ_d` and ≤ every truthful accepted payoff for `q≤τ_d`. The first supremum is at `q=τ_d` if `τ_s≥τ_d` and at `q=1` otherwise; the second infimum is at `q=τ_d` if `τ_s≥τ_d` and at `q=τ_s` otherwise. Comparing: when `τ_s≥τ_d` both equal `G(τ_d)`, so a single reserve works, `c0=1+τ_d²−2τ_dτ_s`; when `τ_s<τ_d` the lower bound exceeds the upper by `(τ_d−τ_s)(2−τ_d−τ_s)>0` and **no** reserve works. Verified by grid search over `q` and `c0` for 5×6 pairs `(τ_d,τ_s)`, and a ±0.02 perturbation of the unique reserve is never truthful (tests). E2: at `τ_d=0.7`, plain Brier has `lo=0.91>hi=0.75` (infeasible); `τ_s=0.7, 0.8, 1.0` give `c0=0.51, 0.37, 0.09`. Intuition: the scoring rule's expected value must not increase in `q` faster than the reserve does, and only a score whose incentive slope at the threshold is non-positive achieves that.

## 3. What goes wrong without it
With `c0=0` and plain Brier every verifier prefers acceptance (`E S>0`), so reports pile at `τ_d` and every job is accepted: loss `E[q]=½`. E1 (uniform truth): 2.78×, 1.96×, 1.33×, 1.10×, 1.01× the optimum at `τ_d=0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9`. With plain Brier plus the best reserve, thresholds up to ½ reach the optimum exactly (`c0*=0.84, 0.79, 0.75`, which equal `1−τ+τ²`), but at `τ_d=0.7` the best reserve on a grid is no better than accepting everything (1.099×). Recentring at `τ_s=τ_d` restores 1.000× at every threshold. Inside the accept region misreporting `r` for `q` still costs exactly `(r−q)²`, unchanged (tested).

## 4. Price of the fix
The excess a truthfully accepted verifier earns over the reserve is `G(q)−G(τ_d)`, and for uniform `q` the total is `[(τ_d−τ_s)³+τ_s³]/3−τ_d(τ_d−τ_s)²` (verified by quadrature), whose derivative in `τ_s` is `τ_d²>0`. So the least-rent feasible design is `τ_s=τ_d` with rent `τ_d³/3` (0.042 at ½), rising to 0.117 at `τ_s=0.8` and 0.167 at `τ_s=1`. The rent is what the verifier is paid, over the reserve, for revealing acceptable jobs; it is the same information-rent object as in `effort-contracts` and `audit-weighted-scoring`.

## 5. Exploration as the alternative
If the protocol may also accept a rejected job with probability ε and pay `S/ε` when observed, the payment has mean exactly `E S` (proper for every `τ_s`, no reserve, tested by simulation), so no threshold ordering is needed. The price: payment variance grows to `E[S²]/ε−(E S)²` (std 0.24 → 1.46 → 2.83 → 5.36 at ε=1, 0.3, 0.1, 0.03, `r=q=0.9`, plain Brier), and the decision loss rises by `ε(1−τ)²/2` (0.0045 at ε=0.1, `τ=0.7`) because truly bad jobs are sometimes accepted. Recentring costs neither, but needs the score designer to know `τ_d` and to commit to it; exploration is the fallback when the decision rule is hidden or changes.

## 6. Limits
One verifier, one job, risk-neutral, deterministic threshold rule, Brier-type reward, uniform truth for the loss and rent numbers (feasibility and the reserve are for every truth). Fully general scores (any proper `G`) have the same structure with `G'(τ_s)=0` replaced by "`G` is non-increasing through `τ_d`" but I have not verified them numerically. Multiple verifiers, budget-balance and unknown `τ_d` are open. Related: Othman–Sandholm and Chen–Kash–Ruberry–Shnayder on decision markets, Oesterheld–Conitzer on decision scoring rules. In this repo: `audit-weighted-scoring` (report-dependent audit rates), `decision-regret-transfer`, `abstention-scoring`, `effort-contracts`.
