Does clock-gating the extra accumulators make register-file weight-stationary scheduling beat output-stationary?

research/accel-gatedprice

Prices clock-gated register-file weight-stationary scheduling from SKY130 and GF180 liberty files: an idle clock gate costs 0.86 of an idle flop (SKY130; 0.99 to 1.02 in GF180), so one gate per 24-bit accumulator cuts the idle cost about 28-fold and flips register-file WS from losing to winning against packed SRAM weight fetches in 35 of 64 cells, in all but one cell at 24 flops per gate. Library values, not measurements.

9 tests

What it found

Follows up accel-pdkprice, where idle flops made weight-stationary (WS) lose to output-stationary (OS) once weights are packed into SRAM words. Reads the idle clock energy of a flop and of an integrated clock gate (ICG) from the SKY130 HD and GF180MCU liberty files: an idle SKY130 dlclkp_1 costs 0.86 of an idle dfxtp_1 (39.7 against 46.4 fJ per cycle), and GF180 gates cost 0.99 to 1.02 of its flops at 3.3 and 5 V in both track heights. One gate shared by G accumulator flops therefore cuts the idle cost about G-fold. With one gate per 24-bit accumulator, WS needs a weight fetch of only 0.08 to 0.9 pJ (ungated: 2.2 to 26 pJ) and beats OS against every packed SKY130 macro in all 64 macro, layer and batch cells but one (an 80-bit macro on the 11×64 layer at B = 16 needs 27 flops per gate). Library characterisation values, a gate-per-group model with the enable logic, area and the register-file read mux unpriced; no RTL built or synthesised.

  • Does the clock-gated weight-stationary engine hold up once it is written in RTL and synthesised?The clock-gated register-file weight-stationary engine in RTL and SKY130 synthesis: bit-exact, same accumulator writes as output-stationary, clock-pin energy 1.0 to 1.07x OS with a gate per set, but area only 7 to 18 % below ungated WS and 1.2 to 5.3x OS (stylised).
  • Does the gated weight-stationary engine's verdict depend on how many lanes it has?The gated weight-stationary engine's lane count swept from 2 to 16 (32 ran out of synthesis memory): bit-exact, clock-pin energy 1.01 to 1.07x OS with a gate per set at every P, area 1.6 to 1.9x OS at B = 4 and flat from P = 4, so the verdict depends on the batch, not the lanes (stylised).
  • Why did the gated weight-stationary engine need so much synthesis memory, and does fixing it change its area verdict?Why the gated weight-stationary engine needed so much synthesis memory: one variable-index read of the accumulator bus; an AND-OR read mux gives bit-exact, same-cycle engines 17 to 28 % smaller, gated WS 1.5x OS area at B = 4 and 2.2x at B = 8 (was 1.6 to 1.9x and 2.9 to 3.0x), and lets P = 64 synthesise (stylised).
  • Does clock-gated weight-stationary scheduling still beat output-stationary on a second open process, with that process's own SRAM?Second-node verdict for clock-gated register-file weight-stationary scheduling from IHP SG13G2 liberty files (idle flop 34 fJ, clock gate 0.75 of a flop, ten SRAM macros with separate read energies): one gate per accumulator makes WS beat OS in all 120 cells, but ungated WS also wins 33 of 60 packed cells because IHP's SRAM costs 72 to 497 flop-cycles per weight against SKY130's 18 to 62. Library values, not measurements.
