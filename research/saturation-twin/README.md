# Saturation twin: what does a linear-actuator simulator say about how long an unstable plant stays in control?

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real plant `x' = a x + sat_U(−K x) + w`, `w ~ N(0,s²)`, `a = 1.2`, `U = 1`; "loss of control" is the first `|x| > L = U/(a−1) = 5`, beyond which even full thrust cannot pull `x` back. The twin is the same plant with an unlimited actuator, gain `K = 1.1026` from its LQR design. The mean exit time from `x=0` solves an integral equation, discretised by a midpoint rule (converges: 245.84/245.52/245.48/245.47 at N=60/120/240/480) and matched to Monte Carlo (real plant s=1.5: 31.98 vs 32.58±0.68; s=2: 13.79 vs 13.89±0.26; twin s=1.5: 1102 vs 1078±25; twin s=2: 78.2 vs 74.5±1.6, 2.3 s.e. low, see the paper). Results, all from `experiments/results.txt`: (1) the twin overstates the time to loss of control by a factor 6,295 at `s`=1 (1.5×10⁶ vs 245 steps), 10⁶ at `s`=0.8, and still 5.7× at `s`=2; (2) for larger noise the ratio shrinks but never reaches 1, and the exact twin values above ~10¹⁰ steps are not resolvable in double precision (marked n/r); (3) `ln T` is linear in `1/s²` for both, with slope ≈3.6–3.7 on the real plant against 12.7–13.2 on the twin (Freidlin–Wentzell prediction for the linear twin `L²(1−m²)/2` = 12.4), so the gap is exponential in `1/s²`, not a constant factor; (4) the twin's *ranking* of gains survives (both are maximised at the deadbeat gain `K=a/b=1.2` on the tested grid) but its scale does not: across `K` from 0.4 to 2.2 the twin's `T` varies by 5 orders of magnitude and the real `T` by 2×; (5) the twin's own saturation rate `P(|Kx|>U)` (0.07 to 0.65 across the noise levels) is a valid warning but does not quantify the loss of life. See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

```bash
cd research/saturation-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 8 tests, ~3 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # ~25 s; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The UAV / field-robot safe-autonomy direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>) and the sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>), where controllers are designed against simulators that omit actuator limits; no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The exit-time equation and large-deviation scaling are standard (Freidlin & Wentzell 1984); control with bounded inputs of unstable plants follows the null-controllable-region literature (Hu & Lin 2001). Companion to `control-twin` (wrong actuator gain; lists saturation as a limitation), `filter-twin`, `latency-twin` and `timestep-twin` in this repository.

Stylised: scalar plant, i.i.d. Gaussian noise, hard saturation, one failure definition (`|x|>L`), one parameter set (`a`=1.2, `U`=1, `q`=1, `r`=0.1) with noise varied; no robot data. Preliminary. MIT.
