# Speed race: what paying the first k of n finishers buys

**Abstract.** Backup-worker schemes (`straggler-backup`) take worker speeds as given. If the protocol pays the `k` earliest finishers a prize `R`, speed is a choice and the round is a contest. For exponential finishing times with a chosen rate `m_i` at linear cost `c·m_i`, a worker whose rate is `r` times its rivals' wins a prize with probability `π(r)=Σ_{j<k} C(n−1,j)·r·B(r+n−1−j, j+1)`, so the payoff depends on rivals only through `r`. The slope at `r=1` telescopes to `π'(1) = (1−k/n)(H_n − H_{n−k})`, giving the symmetric equilibrium speed `m* = R(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})/(nc)` (a global best response on a grid for all cases tried), and, because the harmonic factor cancels, an exact equilibrium round time `E X_(k) = nc/((n−k)R)`. Consequences: `k=1` is the Tullock contest (97% of the prize burned on speed at `n=32`); paying every finisher (`k=n`) gives `π'(1)=0` and no speed at all; under a fixed budget `B=kR` the round time `nck/((n−k)B)` rises with `k`, so speed alone wants winner-take-all and only a statistical floor `k ≥ k_min` (noise floor of SGD) justifies more winners, at exactly `⌈k_min⌉` in every case tried; and recruiting more workers with the prize fixed barely speeds the round (`T → c/R`, total rate bought `→ kR/c`).

## 1. Model
`n` risk-neutral workers each pick a rate `m_i ≥ 0` at cost `c·m_i` and finish at `X_i ~ Exp(m_i)` independently. The first `k` finishers are each paid `R`; the round ends at `X_(k)`. This is the one-shot version of the scheme in which a coordinator keeps the first `k` gradients. Speed is treated as a purchasable resource (faster hardware, less contention, skipping other jobs).

## 2. Win probability
Put the others at rate `m`, worker `i` at `rm`. With `u=e^{−mt}`, `i` is among the first `k` iff at most `k−1` others finished earlier, so
`π(r) = ∫_0^1 r u^{r−1} P(Bin(n−1,1−u) ≤ k−1) du = Σ_{j<k} C(n−1,j)·r·B(r+n−1−j, j+1).`
Tests compare this with an independent numerical integral, with `π(1)=k/n`, with the Tullock ratio `r/(r+n−1)` at `k=1`, and with simulation (E3: `π(1.5)` exact 0.4932, simulated 0.4958 over 60000 races). At `r=1` every term equals `1/n`, and differentiating the Beta function gives the term derivative `(1/n)(1−(H_n−H_{n−1−j}))`, so
`π'(1) = (1/n) Σ_{j=0}^{k−1} [1 − (H_n − H_{n−1−j})] = (1−k/n)(H_n−H_{n−k}).`
The second equality is a telescoping identity, proved by induction on `k` in `race-heterogeneity` (§1) and checked for every `k` up to `n=60` to 1e−11 and by central differences (E1).

## 3. Equilibrium
Since `π` depends only on `r=m_i/m`, a worker's payoff is `Rπ(m_i/m) − c·m_i` and the first-order condition at the symmetric point is `Rπ'(1)/m = c`:
`m* = R(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})/(nc)`, round time `(H_n−H_{n−k})/m* = nc/((n−k)R)`.
Sufficiency is checked, not proved: E2 searches deviations up to `6m*` on a 4000-point grid for `(n,k)` in {(6,1),(6,3),(10,4),(10,8),(24,12)} at `R=3, c=1`, and no deviation beats staying (the best found is within 0.0005 of `m*`). Worker rent is `R(k/n − π'(1))`, which is positive in all cases. The share of the prize pool spent on speed is `n c m*/(kR) = (n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})/k`: at `n=32` it is 0.969 (`k=1`), 0.848 (8), 0.447 (24), 0.099 (31) (E4).

## 4. Design implications
**Paying everyone buys nothing.** At `k=n`, `π≡1`, `m*=0`, and the round time is infinite: a protocol that pays every returned gradient the same has no speed incentive whatever the prize, and speed must come from a deadline or a dropped tail.
**Winner-take-all is fastest for a budget.** With `B=kR`, `E X_(k) = nck/((n−k)B)`: at `n=32, B=10` it is 0.103 (`k=1`), 1.07 (8), 3.2 (16), 99 (31) (E5). Speed per unit of budget is highest when the prize is concentrated, the usual all-pay-contest logic for exponential races.
**Accuracy is what asks for more winners.** Adding the `straggler-backup` noise floor (`k_min=φ/ε`), the rounds needed diverge at `k_min` and total time is rounds × `nck/((n−k)B)`. In E6 (`n=32`, a=1, η=0.2, σ²=2) the optimum is `k*=⌈k_min⌉` for every budget and target tried (`ε=0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.03` give `k*=2, 3, 5, 8`); the rounds saved by a larger `k` never repay the `k/(n−k)` factor in these instances. This is an observation on a small grid, not a theorem.
**Recruiting does not buy speed.** With `k` and `R` fixed, `T = nc/((n−k)R) → c/R` (E7: `k=4`: 5.0, 2.0, 1.14, 1.03, 1.004 at `n=5, 8, 32, 128, 1024`), and total rate bought `n m* → kR/c`. At fixed fraction `k=n/2` it is exactly `2c/R` for every `n`. Speed is bought by prize per unit cost, not headcount.

## 5. Limits
Exponential finishing times with a controllable rate, linear cost (convex costs change the equilibrium, though `π` still depends only on `r`, so `m*` solves `Rπ'(1)/m = c'(m)` at the symmetric point), risk neutrality, a static one-shot game with no repeated-game or reputation effects, homogeneous costs (heterogeneous costs are a Tullock-type contest with asymmetric equilibria and are not studied), no Sybil identities (see `sybil-stake`), no interaction between speed and gradient quality (a rushed worker may return a worse gradient; see `verification-game`), and equilibrium optimality confirmed by grid, not proved. `k=⌈k_min⌉` is empirical (the `π'(1)` identity is proved in `race-heterogeneity`). Heterogeneous costs are studied there. Related: Tullock contests and rent dissipation (Tullock 1980; Konrad 2009), all-pay contests (Baye–Kovenock–de Vries 1996), rank-order tournaments (Lazear–Rosen 1981), order statistics (Rényi 1953), backup workers (Chen et al. 2016), and `straggler-backup`, `contest-precision`, `rollout-commons` in this repository.
