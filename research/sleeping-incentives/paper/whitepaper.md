# When may a module choose to sleep? Timing of wake declarations in a wealth ledger

**Abstract.** `sleeping-ledger` left open the incentive to declare oneself asleep: a module that sleeps on rounds it would lose keeps its wealth. I show the answer depends entirely on *when* the declaration is made. (i) The ledger pays each awake module exactly its leave-one-out saving: the transfer to `i` is `η W_A (1−p_i) S_i`, with `S_i = ℓ̂_{−i} − ℓ̂` the loss the mixture saves by having `i` in the room (exact; max error 2.4e−16 over 300 random rounds). (ii) If declarations are made *after* losses are seen, that saving is fictitious: against an honest rival of equal skill, a module that sleeps iff its loss exceeds `θ` gains wealth at expected rate `η w_A p_B κ(θ)`, `κ(θ)=θ(1−θ)/2` (max `1/8` at `θ=½`), so its share follows `logit s' = ηκ` and it takes the market in about 130 rounds from `s=0.1` at `η=.25` (mean-field prediction 141, measured median 131), while the router's realised loss stays at `0.4998` (equal to the honest 0.5). (iii) If declarations are made *before* the router serves, the same wealth is a real saving: the router's saving is `s·g` and the ledger's growth `η s(1−s) g` with the same `g = E[(½−ℓ_A)1{σ≤θ}]`, which falls from 0.125 (foresight) to 0.032 at signal noise `ν=1` and 0.011 at `ν=3`. (iv) A sleeping tax that deters the post-hoc exploit must be at least `η p_B/2`, which is a fixed-share rate: it halves an honest specialist asleep 90% of the time in 6 rounds (`τ=η/2=.125`), destroying the frozen ledger. Commitment, not taxation, is the fix.

## 1. Setting
Two modules, `A` (strategic) and `B` (honest, always awake), with iid `U(0,1)` losses, i.e. equal skill, so any persistent wealth advantage is a pure timing exploit. Ledger of `sleeping-ledger`: `p_i=w_i/W_A`, `ℓ̂=Σp_iℓ_i`, `w_i ← w_i(1+η(ℓ̂−ℓ_i))` on `A_t`. The results are for this two-module case with uniform losses except (i), which holds for any awake set. Sleeping-experts background: Freund–Schapire–Singer–Warmuth, Blum; wagering/wealth mechanisms: Lambert–Pennock–Shoham, Frongillo et al. The leave-one-out reading and the timing dichotomy are what I add; I have not checked them against a literature on strategic abstention in wagering and cannot claim novelty.

## 2. Ledger pays marginal contribution
Removing `i` from the awake set changes the price-weighted loss from `ℓ̂` to `ℓ̂_{−i}=(ℓ̂−p_iℓ_i)/(1−p_i)`, so `S_i=p_i(ℓ̂−ℓ_i)/(1−p_i)`. The transfer is `w_i η(ℓ̂−ℓ_i)=ηW_A p_i(ℓ̂−ℓ_i)=ηW_A(1−p_i)S_i`. So the ledger is a marginal-contribution payment with the awake wealth as the scale. This holds whenever `i` really is in the mixture the router serves; it is what makes wealth a meaningful audit statistic.

## 3. Post-hoc sleeping is an option, not a skill
If `A` learns `ℓ_A` first and sleeps when `ℓ_A>θ`, then when awake it earns `η w_A p_B(ℓ_B−ℓ_A)` and when asleep 0. Expected gain `η w_A p_B E[(ℓ_B−ℓ_A)1{ℓ_A≤θ}]=η w_A p_B θ(1−θ)/2`; `θ=1` (never sleep) gives exactly 0, honest play breaks even, and the option is worth `η p_B/8` per unit wealth at `θ=½`. Since total wealth is 1 and `B` pays what `A` earns, `ds=ηκ s(1−s)` in mean field and `A`'s share rises from 0.1 to 0.9 in `ln 81/(ηκ)=141` rounds at `θ=½`. Simulation (60 seeds, `T` up to 4000): measured medians 131 (`θ=.5`), 137 (.6), 136 (.4), 204 (.8), 208 (.2) against predictions 141, 146, 146, 220, 220; the prediction is 3–8% slow, a second-order effect I did not model. The router served the wealth mixture before the declarations, so its realised loss is `sℓ_A+(1−s)ℓ_B`: mean 0.4998–0.5000 for every `θ`. The ledger crowns a module that has done nothing for the router. Anything paid out of wealth (fees, rewards, voting weight) inherits the exploit.

## 4. Ex-ante commitment turns the option into skill
Suppose `A` decides from a signal `σ=ℓ_A+νZ` and the router serves only the awake mixture. Router loss is `½ − s g`, `g=E[(½−ℓ_A)1{σ≤θ}]`, and `A`'s share grows `η s(1−s)g`: both are proportional to the same `g`, the value of selective prediction (§2's identity in mean field). Best `θ`=0.5 for every `ν` tried; `g*` = 0.125 (ν=0), 0.120 (.1), 0.087 (.3), 0.032 (1), 0.011 (3): foresight is fully valuable, a noisy signal is worth little, no signal is worth 0. Simulated saving at `ν=0`, `s=.5` is 0.0621 vs formula 0.0625. Being paid for knowing when you are good is what a specialist should be paid for; the post-hoc option is not. Practically: publish the wake set (or a commitment to it) before the router serves and before the label is knowable.

## 5. Why a tax is the wrong fix
Charge sleepers a fraction `τ` of wealth, paid pro rata to the awake (budget balanced). With threshold `θ`, `A` earns `η p_B θ(1−θ)/2 − τ(1−θ)`, concave with maximiser `θ*=½+τ/(η p_B)`. So never sleeping is the best response iff `τ ≥ η p_B/2` (checked on a grid: best `θ` 0.95 at 0.9τ*, 1.000 at 1.1τ*, for `p_B=1, .5, .1`). At `p_B=1` that is `η/2=0.125`, and an honest specialist asleep 90% of rounds halves its wealth every 5.8 rounds (11.9 at τ=.0625, 61 at .0125): the tax *is* fixed share and forgets the regime it was supposed to protect. A tax small enough to be harmless (0.0125) deters only when `p_B≤0.1`, i.e. once `A` already dominates.

## 6. Limits
Two modules, uniform iid losses, one strategic module playing a threshold rule (not the best possible rule for a module that sees the rival's loss too, which earns more); mean-field share dynamics with a 3–8% error; signal model is Gaussian noise on the true loss; wake declaration timing is treated as a binary (before or after), whereas real systems have delays and partial leaks; no analysis of many strategic modules or of collusion; the tax analysis is for a constant `p_B` (the exploit is worst early). Losses are bounded and exogenous.
