# Racing with unequal workers: who drops out when only the first k are paid

**Abstract.** `speed-race` solved the symmetric race in which the first `k` of `n` finishers are paid `R` and workers buy exponential rate at linear cost. Real swarms are heterogeneous. This note (i) proves the telescoping identity `π'(1)=(1−k/n)(H_n−H_{n−k})` that `speed-race` had only checked, (ii) gives a solver for type-symmetric equilibria with unequal costs, (iii) derives a closed-form **exit rule**: slow workers (cost `c_s`) stay out, exactly, iff `c_s/c_f ≥ n_f/(n_f−k)`, because entering at rate `x→0` wins with probability `x·E X_(k)` and `E X_(k)=n_f c_f/((n_f−k)R)` among the fast workers; the rule is verified by best-entry payoffs, changing sign at the threshold for `k=1,2,3` (thresholds 4/3, 2, 4); and (iv) extends the symmetric formula to convex cost `c·x^p`. Paying `k ≥ n_f` never excludes anyone. (v) With linear cost and every worker active, the round time is exactly the homogeneous formula at the mean cost, `E X_(k)=n c̄/((n−k)R)`, whatever the spread of costs: each worker's marginal win probability is `E[X_(k); it is not among the first k]`, so the marginal win probabilities sum to `(n−k)E X_(k)`, and in equilibrium each equals `c_i/R`. (An earlier version reported this law as empirical and 12% low at cost ratio 8; that gap was a truncated numerical integral, corrected 2026-11-19.)

## 1. Identity (proof)
`π'(1)=(1/n)Σ_{j=0}^{k−1}[1−(H_n−H_{n−1−j})]` (Beta-function derivative, see `speed-race`). Let `S_k=Σ_{j<k}(H_n−H_{n−1−j})`. Claim `S_k = k−(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})`. `k=0`: both 0. Step: `S_k−S_{k−1}=H_n−H_{n−k}`; the right side changes by `1−(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})+(n−k+1)(H_n−H_{n−k+1})`, and `H_{n−k+1}=H_{n−k}+1/(n−k+1)` gives `1−(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})+(n−k+1)(H_n−H_{n−k})−1 = H_n−H_{n−k}`. Hence `π'(1)=(k−S_k)/n=(1−k/n)(H_n−H_{n−k})`. ∎ (This replaces the "found by inspection" remark in `speed-race`.)

## 2. Heterogeneous model and solver
Worker `i` picks rate `x_i` at cost `c_i x_i^p` and finishes at `X_i~Exp(x_i)`. With `s=1−e^{−x t}`, the win probability against rivals at rates `m_j` is `π=∫_0^1 P(N(t(s))≤k−1)ds` with `N` a Poisson-binomial count of rivals finished by `t`; it reduces to the Beta sum for equal rivals and to `x/(x+Σm)` at `k=1`. It is homogeneous of degree 0 in all rates, so the elasticity `e_i=x_i∂π_i/∂x_i` depends only on rate ratios and the first-order condition `Re_i=p c_i x_i^p` is the fixed point `x_i=(Re_i/(pc_i))^{1/p}`, solved by damped iteration for type-symmetric profiles. Tests: win probabilities sum to `k`, match simulation (E1: 0.7540/0.4913/0.4913/0.2635 exact vs 0.7538/0.4932/0.4885/0.2645 over 60000 races), the solver recovers `speed-race`'s `m*` and the convex-cost closed form, and no type has a profitable deviation on a 120-point grid up to 4× its rate (E2, max gain ≤3e−4).

## 3. Exit rule
A worker entering at rate `x→0` is paid iff it beats the `k`-th finisher among `n_f` fast rivals, so `π≈x·E X_(k)`, and `E X_(k)=n_f c_f/((n_f−k)R)` at the fast-only equilibrium. Entry pays iff `R·E X_(k) > c_s`: slow workers idle iff
`c_s/c_f ≥ n_f/(n_f−k)`  (k<n_f);  never for `k ≥ n_f`.
With `n_f=4`: thresholds 4/3, 2, 4, ∞ for `k=1,2,3,4`. E6: the best entry payoff of one slow worker is +0.0026, +0.0035, +0.0038 at 0.9× the threshold and −0.0002, −0.0002, −0.0004 at 1.1× (about 0 at the threshold; grid step 0.001). It also gives the design fact that matters: with `n=8` (4+4) and cost ratio 4, slow workers are shut out completely at `k=1,2,3` (E3: slow/fast rate 0.000, 0.000, 0.003), and re-enter as `k` passes `n_f`: slow share of prizes 0.21, 0.33, 0.41, 0.46 at `k=4..7` against a fair 0.5. A tighter prize count is therefore also an entry barrier; the price is the fast-only round time (`k=1`, ratio ≥4/3: 1.333 vs 1.143 homogeneous).

## 4. Round time and a strong entrant
**Proposition.** For any rates, `Σ_i ∂π_i/∂x_i = (n−k)·E X_(k)`. *Proof.* Let `Y_i` be the `k`-th finish time among `i`'s rivals; `π_i=P(X_i<Y_i)=E[1−e^{−x_iY_i}]`, so `∂π_i/∂x_i=E[Y_i e^{−x_iY_i}]=E[Y_i; X_i>Y_i]`. On `{X_i>Y_i}` worker `i` is not among the first `k` and the overall `k`-th finish is `Y_i`, so the term is `E[X_(k); i not in the first k]`. Summing over `i` counts `X_(k)` once for each of the `n−k` unpaid workers. ∎ With linear cost and every worker at an interior optimum, `R ∂π_i/∂x_i=c_i`, hence **`E X_(k)=Σc_i/((n−k)R)=n c̄/((n−k)R)`** exactly. (`π_i` is concave in `x_i`, as `1−e^{−xY}` is concave in `x`, so the first-order conditions characterise the type-symmetric equilibria the solver finds.) E7 checks it on 4+4 workers, `k=4..6`, ratios 1.5–8: all 12 cases within 0.00%. When some workers idle the sum runs over the active ones and the law becomes `n_a c̄_a/((n_a−k)R)` over the `n_a` active workers, which at the exit threshold is the formula used in §3. *Correction:* the first version of this note reported −0.9%, −4.2% and −11.7% gaps at ratio 8 and called the law an empirical regularity that degrades with spread. Those gaps were numerical: `expected_kth` truncated its integral near `t≈19`, which cut off the long rounds that a wide cost spread produces (found by the quality audit of 2026-11-19, `portfolio/quality/audits/2026-11-19-race-heterogeneity.json`). The integral now runs in `ln t` across every rate scale and agrees with adaptive quadrature to 1e−6; the E2 round times at ratios 4 and 8 also changed (9.904→10.000, 15.499→18.000, 8.886→9.000 at `k=4`). One strong entrant (cost 0.25) among seven weak ones (cost 1), `n=8`: it is paid with probability 0.76, 0.75, 0.84 for `k=1,2,4`, and the round is faster than the all-weak race (1.035 vs 1.143, 1.208 vs 1.333, 1.813 vs 2.000, E4).

## 5. Convex speed cost
With `c x^p`, `x*=(R(1−k/n)(H_n−H_{n−k})/(pc))^{1/p}`, still symmetric-exact. At `n=32, B=10, c=1` the winner-take-all conclusion survives (best `k=1` for `p=1, 1.5, 2, 3`, round times 0.103, 0.091, 0.080, 0.067; E5). Recruiting behaves differently: for `p=2, k=4, R=1` the round time keeps falling with `n` (0.548, 0.256, 0.089 at `n=32, 128, 1024`) instead of saturating at `c/R`, because each worker's marginal cost falls as the pool grows, so with convex costs headcount does buy speed.

## 6. Limits
Two cost types with type-symmetric strategies (asymmetric or mixed equilibria within a type are not searched); the no-deviation check is a grid, not a proof; the damped solver does not report convergence and can leave a near-idle type at a small positive rate where the exact equilibrium has zero (0.0009 at `k=1`, ratio 1.5), so idle types are confirmed by entry payoffs, not by the solver; the zero-rate profile below/above the threshold is verified by entry payoffs for `n_f=4` only; the exit threshold assumes the fast types play their fast-only equilibrium (true once slow types idle); the mean-cost law needs linear cost and an interior equilibrium (with convex cost the first-order condition carries `p x_i^{p−1}` and the law does not hold as stated); costs are the only heterogeneity (no quality, no reputation, no Sybils; see `sybil-stake`); one-shot static race, exponential finishing times, risk neutrality. Related: the exponential first-`k` race is the multiple-prize lottery contest of Clark and Riis (1996, *Public Choice* 87), and at `k=1` it is a Tullock contest with asymmetric players, where Stein (2002, *Public Choice* 113) already gives the participation threshold and total effort, so the `k=1` exit rule here restates his; also Baye–Hoppe (2003), the discouragement effect (Konrad 2009), rank-order tournaments (Lazear–Rosen 1981), and `speed-race`, `straggler-backup`, `hetero-pipeline` here.
