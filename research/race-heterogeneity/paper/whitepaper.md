# Racing with unequal workers: who drops out when only the first k are paid

**Abstract.** `speed-race` solved the symmetric race in which the first `k` of `n` finishers are paid `R` and workers buy exponential rate at linear cost. Real swarms are heterogeneous. This note (i) proves the telescoping identity `π'(1)=(1−k/n)(H_n−H_{n−k})` that `speed-race` had only checked, (ii) gives a solver for type-symmetric equilibria with unequal costs, (iii) derives a closed-form **exit rule**: slow workers (cost `c_s`) stay out, exactly, iff `c_s/c_f ≥ n_f/(n_f−k)`, because entering at rate `x→0` wins with probability `x·E X_(k)` and `E X_(k)=n_f c_f/((n_f−k)R)` among the fast workers; the rule is verified by best-entry payoffs, changing sign at the threshold for `k=1,2,3` (thresholds 4/3, 2, 4); and (iv) extends the symmetric formula to convex cost `c·x^p`. Paying `k ≥ n_f` never excludes anyone. Observed but not proved: while every type is active the round time is close to the homogeneous formula at the mean cost, `n c̄/((n−k)R)` (exact to 1e−5 in 6 of 12 cases, 12% low at cost ratio 8).

## 1. Identity (proof)
`π'(1)=(1/n)Σ_{j=0}^{k−1}[1−(H_n−H_{n−1−j})]` (Beta-function derivative, see `speed-race`). Let `S_k=Σ_{j<k}(H_n−H_{n−1−j})`. Claim `S_k = k−(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})`. `k=0`: both 0. Step: `S_k−S_{k−1}=H_n−H_{n−k}`; the right side changes by `1−(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})+(n−k+1)(H_n−H_{n−k+1})`, and `H_{n−k+1}=H_{n−k}+1/(n−k+1)` gives `1−(n−k)(H_n−H_{n−k})+(n−k+1)(H_n−H_{n−k})−1 = H_n−H_{n−k}`. Hence `π'(1)=(k−S_k)/n=(1−k/n)(H_n−H_{n−k})`. ∎ (This replaces the "found by inspection" remark in `speed-race`.)

## 2. Heterogeneous model and solver
Worker `i` picks rate `x_i` at cost `c_i x_i^p` and finishes at `X_i~Exp(x_i)`. With `s=1−e^{−x t}`, the win probability against rivals at rates `m_j` is `π=∫_0^1 P(N(t(s))≤k−1)ds` with `N` a Poisson-binomial count of rivals finished by `t`; it reduces to the Beta sum for equal rivals and to `x/(x+Σm)` at `k=1`. It is homogeneous of degree 0 in all rates, so the elasticity `e_i=x_i∂π_i/∂x_i` depends only on rate ratios and the first-order condition `Re_i=p c_i x_i^p` is the fixed point `x_i=(Re_i/(pc_i))^{1/p}`, solved by damped iteration for type-symmetric profiles. Tests: win probabilities sum to `k`, match simulation (E1: 0.7540/0.4913/0.4913/0.2635 exact vs 0.7538/0.4932/0.4885/0.2645 over 60000 races), the solver recovers `speed-race`'s `m*` and the convex-cost closed form, and no type has a profitable deviation on a 120-point grid up to 4× its rate (E2, max gain ≤3e−4).

## 3. Exit rule
A worker entering at rate `x→0` is paid iff it beats the `k`-th finisher among `n_f` fast rivals, so `π≈x·E X_(k)`, and `E X_(k)=n_f c_f/((n_f−k)R)` at the fast-only equilibrium. Entry pays iff `R·E X_(k) > c_s`: slow workers idle iff
`c_s/c_f ≥ n_f/(n_f−k)`  (k<n_f);  never for `k ≥ n_f`.
With `n_f=4`: thresholds 4/3, 2, 4, ∞ for `k=1,2,3,4`. E6: the best entry payoff of one slow worker is +0.0026, +0.0035, +0.0038 at 0.9× the threshold and −0.0002, −0.0002, −0.0004 at 1.1× (about 0 at the threshold; grid step 0.001). It also gives the design fact that matters: with `n=8` (4+4) and cost ratio 4, slow workers are shut out completely at `k=1,2,3` (E3: slow/fast rate 0.000, 0.000, 0.003), and re-enter as `k` passes `n_f`: slow share of prizes 0.21, 0.33, 0.41, 0.46 at `k=4..7` against a fair 0.5. A tighter prize count is therefore also an entry barrier; the price is the fast-only round time (`k=1`, ratio ≥4/3: 1.333 vs 1.143 homogeneous).

## 4. Round time and a strong entrant
With all types active (`k=4..6`, 4+4, ratios 1.5–8) the simulated equilibrium round time is compared with `n c̄/((n−k)R)`, `c̄` the mean cost (E7): 0.00% at ratios 1.5 and 2, −0.00–0.07% at 4, and −0.9%, −4.2%, −11.7% at ratio 8 for `k=4,5,6`. This is a numerical regularity, not a theorem, and it degrades with spread. One strong entrant (cost 0.25) among seven weak ones (cost 1), `n=8`: it is paid with probability 0.76, 0.75, 0.84 for `k=1,2,4`, and the round is faster than the all-weak race (1.035 vs 1.143, 1.208 vs 1.333, 1.813 vs 2.000, E4).

## 5. Convex speed cost
With `c x^p`, `x*=(R(1−k/n)(H_n−H_{n−k})/(pc))^{1/p}`, still symmetric-exact. At `n=32, B=10, c=1` the winner-take-all conclusion survives (best `k=1` for `p=1, 1.5, 2, 3`, round times 0.103, 0.091, 0.080, 0.067; E5). Recruiting behaves differently: for `p=2, k=4, R=1` the round time keeps falling with `n` (0.548, 0.256, 0.089 at `n=32, 128, 1024`) instead of saturating at `c/R`, because each worker's marginal cost falls as the pool grows, so with convex costs headcount does buy speed.

## 6. Limits
Two cost types with type-symmetric strategies (asymmetric or mixed equilibria within a type are not searched); the no-deviation check is a grid, not a proof; the zero-rate profile below/above the threshold is verified by entry payoffs for `n_f=4` only; the exit threshold assumes the fast types play their fast-only equilibrium (true once slow types idle); the mean-cost regularity is empirical; costs are the only heterogeneity (no quality, no reputation, no Sybils; see `sybil-stake`); one-shot static race, exponential finishing times, risk neutrality. Related: Tullock contests with asymmetric players (Stein 2002; Baye–Hoppe 2003), the discouragement effect (Konrad 2009), rank-order tournaments (Lazear–Rosen 1981), and `speed-race`, `straggler-backup`, `hetero-pipeline` here.
