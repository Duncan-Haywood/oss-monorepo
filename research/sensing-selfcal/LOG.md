## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 6b planned: which corner localiser is right? Renders with a known camera at the real poses

Written before any image was rendered. Milestone 6 found that the corner localiser alone moves flat-board Zhang's f by 0.9–1.3% (4.8 / 7.2 px) and target-free f by 1.8 / 4.2 px, that `cornerSubPix` 11×11 and `findChessboardCornersSB` disagree by more than 1 px on 52 corners, all on the grid's outer ring, and that no ground truth said which is right. Here the truth is known: each camera's 13 views are re-rendered from a known camera and known poses, and every localiser is scored against the exact projections.

- **Renderer.** The true camera of each side is its all-view flat-board Zhang fit on 11×11 corners with k1 and k2 (OpenCV model, no tangential terms), and the 13 board poses are that fit's poses, so each render puts the board where it was in the real image. Each 640×480 pixel is the mean of a 4×4 grid of sub-pixel rays, each undistorted (fixed-point inversion of the radial model), intersected with the board plane and shaded: a flat 10×7-square checkerboard (9×6 inner corners), a white margin of 0.5 square around it (the real board's white border, read off the images), a uniform mid-grey background beyond. Then a Gaussian blur, additive Gaussian noise, 8-bit rounding and JPEG encoding.
- **Matched setting, fixed before any localiser is scored on a render.** Black and white levels are the medians of the real images' square interiors near detected corners; blur σ is fitted to the real images' edge profiles (an erf fit across the board's inner edges); noise σ is the rms of a 3×3 Laplacian in flat square interiors, rescaled to a pixel σ; JPEG quality 90. Also swept: blur 0.5× and 2× the matched value, and noise 0 and 2×. 20 noise seeds per setting.
- **Localisers.** The three of milestone 6: `cornerSubPix` 11×11 and 5×5 half-windows, and SB with `CALIB_CB_ACCURACY`. Error = detected minus true projection, matched by corner index (truth ordering).
- **H33 (the render reproduces the ring).** In the matched setting, at least one outer-ring corner per camera differs by more than 1 px between 11×11 and SB, as on the real images. Refuted if none does on either camera: then the real ring disagreement comes from something the render leaves out (the clipboard edge, clutter, lighting), not from the ideal pattern.
- **H34 (SB is closest to the truth).** Matched setting, pooled over seeds and views: SB's rms corner error is below 11×11's, on the outer ring and in the interior, on both cameras. Refuted if 11×11 or 5×5 is lower on either subset on either camera.
- **H35 (the localiser picks the focal length).** Flat-board Zhang (k1 + k2, as the truth) on SB corners recovers f within 0.2% of the truth on both cameras (median over seeds), and 11×11's |f error| is larger than SB's on both. Refuted if SB is off by more than 0.2% on either camera, or 11×11 is no worse on either.
- **H36 (σ is honest when the world is ideal).** Target-free all-view BA (k1 + k2) on SB corners, z = (f − f<sub>true</sub>)/σ<sub>f</sub> over the 20 seeds: rms z ≤ 1.5 on both cameras. Refuted if above 1.5 on either. On the real images the nested rms z was 3.12 with SB corners (milestone 6); if renders are honest the 2–3× comes from the world, not the localiser on an ideal pattern.
- **H37 (no column structure on an ideal pattern).** In the matched setting, the target-free all-view residuals' adjacent along-column correlation is below 0.2 for every localiser on both cameras (real: 0.44–0.56 with 11×11, 0.32 / 0.56 with SB). Refuted if 0.2 or more for any localiser on either camera.
- **Reported, unscored.** All five predictions in the blur and noise sweep; per-ring error maps; target-free f error per localiser; whether the real-image f shift between localisers (milestone 6) is reproduced.
- **Caveat set in advance.** A render is a model of the image, not the image: it leaves out the clipboard's rounded edge, background clutter, vignetting, defocus that varies with depth, the bend milestones 5–6 found, and the sensor's real noise and demosaicing. A localiser that wins on renders is right *for the ideal pattern*; a real-image gap the renders do not reproduce points at what they leave out.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4c result: the 3-parameter test is information-limited, but testing f alone against a borrowed tail threshold catches a 2% focal change 3–4× as often

- **Runs.** No new solve: milestone 4b's 8,580 stored solves, its splits and disturbances, k ≥ 4, 9,360 decisions (360 unchanged splits per detector). Satterthwaite ν on unchanged splits: median 6.2 (11×11) / 5.9 (SB), 10–90% 3.0–10.1, so the Hotelling threshold has median 68 / 79 against χ²<sub>3</sub>'s 11.3. Empirical 99th percentiles of W on unchanged splits: 17.5 / 14.7 (11×11, left / right), 9.8 / 15.2 (SB); of z<sub>f</sub>²: 2.03 / 1.69 and 4.22 / 1.95, against χ²<sub>1</sub>'s 6.63.
- **H28 held.** F reference: 0 false alarms in 360 on both detectors (95% 0–1.1%).
- **H29 held.** F keeps 19% (11×11) and 22% (SB) of χ²'s ±4% detections at k = 6 (8% against 43%; 13% against 56%).
- **H30 held.** The other camera's W threshold: 2.2% / 0.6% false alarms (11×11, left / right), 0.6% / 2.8% (SB).
- **H31 held.** The oracle threshold on W detects ±2% at k = 6 in 4.6% (11×11) and 10.0% (SB) of splits: no threshold on the 3-parameter statistic rescues it.
- **H32 refuted.** z<sub>f</sub>² against χ²<sub>1</sub> detects ±2% in 3.3% (11×11) and 7.9% (SB), fewer than W against χ²<sub>3</sub> (8.3% / 11.7%); its false alarms are 0.0% / 0.3%. Against χ²<sub>1</sub> the f-only test is too quiet, because the jackknife overstates f's variance by about 3× (99th percentile 1.7–4.2 where χ²<sub>1</sub> says 6.6).
- **Unscored, and the main finding.** z<sub>f</sub>² against the *other camera's* 99th percentile (E<sub>1</sub>) detects ±2% at k = 6 in 41% / 28% (11×11, −2% / +2%) and 40% / 31% (SB), and ±4% in 78–85%, with false alarms 2.2% / 0.6% (11×11) and 5.0% / 0.0% (SB, left / right). The same camera's own threshold (oracle) gives 43% / 28% and 38% / 33%: the borrowed threshold loses almost nothing. A 1% change stays invisible (4–12%). The f-only test cannot see the unit swap (1–4%), which lives in c<sub>x</sub>; an exploratory union (W or z<sub>f</sub>², both thresholds borrowed, not pre-registered) keeps the swap (21% / 73% at k ≥ 4) and the ±2% power (29–43% at k = 6), at 2.8% / 4.2% false alarms (left camera 4.4% / 5.6%).
- **Harmful adoptions** (milestone 4's rule, all scenarios, k ≥ 4): f-only with E<sub>1</sub> 0.47% (11×11) and 0.96% (SB); the union 0.68% / 2.29%.
- **Reading.** Four of five predictions held. The jackknife test's deafness had two sources: for the 3-parameter statistic it is information (the oracle buys nothing), but for f the χ² reference is badly mis-scaled, and a tail threshold borrowed from a sibling unit recovers most of the oracle's power. The practical test is two statistics, f against a borrowed threshold for focal change and W for principal-point moves.
- **Caveat.** The thresholds are 99th percentiles of 180 statistics from splits that reuse 13 views; the E<sub>1</sub> false-alarm rate on the SB left camera (5.0%) shows the borrowed tail does not always match. The union and the E<sub>1</sub> power were not pre-registered.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4c planned: is the jackknife test deaf because of its χ² reference or because 4–6 views carry too little information?

Written before any milestone-4c number was computed. Milestone 4b's jackknife test (J) was quiet (1.7–2.5% false alarms at a nominal 1%) but caught a ±2% focal change in only 7–12% of splits with 6 new views. Two explanations predict different fixes: the χ²<sub>3</sub> reference is wrong for a covariance estimated from 4–7 delete-one solves (then a better reference or a narrower test buys power back), or the views simply do not carry the information (then no threshold helps). Milestone 4c answers it from milestone 4b's 8,580 stored solves (`m4b_viewcov/results.json.gz`), with no new solve, on the same splits, disturbances, detectors and k ≥ 4 scoring.

- **Statistic.** W = dᵀ(J<sub>old</sub> + s²J<sub>new</sub>)⁻¹d on d = θ<sub>new</sub> − θ<sub>old</sub>, θ = (f, c<sub>x</sub>, c<sub>y</sub>), as in 4b; and the one-parameter z<sub>f</sub>² on f alone.
- **Four references for W at 1%.** (χ²) 11.34, as 4b. (F) a Hotelling-type reference with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom from the f variances, ν = (a + b)² / (a²/(m<sub>old</sub> − 1) + b²/(m<sub>new</sub> − 1)), a = J<sub>old,ff</sub>, b = s²J<sub>new,ff</sub>, m the delete-one solves that converged; threshold 3ν/(ν − 2) · F<sub>3, ν−2</sub>(0.99). (E) the other camera's empirical 99th percentile of W on unchanged splits (S0), same detector, k ≥ 4 pooled. (O, oracle) the same camera's own S0 99th percentile, k ≥ 4 pooled: false alarms ≈ 1% by construction, so it is an upper bound on the power any recalibration of the threshold can buy, not a usable test.
- **Two references for z<sub>f</sub>².** χ²<sub>1</sub> (6.63) and F<sub>1, ν</sub>(0.99).
- **H28 (F is safe).** J with the F reference: S0 false alarms (k ≥ 4, pooled) ≤ 1% on both detectors. Refuted if above 1% on either.
- **H29 (F is too strict).** J with F detects a ±4% focal change at k = 6 (pooled over sign and camera) in fewer than half as many splits as J with χ², on both detectors. Refuted if F keeps at least half on either. Milestone 4b found J already overstates the spread (rms z 0.46–0.84), and ν is 3–9 here, so F's threshold is several times χ²'s.
- **H30 (a transferred threshold travels).** J with E: S0 false alarms (k ≥ 4) ≤ 5% on each camera, on both detectors. Refuted if above 5% on any. Unlike milestone 4's borrowed κ, the jackknife already scales to each camera, so only the tail shape is borrowed.
- **H31 (the limit is information).** With the oracle threshold O, a ±2% focal change at k = 6 (pooled over sign and camera) is detected in fewer than 25% of splits on both detectors. Refuted if 25% or more on either.
- **H32 (testing f alone buys power).** z<sub>f</sub>² against χ²<sub>1</sub> detects a ±2% focal change at k = 6 in more splits than W against χ²<sub>3</sub>, on both detectors, with S0 false alarms (k ≥ 4, pooled) ≤ 3%. Refuted if either part fails on either detector.
- **Reported, unscored.** The full power table (±0.5–4% focal, 2–10 px principal point, the swap) for every reference; harmful adoptions under milestone 4's rule with each reference; the threshold values (median ν, F and E thresholds).
- **Caveat carried over.** The splits reuse 13 views, so the 95% Wilson intervals are too narrow, and an empirical 99th percentile from 180 statistics rests on about two of them.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4 planned: the accept/reject test on disjoint views, a real camera swap and exact disturbances

Written before any milestone-4 experiment was run. Proposal milestone 4: turn milestones 1–3 into the decision a robot makes, and measure its false-alarm and false-adopt rates.

- **Old and new from disjoint views.** Milestones 2–3 scored subsets against an all-view solution that contains them (hence the nested variance). A deployed camera's old calibration comes from other images, so here each camera's 13 views are split: an "old" set of 7 views, and a "new" set of k ∈ {3, 4, 5, 6} views drawn from the other 6. 60 splits per camera and k (fixed seeds), both sets solved with the milestone-2 solver (k1 model). The two estimates are then independent apart from the shared board, and the test covariance is Σ<sub>old</sub> + Σ<sub>new</sub> (full 3×3 over f, c<sub>x</sub>, c<sub>y</sub>).
- **Changes.** (S0) none. (S1) focal change: the new set's corners scaled about the image centre by s = 1 ± 0.5, 1, 2, 4%. (S2) principal-point shift of 2, 5, 10 px in u. Both are exact transforms of the pinhole-plus-k1 model, so the new solution is the transformed undisturbed one (checked by re-solving in a test) and the real detector noise is kept. (S3) a real change: old from one camera, new from the other (the stereo pair is two units of the same model; all-view f differs by 0.67%, c<sub>x</sub> by 17 px, c<sub>y</sub> by 10 px). Truth: the camera's all-view target-free solution, transformed for S1/S2, the other camera's for S3.
- **The rule.** χ² = Δᵀ(κ²(Σ<sub>old</sub> + Σ<sub>new</sub>))⁻¹Δ on (f, c<sub>x</sub>, c<sub>y</sub>). Change detected iff χ² > 11.34 (χ²<sub>3</sub>, 1%). Detected and σ<sub>f,new</sub>/f ≤ 0.5% (milestone 2's gate): adopt the new calibration. Detected and imprecise: flag for a target recalibration. Not detected: keep the old one. κ = 1 (reported covariance) and κ = the other camera's milestone-3 nested rms z (1.89 for the left camera, 2.67 for the right), so no factor is fitted on the camera it is scored on.
- **H18 (false alarms).** On S0 with κ = 1 the test fires on more than 5% of splits (nominal 1%). Refuted if ≤ 5%.
- **H19 (the transferred κ).** On S0 with transferred κ, false alarms ≤ 3% pooled and ≤ 5% on each camera. Refuted otherwise.
- **H20 (the real swap).** With transferred κ, S3 is detected in ≥ 90% of splits with k ≥ 4, in both directions. Refuted if below on either.
- **H21 (power for a 1% focal change).** With transferred κ, s = 1.01 and 0.99 are detected in ≥ 80% of splits with k ≥ 5. Refuted below. The power curve over all s, shifts and k is reported.
- **H22 (no harmful adoption).** Over all scenarios, k and both κ, the rule adopts an update whose f error against truth exceeds the old calibration's by more than 0.5% of f in ≤ 2% of splits. Refuted if more. Also reported: the flag rate, and how often the decision leaves an f error ≥ 1%.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 3 planned: is the 2× overconfidence real, and does a cluster-robust σ fix it?

Written before any milestone-3 experiment was run. The proposal's milestone 3 (natural features on a second data set) moves to 3b; this step answers the question milestone 2 left open, on the same 560 subsets, with no new data.

- **Why σ could be too small.** The Gauss–Newton σ assumes independent, equal-variance pixel residuals and a correct model. Corner-detector errors are correlated within an image (blur, lighting, the detector's shared sub-pixel window) and the k1-only lens leaves structured residuals. Both make the reported σ too small by a factor that differs between subsets, which a single inflation factor cannot follow (milestone 2's cross-camera κ).
- **Robust σ, two estimators, no fitted constants.** (a) The cluster-robust sandwich, clustered by view: var f = Σ_views ((J b)·r)² over each view's residuals, with b = (JᵀJ)⁻¹e_f, times the small-sample factor G/(G − 1). (b) The leave-one-view-out jackknife: re-solve the subset k times without one view each (cold start, same solver), var f = (k − 1)/k Σ (f₍ᵢ₎ − f̄)². Ablation: the sandwich clustered by corner (54 clusters) and unclustered (HC0).
- **H8 (the reference is not the cause).** Zhang's all-view f is itself 0.8 / 1.3 px from the target-free all-view f, so part of milestone 2's z could be reference mismatch. Self-consistency test against the target-free all-view solution, with the nested-estimator variance: z_n = (f_sub − f_all) / √(σ_sub² − σ_all²), random subsets of 4–12 views (subsets with σ_sub ≤ σ_all are counted and dropped). Predicted: rms z_n > 1.5 (the overconfidence is real). Refuted if ≤ 1.5.
- **H9 (robust σ is honest).** With σ from the jackknife, rms z against all-view Zhang over random 4–12-view subsets lies in [0.7, 1.4] on each camera, with no fitted factor. Scored separately for the view-clustered sandwich; predicted to hold for the jackknife and to fail (rms z > 1.4) for the sandwich at 4–6 views, where few clusters bias it down.
- **H10 (robust σ ranks better).** As a gate statistic for good (|Δf|/f ≤ 1%) against bad (> 2%) updates, pooled over all subsets of 4–12 views and both cameras, the jackknife σ/f has a higher ROC AUC than the reported σ/f. Refuted if it is equal or lower (paired bootstrap over subsets, 95% interval of the AUC difference).
- **H4, second test.** Accept iff σ_jack / f ≤ 0.5% (the same threshold as milestone 2); target 90/90. Also scored on the near-critical subsets alone. And the transfer test from milestone 1's next steps: the threshold on each σ that maximises min(accept good, reject bad) on one camera, scored on the other.
