# sensing-selfcal research log

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4 planned: the accept/reject test on disjoint views, a real camera swap and exact disturbances

Written before any milestone-4 experiment was run. Proposal milestone 4: turn milestones 1–3 into the decision a robot makes, and measure its false-alarm and false-adopt rates.

- **Old and new from disjoint views.** Milestones 2–3 scored subsets against an all-view solution that contains them (hence the nested variance). A deployed camera's old calibration comes from other images, so here each camera's 13 views are split: an "old" set of 7 views, and a "new" set of k ∈ {3, 4, 5, 6} views drawn from the other 6. 60 splits per camera and k (fixed seeds), both sets solved with the milestone-2 solver (k1 model). The two estimates are then independent apart from the shared board, and the test covariance is Σ<sub>old</sub> + Σ<sub>new</sub> (full 3×3 over f, c<sub>x</sub>, c<sub>y</sub>).
- **Changes.** (S0) none. (S1) focal change: the new set's corners scaled about the image centre by s = 1 ± 0.5, 1, 2, 4%. (S2) principal-point shift of 2, 5, 10 px in u. Both are exact transforms of the pinhole-plus-k1 model, so the new solution is the transformed undisturbed one (checked by re-solving in a test) and the real detector noise is kept. (S3) a real change: old from one camera, new from the other (the stereo pair is two units of the same model; all-view f differs by 0.67%, c<sub>x</sub> by 17 px, c<sub>y</sub> by 10 px). Truth: the camera's all-view target-free solution, transformed for S1/S2, the other camera's for S3.
- **The rule.** χ² = Δᵀ(κ²(Σ<sub>old</sub> + Σ<sub>new</sub>))⁻¹Δ on (f, c<sub>x</sub>, c<sub>y</sub>). Change detected iff χ² > 11.34 (χ²<sub>3</sub>, 1%). Detected and σ<sub>f,new</sub>/f ≤ 0.5% (milestone 2's gate): adopt the new calibration. Detected and imprecise: flag for a target recalibration. Not detected: keep the old one. κ = 1 (reported covariance) and κ = the other camera's milestone-3 nested rms z (1.89 for the left camera, 2.67 for the right), so no factor is fitted on the camera it is scored on.
- **H18 (false alarms).** On S0 with κ = 1 the test fires on more than 5% of splits (nominal 1%). Refuted if ≤ 5%.
- **H19 (the transferred κ).** On S0 with transferred κ, false alarms ≤ 3% pooled and ≤ 5% on each camera. Refuted otherwise.
- **H20 (the real swap).** With transferred κ, S3 is detected in ≥ 90% of splits with k ≥ 4, in both directions. Refuted if below on either.
- **H21 (power for a 1% focal change).** With transferred κ, s = 1.01 and 0.99 are detected in ≥ 80% of splits with k ≥ 5. Refuted below. The power curve over all s, shifts and k is reported.
- **H22 (no harmful adoption).** Over all scenarios, k and both κ, the rule adopts an update whose f error against truth exceeds the old calibration's by more than 0.5% of f in ≤ 2% of splits. Refuted if more. Also reported: the flag rate, and how often the decision leaves an f error ≥ 1%.
- **Ablation, unscored.** The f-only test (1 degree of freedom) and the 4-parameter test with k1.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 6 planned: where does the column-wise residual correlation come from, and does a better corner detector or a column-clustered σ fix the 2×?

Written before any milestone-6 experiment was run (only the corner spacing and the detection rate of the second detector were checked: 25–41 px between neighbours, and `findChessboardCornersSB` finds the board in 26 of 26 images).

- **What milestone 5 left.** In the target-free k1 residuals on all 13 views, corners that are neighbours along a board column correlate at 0.56 / 0.44 (left / right), and corners two apart at 0.73 / 0.65, while neighbours along a row are at about 0. A correlation that rises with distance looks like an offset shared by each column of each image, not a local detector error. Free 3-D points absorb any fixed print error of the board, so whatever is left must change from image to image.
- **Descriptive, not a test.** The share of residual variance explained by a per-(view, board column) mean and by a per-(view, board row) mean, against the null for independent noise (1/6 and 1/9); and how much of the column-mean residual lies perpendicular to the column's line in the image, against along it.

Refuted if it stays at 0.2 or more on either, which puts the cause upstream of the localiser (the image or the board's mounting).
- **H15 (the column offsets are sideways).** The per-(view, column) mean residual is predicted to lie mostly perpendicular to the column's image direction: ≥ 70% of its energy on both cameras (isotropic would be 50%). A sideways shift of a whole column points at the localisation of that column's edge line; a shift along it does not.

- **H17 (a better detector shrinks the 2×).** Re-solve milestone 2's 560 subsets (same views and reference views) with SB corners and the k1 model, and score the nested self-consistency test of H8 against the SB all-view target-free solution. Predicted: rms z ≤ 1.5 (against 2.28 with `cornerSubPix`). Refuted if > 1.5. Also reported: the all-view intrinsics with each detector, and whether c<sub>y</sub> and f move.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 3 planned: is the 2× overconfidence real, and does a cluster-robust σ fix it?

Written before any milestone-3 experiment was run. The proposal's milestone 3 (natural features on a second data set) moves to 3b; this step answers the question milestone 2 left open, on the same 560 subsets, with no new data.

- **Why σ could be too small.** The Gauss–Newton σ assumes independent, equal-variance pixel residuals and a correct model. Corner-detector errors are correlated within an image (blur, lighting, the detector's shared sub-pixel window) and the k1-only lens leaves structured residuals. Both make the reported σ too small by a factor that differs between subsets, which a single inflation factor cannot follow (milestone 2's cross-camera κ).
- **Robust σ, two estimators, no fitted constants.** (a) The cluster-robust sandwich, clustered by view: var f = Σ_views ((J b)·r)² over each view's residuals, with b = (JᵀJ)⁻¹e_f, times the small-sample factor G/(G − 1). (b) The leave-one-view-out jackknife: re-solve the subset k times without one view each (cold start, same solver), var f = (k − 1)/k Σ (f₍ᵢ₎ − f̄)². Ablation: the sandwich clustered by corner (54 clusters) and unclustered (HC0).
- **H8 (the reference is not the cause).** Zhang's all-view f is itself 0.8 / 1.3 px from the target-free all-view f, so part of milestone 2's z could be reference mismatch. Self-consistency test against the target-free all-view solution, with the nested-estimator variance: z_n = (f_sub − f_all) / √(σ_sub² − σ_all²), random subsets of 4–12 views (subsets with σ_sub ≤ σ_all are counted and dropped). Predicted: rms z_n > 1.5 (the overconfidence is real). Refuted if ≤ 1.5.
- **H9 (robust σ is honest).** With σ from the jackknife, rms z against all-view Zhang over random 4–12-view subsets lies in [0.7, 1.4] on each camera, with no fitted factor. Scored separately for the view-clustered sandwich; predicted to hold for the jackknife and to fail (rms z > 1.4) for the sandwich at 4–6 views, where few clusters bias it down.
- **H10 (robust σ ranks better).** As a gate statistic for good (|Δf|/f ≤ 1%) against bad (> 2%) updates, pooled over all subsets of 4–12 views and both cameras, the jackknife σ/f has a higher ROC AUC than the reported σ/f. Refuted if it is equal or lower (paired bootstrap over subsets, 95% interval of the AUC difference).
- **H4, second test.** Accept iff σ_jack / f ≤ 0.5% (the same threshold as milestone 2); target 90/90. Also scored on the near-critical subsets alone. And the transfer test from milestone 1's next steps: the threshold on each σ that maximises min(accept good, reject bad) on one camera, scored on the other.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 2 planned: does the view geometry predict the error, and can σ gate an update?

Written before any milestone-2 experiment was run.

- **Solver fix first.** Milestone 1's solves all stopped on the 1,000-evaluation cap. Fix the gauge by holding view 0's pose (rotation and translation) constant: that removes all 7 similarity directions (a fixed non-zero translation pins the scale), so the normal matrix is invertible and the intrinsics' covariance is the plain inverse, equal to the gauge-invariant pseudo-inverse. Raise the cap and record convergence status for every solve.
- **H2 rerun (was the cap an artefact?).** Rerun milestone 1's 400 random subsets (same seeds) with the fixed solver. Predicted: rms z for 4–12 views stays within ±0.2 of 1.94 (the all-view f already agreed across starts to 0.001 px). Refuted if it moves by more than 0.2.
- **View spread.** For each view, the board normal in the camera frame from the all-view Zhang poses. A subset's spread is the largest angle between two of its normals. All parallel normals is the planar critical case (Sturm and Maybank 1999): f is then unidentifiable.
- **Near-critical subsets.** For sizes 3, 4, 5 and 6, enumerate every subset of the 13 views and keep the 20 with the smallest spread, per camera (160 solves), alongside the 400 random ones.
- **H6 (geometry predicts error).** Pooled over random and near-critical subsets of 4–12 views, Spearman ρ between spread and |Δf|/f (against all-view Zhang) is ≤ −0.3 on each camera. Refuted if ρ > −0.3 on either.
- **H7 (σ flags near-critical subsets).** On the near-critical subsets, rms z ≤ 2, i.e. σ grows as fast as the error. Refuted if rms z > 2.
- **H4, first test (a precision gate).** Accept an update iff σ_f / f ≤ 0.5%. A good update has |Δf|/f ≤ 1%, a bad one > 2% (between is not scored). Pooled over all subsets and both cameras, the gate should reject ≥ 90% of bad updates and accept ≥ 90% of good ones. Refuted if either rate is below 90%. The proposal's χ² test against an old calibration needs a disturbed camera, which this data set does not have; that test waits for milestone 4.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 1 planned: target-free self-calibration against Zhang on a real stereo pair

Written before any experiment was run.

- **Data.** OpenCV's `samples/data` stereo calibration set, `left01..14.jpg` and `right01..14.jpg` (index 10 absent in both), 640×480, a 9×6-inner-corner checkerboard, Apache-2.0. Two real cameras, 13 views each.
- **Reference.** Zhang calibration with the board's known geometry (`cv2.calibrateCamera`, model fx = fy = f, cx, cy, k1, k2) on all 13 views of each camera; interval by bootstrap over views.
- **Target-free solver.** The same detected corners, but the 54 board points become free 3-D unknowns (no planarity, no spacing): initial focal guessed 30% wrong, two-view initialisation from the homography between images (no board metric), PnP for the rest, then bundle adjustment over poses, points and intrinsics (f, cx, cy, k1). Covariance from the Gauss–Newton normal matrix with the gauge fixed, scaled by the residual variance.
- **H1.** On all 13 views, target-free f lies within 2% of Zhang's f for both cameras. Refuted if either is further.
- **H2 (the solver knows when it is wrong).** Over random view subsets of size 4 to 12, the z-score (target-free f − all-view Zhang f) / σ_f(target-free) has rms ≤ 2. Refuted if rms > 2, i.e. the covariance is overconfident.
- **H3 (price of no target).** Target-free σ_f is at least twice Zhang's on the same views, and the ratio falls as views are added. Refuted if the ratio is below 2 at 13 views.
