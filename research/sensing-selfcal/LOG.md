# sensing-selfcal research log

## 2026-11-14 · Milestone 4j planned: the twin reference and the update test on two more units (TUM RGB-D's Kinect and Xtion)

Written before any milestone-4j focal length was looked at. Every result of milestones 1–4i comes from one stereo rig (OpenCV's two cameras, one board, 13 views), and the paper's Section 10 asks first for the twin reference and the robust test "pre-registered on a second unit's views". The TUM RGB-D benchmark (Sturm et al., IROS 2012; CC BY 4.0) publishes checkerboard calibration sequences for each of its cameras, with the authors' own calibration: `freiburg1_rgb_calibration` (a Microsoft Kinect, fx 517.3, fy 516.5, strong distortion d0 0.2624, d1 −0.9531) and `freiburg3_calibration_rgb_depth` (an Asus Xtion whose images come undistorted, fx 535.4, fy 539.2). Two new units, a different board (8×6 inner corners), a different sensor and lens, and a real focal difference between them (3.5% in TUM's figures), which gives the swap scenario a true change instead of a scaled one.

- **Data.** 13 views per unit picked by a rule written into `experiments/m4j_tum/prepare.py` before any solve: full-board detections with 5×5 corners, the even board's 180° ambiguity resolved by the first row pointing right, corner spacing ≥ 15 px, then 13 equal-count blocks in time and the most foreshortened of each block's sharper half. A first version of the rule (sharpest frame per time block) picked boards 7–8 px between corners and 11 of 13 fronto-parallel views; it was replaced after looking only at corner spacing and a contact sheet, no solve. Only corners and frame names are stored (`corners_fr1.json`, `corners_fr3.json`); a person holds the board in every frame, so no image is committed (`fetch.sh` gets the sequences). fr1: 820 of 1,507 frames qualify; fr3: 916 of 1,327.
- **Solves.** Milestone 4f's solver unchanged (k1 only, start 1.3× the k1 Zhang f, references 0 and n // 2): every subset of 3–7 views and all 13, both units, 11,442 solves (`run.py`). Splits, scenarios, J and R exactly as milestone 4g, with the two TUM units in place of left and right: S0, S1 (s = 0.96–1.04) and S3 (the old set from the other unit). The twin and its references exactly as milestone 4i (resid model scored, iid reported, 10,000 draws per split, seed 20261114).
- **H63 (anchor).** fr3's 13-view f lies within 1% of TUM's (fx + fy)/2 = 537.3 px. Refuted outside. (fr1's is reported against 516.9 only: the k1 model cannot follow its d1 = −0.95, so a bias is expected there.)
- **H64 (the linearisation holds on these units).** As H59 with 4 random sets of each size 4–7 per unit, 40 draws each, iid noise at the unit's 13-view rms: the median over sets of sd(nonlinear Δf)/sd(g<sub>S</sub>·ε) lies in 0.8–1.25 for each n on both units. Refuted outside.
- **H65 (the twin reference is near nominal on new units).** R against its per-split twin threshold, S0 false alarms over k = 4–6 in 0.5–2.5% on each unit (milestone 4i: 1.44% and 0.37%). Refuted outside on either.
- **H66 (power carries over).** ±2% at k = 6, pooled over sign and unit, detected in ≥ 25% of splits (milestone 4i: 33.1%). Refuted below.
- **H67 (a real unit swap is caught).** S3 at k = 6 (old 7 views from the other unit, new 6 from this one; the old set's reference drawn from the other unit's twin, as in milestone 4i): R against its per-split twin threshold fires on ≥ 50% of splits on each unit. Refuted below on either.
- **Reported, unscored.** fr1's f against TUM's; both units' 13-view rms; J against its twin reference; one twin threshold per design; R against χ²<sub>1</sub> and against milestone 4h's independent-offset reference (18.45 at 7 + 6; unit-free); the iid model; ±1% and ±4%; k = 4 and 5; harmful adoptions; the twin's and the real scale ratios (milestone 4i's post hoc check).
- **Caveat set in advance.** fr1's lens is not described by k1 alone, so its residuals carry the unmodelled distortion; the resid noise model passes that pattern into the twin, which is the point of a per-unit twin, but a large model error could also bend H64. The 13 views of each unit come from one hand-held sequence of about 50 s, so they are not independent sessions; no interval is given.

At 7 + 4 the twin gives R 0.31 / 0.29 against the real 0.31 / 0.25. So the twin reproduces most of the jackknife's small-set overstatement on the left camera from the views' geometry alone, and the right camera's jackknife overstates its real spread about 1.6× more than its twin says. That is why the right camera sits below the bar: the same extra the independent-offset model missed in milestone 4f (its views do not add independent offsets).
- **Reading.** The jackknife's overstatement at 4–7 views is mostly geometry: a linear twin built from the unit's own solve predicts it, and a reference drawn from that twin is 11–13 at 7 + 6 views rather than the independent-offset model's 18.45. Against it the robust test fires on 1.4% and 0.4% of unchanged updates and catches a 2% focal change from 6 views in a third of splits, 1.8× milestone 4h's honest figure, with nothing borrowed and from solves the robot already ran. It does not reach the 43% of the borrowed threshold or the 54% of χ²<sub>1</sub>, whose 2–3% false alarms rested on cancelling errors. Two side findings change the paper's recommendation. First, with a geometry-aware reference the MAD buys nothing: the plain jackknife catches 34.7%. Second, one threshold per design does almost as well as one per split (30.8%); the per-split spread is real (H62) but barely tracks the real statistics, so most of the gain is the twin getting the design's tail right.
- **Caveat.** One rig, one board, 13 shared views; no interval is given. The twin is linear and is fed the unit's own 13 residual patterns, so error sources the residuals do not show (the right camera's extra spread) leave the test conservative there rather than lenient. That is the safe direction for a fleet, but it means the 0.37% is a property of this unit, not a guarantee.

## 2026-11-14 · Milestone 4i planned: a reference for each split from the unit's own linearised bundle adjustment

Written before any milestone-4i statistic was computed on the real splits. Milestone 4h gave the robust jackknife test (R) a reference with nothing borrowed, from a model in which each view adds an independent offset to f. On this rig it was conservative (0.2–0.5% false alarms) and weak (±2% at 6 new views caught in 19% of splits), because the model ignores the views' geometry: a small set near the critical configuration has delete-one solves that swing far (milestone 4f), which widens R in a way independent offsets do not. A reference that knows the geometry should fire near its nominal 1% and recover part of the power χ²<sub>1</sub> seemed to have (54%). Here the robot's own 13-view solve is the twin: for any view subset S its first-order change in f under a pixel perturbation ε is g<sub>S</sub>·ε, with g<sub>S</sub> = J<sub>S</sub>(J<sub>S</sub><sup>T</sup>J<sub>S</sub>)<sup>−1</sup>e<sub>f</sub> from the subset problem's Jacobian at the twin (`experiments/m4i_geomref/twin.py`). One matrix product then draws every set's f and its delete-one f's, and so z<sub>R</sub><sup>2</sup>, for each (old, new) split, and the split's own 99th percentile is its threshold. A unit can compute it from solves it already ran; nothing is borrowed from another unit.

- **Data.** Milestone 4g's splits, scenarios and real statistics, imported unchanged; the twin is the 13-view 5×5 solve of milestone 4f (re-run to keep its poses and points; it must reproduce the stored f to 10<sup>−8</sup>). No new real solve.
- **Noise models** (the statistic is scale-free, so only the pattern matters). *resid* (scored): each view gets the 13-view fit's residual pattern of a view drawn at random from the unit's own 13, with a random sign, so the within-view correlation milestone 6 found (columns, the bent board) is carried to every view. *iid*: independent N(0, 1) per pixel coordinate (reported). 10,000 draws per split, seed 20261114; designs 7 + 4, 7 + 5, 7 + 6; S3 (swap) uses the other camera's twin for the old set.
- **H59 (the linearisation holds for this solver).** Synthetic corners from each camera's twin plus iid noise at the real 13-view rms, solved by the same cold-start solver as the real runs (8 random sets of each size 3–7, 40 draws each, `check.py`): the median over sets of sd(nonlinear Δf)/sd(g<sub>S</sub>·ε) lies in 0.8–1.25 for each of n = 4, 5, 6, 7 on both cameras. Refuted if any falls outside. (3× the rms and the resid model reported.)
- **H60 (the per-split twin reference is near nominal on real views).** R against its per-split threshold (resid model), S0 false alarms over k = 4–6 in 0.5–2.5% on each camera. Refuted outside on either.
- **H61 (and recovers power).** ±2% at k = 6, pooled over sign and camera, detected in ≥ 25% of splits (milestone 4h's model reference: 18.6%). Refuted below.
- **H62 (geometry matters split by split).** Over the 1,716 splits at 7 + 6, the ratio of the 90th to the 10th percentile of the per-split threshold is ≥ 1.5 on both cameras. Refuted below on either (then one threshold per design would do).
- **Reported, unscored.** The iid model; a per-design twin reference (one 99th percentile pooled over the design's splits); J against its own twin references; ±1% and ±4%; k = 4 and 5; the swap; harmful adoptions; the Spearman correlation of each split's threshold with its real z<sub>R</sub><sup>2</sup> under S0.
- **Caveat set in advance.** The twin is linear and its noise comes from 13 residual patterns of one rig, so a unit whose errors are dominated by something the residuals do not show (a shared offset across views, for instance) would not be described by it. The 4f solves showed that the real spread of f is 2–6× the solver's σ, which a scale-free statistic absorbs only if the extra spread has the same pattern over views as the pixel noise. One rig, one board, 13 shared views: no interval is given.

## 2026-11-14 · Milestone 4h result: a reference derived from the independent-offset model keeps the robust jackknife test at 0.2–0.5% false alarms, but it catches a 2% focal change from 6 views in 19% of splits, not 54%; χ²<sub>1</sub> looked right only because two errors cancel

- **Runs.** No new solve: milestone 4g's splits, scenarios and statistics, imported unchanged. Model references by Monte Carlo, 10<sup>6</sup> unchanged updates per design and offset law (seed 20261114), 400,000 per set size for n<sub>old</sub> = k = 4..40 (`experiments/m4h_modelref/analyse.py`, 30 s). `tests/test_m4h.py` checked first that the vectorised variances equal milestone 4g's, that the plain jackknife's reference is a Welch-t one (99th percentile of z² 9.0–11.0 at 7 + 6), that a reference from one seed fires on 0.8–1.2% of another seed's draws, and that the statistic is scale-free.
- **References.** Gaussian offsets, 99th percentile of z<sub>R</sub>²: 25.84, 22.90 and 18.45 at 7 + 4, 7 + 5 and 7 + 6 views (9 + 4: 23.28), against χ²<sub>1</sub>'s 6.63; of z<sub>J</sub>²: 11.93, 10.44, 9.74. Student-t offsets make R's tail heavier still (7 + 6: 21.56 with t<sub>5</sub>, 31.71 with t<sub>3</sub>) and J's lighter (8.97, 8.39). Under the model, R against χ²<sub>1</sub> fires on 8.5%, 7.2% and 6.1% of unchanged updates at k = 4, 5, 6.
- **H56 held.** R against its Gaussian model reference, S0 over k = 4–6: 0.45% (left) and 0.20% (right) false alarms.
- **H57 held.** R against the model reference, ±2% at k = 6 pooled over sign and camera: 18.6% (per camera and sign 17.7–20.0%), against 53.8% for R against χ²<sub>1</sub>.
- **H58 held.** Under the Gaussian model with n<sub>old</sub> = k = n, P(z<sub>R</sub>² > 6.63) is 10.7% at n = 4, 3.8% at 10, 2.7% at 15 and 2.2% at 20; it reaches 1.6% only at 40. The model's 99th percentile is 12.0 at n = 10 and 8.84 at 20.
- **The cancellation.** On the real views, R against χ²<sub>1</sub> fired on 3.0%, 4.6% and 3.1% (left) and 1.1%, 1.4% and 0.2% (right) of unchanged updates at k = 4, 5, 6, below the model's 8.5%, 7.2% and 6.1%. The robust jackknife still overstates the real spread at these set sizes (milestone 4g, H53: real/predicted 0.88 and 0.48), and that overstatement offsets χ²<sub>1</sub>'s leniency. Milestone 4f found the overstatement fades as sets grow; the model's leniency fades more slowly (still 2.2% at 20 + 20), so on a unit with more views R against χ²<sub>1</sub> should drift towards the model's rates, not stay at 2–3%.
- **Unscored.** At k = 6 the model reference catches ±4% in 66.4–75.1% of splits (χ²<sub>1</sub>: 92.2–94.3%) and ±1% in 2.2–4.2%; at k = 5, ±2% in 6.0–10.8%. With the same calibrated basis the plain jackknife against its own model reference catches ±2% at k = 6 in only 8.6% pooled, so the robust scale does buy power (18.6% against 8.6%) once both tests have a derived reference. The t<sub>5</sub> and t<sub>3</sub> references give 14.8% and 7.8% at ±2%, k = 6. The unit swap is invisible to f alone under every reference (0.3% at k = 6). Harmful adoptions over all designs and scenarios: 0.63% (R, χ²<sub>1</sub>), 0.14% (R, model reference).
- **Reading.** Milestone 4g's 54% is not the power of a 1% test. It is the power of a test whose reference is about 6× too lenient in theory and happens to land near 2–3% on this rig because the jackknife is still too wide at 6 and 7 views. A reference derived from the independent-offset model is the only one in this programme that borrows nothing and rests on a stated assumption, and with it the f-alone test is quiet (0.2–0.5%) but catches a 2% change in only 19% of 6-view updates. Between that and the borrowed threshold's 43% (milestone 4g) lies the price of borrowing nothing at 6 new views. For a fleet the practical choice is (a) the model reference, conservative on this rig, which a robot can compute for any set sizes in about a second, or (b) χ²<sub>1</sub> as an approximate 2–5% test, with its real rate checked on each unit. The paper's recommendation 2 has to change accordingly.
- **Caveat.** One rig, one board, 13 shared views; no interval is given. The model ignores the near-critical behaviour of small sets that milestone 4f found, which is why it is conservative here; a unit whose views add independent offsets would sit at the nominal 1%.

## 2026-11-14 · Milestone 4h planned: a reference for the robust jackknife that comes from a model, not from χ²<sub>1</sub> or a sibling

Written before any milestone-4h statistic was computed on the real solves. Milestone 4g's best test (the robust, MAD-based jackknife R against χ²<sub>1</sub> at 1%) caught a 2% focal change in 54% of 6-view updates at 2.3% false alarms. But χ²<sub>1</sub> was never derived for R: a MAD of 6 or 7 delete-one values is itself noisy, so z<sub>R</sub>² should have a heavier tail than χ²<sub>1</sub>. A first Monte Carlo of the ideal case (each view adds an independent Gaussian offset to f, R and z<sub>R</sub>² formed exactly as `m4g_allsplits/analyse.py` does; 400,000 draws, seed 0; no real solve read) gives a 99th percentile of z<sub>R</sub>² of 18.4 at 7 + 6 views and 22.6 / 25.8 at 7 + 5 / 7 + 4, and says R against χ²<sub>1</sub> would fire on 6.0–8.5% of unchanged updates. If so, milestone 4g's 2.3% on the real views is two errors cancelling: a lenient reference against a robust jackknife that still overstates the real spread (milestone 4g, H53: real/predicted 0.88 and 0.48). Nothing in that cancellation would survive a unit with more views, where the overstatement fades (milestone 4f). Here the test gets a reference from the model itself, which a robot can compute for any set sizes with nothing borrowed, and the model's own verdict on when χ²<sub>1</sub> becomes safe.

- **Data.** Milestone 4f's stored 5×5 solves and milestone 4g's splits, scenarios and statistics, unchanged (`experiments/m4h_modelref/analyse.py` imports them). No new solve.
- **Model references.** For each design (n<sub>old</sub>, k), q<sub>M</sub> = the 99th percentile of z<sub>R</sub>² (and of z<sub>J</sub>² for J) when every view's offset is drawn iid from N(0, 1), by Monte Carlo (10<sup>6</sup> draws, fixed seed; the statistic is scale-free, so τ never enters). Sensitivity: the same with Student-t<sub>5</sub> and t<sub>3</sub> offsets.
- **H56 (the model reference is quiet on real views).** R against q<sub>M</sub> (Gaussian): S0 false alarms over k = 4–6 ≤ 1.0% on each camera. Refuted above on either.
- **H57 (and keeps some power).** R against q<sub>M</sub> (Gaussian): ±2% at k = 6, pooled over sign and camera, detected in 15–35% of splits (R against χ²<sub>1</sub>: 53.8%; J against χ²<sub>1</sub>: 17.7%). Refuted outside.
- **H58 (χ²<sub>1</sub> is not safe for R until large sets).** Under the Gaussian model, P(z<sub>R</sub>² > 6.63) with n<sub>old</sub> = k = n is above 2% for every n ≤ 20. Refuted if it falls to 2% or below at some n ≤ 20.
- **Reported, unscored.** J against its own q<sub>M</sub>; k = 4 and 5; ±1% and ±4%; the swap; the t<sub>5</sub> and t<sub>3</sub> references; the model's P(z<sub>R</sub>² > 6.63) and q<sub>M</sub> for n<sub>old</sub> = k from 4 to 40; the real false-alarm rate of R against χ²<sub>1</sub> per k next to the model's.
- **Caveat set in advance.** The model assumes independent per-view offsets, which milestone 4f found only roughly true (the right camera's A13 / DP ratio 1.2–1.7). The splits share 13 views, so no interval is given. H56 and H57 test one rig.

## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 4g result: on every disjoint split the borrowed threshold catches a 2% focal change in 43%, not 61%; a robust (MAD) jackknife against plain χ²<sub>1</sub> catches it in 54% with nothing borrowed and the same false-alarm rate

- **Runs.** No new solve: milestone 4f's 8,100 stored 5×5 solves per camera; every 4- to 9-view set had all its delete-one solves converge, so every split is usable. 37,752 splits per camera with an old set of 7 views (25,740 / 10,296 / 1,716 at k = 4 / 5 / 6) and 715 with an old set of 9 and a new set of 4, each scored under 10 scenarios (`experiments/m4g_allsplits/analyse.py`, under 5 s). `tests/test_m4g.py` checked the formulae and that the plain jackknife is quiet (< 5%) with a ratio near 1 on synthetic independent-offset data before the real run.
- **H52 refuted.** At k = 6, pooled over sign and camera, J against χ²<sub>1</sub> detects ±2% in 17.7% (inside 10–28%; 60 splits: 17.5%). But J against the borrowed threshold E<sub>1</sub> detects it in 43.3% (outside 51–71%; 60 splits: 61.3%), and E<sub>1</sub>'s left-camera false alarms over k = 4–6 are 4.4% (not ≥ 5%; 60 splits: 11.1%). Re-estimated on every split, the two cameras' S0 99th percentiles of z² under J are 4.62 (left) and 2.02 (right), against 3.56 and 1.03 from milestone 4d's 180 statistics each.
- **Post hoc, computed after H52 failed.** This script reproduces milestone 4d's figure exactly on milestone 4b's 60 splits with 4d's stored thresholds (61.25% of 240 decisions). With 4d's thresholds on every split, ±2% is caught in 57.8% and the left camera's false alarms are 10.4% (right 0.5%). So the splits themselves were representative: the drop from 61% to 43% comes from the threshold. A 99th percentile from 180 dependent statistics was too low, and power and false alarms fell together once it was estimated on every split. The borrowed test is also lopsided: ±2% is caught in 56–61% of splits on the left camera and 26–30% on the right.
- **H53 refuted.** S0, k = 6, real sd of f<sub>new</sub> − f<sub>old</sub> over the root mean predicted variance: R 0.88 (left) and 0.48 (right); J 0.57 and 0.31. The MAD brings the predicted spread from 1.7× to 1.1× the real one on the left camera and from 3.3× to 2.1× on the right, so the right camera's is still twice too wide. At k = 4 and 5 both are much too wide (R 0.25–0.31 at k = 4), because the new set's own delete-one solves sit near the critical set (milestone 4f).
- **H54 refuted, narrowly.** R against χ²<sub>1</sub>, S0 over k = 4–6: 3.4% (left) and 1.2% (right) false alarms; at k = 6 alone, 3.1% and 0.2%. Pooled over both cameras and k = 4–6 it is 2.3%, the same as the borrowed threshold's 2.4% and well above J's 0.3%.
- **H55 held.** R against χ²<sub>1</sub>, ±2% at k = 6 pooled: 53.8% (left 50.0–50.1%, right 54.3–61.0%), 10 points above the borrowed threshold and 3× the plain jackknife, with nothing borrowed from another unit.
- **Unscored.** At k = 6, R catches ±1% in 15.4–16.4% and ±4% in 92.7–94.1% (J: 1.5–2.5% and 69.4–75.4%; E<sub>1</sub>: 13.3–13.4% and 90.7–92.5%). At k = 5 R catches ±2% in 32.0% pooled (E<sub>1</sub> 27.7%, J 8.9%); at k = 4, 11.9–12.0%. An old set of 9 and a new set of 4 is no better than 7 + 4 (R ±2%: 13.8–15.0%): the new set's size limits the test. f alone cannot see the unit swap (0.67% in f): R fires on 1.6–2.6% of swap splits, as on unchanged ones. Harmful adoptions under milestone 4's rule: R 0.28–0.89% of decisions per design, E<sub>1</sub> 0.27–0.75%, J 0.06–0.20%.
- **Sensitivity.** Every 7 + 6 split uses all 13 views, so leaving a view out leaves no k = 6 split; the pre-registered leave-one-view-out range is undefined there (the gap was found on the first run). At k = 5 the ranges over the 13 left-out views are: R ±2% 23.6–37.3%, E<sub>1</sub> 18.8–35.3%, J 5.1–11.4%. False alarms (k = 4–6): R 2.6–4.0% (left), 0.9–1.7% (right); E<sub>1</sub> left 1.2–7.4%.
- **Reading.** Two corrections and one gain. First, the paper's headline power for the borrowed-threshold test (61%) was flattered by a noisy threshold from 180 statistics; on every split it is 43%, and the paper has to say so. Second, a robust jackknife is not an honest error bar (the right camera's is still 2× wide), so H53 fails. But against a plain χ²<sub>1</sub> it gives the best test this programme has found: a 2% focal change is caught in 54% of 6-view updates, at the same false-alarm rate as the borrowed threshold, evenly on both cameras, with nothing taken from another unit. For a robot, that removes the need for a sibling unit or a commissioning-time tail estimate at 6 new views. It does not remove the dependence on set size: at 4 new views every variant is weak (13% at most).
- **Caveat.** One rig, one board, 13 views; the splits are every combination of the same views, so they are far from independent and no interval is given. R's advantage was predicted (H55), but its 3.4% left-camera false alarms are just above the pre-registered bar, and a second unit could put it on either side.

## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 4g planned: every disjoint split, and a robust jackknife that needs nothing borrowed

Written before any milestone-4g statistic was computed. ## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 4f planned: does the jackknife stop overstating f's spread once the view set leaves the critical region?

Written while the milestone-4f solves ran, before any of them was analysed. Milestone 4e found, post hoc, that f's spread per view falls 2–5× between 3 and 5 views and then roughly follows 1/n. It guessed that the same effect is why the leave-one-view-out jackknife overstated the real set-to-set spread of 7-view sets (1.8× and 2.3× on 5×5 corners): deleting one view from a small set lands near the critical set, so a few delete-one solves swing far and inflate the variance. If the guess is right, the overstatement should shrink as the old set grows, and a robot that logs about 10 views could use the jackknife as it is. Here every subset of 3 to 12 of the 13 views is solved on each camera (8,086 per camera, `experiments/m4f_setsize/run.py`; milestones 4d and 4e's 5×5 solves reused), on 5×5 corners only (milestone 6b's unbiased localiser), with milestone 4b's solver, reference views and start.

- **Quantities** (per camera, relative to the all-view f<sub>13</sub>, converged solves only):
  - real sd, disjoint pairs (DP), n = 3..6: √(mean d²/2) over every unordered pair of disjoint n-view sets, d = (f<sub>A</sub> − f<sub>B</sub>)/f<sub>13</sub>. Model-free; needs 2n ≤ 13.
  - real sd, around all views (A13), any n: √(mean (f<sub>S</sub> − f<sub>13</sub>)² / (1 − n/13)) over every n-view set S. Exact if each view adds an independent offset (then var(f<sub>S</sub> − f<sub>13</sub>) = var(f<sub>S</sub>)(1 − n/13)); the only real reference above 6 views.
  - jackknife J(n) = √(mean v<sub>J</sub>) over n-view sets with all n delete-one solves converged, v<sub>J</sub> = (n−1)/n Σ(f<sub>i</sub> − f̄)²/f<sub>13</sub>² (milestone 4b's `jackknife_cov`, f entry).
  - reported R(n) = √(mean σ<sub>f</sub>²)/f<sub>13</sub> (the solver's own covariance).
  - ratio ρ<sub>J</sub>(n) = J(n)/real(n); reference DP for n ≤ 6 where stated, A13 otherwise.
- **H48 (overstatement falls with set size).** On each camera ρ<sub>J</sub>(4) > ρ<sub>J</sub>(6) against DP, and the Spearman correlation of ρ<sub>J</sub>(n) (A13) with n over n = 4..10 is ≤ −0.8. Refuted if either fails on either camera.
- **H49 (honest from about 8 views).** ρ<sub>J</sub>(n) (A13) lies in 0.67–1.5 for each of n = 8, 9, 10 on both cameras. Refuted if any falls outside.
- **H50 (the independent-offset model holds where it can be checked).** At n = 4, 5, 6, A13 over DP lies in 0.8–1.25 on both cameras. Refuted otherwise (and then H48's and H49's A13 reference is suspect above 6 views).
- **H51 (the solver's σ stays small at every size).** R(n)/real(n) (A13) ≤ 0.67 for every n = 4..10 on both cameras (milestone 1: about 2× small on 13 views). Refuted if any exceeds.
- **Reported, unscored.** n = 3, 11, 12; robust versions (1.4826 MAD for the real spread, median v<sub>J</sub> for the jackknife); convergence counts per n; a leave-one-view-out sensitivity (each ratio recomputed with every set holding view v dropped, for each of the 13 v; min–max reported).
- **Caveat set in advance.** All sets are drawn from the same 13 views and overlap heavily, so the per-n figures are far from independent and carry no honest interval beyond the leave-one-view-out range. The A13 reference at n ≥ 11 divides by 1 − n/13 ≤ 0.15 and is too noisy to score. 5×5 corners only: the other localisers carry a bias (milestone 6b) that adds a shared offset to every set.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 3 planned: is the 2× overconfidence real, and does a cluster-robust σ fix it?

Written before any milestone-3 experiment was run. The proposal's milestone 3 (natural features on a second data set) moves to 3b; this step answers the question milestone 2 left open, on the same 560 subsets, with no new data.

- **Why σ could be too small.** The Gauss–Newton σ assumes independent, equal-variance pixel residuals and a correct model. Corner-detector errors are correlated within an image (blur, lighting, the detector's shared sub-pixel window) and the k1-only lens leaves structured residuals. Both make the reported σ too small by a factor that differs between subsets, which a single inflation factor cannot follow (milestone 2's cross-camera κ).
- **Robust σ, two estimators, no fitted constants.** (a) The cluster-robust sandwich, clustered by view: var f = Σ_views ((J b)·r)² over each view's residuals, with b = (JᵀJ)⁻¹e_f, times the small-sample factor G/(G − 1). (b) The leave-one-view-out jackknife: re-solve the subset k times without one view each (cold start, same solver), var f = (k − 1)/k Σ (f₍ᵢ₎ − f̄)². Ablation: the sandwich clustered by corner (54 clusters) and unclustered (HC0).
- **H8 (the reference is not the cause).** Zhang's all-view f is itself 0.8 / 1.3 px from the target-free all-view f, so part of milestone 2's z could be reference mismatch. Self-consistency test against the target-free all-view solution, with the nested-estimator variance: z_n = (f_sub − f_all) / √(σ_sub² − σ_all²), random subsets of 4–12 views (subsets with σ_sub ≤ σ_all are counted and dropped). Predicted: rms z_n > 1.5 (the overconfidence is real). Refuted if ≤ 1.5.
- **H9 (robust σ is honest).** With σ from the jackknife, rms z against all-view Zhang over random 4–12-view subsets lies in [0.7, 1.4] on each camera, with no fitted factor. Scored separately for the view-clustered sandwich; predicted to hold for the jackknife and to fail (rms z > 1.4) for the sandwich at 4–6 views, where few clusters bias it down.
- **H10 (robust σ ranks better).** As a gate statistic for good (|Δf|/f ≤ 1%) against bad (> 2%) updates, pooled over all subsets of 4–12 views and both cameras, the jackknife σ/f has a higher ROC AUC than the reported σ/f. Refuted if it is equal or lower (paired bootstrap over subsets, 95% interval of the AUC difference).
- **H4, second test.** Accept iff σ_jack / f ≤ 0.5% (the same threshold as milestone 2); target 90/90. Also scored on the near-critical subsets alone. And the transfer test from milestone 1's next steps: the threshold on each σ that maximises min(accept good, reject bad) on one camera, scored on the other.
