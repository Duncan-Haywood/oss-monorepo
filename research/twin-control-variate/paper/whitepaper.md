# Twin control variate: certifying real-world policy cost with a few real rollouts and many twin rollouts

*Stylised: scalar plant `x′ = a x + b u + w`, `w ~ N(0,σ²)`, `a=0.9, b=1, σ²=1`, fixed policy `u=−0.4x` (closed-loop coefficient `α=0.5`), stage cost `c x²` with `c=q+rk²=1.016`, horizon `T=30`, rollout cost `J = c Σ_{t=1..T} x_t²`. The twin has gain `b̂` (same `a`, same policy) and can replay a fraction `λ` of the real disturbance variance: `w′ = √λ w + √(1−λ) z`. All numbers below are produced by `experiments/run.py` (`experiments/results.txt`).*

## Question
A digital twin is cheap to roll out and wrong. To certify a policy's real cost `μ = E J_real` you can (i) run `n` real rollouts, (ii) run `N` twin rollouts and trust them, or (iii) combine them. Which, in what proportion, and how much does the twin's error matter?

## Exact results
1. **Rollout cost is a Gaussian quadratic form** `J = w′Mw`, so `E J = σ² tr M`, `Var J = 2σ⁴ tr M²`, and for the twin `Cov(J, J′) = 2σ⁴ λ tr(MM′)` (Mathai–Provost 1992). Hence the exact correlation `ρ = λ tr(MM′)/√(tr M² tr M′²)`. Checked against 60,000 simulated rollouts (mean, variance, covariance within 2–8%, in the unit tests). Real cost: mean 40.19, sd 13.29.
2. **Model error is first order in the bias, second order in the correlation.** Twin gain `b̂ = 0.95, 0.90, 0.80, 0.60`: bias/mean `+2.7%, +5.6%, +12.4%, +30.9%`, but `1−ρ = 4.7·10⁻⁴, 1.9·10⁻³, 8.0·10⁻³, 3.4·10⁻²` at `λ=1`, growing about ×4 per doubled error. At `λ=0.8, 0.5, 0.2` the correlation is `0.80, 0.50, 0.20` almost regardless of `b̂` (`0.794, 0.496, 0.198` at `b̂=0.8`). With the same policy and structure, the twin's value as a control variate is capped by `λ`, the fraction of the real disturbance it can replay (logged wind, terrain, or human inputs) — a stylised statement, since here model error is one gain and the replay fraction is an assumed parameter.
3. **Optimal split and speed-up.** With `n` real and `N ≥ n` twin rollouts (the `n` paired on the same disturbance) and optimal coefficient `β = ρσ_r/σ_t`, `Var = σ_r²[(1−ρ²)/n + ρ²/N]`. At budget `B = n c_r + N c_t` and cost ratio `w = c_t/c_r` the optimum is `N/n = ρ/√((1−ρ²)w)` and `Var = σ_r² c_r/B·(√(1−ρ²)+ρ√w)²`; the speed-up over real-only is `1/(√(1−ρ²)+ρ√w)²` (1 if `ρ²/(1−ρ²) < w`), at most `1/(1−ρ²)` as `w→0`. Examples: `ρ=0.9`: 1.9×, 3.6×, 4.6× at `w=0.1, 0.01, 0.001` (limit 5.3×); `ρ=0.5`, `w=0.1`: 0.95 — the formula says do not use the twin, and a twin with `ρ=0.5` never beats 1.33×. This is multifidelity Monte Carlo (Peherstorfer et al. 2016) specialised to a twin whose correlation we can compute exactly.
4. **Simulation agrees.** 2,000 replicates each, `b̂=0.9`: control-variate variance (simulated / formula) at `n=20`, `N=40, 100, 400, 1000`: `λ=1`: `4.45/4.43, 1.76/1.79, 0.46/0.47, 0.215/0.210`; `λ=0.8`: `5.90/6.02, 4.19/4.33, 3.55/3.48, 3.27/3.31`. Estimator bias is `≤ 0.003` real-rollout sd. Estimating `β` from the `n=20` pairs inflates variance by 0–9% at `λ=0.8` (`3.79` vs `3.55` at `N=400`); negligible at `λ=1`.
5. **The twin alone is biased, and its intervals lie.** Twin-only estimate: MSE `δ² + σ_t²/N`; it beats `n` real rollouts iff `n < n̂ = σ_r²/δ²` (as `N→∞`). `n̂ = 1016, 154, 35, 7.2, 1.1` for `b̂ = 0.98, 0.95, 0.90, 0.80, 0.60`. At `n=20`, `b̂=0.9` twin-only MSE is `5.3` vs `8.83` for real-only (twin-only wins) but only `0.21` for the control variate at `N=1000` with `λ=1`, and `3.3` at `λ=0.8`. Nominal 95% intervals (normal quantile, `b̂=0.8`, `λ=0.8`): twin-only coverage `0.004, 0.000, 0.000, 0.000` for `(n,N)=(10,200),(20,400),(40,800),(20,1600)`; real-only `0.90, 0.92, 0.94`; control variate `0.89, 0.91, 0.94` — both under-cover at small `n` because of the normal quantile (a `t` quantile would help; not run).
6. **Worked budgets** for a 5% (95% half-width) certificate at real cost 1, twin cost 0.01 (real-only needs 168): `b̂=0.95, λ=0.95`: about 28 (6× cheaper; 22 real + 652 twin rollouts); `b̂=0.9, λ=0.8`: 78 (2.2×); `b̂=0.8, λ=0.8`: 79 (2.1×); `b̂=0.8, λ=0.3`: 163 (1.0×) — a twin that cannot replay the disturbance saves nothing however accurate its parameters.

## Limitations
Scalar linear-quadratic plant, one fixed policy, Gaussian disturbances, one parameter of model error; the twin replays a *fraction of variance* `λ`, which is an assumption standing in for state-estimation and log-replay pipelines, not derived from one. Twin model error that changes the policy's *ranking* (rather than one policy's level) is not treated (see `twin-transfer` for the validation blind spot). Costs `c_r, c_t` are per-rollout constants. Intervals use the normal quantile and under-cover at `n ≤ 20`. Nothing here is a robotics benchmark.

## Relevance
To certify a controller on a physical system with a simulator: log the real disturbances, replay them through the twin on the same rollouts, and split the budget by `N/n = ρ/√((1−ρ²)w)`. Never report twin-only intervals for a policy whose real cost matters, but do use twin-only estimates when real rollouts number fewer than `σ_r²/δ²`. Investing in disturbance replay (raising `λ`) pays more than tuning parameters once the twin is roughly right.
