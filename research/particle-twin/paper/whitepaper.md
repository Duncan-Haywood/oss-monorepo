# A Gaussian sensor twin and the particle count it under-sizes

## Question
A bootstrap particle filter needs enough particles that the effective sample size (ESS) stays healthy after each measurement. If the particle count is tuned in a digital twin whose sensor is the datasheet Gaussian, how wrong is it when the real sensor sometimes returns outliers, and is the average ESS the right statistic to tune on?

## Model
Prior `x ~ N(0, P)` per dimension; `N` prior particles `x_i`; measurement `y = x + v`; weights `w_i = exp(−(y−x_i)²/(2R))`; `ESS = (Σw)²/Σw²`. Real noise is a scale mixture (inlier `R` w.p. `1−ε`, outlier `κ²R` w.p. `ε`), so `y ~ N(0, P+R_c)` in component `c`. The twin is `ε = 0`. For large `N`, `ESS/N → E[w]²/E[w²] = ρ(y)`, and the Gaussian integrals give `E[w] = √(R/(P+R)) e^{−y²/(2(P+R))}`, `E[w²] = √(R/(2P+R)) e^{−y²/(2P+R)}`, hence `ρ(y) = c₀ e^{−a y²}`, `c₀ = √(R(2P+R))/(P+R)`, `a = P/((P+R)(2P+R))`. In `d` independent dimensions `ρ_d = Π_j ρ(y_j)`. Since `E e^{−a y²} = (1+2aS)^{−1/2}` for `y ~ N(0,S)`, the mean fraction is `Σ_c w_c [c₀ (1 + 2a(P+R_c))^{−1/2}]^d`. The tail is `P(ρ < θ) = Σ_c w_c · 2(1 − Φ(y_θ/√(P+R_c)))` with `y_θ² = ln(c₀/θ)/a`. Particle sizing for `ESS ≥ n` with probability `1−δ` is `n/ρ(t_δ)` with `t_δ` the `δ`-tail quantile of `|y|`.

## Results
(all from `experiments/results.txt`; `P = R = 1`, `ε = 0.05`, `κ = 10`, seed 7)
1. **The mean hides it.** Mean ESS fraction 0.6708 (twin) vs 0.6446 (real), ratio 0.961; for `d = 2, 4, 8, 16` the ratio is 0.952, 0.950, 0.950, 0.950 (at `d = 16`: 0.00168 vs 0.00160). A sizing based on the mean ESS is off by at most 5% at every dimension. Simulation (N = 2000, 1500 draws): 0.6671 and 0.6575. Both fall geometrically with dimension (twin `0.671^d`), an effect the outliers do not change.
2. **The tail does not.** `P(ESS/N < θ)` twin vs real: 7.5e-2 vs 1.1e-1 at `θ = 0.3`; 1.1e-2 vs 4.6e-2 at 0.1; 1.5e-3 vs 3.4e-2 at 0.03; 2.5e-4 vs 3.1e-2 at 0.01; 3.7e-5 vs 2.8e-2 at 0.003; 6.6e-6 vs 2.6e-2 at 0.001 (ratios 1.5, 4.2, 23, 120, 750, 3960). At `θ = 0.01`, versus the outlier scale `κ = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 30, 100` the probability is 2.5e-4, 1.3e-3, 5.3e-3, 1.6e-2, 3.1e-2, 4.3e-2, 4.8e-2: it rises to `ε = 0.05`, so every outlier collapses the filter once it is far enough out.
3. **Sizing.** `N` for `ESS ≥ 50` with failure `δ`: twin 100, 142, 208, 351, 527, 2133 for `δ = 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.001`; the real sensor's `|y|` quantile is 1.96, 2.63, 3.47, 8.46, 12.9, 23.4 (twin 1.81, 2.33, 2.77, 3.29, 3.64, 4.65) and the large-`N` formula gives 109, 182, 428, 8.7e6, 5.9e13, 2.1e41. Simulation at the twin-sized `N`: `N = 143` (`δ = 0.1`) fails 9.4% (twin) vs 13.3% (real); `N = 528` (`δ = 0.01`) fails 1.07% vs 4.37% (3000 steps each). Once `δ < ε` the large-`N` sizes are astronomically large and not meaningful as numbers (Item 4); the finding is that no feasible `N` meets the target.
4. **Where the large-`N` formula holds.** `N·ρ(y)` vs simulated ESS at `N = 2000` (200 runs): `y = 0`: 1732 vs 1732; 2: 889 vs 890; 4: 120 vs 122; 6: 4.3 vs 13.1; 8: 0.04 vs 4.2; 10: 0.00 vs 2.6; 12: 0.00 vs 2.1. The formula is accurate while `Nρ ≳ 100`, and beyond it ESS floors at about 2–4 (a handful of tail particles dominate), so the real tail is bounded below near ESS ≈ 2, not driven to 0.
5. **Estimator error.** Mean squared deviation of the particle posterior mean from the exact Kalman update `Ky` (3000 steps): `N = 50`: 0.0188 vs 0.848 (×45); `N = 200`: 0.00464 vs 0.679 (×146); `N = 1000`: 0.00097 vs 0.408 (×421). The twin's error falls like `1/N` (factor 19 from 50 to 1000), the real error only halves, because an outlier at `|y| ≫ √P` has no particle near it and the estimate stays within the cloud (|x| ≲ 3) while the Kalman update follows `y`.

## Limitations
Stylised, no sensor logs. One scalar update with prior particles drawn exactly from the prior and a correct `R` in the weights; independent dimensions (correlated states change the dimension scaling); a symmetric outlier component; no resampling over time, informative proposals, tempering or an outlier-robust likelihood, which would change the tail results; the large-`N` formula is an asymptotic and Item 4 shows where it fails. The "exact Kalman" benchmark in Item 5 is the optimal update only if the measurement model is the twin's; with outliers it is itself the wrong target, so Item 5 measures filter-versus-its-own-model error, not distance from the truth. The twin is a naive baseline and an outlier-aware twin would reproduce the real numbers by construction; the contribution is the size of the error from the omission, the closed form for the ESS fraction and its tail, and the warning that the mean ESS is the wrong statistic to size on.

## Next steps
Fit `(ε, κ)` from innovation logs and size on a tail quantile instead of the mean; heavy-tailed likelihoods (Student-t weights) and measurement-dependent proposals; resampling threshold dynamics over a trajectory; connect to `gate-twin` (a gate sidesteps the collapse by discarding the outlier) and to a lidar/radar-based localisation twin.
