# Budget twin: for a fixed budget, how much should go to real data and how much to simulation length when fitting a digital twin?

Pure Python, no dependencies. A shared lab instrument is an M/M/1 queue; its twin takes both rates from `n` real interarrival and `n` real service times and is then simulated for `m` jobs. About the real mean wait the twin's relative variance is `a(ρ)/n + b(ρ)/m` with `a=(1+(2−ρ)²)/(1−ρ)²` (input error, from `input-twin`) and `b=(2+5ρ−4ρ²+ρ³)/(ρ(1−ρ)²)` (Whitt's M/M/1 simulation variance; matched to simulation, 30.4 vs 29.0 at ρ=0.5 and 61.2 vs 61.6 at ρ=0.7). Their ratio `b/a` is 2.23 / 2.03 / 2.01 at ρ=0.5 / 0.8 / 0.9 and tends to 2: one real (arrival, service) pair carries the information of about two simulated jobs, so real data is the scarcer input unless a simulated job costs under about a third of a real pair. With a real pair costing 2 units and a job `κ`, the optimal real-data share of the budget is `√(2a)/(√(2a)+√(κb))` (0.81 at κ=0.05, 0.90 at κ=0.01, 0.57 at κ=0.5, nearly the same at ρ=0.5 and 0.8). In a full fit-then-simulate pipeline at B=1000, κ=0.05, ρ=0.5 the sd of ln of the answer is 0.399 / 0.244 / 0.202 / 0.224 for real shares 0.2 / 0.5 / 0.81 / 0.95 (predicted 0.363 / 0.234 / 0.199 / 0.237). The main practical failure is reporting a simulation-only confidence interval: it is a 95% interval for the *twin*, and its coverage of the real system falls as the run gets longer (n=200, ρ=0.5: 0.79 / 0.69 / 0.47 / 0.23 / 0.17 at m=200…50 000, normal-theory prediction 0.90 / 0.72 / 0.43 / 0.23 / 0.15), while adding the input variance restores 0.92–0.95 for m ≥ 1000 (0.85–0.86 for 200-job runs). See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

**Builds on.** The digital-twin simulation-fidelity and field-robot direction of the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>) and the lab-workcell twins of the HIRO Group (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The method is classical: input-uncertainty analysis (Cheng & Holland 1997; Barton 2012), the M/M/1 simulation variance of Whitt (1989), and the two-source variance decomposition of Ankenman & Nelson (2012). This is the run-length versus real-data trade-off left open in `input-twin`; see also `warmup-twin`, `autocorr-twin` and `queue-twin`.

Stylised: M/M/1 with exponential data from a simulated "real" system (no lab data); delta-method variances (ln-scale sd is used for the pipeline check, ratio RMSE is reported separately); the twin is started in its fitted steady state, so warm-up is excluded; linear costs; one utilisation pair for the split table and three configurations for coverage; 500 fits per cell (coverage standard error about 2 points). Short runs (200 jobs, 20 per replication) under-cover even the twin's own interval, so the coverage prediction is optimistic there. MIT.
