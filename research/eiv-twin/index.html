What does a twin get wrong when its gain is fitted from a noisy logged input, and does more data fix it?

research/eiv-twin

A twin gain fitted from a noisy logged input is attenuated by exactly the reliability ratio λ (0.800 vs 0.802 simulated), more data makes the twin's own 95% interval worse (covers the true gain 53%→0% for n=20→200) with no residual warning, a feedforward controller overshoots by exactly 1/λ, and known-noise or instrument repairs cost variance and fail at small n.

5 tests

What it found

Regressing the plant output on a logged input that carries sensor noise attenuates the fitted gain by the reliability ratio λ = σu2/(σu2+σv2) exactly (0.800 vs 0.802 simulated at λ=0.8). It is bias, not variance, so more data makes it worse: the twin's own 95% interval covers the true gain 53% / 15% / 0% at n=20 / 50 / 200 while covering the attenuated value 94–97%, and the twin's fit residuals are exactly uncorrelated with its regressor, so nothing warns. A feedforward controller built on it overshoots the real plant by exactly 1/λ (1.25× at λ=0.8, 2× at 0.5). A known-noise correction or a second independent measurement as instrument removes the bias (RMSE 0.20 to 0.02–0.05 at n=200–1000), but a 20% misjudged noise variance leaves a ±5% bias and at n=20 with λ=0.5 the repairs are far worse than the biased fit. Stylised: scalar Gaussian plant, simulated “real” system.

More in Digital twins & sim-to-real

  • What does a twin with no actuator deadband promise a proportional loop, and what does the real one do?An actuator deadband the twin lacks: real loop rests at exactly d/k while the twin goes to 0, tolerance ε needs k ≥ d/ε, a fitted linear gain is 2Q(d/s) (excitation-dependent, small-signal gain 0), and bisection compensation leaves exact (d−d̂)/k or a (d̂−d)/(2−k) chatter (8 probes: 0.784 → 0.0005).
  • Does measurement noise, or dither, hide an actuator deadband the twin lacks?Follow-up to deadband-twin: measurement noise flips the linear twin's error from 27× optimistic (s=0.01) to conservative (0.37× at s=0.05), dither helps only without noise, and mild under-compensation beats the exact width under noise (single-seed simulations).
  • How large a manoeuvre can a twin without an actuator rate limit be trusted for?A twin without an actuator rate limit is exact only for x₀ ≤ r/k; beyond it the real loop overshoots (0.90 of x₀ at x₀/r=10⁴) and settles in O(amplitude) steps, with an empirical overshoot threshold 2n(2n−1) for k=1/n.
  • Does a twin with a rigid drivetrain miss the hunting cycle caused by gear backlash?A rigid-drivetrain twin says the PI loop converges but gear backlash makes the real loop hunt at 1.3–2.9× the gap (exactly ∝ h); describing-function prediction 7–10% high, and bistable starts can hide it.
  • How wrong is the stopping distance of a twin that models friction as purely viscous?A viscous-only twin never stops (6.5–17.7 s to reach 1e-3…1e-9 vs 1.95 s real) and its fitted gain b + c·E[v]/E[v²] depends on the logged speed range; fitted on [1,3] it overestimates stopping distance ×1.8–17 below and underestimates above v*=7.3, no single viscous gain beats 0.87 worst error over [0.05,3], affine fit recovers exactly.
  • Does a twin with correct sliding friction still miss stick-slip hunting from static friction?A twin with the right sliding friction says the PID loop converges, and with no stiction it does; a breakaway excess Fs−Fc makes the real loop stick-slip hunt at ≈0.5–0.9 ΔF/kp (independent of Fc), and short runs can mistake a long stick for convergence.
