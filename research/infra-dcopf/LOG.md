## 2026-11-12 · Voltage setpoints as decision variables (plan, written before the run; milestone 3b of `research/grid-linopf`)
- Question: milestone 3 found the repair loses most of its successes on API and SAD once the AC power flows run at the case file's 1.0 pu schedule instead of the AC optimum's (P-clean 2 and 2 of 11, against 6 and 7), and a reactive stage after the dispatch (F+R) makes only 16 of 34 cases fully clean at +0.8 to +4.3 % mean cost. Does a successive linearisation that moves the generator voltage setpoints together with P, from the case-file start, close that gap without the oracle schedule?
- Method (`src/infra_dcopf/vlin.py`, `experiments/run_vlin.py`). Variables: P of every live unit and one voltage setpoint V per bus with a live unit (units at a bus share it; the slack bus included). Finite differences of PYPOWER's AC power flow at the current point, one MW per non-slack unit (as before) and 0.002 pu per voltage setpoint (stepped inward from the nearer bound), give the sensitivities of the slack's P, both ends' |S| of every rated branch, every limited branch's angle difference, every non-generator bus's voltage magnitude and every live unit's Q. QP on the case's costs: linearised balance, flow rows <= rating, angle rows in [ANGMIN, ANGMAX], bus-voltage rows in [VMIN, VMAX], Q rows in [QMIN, QMAX], P in unit limits, V in its bus's [VMIN, VMAX]. Step box: P as in C (none in round 1, then 20 % of range, halved on a rejected step); V box 0.03 pu from round 1, halved with P's. If the hard QP has no solution, an elastic QP: every inequality row gets an l1 slack at 1e4 per unit (MVA, degrees, MVAr; voltage rows scaled by 100 so 0.01 pu costs as 1 MVA), retried at 1e3. Merit: C-ang's (worst overload fraction + slack excursion / baseMVA + worst angle excess in rad) plus 10 x worst bus-voltage excess in pu plus total Q excess / baseMVA. Up to 10 rounds; stop when fully clean. Start: the plain DC-MP dispatch at V = case-file setpoints (milestone 3's start rule). Checks at the method's own V: the same thresholds as milestone 3 (thermal 1 ppm, slack 1e-3 MW, angle 1e-3 deg, V 1e-4 pu, Q 1e-3 MVAr).
- Arms: J (the method above); J+R (milestone 3's reactive stage applied to J's best dispatch when J is not fully clean). Baselines read from `results_vsched.json` (deterministic, same start): F (P-only at 1.0 pu), F+R, and O (P-only at the oracle schedule).
- Predictions. H1: J is fully clean on at least 20 of 34 cases (F+R: 16; refuted below 20). H2: on API and SAD together J is fully clean on at least 10 of 22 (F+R: 9; O's P-only oracle run: 12 fully clean). H3: where J is fully clean its AC cost is within 0.5 % of the AC optimum on average on each set (F+R's means: +1.17, +0.82, +4.28 %); refuted if any set's mean exceeds 0.5 %. H4: J+R adds at most 2 fully clean cases over J (the voltage moves are already in J). H5: J's run time is under 4x milestone 3's per case summed over all 36 (one extra power flow per voltage setpoint per round). No rule changes after this is written; any change is reported as a new variant.

- H3 refuted. Its cost is not near the optimum: mean +1.17 % (TYP), +0.82 % (API), +4.28 % (SAD), worst +10.06 % (SAD case14). P was fixed at a schedule 0.06 pu below the optimum's, where thermal and angle limits bind harder (more current, larger angles for the same transfer), so F buys feasibility with redispatch the voltage stage cannot undo.

PIPS failing is still not proof that no voltage schedule makes F's dispatch feasible.
- Variant tried after the results and dropped: alternating repair and reactive stage. It never takes a second pass, because the stage either returns a fully clean point or fails; an alternation needs a stage that returns a schedule even when it cannot meet every limit (soft V/Q/thermal limits). Not in the committed script.
- Reading: every earlier "clean at the AC optimum's cost" result held voltages at the oracle schedule, and on congested and angle-limited cases that schedule carried most of the result. A repair a market engine can run has to move voltage setpoints with P: next, generator voltage setpoints as decision variables in the linearisation (sensitivities to VG from the same Jacobian), or a schedule a market would have (the previous interval's, or the TYP case's optimum applied to API and SAD).
