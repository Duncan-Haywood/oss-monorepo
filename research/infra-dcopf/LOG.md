# Research log

## 2026-11-12 · Voltage schedule not from the AC optimum (plan, written before the run)
- Question (milestone 3 of `research/grid-linopf`): every result so far ran the AC power flows at the AC optimum's generator voltage schedule, an oracle a market engine without an AC OPF does not have. Does the successive linearisation still repair the DC dispatch when the schedule is the case file's own (PGLib ships VG = 1.0 pu on every generator; the AC optima sit up to 0.06 pu higher on the cases checked), and does a reactive stage after the dispatch clear the voltage and Q limits?
- Method (`experiments/run_vsched.py`, `src/infra_dcopf/vsched.py`): all 36 PGLib v23.07 TYP, API and SAD cases. Start: plain DC-MP (with angle limits, without them if infeasible; the SAD rule). C-ang (the SAD variant: angle rows, same step rules, no tuning) at O = AC optimum's schedule and at F = case-file schedule. F+R: F's final dispatch, then an AC OPF over V and Q with every non-slack unit's P pinned (+-0.01 MW), slack free. DC+R: the same reactive stage on the plain DC start. "P-clean" = thermal, slack and angle clean; "fully clean" adds no bus out of V band and no unit outside Q limits.
- Predictions. H1: F's P-clean count is within 2 of O's on each set (refuted if lower by 3 or more on any). H2: at F, at least half of the P-clean dispatches break a V or Q limit. H3: F+R is fully clean on at least 75 % of the cases where F is P-clean, at a cost within 0.1 % of the AC optimum. H4: DC+R is fully clean on fewer than half as many cases as F+R on API and SAD (refuted if it reaches half). H5: F's P-clean dispatches cost more than the AC optimum (higher losses at 1.0 pu), mean premium above 0.1 % on API. No rule changes after this is written; any change is reported as a new variant.

## 2026-11-12 · Held-out SAD test: outcome (H1 held, H2 half, H3 and H4 refuted narrowly; angle-aware repair clears 7 of 11)
- H1 held: PYPOWER's AC OPF matches PGLib's published SAD objective within 0.005 % on 11 of 12; case300 SAD did not converge (published 565,700 $/h), so 11 cases have an AC optimum.
- H2 half held: MATPOWER's DC OPF with the angle limits is infeasible on 8 of 11 (prediction at least 6). PGLib's published PowerModels DC is infeasible on 7 of the same 11 (plus case300), and not on the same ones: both on case14, 118, 179, 200, 240; only MATPOWER's on case24, 39, 73; only PowerModels' on case30, 57. The susceptance convention (`results_pglib.txt`) decides feasibility at these angle limits. But only 1 of the 3 feasible DC dispatches breaks an AC angle limit (case162, 0.68 deg over), not at least half; case30 and case57 break a thermal or slack limit instead.
- H3 refuted on its first half: the frozen, angle-blind C ends thermal-and-slack clean on 7 of 11 (prediction at least 9) and angle-clean on 3 (prediction at most 3, held). Where it ignores angles it lands 6.5 to 17.6 % *below* the AC optimum's cost with up to 16 angle limits broken by up to 4.9 deg (case24, 39, 73, 118): on SAD the angle limits, not the thermal ones, are what the optimum pays for.
- H4 refuted narrowly: C with linearised angle rows (C-ang; same step rules, no tuning on SAD) is clean on thermal, slack and angle limits on 7 of 11 (prediction at least 8): case14, 24, 30, 39, 57, 162, 200, at rounds 1 to 5, each within 0.001 % of the AC optimum's cost. The four it misses: case73 ends with one branch over its rating by under 1 ppm of loading (prints 100.00 %) and no angle violation; case118 with 3 angle limits over by at most 0.019 deg; case240 with 8 branches at 100.01 %, as on API; case179 because the angle-free DC start has no AC power flow solution (the start rule chosen in the plan; a start from the AC case file's dispatch would differ).
- Reading: the API-tuned rules transfer. The held-out clean rate (7 of 11) equals the tuning-set rate (7 of 11), and the misses have the same shape (within 0.01 % of a limit after 10 rounds), which points at the last-round strictness (next step 1's 99.9 % margin) rather than at overfitting. A constraint class the method did not model (angles) is the real failure of the frozen method, and adding it as two linear rows per branch fixes it.
- Not checked: voltages and Q (C-ang leaves 1 generator outside Q limits on case39 and 4 to 13 on case73, 118, 240, with voltages at the AC optimum's schedule); one AC solver; no repeat on perturbed loads.

## 2026-11-12 · Held-out test on PGLib's SAD cases (plan, written before the run)
- Question (next step 1 of the linearisation entry): C's step rules were tuned on the 11 API cases. Do they hold on a set they never saw? PGLib v23.07's SAD ("small angle difference") cases, fetched today (`data/pglib/*__sad.m`), are the TYP cases with the same loads and ratings but every branch's voltage-angle difference limited to 4.7 to 16.2 degrees (30 in TYP). PGLib's own DC OPF is infeasible on 9 of these 12, so the angle limits bind. PYPOWER's AC and DC OPFs both enforce them (`makeAang`).
- What changes: the AC check gains an angle test (each limited branch's from-to angle difference within [ANGMIN, ANGMAX] + 1e-3 deg). C is run twice. (C-frozen) exactly as committed (thermal and slack rows only), so it is blind to the new constraint. (C-ang) the same step rules, box, elastic penalty and 10 rounds, plus two linear rows per limited branch from finite-difference angle sensitivities taken in the same power flows (d(theta_f - theta_t)/dP_k), and the merit adds the worst angle excess in radians. Start: the plain DC-MP dispatch if its DC OPF (with angle limits) is feasible, else the DC-MP dispatch with angle limits removed (decided now, before seeing which cases need it).
- Predictions. H1: PYPOWER's AC OPF matches the published SAD objective within 0.01 % on at least 11 of 12. H2: plain DC-MP with angle limits is infeasible on at least 6 of 12, and where it is feasible its dispatch breaks an AC angle limit on at least half. H3: C-frozen ends thermal-and-slack clean on at least 9 of the cases with an AC optimum but angle-clean on at most 3 (refuted if it is angle-clean on more: the angle limits would then not be the binding issue). H4: C-ang ends fully clean (thermal, slack, angle) on at least 8 of the cases with an AC optimum, at a cost within 0.1 % of it where clean (refuted below 8). No rule of C is changed after this is written; any change is reported as a new variant.

## 2026-11-12 · AC-informed linearisation: outcome (all four predictions refuted as stated; successive linearisation clears 7 of 11)
- Correction to the plan: 11 API cases have an AC optimum (only case179 does not), not 10; the counts below are of 11.
- H1 refuted: the linearisation at the AC optimum (A, oracle) gives a clean dispatch on 7 of 11, not 9. Where it is clean its AC cost equals the optimum (within 0.001 %). Where not, the QP moves to a cheaper point the first-order model calls feasible and the AC flow puts 0.15 to 3.9 % back over (case24, 57, 300), or a branch whose flow redispatch barely moves makes the QP infeasible (case73).
- H2 refuted: one linearisation at the plain DC dispatch's AC flow (B) is clean on 0 of 11, worse than `corrected_dc`'s 1.
- H4 refuted: B's failures split: residual overloads under 103 % on 5 (worst 100.05 to 100.66 %), a first-order model with no feasible point on 2 (case24, 73), and large curvature on 4 (108 to 167 %, all cases where the plain DC slack sits 50 to 800 MW outside its limits, so the point is far from the answer).
- H3 refuted narrowly: successive linearisation (C) is clean on 7 of 11 within 10 rounds (case14, 24, 30, 39, 118, 162, 240; at rounds 2 to 7), not 9, each at the AC optimum's cost (within 0.001 %; case24 at -0.47 % with 4 buses out of voltage band). The other four end within 0.012 % of their ratings and 0.8 MW of slack limits (case57, 73, 200, 300); case179 (no AC optimum, case-file voltages) ends at 100.1 % and 4.7 MW. Against plain DC (0 of 11) and `corrected_dc` (1 of 11), this is the first method here that repairs most of the congested cases.
- Method changes made after seeing the first runs, on the same cases (no held-out set; a reviewer should weigh C's count with that in mind): (1) a 1 ppm tolerance on rows the AC optimum leaves at 1 + 1e-9 (made A infeasible on case73); (2) C's first version used a fixed step box that excluded every feasible point from the plain DC start (3 of 12 clean); it now takes round 1 without a box, uses an elastic l1-penalised QP when the model has no feasible point (penalty 1e4 $/MVA-h; 1e5 stalled PIPS), and halves the box on a step that does not lower the infeasibility (zig-zags of 2-cycles on case57, 179, 200, 300 without it). B and A are the planned methods unchanged.
- Cost: 1 to 13 s per case on one CPU, n_gen power flows per round (finite differences). Voltages are held at the AC optimum's schedule throughout, as in the AC check, so this tests the active-power side only.
- Next: the same on PGLib's SAD cases as a held-out set; a strict-feasibility margin (e.g. 99.9 % ratings in the last round); analytic sensitivities from the power-flow Jacobian in place of finite differences; a voltage schedule that does not come from the AC optimum.

## 2026-11-12 · AC-informed linearisation on the congested PGLib cases (plan, written before the run)
- Question (next step 1): plain DC overloads branches on all 11 API cases, and derating or the iterated loss-and-rating correction (`corrected_dc`) clears 1 of 11. Does a linearisation of the AC power flow taken at an AC operating point do better? Sensitivities by finite differences of PYPOWER's AC power flow (1 MW per generator, slack compensating, generator voltages at the AC optimum's schedule, the same convention as the AC check): marginal loss factors for the balance row and apparent-flow sensitivities |S| at both ends of every rated branch, with the flows at the point as offsets. A QP on the cases' own costs (PIPS QP), then the same AC check as `run_pglib.py`.
- Three points: (A) the AC optimum (an oracle bound, not deployable); (B) the AC power flow of the plain DC dispatch (one shot, no AC OPF); (C) successive linearisation from (B) with a trust region, up to 10 rounds, stopping when the AC check is clean.
- Predictions: H1, (A) clears every overload on at least 9 of the 10 API cases with an AC optimum, at an AC cost within 0.5 % of it (refuted if fewer than 9, or cost above 0.5 % on any it clears). H2, (B) in one shot clears at least 5 of 11 API cases, beating `corrected_dc`'s 1 (refuted below 5). H3, (C) clears at least 9 of 11 within 10 rounds at an AC cost within 1 % of the AC optimum where one exists (refuted below 9). H4, where (B) fails it is because curvature leaves residual overloads under 103 %, not because the QP is infeasible.

## 2026-11-11 · Line outages: do penalty factors survive a topology change?
- Question (next step 2): factors taken at the pre-outage AC optimum, applied to the DC OPF of the network after a single line trip (the DC model knows the new topology; only the factors are stale). case118 at its own load (k=1.0), the 20 most-loaded lines whose trip keeps every bus supplied, plus 20 others drawn at random (seed 0).
- Prediction (written before running): stale factors beat plain DC on at least 80 % of outages; their gap to the post-outage oracle grows with the tripped line's pre-outage flow; on at least one outage the stale factors do worse than plain DC.
- Outcome: first two held, third refuted. Stale factors beat plain DC on 80 of 80 trips (loads 1.0 and 1.1); mean 0.409 vs plain 1.671 vs oracle 0.367 % on the 20 most-loaded trips at load 1.0, and within 0.015 point of oracle on random trips. Gap to oracle rises with tripped flow (Spearman 0.69; 0.59 at 1.1); worst trip (25-29) keeps 88 % of the oracle gain. Max factor change tracks the gap (Spearman 0.74), a candidate refresh trigger.
- Caveat: case118 ratings do not bind, so no trip congests the network. Next: outages on PGLib API cases.
