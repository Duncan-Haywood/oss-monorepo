# Replication attacks on Shapley and leave-one-out payments for decentralised training contributions

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and exact experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Decentralised training networks must pay contributors of data, gradients or compute in proportion to what they add. Data-valuation payments such as the Shapley value and leave-one-out (LOO) are the standard candidates. We study the simplest attack on them: a contributor submits `k` copies of one contribution. In a Bayesian mean-estimation game with exact computation we show (i) Shapley pays the replicator more for each extra copy even when copies carry no information (correlation 1), rising to, and never beyond, the contributor's stand-alone value, a gain of `(n+2)/2` over honesty against `n` unit-precision others; (ii) an evaluator that treats copies as independent lets the replicator's share of the pot tend to 1; (iii) LOO pays true copies exactly zero, so it resists replication, but it distributes only `1/(n+1)` of the pot to an honest contributor when others are symmetric and so is unusable as a payment rule by itself; (iv) with a per-copy cost `c`, the attack stops paying exactly when `c` exceeds the marginal value of the first extra copy; (v) collapsing suspected duplicates fixes replication but underpays genuinely independent look-alikes (by 45% in our example). Nothing here needs sampling; every number is computed exactly and cross-checked.

## 1. Model
A Bayesian estimator of a Gaussian mean with prior precision `t₀=1` receives submissions. The value of a coalition with total effective precision `P` is the risk reduction `g(P) = 1/t₀ − 1/(t₀+P)`, concave in `P`. There are `n` honest contributors of precision `τ`, and one replicator holding precision `a` submitted as `k` copies. Effective precision of `c` copies with pairwise correlation `ρ` is `ac/(1+(c−1)ρ)`: `ρ=0` is what a naive evaluator that adds precisions assumes; `ρ=1` means a copy is worthless. A coalition's value is `g(a·c/(1+(c−1)ρ) + τ j)` with `c` copies and `j` honest contributors present.

**Exact Shapley.** All `k` copies are symmetric, so a copy's Shapley value is a sum over compositions `(c,j)` of other copies and honest players, weighted `C(k−1,c)C(n,j)/(N·C(N−1,c+j))`, `N=k+n`. This is exact and polynomial in `k,n`. It agrees to `10⁻¹²` with full permutation enumeration on `k=n=3` for `ρ∈{0,0.4,1}` and with the identity `φ = ∫₀¹ E[marginal | each other player present w.p. t] dt` to `10⁻⁶`. Efficiency (payments sum to `g` of the grand coalition) is tested.

## 2. Shapley rewards replication even when copies are worthless
Setting `ρ=1`, `n=9`, `a=τ=1` (E1), the replicator's total payment for `k = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 50, 200` copies is 0.091, 0.145, 0.185, 0.242, 0.321, 0.447, 0.486: strictly increasing (tested for `k<30`), a 5.3× gain at 200 copies, though the copies add nothing to the estimator.

*Why.* In a uniformly random arrival order the replicator is credited at the first arrival of any of its copies. More copies makes the first arrival earlier, when fewer others are present and `g` is steeper. As `k→∞` the first copy arrives first with probability →1, so the group's payment tends to the stand-alone value `g(a)`, an upper bound for `ρ=1` (0.5 here; 0.486 at `k=200`). With unit precisions the honest payment is `g(n+1)/(n+1) = 1/(n+2)`, so the maximum gain from replication is exactly `(n+2)/2`: 1.5, 2.5, 5.5, 16, 51 for `n = 1, 3, 9, 30, 100` (E2, matches the computed ratios). More honest participants make honest payments smaller while the replicator's ceiling stays put, so replication pays relatively more in larger networks.

*Cost.* If each extra copy costs `c`, the optimal `k` in this example is 200 (cap) at `c=0`, 48 at `c=0.001`, 18 at 0.005, 11 at 0.01, 6 at 0.02 and 1 from `c≥0.054` (E4). The threshold `0.054` is the marginal value of the first extra copy. Copying data or gradients is essentially free, so the deterrent from cost is negligible.

## 3. If the evaluator treats copies as independent
With `ρ=0` (a naive evaluator that adds precisions) the group's payment climbs to 0.500 at 10 copies and 0.952 at 200, and its share of the pot from 10% to 95.7% (E1). A replicator with a modest fleet of copies can take most of the reward. The gap between rows `ρ=0` and `ρ=1` in E1 is the price of not detecting duplicates; intermediate correlation assumptions interpolate (E6: a replicator with 10 copies is paid 0.419, 0.375, 0.344, 0.330 under assumed `ρ̂ = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9`, against 0.321 at the true `ρ=1`). Note even perfect knowledge of `ρ` leaves the 0.321 vs 0.091 gap of §2.

## 4. Leave-one-out resists but under-pays
LOO pays each copy `v(all) − v(all minus it)`. For true copies (`ρ=1`, `k≥2`) each is redundant, so the total is exactly 0 (E3): replication is not only unprofitable but ruinous, which also punishes honest submissions that legitimately duplicate others. For a single honest contributor LOO pays 0.0091 against Shapley's 0.0909: with concave value, LOO distributes only 10% of the pot at `k=1`, so it cannot be the payment rule unless the remainder is redistributed by another rule. For `ρ=0`, LOO's total to the replicator rises to 0.026 at 10 copies and then falls (0.005 at 200): it is not monotone, but the replicator is paid 2.9× more than honest at 10 copies.

## 5. The dedup fix and its cost
The robust approach is to pay by Shapley over *clusters* of near-duplicate submissions, so that the replicator's total equals its `k=1` payment. That requires the evaluator to detect duplication reliably, and honest contributors with genuinely independent data of the same kind get collapsed: in E5 two independent honest contributors would be paid together 0.091 instead of 0.167 (45% underpaid). The trade-off (false merge against missed copy) is the same as in `sybil-stake` for identity, and is one more instance of the impossibility of telling copies from independent agreement without side information. For gradient contributions, exact bitwise duplicates are cheap to detect; correlated but non-identical submissions (small perturbations of a copy) are not, and the `ρ̂<1` rows of E6 are what an evaluator pays for that leniency.

## 6. Limitations and connections
The value function is one stylised concave form with a single replicator; heterogeneous precisions and multiple replicators would change constants but not the arrival-order mechanism, which depends only on concavity. The correlation model is a single dial. We take the evaluator's `ρ` as given rather than estimating it. Payments are computed on the estimator's risk reduction, not a held-out metric (see `holdout-market` for what that adds). Related: `sybil-stake` (identity splitting in stake-weighted pools), `verifier-complements` (submodularity and payments), `robust-aggregation`. This is not a claim about any deployed network. Contributions: an exact group-payoff computation, the `(n+2)/2` stand-alone-value ceiling, the LOO comparison, and the cost and detection trade-offs, with executable checks.
