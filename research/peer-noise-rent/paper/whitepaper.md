# Noise rent in multi-task peer prediction

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Peer prediction pays verifiers without ground truth by rewarding agreement with a peer, corrected by a penalty for chance agreement. Its incentive analysis is done at infinite tasks: the Dasgupta–Ghosh payment has mean `2Cov(x_i,x_j)=2p(1−p)g_ig_j`. A network has finitely many tasks, and payments are random. We give the exact finite-sample law of the practical plug-in payment, which is twice the biased sample covariance, so `Var=(2(n−1)/n)²[(μ₂₂−c²)/n+(σ_x²σ_y²+c²)/(n(n−1))]` (matched to full enumeration to 1e-6). It is `√2` less noisy than a scheme that draws fresh penalty tasks (`√2` exactly at the null), and it pays a constant reporter exactly zero. Under limited liability (pay `max(S,0)`), any variance is valuable: a verifier who does no work earns `≈σ/√(2π)` per round (0.020 vs honest 0.208 at n=100; the fresh-penalty version gives 0.028). A deductible `τ` fixes it, with closed form via the normal tail; cutting the rent to 0.001 costs an honest verifier 40% of its pay. Detection and ranking sample sizes are closed form and reach 95% in simulation.

## 1. Setup
State `Y~Bern(p)`; verifier `k` reports `x_k∈{0,1}` with `P(x=1|Y=1)=a_k`, `P(x=0|Y=0)=b_k`, informativeness `g_k=a_k+b_k−1`, conditionally independent across verifiers. For an agreement payment with a chance-agreement penalty the mean is `Pr[agree] − (f_if_j+(1−f_i)(1−f_j)) = 2Cov(x_i,x_j) = 2p(1−p)g_ig_j` (checked to 1e-12). Two estimators from n tasks:
* **fresh:** bonus-task agreement minus agreement of `x_i` and `x_j` on independent penalty tasks. Its variance is `(a(1−a)+π(1−π))/n` with `a=Pr[agree]`, `π=f_if_j+(1−f_i)(1−f_j)`.
* **plug-in:** `S=mean agree − (f̂_if̂_j+(1−f̂_i)(1−f̂_j))`. Since agreement is `1−x_i−x_j+2x_ix_j`, `S=2·(1/n)Σ(x_i−x̄_i)(x_j−x̄_j)=2(n−1)/n · s_ij`. That identity makes the exact law available.

## 2. Exact variance
For i.i.d. pairs the unbiased sample covariance has `Var(s_ij)=(μ₂₂−c²)/n+(σ_x²σ_y²+c²)/(n(n−1))`, `μ₂₂=E[(x_i−μ_i)²(x_j−μ_j)²]`, `c=Cov`. For binary reports the joint table has four cells, so every term is exact. E1: enumerating all `4ⁿ` samples gives the same variance to six digits at n=2,4,6 (e.g. 0.029747), and 40 000-run simulations agree with the plug-in and fresh formulas at n=20, 100 (0.00207 vs 0.00204; 0.00475 vs 0.00476). E2 (symmetric verifiers, n=200): the fresh scheme is 1.39–1.64× noisier at identical mean pay. At the null with `p=½` the ratio is exactly `√2` (fresh `1/2n` against plug-in `1/4n`), because the plug-in estimator does not pay for sampling error in the penalty.

## 3. Noise rent
Suppose pay is floored at zero (limited liability, or slashing that cannot be negative). A verifier who exerts no effort still gets `E[S⁺]`. With `S≈N(0,σ²)` this is `σ/√(2π)`, and it is *larger for noisier schemes*. E3 (n=100, peer `g=0.6`): a fair-coin reporter gets 0.0280 under fresh penalties (normal formula 0.0282) and 0.0199 under plug-in, against 0.208 for an honest `g_i=0.7` verifier. The plug-in scheme pays a *constant* reporter exactly 0 (its sample covariance vanishes identically; tested), so the best lazy strategy against it is randomising, not copying a default. A deductible `τ` (pay `(S−τ)⁺`) makes the rent `σ[φ(τ/σ)−(τ/σ)(1−Φ(τ/σ))]` for a zero-mean lazy score, so `τ` solving rent `=ε` is one line of bisection. Simulated rent matches: ε=0.005, 0.001, 0.0002 need `τ=0.045, 0.083, 0.113`, and the honest verifier's pay falls from 0.208 to 0.163, 0.125, 0.095 (simulated 0.163, 0.126, 0.096). Removing 75–99% of the lazy rent takes 22–54% of the honest pay: the noise cost is not free, and it is shared, so effort must be worth more than the deductible-adjusted pay.

## 4. Sample sizes
Detection: to pass a verifier of informativeness `g` against a lazy coin at `α=β=0.05`, `n=((z_ασ₀+z_βσ₁)/μ)²` with per-task SDs from the exact formula. With `p=½`, peer `g=0.6`: n=329, 115, 56 for `g=0.3,0.5,0.7`, and simulation gives false-pass 0.049, 0.050, 0.046 and power 0.950, 0.944, 0.941 (E4). Ranking: `S₁−S₂=2·biased cov(x_j, x₁−x₂)`, so the same table with `u=x₁−x₂` gives its law. E5: with verifier 1 at `g=0.7` and peer `g=0.6`, ordering 95% correct needs n=870 against `g₂=0.6` (score gap 0.03), 242 at `g₂=0.5`; 72 at `g₂=0.3` (simulated 0.949, 0.949, 0.947). The cost scales as `1/(2p(1−p)g_j Δg)²`: a weak peer inflates everyone's requirement quadratically.

## 5. Limits
Two-signal binary model, conditionally independent verifiers, i.i.d. tasks, known `p` and peer quality when sizing (in practice they are estimated; the normal approximation is used only for rent and deductible, where n=100 already matches to 1%). The plug-in payment is slightly biased (`(n−1)/n`), which the calculation carries. Related: `peer-prediction-effort` (infinite-sample equilibria and gold checks), `focal-laziness` (shared lazy defaults), `audit-weighted-scoring`.
