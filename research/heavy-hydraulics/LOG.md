- Why: 6a found that on the measured excavator open-centre reaches load-sensing pump energy at 108 L/min for 11% more time, and its Next (a) asks whether that time costs more fuel than the pump energy saves. Folder 75 has no fuel or engine signal, so instead of pricing the time with one borrowed rate, this step computes the **break-even rate** f_t*: the fuel rate of the rest of the machine (engine friction, fan, accessories over the extra time) above which no fixed pump beats load-sensing. A builder compares one number from their own engine against it.
- Data: huggingface.co (folder 75) and zenodo.org were both refused by the proxy at 18:20 UTC (403 to CONNECT), so this step reads no raw data. It works from the committed `measured_excavator_pump_size_results.txt` and `measured_excavator_flow_results.txt` only, which is possible because of an identity found while planning (below).
- Identity (derived before the run): under 6a's slowing model a fixed pump runs flat out over the stretched time of every moving sample, so its pumped volume is q_p T_OC,moving, and r(q_p) = q_p T_OC,moving / V_d. Hence, with phi = still time over moving time and mu = mean moving demand, the time ratio is exactly tau(q_p) = (phi + r(q_p) mu / q_p) / (phi + r(219) mu / 219): the time cost is a function of the volume ratio alone. One row was checked by hand before this entry (87.6 L/min: 1.2320 against 6a's 1.232); no other row was computed.
- Between 6a's grid points r(q_p) is bracketed without data: E[max(q_p, q)] is convex in q_p with slope P(q < q_p), so 6a's "short %" column gives each tangent (lower bound) and the chords give the upper bound; below 87.6 L/min the chord runs to r = 1 at q_p = 0. f_t* falls as r rises at fixed q_p, so the two bounds bracket it.
- Break-even: per hour of load-sensing work, open-centre minus load-sensing fuel = b (m* - m) Q_LS / (600 eta) + f_t (tau - 1), with m* = 0.78 (r - 1) p (5u/5z), Q_LS the mean demand over load-sensing time (L/min), b the marginal fuel per shaft kWh and eta the pump efficiency. f_t*(q_p) = b (m - m*) Q_LS / (600 eta (tau - 1)) where m* < m; f_t,max is its maximum over q_p. Central: m 20 bar, p 130 bar, b 0.2332 L/kWh and eta 0.818 (both the loader's, 5n/5x, **borrowed, not measured on this machine**). Swept: m 14, 20, 24.9, 25; p 100, 130, 174; b 0.20, 0.2332, 0.26; eta 0.75, 0.818, 0.95. Reference rates, all borrowed from the loader (5x): low 1.48, rest of machine 5.15, still 5.99 L/h.
- A hand estimate from 6a's table before any code (87.6 L/min, central) gave about 2.4 L/h; disclosed because it informs C2.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 5o: what counts as motion on the measured loader? A noise floor from the data and an open-centre floor (predictions registered before the first run)
- Why: 5n's open-centre counterfactual moved most with `q_min`, the flow below which a section counted as still, which I chose after seeing the first run (0.5 / 2 / 5 L/min). It is a property of the trace, not of the valve: the authors' flows come from piston velocity (5 Hz low-pass), so a held boom carries small spurious flows. This step measures the noise floor instead of choosing it, checks it against a motion test built from piston position alone, and adds the physical floor of any open-centre valve.
- Why no orifice closing law: for any open-centre spool, flow can enter a chamber at p_i only if pump pressure exceeds p_i, and the fixed pump delivers its whole flow at that pressure (the bypass passes the rest). So while a loaded section moves, the open-centre pump power is at least `Q_fix x p_load`, whatever the bypass closing law (3a to 3d); the law only sets the extra drop, which 5n swept as `dp_spool`. With the PVG 32 bypass closing inside the dead band (3d), the 5n step model is the large-k limit. So `dp_spool = 0` is a closing-law-free floor for open-centre while moving.
- Method (`experiments/measured_motion.py`): stationary windows are 1 s windows in which a cylinder's position range is under 0.5 mm (mean speed under 0.5 mm/s, about 0.3 L/min at the lift cylinders' 100.5 cm2 piston area); the noise floor of a section is the 99th percentile of its |P-port flow| in those windows. Position-based motion: a section moves when its piston travels more than d_min over a centred 0.5 s window (d_min 0.5 / 1 / 2 mm).
- **Predictions (written before any of these statistics was computed):**
  - Q1: the noise floor (p99 in stationary windows) of both lift and tilt sections lies in 0.5 to 5 L/min, the range 5n swept. Prior 60%. Refuted if either is outside.

## 2026-11-01 · Milestone 3p: the surplus fraction f of 3n is not free for a fixed pump (an identity, checked numerically)
- Why: 3n and 3o end "source f". Before hunting a bypass-flow curve, I asked whether f is a property of the valve at all. A fixed pump delivers Q at the pressure p it sees, and the surplus Q minus the actuator flows can only leave through the bypass at that same p, which must exceed the heaviest load (otherwise that actuator stalls). So pump power is Q p, the surplus is dropped at p, and the 3m charge (f = 1) is exact for a fixed pump, not the pessimistic end.
- Check (`experiments/fixed_pump_identity.py`, seeded, 2,000 random parallel phases with 1 to 3 actuators at 30 to 220 bar, solved by flow continuity with orifice spools and bypass): with the bypass sized for the surplus, the solved pressure equals p_max + drop to 6e-14 bar and the solved shaft energy equals `cycle_energy` open-centre to 5e-16 relative error. Widening the bypass by 1% to 2x lowers the pressure in all 8,000 cases, and in none of them do the actuators still get all their demanded flow. Pinned by `tests/test_circuits.py::test_fixed_pump_surplus_is_dropped_at_pump_pressure`. 15 tests pass.
- Consequence: the f < 1 rows of 3n (break-even 31.7 to 18.8 bar at 1.2 x) are not a fixed-pump open-centre circuit with a better valve. They describe a pump that delivers less than Q when demand is low (unloading, pressure-compensated or variable), which is the load-sensing family. For the fixed-pump circuit this programme calls open-centre, 3i, 3m (36.1 bar at 1.0 x, 44.5 bar at 1.2 x) stand with f = 1, and no bypass-flow curve can change them. The margin that the 14 to 30 bar range is read against is therefore decided by pump size and rated spool drop, both of which are sourced or swept.
- Limits: steady state, orifice laws, parallel circuit, no relief or pump leakage (a real pump's volumetric loss lowers delivered Q slightly at high p, which would make the charge a little lower); series-parallel or tandem centres and operator feathering are not modelled.
- Next: the paper's threats-to-validity wording for f; milestone 5 (the electric rung).

## 2026-11-01 · Milestone 3o: the "compensator" next step is already inside dp_ls (a model-scope check, no new sweep)
- Why: 3c to 3n each end with "add the compensator". Reading `cycle_energy`: load-sensing charges `q_dem * (p_max + dp_ls)`, so a pre-compensated section's throttling of a lighter actuator down from pump pressure to its own is already in the shaft energy, and the pump margin `dp_ls` is the whole drop from pump to the highest load (spool plus compensator in series, as in a PVG-type section). - Limits: this removes the compensator as an energy term only. What is still unmodelled is compensator dynamics, flow-sharing behaviour of a compensator under pump saturation beyond `flow_share_ls.py`, and a measured bypass curve for f.
