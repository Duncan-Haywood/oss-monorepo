## 2026-10-29 · Milestone 5a: how much of a cycle's pump-shaft energy can boom-lowering recovery return, and does it beat the circuit choice? (planned before running)
- Why: milestone 5 (energy recovery) is the last unbuilt rung before the paper, and 4 found fuel does not repay load-sensing. Is recovery a bigger lever than the circuit? `experiments/regen_bound.py` (stdlib, seconds). Per force-model cycle (`physics_cycle.one`, 20,000 cycles) the recoverable energy is E = (m_arm + m_load) g dh, with m_load the model's bucket-plus-soil mass, m_arm (boom, stick, bucket, effective at the boom cylinder) swept 150 to 600 kg and dh swept 0.5 to 1.2 m. **Neither m_arm nor dh is sourced** (component masses are not published for this class; swept, not chosen). Round-trip efficiency eta_rt is swept 0.3 to 0.92; the literature anchors found by search only (abstracts, not opened): 67.6% potential-energy reuse (dual-power boom, electric excavator, theory and experiment), 86% (digital pump with accumulator, simulation), up to 92% (1.6 t electro-hydraulic, simulation), 44.6% whole-cycle saving (50 t hybrid prototype, experiment). The saving is capped by the lift phase's shaft energy under each circuit (what the recovered energy can displace). Reported as a share of the cycle's open-centre and load-sensing shaft energy (no idle time added, so an upper bound on the share over working hours), next to the LS/OC gap.
- Consistency check I also run: the model's lift phase draws pressure x flow x time of useful work, which should be about m g dh for a real lift; the ratio of the model's lift useful energy to m g dh says whether the force model's lift phase is physical.
- **Predictions (before running):** (1) at the central point (m_arm 300 kg, dh 0.8 m, eta_rt 0.7) the recovered share of cycle shaft energy is under 5% at the median, in both circuits. (2) Even at the upper corner (600 kg, 1.2 m, 0.92) the median share stays under 10%, so recovery tops out well below the 21% LS-over-OC gap at the measured load factor (2f). (3) The model's lift useful energy exceeds m g dh by at least 3x in the median cycle, i.e. the force model overstates lift work, which means its lift pressures are not purely gravitational. Refuted: (1) 5% or more; (2) 10% or more; (3) ratio under 3.
- Limits known in advance: potential energy of the arm only (no swing-brake or cylinder regen); m_arm and dh are swept; the recovery is not simulated dynamically (no accumulator sizing, no 3 s harvest-window limit that a review notes); cycles carry no idle time.

### Outcome of 5a (`experiments/regen_bound.py`, about 10 s; `regen_bound_results.txt`; 5,000 force-model cycles, sourced margins)
- Median recovered share of cycle pump-shaft energy (open-centre / load-sensing), capped by lift-phase shaft energy: central point (300 kg, 0.8 m, eta_rt 0.7) **3.3% / 3.2%**; lowest corner (150 kg, 0.5 m, 0.3) 0.5%; upper corner (600 kg, 1.2 m, 0.92) **10.0% / 10.3%**. The two circuits differ by under 0.3 points, so the choice of circuit barely changes what recovery can return.
- **(1) held** (3.3% and 3.2%, under 5%). **(2) refuted, narrowly:** the upper corner is 10.01% (open-centre) and 10.26% (load-sensing), not under 10%. **(3) held:** the force model's lift useful work is a median 5.6 times m_load g (0.8 m) (5 to 95%: 2.9 to 10.5), so its lift phase is not gravity alone: it also carries a pressure floor (`idle_u`) and the arm's own weight, which I did not separate.
- Reading: with unsourced arm mass and drop height swept over a 4x and 2.4x range, boom-lowering recovery is worth roughly 1 to 10% of cycle shaft energy at working time, 3% centrally, even at a 92% round trip. That is the same order as the load-sensing margin effect, and unlike load-sensing it costs an accumulator or an electric machine that this programme has not priced. I did NOT compare it with the 21% idle-time LS-over-OC gap of 2f: this run adds no idle time, and on a no-idle cycle LS/OC is 1.039 (LS loses, as 2d found), so the planned comparison to 21% was not like-for-like. Idle time lowers every share here further.
- Limits: m_arm, dh, eta_rt swept not sourced (the recovery efficiencies are from search abstracts, not opened papers); no dynamics, accumulator sizing or harvest-window limit; the cap uses the lift phase only; lift phase work is not physical (3), so the cap may be generous.
- Next: separate the lift phase into gravity and friction (so the cap is physical), price an accumulator or e-machine (milestone 4 style), and source a boom/arm mass.

## 2026-10-29 · Milestone 2g: does 2f's idle-time ranking survive a physical idle model for each circuit?
- Not pre-registered: written and run in one step (`experiments/idle_models.py`, `idle_models_results.txt`). Check on the script: its "OC bleed, LS standby 0" cells reproduce 2f's table exactly (0.942, 0.820, 0.786, 0.662), so only the idle charge changed.
- Method: 2f charged idle to open-centre as full pump flow at (p + 6 bar) and to load-sensing with no standby flow. Here open-centre idles through its centre bypass at a neutral pressure p_n (40, 20 or 8 bar) and load-sensing pays its idle demand at (p + 25 bar) plus a standby flow q_sb (0, 2 or 6 L/min) at 25 bar. p_n, q_sb and the idle demand are assumed (swept, no measured source).
- Result (LS/OC median; share of cycles with LS > OC), at the measured load factor 0.346: p_n 40 bar, q_sb 0: 0.643, 3.3%; p_n 20: 0.819, 10.4%; p_n 8: 0.981, 43.9%; p_n 8 with q_sb 6: 1.035, 61.1%. At 0.375 the same corner gives 0.991 (46.9%) to 1.035 (61.2%). At 0.25, p_n 8 gives 0.939 to 1.031.
- Reading: 2f's "load-sensing wins by about 21% at the measured load factor" is not robust. It depends on how much pressure the open-centre bypass holds at neutral. A bypass at 8 bar or lower with a 2 to 6 L/min load-sensing standby flow erases the advantage (ratio 0.98 to 1.04); a 40 bar neutral bypass doubles it (0.64). The 2f figure was a mid-range point, not a lower bound as I wrote there: the two biases I named pushed in opposite directions and the neutral pressure moves the answer more than the standby flow does. The structural conclusion stands: the ranking is set by duty cycle and idle architecture, and H1 stays duty-cycle-dependent.
- Limits: p_n and q_sb are assumptions; no measured idle power of either circuit; electric-excavator load factor from a different machine; the idle demand is the same as 2f's.
- Next: a source for open-centre neutral pressure drop and load-sensing standby flow (a pump or valve datasheet), then the parts-cost step (milestone 4).

## 2026-10-29 · Milestone 2f: does 2d's "load-sensing loses" survive idle time matched to a measured load factor?
- Not pre-registered: I wrote the script and ran it in the same step, so there is no prediction to score. What I can say honestly is that 2e's third prediction (a measured trace "would push LS further against open-centre") was **reasoned in the wrong direction**: low-load time is where a fixed-flow open-centre pump bleeds its whole output, so idle time helps load-sensing.
- Method (`experiments/idle_sensitivity.py`, `idle_sensitivity_results.txt`): append one idle phase (2 to 8 L/min at 10 to 25 bar, assumed) to each 2c force-model cycle, long enough that the cycle's load factor (mean useful power / peak phase power) hits a target; margins as 2d (6 bar spool, 25 bar LS, eta 0.883, dp_m swept); 5,000 cycles per row. Targets include the measured 0.346 (full trace) and 0.375 (80 s digging trace) from 2e.
- Result (LS/OC median; share of cycles with LS > OC): no idle (2d) 1.039; 61.1%. LF 0.5: 0.942; 34.9%. **LF 0.375: 0.820; 12.5%. LF 0.346: 0.786; 8.4%.** LF 0.25: 0.662; 0.6%. LF 0.15: 0.508; 0%. Metered/LS stays 0.64 to 0.66 throughout.
- Reading: 2d's headline (LS loses to open-centre at a 25 bar margin) holds only for cycles with no idle time. At the load factor of the one measured trace available, load-sensing is a median 21 to 21.4% below open-centre in energy and loses in 8 to 12% of cycles. The break-even load factor is near 0.5 to 0.55. So the ranking depends on duty cycle, which is what the programme's question asks; H1 is neither confirmed nor refuted as a structural claim, it is duty-cycle-dependent.
- Limits: the load factor comes from electrical input power of a larger electric machine (2e), not hydraulic power; idle demand and pressure are assumed; open-centre idle is the full-flow bleed (pessimistic for OC, and a real machine may unload the pump), while LS idle ignores standby flow (optimistic for LS). Both biases favour LS, so the 0.79 to 0.82 is probably a lower bound on LS/OC. Still no measured hydraulic trace, so H4 is not tested.
- Next: model OC unloading and LS standby flow (the two biases above), then the parts-cost step (milestone 4).
