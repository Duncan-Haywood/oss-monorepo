## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 2c: do load-correlated pressures from a force model change the rankings? (planned before running)
- Plan: replace random pressures with a force model (`experiments/physics_cycle.py`): dig pressure from Reece's cutting-force form against a breakout force, lift from bucket-plus-soil weight on a lever, swing from inertia and friction, six phases, 45 L/min pump, 180 bar relief, 20,000 cycles. Soil gamma/c/N factors, breakout force, lever ratios and masses are **assumed typical ranges from memory, not opened sources**, so this is a sensitivity test, not an energy figure.
- **Prediction (before running):** ordering holds in over 99%; LS/OC median 0.45 to 0.65 (tied pressures make phases mostly low-pressure, as 2a/2b); Met/LS median 0.75 to 0.9.

### Outcome (`experiments/physics_cycle_results.txt`)
- Ordering OC >= LS >= metered held in only **83.3%** of cycles: prediction failed. Every one of the 3,345 violations has dp_ls > dp_spool (the only way LS can exceed OC in this model), and they sit at slightly higher demand (mean 0.76 of pump flow against 0.70). My reading, not tested: with most phases at low pressure (swing, lower), a 14 to 30 bar load-sensing margin is a large share of the phase pressure, so OC with a 5 to 20 bar spool drop wins when demand is high.
- LS/OC median **0.842** (5 to 95%: 0.63 to 1.12), well above the predicted band and above 2a (0.44) and 2b (0.67). Met/LS median 0.697 (0.58 to 0.83), below 0.85 in 98.1%, below the predicted band.
- Reading: the earlier headline that load-sensing saves roughly a third to a half of open-centre shaft energy depends on pressures being random and high. When most of the cycle is low-pressure (as a swing-dominated cycle is), load-sensing's saving shrinks to about 16% in the median and reverses in 17% of cycles, while independent metering's saving over LS grows. H1 as a structural claim is refuted for realistic margins; H2 is not tested here.
- Limits: every force-model parameter is assumed; the phase structure, speeds (0.4 to 1.0 of pump flow, uniform) and the fraction of relief each phase uses are my choices; no dynamics. H4 still needs a measured pressure trace.
- Next: source the pressures (Williamson 2010 or a public excavator pressure dataset) and replace the assumed ranges; until then milestone 2 is not done.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 2b: the rankings on the measured phase times and pump curve of Juza and Hermanek (2023) (planned before running)
- Source opened this time (the hosts resolve now; the PDF came through WebFetch): Juza and Hermanek, *Research in Agricultural Engineering* 69(1):18-27, doi:10.17221/77/2021-RAE, CC BY-NC 4.0. Machine: UDS 214 telescopic excavator on a Tatra 815 chassis, John Deere 4045 94 kW diesel, Bosch Rexroth A8VO107 double pump, 0.63 m3 bucket, **a much larger machine than the mini-excavator of milestones 1 to 2a**. Measured there and used here: Table 4, the mean cycle of 20 measured cycles in Class II soil: fill 4.01 s, lift 2.21, swing loaded 6.21, dump 4.16, swing back 5.76, lower 2.65 (25.0 s); Table 1, one pump half's measured flow against pressure (158, 156, 153.3, 147, 131.5, 111.7, 77.7, 71.3 L/min at 0, 5, 10, 13, 16, 20, 27, 30 MPa, 1556 to 1600 rpm); Table 2, the open-centre control block's pressure loss 600 kPa (6 bar) and the half-pump's overall efficiency 88.34%.
- Not in the paper: per-phase actuator pressures and flows (it measures component losses, not a pressure trace), so those stay random, as in 2a, now on a 300 bar scale (the 30 MPa test pressure). Which actuators work in which phase is my reading of the phase names (fill: 2 or 3 actuators; lift, dump, lower: 1; swings: 1), not sourced.
- Plan: 20,000 cycles (5 seeds x 4,000) with the six sourced phase times; open-centre pump flow from the interpolated measured Q(p) at the highest load pressure plus 6 bar (this removes the constant-flow assumption of milestones 1 and 2a); demand per phase uniform 0.2 to 1.0 of Q(p_max) shared among the actuators; pump efficiency 0.8834 fixed; dp_ls 14 to 30, dp_m 3 to 10 swept as before (unsourced). Consistency check on my transcription: Q at 27 MPa must equal the paper's Q1 = 1.295 dm3/s.
- **Prediction (before running):** ordering OC >= LS >= metered in 100%. LS/OC median between 0.40 and 0.65, since OC now delivers less surplus at high pressure but the demand fraction is the same range as in 2a. Metered/LS median between 0.70 and 0.90.

### Outcome (`experiments/measured_cycle.py`, seeded, seconds; `experiments/measured_cycle_results.txt`)
- Transcription check passed: Q at 27 MPa is 77.7 L/min = 1.295 dm3/s, the paper's Q1.
- Random pressures per actuator (20,000 cycles): load-sensing/open-centre median **0.667** (5 to 95%: 0.48 to 0.86), **above my predicted 0.40 to 0.65**. Metered/load-sensing median 0.867 (0.78 to 0.93), inside the predicted band; below 0.85 in 36.7% of cycles. Equal pressure within a phase: LS/OC 0.669, metered/LS 0.903 (0.83 to 0.95), below 0.85 in 10.5%.
- **The ordering is not structural once open-centre uses the measured curve: 99.86% (random) and 99.83% (equal pressure), not 100%, so my prediction failed.** The 0.14 to 0.17% of cycles are ones whose demand is close to the whole pump flow at that pressure: load-sensing pays a 14 to 30 bar margin, the open-centre block only the sourced 6 bar, so open-centre wins there. 2a's 100% came from a constant pump flow and an unsourced 5 to 20 bar spool drop.
- Reading: with the measured pump curve (flow falls from 158 to 71 L/min as pressure rises) and a measured 6 bar block loss, open-centre wastes less than in the mini-excavator milestones, so load-sensing's saving shrinks from a median 44% (2a) to 33%. The metered-over-LS saving is similar (13% against 28% in 2a under random pressures; 10% against 13% at equal pressure). This is on one large machine's pump and measured cycle times; the pressures and demands are still random, so it is a sensitivity result, not an excavator energy figure.
- Limits: the paper reports no pressure trace or per-phase flows, so the headline ratios depend on my random demand (0.2 to 1.0 of pump flow) and on dp_ls and dp_m, which are unsourced; actuators per phase are my reading of the phase names; the 94 kW diesel and two pump halves are not modelled (one half only). Real open-centre spools bleed off less than full flow at partial lever, which this still ignores.
- Next: a pressure trace for a measured cycle (H4 still needs one: Williamson 2010's thesis or a public excavator dataset); milestone 2 is closer but not done.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 2a: do the rankings survive a random cycle bounded by a sourced machine envelope? (planned before running)
- Why not the measured cycle yet: the two sources that would give one (Juza and Hermanek, *Research in Agricultural Engineering* 69(1):18-27, 2023, doi:10.17221/77/2021-RAE; Kubota KX018-4 dealer sheet) are on hosts this session cannot resolve (`rae.agriculturejournals.cz`, `kubota.com.au`), so the cycle itself stays unsourced. What a search result summary did give, not opened: KX018-4 pump 17.3 x 2 + 10.4 + 6.2 L/min, a 180 bar figure from a parts site (unverified as the relief setting).
- Plan: replace the one hand-written cycle with 4,000 random cycles per seed x 5 seeds: 4 phases, 1 to 3 simultaneous actuators, each actuator's flow drawn up to its section capacity (17.3, 17.3, 10.4 L/min), total flow under the 45 L/min pump, pressures uniform on 20 to 180 bar, phase times 2 to 6 s. Margins at the sourced-or-swept values of milestone 1 (dp_ls 14 to 30 bar, dp_spool 5 to 20, dp_m 3 to 10, eta 0.75 to 0.92) drawn uniformly. This tests whether the milestone 1 medians are an artefact of one cycle. It is still not a measured cycle.
- **Prediction (before running):** ordering open-centre >= load-sensing >= metered holds in 100% of cycles (structural). Median LS/OC will fall below milestone 1's 0.47 because open-centre runs at 45 L/min against a mean demand well under that. Metered/LS median between 0.75 and 0.9. H3 check per case: at equal actuator pressures, metered/LS stays under 0.85 in over 95% of cycles.

### Outcome (`experiments/envelope.py`, seeded, a few seconds; `experiments/envelope_results.txt`)
- 20,000 random cycles (5 seeds x 4,000): the ordering open-centre >= load-sensing >= metered held in 100% (structural, as predicted).
- Load-sensing/open-centre median 0.442 (5 to 95%: 0.26 to 0.64), per-seed medians 0.440 to 0.444: below milestone 1's 0.47, as predicted; the headline is not an artefact of the one hand-written cycle.
- Metered/load-sensing median 0.717 with random per-actuator pressures (5 to 95%: 0.57 to 0.85), outside my predicted 0.75 to 0.9: the wider 20 to 180 bar spread of random pressures gives metering more to remove than the reference cycle. With equal pressure inside each phase the median is 0.873 (predicted range held).
- **My H3 prediction was framed backwards.** I wrote "metered/LS under 0.85 in over 95% of equal-pressure cycles", which is the opposite of H3 (saving under 15%, ratio above 0.85). The result: ratio above 0.85 in 67.7% of equal-pressure cycles, so H3's "under 15% at zero spread" holds per case in about two thirds, and fails in about one third (ratio up to 0.77 at the 5th percentile). H3 as a per-case claim is refuted; as a median claim it held (0.873). The saving at zero spread comes from the margin difference alone (dp_ls 14 to 30 against dp_m 3 to 10 bar) and is largest in low-pressure phases where a fixed margin is a large fraction.
- Limits: cycles are random, not measured; the 45 L/min pump, 17.3/17.3/10.4 section flows and 180 bar are from a search-result summary of a Kubota KX018-4 listing and a parts site, neither opened; margins are the milestone 1 ranges. Randomly drawn cycles may be unlike real digging, which is exactly why a measured cycle remains the H4 test.
- Next: obtain a measured or physics-derived cycle (needs `rae.agriculturejournals.cz` and a manufacturer sheet host allowed, or a dataset via `add_repo`); until then milestone 2 is not done.

## 2026-10-28 · Milestone 1: do the three circuits order the way theory says, and by how much? (planned before running)
- Hypotheses and plan: see PROPOSAL H1 to H3. Grid: flow demand 3 x pressure spread 4 x pump flow 3 x dp_ls 3 x dp_spool 3 x dp_m 3 x pump efficiency 3 = 2,916 cases on one illustrative four-phase cycle (pressures and flows written from memory, not sourced).
- **Prediction (before running):** open-centre > load-sensing > metered in 100% of cases (this follows from the model's structure, so it is a check of the code, not a finding); load-sensing/open-centre falls as demand falls; metered/load-sensing falls as spread rises; at zero spread metered/LS is above 0.85.

### Outcome (`experiments/run.py`, under 5 s; `experiments/results.txt`; 6 tests pass)
- All three orderings held in 2,916 of 2,916 cases. Because each rung only removes a loss term from the one before it, this is a structural fact, not evidence about real machines.
- Median load-sensing/open-centre shaft energy 0.47 (0.22 to 0.81); by demand 0.31, 0.47, 0.63 for 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 of the cycle flow: H2's direction held.
- Median metered/load-sensing 0.80 (0.68 to 0.97); by spread 0.88, 0.82, 0.78, 0.74 for 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5. At zero spread the median is 0.88 (prediction above 0.85 held), still a 12% saving from the smaller margin alone; H3's "under 15% at zero spread" held on medians, but no per-case check was made.
- Limits: the cycle is illustrative; no dynamics, no line losses, no leakage, no energy recovery; open-centre modelled as full pump flow at the highest load pressure, which is the pessimistic end (real open-centre bleed-off spools do better at partial lever); efficiency is one constant. These numbers say nothing yet about a real machine; H4 is the test.
- Next: a sourced duty cycle (milestone 2). Crossref was unreachable; key citations were confirmed by web search (HEAP is in Automation in Construction, not Science Robotics as the track text says). Williamson (2010) reports measured fuel cuts of a pump-controlled 5 t excavator: the H4 anchor.
