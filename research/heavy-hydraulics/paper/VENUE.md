# Venue fit: can this paper go to its named target venue? (milestone 7w)

**Short answer: not honestly.** Both venues named in `PROPOSAL.md`, the *International Journal
of Fluid Power* (IJFP, River Publishers) and the Bath/ASME Symposium on Fluid Power and Motion
Control, sit behind rules that ask authors to confirm that generative AI did not create the
submission's content. This paper's code, analysis and text were generated by AI agents (Claude
Code) run by run, as the research guardrails require it to say. A submission to either venue
would have to tick a box that is false. The choice of venue is the owner's; this file records
what the venues' own pages say, read on 2026-11-19, so that choice can be made on the facts.

## What the venues say

| Venue | Rule on generative AI | Source (read 2026-11-19) |
|---|---|---|
| IJFP (River Publishers) | Submission checklist, a box authors must tick: "The submission has not been generated by AI tools and is the original work of the authors listed. AI tools may be used to improve grammar, but not to generate 'original' content." | [IJFP submissions page](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/IJFP/about/submissions) |
| IJFP, ethics page | Softer: "If AI has been used in the generation of any content then this must be declared on submission and mentioned clearly in the text." The two pages disagree; the checklist is the one authors sign. | [IJFP publication ethics](http://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/iJFP/pubethics) |
| ASME journals | "ASME prohibits the use of generative AI in the creation of content for journal submissions. Authors are required to confirm that AI was not used in the development of their original work." Whether the Bath/ASME symposium proceedings follow the journal statement was not checked. | [ASME AI Position Statement](https://www.asme.org/Publications-Submissions/Journals/Information-for-Authors/AI-Position-Statement) |
| IEEE publications | Allowed with disclosure: AI-generated content (text, figures, code) is disclosed in the acknowledgments, naming the system and the sections it produced. | [IEEE submission and peer-review policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/) |
| Elsevier journals | Allowed with a declaration statement, but AI "must never be used as a substitute for human critical thinking, expertise and evaluation" and the paper must reflect the author's own analysis. An agent-run paper fits only if a human author has genuinely reviewed and owns the analysis. | [Elsevier generative AI policies for journals](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals) |
| arXiv | Allowed: significant use of generative AI is reported like other tools, every signing author takes full responsibility, AI is not an author. | [arXiv moderation policy](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html) |

The same table, as data, is `research/_shared/venue_policies.json`;
`python3 research/_shared/venue_policy.py` checks every open programme's target venue against it.
On 2026-11-19 heavy-hydraulics was the only programme of 22 whose named venues all prohibit AI
content; 11 name a venue that allows it with disclosure (IEEE, Elsevier), and 10 name venues
not yet in the register (ICML, NeurIPS, CoRL, ACM EC, MLCAD and others).

## IJFP's format requirements, checked

From River Publishers' *Guidelines for Authors Submitting a Journal Paper* (the PDF linked from
the IJFP site) and the LaTeX style file `riverk.cls`. `python3 paper/venue_check.py` checks the
mechanical ones on `manuscript.md`:

| Requirement | Manuscript after 7w |
|---|---|
| Title at most 80 characters | 164; not cut (the title is a venue decision) |
| Abstract of 250 words | 248 (was 356; cut in this step, every number still checked) |
| Keywords | added |
| List of notations and abbreviations | missing |
| Figure and table caption list at the end | missing (`build_tex.py` could emit it) |
| AI-use statement | added as section "AI use", wanted by IEEE, Elsevier and arXiv |
| Initial submission as PDF in the journal template; 500 dpi images | the PDF uses our own two-column class, not `riverk.cls` |
| Author names, affiliations, photograph, short CV, word and figure counts | owner only |

The guidelines state no word or page limit (the proposal's "8 to 12 pages" was our own aim).
IJFP is open access with an article processing charge payable after acceptance, which would
also be an owner decision about money.

## Options for the owner

1. **arXiv preprint** (eess.SY or physics.app-ph), with the AI-use section as written. The policy
   allows it if the owner signs and takes responsibility for the content.
2. **A venue that allows disclosed AI content**, e.g. an IEEE journal or conference, or an
   Elsevier journal if the owner reviews and owns the analysis. Which one fits fluid-power work
   best was not researched in this step.
3. **Keep IJFP or ASME** only if their rules change, or if the owner rewrites the paper
   personally so that the box is true. Ticking it as things stand would misrepresent how the
   work was produced.

Nothing here was submitted, and nothing was sent to any venue.
