# Supplement to "When does a fixed gear pump beat load-sensing on a small excavator?": the gear-pump selection rules in full

*Sections S1 to S5 are the manuscript's former subsections six to ten of Section 3 (milestones 7a to 7u), moved here verbatim at milestone 7v when the manuscript was cut to journal length; only the headings were renumbered. Inside them, "Table 2" is the manuscript's Table 2 and "Figure 3" its Figure 3. Every number is checked against its results file with the manuscript's own claims by `python3 paper/check_numbers.py --paper manuscript.md`, which reads this file too. The full technical report, in milestone order, is `paper/draft.md`.*

## S1 Pump shape moves the crossing less than idle drag

Nor does the crossing depend much on how a pump's efficiency moves away from the index point. Crossing every family's moving-efficiency shape with every family's idle drag (Casappa, Danfoss, Marzocchi and the bench KP30; 16 cells, each swept in E) puts all sixteen crossings between 0.8069 and 0.8300, at most 0.0143 from the pooled 0.8212 (`gear_pump_shape_results.txt`). Idle drag moves the crossing more than shape does (column-mean range 0.0138 against 0.0095 for shape, interaction at most 0.0043): the bench pump's lower drag, 2.85 kW at 85 L/min against 3.32 to 3.64 kW for the catalogue families, puts its column's mean crossing at 0.8114 against 0.8221 to 0.8252, about +0.0162 per kW of idle power. Of the synthetic shapes, a pressure tilt of &plusmn;0.05 per 100 bar moves the Casappa crossing by at most 0.0013, a size slope of &plusmn;0.10 per 85 L/min by up to 0.0086, because the fuel optimum picks a pump size away from 85 L/min in many geometries. Swept continuously, the drag effect is close to linear and does not depend on shape: scaling the Casappa idle-power curve from zero to twice its value moves the crossing from 0.7647 to 0.8983 on the Casappa shape, a slope of 0.0187 per kW of idle power at 85 L/min (0.0026 per bar of equivalent drag pressure), with the four shapes' slopes within 0.0012 of each other and the straight line off by at most 0.0035, slightly convex (`gear_pump_drag_results.txt`). A rule with the drag as a second number, E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8277 + 0.0187 (kW &minus; 3.57), calibrated on the Casappa shape alone, predicts the sixteen factorial cells with an RMS error of 0.0053 against 0.0066 for the single pooled 0.8212. Even a pump with no idle drag at all needs about 0.76 to 0.765.

## S2 Held-out families test the two-number rule

Both rules were then tested on a fifth family that played no part in any fit, index or form choice: Bosch Rexroth's AZPG, read from the vector curves of its data sheet (RE 10093). Its efficiency at the index point is 0.9124, above every family before it, its curves are nearly flat in pressure and size, and its idle power at 85 L/min is 2.58 kW, the lowest yet. Swept in E with its own shape and drag, it crosses 50% at 0.8040. The single pooled number misses that by 0.0172, outside the 0.015 the four families stayed within; the two-number rule, as committed, predicts 0.8091, an error of 0.0052. Swapping halves puts the cause in the drag: the AZPG shape with the Casappa catalogue's drag crosses at 0.8207, the Casappa shape with the AZPG drag at 0.8108 (`gear_pump_rexroth_results.txt`). Its catalogue point, at its own efficiency, makes the fixed pump win in 100.0% of geometries. Crossing all five families' shapes with all five drags (25 cells) puts every crossing between 0.8010 and 0.8300; drag now moves it about twice as much as shape (column-mean range 0.0194 against 0.0096). Refitted on all 25 cells, the rule is E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8241 + 0.0179 (kW &minus; 3.57) (in-sample RMS 0.0040 against 0.0084 for a constant). Leaving each family out in turn, shape and drag together, and fitting on the 16 cells that do not involve it, the drag rule predicts the left-out family's own crossing with errors of +0.0037, +0.0038, &minus;0.0002, +0.0006 and &minus;0.0042 (Casappa, Danfoss, Marzocchi, bench, AZPG; RMS 0.0030), against an RMS of 0.0119 for a constant fitted the same way, and its slope stays between 0.0162 and 0.0198 per kW over the five folds. A third number for shape, the relative change in efficiency from 85 to 150 L/min, does not help a left-out family (RMS 0.0046), so the rule stays at two numbers (`gear_pump_five_results.txt`).

A sixth family, Bosch Rexroth's AZPN (RE 10091; sizes 20 to 36 cm<sup>3</sup>/r), was then digitised the same way as a fresh held-out test. Its largest size gives 75.58 L/min, short of the 85 L/min index point, and its idle power at 85 L/min is 2.49 kW, below every earlier family. As registered, with the index read by extrapolating efficiency over size to 85 L/min (0.9587), it crosses at 0.8342, and the committed rule misses it by +0.0293, worse than a constant (+0.0170). The miss is in the index, not the rule: the model holds the largest size's efficiency past it (0.9250), and the extrapolated index overstates that by the same factor that moves the crossing (0.9648 against 0.9650). Read where the model reads the pump, the AZPN crosses at 0.8050 and the rule as committed predicts 0.8049; refitted on all six families, E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8245 + 0.0183 (kW &minus; 3.57), and left out in turn the six families' crossings are predicted with an RMS of 0.0023 against 0.0120 for a constant. That reading was chosen after the registered test failed, so it is a diagnosis, not a confirmation (`gear_pump_six_results.txt`). It was therefore fixed in the code (the index holds the end size's efficiency outside a family's flow range, as the model does; the five families that reach 85 L/min are unchanged) and tested on a seventh family registered before any number was computed from it: Bosch Rexroth's low-noise AZPU (RE 10 098; ten sizes, 22.5 to 63 cm<sup>3</sup>/r, the largest giving 136.73 L/min, so the fix does not touch it). Its index is 0.8890 and its idle power at 85 L/min 2.45 kW, the lowest of seven. It crosses at 0.8030; the rule as committed predicts 0.8041, an error of &minus;0.0011, and a constant misses by &minus;0.0142. Refitted on all seven families crossed (49 cells), E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8239 + 0.0179 (kW &minus; 3.57), and left out in turn the seven families' crossings are predicted with an RMS of 0.0023 against 0.0120 for a constant (0.0037 against 0.0099 over all 91 held-out cells) (`gear_pump_seven_results.txt`).

An eighth family then tests the held reading where it carries the whole answer: Bosch Rexroth's AZPF (RE 10089; ten sizes, 4 to 28 cm<sup>3</sup>/r), whose largest size gives 58.87 L/min, so 76.6% of the fixed-pump sizes the optimum can pick lie above it and are priced as its size 28 held. Registered before any number was computed from it, its index is 0.9103 and its idle power at 85 L/min 2.61 kW. It crosses at 0.8075; the seven-family rule predicts 0.8067, an error of +0.0008, and a constant misses by &minus;0.0060. Held at size 25 instead, the error is +0.0010. Over all eight families crossed (64 cells), E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8238 + 0.0177 (kW &minus; 3.57), and left out in turn the eight families' crossings are predicted with an RMS of 0.0022 against 0.0113 for a constant (`gear_pump_eight_results.txt`). A builder short of displacement would rather spin that size 28 faster than buy a pump that does not exist. Read off the sheet's own curves at the speed that gives each flow, capped at the rated 3000 r/min, its efficiency at the index point falls only to 0.9043 while its zero-pressure drag rises from 7.7 to 12.3 W per L/min, so its idle power at 85 L/min rises to 2.99 kW. The crossing does not move (0.8075), but the rule, which reads only the drag, now predicts 0.8135, an error of &minus;0.0060. With the AZPF and AZPN both spun, the eight-family fit is E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8230 + 0.0181 (kW &minus; 3.57) and the left-out RMS 0.0035 against 0.0115 for a constant (`gear_pump_spin_results.txt`): the rule holds, but its drag should be read at the speed the pump will run, and a spun pump is its worst case. Spinning needs a step-up drive. Priced from the measured spur-gear system loss of Anderson and Loewenthal [NASA TP-1622] (99.34% at full load, the no-load loss up to 65% of the full-load loss), it adds 0.14 kW of idle power to the AZPF and moves its crossing to 0.8094, so the rule's error is &minus;0.0066; the AZPN's crossing falls to 0.7988 and the rule misses it by &minus;0.0101, because the drive lowers the index point without touching the smaller pumps the optimum picks. With both families geared, the left-out RMS is 0.0051 against 0.0121 for a constant (`gear_pump_stepup_results.txt`): a geared pump should be read by its index and drag without the drive, or the drive priced directly.

## S3 A shape number, and drive drag charged where the optimum picks

A third number, registered for pumps run outside their size range, reads the shape below the index point, k<sub>lo</sub>, the family's efficiency at 57 L/min (the one flow inside every family's size range) over its index, minus one. Pooling fourteen readings (six families and the AZPF and AZPN each held, spun, geared and geared at 98%; 196 cells) and leaving each maker family out with all its readings, E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8200 + 0.0172 (kW &minus; 3.57) &minus; 0.1819 k<sub>lo</sub> predicts the left-out crossings with an RMS of 0.0068 against 0.0084 for the drag rule, 18% better, short of the 20% registered as the bar; on the held families alone it costs nothing (0.0022 either way) and on the geared panel it helps (0.0040 against 0.0051). The size slope above the index, tried in place of it, is worse than two numbers on every panel. The geared AZPN still sits 0.0070 below the three-number rule (`gear_pump_third_results.txt`), so the drag rule stays the headline and k<sub>lo</sub> a partial correction for pumps run outside their size range. The fuel optimum's picks, read over six families' own crossings, centre on 72.0 L/min (p10 58.0, p90 90.5), so a second shape number was registered that averages the family's efficiency over those picks, k<sub>w</sub>. It cuts the pooled left-out RMS to 0.0069, the same 18% as k<sub>lo</sub>, with the lowest held-family error of any rule (0.0017 against 0.0022), and it too leaves the geared AZPN below the rule (&minus;0.0082); the share of picks above a family's largest size does not help (0.0093) (`gear_pump_chosen_results.txt`). Two shape numbers chosen on different grounds reaching the same gain suggests that is what a shape read at 150 bar can buy, and that the AZPN's residual lies elsewhere. Reading the drag at the same picks, kW<sub>w</sub>, the family's idle power averaged over them (0.741 to 0.867 of the idle power at 85 L/min), was registered next. With k<sub>w</sub>, E<sub>50</sub> = 0.7598 + 0.0204 kW<sub>w</sub> &minus; 0.4269 k<sub>w</sub> predicts the left-out crossings with an RMS of 0.0059, 29% below the drag rule and the first rule to clear the registered 20%; the gain comes from spun and geared pumps, whose added drag sits above their largest size, where the optimum seldom picks. On the eight held catalogue families alone it is worse than the drag at 85 L/min (0.0034 against 0.0022), reading each pick at its own outlet pressure (median 151 bar) adds nothing, and the geared AZPN still sits 0.0068 below it (`gear_pump_pickdrag_results.txt`). That trade proved unnecessary. Keeping the drag at 85 L/min on the pump's own catalogue curve and adding only the extra drag of the overspeed or step-up drive, weighted by the picks above the largest size (kW<sub>o</sub>), gives E<sub>50</sub> = 0.7562 + 0.0182 kW<sub>o</sub> &minus; 0.4269 k<sub>w</sub>, whose left-out RMS is 0.0056, 34% below the drag rule, while on the held catalogue families it equals the k<sub>w</sub> rule by construction (0.0017); on the spun and geared panels alone it gives 0.0018 and 0.0030, the lowest of any rule. The geared AZPN still sits 0.0065 below it (`gear_pump_holddrag_results.txt`). This is the selection rule we recommend: for a pump in its size range it reduces to the drag at 85 L/min with the shape number; for one spun or geared past it, the drive's extra drag is charged only where the optimum picks.

## S4 Idle settings: carrying the competitor's standby cost

Every rule so far was fitted at one idle setting (PVG 32 neutral curve, 2 L/min standby, idle at working speed, S = 0.38). Rebuilt at all 18 idle settings and four other idle shares, the recommended rule beats the drag rule in all 22 conditions, by 17% to 43% (left-out RMS 0.0047 to 0.0064), with its drag slope +0.0175 to +0.0195 across the 18 settings and its shape coefficient negative throughout. Its intercept does not transfer: at half idle speed, where the load-sensing pump's standby power halves, the crossing rises by 0.02 to 0.03 and the frozen rule misses by up to 0.0349. Keeping both slopes and refitting only the intercept gives a left-out RMS of 0.0044 to 0.0076, within 1.25 times the fully refitted rule in every condition (`gear_pump_settings_results.txt`). The drag slope also scales with the idle share (+0.0087 at S = 0.20 to +0.0275 at 0.50), so a builder should derive the intercept, and for an unusual idle share the drag slope, from the break-even model at the machine's own idle setting. Carrying the competitor's idle cost removes that step. Pooled over all 22 conditions (4312 cells), E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8234 + 0.0524 S&middot;kW<sub>o</sub> &minus; 0.4350 k<sub>w</sub> &minus; 0.0664 S&middot;P<sub>LS</sub>, with P<sub>LS</sub> the load-sensing pump's standby power and S the idle share, predicts each condition with that condition and the maker family both left out of the fit at an RMS of 0.0051 to 0.0062, no worse than the per-condition refit; the same rule without a setting term misses half idle speed by up to 0.0298, and with P<sub>LS</sub> but not S it misses the idle shares 0.20 and 0.50 by 0.0117 and 0.0111. Trained on one idle speed and scored on the other, it stays within 0.0098 (`gear_pump_idlecost_results.txt`). At idle what moves the crossing is the drag difference weighted by idle time, with the competitor's standby kW counting about 1.27 times the fixed pump's. An independently written replication, registered before this result was known, reaches the same rule with the model's own idle hours per working hour, S/(1 &minus; S), in place of S: an RMS of 0.0056 over the 308 left-out diagonals, 0.0057 on idle shares 0.20 and 0.50 when both are left out of the fit, and 0.0059 on the nine half-speed conditions when trained at working speed only (S form 0.0057, 0.0057 and 0.0066). It puts a standby kW at 1.22 drag kW, and forcing the two to trade one for one costs 22% (`gear_pump_onerule_results.txt`). The 1.22 is a reading of the drag, not a price: the model charges idle as b (P<sub>LS</sub> &minus; P<sub>OC</sub>) h, so the two can only trade one for one, and kW<sub>o</sub>, read at 85 L/min, overstates the drag the optimum pays at its picked sizes (kW<sub>w</sub>/kW<sub>o</sub> 0.786 to 0.867 at the central setting; slope of kW<sub>w</sub> on kW<sub>o</sub> 0.880). With the pick-weighted drag kW<sub>w</sub> in the idle term the fitted ratio is 1.02, and the rule collapses to one price difference, E<sub>50</sub> = 0.8208 + 0.0359 H (kW<sub>w</sub> &minus; P<sub>LS</sub>) &minus; 0.435 k<sub>w</sub>, with H = S/(1 &minus; S): left-out RMS 0.0056 over 308 diagonals (the two-price rule 0.0056), 0.0059 on the unseen half-speed and idle-share conditions, at the cost of the Danfoss family (0.0080 against 0.0061), whose drag at the picks is the highest share of its drag at 85 L/min (`gear_pump_ratio_results.txt`). A builder lacks the pick distribution; read at its median flow instead (72 L/min), the drag still trades with standby near one for one (ratio 1.06), but the one-number rule's left-out RMS rises to 0.0065 and the Danfoss family's to 0.0142, so the two-price rule on kW<sub>o</sub> remains the practical form (`gear_pump_onepoint_results.txt`). Two reads do not rescue it: the mean of the drag at the picks' quartiles (64 and 79 L/min) comes within 2% of kW<sub>w</sub> for 13 of 14 variants, but across variants it carries only 0.859 kW of kW<sub>w</sub> per kW, a slope set by the Danfoss family's bending curve, and the one-number rule's left-out RMS stays at 0.0066 (`gear_pump_twopoint_results.txt`). A one-number rule needs the read's scale across pumps to be one, which a fixed catalogue read does not give; the two-price rule fits that scale.

## S5 What a builder can read in place of the optimum's picks

The form itself is sound: with each pump's drag averaged over the sizes the optimum picks for that pump (an oracle read, from the model's own picks at the central setting), the one-number rule's left-out RMS falls to 0.0042, below the two-price rule's 0.0056, and standby and drag trade 0.98 for one. Fixed reads fail because the optimum sizes a pump with high or steep drag down: the Danfoss family pays 0.782 of its pooled-pick drag at its own picks, and across pumps the own-pick drag carries only 0.743 kW per kW of the pooled-pick read (`gear_pump_ownpick_results.txt`). That read uses each pump's picks at the efficiency of its own crossing, the number being predicted; read the held-out pump's picks instead at the mean crossing of the other maker groups, and the left-out RMS is 0.0047 (0.0049 at unseen half idle speed), so about a third of the oracle's gain was that leak and two thirds survive it (`gear_pump_leak_results.txt`). A builder lacks those picks. A builder-side stand-in, the published pick weight tilted away from the flows where the pump's drag exceeds the six families' mean (one constant, calibrated on the six families' own picks, 2.15 per kW), closes only a fifth of that gap: left-out RMS 0.0053 against 0.0056, standby and drag again trading 0.98 for one. The constant is learned from the Danfoss family alone: calibrated without it, the best tilt is 0.00, so the family that most needs the correction never receives it out of sample (`gear_pump_tilt_results.txt`). A published pick table does no better: averaging the held-out pump's curve over the picks of the modelled pumps nearest it in bend (drag at 79 over 64 L/min), at the same leak-free efficiency, gives a left-out RMS of 0.0075, and 0.0061 with every donor pooled, because no donor bends like Danfoss (1.781) and the table charges that family 1.467 times the drag it pays at its own picks (`gear_pump_table_results.txt`). Rows the model builds for synthetic curves narrow that gap: bending each donor's own idle curve by a power of flow to a printed bend (1.2 to 1.9 in steps of 0.1) and tabulating the model's picks on it, a builder reading the row nearest their pump's bend gets a left-out RMS of 0.0052 (half idle speed 0.0056 against the two-price rule's 0.0059), and Danfoss's error falls from 0.0122 under the pooled table to 0.0063, near the two-price rule's 0.0061; its row still charges it 1.197 times its own-pick drag (`gear_pump_bendtable_results.txt`). The bent curves are synthetic, so this is a sensitivity: a table carries part of the own-pick gain once it has a row for the pump's bend, and a measured second bending family would make that a result. The gain survives a change of form but not of anchor: bending the rows instead by a hinge above 64 L/min or an exponential in flow, both holding the level at 64 L/min, gives 0.0053, but holding the level at 79 L/min makes a steeper row cheaper at small sizes, its picks stay near 72 L/min, and the table scores 0.0059, worse than the two-price rule, with Danfoss at 0.0110 (`gear_pump_bendshape_results.txt`). Which anchor is right is a property of real pumps, and the measured donors bear on it without touching the pump being predicted: the slope of their log drag at 64 L/min on their log bend puts the pivot of a power-law bend at 65.4 L/min for the Danfoss fold, and the same slope within the two families that have several variants puts it at 61.1 L/min, so the measured pumps do not show the anchor at 79. With those anchors the table scores 0.0052 and 0.0050 left out, beats the two-price rule at half idle speed and unseen idle shares as well, and with the within-family anchor its Danfoss error, 0.0054, is below the two-price rule's for the first time (`gear_pump_anchor_results.txt`). Two cautions keep it a sensitivity: the within-family read reflects where each catalogue ends, since a drive's extra drag is charged only above the largest size, and Danfoss's drag at 64 L/min sits 0.251 in log terms above what its donors predict for its bend, a height no bend of a donor curve carries. The practical form stays the two-price rule. Whether a family bends is itself a reading, of the family, the valve and the idle speed together. Two more families read at the central setting do not bend: a second Casappa family, Polaris 30, gives b = 1.272, and a fourth maker's datasheet, Argo-Hytos GP3, gives 1.263, so one family in nine bends, Danfoss at 1.781 (`gear_pump_argo_results.txt`). The GP3 lines meet zero speed at about zero power, as if drawn from one efficiency, so its reading is not a measured drag. The valve's part follows by algebra: a pump with no drag and one efficiency absorbs q p<sub>n</sub> / (600 &eta;), so its bend is 79 p<sub>n</sub>(79) / (64 p<sub>n</sub>(64)), which is 1.318 on the PVG 32 reading and (79/64)<sup>3</sup> = 1.881 on either Salami reading, and a drag proportional to flow pulls b from there towards 79/64. Read as r, b over that drag-free value, the seven other drag-carrying families sit at 0.966 to 1.042 at the central setting and Danfoss at 1.352. At full idle speed Danfoss leads the next family's r by 0.288 to 0.310 on all three readings. At half idle speed it does not: on the PVG 32 reading the Casappa KP30 reads r 1.220 against Danfoss's 1.277, and on the Salami-text reading the KP30 leads, 1.469 against 1.015, because Danfoss's drag is 94.1% of its idle power at 64 L/min there (`gear_pump_floor_results.txt`). A fixed cut on b fails off the central setting (seven of nine families read above 1.55 on either Salami reading at full speed), so a pick table keyed on bend should key on r at the idle speed the machine will run.
