# A forgetting law for sequential training on random tasks, and what modular routing buys

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Decentralised training swarms feed a model a stream of tasks from different contributors, so continual learning and modular architectures are design questions, not afterthoughts. We study the cleanest exactly solvable case: training to convergence on a task is a projection of the error onto the task's null space, and tasks are Haar-random rank-`r` subspaces of `R^d` that share a solution. The expected loss on a just-learned task after `k` further tasks is exactly `(r/d)(1−r/d)(ρᵏ−λᵏ)`, with `ρ=1−r/d` and `λ=ρ−r(d−r)/((d+2)(d−1))`. Forgetting is therefore not monotone: it is zero when the task is learned, peaks (0.044 at `d=12, r=3`, lag 2.5; peak lag ≈ 5 at `r/d=1/8`, ≈ 89 at `d=128, r=1`), and then vanishes because the tasks are consistent. Its sum over all lags is `(r/d)ρ(d/r − 1/(1−λ))`, only 0.31–0.49 of the initial squared error for `r/d ≤ 1/4`. Routing tasks to `m` modules dilutes both rates by `m`; it cuts forgetting at short horizons (average seen-task loss ÷5.9 at `T=4, m=8`), but the seen-plus-fresh-task loss is minimised by a single shared model at every horizon we tried, and at `T=12` two modules forget more than one. When tasks conflict, a shared model's forgetting has a floor `2rτ²/d` that only task-specific modules remove. Verified by exact quadrature at `d=2`, series and grid identities, and Monte Carlo. Stylised: linear regression, exact convergence, isotropic random tasks.

## 1. Model
Let `e = w − w*` be the error and task `j` have row space `Πⱼ` (a Haar-random `r`-dimensional projector). Training to convergence gives `e ← (I−Πⱼ)e` (minimum-norm interpolation, realizable case). The loss on task `i` is `‖Πᵢe‖²`. Forgetting of task `i` is its loss `k` tasks later; it is zero at the moment of learning.

## 2. The identity
For a Haar rank-`r` projector, rotation invariance gives `E[ΠAΠ] = c₁A + c₂ tr(A) I`. Taking `A=I` gives `c₁+dc₂=r/d`. The Beta law `eᵀΠe ~ Beta(r/2,(d−r)/2)` gives `E(eᵀΠe)²=r(r+2)/(d(d+2))=c₁+c₂`. Hence `c₂=r(d−r)/(d(d+2)(d−1))`. (`d=2, r=1` checks by quadrature to 9 places.) The complement `I−Π` is a rank-`(d−r)` projector, so `E[(I−Π)A(I−Π)]=λA+c₂ tr(A) I` where `λ=c₁(d−r)=ρ−dc₂`, and the trace contraction is `ρ=1−r/d`. Starting from `A₀=e₀e₀ᵀ`, the recursion `A_{k+1}=λA_k+c₂ tr(A_k)I` gives `A_k=λᵏA₀+((ρᵏ−λᵏ)/d)‖e₀‖²I`.

## 3. Forgetting
Task 1 is learned from error `e_init`, so `e₀=(I−Π₁)e_init` has `Π₁e₀=0` and `E‖e₀‖²=ρ‖e_init‖²`; later tasks are independent of `Π₁`. So `E‖Π₁e_k‖² = r·((ρᵏ−λᵏ)/d)·ρ‖e_init‖²`:
`F(k) = (r/d)ρ(ρᵏ−λᵏ)‖e_init‖²`.
Since `0<λ<ρ<1`, `F` rises and falls; the peak lag solves `(ρ/λ)ᵏ = ln λ/ln ρ` and `Σₖ F(k) = (r/d)ρ(d/r − 1/(1−λ))`. E1 (`d=12,r=3`, 20000 runs) matches to the third decimal: lags 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 give law/simulation 0.03287/0.03284, 0.04355/0.04358, 0.03888/0.03851, 0.01654/0.01663, 0.00185/0.00184. E2: the peak height is set almost entirely by `r/d` (`0.0606` at `r/d=0.5`, d=32; `0.0620` at d=128; `0.0146` at `r/d=1/16` for both d=128 and d=512), while the peak lag is about `d/r·0.7`: 1.0 at `r/d=½`, 10.8 at 1/16, 88.6 at 1/128. Small tasks in a big model are forgotten late and mildly; large tasks early and hard.

## 4. Modules
Route each task to one of `m` modules uniformly. The number of later tasks hitting a module is Binomial, so `F_m(k) = (r/d)ρ(aᵏ−bᵏ)` with `a=1−(r/d)/m`, `b=1−(1−λ)/m`; the module a task lands on has also been shrunk by earlier visits, and summing over positions gives the exact seen-task average `(r/d)ρ[T x^(T−1) − (x^T−b^T)/(x−b)]/T` with `x=a` (a first version that omitted this factor was caught by the simulation: 0.0205 vs 0.0114 at `T=12`; the corrected law is within 1% at all tested cells). The fresh-task loss is `(r/d)(1−(r/d)/m)^T`. E3 (`d=32,r=4`, 1500 runs each): at `T=4` the seen-task loss falls 0.01230, 0.00738, 0.00402, 0.00209 for `m=1,2,4,8` (÷5.9) while the fresh loss rises 0.073→0.117; at `T=12`, `m=2` is *worse* than `m=1` on seen tasks (0.01397 vs 0.01155): dilution delays the decay of old forgetting as much as it slows its onset. Minimising seen+fresh over `m≤64` returns `m=1` for every `T∈{1,…,96}` at `(d,r)=(32,4)` and `(128,8)`. Reading: with consistent tasks, sharing is positive transfer and modularity buys only short-horizon insurance; the routed-expert designs in `wagering-modular-experts` and `sleeping-ledger` pay for themselves when tasks *conflict*.

## 5. Conflicting tasks
Let task optima be `w*+δⱼ`, `δⱼ~N(0,τ²I/d)`: training gives `e ← (I−Π)e+Πδ`. Rotation invariance makes the stationary error isotropic with `E‖e‖²=τ²`, and a task learned long ago has loss `E‖Π(e−δ)‖²→2rτ²/d` (half from the drifted iterate, half from the task's own offset). E4 (`d=10,r=2,τ²=1`, 1500 runs): loss on an old task at lag 1, 3, 10, 40 is 0.077, 0.195, 0.364, 0.402 against the floor 0.400; stationary `E‖e‖²` 0.996–1.019. Forgetting no longer vanishes, and the floor scales with `r/d`, the fraction of the model each task touches. A module per task removes it and costs `d` parameters per task; the law prices that trade-off.

## 6. Limits and next steps
Linear regression, exact convergence, Haar-random subspaces, uniform routing; no nonlinear features, no SGD noise or finite step size, no learned or task-aware router. The conflict floor is verified by simulation and an isotropy argument, not a full time-dependent derivation. A natural extension is task-aware routing in the conflicting regime and a verification hook: a verifier can audit a claimed continual-learning trace against `F(k)` on replayed tasks. Related: `wagering-modular-experts`, `sleeping-ledger`, `local-sgd-bias`, `market-routing`.
