# Occlusion twin: independent per-beam visibility gets the mean right and the blind probability wrong

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: a lidar with `K` parallel beams at lateral spacing `s` looking through a Poisson field of opaque occluders of radius `ρ` (foliage, crowd, rubble), with centre intensity `μ` per unit lateral length. A beam is blocked iff an occluder centre lies within `ρ` of it, so one beam is clear with probability `p = e^{−2ρμ}` and any set of beams is simultaneously clear with probability `exp(−μ·|union of the intervals (y_k−ρ, y_k+ρ)|)`. The twin gives every beam visibility `p` independently. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`; exact formulas checked against a sampled-field ensemble in the tests and in the experiment): (1) two beams are both clear with probability `exp(−μ(2ρ+min(s,2ρ)))`, which equals `p²` **iff `s ≥ 2ρ`**; at `p = 0.5`, `ρ = 0.5 m` the pair is both blocked with probability 0.354 at `s = 0.5 m` where the twin says 0.250, and 0.500 (a single beam) at `s = 0`; (2) the mean number of clear beams is identical, but the variance is not: for 8 beams at 25 cm, 6.10 vs the twin's 2.00; (3) the sensor is blind (all 8 beams blocked) with probability 0.0815 against the twin's 0.0039 (20.9×; 97× at 5 cm, 5.2× at 50 cm, exactly 1× from 1 m); (4) beams needed for blind probability ≤ 0.01: 7 per the twin and for spacing ≥ 1 m, 10 at 50 cm, 16 at 25 cm, and not reachable with 16 at 10 cm; (5) an ensemble twin that samples occluder fields reproduces the exact value (0.0815 vs 0.0815 ± 0.0006 over 200,000 fields); (6) at fixed single-beam visibility the error is set by occluder size alone: 8 beams at 25 cm are exact for `ρ ≤ 0.125 m` (diameter ≤ spacing), 5.2× too optimistic at `ρ = 0.25 m`, 20.9× at 0.5 m and 53.6× at 1 m (blind 0.2095 vs 0.0039).

```bash
cd research/occlusion-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 11 tests, about 1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # about 30 s; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The lidar sensor-simulation and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>) and the field-robot digital-twin fidelity direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The Boolean (Poisson-disc) model and its coverage formulas are classical (Stoyan, Kendall & Mecke 1995; Hall 1988). Companion to `fog-twin` (extinction along one beam), `dropout-twin` and `rain-twin`: those treat what a beam loses, this treats what neighbouring beams share.

Stylised: simulated "real" system, no lidar data; one-dimensional lateral projection of occluders (beams long compared with `ρ`, end effects ignored), opaque binary occluders of one radius, Poisson placement (no clustering), parallel beams rather than a fan. MIT.
