Does weighting parts by mass change which shop process, and which machine, matters? With a tip load FDM covers 0.30 of parts by count and 0.58 by mass, the laser-versus-CNC order flips, and at budgets 600–800 exhaustive search picks the laser cutter by count but the casting setup by mass (one seed). Stylised, assumed rules and prices, simulated only.

8 tests

What it found

Follow-up to simdesign-processcover: coverage of a sampled bracket family by FDM, CNC, laser and casting, with each part weighted by arm volume (a same-material mass proxy) instead of counted equally. With a tip load, FDM alone covers 0.30 of parts by count and 0.58 by mass, casting 0.22 and 0.46, laser 0.18 either way, and the laser-versus-CNC order flips. At budgets 600–800 on one seed, exhaustive search picks the laser cutter by count but the casting setup by mass; for the large-part family at 800 it picks the CNC router by count and casting by mass. Stylised: assumed rules and prices, one density, single-seed machine choice. Simulated only.

  • Does a mesh-aware stress margin change what manufacturing rules cost a sized bracket?Re-runs simdesign-dfm with a stress knock-down and a finer search mesh, checking designs at h = 0.7 mm: the price ordering (casting ~1.4, CNC ~1.23, FDM 1.0) survives the mesh, the laser-cutting price does not (1.01–1.22, noisy), and a 1.3 margin leaves designs at 0.88–1.02 of the limit (stylised, assumed rule values).
  • Does a patch-averaged stress converge under mesh refinement where the peak element stress does not?Compares peak and patch-averaged von Mises stress under mesh refinement on the L-bracket: the peak rises 18–41% from h = 2.5 to 0.55 mm while a 3 mm patch average moves 1–6%, but it reads only 0.45–0.69 of the peak so needs its own limit (stylised, assumed values).
  • Does a patch-averaged stress change what manufacturing rules cost a sized bracket?Repeats the process-rule bracket search with a 3 mm patch-averaged stress instead of the peak: the ordering of mass prices (casting > CNC > laser ≈ FDM) holds, the laser price spread across seeds shrinks from 0.16 to under 0.005, and designs overshoot their limit by 2–12% on a fine mesh against 19–34% for the peak (stylised, assumed values, uncalibrated limit).
