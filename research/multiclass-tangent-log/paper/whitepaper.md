# Multiclass tangent-log: a payable log score for K fault classes

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
`categorical-scores` showed that bounded K-ary proper scores (Brier, spherical) nearly ignore a dropped rare class and left open a bounded score that does not. We extend the negative-entropy generator `ψ(x)=x ln x` below a cutoff ε by its second-order Taylor polynomial and use the separable generator `G(r)=Σψ(r_j)` on the simplex. The resulting Bregman score is strictly proper on the whole simplex, identical to `−ln r_y` whenever every `r_j≥ε`, and has payment range exactly `1−ln ε`, **independent of K** (`ln(1/ε)+1`; `tangent-log` is the binary analogue, which extends both tails of a two-point generator and has a slightly different range). Its regret is `Σ_j B(p_j‖r_j)`, its worst-case regret is a vertex value, and its local curvature is `½Σψ''(p_j)δ_j²` with `ψ''=1/max(x,ε)`. Per unit of range it has 3× Brier's rare-class incentive at ρ=0.05, 63× at ρ=10⁻³ and 490× at 10⁻⁴; the cutoff maximising it is ε=p_min. Zeroing out a class of mass ρ costs exactly `ρ ln(ρ/ε)+ε/2+ρ+(1−ρ)ln(1−ρ)` (0.0051 at ρ=ε=0.01, vs 1.3·10⁻⁴ for Brier and ∞ for log). Standard Bregman/proper-scoring construction applied to the multiclass case; not new theory.

## 1. Construction
`ψ(x)=x ln x` for `x≥ε`; for `x<ε`, `ψ(x)=ψ(ε)+ψ'(ε)(x−ε)+(x−ε)²/(2ε)`. Then `ψ` is C², `ψ''=1/max(x,ε)>0`, so `G(r)=Σ_jψ(r_j)` is strictly convex. The Bregman loss is
`L(r,y)=Σ_j h(r_j) − ψ'(r_y)`, `h(x)=xψ'(x)−ψ(x)`,
and `E_p L(r,·)−E_p L(p,·)=D_G(p‖r)=Σ_j[ψ(p_j)−ψ(r_j)−ψ'(r_j)(p_j−r_j)]>0` for `r≠p`. For `x≥ε`, `h(x)=x`, so `L(r,y)=1−(ln r_y+1)=−ln r_y` exactly whenever `min_j r_j≥ε` (tests, 1e-12), with no additive constant (unlike the binary two-sided form, where `G(0)=G(1)≠0`). Regret equals KL only when both `p` and `r` are ≥ε coordinatewise. Tests: C² at ε, strict properness on 500 heavy-tailed simplex pairs (many coordinates below ε), regret = sum of scalar Bregman terms (1e-10).

## 2. Payment range `1−ln ε`, independent of K
For fixed `y`, `L(·,y)` is convex (`h` is convex, `−ψ'` is convex because `ψ'` is concave), so its maximum is at a vertex; the vertex `e_j`, `j≠y`, gives `h(1)+(K−1)h(0)−ψ'(0)=1+(K−1)ε/2−ln ε` (`h(0)=ε/2`, `ψ'(0)=ln ε`). The minimum is at `r=e_y`: properness against the point mass gives `L(r,y)−L(e_y,y)=D_G(e_y‖r)≥0`, and `L(e_y,y)=(K−1)ε/2`. The `(K−1)ε/2` terms cancel: range `=1−ln ε`. E1 matches the observed max−min over K∈{2,5,20} and ε∈{0.05,0.001} to 4 decimals (3.9957, 7.9078) on random and vertex reports. Plain clipping of the log score would have maximum payment `ln(1/ε)`, one unit less, but see §5.

## 3. Rare-class incentive per unit of range
Near the truth, regret is `½Σψ''(p_j)δ_j²`; dividing by the range (Brier: range 2, regret `‖δ‖²`) compares incentive per unit of payment. For the shift `δ=(1,−1,0,…)` between a rare class 0 (mass ρ) and a common class 1 at K=5, with ε=ρ, E3 gives tangent/Brier: 1.34 (ρ=0.5), 1.92 (0.2), 3.03 (0.05), 9.3 (0.01), 63 (10⁻³), 490 (10⁻⁴). This is the multiclass version of the `tangent-log` crossover: the score is a rare-class tool; for balanced classes Brier or spherical are competitive per range.

## 4. Choosing ε; worst-case regret
For a truth with smallest mass ρ, curvature per range for the rare shift is maximised at ε=ρ: below ρ the range grows as `1−ln ε` while curvature stays at `1/(2ρ)`, above ρ curvature is capped at `1/(2ε)` (E4: 40.1, 49.2, 55.1 for ε=10⁻⁵,10⁻⁴,3·10⁻⁴; **63.5 at 10⁻³**; 24.8, 9.3, 2.1 for 3·10⁻³, 10⁻², 10⁻¹; grid argmax 0.00100). Worst-case regret is convex in `r` hence a vertex value, `max_j Σ_i B(p_i‖e_j,i)` with `B(x‖0)=x ln(x/ε)+ε/2` for `x≥ε` and `x²/(2ε)` below; per unit range at K=5, ε=ρ it is 0.60, 0.74, 0.82 for ρ=0.05, 0.01, 0.001 against Brier's 0.56–0.62 (E2). So the bounded log score also has a worse worst-case-to-range ratio than Brier; the price of steep local incentives is a large loss when a verifier is confidently wrong about a mid-probability class.

## 5. Dropped classes, and why not just clip
A verifier that reports 0 for a class of mass ρ≥ε and rescales the rest loses exactly `ρ ln(ρ/ε)+ε/2+ρ+(1−ρ)ln(1−ρ)` (matches the computed regret to 1e-12; the rest's rescaling contributes `ρ+(1−ρ)ln(1−ρ)`). E5 (K=5, ε=ρ): 0.0263, 0.0051, 0.0005 for ρ=0.05, 0.01, 0.001 — 8×, 40×, 400× Brier's loss, where log charges ∞ and stake cannot cover it. At ρ=ε it is `≈ε/2+ρ²/2`: bounded and saturating, so deterrence against total blindness is weaker than log's but non-negligible, and payable. The alternative of flooring reports at ε and renormalising keeps log's exact payments but makes truthful reporting impossible when `p_j<ε`: at ε=0.01, ρ=0.001 (K=5) the floored report is 0.0067 in KL from the truth (E6) while the tangent score accepts the truth (regret 0) at one extra unit of range.

## 6. Limits
Risk-neutral verifiers (see `risk-averse-scoring`), one report per task, known truth distribution; a single global ε rather than one per class (per-class cutoffs `ε_j` keep the construction separable but change the range, not tested); the score is not local to the reported probability of the realised class in the extended region, so it cannot be combined with sampled audits without the weighting of `audit-weighted-scoring`. Related: `tangent-log`, `categorical-scores`, `score-recalibration`, `quantized-reports`, `decision-regret-transfer`.
