# A twin that is wrong about correlation sends the UAV to the wrong place, and its update can be worse than no observation

*Stylised: 1-D Gaussian field on 40 cells, Gaussian-shape kernel `exp(−(d/ℓ)²)` with real `ℓ=0.2` plus a 1e-3 nugget, observation noise `r=0.05`, verification region V = cells 24–31 (x∈[0.61,0.79]), corridor sites = cells 0–19 (x<0.5), `k=3` greedy observations. Pure Python; every number is from `experiments/results.txt`. Closed-loop error is exact linear algebra; Monte Carlo and ensemble/repair rows use 100 replications and report standard errors.*

## Question
Targeted observation asks where a mobile sensor should sample so that an update most reduces forecast error in a region that matters later (Bishop, Etherton & Majumdar 2001). The planner needs the forecast error covariance, which in practice comes from a simulator ensemble: a digital twin of the atmosphere. The gap between the twin's covariance and reality then decides both where to fly and how much to trust the update. How much does a wrong covariance cost, and which errors are dangerous?

## Model
Real error `x~N(0,P_r)`, twin covariance `P_t`. Observation `y=x_j+ε`, `ε~N(0,r)`. The twin plans greedily by its own predicted verification trace `Σ_{i∈V}(P_t)_{ii}` after the update and applies the gain `K=P_t[:,j]/(P_t[jj]+r)`. The real error covariance is `P_r − K P_r[j,:] − P_r[:,j]K' + (P_r[jj]+r)KK'`, so the per-cell real benefit of one observation is `2K_i P_r[ij] − K_i²(P_r[jj]+r)`, positive iff the twin gain is below twice the optimal gain `K*_i=P_r[ij]/(P_r[jj]+r)`. For equal variances and kernels `exp(−(d/ℓ)^p)` the gain ratio is `exp(d^p(ℓ_r^{−p}−ℓ_t^{−p}))`, which passes 2 at the harm radius `d_h=(ln2/(ℓ_r^{−p}−ℓ_t^{−p}))^{1/p}` (infinite if `ℓ_t≤ℓ_r`).

## Results
1. **Exact, and checked.** Joseph-form trace versus 20,000-draw simulation of the closed loop: 7.572 vs 7.674±0.067 (`ℓ_t=0.1`), 7.619 vs 7.644±0.069 (`ℓ_t=0.3`). The scanned crossing of the gain ratio through 2 brackets the formula (`ℓ_t=0.25/0.30/0.40`: formula 0.278/0.223/0.192 vs grid crossings in 0.275–0.300, 0.200–0.225, 0.175–0.200), and the benefit changes sign at the crossing cell. A matched twin claims exactly what it delivers.
2. **Over-correlated twins claim large gains and deliver harm.** No observation: real trace 8.008. Matched twin (picks cells 19, 16, 11): 6.249 (−22%). `ℓ_t` 0.05/0.10/0.15: real change −0.0/−5.5/−16.6%, conservative (twin claims 8.008/7.906/7.263). `ℓ_t=0.3`: twin claims 4.224, real 7.619 (−4.9%), and real error *rises* with each further observation (7.274→7.436→7.619). `ℓ_t=0.4`: claims 2.828, real 10.288 (**+28.5%**; already +0.1% after the first observation). The twin's claimed gain is monotone in `ℓ_t` while the real gain peaks at the truth, so a twin cannot be tuned by its own claim. Planning is less sensitive than the gain: picks are cells 19 and then 16/17/15/13 for `ℓ_t` 0.15–0.4 versus 19, 16, 11 for the oracle; harm comes from the update.
3. **Amplitude errors: benign once, dangerous repeatedly.** Twin `=c·P_r`. A single observation has gain ratio at most `(s²+r)/s²=1.05` whatever `c`, and every `c` from 0.1 to 100 gives real trace 6.78 after one observation (oracle 6.779). Over three observations an over-confident twin (`c` small) loses some benefit (c=0.1: 6.744 vs 6.249) and an under-confident one double-counts: c=4: 6.808, c=100: 16.550 (twice the no-observation error), because it never registers that the first update already used the information. Sequential use needs the twin's reported posterior to track the applied gain; variance error is cheap in one shot and expensive in a sequence.
4. **Misplaced variance.** Real error has a hot spot at x=0.40 (no observation 2.010; oracle 1.563, −22%). A flat-variance twin of the right ℓ: +11.6% (first-site gain ratio 1.86, then compounding). A twin with the hot spot at x=0.10 (far from V): −17.2%, since it still picks nearby sites; a twin with it inside V (x=0.70): **+85.4%**. Hot-spot position errors that reach the verification region are the dangerous ones.
5. **A finite ensemble from the correct model already hurts.** 100 sample covariances per size; real trace after 3 observations (no observation 8.008, oracle 6.249): M=5: 17.415±1.658 (95% harmful), M=10: 10.193±0.329 (71%), M=20: 7.760±0.110 (38%), M=50: 6.717±0.032 (0%), M=100: 6.466, M=400: 6.298. The twin's claims at M=5/10/20 (1.87/3.89/4.99) are far below reality: spurious long-range correlations look like information. Gaussian localisation with taper `c=0.3` gives M=5: 7.466±0.150 (19% harm), M=10: 6.879 (8%), M=20: 6.685 (1%), M=50: 6.515, but at M=400 it is worse than no taper (6.449 vs 6.298) because it discards true correlation.
6. **Repair from real field samples.** Fitting `(ℓ,σ²)` from lag-1 statistics of m realisations (correct family assumed): mean `ℓ̂`=0.275/0.249/0.219/0.206/0.195/0.196 for m=3/5/10/20/50/200 (sd 0.214→0.012); mean real trace 8.121/7.552/6.972/6.643/6.341/6.276, harm 24/17/10/4/0/0%. A parametric fit needs far fewer samples than a covariance ensemble of the same accuracy (m=20 beats M=20 untapered), but only when the family is right.

## Limitations
One static update with no dynamics, so no forecast-model error or error growth; stationary 1-D field with one kernel family; point observations at known sites; greedy planning; no travel cost or UAV kinematics; repair assumes the kernel family and known observation noise; all errors are Gaussian. The ensemble row uses the true covariance as the ensemble's source, the best case for a twin. Not evidence about any real forecast system.

## Next steps
Add dynamics (an error-growth model between targeting and verification); non-Gaussian fields; a twin with the wrong kernel shape; adaptive flight paths with travel cost; a scoring-rule audit of the twin's claimed posterior variance (`twin-audit`), which this result suggests is the decisive check; shrinkage estimators for small ensembles.
