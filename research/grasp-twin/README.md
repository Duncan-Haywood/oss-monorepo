# How firmly to grip: what a too-clean friction twin does to a grasp planned in simulation

Pure Python, no dependencies. A two-finger pinch on a fragile object (lab glassware) holds iff `μF ≥ w0`, where `w0 = W(1+a)/2` is the minimum load per finger, and crushes it iff `F > F_c`. With log-normal friction (`ln μ ~ N(m, s²)`) and log-normal crush force (`ln F_c ~ N(f, r²)`), the expected loss in `x = ln F` (force in units of `w0`) is `J(x) = L_s·Φ((−x−m)/s) + L_c·Φ((x−f)/r)`, and its stationarity condition is a quadratic in `x` (closed form, checked against a numeric search to 7 digits and against 400k-grasp Monte Carlo). Base case (chosen, not measured): real `μ = 0.5`, `s = 0.3`, crush at 6 `w0` with `r = 0.15`, `L_s = L_c = 1`; optimum `F* = 4.05 w0`, `J* = 0.0137`. Results, all from `experiments/results.txt`: a twin with too little friction variability squeezes too little and *claims* almost no risk while the real slip rate climbs (twin spread 0.1 vs 0.3: twin says `J = 1.1e-5`, real `J = 0.069`, 4.0× the optimum; spread 0.05: 11.6×); as the spread goes to zero the twin's optimum becomes a plateau where an arbitrary tie-break decides between real slip 0.50 (lower edge) and 0.015 (upper edge). A biased twin friction costs `≈ ½J''(x*)Δx²` (2% of `J*` at ±0.05, 15% at +0.15, 57% at +0.3, and more for pessimistic bias: 85% at −0.3). Widening the twin's spread (domain randomisation) can cancel a known bias exactly (width 0.47 for `+0.3`), but the width that cancels `+0.3` costs 0.020 regret (1.5× `J*`) if the bias is really `−0.3`, so it is not a robust fix. Plug-in force from `n` real friction measurements has regret matched by a delta-method formula to within 5% for `n ≥ 50` (over-predicts at small `n`, e.g. 0.48× at `n=5`): 0.0109 (n=5), 0.0039 (10), 0.00055 (50), 0.00003 (1000). See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

```bash
cd research/grasp-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 7 tests, ~1 s

```

**Builds on.** The manipulation-workcell, sim-to-real-for-manipulation and robots-in-chemistry-labs directions of the HIRO Group (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>); no specific paper from that lab is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by it. Friction cone and force-closure grasp conditions are textbook (Murray, Li & Sastry 1994, *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*, CRC Press); domain randomisation follows Tobin et al. 2017 (*IROS*, arXiv:1703.06907) and Peng et al. 2018 (*ICRA*, arXiv:1710.06537). Companion to the twin projects `twin-transfer`, `randomized-twin`, `occupancy-twin`, `handover-twin`.

Stylised: a static friction threshold (no slip dynamics, no contact compliance, no sensing or reactive re-grasp), independent log-normal friction and crush force, known object weight and transport acceleration, a single force and no real grasp data (all parameters are chosen values, not measurements).
