# Sag twin: a battery twin with no internal resistance certifies every burst and overstates endurance, what does the real cell allow?

*Stylised: a one-cell lumped model (open-circuit curve plus a series resistance), pure Python, every number is from `experiments/results.txt` (deterministic apart from the seeded Monte Carlo in item 7, about 10 s). The "real" system is itself simulated; no measured cell data, and the droop and cutoff values are illustrative per-cell magnitudes, not a datasheet.*

## Question
Energy models in UAV and mobile-robot twins usually treat the battery as an ideal energy tank: power `P` drains charge at `P/e(s)` where `e(s)` is the open-circuit voltage. A real cell has a series resistance `R`, so the terminal voltage sags under load, the same power needs more current, and the pack hits its voltage cutoff with charge still inside. `uav-energy-twin` and `roundtrip-twin` model the power the vehicle needs but state that they have no discharge nonlinearity. What does the resistance-free twin get wrong about endurance, about the largest burst of power (a climb, a gust, a landing flare), and about how much reserve to keep, and does refitting a constant derate fix it?

## Model
Real: open-circuit voltage `e(s) = 1 − d(1−s)` (units of the full-charge EMF `E0`, `s` the state of charge), resistance `R(s) = R0(1 + ρ(1−s))`, load at constant power `P`, cutoff `v ≥ v_c`. Nondimensionalise with `u0 = 4R0P/E0²` (for a flat EMF `u0 = 1` is the maximum-power point `E0²/4R0`), current `i = IR0/E0`, time in units `R0Q/E0` (`Q` the charge capacity), so `ds/dt = −i`. Terminal voltage `v = e − r i` and `v i = u0/4` give `r i² − e i + u0/4 = 0`, `i = (e − √(e² − r u0))/(2r)`, which has no real root past the fold `e² < r u0`. Twin: the same open-circuit curve and `R = 0`, `i_tw = u0/(4e)`, so it keeps the right EMF and drops only the resistance. For a flat EMF with no cutoff the real endurance over the twin's is `η(u) = (1 + √(1−u))/2` exactly (the terminal-voltage ratio `v/e`). The cutoff `v = v_c` means `i = u0/(4v_c)` and `e − ri = v_c`; both are linear in `x = 1−s`, so the cutoff state is closed form, `s_cut = 1 − (1 − v_c − k)/(d + ρk)` with `k = u0/(4v_c)` (clipped at 0), and the largest power deliverable at state `s` is `4v_c(e(s) − v_c)/r(s)`. Defaults: `d = 0.25`, `v_c = 0.70`, `ρ = 0` unless stated. Endurance is midpoint quadrature of `ds/i`, cross-checked by an independent Euler time-stepper.

## Results
1. **Flat EMF.** Real/twin endurance 0.987, 0.947, 0.887, 0.816, 0.724, 0.612, 0.500 at `u0` = 0.05, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 0.95, 1 (quadrature equals `η(u)` to four digits), so the twin overstates endurance by 1.01 to 2.00×. At `u0 = 1.05` the real cell cannot deliver the power at all (no root) while the twin quotes 3.81.
2. **Droop and cutoff.** Real/twin endurance 0.983, 0.966, 0.862, 0.715, 0.571, 0.298, 0.047 at `u0` = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8; the pack stops with 0, 0, 8.6%, 22.9%, 37.1%, 65.7%, 94.3% of its charge unused. The Euler simulation matches the quadrature to 3 decimals (e.g. 15.079 vs 15.079 at `u0 = 0.2`). A power of 0.84 (`4v_c(1−v_c)`) is the most that can start from a full cell; the twin quotes an endurance of 3.89 at `u0 = 0.9`.
3. **Burst floor.** The largest deliverable power falls linearly with charge: 0.840, 0.700, 0.560, 0.420, 0.280, 0.140 at `s` = 1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4, 0.2, 0. A burst of `u2` = 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7 fails below `s` = 8.6%, 37.1%, 51.4%, 65.7%, 80.0% (the twin's floor is 0 for all). A flat 20% charge reserve covers bursts only up to `u2 = 0.28`, a third of the full-charge limit.
4. **Cruise before a burst.** At cruise `u1 = 0.2` (twin 17.5, real 15.08 to cutoff), keeping the burst deliverable leaves 13.44, 10.85, 8.57, 6.18, 3.68 of cruise time for `u2` = 0.28, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7: 77%, 62%, 49%, 35%, 21% of the twin's figure.
5. **Cold.** Multiplying `R0` by `k` at fixed physical power (`u0` = 0.2 warm): real/twin endurance 0.862, 0.476, 0.286, 0.099, 0.012 at `k` = 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, and at `k = 4.5` (`u0 = 0.9`) the pack cannot start. Maximum power from a full cell scales as `1/k`. The multipliers are round numbers, not measured temperature coefficients.
6. **A constant derate does not fix it.** Fitting `η_fit` (real/twin endurance) at one power and predicting `η_fit × twin` elsewhere: fitted at `u0 = 0.1` (`η_fit = 0.966`) the error is +12%, +35%, +124%, +473%, +1978% at `u0` = 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.8, and it predicts 3.76 where the cell cannot start at 0.9; fitted at 0.3 (`η_fit = 0.715`) it is −27% at 0.05 and +65%, +324% at 0.5, 0.7. It is exact only at the fit power.
7. **The resistance fix is cheap.** Least squares through the origin of `e − v` on `i` recovers `R0` with sd 0.0187, 0.0094, 0.0048 (relative) from 10, 40, 160 samples at voltage noise 0.005 `E0` over a log mixing `u0 = 0.1` and `0.4` segments, matching `σ/√Σi²` (0.0190, 0.0095, 0.0048); mean 0.9996 to 1.0000. Unlike a hidden thermal or friction state, the sag is visible at once in the terminal voltage, so this is a cheap twin to fix, provided someone logs voltage and current at high power.
8. **Out-of-model: resistance that grows as the cell empties.** A twin with constant `R0` fitted at full charge, against `ρ` = 1 and 2: it over-predicts endurance by 8.8% and 22.2% at `u0 = 0.1`, 42.3% and 84.6% at `u0 = 0.3`, and its burst-0.5 floor stays at 51.4% against the real 71.7% and 80.0%. So a full-charge fit is not enough even in the right model family; the resistance has to be fitted across the state of charge.

## Limitations
One cell (no pack imbalance, no thermal coupling of heating `I²R` back into `R`), a linear open-circuit curve and linear `R(s)` (real cells have a plateau and a knee, and a polarisation RC branch with its own transient), constant-power loads (motor power for a multirotor depends on thrust and so on voltage), no rate-capacity (Peukert-like) effects beyond those the resistance implies, no ageing, a hard cutoff with no controller response (real vehicles throttle back as voltage falls). The "real" cell is simulated, so model-form error against a real pack is untested. Not evidence about a particular battery.

## Next steps
Add a one-RC polarisation branch and test whether a short high-power log identifies it; couple the heating `I²R` to temperature and resistance (with `thermal-twin`) and look for a thermal-electrical fold; replace constant-power with a rotor-thrust load so sag raises current and power together; use `uav-energy-twin`'s wind distribution with this battery to size the return-to-home reserve; fit `e(s)`, `R(s)` from logged flights.
