## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3b: finite-sample intervals (planned before running)
- Why: H1 failed at milestone 3 because plug-in t intervals undercover on bounded, left-skewed outcomes (real-only itself reaches only 91.2% at n = 30). Question: does a resampling interval repair coverage for `cv` and `ppi_pp`, and at what width cost?
- Intervals compared, same pools and the same replication design as `analyse.py` (n real+twin pairs, m = 9n twin-only contexts, drawn without replacement from the 4,000-context pool; truth is the pool mean of R): `t` (existing plug-in), `boot_pct` (percentile bootstrap, pairs and extras resampled separately), `boot_t` (studentised bootstrap, plug-in standard error recomputed in every resample, lam re-estimated in every resample). B = 399, 2,000 replications per pair, n in {30, 60}, 21 pairs, methods `cv` and `ppi_pp`.
- **H1b:** the studentised bootstrap interval for `cv` has coverage of at least 93.0% on every one of the 21 pairs at n = 30. Refuted if any pair is below 93.0%. The Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.5 points at 2,000 replications, so pairs in 92.5 to 93.5 are reported as close calls. Prior: partial support; I expect it to repair most of the 1 to 4 point twin-specific loss but not all of the skew-driven loss at n = 30, so some payload-mass pairs may still miss.
- **H1c:** mean width of `boot_t` over `t` is at most 1.15 on every pair (the repair is not bought with intervals more than 15% wider). Refuted otherwise.
- Not tested here: a distribution-free bound (empirical Bernstein) for a fixed lam; with an estimated lam it is not valid as written, so it is left for the certification milestone.

### Outcome (21 pairs, 2,000 replications per cell, B = 399; `experiments/m3b_intervals/results/`; MC s.e. about 0.5 points)
- **H1b refuted as pre-registered, but most of the gap closes.** Studentised bootstrap for `cv` at n = 30: mean coverage 93.6% (plug-in t: 92.0%; percentile bootstrap: 90.7%), minimum 90.8% (payload-mass-3, the most skewed pair); 8 of 21 pairs are below 93.0 (13 are at or above), 17 of 21 are at or above 92.5, i.e. within one MC s.e. of the floor. For `ppi_pp` the minimum is 91.1% and 7 of 21 pairs are below 93.0. At n = 60 the `cv` minimum is 92.4% (3 of 21 below 93.0) and the `ppi_pp` minimum 92.95% (1 of 21).
- **The percentile bootstrap is worse than the plug-in t** (every pair below 93.0 at n = 30, mean 90.7% for `cv`): with 30 skewed observations it inherits the skew and the small-sample bias of resampling without correcting the studentisation. Only the studentised version helps.
- **H1c refuted.** The cost is width: `boot_t` over `t` averages 1.14 (`cv`) and 1.19 (`ppi_pp`) at n = 30 and reaches 1.30 and 1.33 on mass-3; at n = 60 the mean is 1.08 and 1.12. So the repair is real but not free, and the residual undercoverage sits on exactly the pairs with the skewest outcome, where a bootstrap-t is known to be weakest.
- Conclusion for the programme: use the studentised bootstrap as the working interval from n = 60; at n = 30 no resampling interval here reaches nominal on bounded, skewed outcomes. A guarantee needs a distribution-free construction (empirical Bernstein or a betting confidence sequence, with lam fixed or sample-split), which is the certification milestone, and it will be wider still. Not yet tested: BCa, or a transformation (logit of the bounded outcome) before the interval.
- Limitations: same pools as milestone 3 (one task, one outcome, truth is the pool mean of 4,000 contexts, resampling without replacement from that pool); model-versus-model pairs.
- Next: milestone 4 (SIMPLER paired tables), or a distribution-free interval for H3 first.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3: benchmark v1, MuJoCo pairs (planned before running the estimators)
- Built `experiments/m3_benchmark/pairs.py`: a torque-driven pendulum arm in MuJoCo (RK4, 5 ms steps, 3 s) tracking a sinusoid with PD plus gravity feedforward. The twin is the nominal model; the "real" model adds an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck disturbance torque (so the paired correlation is below 1 even with no omitted physics) and one omitted effect (joint friction, command delay, sensor noise, actuator gain shortfall, payload-mass error) at one of four swept magnitudes, plus a no-effect pair: 21 pairs. Magnitudes are swept, not fitted, so these are model-versus-model pairs; no datasheet is claimed. Before the magnitudes were set, a 40-context probe gave a paired correlation of 0.999 for every pair, so the disturbance and magnitudes were raised until the pairs spread over roughly 0.25 to 0.94 (a change made to get an informative benchmark, looking only at the correlation and not at any estimator's coverage).
- Population: 4,000 contexts per pair, one twin run and one real run each; the truth for a pair is the mean real outcome over the 4,000.
- Pre-registered analysis (`analyse.py`, 4,000 replications per pair, n real+twin pairs and m = 9n extra twin-only contexts, drawn without replacement): 
  - **H1:** coverage of the 95% intervals of `cv` and `ppi_pp` at n = 30. Refuted if either is below 93.0% (nominal minus 2 points) on any pair. The Monte Carlo standard error of a coverage figure is about 0.34 points, so a pair at 93.0 to 93.7 is a close call and is reported as such. `ppi` and `real_only` are reported for contrast.
  - **H2:** E = variance of real-only divided by variance of the estimator, over the replications. Paired uses the context-matched twin run; unpaired uses twin runs on independent contexts (same n, same extra runs, same estimator). Supported on a pair if the lower 95% bootstrap bound of E_paired / E_unpaired is at least 2; refuted if that interval includes 1 on most pairs. I expect pairs with rho below about 0.5 to give a ratio near 1, since the gain is 1/(1 - rho^2).
  - **H6:** across the 21 pairs, Spearman correlation of E (cv, n = 30) with (a) detectability, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between the marginals of R and T, and (b) the paired correlation rho. H6 is supported if (b) exceeds (a) in absolute value with the bootstrap-over-pairs interval of the difference excluding 0. Refuted if detectability predicts E as well as rho does. Honest prior: E is a near-monotone function of rho by theory, so (b) should be large; whether (a) is small is the open part, since the omitted effects here shift both the mean and the correlation.

### Outcome (21 pairs, 4,000 contexts each, 4,000 replications per pair and n; `experiments/m3_benchmark/results/`)
- **Sanity check against theory.** Measured E of the control variate at n = 30, N = 300 is within 6% of the closed form 1/(1 - 0.9 rho^2) on every pair (mean 3.8% below, the cost of estimating lam). Paired correlations span 0.17 to 0.88.
- **H1 refuted as pre-registered.** At n = 30 the 95% intervals of `cv` and `ppi_pp` fall below 93.0% on 15 and 20 of 21 pairs (worst 88.8% and 86.7%, on the payload-mass pairs). The cause is not only the twin: the real-only t interval also undercovers (minimum 91.2%) because the outcome is bounded and strongly left-skewed (skewness -2.4 on the worst pairs), and the twin estimators lose a further 1 to 4 points on top of that. At n = 60 the minima are 92.4% (real-only), 91.0% (`cv`), 89.8% (`ppi_pp`), so the shortfall shrinks but remains. `ppi` (lam = 1) is not better. Conclusion: plug-in t intervals are not a valid basis for H1; the finite-sample interval the proposal calls for (milestone 3b) is needed.
- **H2 supported.** Paired E over unpaired E has a lower 95% bound of at least 2 on 13 of 21 pairs and an interval that includes 1 on none. It fails the 2x bar exactly where rho is below about 0.75 (E between 1.0 and 1.9). Unpaired twin runs never help: E is 0.96 to 0.98 on every pair, a loss of 2 to 4% from estimating lam on uncorrelated data.
- **H6 mixed.** Spearman(E, rho) = 0.999, but Spearman(E, KS detectability) = -0.954 and with the standardised mean shift -0.855. The interval of |rho| minus |KS| over pairs is [0.008, 0.146], so rho predicts value significantly better, yet detectability also predicts it well: the claim that it does not is refuted on this benchmark. Reason: every omitted effect here both shifts the outcome distribution and decorrelates the twin. The benchmark has no highly detectable, highly correlated pair (a pure offset), which is where H6 would be tested properly.
- Limitations: model-versus-model pairs with swept, not sourced, magnitudes; one task; one outcome; context-paired with a deterministic twin only.
- Next: milestone 3b, a finite-sample interval (studentised bootstrap and an empirical-Bernstein bound) re-tested on the same pools; add offset-type pairs for H6; then milestone 4 (SIMPLER).

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 2 part 1: consolidated estimator library
- Hypothesis written before running: the three projects' closed forms are special cases of one family `mean(R) - lam (mean(T_paired) - mean(T_all))`, so one library with `lam` in {0, 1, power-tuned, OLS} should reproduce their numbers, and PPI (`lam` = 1) should lose to real-only when the twin's sensitivity differs from reality's (twin scale `g` = 2).
- Result: `src/sim2real_inference/` (stdlib only) and 11 tests pass. All four estimators are unbiased against a twin bias of 0.7; the exact fixed-`lam` variance `var_ppi` equals `var_cv` at the optimal `lam`, and matches simulation within 7%; the 95% intervals of PPI, PPI++ and CV cover above 92% at n = 30, N = 300. With `g` = 2, PPI's variance exceeds real-only's while CV's is below it, which is the first concrete case for tuning `lam` (H1 groundwork, not yet a result on benchmark pairs).
- Caught on the way: a test I wrote used the noise-free correlation for a noisy twin and failed at variance ratio 1.50; the code was right, the test was wrong.
- Not done: the synthesis table of what the ~130 `*-twin` projects say about `rho`, and `twin-pilot`'s two-stage procedure is not ported. Next: the synthesis, then milestone 3 (MuJoCo pairs).
