## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6d: is |R - T| a lower-variance target than R? (planned before running)
- Why: 6c's open question. A mean-discrepancy bound L = E[T] - E|R - T| needs an estimate of E|R - T|. It is only cheaper than estimating E[R] if the per-context variance of |R - T| (or its range) is below that of R.
- Check (`experiments/m6d_target_variance/target_var.py`, planned before running), on the 21 m3 pools (4,000 contexts each): Var(R), Var(R - T), Var(|R - T|), and the ratio Var(|R - T|) / Var(R); also the empirical-Bernstein half-width at n = 60 and 240 for each target using its own sample range (bounded-range scaling), to see whether the width saving survives the range term.
- **H6d-i:** Var(|R - T|) < Var(R) on at least 15 of 21 pairs when rho is high (>= 0.8), and on fewer than 10 of 21 across all pairs, because low-rho pairs have a discrepancy as noisy as R itself. Refuted if the split is not like this (both thresholds must hold).
- **H6d-ii:** on one-sided pairs |R - T| = T - R, so Var(|R - T|) = Var(R - T) and the variance ratio equals the control-variate residual ratio 1 - rho-type quantity already measured in m3; checked as an identity on the pool, tolerance 1e-9.
- Not tested: per-episode real pairs (still the main gap), a valid interval for E|R - T| plus the twin mean (that is 3e).

### Outcome (21 pools; `experiments/m6d_target_variance/results/`, 2 tests)
- **H6d-i refuted** (the second threshold failed): Var(|R - T|) < Var(R) on 20 of 21 pairs overall (all 12 with rho >= 0.8), median ratio 0.30. Only delay-3 (rho 0.17) has the discrepancy noisier than R (ratio 1.19). I expected low-rho pairs to lose more often; they mostly do not, because |R - T| is bounded below by 0 and the twin's own variation cancels in the difference.
- **H6d-ii supported but narrow:** only 2 of 21 pairs are strictly one-sided, and there Var(|R - T|) = Var(R - T) exactly (gap 0). 6c's "one-sided" wording should read "nearly one-sided" for the other 19.
- Empirical-Bernstein half-width (own sample range) is smaller for |R - T| than for R on 18/21 pairs at n = 60 and 20/21 at n = 240. So a bound E[R] >= E[T] - (upper confidence bound on E|R - T|) costs fewer real runs than the real-only bound, at a price: it adds the one-sided slack E|R - T| - E[T - R] (zero only when the discrepancy is one-sided), which 3e's construction already pays. This is the same trade as the control variate with lam = 1, so no new estimator results; the variance gain is the reason 3e's twin-assisted gap can approach real-only but not beat it where the discrepancy is two-sided.
- Limitations: one benchmark family (model pairs, not real robots); variance computed on the 4,000-context pool, not a finite-sample interval; EB constants are the Maurer-Pontil ones at delta = 0.05.
- Next: per-episode real pairs remain the main gap for a submittable paper.


## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6c: a quantile or mean discrepancy bound instead of the maximum (planned before running)
- Why: 6b's next step. The oracle maximum |R - T| is 9 to 16 times the real-only gap, so a zero-real-run bound loses. Does an assumption on a quantile, or on the mean of |R - T|, change that?
- **Ceiling lemma.** If P(|R - T| > D_q) <= q with D_q in [0, 1] known, and R, T in [0, 1], then E[T - R] <= E[(T - R)+] <= (1 - q) D_q + q, so L_q = E[T] - (1 - q) D_q - q is valid with no real runs. It is tight in the class (T = 1; R = 1 - D_q with probability 1 - q, R = 0 with probability q, up to the boundary case). If instead E|R - T| <= dbar is known, L = E[T] - dbar is valid (Jensen-free: E[T - R] <= E|R - T|).
- Check (`experiments/m6c_quantile/quantile.py`, planned before running), on the 21 m3 pools, ORACLE inputs (the true quantile and mean over the 4,000 contexts; no procedure can know them, so this flatters the twin): for q in {0.01, 0.05, 0.10} the gap_q = mean(R) - L_q, and for the mean assumption gap_mean = mean(R) - (mean(T) - mean|R - T|). Compare with the real-only empirical-Bernstein gap of 3e at n = 30, 60, 120, 240.
- **H6c-i:** all bounds are valid on 21/21 pairs (deterministic consequences). Refuted if any pair has mean(R) < L.
- **H6c-ii:** the oracle mean-discrepancy bound beats real-only EB at n = 240 on fewer than 5 of 21 pairs, because the gap of any such bound is at least mean|R - T|, which is not small even at rho 0.87 (prior: median mean|R - T| about 0.1 to 0.2 against an EB gap of 0.06). Refuted if 5 or more win.
- **H6c-iii:** the quantile bounds are no better than the mean bound for q at or above 0.05, since the additive q term (0.05) already uses up most of the n = 240 budget. Refuted if any q-bound wins on more pairs than the mean bound at n = 240.
- Not tested: a data-dependent estimate of the quantile with valid adjustment (the 3e route), real-robot data.

### Outcome (21 pools; `experiments/m6c_quantile/results/`, 3 tests)
- **H6c-i supported:** all four oracle bounds valid on 21/21 pairs.
- **H6c-ii refuted, for a reason that is an oracle artefact.** With the oracle mean E|R - T| (0.063 to 0.73, median 0.10) the bound L = E[T] - E|R - T| has gap 0.000 to 0.002 on all 21 pairs and beats real-only EB on 21/21 at every n. This is because on this benchmark the twin omits an effect that only ever lowers the real outcome, so R <= T nearly everywhere and E|R - T| = E[T - R] = the exact bias. Knowing E|R - T| is therefore as good as knowing E[R], and no procedure has it without real data: estimating a mean of |R - T| to within the 0.06 that real-only EB achieves at n = 240 takes real pairs of the same order. The result says that a mean-discrepancy assumption is worth exactly what it costs to certify, not that it is cheap.
- **Quantile bounds do not pay once n is moderate:** gap median 0.235 (q = 0.01), 0.144 (0.05), 0.152 (0.10); they beat real-only EB on 15, 20, 21 of 21 pairs at n = 30 but 4, 15, 17 at n = 60 and 0 at n >= 120. The additive q term plus (1 - q) D_q is a floor near 0.12 to 0.15 whatever the twin quality. **H6c-iii supported** (no q-bound beats the mean bound at n = 240).
- Reading with 6b: worst-case D loses (9 to 16 times the real-only gap), a quantile bound loses beyond n of about 60, and a mean bound wins only as an oracle. The zero-real-run route is closed unless D is estimated from real data, which is milestone 3e's construction; the useful open question is a cheaper estimate of E|R - T| than E[R] itself, which needs a lower-variance target than R, i.e. |R - T| variance versus R variance (not tested here).
- Limitations: oracle inputs on one benchmark whose discrepancy is one-sided; delta = 0.05; one-sided bound.
- Next: per-episode real pairs (milestone 7 gap 1) remain the main gap for a submittable paper; a variance comparison of |R - T| and R as targets.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6b: the floor scales with a known discrepancy bound D, and a zero-real-run ceiling (theory plus an oracle check; planned before running)
- Why: milestone 6 left open an instance-wise statement involving the discrepancy. Two things follow from the same Le Cam pair, and both can be checked on the 21 m3 pools.
- **Corollary (D-floor).** Restrict to laws with |R - T| <= D almost surely (D in (0, 1], R and T in [0, 1]). Then any lower bound L valid at level 1 - delta over that class, using m pairs and any number of twin-only runs, has a law in the class with a perfect twin where P(L <= 1 - D eps*) >= 1/2, eps* = 1 - (2 delta)^(1/m). Proof: as milestone 6 with P1: T = 1, R = 1 - D with probability eps (so mu1 = 1 - eps D and |R - T| <= D), P0: R = T = 1; data are identical with probability (1 - eps)^m, and validity under P1 gives delta >= (1 - eps)^m P0(L > 1 - eps D). Take eps = eps*.
- **Ceiling.** If D is known in advance and the twin law is known (unlimited twin runs), L0 = E[T] - D is valid with zero real runs, gap D. So a known D buys a gap between D eps* and D, independent of m beyond the floor, and the twin helps exactly when D is below what real data alone reach (3e's cost was estimating D from the data, which is what gamma paid for).
- Check (`experiments/m6b_dfloor/dfloor.py`, planned before running): for each of the 21 pools take the oracle D = max |R - T| over the 4,000 contexts (an oracle: no procedure can know it; disclosed, and it flatters the twin), L0 = mean(T) - D, gap0 = mean(R) - L0, and compare with the measured real-only empirical-Bernstein gap of 3e at n = 30, 60, 120, 240 (`m3e_bounded_disc/results/bounded.csv`).
- **H6b-i:** L0 is valid on every pair (gap0 >= 0) by construction; refuted if any pair has mean(R) < L0.
- **H6b-ii:** even with the oracle D, L0 has a smaller gap than real-only EB at n = 240 on fewer than 5 of 21 pairs, because the max of |R - T| over 4,000 contexts is far above the typical discrepancy. Refuted if 5 or more pairs win.
- **H6b-iii:** D below which a zero-real-run bound beats real-only EB is D* = EB gap(n), about 0.06 at n = 240, 0.36 at n = 30; report the pairs' D against it.
- Not tested: a data-dependent D with a valid adjustment (that is 3e), real-robot D.

### Outcome (21 pools; `experiments/m6b_dfloor/results/`, 3 tests)
- **H6b-i supported:** L0 valid on 21/21 pairs (it is a deterministic consequence of |R - T| <= D).
- **H6b-ii supported, more strongly than predicted:** the oracle D = max |R - T| is 0.55 to 0.99 (median 0.72) even at rho 0.87, so L0 has gap 0.26 to 0.73 (median 0.60) and beats real-only empirical Bernstein on 1/21 pairs at n = 30, and on 0/21 at n = 60, 120 and 240 (mean real-only gap 0.363, 0.193, 0.106, 0.060).
- **H6b-iii:** the break-even D is the real-only gap, 0.054 to 0.081 at n = 240; the pools' worst-case D is 9 to 16 times that. A worst-case discrepancy bound is the wrong assumption for a twin whose typical error is small but whose tail is not: a certificate that pays needs a bound on a quantile or a mean of |R - T|, not the maximum, which then needs real data to check (the 3e route).
- **Reading.** Together with milestone 6: the floor is D x 2.3/m, a known D buys at most a factor D over the worst case, and a zero-run ceiling D only wins for D below the real-only gap. Both the floor and the ceiling are far from the 3e construction (gap 0.19 at n = 240), so the open question is a tail-aware discrepancy assumption, not a better concentration inequality.
- Limitations: oracle D flatters the twin and still loses; one benchmark; one-sided; delta = 0.05; the floor is a worst-case statement over one pair of laws.
- Next: replace the maximum with a quantile or an Orlicz-type tail assumption (R - T sub-Gaussian with a known proxy) and derive the matching floor; per-episode real pairs (milestone 7 gap 1).

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 6: a distribution-free floor on any twin-assisted lower bound (theory; the numerical check was planned before running)
- Why: 3e, 3f and 5b all found certified procedures that the twin does not help. Is that a flaw of those constructions or a limit on any procedure? A floor tells us how much room is left.
- **Theorem (floor).** Let L be any lower confidence bound for mu = E[R] with P(L <= mu) >= 1 - delta for every joint law of (R, T) on [0, 1]^2, using m paired runs, any number of twin-only runs and any randomisation. Then for eps* = 1 - (2 delta)^(1/m) there is a law with a perfect twin (R = T = 1) under which P(L <= 1 - eps*) >= 1/2.
- Proof. P1: T = 1 and R = 0 with probability eps independently (mu1 = 1 - eps), P0: R = T = 1. The twin-only runs have the same law under both. The paired data are identical under P1 and P0 with probability (1-eps)^m (every real outcome is 1). Validity under P1 gives delta >= P1(L > 1 - eps) >= (1-eps)^m P0(L > 1 - eps). At eps = eps*, (1-eps)^m = 2 delta, so P0(L > 1 - eps*) <= 1/2. QED. For small eps*, eps* is about ln(1/(2 delta))/m.
- Tightness: on the all-ones sample the exact valid bound is delta^(1/m) (gap 1 - delta^(1/m)), so the floor is tight to within about 1.3 times at delta = 0.05 for m between 10 and 240.
- Check (`experiments/m6_floor/floor.py`, 3 tests): Monte Carlo of P1(data = P0 data) matches 2 delta = 0.10 at eps* for m = 10 to 1000 (0.0987 to 0.1016 with 200,000 draws).
- **Reading.** (i) The floor is order 1/m and holds even for a perfect twin: no procedure can certify a gap below about 2.3/m at 95% in the worst case, so a twin cannot buy the sqrt(var/m) rate down to zero and cannot reduce the 1/m term. The floor is 0.0095 at m = 240; 3e's bounded-discrepancy bound had gap 0.193 (20 times the floor) and real-only empirical Bernstein 0.060 (6 times). So 3e's failure was not forced by the problem: there is room for a better construction, though not for one that wins by more than the sqrt term. (ii) In the zero-variance corner, empirical Bernstein's lower-order term is 7 ln(1/delta)/(3(m-1)), 3.1 times the exact bound, so real-only is already within a small factor of optimal there: the twin can win only where the sqrt term dominates (variance of R high, rho high, m large). (iii) What this does not show: an instance-wise (not worst-case) lower bound involving rho. That is the open half of H3.
- Limitations: a minimax statement over one pair of laws; not a bound that depends on rho or on a checked discrepancy assumption; one-sided; delta = 0.05.
- Next: instance-wise lower bound under a bounded-discrepancy assumption (Le Cam with D-tail mass), then the paper (milestone 7).

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 5b: anytime confidence sequences with the twin as control variate (H5) (planned before running)
- Why: 3d found a fixed-n betting interval with a pilot-estimated control variate about 3 times wider than real-only, because rescaling Z = (R - lam T + lam)/(1 + lam) into [0, 1] costs range. H5 is about sequential use: stop collecting real trials as soon as a decision is clear. Here the twin is free on all N = 4,000 contexts, so E[T] is known, and the betting target can be the paired difference D = R - T (mean E[R] - E[T]), whose variance is small when rho is high, which a variance-adaptive bet can exploit without a pilot (lam = 1).
- Design (`experiments/m5b_anytime/anytime.py`), on the 21 m3 pools, R and T known per context, contexts drawn with replacement (iid), truth = pool mean of R, nmax = 300, delta = 0.05. Three hedged-capital confidence sequences (Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2024, predictable plug-in bets, capital for each candidate mean on a 300-point grid, a candidate is out from the first time its capital reaches 1/delta; running intersection): `real` (R in [0, 1]), `cv1` (D = R - T mapped from [-1, 1] to [0, 1], target shifted by the known mean of T), `cvlam` (first 20 trials fit lam by OLS, then the sequence runs on Z with that lam fixed; the 20 count towards n). Decision task: stop at the first t at which the sequence excludes the bar b = truth - g (so the right answer is "mean R is above b"), g in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}; reported: median and mean stopping time censored at nmax and the share that never stop.
- **H5-i (anytime validity):** in 1,000 replications, the share of runs in which truth is outside the sequence at any t <= 300 is at most 5.0% (+ MC s.e.) for every method and pair; the largest tolerated is 6.0%. Refuted if any cell exceeds 6.0%.
- **H5-ii (value):** `cv1` stops sooner than `real` (median stopping time, g = 0.10) on every pair with rho at least 0.8 (7 pairs in the 3c table) and is no more than 20% slower on pairs with rho below 0.5. Refuted if either half fails. Prior: after 3d I expect the first half to hold only on the best two or three pairs; a bounded-range penalty (D spans [-1, 1]) is the risk.
- **H5-iii (pilot versus none):** `cvlam` is not faster than `cv1` in the median over pairs (the 20-trial pilot is a cost). Refuted if `cvlam` is faster in the median over pairs.
- Not tested: mixture (rather than plug-in) bets, a data-dependent nmax, real-robot logs, tighter delta splits.

### Outcome (21 pairs, 1,000 replications per cell, nmax = 300, delta = 0.05; `experiments/m5b_anytime/results/`)
- **H5-i supported.** Truth left the sequence at some t <= 300 in at most 2.5% of runs for `real`, 0.8% for `cv1`, 0.6% for `cvlam` (limit 6.0%). Optional stopping is safe; the sequences are conservative (median miss 0.5%, 0.0%, 0.0%).
- **H5-ii refuted.** `cv1` is slower than `real` on all 12 pairs with rho at least 0.8 (g = 0.10 median stopping time 72 to 76 trials against 50 to 52). Over all pairs the median stopping time at g = 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.20 is 108 / 50 / 22 for `real` and 144 / 74 / 39 for `cv1`, so the twin costs about 1.4 to 1.8 times the real trials. Correction to my pre-registration: 12 pairs, not 7, have rho at least 0.8 (the "7" came from the 3c table's n = 60 subset). The low-rho half cannot be read as written: on `delay-3` (true mean near 0) the bar truth - 0.10 is below zero, so `real` stops at trial 1 and the ratio (79) is an artefact; on `delay-2` (rho 0.37) the ratio is 1.68, over the 20% allowed.
- **H5-iii supported.** `cvlam` is slower than `cv1` (median 133 against 74 at g = 0.10): the 20-trial pilot and the (1 + lam) rescaling both cost.
- Why: D = R - T spans [-1, 1], so the bounded-range penalty seen in 3c and 3d returns; at rho 0.87 the sd of D is still about half that of R, not small enough to pay for twice the range with a plug-in bet. Reading across 3c to 3f and 5b: no certified or anytime-valid procedure tested here makes the twin pay; its value stays in point-estimate variance (milestones 2 and 3).
- Limitations: model-versus-model pairs on one task; one betting construction (plug-in, no mixture), one grid, delta not tuned; iid draws with replacement from a finite pool; bars defined relative to the pool mean.
- Next: a mixture (rather than plug-in) bet that exploits the small variance of D; a range-free target (variance-adaptive bound on D from the observed range); then milestone 6 theory and 7 paper.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 5a: active selection of real trials (H4) (planned before running)
- Why: milestones 3c to 3f showed the twin cannot shorten a certificate. H4 asks about the other use: where to spend the scarce real trials. When the twin's error D = R - T depends on the context (initial state, payload, reference), sampling contexts in proportion to the predicted size of D should cut the variance of the estimate of E[R] at equal real budget.
- Design (`experiments/m5a_active/active.py`), on the 21 m3 pools (4,000 contexts each, regenerated from the benchmark's seed; R and T known for every context, so the target is the pool mean of R). The twin is free on all N = 4,000 contexts. Estimator for every method: theta = mean(T over all N) + (1/n) sum_i D_i / (N pi_i), D_i = R_i - T_i, contexts drawn with replacement with probabilities pi (unbiased for any pi). Methods: `uniform` (pi = 1/N, i.e. PPI with lam = 1), `uniform_cv` (OLS lam on the sample, the m2 estimator), `active` (stage 1: n/3 uniform trials fit gradient boosting of |D| on the six context features, cross-fitted nothing needed because stage 2 is a new draw; stage 2: n - n/3 trials from pi proportional to 0.2 mean(pred) + pred, pred clipped below at a small floor, so pi is at least 0.2/N times the mean-ratio; the two stages are combined by sample-size weights), `oracle` (pi proportional to |D| + 0.05 mean|D|, the unattainable ceiling that uses the real outcomes of the whole pool). Same real budget n for all, n in {30, 60, 120}, 1,000 replications.
- Heteroscedasticity measure, per pair, before the comparison: 5-fold cross-fitted R^2 of gradient boosting for |D| on the context (`het_r2`).
- **H4-i (validity):** the 95% normal interval from the weighted terms covers at least 93% for `active` on every pair at n = 120 (the weights are bounded; small-n coverage is not tested because 20 stage-2 trials are too few for a CLT). Refuted otherwise.
- **H4-ii (value):** at n = 120, `active` has a lower MSE than `uniform` on every pair with het_r2 above 0.3, and is not more than 10% worse on pairs with het_r2 below 0.05. Refuted if either half fails. Prior: gains of 10 to 30% on the few pairs where the omitted effect interacts with the initial state (delay, gain, sensor); about zero or slightly negative elsewhere, because the n/3 pilot costs more than it returns when |D| is unpredictable.
- **H4-iii (cost of learning the sampler):** `active` MSE exceeds `oracle` MSE by at least 25% at n = 60 on pairs with het_r2 above 0.3 (the learning cost is visible). Refuted if within 25%.
- **Amendments before the full run (after a 20-replication smoke test, no result read for the hypotheses).** (a) The `oracle` as first written (pi proportional to |D|) is degenerate: D has one sign on most pairs, so the weighted terms are constant and its MSE is about zero, which is no ceiling. It is replaced by the same gradient-boosting predictor fit on all 4,000 real outcomes (5-fold cross-fitted), i.e. the sampler with an unlimited pilot. (b) Smallest het_r2 is 0.07, so the "below 0.05" tier of H4-ii is empty; the second half becomes: on the pairs with het_r2 below 0.15, `active` is not more than 10% worse than `uniform` at n = 120 in the median over those pairs.
- Not tested: more than two stages, sequential/bandit allocation, a different predictor of |D|, real-robot pools (the SIMPLER tables have no contexts), and the interaction with lam (the active estimator fixes lam = 1).

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3e: a bounded-discrepancy lower bound (planned before running)
- Why: 3c and 3d showed range-based bounds on R - lam T cannot see the twin's variance gain. Route (b) puts the twin to work through a *checked bound on the discrepancy D = R - T*: if |D| <= b with b far below 1, Hoeffding on D has range 2b, not 1 + lam. The bound b must be checked on a pilot, and the check has a price. Question: at what total number of real trials (pilot included) does a one-sided lower confidence bound on E[R] built this way beat the real-only empirical-Bernstein bound?
- Construction (`experiments/m3e_bounded_disc/bounded.py`), outcomes in [0, 1], 21 m3 pools, total miscoverage delta = 0.05 split 0.01 / 0.02 / 0.02. Pilot = first k = n/3 real+twin pairs: b = max |D| over the pilot; then P(|D| > b) <= gamma = 1 - 0.01^(1/k) with confidence 0.99 (distribution-free, order statistics), and clipping D at b biases its mean by at most E[(|D| - b)+] <= gamma (|D| <= 1). Main pairs (n - k): Hoeffding one-sided (delta 0.02) on clip(D, -b, b), range 2b. Twin mean: Hoeffding one-sided (delta 0.02, range 1) on all N = 10n twin runs (the n pairs inside them). L = mean(T_N) - h_T + mean(clip D) - h_D - gamma. Baseline: Maurer-Pontil empirical-Bernstein one-sided bound on the n real outcomes (delta 0.05). Pool draws without replacement, 1,000 replications, n in {30, 60, 120, 240}.
- **H3e-i (validity):** coverage of both lower bounds is at least 95.0% on every pair and n. Refuted if any cell is below (a bug or wrong range).
- **H3e-ii (value, the proposal's H3):** the bounded-discrepancy bound is tighter (mean truth - L smaller) than real-only EB at n = 240 on every pair with rho >= 0.85, and not tighter at n = 30 on any pair (gamma at k = 10 is 0.37). Refuted if it is tighter at n = 30 anywhere or fails to be tighter at n = 240 on any rho >= 0.85 pair. Prior: the crossover sits between n = 60 and n = 240 and only on the highest-rho pairs; gamma, not the twin, is the binding term.
- Not tested: variance-adaptive bounds on D, a quantile (not max) bound for b, tuned delta split.

### Outcome (21 pairs, 1,000 replications per cell, n in {30, 60, 120, 240}; `experiments/m3e_bounded_disc/results/`)
- **H3e-i supported, trivially.** Both lower bounds cover 100.0% on every one of the 84 cells. Valid, and conservative.
- **H3e-ii refuted, on its second half, and so is the proposal's H3 in this benchmark.** The bounded-discrepancy bound is tighter than real-only empirical Bernstein on **0 of 21 pairs at every n**, including n = 240 and the seven pairs with rho at least 0.85. Mean gap (truth minus bound): real-only 0.363 / 0.193 / 0.106 / 0.060 at n = 30 / 60 / 120 / 240; bounded 0.644 / 0.425 / 0.282 / 0.193. The first half (not tighter at n = 30) holds. My prior of a crossover between n = 60 and 240 was wrong.
- **Why (measured).** The pilot maximum of |D| stays large: mean b is 0.26 to 0.36 at n = 240 even for rho of 0.85 to 0.88 (up to 0.97 on the delay-3 pair), because the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck disturbance and the omitted effects make the discrepancy heavy-tailed even when the correlation is high. A range of 2b near 0.5 to 0.7 is no better than the real outcome's range, so Hoeffding on D gains nothing, while the check on b costs gamma = 1 - 0.01^(1/k) (0.369 at n = 30, 0.056 at n = 240) and the twin-mean term costs h_T. At n = 240 the gamma term alone (0.056) already equals real-only EB's whole gap.
- Reading across 3c, 3d, 3e: for outcomes in [0, 1] with sd near 0.1 and a max-based discrepancy bound, no certified bound built from the worst case beats real-only Bernstein at n up to 240. The twin's variance gain (milestones 2 and 3, within 6% of 1/(1 - rho^2)) is real for point estimates and cannot be certified distribution-free at these n by these constructions. H3 as stated in the proposal is refuted for a max-based b. Open: a quantile bound for b with a bias term that uses E[(|D| - b)+] estimated rather than bounded by gamma (needs a second assumption on the tail of D), variance-adaptive bounds on D, and failure-rate (binary) outcomes, where Clopper-Pearson is the baseline and the proposal's H3 was originally stated.
- Limitations: one outcome and task, model-versus-model pairs, max-based b and a fixed 0.01/0.02/0.02 split, Hoeffding rather than Bernstein on D and T.
- Next: milestone 4 (SIMPLER paired tables, real data), and a binary-outcome version of this test against Clopper-Pearson.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3d: a betting interval (planned before running)
- Why: 3c showed empirical-Bernstein intervals hide the twin's variance gain behind their range term. A betting (hedged-capital) confidence interval (Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2024, JRSS-B) adapts to the observed variance, so it may expose the gain while staying finite-sample valid. Question: does a betting interval for the control-variate estimand beat a betting interval on real data alone, on the same 21 pools?
- Construction (`experiments/m3d_betting/betting.py`): scale values to [0, 1]; for each candidate mean m on a 400-point grid, run a hedged capital process with predictable plug-in bets (lambda_t = min(sqrt(2 log(2/delta) / (v_{t-1} t log(t+1))), 0.5/m style cap, per the paper's PrPl-EB recipe), reject m when the mixed capital reaches 1/delta (the first draft of this entry said 2/delta, a slip: delta is already the total miscoverage; corrected before any full run, see outcome); interval = unrejected set. Real-only: betting CI on all n real outcomes, delta = 0.05. Control variate: first n/3 pairs give lam (OLS slope clipped to [0, 1]); betting CI on R - lam T over the other pairs (delta = 0.025, rescaled from [-lam, 1]); betting CI on T over all N = 10n twin runs (delta = 0.025); sum of the two, union bound. Observation order is randomly permuted per replication. Same pools, n in {30, 60}, 1,000 replications.
- **H3d-i (validity):** coverage at least 95.0% for both intervals on every pair and n (MC s.e. about 0.5 points; betting intervals are usually slightly conservative). Refuted if any cell is below 95.0%, which would point to a bug.
- **H3d-ii (value):** control-variate width over real-only width is below 1 on every pair with rho >= 0.85 at n = 60, and above 1 on pairs with rho < 0.3. Refuted if either half fails. Prior: the variance gain should now show, but the union-bound split and the pilot third cost width, so I expect the ratio near 1 at n = 30 and below 1 only at the highest rho.
- Not tested: tuned delta split, a larger pilot share, a joint (non-union) bound.

### Outcome (21 pairs, 1,000 replications per cell, n in {30, 60}; `experiments/m3d_betting/results/`)
- **H3d-i supported, trivially.** Coverage is 100.0% for the control-variate interval on every cell and at least 99.5% for real-only (minimum over 42 cells), at both variance priors tried (0.25, the paper default, and 0.005). Valid, and far too conservative.
- **H3d-ii refuted: the betting interval is no better than Bernstein for this purpose.** Control-variate width over real-only width averages 3.15 at n = 30 and 2.89 at n = 60 (range 1.92 to 3.35), including all seven pairs with rho at least 0.85 at n = 60 (3c: 2.97 to 3.01). The low-rho half of the prediction holds (one pair). Real-only betting widths are 0.293 (n = 30) and 0.159 (n = 60), against a sample sd of 0.1 to 0.15.
- **Why (measured).** The paper's plug-in bet is capped at 1/2 over the candidate mean to keep capital positive on a [0, 1] range. With outcome sd near 0.1 the variance-optimal bet is far above the cap: the cap binds on 99 to 100% of bets at n = 60 (`none-0`: 100%, `delay-3`: 99%, 200 draws each). Capped bets grow capital at a rate set by the range, not the variance, so the interval's width is again range-limited, and changing the variance prior makes no difference (widths agree to three digits). The control-variate version pays range (1 + lam), a split error budget and a pilot third on top.
- Reading across 3c and 3d: any bound whose guarantee leans on the worst-case range [0, 1] hides the twin's variance reduction at n of 30 to 60. Two genuinely different routes remain, both untested: (b) a bound on the paired residual's range that is itself checked on a pilot (a bounded-discrepancy assumption, H3 as stated), and (c) a test that does not need an interval at all (a certification threshold test with a pre-registered discrepancy budget).
- Limitations: one betting construction (hedged capital, PrPl-EB); other bet schedules (e.g. cap 0.9 with a positivity guard, or mixture methods) may do better and were not tried; same single-task pools; 1,000 replications; union-bound split fixed at 50/50.
- Next: milestone 3e, a bounded-discrepancy certification test (route b/c) with the discrepancy bound estimated on a held-out pilot and its failure probability accounted for; then milestone 4.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3c: a distribution-free interval (planned before running)
- Why: milestone 3b showed no resampling interval reaches nominal at n = 30 on bounded, skewed outcomes. A guarantee needs a construction that does not lean on normality. Question: what does a finite-sample-valid interval for the control-variate estimand cost in width, and does the twin still shorten it?
- Construction (`experiments/m3c_dfree/dfree.py`): outcomes lie in [0, 1], so R - lam T lies in [-lam, 1] for lam in [0, 1]. Split the n pairs: the first n1 = n/3 give lam (OLS slope clipped to [0, 1]); the other n2 = n - n1 give A = mean(R - lam T) with an empirical Bernstein bound (Maurer and Pontil 2009, two-sided, delta = 0.025, range 1 + lam); all N = n + m twin runs give B = mean(T) with an empirical Bernstein bound (delta = 0.025, range 1). Estimate A + lam B, half-width the sum of the two. The union bound makes it valid whatever the dependence between the parts. Real-only baseline: empirical Bernstein on all n real outcomes, delta = 0.05. Pool sampling is without replacement, which only tightens the bounds.
- **H3b-i (validity):** coverage of both intervals is at least 95% on every one of the 21 pairs at n = 30 and 60. Refuted if any pair falls below 95.0% (MC s.e. at most 0.5 points at 1,000 replications; coverage will be far above nominal, so a miss would be a bug or a wrong range).
- **H3b-ii (value):** the control-variate EB width over the real-only EB width is below 1 on every pair with paired correlation rho >= 0.85 at n = 60, and above 1 on pairs with rho < 0.3 (the twin costs width when it is uncorrelated). Refuted if either fails. Prior: the Bernstein terms are dominated by the range term at n = 30, so the gain may be small there; I expect the sign of the effect but not its size.
- Not tested: tuning delta between the two parts, a betting confidence sequence, a bounded-discrepancy certification test (H3 proper).

### Outcome (21 pairs, 1,000 replications per cell, n in {30, 60}; `experiments/m3c_dfree/results/`)
- **H3b-i supported, trivially.** Both intervals cover 100.0% on every pair and n (the minimum over all 42 cells). They are valid and far too conservative to be useful as stated.
- **H3b-ii refuted, and the twin costs width.** The control-variate EB interval is 2.1 to 3.1 times as wide as the real-only EB interval on every pair (mean 2.99 at n = 30, 2.89 at n = 60), including the seven pairs with rho at least 0.85 at n = 60 (ratios 2.97 to 3.01). The low-rho half of the prediction (ratio above 1 at rho < 0.3) holds, but only one pair has rho < 0.3 and the high-rho half fails everywhere.
- **Why.** The Bernstein bound is dominated by its range term, not its variance term: on `none-0` at n = 60 the real-only range term is 0.146 against a variance term of 0.027 (sd of R is 0.078). The control-variate construction pays for a wider range (1 + lam), splits the error budget in two (delta 0.025 each), spends a third of the pairs on lam, and adds the bound on the twin mean; none of that is recovered by a variance reduction that the range term hides. So the 1/(1 - rho^2) gain of milestones 2 and 3 is real for the variance and invisible to a worst-case-range bound at these n.
- Consequence for the programme: a generic empirical-Bernstein interval is the wrong certification tool. The remaining routes are (a) a betting confidence sequence, whose width tracks the empirical variance rather than the range, (b) a bound that uses the observed range of the paired residual, and (c) a bounded-discrepancy assumption checked on the pilot (H3 as stated). Not run: any of them, and the choice of lam from a larger share of pairs.
- Limitations: same single-task pools; 1,000 replications; the bound is not tuned (delta split, pilot share fixed at one third).
- Next: a betting-style interval for the same estimand (route a), then milestone 4.

The Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.5 points at 2,000 replications, so pairs in 92.5 to 93.5 are reported as close calls. Prior: partial support; I expect it to repair most of the 1 to 4 point twin-specific loss but not all of the skew-driven loss at n = 30, so some payload-mass pairs may still miss.
- **H1c:** mean width of `boot_t` over `t` is at most 1.15 on every pair (the repair is not bought with intervals more than 15% wider). Refuted otherwise.
- Not tested here: a distribution-free bound (empirical Bernstein) for a fixed lam; with an estimated lam it is not valid as written, so it is left for the certification milestone.

### Outcome (21 pairs, 2,000 replications per cell, B = 399; `experiments/m3b_intervals/results/`; MC s.e. about 0.5 points)
- **H1b refuted as pre-registered, but most of the gap closes.** Studentised bootstrap for `cv` at n = 30: mean coverage 93.6% (plug-in t: 92.0%; percentile bootstrap: 90.7%), minimum 90.8% (payload-mass-3, the most skewed pair); 8 of 21 pairs are below 93.0 (13 are at or above), 17 of 21 are at or above 92.5, i.e. within one MC s.e. of the floor. For `ppi_pp` the minimum is 91.1% and 7 of 21 pairs are below 93.0. At n = 60 the `cv` minimum is 92.4% (3 of 21 below 93.0) and the `ppi_pp` minimum 92.95% (1 of 21).
- **The percentile bootstrap is worse than the plug-in t** (every pair below 93.0 at n = 30, mean 90.7% for `cv`): with 30 skewed observations it inherits the skew and the small-sample bias of resampling without correcting the studentisation. Only the studentised version helps.
- **H1c refuted.** The cost is width: `boot_t` over `t` averages 1.14 (`cv`) and 1.19 (`ppi_pp`) at n = 30 and reaches 1.30 and 1.33 on mass-3; at n = 60 the mean is 1.08 and 1.12. So the repair is real but not free, and the residual undercoverage sits on exactly the pairs with the skewest outcome, where a bootstrap-t is known to be weakest.
- Conclusion for the programme: use the studentised bootstrap as the working interval from n = 60; at n = 30 no resampling interval here reaches nominal on bounded, skewed outcomes. A guarantee needs a distribution-free construction (empirical Bernstein or a betting confidence sequence, with lam fixed or sample-split), which is the certification milestone, and it will be wider still. Not yet tested: BCa, or a transformation (logit of the bounded outcome) before the interval.
- Limitations: same pools as milestone 3 (one task, one outcome, truth is the pool mean of 4,000 contexts, resampling without replacement from that pool); model-versus-model pairs.
- Next: milestone 4 (SIMPLER paired tables), or a distribution-free interval for H3 first.

## 2026-10-24 · Milestone 3: benchmark v1, MuJoCo pairs (planned before running the estimators)
- Built `experiments/m3_benchmark/pairs.py`: a torque-driven pendulum arm in MuJoCo (RK4, 5 ms steps, 3 s) tracking a sinusoid with PD plus gravity feedforward. The twin is the nominal model; the "real" model adds an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck disturbance torque (so the paired correlation is below 1 even with no omitted physics) and one omitted effect (joint friction, command delay, sensor noise, actuator gain shortfall, payload-mass error) at one of four swept magnitudes, plus a no-effect pair: 21 pairs. Magnitudes are swept, not fitted, so these are model-versus-model pairs; no datasheet is claimed. Before the magnitudes were set, a 40-context probe gave a paired correlation of 0.999 for every pair, so the disturbance and magnitudes were raised until the pairs spread over roughly 0.25 to 0.94 (a change made to get an informative benchmark, looking only at the correlation and not at any estimator's coverage).
- Population: 4,000 contexts per pair, one twin run and one real run each; the truth for a pair is the mean real outcome over the 4,000.
- Pre-registered analysis (`analyse.py`, 4,000 replications per pair, n real+twin pairs and m = 9n extra twin-only contexts, drawn without replacement): 
  - **H1:** coverage of the 95% intervals of `cv` and `ppi_pp` at n = 30. Refuted if either is below 93.0% (nominal minus 2 points) on any pair. The Monte Carlo standard error of a coverage figure is about 0.34 points, so a pair at 93.0 to 93.7 is a close call and is reported as such. `ppi` and `real_only` are reported for contrast.
  - **H2:** E = variance of real-only divided by variance of the estimator, over the replications. Paired uses the context-matched twin run; unpaired uses twin runs on independent contexts (same n, same extra runs, same estimator). Supported on a pair if the lower 95% bootstrap bound of E_paired / E_unpaired is at least 2; refuted if that interval includes 1 on most pairs. I expect pairs with rho below about 0.5 to give a ratio near 1, since the gain is 1/(1 - rho^2).
  - **H6:** across the 21 pairs, Spearman correlation of E (cv, n = 30) with (a) detectability, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between the marginals of R and T, and (b) the paired correlation rho. H6 is supported if (b) exceeds (a) in absolute value with the bootstrap-over-pairs interval of the difference excluding 0. Refuted if detectability predicts E as well as rho does. Honest prior: E is a near-monotone function of rho by theory, so (b) should be large; whether (a) is small is the open part, since the omitted effects here shift both the mean and the correlation.

### Outcome (21 pairs, 4,000 contexts each, 4,000 replications per pair and n; `experiments/m3_benchmark/results/`)
- **Sanity check against theory.** Measured E of the control variate at n = 30, N = 300 is within 6% of the closed form 1/(1 - 0.9 rho^2) on every pair (mean 3.8% below, the cost of estimating lam). Paired correlations span 0.17 to 0.88.
- **H1 refuted as pre-registered.** At n = 30 the 95% intervals of `cv` and `ppi_pp` fall below 93.0% on 15 and 20 of 21 pairs (worst 88.8% and 86.7%, on the payload-mass pairs). The cause is not only the twin: the real-only t interval also undercovers (minimum 91.2%) because the outcome is bounded and strongly left-skewed (skewness -2.4 on the worst pairs), and the twin estimators lose a further 1 to 4 points on top of that. At n = 60 the minima are 92.4% (real-only), 91.0% (`cv`), 89.8% (`ppi_pp`), so the shortfall shrinks but remains. `ppi` (lam = 1) is not better. Conclusion: plug-in t intervals are not a valid basis for H1; the finite-sample interval the proposal calls for (milestone 3b) is needed.
- **H2 supported.** Paired E over unpaired E has a lower 95% bound of at least 2 on 13 of 21 pairs and an interval that includes 1 on none. It fails the 2x bar exactly where rho is below about 0.75 (E between 1.0 and 1.9). Unpaired twin runs never help: E is 0.96 to 0.98 on every pair, a loss of 2 to 4% from estimating lam on uncorrelated data.
- **H6 mixed.** Spearman(E, rho) = 0.999, but Spearman(E, KS detectability) = -0.954 and with the standardised mean shift -0.855. The interval of |rho| minus |KS| over pairs is [0.008, 0.146], so rho predicts value significantly better, yet detectability also predicts it well: the claim that it does not is refuted on this benchmark. Reason: every omitted effect here both shifts the outcome distribution and decorrelates the twin. The benchmark has no highly detectable, highly correlated pair (a pure offset), which is where H6 would be tested properly.
- Limitations: model-versus-model pairs with swept, not sourced, magnitudes; one task; one outcome; context-paired with a deterministic twin only.
- Next: milestone 3b, a finite-sample interval (studentised bootstrap and an empirical-Bernstein bound) re-tested on the same pools; add offset-type pairs for H6; then milestone 4 (SIMPLER).
