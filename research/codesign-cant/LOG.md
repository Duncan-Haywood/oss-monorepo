## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 7: can the controller cancel the side force with numbers it already has? A position-loop feed-forward (predictions written and committed before the full `experiments/m7_ff/run.py` run)

- Why: milestone 6b found that on a rigid frame the attitude loop's steady counter-torque to the gust makes a side force (lat·τ) that adds to the wind push on one horizontal axis: that axis holds position 1.9–3.4× worse after a wind step, and one seed in six diverges on 1–2 of 64 episodes. Its first listed next step was a feed-forward that cancels it.
- Method (`poshold.rollout(ff=...)`): the mixer cannot cancel the force (four rotors, four commands; the side force is fixed by the torque), but the position loop can lean against it. The controller knows lat (its geometry), what the mixer commanded and the nominal motor lag, so it predicts the delivered torque through the nominal lag (as INDI does in `aero`), turns it into the side force, low-passes it with time constant τ_ff and subtracts F/m_nominal from the acceleration the tilt must make. Causal (previous step's command), nominal plant only (the randomised mass, lag and torque scale stay unknown). No new sensor.
- Pre-check, seen before these predictions were written (and so not counted as tests): milestone 6b's force-aware designs with the feed-forward switched on, not retrained, scored on the 64 held-out episodes. The axes even out (hurt ≈ helped in nearly every design), but a fast filter destabilises them: τ_ff 0.05 s diverges on 2–70% of episodes, 0.2 s on up to 52%, 0.5 s on 0–17%. The loop "tilt reference → torque → predicted side force → tilt reference" has gain lat·k_p·I/(m g) and the motor lag in it. So the full run lets CMA-ES choose τ_ff as a 13th coordinate (log-uniform 0.05–2 s) instead of us picking it from held-out data. A smoke run (64 evaluations, 2 seeds, 8 test episodes; not evidence) checked the pipeline.
- Plan: `experiments/m7_ff/run.py`. Retrain the force-aware arm with the feed-forward on under both rigid geometries (`above`, `below`), milestone 6/6b's CMA-ES, budget (3200), 16 training episodes, 6 seeds, two starts: 24 runs. Score on the 64 held-out episodes at lever 0, 1 and 2, force on and off; also with the feed-forward switched off (ablation), and milestone 6b's designs with the feed-forward added at τ_ff 0.2, 0.5, 1 and 2 s (no retraining).
- Predictions (lever 1 unless stated):
  - S1 (re-score, 6b designs + feed-forward at τ_ff 1 s): late-window hurt/helped axis ratio between 0.8 and 1.25 (mean) in both geometries, against 1.91 and 3.41 without it; the hurt-axis late RMS falls in at least 5 of 6 seeds per geometry.
  - S2 (H3, retrained with the feed-forward): mean on/off position ratio at most 1.05 in both geometries.
  - S3: retrained with the feed-forward against milestone 6b's force-aware designs: whole-episode position RMS lower in at least 4 of 6 seeds per geometry, and J better in at least 4 of 6.
  - S4: retrained with the feed-forward against no cant (milestone 6b's `nocant`): yaw RMS at least 3° lower in 6 of 6 seeds and J better in at least 5 of 6, both geometries.
  - S5: chosen τ_ff median at least 0.3 s in both geometries.
  - S6: chosen cant median 30–45° in both geometries (the feed-forward removes a cost of cant, so it should not fall below milestone 6b's 31–33°).
  - S7 (lever 2, out of distribution): the retrained feed-forward designs diverge on fewer episodes (mean share) than milestone 6b's force-aware designs, in both geometries.
- Analysis: per-arm means and per-seed values, paired differences by seed with sign counts; six seeds, no test beyond the sign count.
### Outcome (`experiments/m7_ff/results.txt`, 24 CMA-ES runs of 3200 evaluations, about 40 minutes on 4 cores; simulated)
- **The feed-forward evens the axes.** Retrained with it, the late-window hurt/helped axis ratio is 1.08 (above) and 1.10 (below), against 1.91 and 3.41 for milestone 6b's designs. Switching the feed-forward off on the same retrained designs brings the split straight back (2.10 and 3.33), so the controller term, not a different airframe, does it. Median hurt-axis late RMS falls from 0.047 to 0.037 m (above) and from 0.040 to 0.030 m (below).
- **It does not pay overall: it trades a steady, one-axis loss for a rare divergence.** Above, retrained with it against milestone 6b: J +0.009 (better in 5 of 6), position −0.001 m (lower in 5 of 6), the same single diverged episode. Below: J −0.33 (better in 4 of 6), position +0.036 m (lower in 3 of 6), and two seeds diverge on 4 of 64 held-out episodes each (milestone 6b: one seed, 2). The same retrained `below` designs with the feed-forward switched *off* diverge on none and score J −1.52 against −2.05 with it on. Sixteen training episodes did not show CMA-ES the tail the feed-forward creates.
- **Why:** the feed-forward closes a loop (tilt reference → attitude torque → predicted side force → tilt reference) with the motor lag inside it, gain lat·k_p·I/(m g), and an uncertain plant (lag 1–2× nominal, torque 0.7–1.0×). A fast filter destabilises it (milestone 6b designs + feed-forward: 16% of episodes diverge at τ_ff 0.2 s below, 3% at 0.5 s, 1.3% at 1 s, 0.8% at 2 s; 0.5% without). A slow filter is safe but only half-cancels the 3 s wind step's side force.
- **S1 held:** milestone 6b designs + feed-forward at τ_ff 1 s: hurt/helped 1.22 (above) and 1.20 (below); hurt-axis late RMS lower in 5 of 6 seeds in each.
- **S2 refuted:** mean on/off position ratio 1.13 (above) and 1.60 (below), against the 1.05 predicted. Medians 1.01 and 1.02: as in milestone 6b, the typical design does not notice the side force and the mean is the divergence tail.
- **S3 held above, refuted below** (position lower in 3 of 6, not 4).
- **S4 held:** against no cant, yaw −5.10° (above) and −7.19° (below), 6 of 6 seeds each; J better in 5 of 6 in each. Cant still pays with the feed-forward.
- **S5 held, barely below:** chosen τ_ff median 0.75 s (above) and 0.31 s (below; 0.17–2.0 s). The `below` designs that chose fast filters (0.17, 0.18 s) are the two that diverge.
- **S6 held:** chosen cant median 34.4° (above) and 32.9° (below), 1–2° more than without the feed-forward.
- **S7 held above, refuted below:** at lever 2 the retrained feed-forward designs diverge on 4.9% of episodes against 6.0% (above) but 13.0% against 8.6% (below).
- Four of seven predictions held in both geometries (S1, S4, S5, S6); S2 was refuted in both, S3 and S7 in one.
- Reading: the side force of a canted rotor can be cancelled with numbers every flight controller has, and doing so removes the one-axis position penalty in the typical design. But the cancellation term feeds the commanded torque back into the attitude reference, which adds a divergence tail under motor-lag and torque uncertainty that a 16-episode training set did not expose. For a builder: if you add it, filter it slowly (1 s or more); the gain is millimetres to a centimetre of late-window error on one axis.
- Limits: as milestone 6b (assumed lever, one wind state, perfect altitude hold, six seeds, two starts, no hardware), plus a feed-forward of fixed gain 1 with only its filter tuned. Preliminary.
- Next: feed forward only the steady part, the attitude integrator's state (which is the controller's estimate of the gust torque), instead of the whole commanded torque, so no fast loop closes; train on more episodes, or penalise divergence, so CMA-ES sees the tail; a cant pattern whose side-force map is a rotation; RotorPy (milestone 5); motor data (milestone 3).
## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 5: does the design ranking survive RotorPy with canted rotors? (H5; predictions written and committed before `experiments/m5_rotorpy/run.py` was run)

- Why: every result so far comes from one stylised simulator (Euler-angle rigid body, a first-order *torque* lag, a gust *torque* rather than aerodynamics, perfect altitude hold). H5 asks whether the co-designed ranking survives a higher-fidelity, independently written simulator. Milestone 3 (motor data) stays blocked on sources, so this run takes milestone 5.
- Built: `src/codesign_cant/rotorpy_twin.py`. RotorPy 3.0.0 (Folk et al. 2023, MIT, `pip install rotorpy==3.0.0`) assumes every thrust axis is body z. `CantedMultirotor` subclasses its `Multirotor` and replaces only the wrench: each rotor thrusts along its canted axis n_i from `allocation.layout`, its rotor drag (H force) and induced inflow act in the rotor's own frame, its reaction torque turns with n_i, and the frame drag can act at a centre of pressure above the centre of mass. Everything else is RotorPy's: quaternion rigid body with gyroscopic terms, first-order motor *speed* dynamics with thrust ∝ ω², `solve_ivp` (RK45). Tests check that at α = 0 the wrench equals RotorPy's own `compute_body_wrench` to 1e-12 (aero on and off), that hover thrust balances weight with zero torque and side force at 0–60°, and that the mixer's thrust change delivers the requested torque.
- Parameters: mass, inertia, arm, propeller, hover and maximum thrust, motor lag (as the motor time constant) and frame area from `aero.size`, the same as the stylised model. The aerodynamic coefficients RotorPy needs and the stylised model lacks come from RotorPy's AscTec Hummingbird file, scaled to each propeller by momentum-theory exponents: k_η (C_T = 0.1145, implied by the Hummingbird's k_η at D = 0.2 m) with D⁴, rotor drag k_d and inflow k_z with D³, translational lift k_h with D². The scaling is an assumption; one condition doubles the coefficients.
- Controller: the stylised laws re-implemented for one vehicle with the same gains, the same one-step-old noisy measurements, the same attitude-priority mixer and the same INDI nominal model, plus an altitude loop (2 rad/s, critically damped; assumed) because RotorPy does not hold altitude for free.
- Plan: `experiments/m5_rotorpy/run.py`. The 60 milestone-4 designs (PID, INDI, geometric × cant bound {0, 80°} × 10 seeds), unchanged and not retuned, on the same held-out task (32 episodes of seed 9999; the stylised scores are recomputed at 32). Four RotorPy conditions: `torque` (aero off, the stylised gust torque injected: isolates rigid-body, motor and altitude effects), `aero` (RotorPy aerodynamics driven by the stylised wind as a world wind, frame drag at the centre of mass), `aero_cop` (frame drag at the stylised lever 0.05 + 0.3 L above), `aero_cop2` (rotor-drag coefficients doubled). Score J = −RMS − 0.3 P/100 W as in milestone 4.
- A smoke run (6 designs, seed 0, 4 episodes; not evidence) checked the pipeline: RotorPy RMS 0.4–1.2° above stylised, cant advantage 4.2–5.2° in every condition, no divergences. The predictions below were written after seeing it.
- Predictions:
  - F1 (H5): Spearman ρ of J over the top ten (by stylised J) is at least 0.5 in `torque` and in at least two of the three aero conditions. Risk: the top ten are likely all canted designs within about 1° of each other, so a shift of that size could scramble them.
  - F2: ρ of J over all 60 designs is at least 0.85 in every condition.
  - F3: in `torque`, mean RMS is 0.3–1.5° above the stylised mean (motor-speed dynamics, a real altitude loop).
  - F4 (H2/H4 carry over): for each law and condition, cant lowers RMS in at least 9 of 10 seeds, and the mean advantage is within 1° of the stylised one.
  - F5 (H4's refutation test): INDI without cant is worse than PID with cant in at least 9 of 10 seeds in every condition.
  - F6: divergent episodes (|roll| or |pitch| ≥ 60°) are under 1% of episodes in every condition.
- Analysis: Spearman ρ (scipy), per-arm means, paired differences by seed with sign counts. No test beyond the counts.
### Outcome (`experiments/m5_rotorpy/results.txt`, 60 designs × 4 conditions × 32 episodes, about 60 minutes on 4 cores; simulated)
- **H5 is refuted as written, and the post-hoc check shows the criterion cannot be met.** Spearman ρ of J over the top ten (by stylised J) is 0.21 (`torque`), 0.20 (`aero`), 0.30 (`aero_cop`) and 0.25 (`aero_cop2`), all below 0.5. F1 refuted. But the top ten are all canted INDI or geometric designs whose stylised J spans 0.064 (−13.35 to −13.29), so they are tied. Re-scoring them in the *stylised* simulator on five fresh 32-episode sets gives a top-ten ρ of 0.26 on average (−0.22 to 0.47; `noise_ceiling.py`, written after reading the outcome). RotorPy agrees with the stylised model about as well as the stylised model agrees with itself on new episodes. The top-ten test measures episode noise, not fidelity.
- **The ranking that a designer acts on survives.** Over all 60 designs ρ is 0.80 (`torque`) and 0.88–0.91 (aero conditions), against a self-agreement ceiling of 0.95–0.97. F2 held in the aero conditions and failed in `torque` (0.799). The six law × cant arms keep their order: ρ 1.00 (`aero`, `aero_cop2`) and 0.94 (`torque`, `aero_cop`). Geometric control with cant is the best arm in every simulator.
- **Cant still wins in RotorPy, in 10 of 10 seeds for every law in every aero condition:** PID −4.8 to −5.8° (stylised −5.6°), geometric −4.7 to −5.1° (−4.9°), INDI −3.1 to −3.5° (−4.2°). In `torque` it wins 10 of 10 for PID (−7.3°) and geometric (−6.0°) but only 8 of 10 for INDI (−1.9°). F4 held in the aero conditions, except INDI under `aero` (1.03° from stylised against a 1° margin), and failed in `torque`.
- **INDI loses most in the move.** Canted INDI designs lose 1.8–2.1° of RMS in the aero conditions and 4.2° in `torque`, against 1.4–1.8° and 2.9° for PID and 0.9–1.1° and 2.8° for geometric control. INDI's increment rests on its model of the actuator: a first-order torque lag. In RotorPy the actuator is a first-order *speed* lag with thrust ∝ ω². The model-based law is the one most hurt when the model changes, and its paper claims need the high-fidelity check.
- **The stylised gust torque is harsher than RotorPy's aerodynamics.** `torque` (stylised disturbance, RotorPy body and motors) adds 3.4° of RMS on average and 54 divergent episodes of 1920 (2.8%), 43 of them on uncanted designs. F3 (0.3–1.5° added) and F6 (under 1%) failed there. With RotorPy's own aerodynamics driven by the same wind, the added error is 1.3–1.5° and there are 1–3 divergent episodes (≤ 0.16%); F6 held. Two reasons, neither tested separately: in RotorPy the vehicle drifts with the wind, so airspeed and drag fall, while the injected torque does not; and the randomised thrust loss (0.7–1.0) makes the altitude loop raise the collective, eating torque headroom the stylised model kept.
- **F5 held everywhere:** INDI without cant is worse than PID with cant in 10 of 10 seeds in every condition (+3.1 to +3.9°). H4 survives the move.
- Doubling the rotor-drag coefficients (`aero_cop2`) changes mean RMS by under 0.25° against `aero_cop`: the conclusions do not hang on the assumed drag scaling.
- Reading: the stylised simulator ranks design *classes* correctly (cant over no cant, geometric ≥ INDI ≈ PID with cant) and individual designs well (ρ 0.88–0.91 against a ceiling of 0.95–0.97). It cannot rank designs that it scores within 0.1 of each other, and neither can it against itself. It is optimistic by 1–2° of RMS, most for INDI.
- Proposed (post hoc, for the paper): replace H5's top-ten test with (a) arm-level rank agreement and (b) all-design ρ as a fraction of the simulator's own fresh-episode ceiling, with a pre-registered threshold for the next fidelity step.
- Limits: designs are not retuned in RotorPy (a retune would likely recover part of INDI's loss); the aerodynamic coefficients are the Hummingbird's scaled by momentum-theory exponents, not measured for these propellers; the altitude-loop gains are assumed; 32 episodes; one RotorPy version (3.0.0) with RK45; no hardware. Preliminary.
- Next: retune the top designs inside RotorPy to separate model mismatch from overfitting; give INDI a speed-lag actuator model; motor data (milestone 3); paper draft (milestone 7).


## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 6b: which gust-torque sign does a real frame have? Neither: a rigid frame is helped on one axis and hurt on the other (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m6b_cop/run.py` was run)

- Why: milestone 6 found that H3 hangs on the sign of the gust torque (on/off position ratio 0.96 with the inherited sign, 1.21 flipped) and left "derive which sign a real frame has" as the next step.
- Derivation (`poshold.cop_signs`, checked in `tests/test_poshold.py` against `np.cross`): drag F = (F_x, F_y, 0) acting at the centre of pressure r = (0, 0, h), z up, gives τ = r × F = (−h F_y, h F_x, 0). In `rollout` the roll torque goes with F_y and the pitch torque with F_x, each with its own sign, so a rigid body has signs (roll, pitch) = (−sign h, +sign h). **Milestone 6's (+1, +1) and its flip (−1, −1) are not rigid bodies**: each puts the centre of pressure below the centre of mass on one axis and above it on the other. The side-force map from the allocation rows is F_y = lat·τ_x, F_x = lat·τ_y (a reflection, not a rotation, because the canted layout is chiral), so the steady counter-torque −τ makes a side force −lat·(ds_roll·k)F_y on y and −lat·(ds_pitch·k)F_x on x, k = lever. With opposite signs, **the side force opposes the drag on one axis and adds to it on the other for either sign of h** (h above: y hurt, x helped; h below: x hurt, y helped). The magnitude of the effect is lat·lever, about 0.6–0.8 of the drag at milestone 6's designs. So the question "which sign" becomes "how large is the lever", and the hurt axis should show it.
- Plan: `experiments/m6b_cop/run.py`. Retrain milestone 6's `aware` and `nocant` arms under both rigid geometries (`above`, `below`), same 12-parameter CMA-ES, budget (3200), training episodes (16), 6 seeds and two starts: 48 CMA-ES runs. Score each design on the 64 held-out episodes with the side force on and off, at lever 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 times the inherited 0.05 + 0.3 L (assumed; no frame data), with late-window (3–6 s) position RMS per world axis. Also re-score milestone 6's designs under both geometries (no retraining).
- A smoke run (64 evaluations, 2 seeds, 4 training and 8 test episodes; not evidence) checked the pipeline. It showed milestone 6's designs with a late-window hurt/helped axis ratio of about 3 under `below`. The predictions below were written after seeing it.
- Predictions (all at lever 1 unless stated):
  - R1 (re-score, milestone 6 aware designs): the on/off position ratio lies between milestone 6's two values (0.96 and 1.21) in both geometries; the late-window hurt/helped axis ratio is at least 1.5.
  - R2 (H3, retrained aware): mean on/off ratio at most 1.2 in both geometries, so H3 is not refuted for a rigid frame at the inherited lever.
  - R3: retraining under the geometry lowers the on/off ratio against the re-scored milestone 6 design in at least 4 of 6 seeds, in each geometry.
  - R4: cant lowers held-out yaw RMS by at least 3° in 6 of 6 seeds and improves J in at least 5 of 6, in both geometries.
  - R5 (lever sweep, retrained aware): the on/off ratio at lever 2 exceeds that at lever 1 in at least 5 of 6 seeds, and its mean crosses 1.2 in at least one geometry.
  - R6: late-window hurt/helped axis ratio within 0.85–1.15 for `nocant` (no side force, so nothing breaks the symmetry) and above 1.3 for `aware`.
  - R7: chosen cant median 25–45° in both geometries.
- Analysis: per-arm means and per-seed values, paired differences by seed with sign counts; six seeds, no test beyond the sign count.
### Outcome (`experiments/m6b_cop/results.txt`, 48 CMA-ES runs of 3200 evaluations, about 75 minutes on 4 cores; simulated)
- **The axis split is real and large.** For retrained force-aware designs at the inherited lever, the late-window (3–6 s) position RMS on the axis where the side force adds to the drag is 1.91× (above) and 3.41× (below) the other axis. Without cant the two axes match (0.96 in both). With the force switched off the hurt axis drops from 0.054 to 0.030 m (above) and from 0.057 to 0.021 m (below). R6 held.
- **Without the gust torque the side force costs nothing.** At lever 0 the on/off ratio is 1.002 in both geometries, all 12 seed-designs at 1.00. The whole cost of the side force runs through the attitude loop's steady counter-torque to the gust.
- **R1 held:** milestone 6's designs re-scored under the rigid geometries give on/off 1.208 (above; per seed 1.04–1.69) and 1.071 (below), between milestone 6's 0.96 and 1.21. Hurt/helped axis ratio 2.08 and 2.76.
- **R2 held above, refuted below, by one seed each way.** Mean on/off is 1.142 (above) and 1.261 (below), but the medians are 1.028 and 1.033. Five of six seeds sit at 1.02–1.04 in each geometry. The mean is carried by one seed per geometry (1.72 above, 2.41 below), where 1 or 2 of the 64 held-out episodes diverged and were scored at the 5 m cap. H3's 20% line is crossed by the mean below, not by the typical design: the risk is a rare divergence, not a steady loss.
- **R3 held:** retraining under the geometry lowers the on/off ratio against the re-scored milestone 6 design in 5 of 6 seeds in each geometry (the outlier seed goes the other way below, so the mean difference is +0.19 there).
- **R4 held:** cant lowers yaw RMS by 5.10° (above) and 7.73° (below), 6 of 6 seeds each, and improves J in 5 of 6 (above, +0.26) and 6 of 6 (below, +0.66). Cant still pays for a rigid frame.
- **R5 held, with a caveat:** at twice the lever the on/off ratio rises in 5 of 6 seeds in each geometry, and its mean is 1.36 (above) and 1.19 (below). But lever 2 doubles the gust torque on designs trained at lever 1: 6–27% of episodes diverge, including 10–27% without cant, so lever 2 is mostly an out-of-distribution attitude stress test. There cant's J advantage grows (+1.23 above, +6.36 below), because the uncanted vehicle diverges more.
- **R7 held:** chosen cant median 33.2° (above) and 31.1° (below), 3–6° less than under milestone 6's non-rigid signs (36–38°).
- All seven predictions held except R2 in the `below` geometry, which failed on one seed's divergences.
- Reading: no frame has milestone 6's helpful sign on both axes. A rigid canted quadrotor with its centre of pressure off the centre of mass holds position about 2–3× worse along one horizontal axis after a wind step, whichever side the centre of pressure is on. The averaged cost stays small for typical designs (median 1.03×). The tail is worse: one seed in six diverges on 1–2 episodes in 64. The axis is set by the chirality of the cant pattern together with the side of the offset. A designer can choose which axis is hurt but cannot remove the effect except by shrinking the lever (centre of pressure at the centre of mass) or the cant.
- Limits: the lever and its scale are assumed (0.05 + 0.3 L, swept 0–2×), not measured for a real frame; one wind state drives drag and torque; the disturbance torque is applied from world-frame wind to body axes (yaw ±30° mixes the axes partly); perfect altitude hold; six seeds and two starts; lever 2 was not retrained; no hardware. Preliminary.
- Next: a feed-forward that cancels the counter-torque's side force (the mixer knows lat), or a cant pattern whose side-force map is a rotation rather than a reflection; measure a real frame's centre of pressure (wind-tunnel or CFD literature) to pin the lever; RotorPy check (milestone 5); motor data (milestone 3).

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 6: does the side force of a canted rotor cost position hold more than cant's yaw gain is worth? (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m6_poshold/run.py` was run)

- Why: H3. A canted rotor makes a horizontal body force whenever the mixer makes roll or pitch torque. `codesign-aero-cant-poshold` tested this with a per-axis torque box, a side force √2 too large (tan α / (L/√2)) and 2 s episodes, too short for drift. Milestone 3 (motor data) stays blocked on sources (see milestone 4's entry), so this run takes milestone 6.
- Model (`src/codesign_cant/poshold.py`): the side force is derived from the allocation rows, F_y = lat·τ_x and F_x = lat·τ_y with lat = sin α / (L cos α − κ sin α), and yaw torque makes none (tests check it against `allocation.layout` on 100 random designs). It acts on the *delivered* torque, after the attitude-priority mixer (collective at hover) and the motor lag. Episodes are 6 s: start 0.5 m off, yaw steps ±30° every 1.5 s, and the mean wind steps by ±3 m/s per axis at 3 s. Translation, drag and noise are `-poshold`'s (assumed, inherited). J = −sqrt((pos/0.10 m)² + (yaw/10°)²) − 0.03·P_hover/100 W, which is milestone 4's 0.3° per 100 W in composite units.
- Plan: `experiments/m6_poshold/run.py`, 12-parameter CMA-ES (design, six attitude PID gains, three position gains), 3200 evaluations, 16 training episodes, 6 seeds, two starts per seed (cant at 0.4 and 0.8 of the bound), arms `nocant` (α = 0), `aware` (α ≤ 80°, side force in training) and `unaware` (α ≤ 80°, side force off in training). Held-out: 64 episodes (seed 9999), each design scored with the force on, off, and in calm air (no wind step).
- A smoke run (32 evaluations, 2 seeds, 4 episodes; not evidence) hinted that the force may *help* the force-aware designs (position RMS on/off 0.80). The predictions below were written after seeing it.
- Predictions:
  - Q1 (H3): for `aware` designs, held-out position RMS with the force on is at most 1.2× the same designs with it off (mean ratio over seeds). H3 is refuted if the mean ratio exceeds 1.2.
  - Q2: `unaware` designs scored with the force on have higher position RMS than `aware` designs in at least 5 of 6 seeds.
  - Q3: cant lowers held-out yaw RMS against no cant by at least 3°, in at least 5 of 6 seeds (force on).
  - Q4: cant improves J (force on) against no cant in at least 4 of 6 seeds.
  - Q5: cant does not buy position: the paired position RMS difference (aware minus no cant, force on) lies within ±0.05 m.
  - Q6: the chosen cant under position hold has median 25–45° (milestones 2 and 4: 28–37°).
  - Q7: the wind step costs position: late-window (3–6 s) position RMS is at least 10% higher than in calm air for every arm (mean over seeds).
- Analysis: per-arm means and per-seed values; paired differences by seed with SD and sign counts. Six seeds, no test beyond the sign count.
### Outcome (`experiments/m6_poshold/results.txt`, 36 CMA-ES runs of 3200 evaluations, about 55 minutes on 4 cores; simulated)
- **Q1 held (H3 not refuted in the inherited geometry):** for force-aware designs, position RMS with the side force on is 0.961× the same designs with it off (per seed 0.90, 1.24, 0.89, 0.89, 0.94, 0.91); one seed of six crosses 1.2. After the wind step the force *helps*: 0.714× in the 3–6 s window.
- **Q2 refuted:** designs trained without the force are worse than force-aware designs once it is switched on in only 2 of 6 seeds by position (mean +0.034 m, SD 0.137), and lose J in 4 of 6 (mean −0.35, SD 1.28). Two seeds carry the difference (0.387 against 0.097 m and 0.232 against 0.316 m go opposite ways). Ignoring the force is not the disaster `-poshold` found (1.07 m), partly because the corrected force is √2 smaller.
- **Q3 held:** cant lowers yaw RMS by 4.71° (SD 0.96), 6 of 6 seeds (14.88° to 10.18°).
- **Q4 held, barely:** cant improves J in 4 of 6 seeds, mean +0.06 (SD 0.84): not distinguishable from zero at six seeds.

- Found, not predicted: why the force helps. In the inherited model the gust that pushes the vehicle sideways also makes a roll/pitch torque of fixed sign (centre of pressure on one side of the centre of mass). The attitude loop's steady counter-torque then makes a side force that *opposes* the drag. With lat ≈ 7.8 N per N·m and a centre-of-pressure lever of about 0.1 m, it cancels a large share of the wind push for free. That is a geometric assumption, not a property of cant.
### Post-hoc check, written after reading the outcome (`experiments/m6_poshold/dist_sign.py`, `dist_sign.txt`; re-scoring only, no retraining)
- With the gust torque's sign flipped (centre of pressure on the other side, so the counter-torque's side force adds to the drag), the same force-aware designs lose **1.206×** position RMS with the force on (per seed 1.18–1.24, all six at or near the H3 line) and 1.62× in the late window. **H3 is refuted in that geometry** by the proposal's own 20% criterion, though without retraining for it.
- Cant still wins the composite there, in 6 of 6 seeds (J +0.19), because the 4.7° yaw gain is worth more than the position it costs. The force-ignoring designs lose 1.59× (up to 2.64×).
- Reading: whether the side force costs position hold depends on where the centre of pressure sits relative to the centre of mass. In one placement the force fights the wind; in the other it adds to the wind push and costs about 20%. Which physical placement maps to which sign of the inherited model has not been derived here; that is the next step. Either way, cant's yaw gain still pays under the composite. Frame designers can control this sign, and it belongs in the paper.
- Limits: inherited translation, drag and noise model; the gust torque and drag share one wind state; perfect altitude hold; six seeds and two starts; the composite weights (0.1 m ↔ 10°) and λ are choices; no retraining under the flipped sign; no hardware. Preliminary.
- Next: retrain under the flipped sign and sweep the centre-of-pressure lever; a RotorPy check of the top designs (milestone 5); motor catalogue data (milestone 3) when a source is reachable.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4: does a stronger attitude law shrink the cant advantage? INDI and geometric control (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m4_controllers/run.py` was run)

- Why: every result so far is for one per-axis PID law. H4 says a stronger law narrows but does not remove the cant advantage, and is refuted if INDI without cant matches PID with cant. A reviewer at RA-L will ask this first: maybe cant only patches a weak controller.
- Order: milestone 3 (motor catalogue data) was attempted first and deferred. T-Motor's site is reachable but its thrust tables are images and its prices sit on a store that returns 403 through the proxy; the Tyto Robotics database serves its tests through an internal search API that this session may not call. Milestone 4 needs no outside data.
- Built: `aero.rollout(ctrl=...)` with two new laws, both reading the same noisy, one-step-old angles and rates as the PID law and sending their torque request through the same mixer.
  - `indi` (Smeur, Chu and de Croon 2016): attitude P loop to a rate demand, rate P loop to a desired angular acceleration ν; torque = (filtered estimate of the delivered torque) + g·I_nom·(ν − filtered angular acceleration). The torque estimate runs the command through the design's nominal lag and limits; the plant's randomised torque scale (0.7–1.0), inertia (0.9–1.3×) and lag (1–2×) stay unknown to it. Gyro and torque estimate share a tuned two-pole low-pass filter. Tuned: K_att and K_rate for roll/pitch and for yaw, filter cut-off (5–100 Hz), effectiveness scale g (0.5–2).
  - `geo` (Lee, Leok and McClamroch 2010): τ = I_nom(−k_R e_R − k_Ω Ω − k_I ∫e_R) + Ω × I_nom Ω, with the SO(3) error e_R = vee(R_dᵀR − RᵀR_d)/2. Tuned: k_R, k_Ω, k_I for roll/pitch and for yaw.
- Plan: `experiments/m4_controllers/run.py`. Law {pid, indi, geo} × cant bound {0, 80°}, attitude-priority mixer with collective at hover, TWR 2, λ = 0.3° per 100 W (milestone 2's middle price), **10 seeds**, three CMA-ES starts per seed (cant started at 4, 32, 64°), 1920 evaluations per start, best training score kept: 180 CMA-ES runs. The PID arms use `restarts.py`'s CMA-ES seeds, so their seeds 0–4 must reproduce milestone 2. Held-out: 64 wide-plant episodes, plus the same designs at twice the gust intensity (gust_std 8) as an out-of-distribution check.
- Predictions:
  - S1 (reproduction): PID seeds 0–4 give milestone 2's restart numbers (held-out RMS 17.29, 17.45, 19.84, 21.08, 18.48° without cant; 13.17, 13.51, 13.26, 12.94, 12.96° with cant) exactly.
  - S2: INDI beats PID on held-out RMS in both cant arms, paired, in at least 8 of 10 seeds (it cancels the gust torque and the inertia mismatch that PID must absorb with gain).
  - S3 (H4, first half): the cant advantage narrows under INDI against PID (paired shrinkage positive in at least 7 of 10 seeds).
  - S4 (H4, second half): cant still helps under INDI: cant minus no cant negative in at least 8 of 10 seeds, by more than 1° on average.
  - S5 (H4's refutation test): INDI without cant does not match PID with cant: INDI-no-cant RMS is above PID-cant RMS in at least 8 of 10 seeds. Reason: without cant the vehicle is authority-limited in yaw (milestone 2: yaw scaled on 100% of steps), and no law creates torque the rotors cannot make.
  - S6: geometric control is within ±1° of PID in both arms (milestone 0 found gyroscopic compensation worth nothing at these angles, and e_R ≈ the Euler error below 30°).
  - S7: the chosen cant under INDI and geo has a median of 25–40°, as under PID.
  - S8: at twice the gust intensity the cant advantage keeps its sign for all three laws (mean negative, at least 8 of 10 seeds).
- Analysis: per-arm means and per-seed values of held-out RMS, roll and yaw RMS, gust-8 RMS, composite J, hover power, chosen cant, mixer saturation shares; paired differences by seed with SD and sign counts. With 10 seeds a sign count of 9 or 10 is p ≤ 0.021 two-sided (sign test); stated where it applies, no other test.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 2: combined commands on one thrust polytope, and a price on hover power (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m2_polytope/run.py` was run)

- Why: milestone 1 kept the per-axis torque box: roll, pitch and yaw could each saturate at their pure-axis limit at the same time. A real mixer cannot do that. The roll, pitch, yaw and Fz rows of the canted-X wrench map are orthogonal sign patterns (a 4×4 Hadamard matrix), so with the collective held at hover the attainable set is |x ± y ± z|-type: per rotor, −Th ≤ (√2/4kr)(±τr ± τp) + (1/4ky)(±τy) ≤ Tmax − Th. At TWR 2 a full roll command alone uses all the thrust swing and leaves no yaw. Milestone 1 also found the cant optimum held up by an attitude-only objective that leaves hover power free up to the $300 cap.
- Built: `allocation.mix_priority`, an attitude-priority mixer (roll and pitch scaled together first, yaw gets what is left, as PX4 and Betaflight do; collective held at hover, or free as in airmode) with exact largest-scale solutions; tests check delivered torque against `layout`, thrust bounds, hover Fz, and that 0.1% more of either scaled part leaves the polytope. `aero.rollout(mixer=...)` and `aero.score(lam=...)`, J = −RMS − λ·P_hover/100 W.
- Plan: `experiments/m2_polytope/run.py`, the milestone-1 co-design (1920 evaluations, 5 seeds, 64 held-out episodes, c = 0.081, actuator `hover`, TWR 2) over mixer {`box`, `prio`} × cant bound {0, 80°} × λ {0, 0.1, 0.3, 1} deg per 100 W, plus `prio_free` (collective free) at λ {0, 0.3} × cant {0, 80°}. 100 CMA-ES runs. λ spans the range where the energy term goes from negligible (0.1: a 1.5 kW design pays 1.5°) to dominant (1: 15°); milestone-1 designs drew 160 W to 1.5 kW at near-equal RMS, so hover power was free to the optimiser.
- Predictions:

  - Q2: under `prio` the held-out RMS rises against `box` for both no cant and cant (combined commands lose authority), by more than 1° for no cant.
  - Q3: cant's paired advantage at λ = 0 is larger under `prio` than under `box` (more negative than −4.81°), because the larger ky needs less thrust swing per unit yaw torque, so yaw is starved less by roll and pitch.
  - Q4 (tests H1): with energy priced the chosen cant falls; at λ = 0.3 the median chosen cant is below 25° under `prio` (H1 predicts 5–20°).

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 1: are the earlier cant results artefacts of the actuator model? (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m1_allocation/run.py` was run)

- Why: the seven `codesign-aero-cant*` projects gave the attitude simulator two hand-written per-axis torque limits, roll 4 (Tmax − Th) r cos α and yaw 2 (Tmax − Th)(κ cos α + r sin α), with r = L/√2 and L the arm length. Writing out the full 6×4 map from rotor thrusts to body wrench (`src/codesign_cant/allocation.py`) and maximising each pure torque with a linear programme (thrusts in [0, Tmax], other torques zero, no net side force) gives, with Δ = min(Th, Tmax − Th) at hover or Tmax/2 with free collective:
  - roll 4Δ (L cos α − κ sin α)/√2: the earlier formula is right at TWR 2 apart from a small κ sin α term, but grows without bound above TWR 2 because it lets the low rotors go below zero thrust;
  - yaw 4Δ (L sin α + κ cos α): the earlier formula has a factor 2 where 4 belongs (it raises one diagonal pair without lowering the other) and the tilt moment arm r = L/√2 where L belongs (the tangential force acts at the rotor, a distance L from the centre). At TWR 2 it understates yaw authority by 2× with no cant and up to 2√2× with cant;
  - side force per unit roll torque sin α / (L cos α − κ sin α); `codesign-aero-cant-poshold` used tan α / r, √2 too large.
  - Closed forms agree with the LP to 1e-15 on 300 random designs (`tests/test_allocation.py`).
- Plan: `experiments/m1_allocation/run.py` reruns the twrceil co-design (1920 evaluations, 5 seeds, 64 held-out episodes, c = UIUC median 0.081) with cant bound 0/35/80°, TWR fixed at 2 (as `-propdata`) or free in 1.5–6 (as `-twrceil`), and actuator model `prior` (the earlier formulas), `hover` (allocation, collective at hover) or `free` (allocation, collective free; TWR-free arms only).
- Predictions:
  - P1 (reproduction): the `prior` arms reproduce the earlier `results.txt` numbers for the matching cells (twrceil: c 0.081, ceiling 6: no cant 13.65°, cant ≤ 35° 11.22°) to within 0.01°.
  - P2: with `hover` limits the no-cant design improves by more than 2° RMS against `prior` at TWR 2 (its yaw authority doubles).
  - P3: cant's advantage at least halves under `hover` against `prior` (paired mean, TWR 2, bound 80°: −4.6° in `-propdata`).
  - P4: cant still helps under `hover` (paired mean below zero, better in at least 4 of 5 seeds at bound 35°, TWR 2).
  - P5: the chosen cant falls: median under 30° at bound 80° with `hover` (36–41° in `-propdata`), because each degree now buys more yaw.
  - P6: with TWR free and `hover` limits the optimiser picks TWR below 2.6 on average (authority stops growing at TWR 2); with `free` limits it picks more than with `hover`.
