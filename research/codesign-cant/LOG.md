## 2026-11-19 · Milestone 7ad: why do a quarter of the tunes never settle on a 1 m step? The integrator's slow pole, and conditional integration as a fix without retuning (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m7ad_settle/run.py` was run at full budget; outcome below)

- Why: 7y-7ac found that in every training set one to five of twelve designs end a 1 m step 5-8 cm short of the hold point, and section VII's builder rule (a 125 ms ceiling) is held back by exactly those designs. 7ac proposed a position-kp floor in training. Before building it I looked at 7ac's 72 response-tested designs: the unsettled ones span kp 4.4-8.7 on the response field (11.4-26.6 1/s<sup>2</sup> decoded), so a kp floor would not touch them; what separates them is the position integral-to-proportional ratio ki/kp (all 18 unsettled designs have ki/kp ≥ 0.066; 53 of 54 settled ones are below 0.070).
- Mechanism (written down before the test): the outer loop is a PID on a double integrator, s<sup>3</sup> + kd s<sup>2</sup> + kp s + ki, whose slowest root sits near −ki/kp when ki is small. What the integrator stores during the approach leaves a tail of size about (ki/kp)·I<sub>0</sub> that decays as exp(−(ki/kp)·t): a 6 s flight sees the largest residual near ki/kp ≈ 1/6 s<sup>−1</sup>, the range the unsettled designs sit in. Training never sees a set-point step (it scores gusts with a 3 m/s mean wind and ±0.5 m start offsets, where integral action pays), so nothing pushes against it.
- Looked at before writing these: `experiments/m7ad_settle/model.py`, a three-state model of the outer loop alone (PID, 7k's A_P cap, the 45° tilt limit as a g·tan 45° bound, the ±2 m·s integrator clip; no attitude loop, motor lag, mixer or side force), on 7ac's 72 designs: its mean distance over the last second matches the full simulator's median (r 0.998) and its 5 cm call matches all 72 (in sample, though the model has no fitted parameter). Also run on the model only, never the simulator: the 96 never-response-tested designs below (model calls 15 unsettled, and 15 of 96 sit within 1 cm of the 5 cm line, against 7 of 72 for 7ac's), and conditional integration on 7ac's 72 (model: 0 unsettled at b = 0.25 m and 0.1 m, worst tails 1.9 and 0.7 cm). A smoke run (8 episodes, seed 4242) checked the script end to end.
- Plan (`experiments/m7ad_settle/run.py`): **A**, out of sample: 7y's step test (lever 1, 45°, calm, seed 9999, 64 episodes) under the 33, 36 and 39.1 ms laws on 96 designs never response-tested: 7t's `aware lag36`, 7v's `aware lagrand`, 7w's `aware lag45`, 7x's `aware lag52` and the 7l arm's `lag36`, `lag39`, `lagrand`, `lag45` tunes. **B**, a fix a builder can make without retuning: conditional integration (the position integrator runs only while the error norm is below b; `poshold.PINT_BAND`, default None leaves every earlier result unchanged) at b = 0.25 m and 0.1 m, on 7ac's 72 designs: 7y's step, 3 and 6 m/s wind-step tests under the three identified laws, and 7z's held-out stress set (seed 9999, 64 episodes, four capped conditions, eight laws). b = None re-flies the response tests as a reproduction check; the stress baseline is 7ac's results.json (same designs, seed and code path). Neither band is sourced: 0.25 m is a quarter of the step, 0.1 m twice the settle band. Conditional integration is the textbook anti-windup remedy [astrom2006].
- Definitions: a design is *unsettled* in the simulator when more than half of its 64 step episodes end outside the 5 cm band (7y's settle time is NaN); the model calls it unsettled when its last-second mean distance exceeds 5 cm. Identified total as in section VII (L2-45 diverged flights over the 33, 36 and 39.1 ms laws), here summed over all six sets and both geometries; 7ac's baseline sum is 820 of 13,824.
- Predictions:
  - AD1 (prior 80%): out of sample the model's call agrees with the simulator's at the 36 ms law on at least 90 of the 96 designs.
  - AD2 (prior 80%): Pearson r between the model's last-second distance and the simulator's (median over episodes) is at least 0.95 on the 96, at 36 ms.
  - AD3 (prior 85%): the call is a property of the outer-loop gains, not the motor: the simulator's call is the same at 33, 36 and 39.1 ms for at least 94 of the 96.
  - AD4 (prior 75%): b = 0.25 m fixes it: at most 1 of 7ac's 72 designs is unsettled at 36 ms (18 at b = None).

  - AD7 (prior 55%): the tighter band costs more: b = 0.1 m's identified total exceeds b = 0.25 m's.
  - AD8 (prior 70%): b = 0.25 m lowers overshoot: the median over the 72 designs of the median step overshoot at 36 ms is lower than at b = None.
- Reading fixed in advance: AD1-AD3 held say the defect is a property of the outer-loop gains alone, so a builder can screen a tune with a three-state simulation, and the fix belongs to the position loop, not the lag randomisation. ## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 6b: which gust-torque sign does a real frame have? Neither: a rigid frame is helped on one axis and hurt on the other (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m6b_cop/run.py` was run)

- Derivation (`poshold.cop_signs`, checked in `tests/test_poshold.py` against `np.cross`): drag F = (F_x, F_y, 0) acting at the centre of pressure r = (0, 0, h), z up, gives τ = r × F = (−h F_y, h F_x, 0). In `rollout` the roll torque goes with F_y and the pitch torque with F_x, each with its own sign, so a rigid body has signs (roll, pitch) = (−sign h, +sign h). **Milestone 6's (+1, +1) and its flip (−1, −1) are not rigid bodies**: each puts the centre of pressure below the centre of mass on one axis and above it on the other. With opposite signs, **the side force opposes the drag on one axis and adds to it on the other for either sign of h** (h above: y hurt, x helped; h below: x hurt, y helped). The magnitude of the effect is lat·lever, about 0.6–0.8 of the drag at milestone 6's designs. So the question "which sign" becomes "how large is the lever", and the hurt axis should show it.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4: does a stronger attitude law shrink the cant advantage? INDI and geometric control (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m4_controllers/run.py` was run)

- Built: `aero.rollout(ctrl=...)` with two new laws, both reading the same noisy, one-step-old angles and rates as the PID law and sending their torque request through the same mixer.
  - `indi` (Smeur, Chu and de Croon 2016): attitude P loop to a rate demand, rate P loop to a desired angular acceleration ν; torque = (filtered estimate of the delivered torque) + g·I_nom·(ν − filtered angular acceleration). The torque estimate runs the command through the design's nominal lag and limits; the plant's randomised torque scale (0.7–1.0), inertia (0.9–1.3×) and lag (1–2×) stay unknown to it. Gyro and torque estimate share a tuned two-pole low-pass filter. Tuned: K_att and K_rate for roll/pitch and for yaw, filter cut-off (5–100 Hz), effectiveness scale g (0.5–2).
  - `geo` (Lee, Leok and McClamroch 2010): τ = I_nom(−k_R e_R − k_Ω Ω − k_I ∫e_R) + Ω × I_nom Ω, with the SO(3) error e_R = vee(R_dᵀR − RᵀR_d)/2. Tuned: k_R, k_Ω, k_I for roll/pitch and for yaw.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 1: are the earlier cant results artefacts of the actuator model? (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m1_allocation/run.py` was run)
