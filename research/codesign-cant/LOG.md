## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 7f: sweep the linear stability margin used as a training constraint (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m7f_sweep/run.py` was run at full budget; outcome below)

- Why: milestone 7e's first next step. 7e tested one margin (ki <= 0.8x the linear ceiling, M = 1.25) and found it costs about 0.7% of held-out cost and removes every divergence a 45° tilt limit exposes. A builder needs the curve, not one point: how much margin is enough, and what does more cost?
- Setup (`experiments/m7f_sweep/run.py`): milestone 7e's training exactly (12-coordinate `integ` arm, 3200 CMA-ES evaluations, 16 training episodes, 6 seeds, 2 starts) for the centre-of-pressure-above geometry only (7e: the constraint does not bind below), at M = 1.0 (only "stable at every plant corner at its own ki"), 1.11 (0.9x), 1.43 (0.7x), 1.67 (0.6x). M = 1.25 is 7e's designs and the unconstrained arm is 7b's `integ retrained`; all seven arms are re-scored by one function on the 64 held-out episodes (seed 9999), lever 0, 1, 2, at tilt-command limits 20° and 45°.
- Looked at before writing these: a 64-evaluation smoke run (2 seeds, 4 training episodes, scored on seed 4242, not the held-out seed; too short to converge, its numbers are not evidence). It showed that 86-87% of the first candidates are rejected at M >= 1.43, so the start point is far from feasible there.
- Predictions:
  - F1 (prior 85%): at every M, every seed's best design is feasible (best training score above -100).
  - F2 (prior 75%): the mean share of training candidates rejected rises strictly with M across 1.0, 1.11, 1.25 (7e: 0.13), 1.43, 1.67.
  - F3 (prior 70%): the constraint binds for every M >= 1.11: ceiling multiple in [M, M + 0.15] in at least 5 of 6 seeds.
  - F4a (prior 55%): the price is monotone: mean held-out J at 20°, lever 0 does not increase from one arm to the next across unconstrained, 1.0, 1.11, 1.25, 1.43, 1.67.
  - F4b (prior 60%): at M = 1.67 the paired price at 20°, lever 0 is still at least -0.05 (7e at 1.25: -0.011).
  - F5 (prior 60%): at 45°, lever 0, the M = 1.0 designs diverge on at least 1 of 384 flights (they sit on the boundary, like the unconstrained designs' 13), and every arm with M >= 1.11 diverges on 0.
  - F6 (prior 45%): at 20°, lever 2 (outside training), total divergences do not increase with M across 1.0, 1.11, 1.25 (7e: 4), 1.43, 1.67.
- Analysis: per-arm means, paired per-seed differences against unconstrained (mean, se, sign count), divergence counts. No test beyond the counts with six seeds.

### Outcome (`experiments/m7f_sweep/results.txt`, 48 new CMA-ES runs of 3200 evaluations, 4055 s on 4 cores; 42 designs re-scored on 64 held-out flights at 6 conditions; simulated)
- **The optimiser sits on whatever bound it is given.** Ceiling multiples: M = 1.0: 1.00-1.07; 1.11: 1.12-1.14 (5 seeds; seed 2 dropped the integrator, ki/kp 0.03); 1.43: 1.43-1.53; 1.67: 1.67-1.74. ki/kp falls from 3.6-4.3 (M = 1.0) to 2.4-3.2 per s (M = 1.67); cant 31-34° and hover power 475-486 W in every arm.
- **The price is flat past 0.9x.** Held-out J at 20°, lever 0, paired against unconstrained: M = 1.0 -0.001 (se 0.004), 1.11 -0.012 (0.010), 1.25 -0.011 (0.003), 1.43 -0.010 (0.003), 1.67 -0.012 (0.006). Position RMS 0.105 m in every arm. Cutting ki from 0.8x to 0.6x of its ceiling costs nothing measurable; the other gains take up the slack.
- **Robustness has a knee at 0.8x.** Diverged held-out flights (of 384), unconstrained / 1.0 / 1.11 / 1.25 / 1.43 / 1.67: 45° limit, lever 0: 13 / 9 / 3 / 0 / 0 / 0; 45°, lever 1: 13 / 9 / 2 / 0 / 0 / 0; 20°, lever 2: 15 / 12 / 9 / 4 / 4 / 4; 45°, lever 2: 45 / 32 / 22 / 12 / 13 / 12. Below 0.8x no count improves further.
- **Training cost.** Share of candidates rejected 0.11 / 0.10 / 0.13 (7e) / 0.14 / 0.15; wall time per run is no worse than unconstrained.
- **Verdicts.** F1 held (lowest best -1.69). F2 refuted, narrowly (0.11 at M = 1.0 against 0.10 at 1.11). F3 held (5 of 6 at 1.11, 6 of 6 at 1.25, 1.43 and 1.67). F4a refuted: the price is not monotone, it steps from 0.000 to about -0.011 between M = 1.0 and 1.11 and stays there. F4b held (-0.012 against a -0.05 bar). F5 split: M = 1.0 diverges (9 of 384), but so does 1.11 (3 of 384). F6 held (12, 9, 4, 4, 4).
- Reading: for a builder adding the linear check to a tuning loop, 0.8x the ceiling is the knee: 0.9x still leaves 3 of 384 flights diverging once the tilt limit is lifted, and anything from 0.8x down to 0.6x gives the same robustness at the same cost (about 0.7% of held-out J). "Stable at every plant corner" alone (M = 1.0) is not enough: it removes a third of the loose-limit divergences, not all of them. The residual lever-2 divergence at a 45° limit (12 of 384 at every M >= 1.25) is not a margin effect.
- Limits: one geometry (`above`), six seeds, one simulator and its assumed plant ranges; the margin is on ki only (the rest of the loop only has to be stable, no margin); 64 held-out flights per design per condition. Simulated; preliminary.
- Next: what drives the residual lever-2 divergence (saturation in the mixer, the 60° tilt bound, or the outer position loop); a margin on the whole loop (spectral radius bound below 1) instead of on ki alone; the paper's stability section with this curve as its figure.

## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 7e: train with milestone 7c's linear stability margin as a CMA-ES constraint (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m7e_margin/run.py` was run at full budget)

- Why: milestone 7c's second next step. Left alone, CMA-ES put the `integ` designs' attitude integrator gain on the linear stability boundary (ceiling multiples 0.88-1.11), with nothing between the design and the edge of the plant set. 7c's advice to a builder, "stay at about 0.8x the ceiling", was checked only by rescaling designs after training. Here the margin is a training constraint: does a design trained inside it lose cost, and does it diverge less?
- Setup (`experiments/m7e_margin/run.py`): milestone 7b's `integ` arm (12 coordinates; milestone 6's CMA-ES, 3200 evaluations, 16 training episodes, 6 seeds, 2 starts; both rigid geometries). A candidate is feasible if `linstab.worst_radius` (both axes, the eight `wide` plant corners) is below 1 at its own ki and at 1.25x its ki, i.e. ki <= 0.8x ceiling and the rest of the loop stable at every corner. Infeasible candidates are not simulated and score -100 - 10 min(rho - 1, 10). Held-out: 64 episodes, seed 9999, lever 0, 1, 2, side force on; compared, seed for seed, with milestone 7b's unconstrained `integ retrained` designs re-scored by the same function.
- Looked at before writing these: a 64-evaluation smoke run (training seeds only, held-out seed untouched; too short to converge, its numbers are not evidence); 4% of uniform random designs are feasible and the CMA-ES start point is not (rho 1.029); the slowest mode of every design sits at rho 0.9997-1.0002, so feasibility is decided by a slow mode; of the 7b designs cut to 0.8x ki, 10 of 12 would pass the test (`above` seeds 1 and 5 fail; `below` seed 1 is unstable at a corner even with ki cut).
- Predictions:
  - E5 (prior 90%): every seed's best design is feasible (no best score at or below -100).
  - E1 (prior 70%): the constraint binds in `above`: ceiling multiple between 1.25 and 1.45 in at least 4 of 6 seeds.
  - E2 (prior 60%): the price is small: mean paired held-out J (constrained minus unconstrained) at lever 0 is at least -0.05 in both geometries.
  - E3 (prior 70%): no held-out divergence at lever 1 in any of the 12 constrained designs (unconstrained: 2, `below` seed 1).
  - E4 (prior 35%): at lever 2 (outside the training range) the constrained designs diverge on fewer held-out episodes in total than the unconstrained ones, in both geometries (unconstrained: 15 `above`, 42 `below`). Low prior: milestone 7c found divergence there is saturation-limited, which the linear test does not see.
- Analysis: paired per-seed differences, mean and se, counts of diverged episodes; no test beyond the counts with six seeds.
### Outcome (`experiments/m7e_margin/results.txt`, 24 CMA-ES runs of 3200 evaluations, about 34 minutes on 4 cores; follow-up `tilt.txt`, under 2 minutes; simulated)
- **Training stayed feasible.** 13% of `above` and 8% of `below` training candidates were rejected by the linear test (range 4-21%); every seed's best design is feasible (lowest best score -3.36). The test is cheap: the 24 runs took about 34 minutes against milestone 7b's 50.
- **The constraint binds in `above` and the optimiser sits on it.** Ceiling multiples 1.27, 1.28, 1.30, 1.28, 1.26, 1.30 (unconstrained: 0.88-1.11); ki/kp 3.3-3.9 per s against 3.7-4.4. Cant 30-33° against 32-36°. In `below`, ki/kp stays 0.09-0.24 and ceiling multiples 2.1-4 (the ki margin does not bind there; the constraint acts only by requiring the whole loop to be stable at every corner, which `below` seed 1's unconstrained design was not).
- **Price.** Held-out J, constrained minus unconstrained, paired by seed: `above` -0.011 at lever 0 (se 0.003, worse in 6 of 6, about 0.7% of J), -0.009 at lever 1; `below` -0.000 at lever 0, +0.198 at lever 1 (se 0.199; seed 1's two divergences gone). Position RMS unchanged to the third decimal in `above` (0.105 m).
- **Divergence.** Lever 0 and 1: none in any of the 12 constrained designs (unconstrained: 2, `below` seed 1, lever 1). Lever 2 (outside training): `above` 4 of 384 against 15 (fewer in 6 of 6 seeds; J +0.60, se 0.22, better in 6 of 6); `below` 45 against 42 (J -0.15, se 0.40).
- **Verdicts.** E5 held. E1 held (6 of 6 in [1.25, 1.45]). E2 held (-0.011 and -0.000 against a -0.05 bar). E3 held (0 of 768 at lever 1). E4 refuted: fewer lever-2 divergences in `above` (4 against 15) but not in `below` (45 against 42).
- **Follow-up (`experiments/m7e_margin/tilt.py`, written after the results above were read; milestone 7d's next step).** Tilt-command limit 20/30/45/59°, lever 0: `above` unconstrained diverge on 0/2/13/13 of 384 flights (position RMS 0.105 -> 0.257 m at 45°), constrained on 0/0/0/0 (0.103-0.105 m); `below` unconstrained 0/2/2/2, constrained 0/0/1/1. Lever 1, 45°: `above` 13 against 0; `below` 6 against 6. The 13 at 45° match milestone 7d's 3-4% for designs on the ceiling.
- Reading: for the `above` geometry, where the integrator carries the gust torque, the 0.8× linear margin as a training constraint costs about 0.7% of held-out cost and removes every divergence that a looser tilt limit or a doubled centre-of-pressure lever exposes in designs tuned onto the boundary. It is a cheap certificate a builder can add to any tuning loop: one 11-state eigenvalue check per candidate at eight plant corners. Where the integrator does little (`below`), the constraint changes little, and the lever-2 divergence there has another cause.
- Limits: same simulator and plant ranges as milestones 6-7d; six seeds; one margin (0.8×); the lever-2 and tilt-limit scorings are single held-out sets (64 flights per design); the tilt-limit check is post hoc. Simulated; preliminary.
- Next: a margin sweep (0.6-1.0×) to trace cost against robustness; the same constraint for the commanded-torque feed-forward; what drives `below`'s lever-2 divergence; the describing-function analysis 7d proposed; the paper's stability section.

## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 7d: what keeps a linearly unstable integrator feed-forward bounded, the integrator clip or the episode length? (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m7d_clip/run.py` was run)

- Why: milestone 7c found its linear test necessary but not sufficient in integrator mode. Milestone 6b's `above` designs at 1.25× their ki ceiling are linearly unstable at 80–100% of held-out plants (growth 0.50–0.66 per s) yet none diverged, and the bolted-on designs (growth 3.9–7.4 per s) diverged on only 11–44% of episodes. 7c's untested explanation: the attitude integrator's clip (±0.3 rad s) caps the feed-forward. A rival explanation it did not rule out: a growth of 0.5 per s needs about 9 s to amplify 100×, longer than the 6 s episode, so the unstable mode may simply not have time.
- Built: `poshold.rollout` takes three probes that leave the default rollout bit-for-bit unchanged (`tests/test_poshold.py`): `integ_clip` (the attitude integrator clip), `ffi_clip` (the ±1 clip on ki·integ in the integrator feed-forward), `steps` (episode length; same seed keeps the first draws and the four yaw signs), and `diag`, which records per episode the share of alive steps each saturation is active (integrator at clip, feed-forward at clip, tilt command at 20°, mixer scaling roll/pitch) and the time of divergence.
- What was looked at before writing these: a smoke run on 4 episodes of seed 4242 that printed array shapes only. No 7d number on the held-out set.
- Plan: `experiments/m7d_clip/run.py`. Milestone 7c's 24 `above` integrator-mode design-conditions with the feed-forward on (bolted-on 6b+integ, 6b at 1.25× and 0.8× its ceiling, integ retrained; 6 seeds each), the 64 held-out episodes (seed 9999), clip arm {base: 0.3 and 1; noint: integrator clip removed; none: both removed} × horizon {6 s, 18 s} × lever {0, 1}. Also the 11-state linear map iterated from each episode's initial offset alone (no gust, wind or noise) to the first time |p| > 5 m or 60° tilt.
- Predictions (lever 0 unless stated; "unstable" = the linear model at the episode's own plant):
  - V1 (reproduction): base, 6 s reproduces 7c's per-design divergence counts exactly at both levers.
  - V2 (7c's conjecture, fast instability): removing the integrator clip at least doubles the bolted-on designs' divergences at 6 s (7c: 84 of 384).
  - V3 (7c's conjecture, slow instability): with the integrator clip removed and 18 s episodes, the 1.25× designs diverge on at least half the episodes the linear model calls unstable (pooled over 6 designs).
  - V4 (the rival, horizon alone): with the clip kept, 18 s episodes make the 1.25× designs diverge on at most 10% of all 384 episodes (if the clip is what bounds them, more time does not help).
  - V5 (control): the 0.8× and retrained designs diverge on at most 1 of 64 episodes per design in every clip arm and horizon.
  - V6 (mechanism): at base, 6 s, the roll/pitch integrator sits at its clip on at least 5% of alive steps (median episode) for the 1.25× designs, and on more for the bolted-on designs than for the 0.8× designs.
  - V7 (linear, offset only): for the 1.25× designs the linear model reaches |p| > 5 m or 60° within 18 s in at least half of its unstable episodes and within 6 s in at most 10%.
  - V8: removing the feed-forward clip as well (none) diverges at least as often as noint, pooled, in every set and horizon.
- Analysis: counts per design and pooled, median divergence time, median saturation shares. No test beyond the counts.

### Outcome (`experiments/m7d_clip/results.txt`, 24 design-conditions × 3 clip arms × 2 horizons × 2 levers × 64 held-out episodes, about 3 minutes on 4 cores; simulated)
- **Correction to the plan's wording:** the 18 s episodes keep the seed, the 64 plant draws and the four yaw signs, but the gust, noise, initial offsets and wind-step signs are drawn afresh (the random arrays change length). They are new flights on the same plants, not the 6 s flights continued.
- **The integrator clip never acts.** In every set, clip arm and horizon the median share of alive steps with the roll/pitch attitude integrator at ±0.3 is 0.000, and removing it changes no divergence count at all (bolted-on: 84 and 84 of 384 at 6 s; 76 and 76 at 18 s). Milestone 7c's explanation is wrong. Removing the ±1 feed-forward clip too (it is active on 36–44% of the bolted-on designs' steps) moves the totals by at most 3 episodes either way.
- **More time does not explain it either.** The linear model, iterated from each flight's initial offset alone, reaches 5 m or 60° within 6 s in 60% of the 1.25× designs' unstable flights and within 18 s in 87%; the simulated flights at 1.25× diverge on 0 of 384 at 6 s and 0 of 384 at 18 s, and their median position error falls from 0.110 to 0.084 m.
- **The saturation that is active is the tilt command.** At the default 20° cap it is clipped on 72–75% of the bolted-on designs' alive steps and 13–24% of the 1.25× designs', against 0.3–2.7% for the 0.8× and retrained designs.
- **Post hoc (`experiments/m7d_clip/diag.py`, written after results.txt was read): widening the tilt-command clip releases the instability.** Lever 0, 384 flights per set, clip 20 / 30 / 45 / 59°: bolted-on 84 / 360 / 384 / 384 diverged at 6 s; 1.25× 0 / 4 / 166 / 212 at 6 s and 0 / 6 / 318 / 339 (88%, against the 95% the linear model calls unstable) at 18 s. The 0.8× designs: 0 at every clip and horizon. The retrained designs, which sit on the ceiling (0.88–1.11×): 0 / 2 / 13 / 13 at 6 s, 7 of the 13 in the seed at 0.88×. With the 20° clip the 1.25× designs hold position but their tilt tracking error is 8.7–10.0° RMS against 2.6–3.6° at 0.8×: a bounded oscillation against the tilt limit, not a stable loop.
- **Verdicts.** V1 held (exact at both levers). V2 refuted (84 → 84). V3 refuted (0 of 367 linear-unstable flights diverged). V4 held, but only because nothing diverges with any clip arm; the rival it tested is refuted by V7. V5 held (0 in every arm). V6 refuted (integrator at its clip on 0.000 of steps in every set). V7 split: within 18 s held (0.87), within 6 s refuted (0.60 against ≤ 0.10). V8 refuted (bolted-on, 18 s, lever 0: 73 with both clips removed against 76).
- Reading: milestone 7c's "bounded" slow instability was held by the 20° tilt-command limit, the outermost saturation in the position loop, not by the integrator clip and not by the short episode. The linear test's false alarms are real instabilities the tilt limit happens to contain as an oscillation; raise the limit (an aggressive position mode, a racing profile) and they diverge at close to the predicted rate. So the linear ki/kp ceiling is a better rule than 7c gave it credit for, and the 0.8× margin is the only setting tested that survives every tilt limit. For a builder: designs past the ceiling fly only because the tilt limit is tight, and a design tuned onto the ceiling (as CMA-ES does) loses 3–4% of flights once the limit is lifted.
- Limits: one geometry (`above`), lever 0 for the tilt sweep, 6 designs per set, same simulator and assumed plant ranges; the tilt sweep is post hoc and exploratory; no describing-function analysis of the limit cycle yet. Preliminary.
- Next: a describing-function prediction of the oscillation amplitude under the tilt limit, to turn "bounded" into a number; CMA-ES with the 0.8× ceiling as a constraint, scored at 20° and 45° tilt limits; the paper draft's stability section.

## 2026-11-13 · Milestone 7c: a linear stability test for the side-force feed-forward, checked against the 36 milestone-7b designs (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m7c_stab/run.py` was run)

- Why: milestone 7b found that the integrator feed-forward runs away when bolted onto designs whose attitude integrator is strong, and a post-hoc quasi-steady criterion failed to separate the diverging designs. Its first listed next step: a linearised stability analysis of the full attitude-plus-position loop, to give a design rule that can be checked against those designs.
- Built: `src/codesign_cant/linstab.py`. Around hover (zero tilt, rate, torque and yaw; no clip or mixer saturation; noise off) every Euler step of `poshold.rollout` is linear and the two horizontal axes decouple; gust and wind enter additively, so they do not change stability. One axis is an 11-state discrete map (position, velocity, position integrator, tilt and the one-step-old tilt the controller reads, rate and its old copy, attitude integrator, delivered torque through the randomised lag, and milestone 7's torque estimate and filtered feed-forward). The pitch axis has the side force +lat·τ, the roll axis (in −y) −lat·τ, so each vehicle has one axis where the side force helps the tilt and one where it fights it; both are checked. `step` copies `rollout` line by line and `matrix` builds the map from unit vectors. `tests/test_linstab.py` checks the linear model against `rollout` itself (noise, gust and wind off, 0.1 mm offset; one milestone-7 design, four plant/feed-forward cases): position RMS agrees to 1e-3. `ki_ceiling` bisects the largest multiple of a design's attitude ki that keeps every one of the eight corners of the plant randomisation (torque 0.7/1.0, mass 0.9/1.3, lag 1/2× nominal) and both axes stable.
- What was looked at before writing these predictions: only the test above (one design, not a stability verdict on any of the 36) and milestone 7b's published per-design divergence counts. No spectral radius of any milestone 6b/7/7b design has been computed.
- Plan: `experiments/m7c_stab/run.py`. (a) For milestone 7b's designs (6b aware with feed-forward off, 6b+integ, integ retrained with it on and off; both geometries, 6 seeds: 48 design-conditions) and milestone 7's retrained commanded-torque designs (12), compute the spectral radius at each of the 64 held-out episodes' own plant draw (seed 9999), and re-run the held-out rollouts to get per-episode divergence at lever 0 and 1; classify. (b) `ki_ceiling` for every design. (c) A new, out-of-sample check of the rule: milestone 6b's 12 force-aware designs with the integrator feed-forward on and ki set to 0.8× and 1.25× their own ceiling (24 new design-conditions, never trained or scored before), held-out rollouts at lever 0 and 1.
- Predictions:
  - U1: at lever 0, over all 48 × 64 integrator-mode and feed-forward-off episode pairs, "linearly unstable at this episode's plant" classifies divergence with accuracy ≥ 0.95 and catches ≥ 90% of diverged episodes.
  - U2: all six milestone 6b `above` designs with the integrator feed-forward are unstable at the worst corner (ceiling multiple < 1); all six retrained `above` designs have ceiling multiple > 1; every `below` design has ceiling multiple > 1 (both sets), and every design with the feed-forward off is stable at all eight corners.
  - U3: the ceiling, as a ki/kp bound, is lower in `above` than the retrained designs' own ki/kp by less than a factor of 2 (CMA-ES sat near the edge: retrained ceiling multiple between 1 and 2 in at least 4 of 6).
  - U4: milestone 7's commanded-torque designs: the two `below` seeds that diverged on 4 of 64 held-out episodes at lever 1 are linearly unstable at one or more of the 64 held-out plants; the other ten are stable at all 64.
  - U5 (out of sample): at 0.8× the ceiling, none of the 12 rescaled designs diverges on more than 1 of 64 held-out episodes at lever 0, and the total at lever 1 is at most 4. At 1.25× the ceiling, the observed divergence share at lever 0 is within 0.10 of the share of held-out plants the linear model calls unstable, for at least 10 of 12 designs.
  - U6: at lever 1 (gust torque on), the divergence share is at least the linear prediction minus 0.02 for every design-condition (gust can add divergences through saturation, rarely remove them).
- Analysis: confusion counts and per-design shares; no statistical test beyond the counts.

### Outcome (`experiments/m7c_stab/results.txt`, 84 design-conditions × 64 held-out episodes, about a minute on 4 cores; simulated)
- **Analysis bug found and fixed before any verdict was read off.** The first full run called every design marginally unstable (ρ = 1 exactly): in integrator mode milestone 7's torque-estimate state is never updated, so it sits in the map as a held state with eigenvalue 1, and at ki = 0 the unused attitude integrator does the same, which set every `ki_ceiling` to 0. Both are unobservable, so the rollout-matching test could not see them. Fixed (`linstab.step` zeroes the unused state; the ceiling's lower bracket is checked at 10⁻³ × ki) with a new test (`test_integrator_mode_has_no_held_state`); the numbers below are from the fixed code. The model and the predictions did not change.
- **Linear instability is necessary for divergence in integrator mode, not sufficient.** Over the 48 milestone-7b design-conditions × 64 episodes at lever 0, no linearly stable episode diverged (0 of 2688) and every diverged one was linearly unstable (84 of 84), but 300 unstable episodes did not diverge: accuracy 0.902. All 300 are milestone 6b's `above` designs with the integrator feed-forward bolted on, which the model calls unstable at every held-out plant (growth rate 3.9–7.4 per s); 11–44% of their episodes diverge, and the rest hold position badly (median episode RMS 0.16–0.23 m against 0.10).
- **CMA-ES tuned the retrained designs onto the linear boundary.** Retrained with the integrator feed-forward (`above`), their ki/kp is 3.7–4.4 per s and the linear ceiling at the eight plant corners is 3.5–4.6: ceiling multiples 1.04, 1.00 (0.998), 1.01, 1.05, 1.11 and 0.88. Milestone 6b's designs, tuned without the feed-forward, sit at 2.3–3.4× their ceiling. - Why: milestone 6 found that H3 hangs on the sign of the gust torque (on/off position ratio 0.96 with the inherited sign, 1.21 flipped) and left "derive which sign a real frame has" as the next step.
- Derivation (`poshold.cop_signs`, checked in `tests/test_poshold.py` against `np.cross`): drag F = (F_x, F_y, 0) acting at the centre of pressure r = (0, 0, h), z up, gives τ = r × F = (−h F_y, h F_x, 0). In `rollout` the roll torque goes with F_y and the pitch torque with F_x, each with its own sign, so a rigid body has signs (roll, pitch) = (−sign h, +sign h). **Milestone 6's (+1, +1) and its flip (−1, −1) are not rigid bodies**: each puts the centre of pressure below the centre of mass on one axis and above it on the other. The side-force map from the allocation rows is F_y = lat·τ_x, F_x = lat·τ_y (a reflection, not a rotation, because the canted layout is chiral), so the steady counter-torque −τ makes a side force −lat·(ds_roll·k)F_y on y and −lat·(ds_pitch·k)F_x on x, k = lever. With opposite signs, **the side force opposes the drag on one axis and adds to it on the other for either sign of h** (h above: y hurt, x helped; h below: x hurt, y helped). The magnitude of the effect is lat·lever, about 0.6–0.8 of the drag at milestone 6's designs. So the question "which sign" becomes "how large is the lever", and the hurt axis should show it.
- Plan: `experiments/m6b_cop/run.py`. ## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4: does a stronger attitude law shrink the cant advantage? INDI and geometric control (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m4_controllers/run.py` was run)

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 1: are the earlier cant results artefacts of the actuator model? (predictions written and committed before `experiments/m1_allocation/run.py` was run)

- Why: the seven `codesign-aero-cant*` projects gave the attitude simulator two hand-written per-axis torque limits, roll 4 (Tmax − Th) r cos α and yaw 2 (Tmax − Th)(κ cos α + r sin α), with r = L/√2 and L the arm length. Writing out the full 6×4 map from rotor thrusts to body wrench (`src/codesign_cant/allocation.py`) and maximising each pure torque with a linear programme (thrusts in [0, Tmax], other torques zero, no net side force) gives, with Δ = min(Th, Tmax − Th) at hover or Tmax/2 with free collective:
  - roll 4Δ (L cos α − κ sin α)/√2: the earlier formula is right at TWR 2 apart from a small κ sin α term, but grows without bound above TWR 2 because it lets the low rotors go below zero thrust;
  - yaw 4Δ (L sin α + κ cos α): the earlier formula has a factor 2 where 4 belongs (it raises one diagonal pair without lowering the other) and the tilt moment arm r = L/√2 where L belongs (the tangential force acts at the rotor, a distance L from the centre). At TWR 2 it understates yaw authority by 2× with no cant and up to 2√2× with cant;
  - side force per unit roll torque sin α / (L cos α − κ sin α); `codesign-aero-cant-poshold` used tan α / r, √2 too large.
  - Closed forms agree with the LP to 1e-15 on 300 random designs (`tests/test_allocation.py`).
- Plan: `experiments/m1_allocation/run.py` reruns the twrceil co-design (1920 evaluations, 5 seeds, 64 held-out episodes, c = UIUC median 0.081) with cant bound 0/35/80°, TWR fixed at 2 (as `-propdata`) or free in 1.5–6 (as `-twrceil`), and actuator model `prior` (the earlier formulas), `hover` (allocation, collective at hover) or `free` (allocation, collective free; TWR-free arms only).
- Predictions:
  - P1 (reproduction): the `prior` arms reproduce the earlier `results.txt` numbers for the matching cells (twrceil: c 0.081, ceiling 6: no cant 13.65°, cant ≤ 35° 11.22°) to within 0.01°.
  - P2: with `hover` limits the no-cant design improves by more than 2° RMS against `prior` at TWR 2 (its yaw authority doubles).
  - P3: cant's advantage at least halves under `hover` against `prior` (paired mean, TWR 2, bound 80°: −4.6° in `-propdata`).
  - P4: cant still helps under `hover` (paired mean below zero, better in at least 4 of 5 seeds at bound 35°, TWR 2).
  - P5: the chosen cant falls: median under 30° at bound 80° with `hover` (36–41° in `-propdata`), because each degree now buys more yaw.
  - P6: with TWR free and `hover` limits the optimiser picks TWR below 2.6 on average (authority stops growing at TWR 2); with `free` limits it picks more than with `hover`.
- Analysis: per-arm mean and per-seed held-out RMS; paired cant-minus-no-cant differences by seed with SD and sign count. Five seeds, no significance test claimed beyond the sign count.
### Outcome (`experiments/m1_allocation/results.txt`, 75 CMA-ES runs, about 9 minutes on 4 cores; simulated)
- **P1 held:** the `prior` arms reproduce the earlier numbers exactly (twrceil ceiling 6: 13.65° and 11.22°; propdata TWR 2: no cant 17.06°, bound 80° 12.46°).

- **P4 held:** cant helps in 5 of 5 seeds in every actuator model and TWR arm.
- **P5 refuted:** the chosen cant does not fall. At bound 80° under `hover` it is 35–37° (median 35°), the same plateau as before; at bound 35° it sits on or near the cap (31–35°).
- **P6 held:** with TWR free, `hover` limits make the optimiser pick TWR 2.0–2.3 (the earlier "interior optimum near TWR 5" disappears: above 2 the low rotors cannot go lower); `free` limits pick 3.3–4.7.
- Found, not predicted: the cant optimum is held up by the objective, not the physics. The cant ≤ 35° design under `hover`, TWR 2, weighs 3.17 kg and costs $273 against 1.74 kg and $143 without cant (5-seed means), and 35° of cant needs cos(35°)^−1.5 = 1.35× the ideal hover power. The score is attitude error alone, with cost only as a $300 cap, so the optimiser spends mass and power on cant up to the cap. Any claim about the *best* cant angle needs an objective that prices power or endurance.
- Reading: the earlier formulas were wrong in three places (yaw authority 2× to 2.8× too small, roll authority too large above TWR 2, side force √2 too large), but cant's attitude-error advantage, and its 35° plateau under this objective, survive the correction. What does not survive is the motor-headroom story of `-twr` and `-twrceil`: whether extra TWR helps depends on whether the mixer may give up altitude (`free`) or not (`hover`), which those projects did not model.
