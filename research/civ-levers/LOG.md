## 2026-11-12 · Plan: do the levers act cell by cell? (milestone 7, fourth run, written before the full run)

- Why: the third run refuted H3 on a pooled number (mean collapses in cells that collapse: 31.2 none, 31.2 elite cap, 31.0 welfare). A one-cell pilot I ran before writing this (rho 0.03, m 0.02, k 4, g 2, lag 200, threshold 0.3) disagrees: 16 collapses with no lever, 0 with an elite cap of 0.06 (or 0.05, 0.04), 15 and 16 with welfare 0.2 and 0.4, while welfare raised mean population from 0.45 to 0.65 and 0.89. So the pooled null may hide cell-level effects that cancel. The pilot is disclosed; the hypotheses below are tested on the full grid, which the pilot did not see beyond that one cell.
- Design: the third run's grid (576 cells: rho, m, k, g, lag = plag, collapse threshold) and its settings (2500 years, first 500 dropped, e0f 0.05, me 0.04). Levers, each compared with the same cell's no-lever run (paired): elite cap 0.04, 0.05, 0.06, 0.08, 0.10 (E* = 0.05); welfare 0.1, 0.2, 0.4 of rent; tax tau 0.2 and 0.45 (baseline 0.3; tau also resets the aspiration e0, so it acts through state strength). Deterministic model: one run per cell, so the paired test is a sign test over cells, not over seeds.
- H1 (elite cap): among cells that collapse with no lever, an elite cap of 0.06 cuts the collapse count in more than half, two-sided sign test p < 0.01. Refuted otherwise.
- H2 (Malthusian absorption): welfare 0.2 raises mean population in most collapsing cells but does not cut collapses: the count falls by 2 or more in fewer than half of them, and the peasants' mean income (wage plus transfer) stays within 10% of subsistence. Refuted if welfare cuts collapses by 2 or more in more than half the collapsing cells.
- H3 (dose): the share of collapsing cells that an elite cap cuts rises as the cap tightens from 0.10 to 0.04. Refuted if it is not monotone.
- H4 (why the pooled number hid it): some levers start collapses in cells that had none, so the pooled mean over cells that collapse changes less than the paired comparison. Reported as counts: cells that start and stop collapsing under each lever.
- Caveats written now: every parameter is swept, not measured; reset sizes are chosen; the levers are the model's, not real policies; nothing here says anything about a real state.
- Outcome (`experiments/history/levers_results.txt`, 576 cells x 11 lever settings, 32 s on 4 cores; 330 cells collapse with no lever): **H1 refuted**: an elite cap of 0.06 cuts collapses in 15 of 330 (sign p 6e-5, but far from half) and stops them in 4; the cap binds about 1% of years. **H2 half held**: welfare 0.2 cuts collapses by 2 or more in 23 cells (0.07) while raising mean population 47%; but peasants' mean income is 1.90 times subsistence, not within 10%, so the absorption mechanism is wrong: repeated collapses keep population well below the Malthusian point. **H3 held**: cells cut by the cap 1, 6, 15, 40, 89 as it tightens 0.10 to 0.04. **H4 held only for the lower tax**: tau 0.2 starts collapses in 70 quiet cells; the caps start none, welfare one. The pooled mean hid the effects mainly because it conditions on cells that still collapse (tau 0.45: 31.2 to 29.9 while 207 cells are cut and 66 stop).
- Not pre-registered, added after seeing the table: the tax lever dominates (tau 0.45 cuts 207, two or more in 184, stops 66; tau 0.2 adds collapses in all 179 cells it changes). Decomposing log PSI over post-burn years: in collapsing cells only log(1/S) is positive on average (+0.49; log(E/E*) -1.30, log(s0/w) -0.63), and collapse frequency rises with state cost g (30, 62, 107, 131 of 144 cells at g 2, 4, 8, 16). So this closure's collapses are fiscal crises, not elite overproduction. Run-averaged logs dilute spikes before collapses (the pilot cell's elites peak at twice E*), so a decomposition at collapse times is the next check, with an elite term made strong enough to compete.

## 2026-11-11 · Plan: how big must a committed minority be to flip a convention? (milestone 3, second run, written before the run)

- Why: milestone 3 left Centola et al. (2018)'s 25% tipping point unchecked. That paper (*Science* 360, 1116, doi 10.1126/science.aas8827) ran 10 online groups of 20 people; once a group had a naming convention, confederates pushed an alternative, and the convention flipped only when they were about a quarter of the group. The paper's supplement and the accepted manuscript were not reachable from this session (both hosts refused by the proxy), so I do not reproduce their model's exact settings; I use the model class they and Baronchelli's naming-game work describe: each person remembers the last M names they heard and says the one most frequent in memory.
- Models: `src/civ_levers/convention.py`. (a) Xie et al. (2011, *Phys. Rev. E* 84, 011130, arXiv 1102.3931) binary agreement naming game with committed agents; their mean-field critical fraction is p_c about 0.0979, which I re-derive from the four interaction cases and check by integrating the ODE (analytic check). (b) Memory-M majority game: each round every uncommitted person hears one random neighbour's current name, pushes it into a FIFO memory of length M (filled with the old name at the start), and next says the majority name (ties at random); committed people always say the new name and never update. Mean-field map: x' = P(Bin(M, f + (1 - f) x) > M/2) + half the tie mass; f_c(M) is the smallest f for which iterating from x = 0 reaches x > 0.5.
- H1 (theory): mean-field f_c(M) rises with M (odd M) from 0 at M = 1 and stays under 0.5; the M where it crosses 0.25 is reported. Refuted if not monotone in odd M or if f_c >= 0.5 for some M <= 51.
- H2 (finite groups): in simulated groups of N = 20 on a complete graph (the experiment's size), the critical mass (smallest committed count flipping at least half of 40 runs to a new-name majority among the uncommitted within 200 rounds) is within 0.05 of mean-field f_c(M) for M in 3 to 15. Refuted if any of those M is off by more than 0.05.
- H3 (where to put the committed, the civ-cascades question): on a ring lattice (Watts-Strogatz n 1000, k 8, p 0), at the M whose mean-field f_c is nearest 0.25, a clustered patch of committed people flips its neighbourhood at a smaller fraction than randomly placed ones; with rewiring p = 1 the advantage vanishes. Refuted if patch f_c >= random f_c on the lattice.
- Caveats written now: the hear-one-neighbour update differs from the experiment's pairwise play; M is not measured for people; 200 rounds is a chosen horizon; nothing here is about steering a real group.
- Outcome (`experiments/convention/results.txt`, 34 s on 4 cores): **Check held**: the re-derived Xie et al. ODE gives p_c = 0.0979, the published value; their game simulated on 1000 people stays at the old word for p <= 0.08 and flips at p >= 0.10. **H1 held**: mean-field f_c(M) rises with odd M, 0.111 (M = 3, 1/9 to 1e-7), 0.199 (7), 0.242 (11), 0.257 (13), 0.324 (31), 0.417 (201), so still under 0.5; it crosses 25% between M = 11 and 13. Even M equals M - 1 (the random tie-break makes the extra slot neutral; a test checks it). **H2 held**: in groups of 20, the simulated critical count is within 0.027 of mean field for every M in 3 to 15 (M = 9 off most, a one-person grid: f* moves in steps of 0.05); f* = 0.25 for M = 9 and 11 to 15. Not pre-registered: with a 50-round horizon f* rises by one person for most M (0.30 at M = 11 to 15, up to 0.058 off), so the measured tipping point also depends on how long the experiment runs. **H3 refuted, and reversed**: on the ring lattice (n 1000, k 8, M 12) a clustered patch never flips the rest (final new-name share 0.05 or less up to f = 0.5), while randomly placed committed people flip it from f = 0.10, under half the mean-field 0.242. Rewiring narrows the gap (patch 0.275 against random 0.125 at p = 0.1; 0.225 against 0.175 at p = 1) but the patch never wins. Spread-out seeds (pairwise distance >= 3) cannot be placed past f of about 0.05 to 0.07 and flip nothing there.
- Reading: in this model "25%" is a statement about memory: it holds for people who weigh about the last 11 to 13 names they heard, and moves from 11% to over 40% as memory goes from 3 to 201. Placement reverses the complex-contagion advice of the first milestone-3 run: a contagion is progressive (adopters stay), so a patch reinforces its edge, but here the people at a patch's edge still hear old-name neighbours from the other side and at most half their contacts are committed, which a majority rule never accepts; scattered committed people instead push many neighbourhoods just over the line. On a local network the critical mass is lower than mean field, not higher, because local fluctuations tip pockets that then grow.
- Caveats: one network size and one M on the lattice; 10 graphs per lattice cell, read on a 0.025 grid with a mean-share > 0.5 criterion (no intervals); hear-one-neighbour updating, not the experiment's paired play; N counts the committed; the paper's own model settings were not readable here, so this is a check of the model class, not of their figure.

## 2026-11-08 · Plan: can the GSS panel pin down the tolerance epsilon? (milestone 6, `civ-calibrate`, first run, written before the run)

- Data: the General Social Survey 2016-2020 panel (NORC, release 1, public download, `GSS_2020_panel_stata.zip`): the same respondents re-asked in 2016 and 2020 (4 years) and 2018 and 2020 (2 years). The cumulative GSS file has no respondent linkage, so it cannot be used. Items: the attitude items asked in both waves on 4 to 10 point scales, rescaled to [0, 1].
- Method: the panel has no interaction partners, so I use the mean-field Deffuant map. An agent at x0 moves to E[x1 | x0] = x0 + kappa * sum_j p_j (x_j - x0) 1{|x_j - x0| <= eps}, with p the wave-1 distribution of that item. kappa lumps the mu, the contact rate and the years. Fit (kappa, eps) per item to the binned mean of x1 by x0 (least squares, cell-resampled bootstrap, 400 draws). The `eps = infinity` model is a plain pull to the population mean, which is also exactly what regression to the mean and measurement error produce, so the question is whether a finite eps beats it.
- H1: for at least half of the items, a finite eps fits the E[x1 | x0] curve better than eps = infinity at the 5% level (bootstrap on the SSE difference). Refuted otherwise: then the panel cannot identify eps.
- H2: where eps is identified, its estimate is in 0.1 to 0.5 of the scale range, the range `civ-levers` swept (0.2 to 0.4). Refuted if the median estimate is outside.
- H3 (backfire): the binned curve shows movement away from the other side's mean at large distance (repulsion): E[x1 - x0 | x0] has the wrong sign at the tails, beyond sampling error, in some item. Refuted if no item shows it.
- Caveats written now: measurement error and ceilings confound a pull-to-the-mean reading; a pair-free mean-field map cannot tell who talked to whom; items are ordinal; 2016 to 2020 respondents are survivors, not the whole population; the answer is about attitudes in the US 2016 to 2020, not a parameter of any other society. Raw microdata is not committed; the per-item x0 by x1 cross-tables are.

- Outcome (`experiments/calibrate/results.txt`, `power.txt`; 70 tables from 40 items, 42,687 pairs): **H1 refuted**: finite eps beats the unbounded pull in 3 of 70 tables at 5% (3.5 expected by chance); 84% of tables pick eps unbounded. **H2 not testable**: the 3 hits (eqwlth 2018-2020 0.70, marblk 0.75 and 0.50) are at chance level, and the bootstrap intervals are mostly wide (0.5 to 1.0). **H3 refuted**: 0 of 134 tail bins move away from the mean with z <= -2. Power check I added after seeing the result: at true eps 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 the design detects it 1%, 14%, 27%, 43% of the time (mean of 7 items; homosex dropped, its 4-level null threshold is degenerate), so "no finite eps" is not supported either: the data are silent. Median kappa_inf is 0.50, i.e. a half pull to the mean in 2 to 4 years, which regression to the mean also gives. Decision: eps stays swept; milestone 6 needs interaction data (who talked to whom) to go further.

## 2026-11-08 · Plan: a collapse reset for the toy civilization (milestone 7, third run, written before the run)

- Model: `history.run(collapse_thr=...)`. When instability psi reaches the threshold (and 50 years have passed since the last collapse) a state collapse fires: elites lose 50% (`collapse_elite`), peasants 20% (`collapse_pop`), fiscal strength drops to a tenth, lagged stress and elite income reset. This is the missing ingredient named in the 2026-10-31 entry (collapse resets stress). Thresholds and loss fractions are not measured; they are swept. Published check: secular cycle period 150 to 400 years (Turchin and Nefedov 2009), and a visible amplitude.
- H1: with the reset, population swing (n_cv) exceeds 0.10 in some cells, which no earlier variant reached (max 0.089). Refuted if max n_cv stays under 0.10 over the grid.
- H2: with the reset, at least one cell has a dominant period in 150 to 400 years AND n_cv >= 0.10. Refuted otherwise.
- H3: a lever (elite cap or welfare) lengthens the mean interval between collapses against no lever. Refuted if collapse counts do not fall with either lever in the cells that collapse.
- Amendment before the run: a first look showed psi never exceeds 0.15 in the existing grid (at equilibrium PSI = 1/S, so psi is 0.03 to 0.1 at k = 4 and about 0 at k = 16 for g <= 2), so a threshold of 0.3 to 0.7 never fires. The grid moves to g 2 to 16, k 4 and 8, thresholds 0.1, 0.3, 0.5.
- Analysis: grid over rho, m, k, g, lag (plag set equal), collapse_thr, three lever settings; deterministic model (no noise), so no intervals; the result is about the model only.
- Outcome (`experiments/history/collapse_results.txt`, 576 cells x 3 lever settings, deterministic): **H1 held**: the largest swing is 0.418 of the mean (earlier variants: 0.089). **H2 held in the letter, with a caveat I checked**: 55 cells have an autocorrelation period of 150 to 400 years and swing >= 0.10, but in 29 of them the median gap between collapses is 50 to 99 years, pinned at the 50-year refractory floor, so their period is an artefact of chained collapses. Only 16 cells (all at lag 200, i.e. long elite memory) have collapses spaced 150 to 400 years apart, with swings 0.13 to 0.31: those are the first cycles in this model that pass both the published period check and an amplitude check. **H3 refuted**: collapse counts are the same with no lever, an elite cap of 0.06 and a welfare transfer of 0.2 (mean 31.2, 31.2, 31.0 collapses in cells that collapse; 16, 17, 16 clean cells), so this closure still cannot say which policy damps cycles. Caveats: the reset sizes (50% elites, 20% peasants, state to a tenth) and thresholds are chosen, not measured; peasants and elites both fall at a collapse by assumption; one elite-cap and one welfare level only.

## 2026-11-05 · Why q_c was 300 there and 55 here: Poisson traits (milestone 4, fourth run)

- Plan (written before the run): reading Castellano, Marsili and Vespignani (2000, arXiv cond-mat/0003111) again, their traits are drawn from a **Poisson distribution with mean q** ("we relax the constraint of integer q by using a Poisson distribution Prob(sigma = k) = q^k e^-q / k!"), not uniformly on 1..q as in the last three runs. Two Poisson(q) draws agree with probability about 1/(2 sqrt(pi q)), so Poisson q = 300 has the same initial collision rate as a uniform alphabet of 2 sqrt(pi 300) = 61 traits. That is close to the uniform q_half of 55 found at L = 80, so the "unreproduced" q_c may be a definition mismatch, not a bug. Add `axelrod.c` mode `poisson` (sixth argument; q is then the real mean) and sweep F = 10 at L = 20, 40, 80 over q = 100 to 500.
- H1: at L = 80 the Poisson q_half lies in 200 to 400 (the published q_c is about 300). Refuted if outside.
- H2 (the mapping, sharper): q_half(Poisson) = q_eff^2 / (4 pi) with q_eff the uniform q_half at the same L (55 at L = 80 gives 240; 50 at L = 40 gives 199; 43 at L = 20 gives 147). Refuted if any of the three measured q_half is outside 0.8 to 1.25 of that.
- Caveats to carry: Poisson and uniform alphabets differ beyond the collision rate (tails, trait frequencies), so H2 may fail by a modest factor; 8 to 40 runs per cell.
- Outcome (`experiments/culture/poisson_results.txt`, 40 runs per cell, F = 10): **H1 held**: q_half = 135.2 [129, 146], 179.6 [177, 184], 230.0 [226, 235] at L = 20, 40, 80, rising toward the published 300. **H2 held**: predictions 147, 199, 240 against measured 135, 180, 230, ratios 0.92, 0.90, 0.96, all inside 0.8 to 1.25, with the measured value consistently about 5 to 10% under the mapping.
- Reading: the "unreproduced q_c" of the last three runs was a parameterisation mismatch (uniform alphabet of q traits against Poisson mean q), not a bug in the C core. Poisson q = 300 is equivalent to about 61 uniform traits, which is where the uniform runs put the transition (55 at L = 80). The remaining gap (230 at L = 80 against about 300 at L up to 150) is consistent with the slow growth in L; L = 150 not run. The q grid is coarse (50 apart) and the bootstrap interval covers run noise only.
- Caveats: one F; q_half is a 0.5 crossing, not their q_c from the jump; the reading of their text is from the arXiv v1 PDF.

Refuted if payoff at n = 40 stays within 10% of the no-punishment payoff.
- Outcome (`experiments/commons/results.txt`): **H1 held** (7.5 falls to 1.9 by round 10). **First finding was a null**: with no behavioural response (react 0) punishment changes no contribution and only lowers payoff (21.1 to 19.8). Contributions are driven entirely by the assumed response rule, so `react` is the unmeasured parameter and is swept: round-10 contribution 5.4, 10.2, 12.2 at react 0.5, 1, 2 (4-person partner groups). **H2 held** under that assumption. **H3 held**: at react 1, stranger groups of 8, 16, 40 reach 14.4, 15.3, 16.1 but payoff falls to 18.1, 11.4, 2.9 against 21.0 without punishment, because every punisher fines every lower contributor, so total cost scales with group size.
- Caveats: the response rule, type shares and punisher share are assumed, not fitted; the published rounds were not compared numerically (the paper's figures were not fetched in this run), only the qualitative decay and punishment-sustains shape. The all-to-all punishment rule inflates the large-group cost; a rule where one punisher fines at most one target, or a pooled institution, is the next test.
- Next: fit react and lam to Fehr and Gächter's published round means (get the table from the paper), then compare a pooled sanctioning institution (Sigmund et al. 2010 style) with peer punishment at n = 40.

## 2026-10-28 · Plan: Axelrod's culture model with a broadcast (milestone 4, written before the run)

- Model: `src/civ_levers/culture.py`. Axelrod (1997) on an L x L lattice (von Neumann, no wrap), F features, q traits; broadcast = with probability B per step a site meets a fixed media culture by the same rule (global field, Shibanai, Yasuno and Ishiguro 2001). Not measured: F, q, B, all swept.
- Checks first: Axelrod's base case (F 5, q 10, 10x10) reported about 3.2 regions; Castellano, Marsili and Vespignani (2000): for F = 10 a sharp drop of the largest region near q_c about 300 (lattices up to L = 150), for F = 2 a continuous one. At L = 20 to 40 q_c will sit lower (finite size), so I check shape (sharp for F >= 3, continuous for F = 2) and that q_c rises with L, not the number.
- H1: the order-disorder transition appears: largest region S near 1 at small q, near 0 at large q, steeper for F = 10 than F = 2. Refuted if S is not monotone falling in q.
- H2: a weak broadcast homogenises at small q but fragments at large q: at q 20 or 40 (disordered without media) B = 0.01 raises S. Refuted if S at B = 0.01 is no larger than at B = 0 at q = 40.
- H3: a strong broadcast (B = 0.3) leaves most sites with the media culture at every q (media-share > 0.5). Refuted otherwise.
- Analysis: 20 runs per cell (300 for the base case), intervals are 1.96 standard errors; results are about simulated agents under a stated rule only.
- Outcome (`experiments/culture/results.txt`, 20 runs per cell): **H1 held in shape, not in the published number**: S falls with q for F = 2 (0.75 at q 2 to 0.05 at q 8, L = 24, gradual), F = 3 (1.00 at q 5, 0.48 at q 10, 0.05 at q 15) and F = 10 (1.00 at q 20, 0.20 at q 50, 0.02 at q 100), sharper for F >= 3. But the F = 10 drop sits at q about 30 to 50 at L = 12 and 24 where Castellano et al. report q_c about 300 (L up to 150); not reproduced, and I could not run L = 50 or 100 in pure Python in the time (the attempt was dropped). Base case (F 5, q 10, 10x10): 4.55 +- 0.36 regions against Axelrod's 3.2: higher, not matched (his sample was small and his region count rule may differ). **H2 refuted**: at q = 40 S is 0.03 at every B from 0 to 0.3 (293 to 297 regions); at q = 20 B = 0.01 changes nothing (0.47 against 0.49) and B >= 0.03 fragments further (S 0.33, 0.13, 0.06). **H3 refuted**: media-share is 0.97 at q = 5 / B = 0.1 but 0.50 at q = 10 / B = 0.3 and 0.27 at q = 20. A strong broadcast **fragments** a society that is one culture without it: q = 10, S 0.99 at B = 0 down to 0.26 at B = 0.3 (regions 2 to 76); q = 5, regions 1 to 29. Mechanism visible in the rule: a site with zero overlap with the media never interacts with it, so sites far from the media freeze as isolated cultures (media-share 0.13 at q = 40 is those that started on it by chance). Caveat: one media culture, global field only, no noise (Klemm et al. 2003 show noise changes the picture); F, q, B and L are swept, not measured.

## 2026-10-28 · Plan: where to seed a complex contagion (milestone 3, written before the run)

- Model: `src/civ_levers/cascades.py`. Progressive threshold contagion on a Watts–Strogatz graph (n 1000, k 8, rewiring p swept 0 to 1): a person adopts once at least T neighbours have (T = 1 simple, 2 and 3 complex), synchronous rounds until nothing changes. Seeding rules at a fixed budget: random people, highest-degree people, a clustered patch (a node and its nearest neighbours), and spread-out seeds (greedy, pairwise graph distance >= 3). Not measured: the threshold T and k, which are swept.
- Checks before the sweep (analytic): on the ring lattice (p = 0, k = 8) T = 2 spreads from a patch of 2 adjacent seeds and covers the ring, T = 1 from one seed covers any connected graph, and T larger than the degree never spreads. Compared with Centola and Macy (2007): complex contagion needs wide bridges, so a little rewiring (random shortcuts) should not help T = 2 from a single patch the way it helps T = 1.
- H1: for T = 1, final adoption from one seed rises with p and reaches 1 by p = 0.1 (small world). Refuted if adoption at p = 0.1 is below 0.9 of its value at p = 1.
- H2: for T = 2 from a clustered patch of 5, adoption is high (>= 0.9) at low p and falls toward 0 as p -> 1 (clustering lost); random seeds of the same budget do worse at low p but better at high p. Refuted if the patch is not the best rule at p <= 0.05.
- H3: at T = 3 the tipping budget (smallest seed count that reaches >= 0.9) is larger than at T = 2 for every p, and hub seeding does not beat random seeding on a low-degree-variance graph (WS). Refuted otherwise.
- Analysis: 20 graphs per cell, Wilson/normal 95% intervals on the mean adoption; nothing from real data, so conclusions are about simulated people under stated rules only.
- Outcome (`experiments/cascades/results.txt`, 20 graphs per cell, seeds fixed): **H1 not testable as stated**: T = 1 from one seed covers all 1000 people at every p including the lattice (p = 0), so only speed, not the final fraction, depends on rewiring (rounds not reported). **H2 half held**: a clustered patch of 5 covers 1.00 at p <= 0.1 and 0.85 +- 0.16 at p = 0.3, the best rule at every low p; but the second half failed, at p = 1 no 5-seed rule gets past 1% (random 0.006), so there is no regime where random seeds beat a patch at this budget. Spread-out seeds never start anything at T = 2 (0.005 = the seeds themselves). **H3 half held**: the tipping budget at T = 3 exceeds T = 2 for every p (patch: 3, 3, 5 then none up to 40, against 2, 2, 3, 3, 8, 40 at T = 2). Hub seeding **did** beat random at T = 2 (budget 8 to 12 against 12 to 20 once p > 0), a refutation of the second half, probably because rewiring creates degree variance that raises the odds of two hubs sharing a neighbour; at p = 0 degrees tie, so hubs are random. Qualitatively matches Centola and Macy (2007) (complex contagion needs redundant, clustered ties); a quantitative check against their figures and Centola et al. (2018)'s 25% tipping point (a different model, a naming game) is not done.
- Caveats: T, k and n are swept or chosen, not measured; WS graphs have no communities or heavy tails; random-seed cells have wide intervals because outcomes are bimodal (a seed pair either shares a neighbour or not).


## 2026-10-26 · Plan: contact that widens tolerance (milestone 2, written before the run)

- Model: `src/civ_levers/contact.py`. Each agent has its own eps_i; an accepted cross-camp meeting raises eps_i by `gain` (cap 0.5), a repelled one lowers it by `loss`. Stand-in for Pettigrew and Tropp (2006); the gain and loss rates are not measured and are swept.
- H1: with gain > 0, bridging at dose 0.25 merges the camps in settings where fixed-eps bridging merged none (eps 0.2 to 0.3, no backfire). Refuted if merged stays 0 of 10 at every gain up to 0.05.
- H2: under backfire (tau 0.7) widening does not rescue bridging, since repelled meetings shrink eps (loss > 0) and repulsion pushes the camps apart faster than tolerance grows. Refuted if bridging with gain 0.02 merges at tau 0.7 with loss 0.02.
- H3 (identity): if committed moderates are dismissed with probability q as the other camp's, a 20% moderate share stops merging by q = 0.5. Refuted if the merged count at q = 0.5 stays within one seed of q = 0.
- Analysis: 10 societies per cell, same society as civ-levers (n 1000, 400 sweeps); merged = gap < 0.1; Wilson intervals on merged counts.
- Outcome of the first run (accepted-contact rule): gain had **no effect in any of 135 bridging cells** (merged 0 of 10 everywhere, mean eps unchanged to two decimals). Reason: camps start 0.6 apart, so a cross-camp meeting is never within eps 0.2 to 0.3 and tolerance never gets a chance to grow; contact that only counts when already accepted is circular. H1 refuted for this rule. Under backfire eps collapses (floor reached) and extremes reach 0.6 to 1.0 as in civ-levers. Identity (H3): refuted for eps 0.3 (10 of 10 merge at every q to 0.75, the committed voices are heard a quarter of the time and that suffices); at eps 0.2 the 0.5 voice is out of reach anyway (0 of 10 at all q).
- Follow-up logged before running: **exposure rule** (widen on any cross-camp meeting not repelled). H1b: this merges at least some no-backfire settings at gain >= 0.02. H2b: under tau 0.7 still no merge.
- Outcome of the exposure run (`experiments/contact/results.txt`): H1b held, merged 10 of 10 in the no-backfire cells at gain 0.05 and bridging dose 0.25 for eps 0.2, 0.25, 0.3 (3 to 7 of 10 at dose 0.1). H2b held: 0 of 36 exposure cells merge at tau 0.7, though at tau 0.8 gain 0.05 merges 2 to 10 of 10. Caveat: gain rates and the mere-exposure rule are assumptions; next, calibrating them (milestone 6) and 2-D opinions.
