# Interference twin: an interference-free or mean-noise-rise radar twin vs real mutual interference between robots

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system (simulated): a victim radar (noise power 1, echo `S(r) = 1000 (50 m / r)^4`, detect iff `S/(1+I) ≥ 10`, so clear-air range 158.1 m) with `K = 4` other robots' radars at distances `d` with `d²` uniform on `[5², 200²]` m². Each interferer's chirp overlaps a victim frame independently w.p. `q = 0.1` and then adds `c/d²`, `c = 5·10⁵` (heavy-tailed: a near robot swamps the echo). Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`): (1) for one interferer the per-frame detection probability has a closed form, `(1−q) + q·P(d² ≥ c/(S/T−1))`, which matches Monte Carlo to ≤0.003 (e.g. 0.8247 vs 0.8256 at 80 m, q = 0.2); (2) with `K = 4, q = 0.1` the mean interference is 36.9× the noise; a twin whose noise floor is raised by that mean, always on, has range 63.7 m, where the real per-frame Pd is 0.858 — the real range at Pd ≥ 0.9 is 58.4 m (the twin is 9% optimistic) — while the clear-air twin's 158 m is reached with Pd 0 (a hit frame is lost, a clean frame, probability 0.656, is detected out to 158 m, so real Pd plateaus at 0.656 from about 90 m to 158 m: the real system is a mixture, not a shorter-range version of the clear system); (3) for missions that need `m` of `N = 10` frames, with geometry fixed within a mission, a mission requiring only 3 detections is predicted perfectly well by the clear-air twin (Brier 0.0027, same as the exact model) while the mean-rise twin is badly pessimistic (0.690); for 9-of-10 the clear-air twin fails (Brier 0.636), the mean-rise twin 0.130, an i.i.d.-per-frame-geometry ensemble 0.1011, the geometry-blind exact model 0.1007 (a negligible gain over i.i.d.), and a model that knows the mission's geometry 0.0892; (4) the mean-rise twin's range stays within 7–12% above the real Pd ≥ 0.9 range for `q ≥ 0.05` (75.3 vs 70.2 m at q = 0.05; 45.3 vs 40.4 m at q = 0.4) but at `q = 0.02` it reports 92.9 m while the real Pd ≥ 0.9 range is the full clear-air 158.1 m.

```bash
cd research/interference-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 10 tests, under 1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # about 20 s; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The radar sensor-simulation and multi-robot direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>) and the field-robot simulation direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Mutual FMCW interference is studied in the automotive-radar literature (e.g. Kunert 2012; Goppelt et al. 2010). Companion to `radar-detection-twin`, `radar-clutter-twin` and `rain-twin`.

Stylised: simulated "real" system, no radar measurements; Friis `1/d²` one-way interference, uniform-area placement, independent hits across frames and interferers, no waveform-level model (no ghost targets, no chirp-slope or sweep parameters, no mitigation such as interference blanking), hard SINR threshold, one target, `c` and `q` illustrative. The `q ≥ 0.05` range comparison depends on a single parameter set. MIT.
