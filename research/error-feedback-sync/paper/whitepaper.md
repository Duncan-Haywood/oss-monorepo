# Error feedback in compressed local SGD: the floor comes back, the step budget does not

*Stylised: independent quadratic modes, Gaussian gradient noise, identical workers, independent Bernoulli sparsifiers, fixed outer step `α`, no momentum. Pure Python; the exact results are checked against a literal simulation (`tests/`, `experiments/results.txt`). Top-k is simulation only.*

## Question
`compressed-sync` showed that an *unbiased* compressor multiplies the noise floor of DiLoCo-style local SGD by `1+ω = 1/ρ` and that more workers restore the stable step but not the floor. Practical systems instead send *biased* sparse displacements (top-k) with **error feedback**: the unsent part is kept and added to the next message. What does the residual do to the floor, to the stability limit, and to the bias that dropping introduces?

## Model
One mode of outer curvature `s` (modes are independent, losses add). A worker's displacement is `d_i = s x − n_i`, `Var n_i = V`. With residual `e_i` it forms `u_i = e_i + d_i` and sends `m_i u_i`, `m_i ~ Bernoulli(ρ)`; it keeps `e_i' = (1−m_i)u_i`. The server applies `x' = x − (α/N) Σ m_i u_i`. Baselines at the same expected bytes: *unbiased* (send `m_i d_i/ρ`) and *dropped* (send `m_i d_i`, no memory).

## Exact results
1. **The second moments close.** With `X = E x²`, `C = E x e_i`, `Q = E e_i²`, `R = E e_i e_j` (`i≠j`), the recursion `(X,C,Q,R)' = M(X,C,Q,R) + c₀V` is linear; stationary values solve a 4×4 system. Simulation agrees to 1% for `X` and to 0.6% for `Q` (`N=4…16`, `ρ=0.1…0.5`, `α=0.5…1.5`).
2. **Stability limit: `z = αs < z_max = 2(2−ρ)Nρ / (Nρ² + 4(1−ρ))`.** Verified against the spectral radius of `M`. For `N=1` it is `2ρ/(2−ρ)` (0.286 at `ρ=¼`), *below* the unbiased limit `2ρ`; for `N=4, ρ=¼` it is 1.077, for `N=16` 3.50, and as `N→∞` it tends to `2(2−ρ)/ρ`, far above the uncompressed 2. Error feedback is heavy-ball-like: a big cohort tolerates steps the uncompressed method cannot, a small one tolerates about half what unbiased sparsification does.
3. **Floor in closed form.** `Var x = αV/(Ns(2−z)) · [1 − z/2 + (1−1/N)·k·z(2−z)/2] / (1 − z/z_max)`, `k = 2(1−ρ)/(ρ(2−ρ))`. It equals the solved 4×4 system to 3·10⁻¹³ over a grid, and reduces to the uncompressed floor at `ρ=1` or `N=1, z→0`.
4. **The floor comes back.** At small `z` the ratio to the uncompressed floor is `1+O(z)`, not `1/ρ`: at `N=16`, `α=0.05`, `ρ=¼` it is 1.08 (unbiased: 4.01), at `ρ=0.1` it is 1.23 (unbiased: 10.07); at `α=0.5` it is 1.70 and 3.33 against 4.11 and 10.87. The mean dynamics explain it: the slow eigenvalue is `≈ 1−αs` (0.9786 vs 0.98 at `z=0.02, ρ=¼`), *not* `1−αρs` as when dropping (0.995), so error feedback keeps the uncompressed speed. At equal speed, dropping needs `α/ρ` and pays exactly the unbiased floor (4.01 and 4.04 at `ρ=¼`, table 3). Once `z` is large the eigenvalues turn complex with modulus `√(1−ρ)`, capping the speed (0.9747 at `ρ=0.05`).
5. **What the residual buys is unbiasedness, at a variance price.** If worker `i` (optimum `b_i`) sends at rate `ρ_i`, dropping converges to the rate-weighted mean `Σρ_ib_i/Σρ_i` (0.4902 against the true 0.4167 for six workers with rates 0.05–0.9; measured 0.4903); with the residual `E sent_i = E d_i` at stationarity, so the fixed point is the plain mean whatever the rates (measured 0.4167). The variance is where it hurts: at `α=0.4` error feedback's excess variance is 0.588 against dropping's 0.0065 (mean squared error 0.588 vs 0.0119); they cross near `α≈0.02` (`α=0.03`: 0.0067 vs 0.0059; `α=0.01`: 0.00086 vs 0.0056; `α=0.003`: 0.0001 vs 0.0055), so the residual needs small steps to pay for itself.
6. **Top-k (simulation).** On eight modes with noise scales spread 25× (`k=2, N=8, α=0.5`) the loss is 1.10× uncompressed with top-k and feedback, 1.90× for rand-k with feedback (exact), 4.28× for unbiased rand-k, and 0.97×/0.90× for the dropped versions (which are slower, as above): sending the largest coordinates first shortens the delay a residual adds.

## Limitations
Homogeneous curvature and a common optimum in results 1–4 (there dropping is unbiased, only slower), independent Bernoulli masks rather than data-dependent top-k (whose floor has no closed form here), unequal-rate variance only by simulation, no momentum or outer optimiser, and moment stability is not almost-sure stability. Result 5 uses a single shared curvature; with drifting workers the bias also depends on `H`.

## Relevance
For bandwidth-limited open training: unbiased compression taxes the floor by `1/ρ`; feedback does not, but it needs a step budget — check `α s < z_max` with your cohort size before turning it on (a single peer sending 5% of coordinates must stay below `z≈0.05`), and use it when send rates differ across peers (heterogeneous links) and the fixed point matters, not when peers are exchangeable, where dropping with a rescaled step is as good. Companion to `compressed-sync`, `partial-participation`, `noisy-local-sgd`, `local-sgd-bias`.
