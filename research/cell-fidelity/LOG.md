# cell-fidelity research log

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 4 outcome (search replay): a whole-pool DFN@10 screen is slower than plain GP-EI; the cheapest screens win on small pools, and GP-EI catches up by 96 designs
- Replay of 12 methods on 30 pools of 64 of the 128 Sobol designs, each evaluation charged its median baseline fresh-run cost (`experiments/search/results.txt`). T1 = charged seconds until a design within 1% of the pool's best DFN@100 has been run at DFN@100.
- H7 refuted: DFN@10 screen-then-verify has median T1 253.3 s against 220.4 s for GP-EI on DFN@100 (ratio 1.15, pre-registered bar 1/3), and at a budget of 73.5 s (GP-EI/3) it has not finished screening (regret 100%). Its first verified design is always within 1% (one DFN@100 call), but screening 64 designs at 3.6 s costs 229 s, more than GP-EI's median 9 DFN@100 calls.
- H8 refuted by its letter, not in substance: successive halving over DFN cycles reaches 1% at 246.8 s against 253.3 s, lower on every pool (Wilcoxon p = 1e-7), a 2.6% saving. Charging resumed runs only the extra cycles brings halving to 184.8 s.
- H9 supported: the SPM@2 screen (0.33 s a design) has median T1 168.2 s against 253.3 s for DFN@10 (p = 0.0003), and beats GP-EI (p = 0.01). Fastest overall: SPM@100 screen 153.2 s (95% 141.0 to 165.5) and DFN@2 screen 159.6 s, about 30% under GP-EI.
- H10 supported: the trust flag speeds SPMe screening, 417.8 s against 699.5 s (p = 8e-5), because the DFN-best designs are among those SPMe flags. SPMe stays the slowest screen, slower than random DFN at the median (612 s).
- MF-GP-EI (screen score as a fifth GP input) adds nothing on this pool: with DFN@10 it equals the screen (its start is already within 1%), with SPM@2 it is slower than the plain SPM@2 screen (192.7 against 168.2 s), because EI leaves the screen's order.
- Not in the plan, pool-size sweep (30 seeds each): median T1 for GP-EI / SPM@2 / SPM@100 / DFN@2 / DFN@10 screens is 147 / 54 / 66 / 46 / 82 s at 16 designs, 220 / 168 / 153 / 160 / 253 s at 64 and 220 / 240 / 199 / 252 / 368 s at 96. Screening cost grows with the pool and GP-EI's barely does, so whole-pool screening is the right tool only for small candidate sets; a continuous search needs a model on the cheap rung, which is what the BOCA-style baseline (not run yet) should test.
- Caveats: pools are drawn from one set of 128, so seeds share designs and the Wilcoxon p-values overstate independence; costs are one design's timings, so the screens' constant per-design cost hides that thick designs run up to 67 s under DFN.
- Output: `experiments/search/results.txt`, `summary.json`, `search.svg`.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 4: which multi-fidelity search finds the DFN-best design for the least wall time? (plan, written before the run; H7 to H10 are this entry's)
- Question: milestones 2 and 2a ranked single screens; a design loop also pays to screen. On the cached benchmark (`experiments/cycles/bench.jsonl`, no new PyBaMM runs), which search reaches a design within 1% of the best 100-cycle DFN score for the least simulated wall time: plain GP-EI on DFN, a screen with a cheap (model, cycles) cell then DFN verification in screen order, successive halving over DFN cycle counts, or GP-EI on DFN using a cheap screen as an extra input?
- Replay: 30 seeds; seed s draws a pool of 64 of the 128 Sobol designs (`random.Random(s).sample`), so methods are compared paired on the same pools. Each evaluation is charged the median baseline wall time of a fresh run of that (model, cycles) cell from `experiments/cycles/cost.json` (e.g. DFN@100 24.5 s, DFN@10 3.6 s, SPM@2 0.3 s); a design counts as found only once run at DFN@100. Target: within 1% of the pool's best DFN@100. Primary metric T1: charged seconds until the best DFN@100 seen is within 1%. Every method continues until the whole pool is DFN@100-verified, so T1 is finite.
- Methods: (a) random order at DFN@100; (b) GP-EI at DFN@100 (5 random starts, isotropic RBF on the 4 inputs scaled to [0, 1], length scale chosen from a grid by marginal likelihood, standardised y); (c) screen-then-verify for SPM@2, SPM@100, SPMe@100, DFN@2, DFN@10 (screen the whole pool, then DFN@100 in descending screen score); (d) SPMe@100 plus milestone 1's flag: the top 5 by SPMe, then every design with whole-run `r_min <= 0.1` in SPMe order, then the rest; (e) successive halving over DFN at 2, 10, 30, 100 cycles with eta = 3 from the whole pool (Hyperband's most exploratory bracket), every rung charged a fresh run, then the remaining designs verified in order of their last rung's score; an incremental-cost variant (a resumed run pays only the extra cycles) is reported beside it; (f) GP-EI at DFN@100 with the DFN@10 or SPM@2 screen score as a fifth input (input-augmented multi-fidelity GP, after Kennedy and O'Hagan's autoregressive idea), the screen's top design as the start.
- H7 (proposal H3, in wall time): DFN@10 screen-then-verify reaches T1 at median at most a third of GP-EI on DFN's median T1. Refuted if its median regret at a budget of a third of GP-EI's median T1 exceeds 2%.
- H8: successive halving over DFN cycles does not beat the DFN@10 screen. Refuted if its T1 is lower in a paired Wilcoxon test at p < 0.05.
- H9: the cheapest screen, SPM@2 (0.3 s a design), has a lower median T1 than the DFN@10 screen. Refuted if higher at p < 0.05 (paired Wilcoxon).
- H10: the trust flag does not slow SPMe screening: SPMe@100 with the flag has median T1 no higher than SPMe@100 alone. Refuted if higher at p < 0.05.
- Also reported: median and 95% bootstrap interval of T1 per method, median regret at budgets of 60, 120, 240 and 480 s, the number of DFN@100 calls to T1, and the paired Wilcoxon p for every method against GP-EI on DFN.
- Limits fixed now: a pool replay, not a continuous search; costs from one design's timings; BOCA (continuous-fidelity BO) is not run in this step.
- Script: `experiments/search/run.py`; outcome logged above this entry.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 2 outcome (dense benchmark): on 128 unbiased designs 10-cycle DFN beats 100-cycle SPMe at the same cost, and SPM beats SPMe
- Context: this ran in parallel with milestone 2a (#14800, below), neither run seeing the other; 2a asked the same question on the 49 stratified designs, this asks it on a fresh 128-design Sobol sample, so it is the out-of-sample replication 2a's plan change called for. Both agree: DFN@10 against SPMe@100 is 0.977 against 0.834 on the 49 and 0.962 against 0.641 on the 128.
- Benchmark: 128 Sobol designs x SPM, SPMe, DFN at 100 cycles, 384 runs, all committed per cycle (`experiments/cycles/bench.jsonl`). DFN completed all 128; SPMe failed on 7 (5%); SPM on none. Prefix check exact (relative difference 0 for all three models) and the baseline reproduces `energy-cell-dfn` exactly (SPMe 109.14, DFN 116.26 Wh/kg).
- H1 supported: Spearman with DFN@100 is 0.962 for DFN@10 against 0.641 for SPMe@100, a difference of +0.32 (paired bootstrap 95% +0.18 to +0.48).
- H2 supported, narrowly: DFN@10 takes 3.6 s and SPMe@100 3.7 s on the baseline (median of 3, idle machine), ratio 0.98.
- H3 supported: DFN@10 screen plus top-10 DFN@100 verify finds the DFN best of the 128 (162.3 Wh/kg, regret 0%; in fact from k = 1). The SPMe@100 screen misses it at every k up to 20 (regret 1.98%) because SPMe ranks the DFN-best design 41st, scoring it 121.0 (gap 0.25).
- Not in the plan, and the finding to follow up: the plain SPM, which has no electrolyte at all, ranks better than SPMe (Spearman 0.955 at 100 cycles), and still does over the 121 designs SPMe completed (0.984 against 0.769), so SPMe's problem is not only its solver failures. SPM over-scores by a median +7.1 Wh/kg and SPMe under-scores by -8.7, but SPM's error is closer to a uniform shift. SPM is not a safe screen for the single best design (top-1 regret 9.4%, it scores the DFN best at 182.7), only for a shortlist (regret 0 from k = 5).
- Also not in the plan: on this unbiased sample milestone 1's label marks 15 of 128 designs (12%) untrusted under SPMe, against 7 of 49 in the stratified sample; SPMe's minimum salt concentration over the 100-cycle run flags them with AUC 0.994, and `r_min <= 0.1` catches all 15 with 8 false flags. (Milestone 1's flag used a 2-cycle run; this one is the whole run's minimum, so it is a replication of the signal, not of the exact test.)
- SPMe's agreement falls with cycle count (0.736 at 2 cycles, 0.641 at 100): its error grows as degradation accumulates, while DFN's short runs converge to the 100-cycle ranking (0.942, 0.962, 0.980 at 2, 10, 30).
- Plan change (agrees with 2a's): the milestone 4 search uses (model, cycles) as a two-axis fidelity space with DFN at short horizons and SPM as the cheapest rung; SPMe stays only as the source of the trust flag. Milestone 3's theory should explain why the SPMe correction hurts design ranking when it is meant to help voltage.
- Output: `experiments/cycles/results.txt`, `summary.json`, `cost.json`, `prefix.json`, `bench.jsonl`, `cycles.svg`.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 2 (dense benchmark): is the cycle count a better fidelity axis than the model? (plan, written before the run; numbered H1 to H3 within this entry)
- Question: milestone 1 found the cost is in the cycle count (100-cycle DFN 27.9 s, 2-cycle DFN 1.3 s), not in the model. Does a short DFN run rank designs by their 100-cycle DFN score as well as a full 100-cycle SPMe run does, at lower cost? The answer decides which axis the multi-fidelity search of milestone 4 spends its budget on.
- Data: a new paired benchmark, 128 designs from a scrambled Sobol sequence over the `energy-cell` bounds (`scipy.stats.qmc.Sobol`, d = 4, seed 0, `random_base2(7)`), not chosen by any search, so it is not the stratified sample of milestone 1. Each design runs once per model (SPM, SPMe, DFN) for 100 cycles of the `energy-cell` protocol (OKane2022, 5 A discharge to 2.5 V, 5 A charge to 4.2 V, SEI and plating on), one fresh process per run. Each run stores the discharge energy of every cycle, so the score at cycle n (Wh/kg of cycle n, `energy_cell.cell.evaluate`'s rule: 0 when the run stops before cycle n) comes from the same run for n = 2, 10, 30, 100. Prefix validity (an n-cycle run equals the first n cycles of a 100-cycle run) is checked on the baseline before use. The baseline's 100-cycle SPMe and DFN scores must reproduce `energy-cell-dfn`'s 109.14 and 116.26 Wh/kg within 0.5%.
- Cost: wall time of an n-cycle run on the baseline for each model and n, fresh process, median of 3, on an otherwise idle machine (the benchmark runs three at a time, so its own timings are not used for cost).
- Target: DFN at cycle 100 (DFN@100). Designs DFN fails on (score 0) stay in, as ties at the bottom.
- H1: Spearman(DFN@10, DFN@100) is at least Spearman(SPMe@100, DFN@100). Refuted if it is lower by more than 0.05.
- H2 (cost): a 10-cycle DFN run costs no more than a 100-cycle SPMe run on the baseline. Refuted if it costs more than 1.5 times as much.
- H3 (screen then verify): screening all 128 with DFN@10 and DFN-verifying the top 10 at 100 cycles finds a design within 1% of the DFN@100 best of the 128; refuted if the regret exceeds 2%. The same for SPMe@100 as the screen is reported beside it.
- Also reported (no hypothesis): the full grid (SPM, SPMe, DFN) x (2, 10, 30, 100 cycles) of Spearman and top-10 overlap against DFN@100 with bootstrap intervals, and each cell's cost, so the cheapest cell that ranks as well as SPMe@100 can be read off.
- Script: `experiments/cycles/run.py`; outcome logged above this entry.

## 2026-11-11 · Outcome 2a: a 10-cycle DFN run ranks 100-cycle DFN far better than a 100-cycle SPMe run, at the same wall time
- H4 supported: Spearman(DFN at cycle 10, DFN at cycle 100) = 0.977 against 0.834 for 100-cycle SPMe; paired bootstrap of the difference over designs 0.138 (95% 0.057 to 0.271). DFN at cycle 30 reaches 0.990, and even DFN at cycle 2 (0.915) beats every SPM and SPMe rung (best: SPMe at cycle 10, 0.883).
- H5 at the boundary, read as equal cost, not cheaper: median DFN wall time to the end of cycle 10 is 4.0 s against 4.1 s for a whole SPMe 100-cycle run (ratio 0.98, supported by the plan's measure); cycle-end to cycle-end it is 4.0 against 3.7 s (1.07). Not refuted (threshold 1.5).
- H6 supported: DFN-verifying the top 5 by DFN at cycle 10 finds the DFN best of the 49 (regret 0) where the top 5 by SPMe 100 misses by 2.1 Wh/kg. At k = 1 DFN at cycle 10 picks a design 2.5 Wh/kg short (SPMe 100: 2.1).
- The reduced models get *worse* with horizon: SPMe's Spearman peaks at cycle 10 (0.883) and falls to 0.834 at 100, so its degradation path drifts from DFN's. DFN keeps only 58 to 74% (median 61%) of its cycle-10 energy by cycle 100, so the short run ranks well despite heavy fade, not because nothing fades.
- The 7 milestone-1 untrusted designs: mean absolute rank error 4.6 places under DFN at cycle 10 against 16.3 under SPMe 100; the three SPMe solver failures solve under DFN.
- Negative: a linear fade extrapolation of DFN from cycles 2 and 10 to cycle 100 ranks at Spearman 0.01 (early fade is not linear); rank on the raw cycle-10 score instead.
- Reproduction: the SPMe 100-cycle re-runs match the committed `spme_now` scores exactly (max difference 0) and a DFN 100-cycle re-run of design 0 matches its committed 116.256 Wh/kg.
- Plan change for milestone 2 (dense Sobol benchmark): cache DFN per cycle up to 100 (one run gives every rung) plus SPMe 100, and run the search comparison with cycle count as the fidelity axis; the trust flag from milestone 1 stays as a check for searches that do use SPMe.
- Output: `experiments/horizon/results.txt`, `horizon.jsonl`, `summary.json`, `check.json`, `horizon.svg`.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 2a: is a short DFN run a better screen than a full SPMe run? (plan, written before the run)
- Question: milestone 1 found that cycle count, not model reduction, carries most of the cost (DFN 100 cycles 27.9 s against 1.3 s for 2 cycles on the baseline). If the ranking of designs by DFN at cycle 10 already matches the 100-cycle DFN ranking, a short-horizon DFN screen beats the full-horizon SPMe screen the multi-fidelity literature would reach for. This is the fidelity grid the outcome entry below asked for (model x cycles), run before the dense Sobol benchmark (proposal milestone 2) so that benchmark caches the right rungs.
- Data: the same 49 designs, the same protocol and OKane2022 parameters, PyBaMM 26.10.1.1 (as committed). New runs, one process per design and model: SPM 100 cycles, SPMe 100 cycles, DFN 30 cycles, recording the discharge energy of every cycle and the wall time at the end of every cycle (PyBaMM `on_cycle_end` callback, set-up included). Score at cycle n = discharge Wh of cycle n / stack mass, the `energy-cell` score with `cycles = n`; 0 if the run did not complete n cycles. Reference: the committed 100-cycle DFN scores (`energy-cell-dfn/experiments/dfn.jsonl`); a check re-runs DFN 100 cycles on design 0 against its committed 116.26 Wh/kg.
- Comparator: 100-cycle SPMe against 100-cycle DFN, Spearman 0.834 on the committed scores (computed before this plan; it is the milestone 1 data).
- H4 (ranking): Spearman(DFN at cycle 10, DFN at cycle 100) is at least 0.90. Refuted if below 0.834 (no better than full SPMe).
- H5 (cost): the median over the 49 of DFN wall time to the end of cycle 10 is at most the median SPMe 100-cycle wall time. Refuted if above 1.5 times it.
- H6 (screen): DFN-verifying the top 5 by DFN cycle 10 has regret to the DFN best of the 49 no larger than the top 5 by SPMe 100 (2.1 Wh/kg). Refuted if larger.
- Also reported (no hypothesis): Spearman and top-k regret for every rung (SPM, SPMe at 2, 10, 30, 100; DFN at 2, 10, 30), a linear extrapolation of DFN to cycle 100 from cycles 2 and 10, and where the 7 milestone-1 untrusted designs rank under DFN at cycle 10.
- Script: `experiments/horizon/run.py`; outcome logged above this entry.

## 2026-11-11 · Outcome: the flag works (AUC 0.997), but cycle count, not model reduction, is where the cost is
- H1 supported: SPMe's 2-cycle minimum electrolyte concentration (`r_min`) separates the 7 untrusted designs from the 42 trusted with ROC AUC 0.997 (bootstrap 95% 0.980 to 1.000). The 3 solver failures count as `r_min = 0` by the plan; over the 46 designs SPMe solved, AUC is 0.994 (4 untrusted). A flag at `r_min <= 0.10` catches all 7 with 1 false flag (design 30, gap 0.17); at `r_min <= 0` it catches 6 with none.
- H2 supported: Spearman(`r_min`, relative gap) = -0.833 over 49.
- H3 refuted, and this is the finding that changes the plan: a 2-cycle run costs SPM 0.3 s, SPMe 1.2 s, DFN 1.3 s (median of 3, baseline design, fresh process, set-up included), so SPMe/DFN = 0.88. Extra run, not in the plan: at 100 cycles, SPM 1.0 s, SPMe 3.9 s, DFN 27.9 s (one run each), SPMe/DFN = 0.14. Going from 100 cycles to 2 saves 21x on DFN; going from DFN to SPMe at 100 cycles saves 7x. A 2-cycle *DFN* run costs no more than the 2-cycle SPMe flag, so the cheapest trustworthy screen may be short-horizon DFN, not SPMe.
- Replay: DFN-verifying the top 10 by SPMe already returns the DFN best of the 49 (163.0); the top 1 to 5 returns 160.9 (regret 2.1, 1.3%). Adding the flagged designs costs 8 DFN calls and finds nothing better, because the designs SPMe mis-scores are not the DFN optimum in this sample. The flag's value is in not pruning a region, not in the best design here.
- Plan change: milestone 2 adds the cycle count as a fidelity axis (2, 10, 30, 100 cycles x SPM, SPMe, DFN) and asks whether early-cycle DFN ranks 100-cycle DFN as well as 100-cycle SPMe does.
- Output: `experiments/trust/results.txt`, `probe.jsonl`, `cost.json`, `cost100.json`, `summary.json`, `trust.svg`.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 1: can the cheap model flag where it cannot be trusted? (plan, written before the run)
- Question: `energy-cell-dfn` found that SPMe and DFN agree on most of 49 `energy-cell` designs (median gap 9%) but disagree badly on a few (SPMe 0 to 27.6 Wh/kg where DFN gives 120 to 146), and traced the failures to electrolyte salt depletion. A multi-fidelity search needs to know *before* paying for DFN which cheap scores to distrust. The candidate indicator is free: SPMe's own minimum electrolyte concentration, read from a short SPMe run.
- Data: the 49 designs of `energy-cell-dfn/experiments/selected.json` with their committed 100-cycle SPMe and DFN scores (`dfn.jsonl`, not re-run). New runs: each design under SPMe for 2 cycles (5 A discharge to 2.5 V, 5 A charge to 4.2 V; the `energy-cell` protocol and OKane2022 parameters), recording the minimum electrolyte concentration over space and time divided by the initial 1000 mol/m3 (`r_min`), whether the solve failed, and wall time. A solver failure counts as `r_min = 0`.
- Label (fixed now): a design is *untrusted* when SPMe's cycle-100 score is below DFN's by more than 25% of DFN's (`(DFN - SPMe) / DFN > 0.25`), or SPMe scored 0.
- H1: `r_min` separates untrusted from trusted designs with ROC AUC at least 0.90. Refuted if AUC < 0.80.
- H2: across all 49, `r_min` and the relative gap `(DFN - SPMe) / DFN` have Spearman correlation at most -0.5. Refuted if above -0.3.
- H3 (cost): one 2-cycle SPMe run costs under a tenth of a 2-cycle DFN run on the baseline design (wall time, 3 repeats each, one process). Refuted if the ratio is above 0.25.
- Also reported (no hypothesis): the screen-then-verify replay on the committed scores (DFN on the top-k by SPMe, with and without the flagged designs added), and its regret to the DFN best of the 49.
- Script: `experiments/trust/run.py`; outcome logged above this entry.
