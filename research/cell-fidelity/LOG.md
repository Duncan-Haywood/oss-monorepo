## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 3 (theory, part 2b): can SPMe's own salt-depletion output say *where* its error falls, well enough to plan the DFN budget and to correct the screen? (plan, written before the run; H21 to H24 are this entry's)
- Question: part 2a found that no independent-noise model predicts SPMe's screening cost (it under-predicts SPMe@30 and SPMe@100 by 3.6x and 4.4x), because SPMe's large residuals sit on particular designs, and asked for the residual's location from the physics. Here the location is modelled from SPMe's own solution and tested three ways: as a fit, as a planner, and as a correction to the screen.
- Mechanism (to write out in `THEORY.md`, part 2b; a scaling argument, not a theorem): in PyBaMM 26.10's SPMe the concentration overpotential is chi RT/F times the difference of the x-averages of log c_e over the two electrodes (`electrolyte_conductivity.Composite`, log clipped at a small tolerance), with one reaction rate for the whole electrode. As the cheap solution's minimum salt ratio r = min c_e / c_e0 falls to zero, that term grows like -ln r and pulls the voltage to the 2.5 V cut-off early. The DFN has the same log, but its reaction moves away from the depleted region, so its voltage does not follow ln of the minimum. So SPMe's extra energy loss, and hence its affine residual e = f - (a s + b), should scale with g_log(r) = -ln max(r, 0.01), not with the asymptotic order (1 - r)^2 of the expansion's correction.
- Data: cached scores (`experiments/cycles/bench.jsonl`, `cost.json`, milestone 4's 30 pools of 64). One new PyBaMM pass: SPMe, 100 cycles, on the same 128 designs, same protocol and code path as `cycles/run.py`, recording r_min per cycle (and the most negative x-averaged concentration overpotential per cycle) so that SPMe@n's feature comes from its own first n cycles; its per-cycle energies must reproduce `bench.jsonl` (check reported). The 7 designs whose SPMe run fails have r = 0 by convention (clipped to 0.01).
- Features, fixed now: g_log = -ln max(r, 0.01) (primary); g_quad = (1 - r)^2; g_flag = 1[r <= 0.1] (milestone 1's flag); g_eta = -min eta_c in volts (extra, no verdict).
- H21 (form): on SPMe@100 over all 128 designs, 5-fold cross-validated RMSE (folds fixed by `random.Random(0)`) of the regression f ~ a + b s + beta g: g_log beats g_quad and g_flag, and is at most 0.7 of the regression with no g. Refuted if g_quad or g_flag is lower than g_log. Not blind: part 1's H13 already found Spearman(e, r) = -0.82.
- H22 (planning): the location model e = beta g_log + N(0, sigma0^2), with a, b, beta, sigma0 fitted on a pilot of 16 random designs (20 pilots, median prediction), gives E[R] by part 2a's quadrature with per-design means f - beta g_log. On pools of 64, within a factor of 2 of the observed mean R on at least 3 of the 4 SPMe cells (2, 10, 30, 100 cycles). Refuted if 2 or fewer. Same convention as part 2a: the formula sees the pool's true f; only the residual model is fitted on the pilot.
- H23 (corrected screen, SPMe@100): online corrected screen-then-verify: screen the whole pool, verify in descending s for the first 4 DFN@100 calls, then refit f ~ a + b s + beta g_log by least squares on the verified designs after every call and verify the unverified design with the largest prediction. On pools of 64 under milestone 4's costs, median T1 below SPMe@100 + flag's (417.8 s), paired Wilcoxon p < 0.05. Refuted if its median T1 is higher.
- H24 (corrected screen, cheap rung): the same online correction over SPMe@2 (with its 2-cycle r) has median T1 at most the SPM@2 screen's 168.2 s on pools of 64. Refuted if above 1.2 x 168.2 s.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 4b: does a co-kriging search that picks design and fidelity each step keep the screens' small-pool lead and GP-EI's large-pool one? (plan, written before the run; H18 to H20 are this entry's)
- Question: milestone 4 found that whole-pool screening with a cheap rung (SPM@2, DFN@2) beats GP-EI on DFN@100 on pools of 16 to 64 but loses by 96, because screening pays per candidate. The proposal's evaluation asks for a two-fidelity co-kriging BO (Kennedy and O'Hagan) as the multi-fidelity baseline; this step runs it, with two published fidelity-selection rules, on the cached benchmark (no new PyBaMM runs).
- Model: autoregressive co-kriging, f_h(x) = rho f_l(x) + delta(x), independent GPs (squared-exponential, isotropic, inputs scaled to [0, 1]) for f_l and delta, constant means per fidelity, signal variance profiled out, length scales, rho and the delta variance ratio by grid maximum likelihood, refit every 5 observations. Low rung SPM@2 or DFN@2, high rung DFN@100. Start: the same 5 random designs as GP-EI at DFN@100, plus those 5 at the low rung.
- Rules: (i) augmented EI (Huang, Allen, Notz and Miller 2006): EI of f_h at x times, for a low-rung query, the posterior correlation of f_l(x) and f_h(x) and the cost ratio c_h / c_l; the (design, rung) pair with the largest value is run. (ii) MF-UCB after Kandasamy et al. (2016): pick x by the upper confidence bound of f_h (beta_t = 0.2 d log 2t, their practical setting); run it at the low rung if sqrt(beta_t) times the posterior sd of f_l(x) exceeds gamma = sqrt(c_l / c_h) times the fitted delta sd, else at DFN@100.
- Replay: as milestone 4 (same `random.Random(s).sample` pools, 30 seeds, T1 = charged seconds until a design within 1% of the pool's best DFN@100 has been run at DFN@100), at pool sizes 16, 32, 64, 96. References re-run in the same script: GP-EI at DFN@100 and the SPM@2, SPM@100 and DFN@2 whole-pool screens. A search stops once it hits the 1% target (T1 does not depend on what comes after).
- Costs: (A) each evaluation charged the cell's median baseline fresh-run cost (`experiments/cycles/cost.json`), as milestone 4; (B) each design charged its own cost, cost_base(cell) x seconds_j(model) / median over designs of seconds(model), from the 100-cycle benchmark timings (`bench.jsonl`, run three at a time, so the ratio, not the level, is used). DFN designs range 19 to 67 s there.
- H18: on pools of 64, co-kriging with augmented EI and SPM@2 as the low rung has lower median T1 than GP-EI at DFN@100 (220 s in milestone 4), paired Wilcoxon p < 0.05. Refuted if its median T1 is higher than GP-EI's.
- H19 (the envelope): the better of the four co-kriging variants (2 rules x 2 low rungs), chosen once on pools of 64 under cost A, has median T1 at most GP-EI's on pools of 96 and at most 1.5 times the best whole-pool screen's on pools of 16. Refuted if more than 10% above GP-EI at 96 or more than 2 times the best screen at 16.
- H20 (cost heterogeneity): under cost B the order of the methods' median T1 on pools of 64 agrees with cost A's at Spearman >= 0.8. Refuted below 0.6.
- Also reported: the 95% bootstrap interval of the median, DFN@100 and low-rung calls to T1, and the paired Wilcoxon p of every method against GP-EI. Caveats fixed now: pools share designs, so p-values overstate independence; the grid of hyperparameters is coarse; the pool replay is not a continuous search.

Hypotheses unchanged; the smoke-test T1 values (2 seeds) were seen.

## 2026-11-12 · Milestone 3 (theory, part 2a): does an average-case noise model predict screen-then-verify calls where the worst-case bound failed? (plan, written before the run; H15 to H17 are this entry's)
- Question: part 1's worst-case corollary (H14) called screen against GP-EI right on only 8 of 16 pool sizes, because one large residual widens its band for every design. Here the screen's affine residual is treated as noise. Proposition (to prove in `THEORY.md`): if the affine-mapped screen is s' = f + e with e independent across designs and density g, and T is the set of designs within tolerance, then exactly E[R] = 1 + sum over x not in T of P(s'_x > max over y in T of s'_y), and for Gaussian e with sd sigma that probability is the one-dimensional integral of phi((t - f_x)/sigma)/sigma times the product over y in T of Phi((t - f_y)/sigma). Ranking only needs sigma relative to the spacing of f near the top, so sigma / (density of f in the top few percent) is the screen's average-case cost.
- Data: cached only (`experiments/cycles/bench.jsonl`, `cost.json`, milestone 4's pools), no new PyBaMM runs. Same 11 screen cells and target DFN@100 as part 1, tau = 1%.
- Residual (fixed now): least-squares affine fit of f on s over all 128 designs, e = f - (a s + b); the primary sigma is RMS(e) over the 128, failures (s = 0) included. Extras, no verdicts: sigma from the fit on part 1's top set S_q, and a robust sigma (1.4826 x MAD).
- Models: G, Gaussian e with the primary sigma (exact E[R] by quadrature); B, e drawn with replacement from the cell's own 128 residuals, independent of the design (2000 Monte Carlo draws per pool). B keeps the residual's distribution and drops its link to the design; observed R keeps both.
- H15 (the noise model predicts calls): on milestone 4's 30 pools of 64, model G's mean E[R] over the pools against the observed mean R, over the 11 cells: Spearman >= 0.8 and within a factor of 2 on at least 8 of 11. Refuted if Spearman < 0.6 or fewer than 6 within a factor of 2.

Not blind: the pools come from the same 128 designs the sweep used, and the sweep's medians were in milestone 4's outcome.
- H17 (design-linked residual): for SPMe@100 the independent-residual model B under-predicts the observed mean R on pools of 64 by at least a factor of 2, because SPMe's residual is largest on the designs DFN scores highest (salt depletion in thick, high-energy designs, H13); for SPM@100 and every DFN cell B is within a factor of 2. ## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 3 (theory, part 1): what kind of screen error costs DFN calls, and when does whole-pool screening lose? (plan, written before the run; H11 to H14 are this entry's)
- Question: milestones 2 and 4 found that SPM, whose raw error against DFN is as large as SPMe's (median +7.1 against -8.7 Wh/kg), screens far better, and that every whole-pool screen loses to GP-EI as the pool grows. This step states the theory that should explain both and tests it on the cached data (`experiments/cycles/bench.jsonl`, no new PyBaMM runs).
- Theorem (to write out in `THEORY.md`): pool P, target f (DFN@100), screen s, x* = argmax f, screen-then-verify runs f in descending s. For any u >= 0 and c, let kappa = max over S of |u s(x) - c - f(x)|, where S contains x* and every x with s(x) >= s(x*). Then every design screened ahead of x* has f(x) >= f* - 2 kappa, so (i) top-1 regret <= 2 kappa, (ii) the number R of DFN@100 calls to reach (1 - tau) f* is at most 1 + #{x in P : f* - 2 kappa <= f(x) < (1 - tau) f*}, and (iii) R = 1 when 2 kappa <= tau f*. The best kappa is an affine Chebyshev fit (a linear programme). Corollary: T1 = N c_s + R c_h <= c_h + N (c_s + p(2 kappa) c_h), with p(w) the fraction of the pool within w of the top but outside tolerance, so a screen has an effective cost per candidate c_s + p c_h and loses to a search needing m DFN calls once N > (m - 1) c_h / (c_s + p c_h).
- Diagnostics computed: kappa over all designs (kappa_all) and over S_q = the top q = 25% by s together with the top 25% by f (kappa_q; the theorem applies when x* is in the screen's top 25%, reported), for the 11 (model, cycles) screen cells other than DFN@100, on the 128 and on each pool; raw error = median |s - f|; R per cell over the 30 pools of 64 of milestone 4 (same `random.Random(s).sample` pools).
- C1 (a check, not a hypothesis): bounds (i) and (ii) hold on every (cell, pool) with kappa_all of that pool. Any violation means the derivation or the code is wrong.
- H11 (the residual, not the raw error, predicts screening cost): over the 11 cells, Spearman(kappa_q on the 128, median R over pools) >= 0.7 and exceeds Spearman(raw median |s - f|, median R) by at least 0.2. Refuted if Spearman(kappa_q, median R) < 0.5.
- H12 (SPM against SPMe): on the 121 designs both completed, SPM@100's kappa_all is at most half SPMe@100's. Refuted if SPM's is not smaller.
- H13 (mechanism): the least-squares affine residual of SPMe@100 against DFN@100 (121 designs) is correlated with SPMe's whole-run r_min more strongly than SPM@100's residual is: |Spearman| for SPMe exceeds SPM's by at least 0.2. Refuted if SPM's is at least as large. Not blind: milestone 2 already found r_min flags SPMe's large gaps (AUC 0.994); the SPM side and the residual form are new.
- H14 (crossover): the corollary with p from kappa_q on the 128 and m = GP-EI's median DFN@100 calls on pools of 64 (9) predicts whether each of the four screens in milestone 4's pool sweep (SPM@2, SPM@100, DFN@2, DFN@10) has lower median T1 than GP-EI at pool sizes 16, 32, 64, 96. Supported if at least 13 of 16 calls are right, refuted if 10 or fewer. Not blind: the sweep's medians are in milestone 4's outcome; the formula has no fitted parameter, but the bound is conservative and may call losses that are wins.

- MF-GP-EI (screen score as a fifth GP input) adds nothing on this pool: with DFN@10 it equals the screen (its start is already within 1%), with SPM@2 it is slower than the plain SPM@2 screen (192.7 against 168.2 s), because EI leaves the screen's order.
- Not in the plan, pool-size sweep (30 seeds each): median T1 for GP-EI / SPM@2 / SPM@100 / DFN@2 / DFN@10 screens is 147 / 54 / 66 / 46 / 82 s at 16 designs, 220 / 168 / 153 / 160 / 253 s at 64 and 220 / 240 / 199 / 252 / 368 s at 96. Screening cost grows with the pool and GP-EI's barely does, so whole-pool screening is the right tool only for small candidate sets; a continuous search needs a model on the cheap rung, which is what the BOCA-style baseline (not run yet) should test.
- Caveats: pools are drawn from one set of 128, so seeds share designs and the Wilcoxon p-values overstate independence; costs are one design's timings, so the screens' constant per-design cost hides that thick designs run up to 67 s under DFN.

## 2026-11-11 · Milestone 4: which multi-fidelity search finds the DFN-best design for the least wall time? (plan, written before the run; H7 to H10 are this entry's)
- Question: milestones 2 and 2a ranked single screens; a design loop also pays to screen. On the cached benchmark (`experiments/cycles/bench.jsonl`, no new PyBaMM runs), which search reaches a design within 1% of the best 100-cycle DFN score for the least simulated wall time: plain GP-EI on DFN, a screen with a cheap (model, cycles) cell then DFN verification in screen order, successive halving over DFN cycle counts, or GP-EI on DFN using a cheap screen as an extra input?
- Replay: 30 seeds; seed s draws a pool of 64 of the 128 Sobol designs (`random.Random(s).sample`), so methods are compared paired on the same pools. Each evaluation is charged the median baseline wall time of a fresh run of that (model, cycles) cell from `experiments/cycles/cost.json` (e.g. DFN@100 24.5 s, DFN@10 3.6 s, SPM@2 0.3 s); a design counts as found only once run at DFN@100. Target: within 1% of the pool's best DFN@100. Primary metric T1: charged seconds until the best DFN@100 seen is within 1%. Every method continues until the whole pool is DFN@100-verified, so T1 is finite.
- Methods: (a) random order at DFN@100; (b) GP-EI at DFN@100 (5 random starts, isotropic RBF on the 4 inputs scaled to [0, 1], length scale chosen from a grid by marginal likelihood, standardised y); (c) screen-then-verify for SPM@2, SPM@100, SPMe@100, DFN@2, DFN@10 (screen the whole pool, then DFN@100 in descending screen score); (d) SPMe@100 plus milestone 1's flag: the top 5 by SPMe, then every design with whole-run `r_min <= 0.1` in SPMe order, then the rest; (e) successive halving over DFN at 2, 10, 30, 100 cycles with eta = 3 from the whole pool (Hyperband's most exploratory bracket), every rung charged a fresh run, then the remaining designs verified in order of their last rung's score; an incremental-cost variant (a resumed run pays only the extra cycles) is reported beside it; (f) GP-EI at DFN@100 with the DFN@10 or SPM@2 screen score as a fifth input (input-augmented multi-fidelity GP, after Kennedy and O'Hagan's autoregressive idea), the screen's top design as the start.
- H7 (proposal H3, in wall time): DFN@10 screen-then-verify reaches T1 at median at most a third of GP-EI on DFN's median T1. Refuted if its median regret at a budget of a third of GP-EI's median T1 exceeds 2%.
- H8: successive halving over DFN cycles does not beat the DFN@10 screen. Refuted if its T1 is lower in a paired Wilcoxon test at p < 0.05.
- H9: the cheapest screen, SPM@2 (0.3 s a design), has a lower median T1 than the DFN@10 screen. Refuted if higher at p < 0.05 (paired Wilcoxon).
- H10: the trust flag does not slow SPMe screening: SPMe@100 with the flag has median T1 no higher than SPMe@100 alone. Refuted if higher at p < 0.05.
- Also reported: median and 95% bootstrap interval of T1 per method, median regret at budgets of 60, 120, 240 and 480 s, the number of DFN@100 calls to T1, and the paired Wilcoxon p for every method against GP-EI on DFN.
- Limits fixed now: a pool replay, not a continuous search; costs from one design's timings; BOCA (continuous-fidelity BO) is not run in this step.
- Script: `experiments/search/run.py`; outcome logged above this entry.
