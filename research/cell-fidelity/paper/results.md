The intervals in Section 4.6 were computed for this section, after every milestone had reported; they

## 4.1 Design pools and the quantity scored

The section uses five pools of DFN designs drawn from the same four-parameter Sobol space (negative and
positive electrode thickness, porosity, N/P ratio) on four parameter sets (`Ai2020`, `Chen2020`,
`OKane2022`, `NCA_Kim2011`), each simulated to cycle 100 with the x-resolved plating child of 5zk and
`OKane2022`'s plating parameters:

| pool | designs (Ai, Chen, OKane, NCA) | first used | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5zk | 40 (8, 12, 12, 8) | 5zk | training |
| 5zy | 32 (8 each) | 5zy | training |
| 5zz | 32 (8 each) | 5zz | training |
| 7g | 32 (8 each) | 7g | fresh, scored once by frozen rules |
| 7h | 64 (16 each) | 7h | fresh, scored once by frozen rules |

The scored quantity is the error of a forecast of cycle-99 lithium loss made from the first k cycles of a
design's DFN run (the "real forecast", cell 000 of 5zu's error budget: every input to the fade law forecast,
none measured). A design search pays k DFN cycles per design. The accuracy target is on the tail: the upper
quartile (q75) of the per-design error over a pool must be at most t times the q75 obtained by running every
design of that pool to cycle 50 (U50), with t = 1.25 unless stated.

## 4.2 Where the ten-cycle forecast's error comes from (7a)

On the 64 designs of pools 5zy and 5zz, none of which informed 5zu's error budget, every direction of that
budget replicates (H256 to H259 supported): the anode OCP rise read by the node lever is the largest input on
`OKane2022` and `NCA_Kim2011` (set-median Shapley value &phi;<sub>M</sub> 0.007 and 0.038), the non-OCP plating-overpotential
drift on `Chen2020` (0.015), and the charge-capacity factor contributes nothing anywhere (|&phi;<sub>q</sub>|
&le; 0.004). Its sizes do not replicate. With every input measured, the forecast still misses cycle-99 lithium
loss by a set median of 3.4% on `OKane2022` and 3.2% on `Chen2020`, against 1.5% and 1.4% on 5zk's 40; out
of sample more than half of the ten-cycle error on the two LG M50 sets (set-median shares 0.57 and 0.62) is the
fade law's own term. On `Ai2020` measuring the inputs makes the forecast worse (2.9% to 8.3%). A post hoc
Mann&ndash;Whitney test (two-sided, unplanned) puts 5zk's LG M50 floor below the fresh pools' at p = 0.028:
the in-sample budget was flattering.

## 4.3 The law floor is per-design drift, not a bend (7b)

Lengthening the law's line window from 10 to 50 cycles barely moves the all-measured error on LG M50
(set median 1.8% to 1.6% on `Chen2020`, 1.9% to 1.4% on `OKane2022`), while the real forecast falls from
5.6% to 2.7% and 6.7% to 2.1%: longer windows fix the forecast's inputs, not the law (H260 refuted). With
measured inputs R(L) is a straight line in cycle number at the set median (late-to-early slope ratio 0.99 on
both LG M50 sets) but each design's rate drifts by a few per cent in either direction, so a bend borrowed
from other designs makes the forecast worse (H262 refuted on three of four sets). `Ai2020` is the exception:
its rate accelerates on every design (slope ratio 1.27, IQR 1.24 to 1.34), and the borrowed bend cuts its
floor from 8.3% to 4.8%.

## 4.4 Ten cycles tell which designs will drift (7c)

The drift multiplier m of each design (its law rate over cycles 10 to 99 against its ten-cycle slope)
tracks where the plating sits at cycle 2 (Table 1, &rho; O1): Spearman &rho; = 0.80 on both LG M50 sets,
Holm-adjusted permutation p = 0.0003, and the correlation holds inside each pool (post hoc). On `Ai2020`
(&rho; = 0.37) and `NCA_Kim2011` (&minus;0.08) it is not significant. A ten-cycle correction built on the
observable does not lower the set-median floor (H266 refuted: 1.8% to 2.3%, 1.9% to 2.1%) because the typical
design drifts by about 4%, near the correction's own error; post hoc it halves the upper quartile (8.0% to
3.5%, 7.3% to 3.0%). On the real forecast it beats the line on all four sets (H268). The useful output of 7c
is therefore a risk score, S<sub>C</sub> = |m&#770;<sub>C</sub> &minus; 1|, not a correction.

## 4.6 Out of batch: the honest saving (7e to 7h)

4 of 12 folds, against 7 of 12 for a uniform length (H276 refuted). A margin chosen by nested
leave-one-pool-out (7f) makes the rule hold; Table 2 gives every out-of-batch look at it.

Three findings carry the paper.

1. **The frozen rule replicates on designs nothing had simulated.** 7f's 12 frozen threshold policies met
   the target in 9 of 12 on the 7g pool and again 9 of 12 on the 7h pool (H281, H285 supported). Pooled,
   18 of 24 (0.75; Wilson 0.55 to 0.88). The three policies of a set share one fresh pool, so the 24 are 8
   clusters; a set-clustered bootstrap gives 0.67 to 0.88 for the hit rate and 36.7 to 43.8 cycles per
   design (12% to 27% saved), centred on 40.5 (19%). The same two policies, the most permissive of 7f
   (`Chen2020` and `NCA_Kim2011` trained on 5zk), missed on both fresh pools; the worst miss is
   `Chen2020`'s on 7h at 1.58 times the target (q75 0.089 against 0.056, at 25 cycles per design).
2. **At the same hit rate, triage saves about three times what a uniform run length does.** With each of
   the five pools held out in turn (lopo5, 20 folds) the threshold rule and the uniform rule each meet 19 of
   20, at 43.5 and 48.0 cycles per design (13% and 4% saved). Fold by fold the threshold rule is cheaper on
   14, dearer on 4 and tied on 2 (exact sign test p = 0.031); its mean saving is 4.5 cycles per design, but a
   bootstrap that resamples whole parameter sets gives &minus;8.5 to +0.25, so the advantage is consistent
   across folds and not yet established across chemistries. Only the 4 folds that hold out 7h are blind.
3. **Without a margin set out of batch, triage does not hold.** At zero margin the threshold rule meets 3
   of 12 7f folds and 0 of 4 refits on 7g; widening the flagged fraction alone (F+&Delta;) meets 6 of 12 and
   5 of 12 frozen, because an extension length picked in hindsight is too short on most folds. The margin,
   not the score, is what makes the rule hold; with four training pools it settles the extension at 50
   cycles by itself (H288: the free-K1 and K1 = 50 rules pick the same policy on every set).

Four findings, all descriptive (no hypothesis is tested in this section):

1. **The correction's whole saving is on the plating cells.** On the four committed sets every corrected
   rule costs more than &theta; (+1.38 to +3.25 cycles per design, 0 or 1 folds cheaper, 5 to 8 dearer) and
   meets 16 or 17 folds against 19. At f = 30 every corrected rule stops every design at ten cycles and meets
   10 of 10. The six-set gains of &minus;9.7 to &minus;10.9 cycles are those f = 30 folds averaged in; none of
   the six-set intervals excludes zero, because the set bootstrap resamples only six clusters.
2. **The three gates add nothing beyond two decisions.** Fold for fold, the plating-share gate makes the
   out-of-fold error gate's choice on all 30 six-set folds (&theta;P = &theta;N), and the drift-size gate
   makes the ungated honest rule's choice on all 30 (&theta;D = &theta;H). On these sets, then, there are only
   two corrected policies: "correct everywhere" and "correct everywhere except `Ai2020`". The mechanism signal
   of 7y did not separate the sets any differently from 7x's statistics.
3. **Hindsight cannot rescue the correction on the committed sets either.** The per-set oracle picks the
   straight forecast on all four (and so equals &theta;: 19 of 20 at 43.5). Its six-set saving (&minus;11.83,
   10 cheaper, 0 dearer, p = 0.002) is the ceiling for any gate that chooses per set between &theta; and
   &theta;H. The best deployable gate (&theta;N, &theta;P) reaches 89% of that saving (&minus;10.58) but
   gives up two committed folds to do it (17 of 20 against 19).
4. **At f = 10 the correction is cheap but unreliable.** The corrected rules cost 15.8 to 23.8 cycles per
   design against &theta;'s 32.8 and meet 6 of 10 folds against 7 (U meets 8). `Mohtat2020` at f = 10 is the
   hardest condition in the programme: no rule meets more than 3 of its 5 folds.

8. **The plating-share gate is the one that fails.** On the plating-free cell it switches the correction off
   on every fold (&theta;P = &theta;, 49.2 cycles), while the drift-size gate and the out-of-fold error gate
   keep it on (&theta;D = &theta;N = &theta;H, 10.0 cycles, 5 of 5 met). Before 7zc the three gates came down
   to two decisions on every cell (finding 2); this cell is the first on which &theta;P and &theta;N part.
   So plating share was standing in for drift on the plating cells, and the gate should read drift (or the
   out-of-fold error, which tracks it), not mechanism.
9. **Against triage alone, the eight-set intervals of the gated rules now exclude zero.** &theta;N saves
   15.4 cycles per design (&minus;28.0 to &minus;3.1; 20 cheaper, 5 dearer, p = 0.004) and &theta;D 14.7
   (&minus;27.7 to &minus;1.8; p = 0.036), both meeting 36 of 40 folds against &theta;'s 35; &theta;P's interval
   still reaches +0.12. Of the two, &theta;N is cheaper because it also switches the correction off on
   `Ai2020`; &theta;D is the one with a mechanism behind it. This rests on one engineered cell, built to
   drift after the gates were fixed: with seven conditions no honest-margin interval excluded zero
   (finding 6), so the eighth cell is what moves it, and the reading is "the correction pays wherever the
   drift is large and one-directional", not a new breadth result.
10. **A gate that tests the drift's direction (7ze) is right on seven of the eight sets.** gS, the drift
   gate plus a direction share of at least 0.80 among the training designs' multipliers, is off on

   &theta; (&minus;24.33 to +2.33; 10 cheaper, 4 dearer, sign p = 0.18) and meets 28 of 30 folds, the same
   count as &theta; and one more than any other corrected rule (&theta;N, &theta;P 27; &theta;H, &theta;D 27;
   &theta;C 26). It is the first corrected rule that gives up no folds to triage on six conditions, though with
   six clusters its interval still crosses zero. At f = 10 gS is off on every fold, so &theta;S equals &theta;
   there (7 of 10 at 32.8 cycles), one fold better than every size-only gate (6 of 10) at 9 to 17 more
   cycles: the direction test trades the f = 10 saving for the fold. Against the oracle, &theta;S recovers
   90%, 92% and 95% of the per-set ceiling on six, seven and eight conditions (&minus;10.67 / &minus;11.83,
   &minus;12.03 / &minus;13.03, &minus;15.42 / &minus;16.30), and gS decides differently from gD on 19 of
   the 55 folds, every one a fold where gD was on and the training drift ran both ways (`tables.txt`, the
   direction-aware gate view).

For a practitioner this is the conditional step of the recipe (section 5.1): correct the ten-cycle forecast
when a gate on the training pools' drift size (or the out-of-fold error gate) says so, and otherwise run the
straight forecast with triage. Add the direction test (finding 10): correct only when at least 80% of
the training designs drift the same way. That switches the correction off on three of the four published
sets, though it still corrects `NCA_Kim2011`, which drifts one way (median 0.23) and misses a held-out pool
when corrected. Whether a cell is plating-limited is not
the condition: the plating-free cell of 7zc gains as much as the plating cells. What no gate tried does is
separate `NCA_Kim2011` (one-directional drift, misses a held-out pool when corrected) from the cells where
the correction pays, so on the committed sets the direction-gated rule meets 18 of 20 folds against triage's 19
(the size-only gates 17).

## 4.8 Results that did not hold

Reported so that a reader can calibrate the positive ones:

- **A batch-relative threshold** (R50: flag when S<sub>C</sub> exceeds a multiple of the batch median),
  proposed in 7h to absorb the score's scale drift seen on 7g, did not make the flagged share more stable
  (H287 refuted: mean change 0.095 against 0.069 for the absolute threshold) and met 18 of 20 lopo5 folds
  against 19. It also needs a batch, where the absolute threshold scores one design at a time.
- **In-sample savings shrink by two thirds out of batch:** 33% to 61% in hindsight (7e) against 13% to 21%
  for the same score with an out-of-batch margin.
- **Input fixes for the ten-cycle forecast stopped transferring** once tested on fresh designs: the
  per-node anode thickness line (5zy, 5zz: wins 14 of 29 fresh designs) and nearest-neighbour node-gain
  profiles (5zx). 7a explains why: on fresh LG M50 designs about half the error is the law's floor.

## 4.9 Threats to validity

Every number is a DFN simulation in one PyBaMM version (26.10.1.1) with one fade-law form, one plating
exponent (&kappa; = 25.3) and `OKane2022`'s plating parameters on all four sets; fade is modelled, not
measured, and the score S<sub>C</sub> reads a model internal (cycle-2 plating depth) that a real cell exposes
only through a diagnosis. Pools hold 8 to 16 designs per set, so a pool's q75 rests on one to four designs and
the Wilson intervals in Tables 2 and 3 are wide; the 12 frozen policies of a look are effectively 4 tests. In 7f and
lopo5, S<sub>C</sub> on the 104 is a leave-one-out fit on all pools' drift, which shares information across
pools and flatters any S<sub>C</sub> rule; the fresh pools' S<sub>C</sub> comes from a fit on the 104 only.
R50 was designed after seeing 7g's misses. The clustered bootstraps resample 8 (pooled) or 4 (paired)
clusters, too few for a percentile interval to be more than indicative.
