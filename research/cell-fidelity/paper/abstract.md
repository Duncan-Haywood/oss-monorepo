# Abstract: ten DFN cycles and a drift score, not fifty, for ranking lithium-ion electrode designs by fade

Draft abstract of the `cell-fidelity` paper (target: *Journal of Power Sources* or *Journal of The
Electrochemical Society*; `PROPOSAL.md`, "Target venue"), written around Tables 1 to 3 of
[`results.md`](results.md) after milestone 7za, recipe revised after 7zd. Produced with AI assistance (Claude). Every number is
read from [`tables.txt`](tables.txt) (regenerated by `python3 research/cell-fidelity/paper/tables.py`) or
from the `LOG.md` entry named in brackets; nothing here is new.

---

Electrode design searches rank candidates by simulated capacity fade, and the porous-electrode (DFN) model
that resolves lithium plating costs a full cycling simulation per design. We ask how few DFN cycles a
search can pay per design and still rank designs by their cycle-100 lithium loss to within a fixed tail
accuracy. A fade law fitted to the first ten cycles forecasts cycle-99 loss with an upper-quartile error 1.7
to 3.4 times that of a fifty-cycle fit (Table 1), because individual designs drift from their early slope.
A drift score read at cycle 10 from where the plating sits, at no extra simulation cost, flags the designs
that will drift; only those run on to cycle 50, with the flagging threshold's margin set by nested
leave-one-pool-out. On 200 PyBaMM 26.10.1.1 DFN designs over four published parameter sets, held out one
pool at a time, the rule met its tail target on 19 of 20 folds at 43.5 DFN cycles per design, against 19 of
20 at 48.0 for the best uniform run length (paired sign test p = 0.031; Table 2), and frozen policies met
18 of 24 pool tests on 96 designs no step had simulated, at 40.5 cycles. Run as a one-process-per-design
tool that stops or extends its own solver, it saved 17% of wall time per design [7n]. Across chemistries the
saving is consistent fold by fold but not yet secure: the set-clustered 95% interval of threshold minus
uniform is &minus;8.5 to +0.25 cycles per design on the four sets, &minus;7.55 to &minus;1.43 on seven
set-conditions with three cells made to plate by a pre-registered rate ladder at their probe-chosen rates
[7z], and &minus;6.86 to +2.48 with all three at the faster rate. Where the drift is large and runs
one way, a correction of the ten-cycle forecast fitted on training pools removes most of it: on three tuned
plating cells, the third built after the rule and its gates were fixed and drifting in the opposite
direction, the corrected rule stops at about ten cycles per design and saves 29.8 cycles per design against
the uniform run length (&minus;30.0 to &minus;29.4; 15 of 15 folds cheaper) while meeting 14 of 15 folds
[7za; Table 3], and on an engineered cell that drifts without plating it saves 40 while meeting 5 of 5
[7zc]. Gated on the training designs' drift size, the correction saves 14.7 cycles per design against
triage alone over eight set-conditions (&minus;27.7 to &minus;1.8; 36 of 40 folds met against 35), an
interval that excludes zero only with the engineered cell included; a gate on plating share switches the
correction off on that cell and is dropped. On the four published sets the correction still costs 1.4 to
3.3 cycles per design more than triage and meets fewer folds, and no gate tried separates them from the
cells where it pays (Table 3). The recipe for practitioners is therefore drift-score triage, plus the corrected forecast
gated on drift size or out-of-fold error rather than on mechanism, at a cost of +1.9 to +3.3 cycles per
design and two folds on the published sets, where the gates cannot tell two-way drift from one-way. All
results are model-to-model; a check against measured cells remains open.

**What changed from the earlier framing.** The README's headline once rested on the four-set interval
alone. Since then 7r to 7za added three tuned cells; the abstract now states the interval as a range over
how the tuned cells are made, and gives the correction its own sentence with its out-of-sample check.
After 7zc and 7zd the correction's condition changed from "plating-limited" to "large one-directional
drift": a plating-free cell gains as much as the plating cells, and the plating-share gate is the one gate
that gets it wrong. Two
claims are deliberately absent: that the threshold rule beats a uniform run length across chemistries
(no interval that includes every tuned rate excludes zero), and that a gate says when to correct on the published sets (none does; even a hindsight per-set oracle
chooses the straight forecast there).
