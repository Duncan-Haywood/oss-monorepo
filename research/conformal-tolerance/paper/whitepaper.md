# Conformal tolerances: how many honest runs before a slashing threshold is safe, and what the margin costs

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Every tolerance-based verifier in this repo (`freivalds-float`, `reproducible-refereed-training`, `spot-check-slashing`, `interval-elicitation`) needs a number `t`: slash a worker whose drift score exceeds `t`. In practice `t` comes from a finite set of honest calibration runs. Split-conformal calibration (Vovk; Angelopoulos–Bates) takes `t = X_(k)`, the `k`-th order statistic of `n` honest scores, and its false-slash law is exact and distribution-free because it depends only on ranks. I work out what that means for slashing. (i) For a fresh honest run the false-slash probability is exactly `(n+1-k)/(n+1)`, but the rate *conditional on the calibration set* is `Beta(n+1-k, k)`: with the standard `k=⌈(n+1)(1-α)⌉` only 51–56% of calibration sets give a rate ≤ α (α=1%, n=500–20000), because the sampling sd `≈√(α/n)` is comparable to α until `n ≫ 1/α`. (ii) A threshold that is ≤ α with probability ≥ 1-δ (PAC) exists iff `n ≥ ln δ / ln(1-α)`: 459 runs at α=δ=1%, 4603 at α=0.1%; at the minimum the tolerance is the sample maximum. (iii) The margin is paid in hiding room: for Pareto(a) drift the PAC threshold exceeds the true (1-α)-quantile by `(w/α)^{-1/a}` (`w` = the PAC rank's nominal tail mass), 1.71× (a=3) and 2.93× (a=1.5) at n=500, 1.26× and 1.59× at n=2000, 1.03–1.05× at n=10⁵; since a cheater hides corruptions of size ∝ `t`, calibration runs directly buy hiding room. (iv) Gaussian plug-in tolerances are not safe: on lognormal drift with exact mean and sd they slash honest workers 2.4–17× more often than nominal at α ≤ 1%. (v) Pooling calibration across hardware classes gives the right marginal rate only at the calibration mix: with three classes at median drift 1:2:4, the slow class is slashed 4.8% (nominal 1%), the fast class 0%, and the fast class can hide 2.9× more than its own quantile would allow; per-class (Mondrian) calibration costs 3×459 runs. (vi) On emulated float32 Freivalds residuals a conformal tolerance sits at 1.9× the mean residual. Synthetic and emulated data; the formulas are exact, the application is stylised.

## 1. Setup
Honest scores `X_1..X_n, X_new` exchangeable and continuous (ties broken at random). The verifier slashes iff `X_new > X_(k)`; `k>n` means no tolerance (never slash). Then `P(X_new > X_(k)) = (n+1-k)/(n+1)` over the joint draw, for every distribution — tested for exponential, Pareto(1.5), lognormal(σ=2) within 0.008 by simulation. With `k=⌈(n+1)(1-α)⌉` this is ≤ α. For a slashing protocol this is the wrong quantity: the worker's stake faces the *conditional* rate `1-F(X_(k))` of the tolerance actually deployed.

## 2. Conditional rate and the PAC condition
`F(X_(k))` is `Beta(k, n+1-k)`, so the conditional rate has mean `(n+1-k)/(n+1)` and variance `ab/((a+b)²(a+b+1))` with `a=n+1-k, b=k` (checked: 3 ranks × 30,000 calibration sets, variance within 8×10⁻⁴). `P(rate ≤ α) = P(Bin(n,1-α) ≤ k-1)`, verified by simulation within 0.01. For the standard rank this is 0.560, 0.543, 0.519, 0.510 at n=500, 1000, 5000, 20000 (α=1%): it tends to ½, the median of a Beta, not to 1. The PAC rank is the smallest `k` with that probability ≥ 1-δ; `k=n` (the sample maximum) gives `1-(1-α)^n`, hence `n ≥ ln δ/ln(1-α)` (E1: 45, 230, 2302, 23025 runs for α = 5%, 1%, 0.1%, 0.01% at δ=10%). Simulated at n=2000 (α=δ=1%, 400 calibration sets each): the standard rank meets α for 0.53 (lognormal) and 0.52 (Pareto-3) of sets, exact 0.530; the PAC rank 1.000 and 0.995 (exact 0.9952).

## 3. The margin is hiding room
The PAC rank sits above the nominal quantile. For Pareto(a) drift `q(w)=w^{-1/a}`, so the threshold relative to the true (1-α)-quantile is `(w/α)^{-1/a}` with `w=(n+1-k)/(n+1)`. E4 (α=δ=1%): n=500 → ×1.31 (a=6), 1.71 (a=3), 2.93 (a=1.5); n=2000 → 1.12, 1.26, 1.59; n=10⁵ → 1.01, 1.03, 1.05. Simulated median at n=2000: 1.266 (Pareto-3, formula 1.260), 1.287 (lognormal σ=1). In `freivalds-float` a corruption of Frobenius norm 1.483 `t` is missed half the time, so a 26% inflation of `t` is a 26% larger corruption hidden at the same rate, and heavy tails (small `a`) need far more calibration runs for the same room. This gives a concrete price for `n`: verifier compute spent on honest calibration vs the hiding budget conceded.

## 4. Plug-in tolerances and hardware mixes
E5: fitting a Gaussian (exact mean and sd, no sampling noise) to lognormal(0,σ) drift and setting `t=μ+z_{1-α}sd` yields true false-slash rates of 0.064/0.024/0.0073 (σ=0.25) and 0.050/0.029/0.017 (σ=1) at nominal 5%/1%/0.1%; skew makes the Gaussian tail too light in the wrong place. Conformal calibration has no such failure at any n, only variance. E6: three hardware classes with median drift 1:2:4, lognormal σ=0.5, calibration mix (0.5, 0.3, 0.2), α=1%: the pooled tolerance 9.18 slashes the slow class at 4.83%, the mid class 0.115%, the fast class ~0, and its hiding room relative to the per-class quantile is 2.87×, 1.43×, 0.72×. The marginal rate is right only at the calibration mix (0.010) and becomes 0.029 at mix (0.1, 0.3, 0.6). A silent driver update scaling fast-class drift by c=1.5, 2, 3 moves the false-slash rate from 0 to 0.0001, 0.0012, 0.0126 under the pooled tolerance: shift is invisible until it matters. Per-class calibration restores per-class validity at 459 runs per class (1377 in total; 2295 runs at the calibration mix to see 459 of the 20% class).

## 5. Float32 check
E7: 199 honest emulated-float32 residuals `‖A(Br)−Cr‖` (n=12) set a conformal tolerance (rank 196, α=2%) of 8.68×10⁻⁷ = 1.91× the mean residual (Gaussian fit mean+2.05 sd: 8.29×10⁻⁷). On 600 fresh honest products the false-slash rate is 0.032 (Gaussian fit 0.040) against marginal 0.020: 2 binomial standard errors, but the conditional-rate sd of this calibration set is 0.010, so it is one draw from §2, not evidence against the formula. A one-probe Freivalds corruption of Frobenius norm `t/1.483` (1.29× the mean noise) is missed half the time.

## 6. Limits
Exchangeability is assumed and, as the shift rows show, is the weak point: temporal drift, hardware changes, or a prover who poisons calibration (e.g. supplies the "honest" runs) break it; rolling recalibration, weighted conformal for known covariate shift, and adversarial calibration are not treated here. The hiding-room formula is for Pareto tails and nominal tail mass; other tails follow by substituting their quantile function. Scores are scalar; no multi-test or sequential control (see `sequential-slashing`, `forecast-duel` for anytime-valid alternatives). Related: Vovk et al. (2005); Angelopoulos and Bates, *A gentle introduction to conformal prediction* (2021); Vovk (2012) on conditional validity and the training-conditional (PAC) guarantee; Freivalds (1977); Gensyn's Verde/RepOps. In this repo: `freivalds-float`, `reproducible-refereed-training`, `property-elicitation-verification`, `tail-risk-elicitation`, `verification-game`.
