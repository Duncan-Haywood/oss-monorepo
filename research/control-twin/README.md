# Control twin: what does a controller designed in a twin with the wrong actuator gain cost in the real world?

Pure Python, no dependencies. A robot's controller (here scalar LQR, plant `x' = a x + b u + w`, cost `q x² + r u²`) is often designed inside a digital twin whose actuator gain `b_t` is a guess. The real cost under any fixed gain `K` is exact: `(q + rK²)W/(1 − (a−bK)²)` when `|a−bK|<1`, infinite otherwise. With real `a`=0.9, `b`=1, `q`=1, `r`=0.1: a twin that believes the actuator is half as strong (`m = b_t/b` = 0.5) claims cost 1.245 but the real cost is 1.508 (regret +40%), at `m`=0.3 claims 1.52 against 4.25 (+296%), and at `m`=0.2 the real loop is **unstable** (pole −1.02) while the twin claims a finite 1.87. Unlike the estimator analogue in `filter-twin`, a controller *does* have an instability cliff: as `r→0` the loop diverges iff `m < a/(a+1)` (0.474 at `a`=0.9, matched to 4 digits), and for an open-loop-unstable plant (`a`>1) also for a too-strong twin (`m` > 6.0 at `a`=1.2, r→0). With a control penalty the unstable set is a *band* (`m` ∈ (0.089, 0.214) at `a`=0.9, `r`=0.1) and the loop is stable again below it, so regret is non-monotone (`m`=0.1 is infinite, `m`=0.01 costs +18%). A too-strong twin is safer: factor 2 costs +20% against +40% for factor ½, and factor 3 +48% against +188%. **Identifiability negative result:** running the twin's controller on the real robot identifies only the closed-loop pole `a−bK`; `a` and `b` separately are not identifiable without exploration noise (information matrix singular; the regression is singular in simulation). With dither of std `d` the least-squares covariance is closed-form (`Var b̂ = W/(nd²)`) and the certainty-equivalent retune has a delta-method regret `½tr(HΣ)` that matches simulation (e.g. `m`=0.5, `d`=1: 0.0018 vs 0.0018 at `n`=400, 0.00011 vs 0.00011 at `n`=6,400; regret·`n` roughly constant, `∝ 1/n`). Dither costs real performance while collecting (excess 0.016/0.145/1.608 per step at `d`=0.1/0.3/1.0), so the total-cost-optimal dither shrinks with the data budget (best on the tested grid `d`=0.5 at `n`=400, 0.3 at `n`=1,600 for a 50,000-step horizon). See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

**Builds on.** The UAV / field-robot safe-autonomy direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>) and the sim-to-real manipulation direction of the HIRO group (<https://hiro-group.ronc.one/>), where controllers are designed against simulated actuators. No specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The LQR design is classical (Kalman 1960; Anderson & Moore 1971); the lack of identifiability under closed loop and the need for excitation are standard in adaptive control (Åström & Wittenmark 1995; Ljung 1999), and the retune is plain certainty equivalence with least-squares identification. Companion to `filter-twin` (the estimation dual), `timestep-twin` and `latency-twin` in this repository.

Stylised: scalar linear-Gaussian plant, twin wrong only in `b` (`a` known), stationary regime, fixed gain, no actuator saturation, no robot data. The delta-method regret is asymptotic and the dither trade-off is a grid search on one setting. Preliminary. MIT.
