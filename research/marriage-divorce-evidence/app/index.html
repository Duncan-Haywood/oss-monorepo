Divorce odds calculator for couples

Enter two profiles to see what five explainable machine-learning models, trained on 20 years of U.S. survey data on married couples, estimate for your odds of divorce in each year of marriage — and which changes they associate with lower risk. Everything runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Read this first. These are statistical associations in one older U.S. cohort (people born 1957–1964, observed 2000–2022), not a forecast for you and not causes. The best model beats a flat average rate by only about 3% on held-out people. Relationship quality, conflict, fidelity and commitment — which matter a great deal — are not in the data. Use it to explore the evidence, not to choose or judge a partner.

      How it works and what it cannot tell you

      • Data. National Longitudinal Survey of Youth 1979 (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics): 40,818 two-year intervals from 5,735 married respondents and 726 legal divorces, survey-weighted. Respondents were 35–64; marriages up to 49 years long.
      • Models. Two additive Poisson regressions, a spline model with schooling-pair and earnings-role interactions, and two shallow Poisson decision trees — all trained in scikit-learn, exported to JSON and run here by a small JavaScript engine that reproduces scikit-learn’s predictions to 10−9 (tested).
      • Per year. Each model gives an annual divorce rate for the couple at each marriage year (ages and duration advance; everything else stays as entered). A year’s divorce probability is 1 − e−rate, and the chance of still being married multiplies those years together.
      • Couple. The survey interviewed one spouse. The app scores the couple from both partners’ points of view and averages them. Same-sex couples were not in the training data, so the app shows no number for them rather than guessing.
      • Outside the data. Years where either partner is under 35 or over 64 are outside what the models saw; the chart shades them and the numbers there are extrapolation. Younger cohorts, recent marriages, wealth, occupation, children and relationship quality are not covered.
      • Not causal, not personal. Coefficients describe who divorced in one cohort. No validated “ideal partner”, safe anniversary or personal forecast is established. Full methods: ml_couples/METHODS.md and the model card.
