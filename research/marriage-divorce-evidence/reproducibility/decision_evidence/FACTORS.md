# How strong is the evidence for each thing the app lets you change?

Generated by `scripts/build.py` from `factor_grades.json`; do not edit by hand. The grade is the strongest design that estimates an effect of the factor **on divorce or separation**: experimental > quasi-experimental > prospective observational (adjusted) > cross-sectional. Event-history models of dated survey marital histories (NSFG, SIPP) with covariates fixed before the outcome count as adjusted longitudinal evidence; zero-order estimates and one-time retrospective online surveys count as cross-sectional. Studies of other outcomes (relationship quality, poverty), and studies where the factor was not what was randomised (marked indirect), are listed but do not raise the grade. Every number below is copied from a mechanically verified record in [`evidence.json`](evidence.json) or the project's [literature evidence](../literature_evidence/EVIDENCE.md).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Age / timing of marriage** | Timing a marriage: marrying later rather than sooner | prospective observational adjusted | later marriage, lower risk (steepest below the early twenties, flattening after about 25-28) | Teen marriage roughly doubles or more the hazard relative to marrying in the early-to-mid twenties (adjusted HR 2.42 for 18 or younger vs 22-24; this project 2.63 at 17 vs about 23). Beyond the late twenties the gains are small. | Consistent across many cohorts and surveys after adjustment, so waiting past the teens and early twenties is a reasonable bet, but no experiment or instrument estimates the effect on divorce itself. The only quasi-experimental study (state marriage-age laws) shows teen marriage causes later poverty, not divorce. |
| **Finishing education (degree before marriage)** | Finishing a degree before marrying; choosing a partner by education | prospective observational adjusted | more education, lower risk | A bachelor's degree is associated with roughly 30-40% lower hazard after adjustment (this project 0.63; Killewald 2016: wife 0.71, husband 0.57). | Robust association; how much is education itself versus the traits and circumstances that lead to college is not identified. |

Expect small gains in relationship quality; a lower divorce risk is plausible for some higher-risk married couples but is not reliable across programmes or populations. |
| **Couples counselling / therapy** | Seeking couple therapy when distressed | experimental | better relationship satisfaction; effect on divorce vs no therapy not established | Behavioural couple therapy vs no treatment: d = 0.585 (30 RCTs).
