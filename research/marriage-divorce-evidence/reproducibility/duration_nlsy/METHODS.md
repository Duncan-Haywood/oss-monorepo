# Marriage duration: conditional annual incidence and fixed-band comparisons

## Scope and risk population

This analysis asks how the **observed annual rate of subsequent legal divorce** differs with years already married. It uses the complete joint-couple model sample from the preceding profile analysis: NLSY79 respondents born 1957–64, currently married with a confirmed opposite-sex co-resident spouse at baseline, positive known combined wages, both spouses' schooling, and valid marriage-duration/order data. Nominal survey intervals run from 2000→2002 through 2020→2022. This restricted sample has 34,359 observed intervals, 5,295 respondents and 592 divorces. It is not the broader married-or-separated denominator from the earlier annual analysis.

Exposure ends at the first recorded legal divorce, widowhood or next interview. Separation within an observed interval does not censor, but the next baseline must meet the co-resident married sample restriction. Absent follow-up is not counted as a divorce-free interval. Marriage duration is reconstructed from the most recent valid marriage-start month at or before baseline. The underlying event/roster/wage selection, timing, weights and limitations are documented in the previous annual, pairing and couple-profile methods.

This is a **left-truncated midlife/later-life cohort**. Marriages that ended before they could enter these observed risk sets are not recovered as early-marriage person-time. Early-duration observations often concern later first marriages or remarriages, not a representative newlywed cohort beginning at age 20–30. Later-duration observations necessarily select surviving marriages. The resulting rates describe observed future incidence conditional on being in these risk sets; they are not inception-cohort lifetime risks or personal predictions.

## Exact allocation of observed time to duration bands

Predefined bands are 0–<5, 5–<10, 10–<15, 15–<20, 20–<25, 25–<30, 30–<40 and 40+ years. Each interval's actual observed person-time is split at any crossed band boundaries. For example, an interval beginning at 4.5 married years and ending at 6.5 contributes 0.5 years to the first band and 1.5 to the second. A terminal divorce or widowhood contributes its event only to the **last positive-length segment**. An event exactly at a boundary is assigned to the band ending there; dates have month rather than day resolution. We never assign the whole interval to its baseline-duration bin.

The split creates 46,227 person-time segments. It preserves all 69,185.42 unweighted person-years, all 592 divorce events, all 211 widowhood censors, and weighted exposure exactly, up to floating-point tolerance. Each segment retains baseline education, earnings, wave, respondent sex, survey weight and marriage order. Attained age is approximated at the segment midpoint by baseline interview age plus elapsed years; this does not pretend birth/interview day precision.

## Crude rates and adjusted model

Crude annual incidence is weighted divorce events divided by weighted observed exposure in each duration band, expressed per 1,000 person-years. Repeated segments and intervals are clustered by respondent for uncertainty. A respondent can contribute to several duration bands. With only two divorces in the 40+ band, its interval is very wide and it cannot establish a reliable late-life threshold.

A joint weighted Poisson event-rate model uses log segment exposure as an offset and includes categorical duration band, both spouses' schooling-year tiers, combined wage quartile, earnings role, attained age, first/subsequent marriage order, baseline wave, and respondent sex. Unlike the earlier profile model, continuous marriage duration is replaced by these categorical bands. The 36-column design is full rank. The model assumes additive log-rate associations and a common duration association across included covariate groups; it does not fit age/order interactions or a discovered change point. Attained age and marriage duration are related, so their separation depends on model structure and observed variation.

Adjusted margins apply each duration band to one **common observed target distribution at attained ages 55–<65**, using baseline survey weight×observed segment exposure. The model itself uses all observed ages. Restricting the standardization target avoids assigning a forty-year marriage to a 35-year-old. The target contains 3,292 respondents, 60 divorce events and 16,863.25 observed person-years. Some target-duration cells remain sparse; adjusted margins borrow information from all fitted ages and bands under the additive model. They are model-standardized late-midlife reference rates, not directly observed rates for every possible combination or a forecast for a younger couple. All target support counts are supplied. The target distribution is treated as fixed for uncertainty.

## Differences, ratios and multiplicity

Seven **predefined adjacent-band** contrasts compare each band with its predecessor. These compare interval-average hazards, not an instantaneous change at an anniversary. Standardized absolute rate differences use their shared coefficient covariance and delta-method normal confidence intervals. Rate ratios use log-delta intervals. Two-sided Wald p-values test the corresponding log-rate ratio. Holm correction controls multiplicity across these seven comparisons; the displayed ordinary 95% intervals themselves are not familywise simultaneous intervals. No band boundaries are moved to improve significance and no change-point scan is performed.

An exploratory omnibus seven-degree-of-freedom respondent-cluster Wald test assesses whether all duration indicators jointly equal zero. Its p-value concerns a general association across fixed bands, not a particular step or causal threshold. It is distinct from the seven adjacent comparisons. Lack of a significant adjacent difference does not prove the absence of an underlying threshold, especially with sparse late-duration events and coarse bands.

## Interpretation of going-forward risk

A rate at 20–<25 married years describes observed divorce incidence during those years among eligible marriages still contributing time. It is **not** the chance of divorce at exactly the twentieth anniversary, and it is not a five-year probability conditional on reaching year 20. We do not multiply an annual rate by five or use 1−exp(−5×rate) as a substitute for a properly estimated conditional five-year cumulative incidence. Hazards, age, calendar time, separation state and competing widowhood can change over that period. A conditional multi-year risk analysis would require an appropriate landmark/survival design with follow-up and competing risks.

## Uncertainty and reproducibility

Each segment uses the official sampling weight of its interval's baseline wave. Log-rate crude confidence intervals use respondent-cluster linearization. Regression uses a respondent-cluster sandwich covariance with finite-sample correction. These are weight-adjusted, person-cluster **approximations**, not full survey-design confidence intervals: original PSU/stratum/household dependence, uncertainty in weights, selection due to complete observations and nonresponse, and possible duplicated couple membership are not fully represented. Sparse events, topcoded/proxy wages, schooling years rather than credentials, retained midlife marriages and incomplete histories limit generalization. No causal duration effect is claimed.
