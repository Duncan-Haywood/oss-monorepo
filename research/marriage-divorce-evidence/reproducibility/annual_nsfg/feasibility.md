# NSFG annual comparison feasibility

The 2017–2019 public files do not support defensible exact annual first-marriage divorce incidence or annual marital-duration hazard estimates without additional assumptions.

1. MARDAT01 and MARDIS01 retain only calendar year; century-month dates are restricted. Their subtraction misclassifies actual elapsed duration by nearly a year. End dates may identify separation rather than legal divorce. A calendar-year event count also requires clearly reconstructed start-of-year risk sets, survival/exposure and competing events; the broad public date resolution is insufficient for exact person-time.
2. MAR1DISS gives duration to dissolution OR interview in 0–12,13–24,25–36,37–48,>48 months. Within a yearly duration interval, we cannot establish whether censoring preceded the failures, nor the fraction of person-time contributed by censored marriages. An event-to-observed-risk-set ratio would be a grouped approximation, not an exact one-year risk or rate. With exposure assumptions, actuarial life tables can be calculated, but putting them alongside directly observed ACS past-year divorce prevalence would falsely suggest identical definitions.
3. Interview covariates (degree, income, employment, religious attendance) may occur after dissolution. First-husband education is retrospectively reported at current/past highest attainment rather than explicitly at wedding. They cannot define prospectively low-risk profiles.
4. Current religious attendance is measured once. ATTND14 is only asked to respondents screened under25 and is not usable to measure pre-dissolution attendance in the main age25–49 population. Religion raised is an affiliation measure, not repeated religious practice.

## Recommendation

Do not annualize historical cumulative prevalence by dividing by years, and do not transform the prevalence with `1-(1-p)^(1/t)` to create a purported observed annual rate. Neither is justified by heterogeneous time since first marriage and censoring. Do not present first48-month grouped hazards as apples-to-apples equivalents of ACS annual marital events. Keep NSFG historical first-marriage and pairing results as a separate outcome family.

For annual comparisons, use ACS's directly asked divorce-in-past12-month indicator and explicitly stated denominator. It is a past-year divorce event prevalence, not lifetime risk, and interview socioeconomic measures still follow the event for many respondents. NSFG can add descriptive current-religion associations with historical outcomes, not annual religion effects.

## Data needed for prospective religion and profile analysis

Obtain longitudinal observations with dates of first marriage, legal divorce, separation, spouse death, survey entry/exit; pre-event and repeated attendance frequency; pre-event income, employment, degree and partner degree; age, cohort and relevant confounders; longitudinal survey and attrition weights plus design/replicate variables. Construct annual or monthly marriage-at-risk episodes. Lag covariates before each interval; estimate discrete-time or survival hazards with duration controls and competing risks. Standardize predictions to a shared population and fixed horizon; give uncertainty and external validation. Distinguish predictive profiles from causal effects, and account for selection into marriage and attendance changes driven by marital problems. PSID may support employment/income history; suitable repeated religious-practice coverage must be verified rather than assumed. Restricted NSFG dates alone would improve event timing but cannot recover pre-event religious-practice history from a single current measure.

## Narrow calendar-year exception

Year-level dates can support a narrowly defined retrospective calendar-year event proportion for marriages already in existence before that year: e.g., among first marriages beginning before2016 and intact on January1,2016, the fraction ending in separation/divorce/annulment during calendar2016. The year-only dates suffice for that boundary-based definition, unlike exact elapsed-duration or person-year exposure rates. Select a calendar year fully preceding every interview; ascertain those with first-marriage year<2016 and end year>=2016 or intact, then count endyear2016 with MAREND01=1/2. The population remains the respondents who survived and were represented in the 2017–2019 interview sample, not the national population of married people in2016, and attained education/current attendance still follow the event. It excludes marriages formed during the target year. It mixes historical marriage cohorts/durations and separation/legal divorce timing, with limited events. This is potentially reportable as its own retrospective measure with clear design limitations, but does not solve the requested current annual apples-to-apples profile comparison; it was not estimated here. Thus the restriction is to exact annual hazards/rates and comparable population interpretation, not a claim that no calendar-year event proportion can ever be computed from year dates.
