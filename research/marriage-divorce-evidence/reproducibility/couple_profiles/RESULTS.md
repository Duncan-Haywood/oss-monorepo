# Standardized whole-couple results

Using the same age, marriage-duration, marriage-order, calendar-wave and respondent-sex distribution, the supported both-16+-schooling/top-total-wage-quartile profile with balanced earnings has an estimated 6.29 annual divorces per 1,000 person-years (approximate 95% CI 4.66–8.48). With husband-majority earnings while both spouses earn positive wages, it is 4.56 (3.40–6.11). The direct difference is −1.72 per 1,000 (−3.12 to −0.33). Actual supporting cells contain 19 and 16 divorce events respectively, so these remain modest and historical samples, not individual forecasts.

The both-16+/top-total-wage-quartile/husband-only configuration has just four observed divorces. Its model profile estimate is suppressed by the joint-support screen. These data cannot establish that high male earnings plus zero female earnings is the optimal full configuration.

Holding other measured covariates at the same distribution, total wage quartile Q4 versus Q1 shows essentially no independent difference: +0.04 annual divorces per 1,000 (−2.57 to +2.65), rate ratio 1.005 (0.736–1.372). Thus the lower estimated rate for some high-resource complete profiles should not be attributed automatically to their total earnings.

Both spouses having 16+ rather than 12 schooling years has a standardized difference of −3.41 per 1,000 (−5.58 to −1.24), rate ratio 0.619 (0.454–0.843), conditional on the included earnings and demographic covariates. These are schooling years, not confirmed bachelor's/graduate credentials, and the model does not establish causality.

Husband-only versus balanced earnings, at the same total-wage and covariate distribution, differs by −0.58 per 1,000 (−3.02 to +1.86): no clear advantage. Wife-majority/both-positive versus balanced is +3.75 (−0.02 to +7.52), with substantial uncertainty; the absolute interval includes zero despite a borderline positive log-ratio interval. Avoid treating this as a firm female-earner penalty.

The results rely on an additive association model, common-target standardization, midlife marriages surviving to observation, complete cases, and approximate survey uncertainty. They are exploratory, not a ranking of ideal spouses or a personal-risk calculator. Full profiles, support counts, direct contrasts and reproducible calculations are supplied in the adjacent CSVs and scripts.
