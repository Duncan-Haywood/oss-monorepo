# Complete-couple profiles on one common target distribution

## Question and target

The objective is to compare whole baseline couple configurations on the same annual legal-divorce incidence scale, avoiding comparisons of a high-income male marginal statistic with an unrelated female marginal statistic. We use the validated NLSY79 predissolution spouse-pair intervals described in `../pairing_nlsy/METHODS.md`: opposite-sex, currently married, co-resident spouses at baseline, waves 2000→2002 through 2020→2022, with month-resolved exposure until first legal divorce, widowhood or next interview. Separation within an interval does not censor. These are married-respondent person-years, not an independently enumerated registry of unique couples. The population is the cohort born 1957–64 during midlife/later life, not newlyweds of every generation.

The model sample requires positive known combined wage/salary earnings, both spouses' baseline schooling tiers, current age, current-marriage duration, and known first/subsequent marriage order. It contains 34,359 intervals, 5,295 respondents, 592 observed divorces and 69,185.42 unweighted person-years. Both-zero wages and missing wages are not recoded as balanced earnings and are outside this model. Missing histories/follow-up/covariates and selection into baseline co-resident marriage remain limitations.

## Joint model and measurement

A single weighted Poisson event-rate model has a log exposure offset and includes both spouses' schooling tiers; weighted within-wave combined positive-wage quartile; earnings roles (wife share 40–60% balanced, husband-majority/both positive, husband-only wages, wife-majority/both positive, wife-only wages); current age; current-marriage duration; first/subsequent marriage order; baseline wave; and respondent sex. We do not include mismatch direction in addition to the actual two schooling levels here. The model assumes additive log-rate associations without profile interactions; profiles borrow information through that assumption. It is an association model, not a calibrated or validated individual prediction tool.

Schooling tiers are <12, 12, 13–15, 16–17 and 18+ completed years. They are not verified degree categories. 'Both 16+' standardizes over a **fixed mixture** of the four observed 16–17/18+ husband×wife combinations among model-sample couples with both spouses at least 16 years, weighted by baseline-weighted person-years. Those mixture weights are saved. It does not equate 16 years to a bachelor's credential, nor distinguish master's, professional or doctoral degrees.

Wages are respondent/spouse salary, wages, commissions and tips from the same preceding calendar year, not combined family income, investment income, assets, pensions or self-employment profit. Both partners use the same period and revised topcodes when available. Earnings-role ratios are undefined at zero total wages. Total-wage quartiles are computed within each baseline wave; Q4 is not a fixed $200,000 threshold. Zero wife wages do not establish zero resources or financial dependence.

Current-marriage duration uses the latest valid marriage-start month before baseline. First/subsequent order is reconstructed from the largest reported marriage-start sequence ordinal (1–7) at or before baseline. A known ordinal 2 or greater is subsequent even if an earlier start is missing; a known ordinal 1 with no later pre-baseline start is first. Unknown order is excluded, not classified first. These controls use historical events already predating baseline; future degree or earnings reports are not used.

## Standardization

Every displayed profile is applied to the **same** model-sample distribution of current age, marriage duration, first/subsequent order, wave and respondent sex. Target weights are baseline survey weight×observed exposure, normalized to one. We predict annual rate lambda=exp(X beta) for each target row after setting the profile's schooling composition, combined-wage quartile and earnings role, then average over this fixed target. For 'both 16+' we additionally average over the fixed schooling-pair mixture described above. All profile differences therefore share a common covariate distribution instead of being unadjusted differences between differently aged populations.

The direct combined-wage Q4-versus-Q1 contrast sets only the wage quartile and preserves each target row's other observed predictors. The schooling both-16+-versus-both-12 contrast sets only those schooling compositions and preserves other predictors. Earnings-role contrasts set only role, retaining the same total-wage distribution and schooling covariates. These comparisons hold covariates fixed statistically; they are not interventions and do not include indirect associations through income, marriage timing or other pathways. Some set combinations are less represented in the data; additive-model extrapolation remains possible even when marginal support is large.

For standardized rate R, its beta gradient is the weighted average of lambda×X. Log-delta intervals use var(log R)=gradient(R)' V gradient(R)/R², ensuring positive rate limits. Direct absolute differences use the gradient difference and symmetric normal intervals; direct rate ratios use the difference between log-rate gradients and exponentiated normal intervals. Both sides of a contrast use the same coefficient covariance, so overlapping profile confidence intervals are not the test of their difference. Absolute differences are the main comparison: borderline log-ratio intervals can differ in apparent significance because of nonlinear interval scales. The target distribution and schooling mixture are treated as fixed; uncertainty in their estimation is not propagated.

## Support and selection of archetypes

Archetypes were selected for interpretable contrasts rather than by searching a complete grid for the lowest fitted risk. This is an **exploratory analysis, not a preregistered confirmatory comparison**. We inspected joint-cell support and use a transparent display screen of at least 100 distinct respondents and 10 observed divorce events. Counts refer to actual jointly observed configurations in the complete model sample; the same person can contribute to more than one configuration over time. They describe support, not independent replication of a standardized prediction.

Six archetypes meet this screen. Three college-schooling/high-total-wage configurations with husband-only, wife-majority, or wife-only earnings do not, despite superficially appealing point estimates in some raw cells. Their support counts and raw rates remain visible, but standardized profile predictions and confidence intervals are suppressed. In particular, the both-16+/Q4/husband-only configuration has only four divorce events, so it cannot responsibly be called the best full profile. Marginal role contrasts use the broader sample and do not establish support for every particular whole-couple combination.

## Uncertainty and verification

Each interval uses its baseline-wave survey weight. Weight normalization for optimization does not alter fitted coefficients. Sandwich covariance clusters repeated observations by respondent and includes a finite-sample correction. These are **weight-adjusted, respondent-cluster approximate confidence intervals**, not full survey-design intervals: survey PSU/stratum structure, dependence between spouses/household members, nonresponse selection and uncertainty in weights are not fully represented. Multiple comparisons are exploratory and uncorrected.

The fitted 30-column design is full rank. Normalized likelihood score is below 5e-18; weighted fitted and observed event counts agree within 1e-12. Analytic standardized-rate gradients were checked by centered finite differences (maximum error below 2e-12). An independent verifier reconstructs the design from saved coefficient term names and input rows, reproduces every supported standardized rate and log-delta interval, verifies fitted-count/score identities, and checks direct contrast arithmetic and sparse-profile suppression. Coefficients, covariance, target-ready input, schooling mixture, support counts, code and audits are included for reproducibility.

Run `python3 analyze_couple_profiles.py` then `python3 verify_couple_profiles.py` with NumPy/pandas installed. The saved `couple_profile_input.csv` includes the reconstructed marriage-order control; reconstructing it from scratch also uses the paired baseline extract and codebook from the preceding analysis. No religion, politics, fidelity or spouse traits measured at incompatible times are silently added to these profiles.
