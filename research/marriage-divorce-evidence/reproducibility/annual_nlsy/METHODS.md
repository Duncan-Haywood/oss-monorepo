# Prospective annual legal-divorce incidence from NLSY79

## File hierarchy and primary choice

**Primary:** `legally_married_primary/` includes respondents who are legally married **or separated** at the beginning of an observed survey interval. Separation does not terminate legal marriage. Root-folder CSV outputs contain the earlier **currently-married-only baseline sensitivity**; they must not be labeled the primary legal-marriage denominator.

The primary `annual_crude_rates.csv` gives annual event incidence per 1,000 observed person-years. `annual_adjusted_without_separation_state.csv` adjusts jointly for education, family-income quartile, respondent annual work hours, baseline religious attendance, age, age at first marriage, and wave. `annual_adjusted_rate_ratios.csv` additionally adjusts for already being separated at interval start. Both are descriptive; separation can lie on the pathway from other predictors to legal divorce, so adding it changes the question and can introduce selection effects. Neither model estimates a causal effect.

## Construction of risk and elapsed time

We pair nominal survey waves 2000→2002 through 2020→2022, using actual interview months/years. An interval enters if baseline marital status MARSTAT-KEY is 1 (married) or 2 (separated), baseline SAMPWEIGHT is positive, and follow-up status and dates are valid. We require a valid follow-up interview, so absent-follow-up intervals are excluded, not assumed divorce-free. Follow-up status 0 after legal marriage is inconsistent and excluded along with negative missing codes.

For all three possible follow-up marital transitions, code 3 is legal divorce and code 6 widowhood. First legal divorce ends follow-up with event=1; first widowhood ends it with event=0. Otherwise follow-up ends at the next interview. **Separation never censors follow-up.** An already-separated respondent can therefore contribute legally-married time and a subsequent divorce. Terminal events without valid dates, outside the interview interval, or in the same month as the baseline interview are excluded as ambiguous. A terminal follow-up status without a dated terminal transition is also excluded. Interview dates and event dates have month resolution, not exact days; elapsed exposure is the difference in month indices divided by 12, conventionally placing both at midmonth. Multiple subsequent marriages/divorces inside one interval are not modeled after its first terminal event; a later observed married/separated baseline can re-enter.

The target is an **incidence rate conditional on observed legally-married intervals**, not cumulative ever-divorced prevalence, full lifetime divorce risk, first-marriage survival, or a count of all marriage episodes. Widowhood is handled by cause-specific censoring; no cumulative-incidence function is estimated. Rates per 1,000 person-years are not exactly annual probabilities. One could convert a constant hazard r to 1−exp(−r), but no constant-hazard lifetime projection is made.

## Time ordering and predictors

Every interval's predictors are measured at its baseline interview, before the subsequent event interval. The cohort was born 1957–64; observed baseline ages span 35–64. Follow-up runs from April 2000 to September 2023. These estimates do not directly describe people marrying today in their twenties or early thirties.

Education uses only degree reports available **at or before the baseline wave**, beginning with the broad 1988 highest-degree question, then newly reported degrees. It never uses the cross-round latest HIGHEST_DEGREE_EVER variable. Specific raw Q3-10B/Q3-10D codes: 2 associate, 3 BA, 4 BS, 5 master, 6 PhD, 7 professional. Prior valid academic degrees are carried forward; higher observed levels supersede lower ones, while nonspecific other code 8 does not erase a specific known degree. A high-school-equivalent degree with 13+ completed grades is labeled some college. For respondents lacking a specific reported degree, contemporaneous revised highest grade, or current highest-grade Q3-4, can identify <12, 12, or 13–15 grades; >=16 grades alone does not establish a bachelor's credential. Thus upper-degree groups use reported credentials, not years-of-schooling guesses. Degree-report gaps and inconsistencies remain possible. Graduate degrees are pooled only in adjusted models because detailed doctorate/professional events are sparse.

Family income is the baseline wave's TNFI_TRUNC, referring to the preceding calendar year, not the respondent's salary. It is topcoded by the NLS. We compute weighted quartiles separately in each baseline wave among eligible intervals with valid income. The quartile cutpoints are saved. Relative income positions can therefore be compared across waves without pretending that a nominal dollar has constant purchasing power. Work hours are baseline HRSWK-PCY, respondent hours during the prior calendar year: zero, 1–1,999, or 2,000+. Hours may be valid despite some unaccounted time, so they are exploratory employment-intensity measures.

Religious practice is reported service attendance in 2000 (R65336.00), a fixed pre-follow-up measure: weekly-or-more codes 1–2, less-than-weekly codes 3–5, never code 6. Later attendance can change. A separate sensitivity refreshes attendance in 2012 (T32014.00) when a valid response is available, using it only for baseline waves 2012 onward. Missing 2012 responses retain the prior 2000 value, rather than treating a questionnaire skip as never. These two full-sample attendance questions are used deliberately; later selected-universe questions are not substituted. Religious attendance is neither belief strength nor spouse agreement.

Age is baseline AGEATINT; age at first marriage is historical AGE1M (the marriage necessarily predates a married baseline). Current-marriage duration and marriage order are not available in this compact extract and are not controlled. Wave effects absorb the common cohort/calendar progression; the age coefficient with wave controls reflects narrow birth-cohort differences and should not be extrapolated as a ten-year aging intervention.

## Weighting, uncertainty, and regression

Crude rate = sum(weight × divorce event) / sum(weight × observed exposure). Each interval uses the official sampling weight for its own baseline survey wave, not the latest-2022 weight. Repeated observations from a person are clustered in the variance calculation. For rate r, the cluster contribution is the sum over a person's intervals of weight×(event−r×exposure); variance is G/(G−1) times sum(cluster contribution²) divided by weighted exposure². Log-rate 95% intervals avoid negative limits. No-event cells have undefined sampling intervals here, rather than a misleading 0–0 confidence interval. Fewer than ten observed events are flagged.

Adjusted models maximize a weighted Poisson likelihood with exposure offset, equivalent to a piecewise exponential event-rate model for these terminal-event intervals. We normalize weights to mean one for numerical fitting. Coefficients exponentiate to event-rate ratios; robust sandwich uncertainty clusters by person and uses a finite-sample correction. It does not assume Poisson count variance for confidence intervals. We include age (per ten years), first-marriage age (per five years), categorical predictors and baseline-wave indicators. The model is an association model, not a validated individual prediction tool. We retain the separation-state version and the version without this downstream marker to expose sensitivity rather than silently choosing a favorable result.

**These are weight-adjusted, respondent-cluster approximate intervals, not full complex-survey-design confidence intervals.** Available extract metadata does not supply survey PSU/stratum design variables; clustering across household members or survey PSUs and uncertainty in weights are omitted. Survey weights do not automatically correct selection caused by requiring the next interview or complete covariates. Model complete-case counts and event counts are saved separately by sex. Income missingness, sparse degree cells, religious-measure aging, survivorship into marriage at midlife, residual confounding, timing of separation, and topcoding limit interpretation.

## Profiles and verification

Profiles are prespecified descriptive combinations rather than the minimum observed among many searched combinations. They require a currently married (not already separated) baseline, college credential or higher, above-median family income within wave, and specified work hours. Attendance variants are shown for comparison; a lower raw cell does not show religion caused a reduction. Compare these profiles with the currently-married baseline stratum, not the all-legally-married primary rate, which also contains already-separated respondents. Profile support includes respondents, intervals, divorce events, person-years and uncertainty. The same respondent may occupy different profiles over time.

The implementation checks full-rank regression matrices, convergence, normalized Poisson score, and agreement between weighted fitted and observed events. A separate standard-library calculation verifies crude weighted rates from saved intervals. Exact exclusion counts, retained people, observed divorce/widow counts, and exposure are saved in `annual_audit.json`; model sample and score checks are in model audit JSONs. Source remains the official 2022-round archive and checksum documented in `../longitudinal/source_manifest.json`. Run `python3 extract_annual_nlsy.py` then `python3 analyze_annual_nlsy.py --include-separated`; NumPy and pandas are required for analysis. Omitting the flag produces the currently-married-only sensitivity.
