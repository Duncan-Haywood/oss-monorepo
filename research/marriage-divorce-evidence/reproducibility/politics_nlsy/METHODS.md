# Respondent political affiliation and future annual divorce incidence

Source: public NLSY79 full1979–2022archive https://nlsinfo.org/cohort-data/nlsy79_all_1979-2022.zip . Run extract_politics.py, then analyze_politics.py. Annual endpoints/exposure are inherited from sibling annual_nlsy/legally_married_primary/nlsy_annual_intervals.csv and its documented source extraction and date audits. Full selected questions and value labels are retained in selected_codebook.json.

Official2008questionnaire: https://www.nlsinfo.org/sites/default/files/attachments/121210/17.Attitudes.html . Official documentation confirms the NLSY79political questions in2008: https://www.nlsinfo.org/content/cohorts/nlsy79-children/topical-guide/attitudes/introduction (the page chiefly concerns Children/Young Adults, but explicitly identifies NLSY79questions T21839–T21845). These are original NLSY79respondents born1957–1964, not their children.

## Measurements

Party identification T2184000(2008): Democrat, Republican, Independent, volunteered other party, volunteered no preference. Codes0and negatives are not treated as substantive party categories. Party strength T2184100asks strong/not very strong of identified Democrats/Republicans. T2184200asks other respondents whether closer to Republican, Democratic, equally close or neither. The sensitivity party-plus-lean classification preserves the different question: it does not silently label all independents as partisans. T2184300measures following politics(always, most, half, occasional, never). No spouse party is available in this selected source. Respondent party does not identify political agreement within a marriage, ideology, personal values, behavior, or a partner's views. Religion is not used to infer politics.

## Prospective design

Baseline2008respondents currently married OR separated(legally married), with valid baseline weight, interview/event dates and observed2010follow-up. Respondents are approximately44–51at baseline. Follow only the marriage present at baseline from interview until first legal divorce, widowhood(censor) or2010interview. Rate numerator is first legal divorce; denominator is observed married/separated person-time, using recorded interview/event months. A subsequent marriage's divorce is not included after widowhood or first divorce. This is a prospective annual incidence rate per1,000person-years, not current divorced prevalence, cumulative marriage failure probability or lifetime divorce risk. Baseline separated respondents are retained and separated status is adjusted explicitly.

Use2008sampling weights, not2000or2022weights. They account for original survey weighting and baseline nonresponse, but not all two-wave outcome attrition or party-item missingness. Complete cases condition on observed follow-up. The cohort consists of survivors/residents of existing marriages at2008, not all current American couples or newlyweds. Party identification may change afterward, and the2008/2010political and economic context is historically specific.

## Estimation and uncertainty

Crude rates use weighted legal divorces divided by weighted person-years. Approximate95%CIs use respondent-level robust weighted-ratio variance and log-rate intervals; zero-event rows have missing CIs, not zero-width certainty. Each person contributes one interval. These are not full surveyPSU/stratum variances and may understate uncertainty. Cells with fewer than10events are flagged. All measured party and engagement groups are shown rather than selecting favorable minima.

The primary weighted Poisson log-rate model uses exposure offset and adjusts party for respondent sex, baseline age, age at first marriage and married/separated baseline status. The extended model additionally adjusts education, baseline family-income quartile, contextual broad race/ethnicity and respondent religious attendance measured in2000(EIGHTyears before political baseline). That religion measurement is explicitly stale; it must not be called current2008religion or a measure of spouse agreement. The race adjustment is contextual confounding control, not a recommended trait or a hierarchy. Current model does not control current marriage duration/order or marital quality, and does not make a causal estimate. The narrow baseline-age range limits age variation.

Other-party respondents remain in raw tables but are omitted from fitted party models because of tiny sample support. Model party categories are Democrat, Republican, Independent, no preference. All effects are main effects; no party-by-sex interactions or political mismatch effects are estimated. Extended models have relatively few events per parameter, so precision and specification are limited. No multiplicity correction is used; all analyses are exploratory.

Poisson standard errors use independent-respondent sandwich covariance. Weighted target predictive margins average each counterfactual party category's fitted rate over the weighted eligible sample's other covariates. These are descriptive model-standardized associations, not identified interventions or advice to adopt a party. Margin CIs condition on the empirical target distribution and coefficient covariance, not a full survey bootstrap; profiles may extrapolate across uncommon covariate combinations. Formal differences versus Democrat use matched covariance gradients, not overlapping-CI logic. Rates are reported per1,000person-years and differences on that same scale.

## Results and model-sample sensitivity

Crude annual divorce rates per1,000person-years: Democrats19.08(approx95%CI14.29–25.49;1,798people/69events); Republicans12.33(8.21–18.52;1,226/30); Independents18.76(12.54–28.07;805/30); no preference15.17(7.21–31.94;342/10). Other party has20people andzeroevents, insufficient for inference.

Primary Republican-versus-Democrat adjustedRR0.876(0.507–1.514),4,069people/136events. Extended adjustedRR0.530(0.288–0.973),3,366people/112events, including only20Republican events. To distinguish sample change from added adjustment, the primary specification was refitted on exactly the extended complete-case sample:RR0.693(0.367–1.308). Differences reflect BOTH sample selection and covariate adjustment. The extended interval only narrowly excludes1under approximate, exploratory, uncorrected inference; it must not be singled out as definitive. Missingness, modest event counts, context and omitted marital-history controls prevent a reliable general ranking of parties by divorce risk.

The same-complete-case check is saved as model age_sex_same_extended_complete_cases in all adjusted outputs. Every model includes separation status and age at first marriage despite the abbreviated model names. Full coefficient tables, standardized rates and formal differences are retained for transparent review.
