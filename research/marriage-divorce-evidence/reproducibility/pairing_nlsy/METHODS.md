# Predissolution spouse education and earnings pairings

This original analysis links baseline household-roster spouse characteristics and the respondent's/spouse's prior-calendar-year wages to **subsequent** legal divorce, using the month-resolved at-risk intervals already constructed in the annual NLSY79 analysis. It never attaches a replacement spouse observed after divorce to the earlier marriage.

## Population and risk

Start with the annual pipeline's observed intervals for waves 2000→2002 through 2020→2022. Restrict to a currently married baseline and exactly one co-resident household member coded SPOUSE (HHI_FINAL_RELCODE=1), in-house status 0/1 when that status field exists (2000/2002), and confirmed opposite-sex spouse (HHI_FINAL_GENCODE male=1/female=2). Cohabiting partners (relationship code 33), already-separated baselines without a current spouse in the roster, ambiguous rosters and unconfirmed sex pairings are excluded. This is a **baseline co-resident married population**, not all legally married/separated adults. The restriction is on baseline information, not surviving married through follow-up: future divorces are retained. It does not eliminate follow-up nonresponse or selection into co-residence. Rates cannot be generalized to couples already separated at baseline.

Legal divorce is transition code 3. Exposure ends at first subsequent divorce, widowhood (competing censor), or next interview; separation does not censor within an interval. Dates have month resolution. Full endpoint construction, exclusion rules, baseline weights, and cohort limitations appear in `../annual_nlsy/METHODS.md`. Respondents born 1957–64 contribute midlife and later-life intervals. Analyses are based on married respondents' reports, not a registry of unique spouse pairs; some spouses could themselves be sample members. Respondent-cluster uncertainty does not account for such couple/household dependence or survey PSUs.

## Schooling pairings

Spouse schooling is the current baseline spouse's HHI_FINAL_GRADECODE. Respondent schooling uses revised highest-grade variables and newly reported current highest grades available at or before each baseline, carrying forward the highest prior valid grade. Future highest attainment is never used. Both sides use the **same schooling-year tiers**: under 12, 12, 13–15, 16–17, and 18+ completed years. The released roster may carry prior information forward for an unchanged member. These are **not verified degree tiers**: 16 years does not establish a bachelor's credential, 18 years does not necessarily establish a master's, and 20 years cannot distinguish a PhD from a professional degree. Consequently the output supports schooling-tier matching, not detailed credential-pair comparisons.

Direction is husband-higher, same tier, or wife-higher. The full husband×wife schooling matrix preserves both levels. Adjusted mismatch models include both partners' schooling tiers as additive categorical terms, plus mismatch direction, earnings-role category, total couple-wage quartile, respondent work hours, current age, current-marriage duration, and wave (plus respondent sex in pooled estimates). Since mismatch is deterministic given the complete pair of levels, it cannot be separated from a fully saturated pair-level model. Its adjusted coefficient relies on the specified **additive level assumption**; it is not an experimentally isolated mismatch effect. Detailed matrix cells should be inspected alongside this model.

## Paired wages and direction

Q13-5 is respondent wage/salary income and Q13-18 current-spouse wage/salary income, for the **same prior calendar year** at each baseline. Revised-topcode variables are preferred when available, then ordinary truncated releases, then the base variable. These include wages, salary, commissions and tips; they are **not total income**, self-employment profit, assets, pension income or family income. Spouse wages are proxy-reported by the respondent. Topcoded positive earnings can distort ratios at the upper end. Missing or skipped values are never recoded zero. Only explicit reported zeros count as zero wages, and zero wages do not prove financial dependence or zero resources.

For known positive combined wages, wife share is wife's wages/(husband's wages+wife's wages). Prespecified groups are husband-only wages; husband majority with both positive (<40% wife); balanced (40–60% wife, inclusive); wife majority with both positive (>60% wife); wife-only wages. Both-zero wages are shown separately with an undefined share, and excluded from relative-share regression rather than assigned 50/50. The main relative-wage model controls the **combined amount** of both spouses' wages using weighted within-wave positive-couple-wage quartiles, both partners' schooling tiers, respondent work hours, age, current-marriage duration, and wave. Thus the balancing comparison is not merely rich couples versus poor couples. Its functional forms, missing covariates, reverse causation through pre-divorce changes, and unmeasured factors still preclude causality.

The paired earnings matrix uses **common** positive individual-spouse wage quartile cutpoints within each wave, pooling husbands' and wives' positive wages with equal respondent weights for each partner. Husband Q4 and wife Q4 therefore use the same dollar threshold in a wave; these are not separate sex-specific salary scales. Zero wages form their own row/column. This compares relative earnings levels across calendar years without treating nominal dollars as constant purchasing power. Cutpoints are saved for inspection. Q4 means the upper quarter of positive observed spouse wages, not a fixed $100,000/$200,000 salary. A prespecified high-husband-wage table isolates husband Q4 and compares wife-zero, husband-majority/both-positive, balanced, and wife-majority/both-positive cells. It directly tests the suggested configuration descriptively; it does not search for the minimum among many cells.

## Duration, weights and uncertainty

Current-marriage duration is baseline interview month minus the latest valid reported marriage-start month at or before baseline, using the cross-round sequence of up to seven marriage starts. This uses the date of an event that already occurred; it does not use future educational or earnings outcomes. Missing/inconsistent marriage-start histories may still affect duration. Complete duration is required for adjusted models; unknown durations remain in descriptive tables.

Rates are baseline-wave-weighted divorce events divided by baseline-wave-weighted observed person-years. Confidence intervals cluster repeated intervals by respondent. Weighted Poisson models use exposure offsets (piecewise exponential rate models), respondent-cluster sandwich covariance, and baseline-wave indicators. Pooled models adjust for respondent sex; separate male- and female-respondent models are also supplied rather than implying husbands and wives in these samples are all paired with each other. Reported confidence intervals are **weight-adjusted, person-cluster approximations**, not full survey-design intervals. Fewer than ten observed events are flagged; zero-event cells receive undefined intervals rather than a false 0–0 interval. No stable ranking should be inferred from sparse matrix cells.

These are associations of **predissolution pair characteristics** with subsequent recorded legal divorce, not current-marriage-only spouse matching among survivors, and not evidence that changing a spouse's earnings would change divorce risk. Current-marriage survival into the observed ages, co-residence, missing spouse wages, incomplete next interviews, proxies, topcoding, and unobserved relationship quality remain limitations.

## Reproduction

Run `python3 extract_pairing_nlsy.py`, after the annual pipeline has created its intervals, then `python3 analyze_pairing_nlsy.py`. Extraction uses the official cached archive if available and otherwise downloads it. Analysis requires NumPy and pandas. Source URL/checksum are documented in `../longitudinal/source_manifest.json`. Outputs include baseline pair intervals, complete schooling/wage matrices, prespecified high-husband-wage profiles, adjusted rate ratios, wage cutpoints, coverage counts and regression convergence/score checks. Existing analyses are never overwritten.


Official documentation supporting the household linkage: [NLSY79 household composition](https://nlsinfo.org/content/cohorts/nlsy79/topical-guide/household/household-composition) and [marital transitions/spouse identification](https://nlsinfo.org/content/cohorts/nlsy79/topical-guide/marriage-and-children/marital-status-marital-transitions-and). We require both a married marital-history status and a spouse-coded current roster member; event history takes precedence when these conflict.

`high_husband_zero_wife_rate_ratios.csv` additionally reports prespecified crude wife-zero-versus-positive-wife comparisons within husband Q4, with respondent-cluster covariance between cells. The wife-zero versus wife-Q4 rate ratio is 1.05 (approximately 0.56–1.95), providing no clear evidence that the wife-zero configuration outperforms two high-wage spouses. Four comparisons are supplied for transparency, without multiple-comparison correction or selecting a winning configuration.
