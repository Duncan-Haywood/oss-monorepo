# ACS 2023 demographic composition and annual divorce events

Original national ACS 2023 one-year person microdata, retrieved from official Census ZIP already preserved in ../acs/csv_pus.zip. Source https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/pums/2023/1-Year/csv_pus.zip . Definitions from official 2023 PUMS dictionary ../acs/data_dictionary_2023.pdf and text; full prior source metadata ../acs/manifest.json. Run `python3 analyze.py` (pandas/numpy; Python3) to reproduce from ZIP. Group-level age/sex weighted totals including all80 replicate weights are saved in age_sex_cell_totals.csv.gz. No external demographic data or published divorce estimates were substituted.

## Populations, numerators, measures

U.S. resident ACS person-file respondents ages30–64. Analyze two explicit denominators: ALL adults in that age range (general population, including never married and widowed), and an approximate currently/recently marriage-exposed population defined as currently married or separated (MAR1/4) OR divorced in past12months (MARHD1). Numerator is a reported legal divorce in past12months (MARHD1), not separation or current divorced status. All event respondents are retained in the approximate exposed denominator by construction. This reconstructs neither exact start-of-year risk sets nor person-years; remarriage, deaths, migration, and marriages formed within year can affect interpretation. Label results annual divorce-event PERCENT, not exact hazard or prospective lifetime risk. A low per-general-adult event percentage can reflect lower marriage prevalence/exposure as well as fewer events among marriages.

For each group, report unweighted respondent/event counts, weighted population/event totals, crude annual event percent, age/sex-standardized annual event percent, share of the selected denominator population, share of all divorce-event respondents, and event-composition representation ratio = event share / population share. Ratios above1 indicate greater share among divorce events than in that selected population. They are descriptive composition ratios, not causal individual risks. Algebraically, with a fixed population denominator, this ratio equals the subgroup's crude annual event proportion divided by the overall crude annual event proportion; it is not independent corroborating evidence. The general-population and approximate-exposed representations answer different questions. Entry-duration and foreign-birth-region rows describe foreign-born subgroups relative to the ENTIRE selected population; their shares sum to the foreign-born share, not100%.

## Group definitions

Race/Hispanic origin: Hispanic of any race (HISP not1); among non-Hispanic respondents, RAC1P distinguishes White alone, Black alone, American Indian/Alaska Native alone(codes3–5 combined), Asian alone, Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander alone, Some Other Race alone, Two or More Races. These categories are self-reported administrative classifications, not biological causes. Hispanic origin is an ethnicity, not synonymous with race or nationality. Broad grouping can obscure variation; no selected country ranking is constructed.

NATIVITY1 native,2 foreign born. Native includes born in U.S. states, U.S. territories, or abroad to U.S. citizen parent(s). Citizenship groups use CIT1 bornU.S.,2 bornterritories,3 abroad citizenparents,4 naturalized,5 noncitizen. Neither nativity nor current citizenship establishes former nationality or an immigration pathway.

Among foreignborn only: entry duration =2023-YOEP, grouped0–4,5–9,10–19,20+years. These are approximate years since reported entry, not exact interview-date durations, lawful status, length of uninterrupted residence, or age at marriage/migration. Year-of-entry definitions and bottomcoding apply.

Birthplace world regions use official predefined WAOB: LatinAmerica3, Asia4, Europe5, Africa6, NorthernAmerica7, Oceania/atsea8. Foreignborn only; native citizenparent births abroad are excluded from these rows. POBP birthplace codes were read to preserve source compatibility, but no previousnationality inference or country-specific ranking is made. Census WAOB includes detailed geographical conventions described in the dictionary; names follow official world-area definitions rather than a newly inferred continent scheme.

## Standardization and uncertainty

Fourteen cells =sex2 times five-year ages30–34,35–39,...60–64. For each denominator separately, directly standardize each subgroup's cell event proportions to the age/sex distribution of the full pooled denominator, recomputing the target distribution for every replicate. Thus groups within a population share the same standard, and uncertainty in that standard is retained. This adjusts ONLY interview age/sex composition; education, income, marriage history, nativity-selection, duration of marriage and other factors remain uncontrolled. Standardizing all-adult and exposed outcomes uses different target populations, so they should not be conflated.

Point weights PWGTP;80 person replicate weights PWGTP1–80. Successive-difference-replication variance =4/80 sum((replicate estimate−point estimate)^2), using all transformation/standardization steps within each replicate. Rates and shares use normal95%CI clipped to0–100%; representation ratios use normal95%CI onlog scale. Sparse flag: fewer100unweighted events or any age/sex subgroup cell fewer30respondents, a transparent caution rule rather than every Census publication criterion. No group comparisons are labeled significant merely by ranking point estimates; no multiplicity adjustment or causal claims.

## Unsupported requests and limits

ACS does not identify previousnationality from birthplace. The NOP(parentnativity) variable exists only for eligible ownchildren age17orunder, so it cannot classify secondgeneration adults30–64; adult birthplace cannot substitute for parents' birthplace. ACS cannot establish whether a respondent's parents/siblings/extendedfamily reside abroad or in the U.S. Co-resident household relationships identify only people sharing the surveyed household and cannot establish all-family location. Transnational marriages and family abroad are therefore unmeasured here.

Associations may reflect age at marriage, marriage prevalence and duration, socioeconomic circumstances, migration timing/selection, reporting differences, remarriage, and other observed/unobserved factors. Current place of residence and demographics do not supply individual compatibility or partner-preference advice. ACS sampling/coverage, allocation and nonresponse remain limitations. Public microdata omit geographic detail and topcode selected fields. Year2023 is a dated snapshot, not a claim about current2026 divorce levels.

## Validation and sparse residuals

`validate.py` confirms20 partition identities across ALL81weights: mutually exclusive race/ethnicity, nativity and citizenship groups sum to full denominator/events under both populations; entry-duration and birth-region groups sum to foreignborn totals. Overall denominator/event counts match the prior annual ACS analysis exactly. No missing foreignborn YOEP was detected. A rare Census-coded foreignborn/territory-birth residual (91all-adult respondents,1event) is retained in raw outputs for completeness, not interpreted or ranked. Direct standardization is omitted if ANY age/sex cell has zero weighted denominator; raw results and sparseflag remain available. NorthernAmerica and Oceania groups have few events and are likewise flagged sparse. Source URLs and checksums are in sources.json.
