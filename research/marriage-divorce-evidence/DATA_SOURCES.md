# Sources, inputs and redistribution

- ACS2023one-year national person PUMS: https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/pums/2023/1-Year/csv_pus.zip . Official dictionary and weighting documentation are recorded under reproducibility/acs. All80replicate weights are used where specified.
- NSFG2017–2019male/female public files: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nsfg/nsfg_2017_2019_puf.htm . Official strata/PSUs/weights and questionnaires establish domains and spouse question routing.
- NLSY79through2022: https://nlsinfo.org/cohort-data/nlsy79_all_1979-2022.zip . Exact source SHA256 and selected codebooks are recorded under longitudinal and derivative modules.
- UN World Population Prospects 2024, single-year death rates by age and sex (`mx1dt`) and life expectancy (`e01dt`), used for competing mortality in the lifelong-marriage estimate (`reproducibility/un_wpp2024_mortality/`, derived table `app/data/us_mortality_wpp2024.json`). Fetched from the `wpp2024` R data package on GitHub (PPgp/wpp2024, commit 2da7768), because SSA, CDC and HMD were unreachable. SHA-256 values are in `SHA256SUMS`, retrieved 2026-10-20. Licence: CC BY 3.0 IGO, © 2024 United Nations, DESA, Population Division. The committed derivative is U.S. only, ages 15–100, years 2024–2100, rounded. Life expectancy recomputed from the rates matches the UN e0 within 0.02 years (1950–2023).
- Fair (1978) extramarital-affairs surveys: *Redbook* 1974 (6,366 married women) and *Psychology Today* 1969 (601 married men and women), used for marriage happiness and reported affairs, not divorce (`reproducibility/fair_affairs/`, app bundles `app/models/fair_redbook1974.json` and `fair_pt1969.json`). Copies are statsmodels v0.14.4 `statsmodels/datasets/fair/{fair,fair_pt}.csv` from GitHub ("included with permission of the author"). The 601-person file is cross-checked row by row against Rdatasets `csv/AER/Affairs.csv` (R package AER, GPL). SHA-256 values are in `SHA256SUMS`, retrieved 2026-10-19. Raw files are not committed. Citation: Fair, R. C. (1978), "A Theory of Extramarital Affairs", *Journal of Political Economy* 86(1): 45–61.
- Thornes & Collard (1979) pre- and extramarital sex by divorced or married status and gender (1,036 British people), via Gilbert (1981) and R package `vcd` (GPL-2) `PreSex`, fetched from Rdatasets `csv/vcd/PreSex.csv` (SHA-256 `45c02fcd…7885`). It is a case-control-style comparison, so only odds ratios are reported (`reproducibility/thornes_collard_presex/`) and there is no app number.
- NSFG 2002 (Cycle 6) female respondent file, used for first-marriage dissolution from the wedding day (`reproducibility/nsfg2002_first_marriage/`, app bundle `app/models/nsfg2002_first_marriage.json`). Official: https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Datasets/NSFG/2002FemResp.dat and `stata/2002FemResp.dct` (documentation https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nsfg/nsfg_cycle6.htm). CDC was blocked from the cloud session, so `download.sh` takes Allen Downey's byte copies in github.com/AllenDowney/ThinkStats2 (`code/2002FemResp.dat.gz`, `code/2002FemResp.dct`). SHA-256 values are in `SHA256SUMS` (data `41ebe8ba…1649`, dictionary `1058302d…15f1`), retrieved 2026-10-19. NCHS publishes no checksum, so the copy was checked against official facts: 7,643 records and weights summing to 61.56 million women aged 15–44. Terms: NCHS public-use data for statistical research only, with no attempt to identify respondents. Redistribution is not clearly allowed, so only the script, checksums, aggregate tables and fitted model parameters are committed. Citation: NCHS, National Survey of Family Growth, Cycle 6 (2002).

Analysis scripts acquire original files and stream compact extracts. Individual respondent IDs are survey IDs, not identified spouses; no record linkage to real identities is performed. Git excludes person-level analytical records and archives. The full local handoff retains the existing analytical subsets and raw NSFG files so you can rerun without downloading everything. Data-provider terms apply to the original public microdata; this repository’s MIT license applies to authored project code, not a new license on government/provider datasets.

Never substitute wealth for wage income, current occupation for pre-divorce occupation, current divorced status for ever divorced, or a retrospective history for an annual hazard. See source-specific methods for complete denominators and missingness.
