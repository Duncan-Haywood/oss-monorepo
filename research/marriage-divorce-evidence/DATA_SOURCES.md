# Sources, inputs and redistribution

- ACS2023one-year national person PUMS: https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/pums/2023/1-Year/csv_pus.zip . Official dictionary and weighting documentation are recorded under reproducibility/acs. All80replicate weights are used where specified.
- NSFG2017–2019male/female public files: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nsfg/nsfg_2017_2019_puf.htm . Official strata/PSUs/weights and questionnaires establish domains and spouse question routing.
- NLSY79through2022: https://nlsinfo.org/cohort-data/nlsy79_all_1979-2022.zip . Exact source SHA256 and selected codebooks are recorded under longitudinal and derivative modules.
- UN World Population Prospects 2024, single-year death rates by age and sex (`mx1dt`) and life expectancy (`e01dt`), used for competing mortality in the lifelong-marriage estimate (`reproducibility/un_wpp2024_mortality/`, derived table `app/data/us_mortality_wpp2024.json`). Fetched from the `wpp2024` R data package on GitHub (PPgp/wpp2024, commit 2da7768), because SSA, CDC and HMD were unreachable. SHA-256 values are in `SHA256SUMS`, retrieved 2026-10-20. Licence: CC BY 3.0 IGO, © 2024 United Nations, DESA, Population Division. The committed derivative is U.S. only, ages 15–100, years 2024–2100, rounded. Life expectancy recomputed from the rates matches the UN e0 within 0.02 years (1950–2023).
- Fair (1978) extramarital-affairs surveys: *Redbook* 1974 (6,366 married women) and *Psychology Today* 1969 (601 married men and women), used for marriage happiness and reported affairs, not divorce (`reproducibility/fair_affairs/`, app bundles `app/models/fair_redbook1974.json` and `fair_pt1969.json`). Copies are statsmodels v0.14.4 `statsmodels/datasets/fair/{fair,fair_pt}.csv` from GitHub ("included with permission of the author"). The 601-person file is cross-checked row by row against Rdatasets `csv/AER/Affairs.csv` (R package AER, GPL). SHA-256 values are in `SHA256SUMS`, retrieved 2026-10-19. Raw files are not committed. Citation: Fair, R. C. (1978), "A Theory of Extramarital Affairs", *Journal of Political Economy* 86(1): 45–61.
- General Social Survey panels (NORC at the University of Chicago): the 2006–2010, 2008–2012 and 2010–2014 three-wave panels, the 2016–2020 panel and the 1972–2024 cumulative file, official Stata files from https://gss.norc.org (`reproducibility/gss_panel/`, app bundle `app/models/gss_panel_marriages.json`). SHA-256 values are in `reproducibility/gss_panel/SHA256SUMS`; panel sizes match NORC's release notes and the 2016–2020 codebook. GSS data are free to use with citation (Davern, Bautista, Freese, Herd and Morgan, General Social Survey 1972–2024, NORC); only scripts, checksums and aggregate results are committed.
- NLSY79 full public release (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics), https://nlsinfo.org/cohort-data/nlsy79_all_1979-2022.zip, no login, used to rebuild the couple models from official files and for the first-marriage lens (`reproducibility/nlsy79_full/`, app bundle `app/models/nlsy79_first_marriage.json`). The zip's SHA-256 matches the one already recorded; person-level extracts are not committed.
- HCMST, *How Couples Meet and Stay Together* (Rosenfeld, Thomas & Falcon; Stanford University): the 2009–2017 waves (4,002 respondents; 3,009 partnered) and the 2017–2022 waves (3,510 respondents; 2,862 partnered), from https://data.stanford.edu/hcmst and https://data.stanford.edu/hcmst2017 (`reproducibility/hcmst/`, app bundle `app/models/hcmst_couples.json`). SHA-256 values are in `reproducibility/hcmst/SHA256SUMS`. The data use agreement asks users not to re-identify respondents, not to charge for redistribution, to cite the study and to notify the principal investigator; redistribution is not clearly allowed, so only scripts, checksums, aggregate tables and fitted model parameters are committed. Sample counts match Stanford's documentation.
- HCMST 2009–2017 unmarried couples, before the wedding (`reproducibility/hcmst_dating_to_marriage/`, app bundles `app/models/hcmst_dating_marriage.json` and `hcmst_dating_breakup.json`). It uses the same four Stanford files and checksums as the HCMST couples lens. The Stanford Digital Repository record (https://purl.stanford.edu/ns183dp7831, read 2026-10-20) gives the licence as **ODC Public Domain Dedication and License (PDDL) 1.0**, with users agreeing not to identify anyone. Only code, checksums, aggregates and model parameters are committed. The external check reuses the HCMST 2017–2022 small public version 2.2 file (Stanford druid `hg921sg6829`, same SHA-256 as `reproducibility/hcmst/SHA256SUMS`).
- NSFG, all public-use cycles with marriage histories: female respondent files for 1995 (Cycle 5), 2002, 2006–2010, 2011–2013, 2013–2015, 2015–2017, 2017–2019 and 2022–2023, and male files from 2002 on, from the official https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Datasets/NSFG/ (`reproducibility/nsfg_pooled_first_marriage/`, app bundle `app/models/nsfg_pooled_first_marriage.json`). SHA-256 for all 28 files are in that folder's `SHA256SUMS`; every file's record count matches the official sample size, and weighted totals match NCHS's published populations. The CDC 2002 data and dictionary are byte-identical to the mirror copies the 2002 lens used. NCHS terms as for NSFG 2002 above: statistical research only, no attempt to identify respondents; only scripts, checksums, aggregates and fitted parameters are committed.
- Browser-training extract of the same NSFG 1995–2023 women's first marriages, `app/train/nsfg_pooled_women.json.gz` (written 2026-10-20 by `app/train/tools/build_extract.py`; SHA-256 b0cc01529eea6353d6377ee972413e2f8c47523f4cc3b6dd0af1297a10be4c6f). It has 29,202 rows with months to the end of the marriage, the event, the normalised weight, the fold and 18 pre-wedding inputs, and no cycle, design or interview-time variables. Licence: the NSFG public-use files are works of the U.S. Government, not subject to copyright (17 U.S.C. 105; https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html), so this subset is redistributed. The NCHS data-use terms (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data_access/restrictions.htm, checked 2026-10-20) are carried in the file's `licence` field: statistical reporting and analysis only, no attempt to learn anyone's identity, no linkage with other data. Citation: National Center for Health Statistics, National Survey of Family Growth, 1995–2023 public-use files.
- Smith, Block & Karney, "Baseline Predictors of Divorce" (OSF project hxn84, https://osf.io/hxn84), the public data, codebook, R code and supplement for *Individual, Interpersonal, and Socioeconomic Predictors of Divorce: A 12-Year Prospective Study*: 368 low-income, mostly Hispanic Los Angeles newlywed couples, baseline 2009–10, followed to 2021–22 (`reproducibility/newlywed_la_karney/`, app bundle `app/models/newlywed_la_couples.json`). - NLSY97 rounds 1–21 (1997–2023/24), all-variables public release from the BLS National Longitudinal Surveys, used for first-marriage separation or divorce from the wedding month (`reproducibility/nlsy97_first_marriage/`, app bundle `app/models/nlsy97_first_marriage.json`). Official: https://nlsinfo.org/cohort-data/nlsy97_all_1997-2023.zip (475,462,343 bytes, server Last-Modified 2026-01-23; SHA-256 `8c513e48…3c28` in `SHA256SUMS`, retrieved 2026-10-20). The 8.1 GB CSV inside is never unzipped; `select_vars.py` and `extract.py` stream about 7,900 of its 305,325 columns. Terms: public-use BLS data (no geocode or restricted file); users may not attempt to identify respondents. Only scripts, the checksum, aggregate tables and fitted model parameters are committed. Citation: Bureau of Labor Statistics, U.S. Department of Labor. National Longitudinal Survey of Youth 1997 cohort, 1997–2023 (rounds 1–21). Produced and distributed by the Center for Human Resource Research (CHRR), The Ohio State University.
- NLSY97 first cohabiting unions, before the wedding (`reproducibility/nlsy97_cohabitation_to_marriage/`, app bundles `nlsy97_cohab_marriage.json` and `nlsy97_cohab_split.json`). Attribution: Adapted from Statistics Canada, General Social Survey, Cycles 10 (1995), 20 (2006), 25 (2011) and 31 (2017): Family, public use microdata files. This does not constitute an endorsement by Statistics Canada of this product.

Person-level harmonized extracts for pooled modelling are written only outside the repository (`HARMONIZED_DIR`).

Analysis scripts acquire original files and stream compact extracts. Individual respondent IDs are survey IDs, not identified spouses; no record linkage to real identities is performed. Git excludes person-level analytical records and archives. The full local handoff retains the existing analytical subsets and raw NSFG files so you can rerun without downloading everything. Data-provider terms apply to the original public microdata; this repository’s MIT license applies to authored project code, not a new license on government/provider datasets.

Never substitute wealth for wage income, current occupation for pre-divorce occupation, current divorced status for ever divorced, or a retrospective history for an annual hazard. See source-specific methods for complete denominators and missingness.
