# Robust outer aggregation in local SGD: an exact floor, a bias that workers cannot average away, and what H does about it

*Stylised: independent quadratic modes, Gaussian gradient noise, identical honest workers, fixed outer step `α`, no momentum, attackers that know the current iterate and shift their displacement by a fixed offset. Pure Python; every formula is checked against a literal simulation (`tests/`, `experiments/results.txt`).*

## Question
`noisy-local-sgd` fixed the stationary noise floor of DiLoCo-style local SGD when the server *averages* the workers' displacements. In a permissionless network some workers are Byzantine, and the standard defences replace the mean by a coordinate-wise median or trimmed mean. What does that cost when nobody attacks, what does it leave when some do, and do more workers, a smaller outer step, or a longer inner loop help?

## Model
Mode `j` has curvature `a_j`. An honest worker runs `H` inner steps and returns `d = s x − n`, `s = 1−q^H`, `q = 1−ηa`, `n ~ N(0, V_w)`. `f` of the `N` workers are attackers who send `s x + Δ√V_w`. The server applies `x' = x − α A({d_i})` per mode with a translation-equivariant aggregator `A` (mean, median, `t`-trimmed mean).

## Exact results
1. **Translation equivariance makes the loop exactly linear.** `A = s x + B` with `B = √V_w · b` independent of `x`, so `x' = (1−αs)x − αB` and `E x = −√V_w E[b]/s`, `Var x = α V_w Var(b)/(s(2−αs))`. The loss is `Σ(a_j/2)[α V_{w,j} v /(s_j(2−αs_j)) + V_{w,j} m²/s_j²]` with `m = E b`, `v = Var b`. The aggregator only enters through the two numbers `(m, v)`; the stability limit `α s < 2` is exactly that of averaging. For the mean without attackers `m=0, v=1/N` recovers `noisy-local-sgd`. Simulation of the literal outer loop is within 1.3% (15 workers, three modes, five configurations), and a run in which every worker executes the `H` noisy gradient steps itself is within 1% of the reduction.
2. **The price of robustness with no attackers is a variance multiplier, exactly.** `N·Var(b)` for the median (order-statistic moments by quadrature) is 1.346 (`N=3`), 1.434, 1.495, 1.525 (`N=15`), 1.549, 1.564 (`N=101`), tending to `π/2 = 1.571`: the floor is up 57% at large `N`, at any `α`, `H` and curvature. A one-per-side trimmed mean costs only 1.036× at `N=15` and 1.005× at `N=101`; a 25%-trimmed mean about 1.13–1.20×.
3. **Under attack the bias is bounded, does not depend on `α` or `N`, and the mean's is not.** With `f` attackers pushing one way (worst case for any monotone aggregator), the median's standardised bias `m` is the mean of honest order statistics: `Φ(z) = 1/(2(1−ε))`, `ε=f/N`. It is 0.083σ at `ε=1/16`, 0.180σ at `1/8`, 0.431σ at `1/4`, 0.842σ at `3/8` for large `N` (finite `N=16` is within 1.5% of these at `1/8`); `1.2533 ε` is accurate only for small `ε`. The mean's bias is `εΔ`, unbounded. The median therefore has the smaller loss whenever the attack offset exceeds `z(ε)/ε` honest-noise sigmas: 1.34σ at `ε=1/16`, 1.44σ at `1/8`, 1.72σ at `1/4`, 2.24σ at `3/8` (1.43σ measured at `N=15, f=2`). Below that offset the mean is better: attackers hidden inside the noise are cheaper to tolerate than to robustify against (at `N=15, f=2`, `α=0.2`: total loss 0.057 for the mean at Δ=1σ versus 0.117 for the median; 0.50 versus 0.117 at 3σ, and 557 versus 0.117 at 100σ).
4. **More workers do not remove the bias.** The bias loss is independent of `α`; the variance loss falls as `1/N`. At `ε=1/8` (`H=4`, `α=0.2`, median) total loss is 0.1028 at `N=8`, 0.1019 at 16, 0.1017 at 32, 0.1016 at 128: the variance part goes from 0.0053 to 0.0004 while the bias part *rises* from 0.0975 to 0.1012 toward its `N→∞` limit. Shrinking `α` (or momentum tuning) only removes the variance part. The remaining lever is the attacker fraction, and `H`.
5. **A longer inner loop shrinks the bias.** Because a displacement is the average of `H` noisy steps, its noise relative to its signal falls: `V_w/s² = η²σ²/(1−q²) · (1+q^H)/(1−q^H)`. The bias loss of the median at `N=15, f=2`, mode `a=0.25`, `η=0.2` is 0.0726 at `H=1`, 0.0182 at `H=4`, 0.0048 at `H=16`, 0.0020 at `H=64` and saturates at 0.0019: the bias in parameter space tends to `E b ×` (one worker's own stationary SGD standard deviation `ησ/√(1−q²)`). Robust aggregation cannot do better than the noise of a single worker times `z(ε)`; syncing less often gets you there faster (a 39× reduction from `H=1` to 256).
6. **Trim as much as you can afford; trimming exactly `f` is a poor choice.** The trimmed mean needs `t ≥ f` to be finite at all, and against worst-case attackers its bias falls steadily with `t` while its variance rises only slightly: at `N=32, f=4` total loss is 0.2008 at `t=4`, 0.1454 at `t=6`, 0.1124 at `t=10`, 0.1017 at `t=15` (the median). Worst-case bias at `t=f` versus the median: 0.252σ vs 0.179σ at `f=4`, 0.527σ vs 0.427σ at `f=8`, 0.069σ vs 0.040σ at `f=1`. The extra variance of the median, 0.0015 versus 0.0010, is negligible against the bias whenever an attacker is present.

## Limitations
Shared curvature (no client drift), Gaussian noise (the median's `π/2` and the bias formulas use Gaussianity; heavy tails favour the median more), one-sided constant-offset attackers (worst case for the bias but not necessarily for the variance, which we do not optimise over), attackers coordinated across all `f` seats but independent of the round's honest noise, coordinate-wise aggregators only (no geometric median or Krum), and trimmed-mean variances from 10⁵–10⁶ Monte Carlo draws (the median's are exact). Modelling `f` as fixed; a random number of attackers per round would need the mixture.

## Relevance
For open training networks: check the Byzantine budget against the noise, not against the worker count. If the plausible attack offset is above about 1.5 honest-noise sigmas, use a median-like aggregator and accept the 1.57× variance premium; if attackers can only hide in the noise, plain averaging is better. Recruiting more workers does not reduce the attack bias, which is set by `ε` and by how much local averaging happens in a round, so pair robust aggregation with longer inner loops and with audits or slashing that lower `ε`. Companion to `noisy-local-sgd`, `compressed-sync`, `partial-participation`, `robust-aggregation` and `decentralized-verification-markets`.
