# A twin that treats the load as rigid certifies every bandwidth; the real loop is unstable above `δ/ζ` of the flexible-mode frequency

*Stylised: a two-mass linear plant under PD control, pure Python, every number is from `experiments/results.txt` (deterministic, about 20 s). The "real" system is itself simulated; no lab or field data. Negative and out-of-model results are marked.*

## Question
Drives, arms and gimbals are routinely simulated as one rigid inertia. The real structure has a flexible mode (a compliant gearbox, belt, shaft or link). If the twin is validated by step responses at a bandwidth well below that mode, how high may the controller bandwidth then be pushed on the strength of the twin, and what sets the real limit?

## Model
Motor inertia `J1` and load inertia `J2` joined by a spring `k` and damper `c`; force `u` on `J1`; `M = J1 + J2`. Twin: one inertia `M`. Controller `u = kp (r − y) − kd ẏ`, tuned on the twin as `kp = M w²`, `kd = 2ζ M w` (closed loop `M s² + kd s + kp`, stable for every `w > 0, ζ > 0`). Flexible-mode frequency `ws² = kM/(J1J2)`, modal damping ratio `δ = c/(2 ws J1J2/M)`, bandwidth ratio `ε = w/ws`, mass ratio `μ = J1J2/M²`. With load-side sensing (`y` = load position, non-collocated) the closed-loop characteristic polynomial is `J1J2 s⁴ + cM s³ + (kM + kd c) s² + (kp c + kd k) s + kp k`; in `p = s/ws`,
`p⁴ + 2δ p³ + (1 + 4ζδε) p² + (2ζε + 2δε²) p + ε²`,
in which `μ` has cancelled. With motor-side sensing (collocated) the polynomial is `J1J2 s⁴ + (cM + kd J2) s³ + (kM + kp J2 + kd c) s² + (kp c + kd k) s + kp k`. Stability is tested by Routh–Hurwitz for a quartic (`a3a2 > a4a1`, `a3a2a1 > a4a1² + a3²a0`, all coefficients positive) and, separately, by RK4 simulation of the four-state closed loop.

## Results
1. **The critical bandwidth does not depend on the mass ratio (exact).** Because `μ` cancels from the normalised polynomial, the largest stable `ε_c` is identical for `μ` = 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.25 (0.071415910590 at `δ = 0.05, ζ = 0.7`, spread 0). It is the smallest positive root of the cubic `δ²ε³ + (2ζδ − 4ζδ³)ε² + ζ²(1 − 4δ²)ε − δζ = 0` (the third Hurwitz condition); bisection on the Routh test agrees with that root to 1.7·10⁻¹⁶ over 18 `(δ, ζ)` pairs. So the limit is a property of the flexible mode's damping and the twin's chosen damping ratio, not of how the inertia is split.
2. **Small-damping law `ε_c ≈ δ/ζ`.** Equivalently the controller's decay rate `ζ w` must stay below the modal decay rate `δ ws`. `ε_c ζ/δ` is 0.9999–1.0001 at `δ = 0.005` and 0.891–1.085 at `δ = 0.2` over `ζ` = 0.5…1. At `ζ = 1/√2` the cubic evaluated at `ε = δ/ζ` equals `−2√2 δ⁵`, so the law holds to `O(δ⁵)` there (1.0027 at `δ = 0.2`). Worked values (`ζ = 0.7`): `δ = 0.01` gives `ε_c = 0.0143`, `δ = 0.05` gives 0.0714, `δ = 0.2` gives 0.286. **With no modal damping (`δ = 0`) the loop is unstable at every gain tried** (`ε` = 10⁻⁶ … 10), a structural property of load-side PD on a two-mass plant with a double-integrator rigid mode.
3. **Motor-side sensing is unconditionally stable here (positive result).** 7 320 grid points (`μ` five values, `δ` = 0…1, `ζ` = 0.1…3, `ε` from 10⁻³ to 10³): none unstable. This is the usual passivity/collocation argument (Preumont 2011); I verified it on the grid only and did not prove it. The twin is therefore right about collocated sensing and wrong about non-collocated sensing with the same rigid model, so the sensor location is a modelling decision the twin must carry.
4. **Step-response validation does not see the problem.** At `δ = 0.05, ζ = 0.7` (`ε_c = 0.0714`), a unit-step response of twin and real plant differ by at most 2·10⁻⁵ of the step at `ε = 0.05 ε_c`, 2.1·10⁻³ at `0.5 ε_c`, and 6.2·10⁻³ at `0.9 ε_c`; 2% settling times are 93.0 vs 94.6 at `0.9 ε_c`. The same loop at `1.1 ε_c` is unstable, so a twin that matches to under 1% gives no warning that the margin is a factor 1.1 away. The comparison is of settled responses at each gain; a validation run that excites the flexible mode directly would see it, and I did not test that.
5. **Simulation agrees with Routh on both sides.** `δ = 0.2, ζ = 0.7`: at `0.7 ε_c` and `0.9 ε_c` the load oscillation decays (peak 1.3·10⁻¹⁶ and 9.8·10⁻⁷ after 600 time units); at `1.1 ε_c` and `1.6 ε_c` it grows to 9.9·10³ and 1.7·10²⁸.
6. **Modal damping is the parameter to certify, and it maps one-to-one.** A twin that includes the mode but states damping `f·δ` certifies `ε_c(fδ)`, a ratio 0.500, 1.25, 1.50, 2.00, 3.00, 4.999 for `f` = 0.5…5 at `δ = 0.01`, and 0.498 to 7.71 at `δ = 0.2` (nonlinear at large `δ`). A design at fraction `m` of the certified bandwidth stays stable for a damping overestimate up to `f_max` = 1.000, 1.250, 2.000, 3.989 for `m` = 1, 0.8, 0.5, 0.25 (`δ = 0.05`): `f_max ≈ 1/m`. Modal damping is also the quantity least well known from CAD and hardest to measure (ring-down tests), so the twin-certified bandwidth should use a lower confidence bound on `δ`, not its estimate.

## Limitations
One flexible mode, linear plant, PD with no filtering, noiseless and continuous-time controller (RK4 simulation), no actuator lag, no sampling delay (which would lower `ε_c` further; see `latency-twin`), viscous structural damping only, no measurement of the flexible mode. The "real" plant is the same model family as the flex-aware twin, so model-form error is not tested. Notch or low-pass shaping, which would raise the limit, is not studied. Collocation stability is checked on a grid, not proved. Not evidence about any particular drive or arm.

## Next steps
A notch or observer-based remedy and the twin fidelity needed to tune it (the twin then needs `ws` to within the notch width); identifying `δ` from a ring-down with a confidence bound and propagating it into a certified bandwidth; adding sample delay and actuator lag; several modes (spillover); combining with `backlash-twin`, whose gap is exactly the nonlinear limit of a stiff flexible joint.
