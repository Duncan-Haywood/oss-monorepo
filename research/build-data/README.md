# Do open permit records predict how long a permit and the build behind it take, better than each city's own averages?

Research programme (proposal stage), AI-assisted: do open permit feeds predict permit and build duration across cities? See [`PROPOSAL.md`](PROPOSAL.md) and [`LOG.md`](LOG.md). Relates to the construction data warehouse and `ventures/wall-framing-takeoff`. Milestone 1 is done: `src/permits.py` loads Chicago, San Francisco and Austin onto one schema (`datasheets/`, `results/manifest.json`, `results/lags.json`). Reproduce: `python3 src/permits.py pull --out DIR` then `python3 src/describe.py DIR`. Finding so far: the median permit issue lag is 0 days in all three cities, so the target is zero-inflated. Milestone 2's harness (`src/harness.py`) and milestone 3's LightGBM quantile models (`src/boosted.py`) have been run on the real feeds, test year 2024 (forward split: train to 2022, early-stopping year 2023). Headline (H1 holds): the boosted model beats the per-city, per-permit-type empirical-quantile baseline on pinball loss in 13 of 15 city/target/quantile cells, by 2 to 36% (San Francisco and Austin, both targets, every quantile); in Chicago application-to-issue it wins at the median (-9.7%) and loses at the 0.1 and 0.9 quantiles (+0.3%, +6.5%). The same-day (zero-lag) classifier beats the baseline's Brier score only in Austin and San Francisco application-to-issue, not in Chicago. Full table: [`results/m3_table.md`](results/m3_table.md). Limits: one test year, 3 seeds, no tuning beyond a fixed fast configuration, boosted intervals are not yet conformalised (milestone 4). Milestone 4 (conformal, `src/conformal_run.py`, `results/conformal_*.json`): H2 is refuted. In test year 2024 conformal coverage of the nominal 80% interval is 0.816 and 0.813 in Austin but 0.860 Chicago a2i, 0.891 SF a2i and 0.834 SF i2c; in Chicago and SF a2i the conformal step changes nothing because same-day permits tie the scores at 0 and the quantile cannot go negative. Where the raw interval under-covers it widens by 3.5 to 22 days and gains 4 to 11 points. Details: [`LOG.md`](LOG.md), session 8. Milestone 5 (`src/importance.py`, [`results/m5_table.md`](results/m5_table.md)): H3 holds as worded (k80 = 2 to 4 of 6 to 7 fields; work class leads in Austin and Chicago, permit type in SF a2i, log cost in SF i2c; month and weekday carry nothing), but permutation importance is not a decomposition of the gain over the baseline (sums are 1.7 to 20 times the gain), so the claim is about reliance. Milestone 6 (`src/transfer.py`, `results/transfer_*_2024.json`): H4 holds. A model trained on the other cities is worse than the held-out city's own per-type baseline in 13 of 15 city/target/quantile cells (+2% to +137%), better only at SF i2c q0.1 (-2.8%), and Austin's transfer interval covers 61 to 63% for a nominal 80%; an own-city model on the same harmonised features does beat the baseline in Austin, so the loss comes from moving between cities. Limits: i2c has one source city. Milestone 7 (`src/pooled.py`, [`results/m7_table.md`](results/m7_table.md)): H5 holds on its main claim and its secondary claim is refuted. A model pooled with the other cities plus n rows of the new city reaches that city's full-history per-type baseline only after more than 1000 local rows in 3 of 5 city/target pairs (never within 10,000 in Chicago and SF a2i); and a per-type baseline from just 300 local rows is usually as good as the pooled model, which beats it in 3 of 15 cells. One seed, source rows capped at 200,000 per city. Reproduce M3: `python3 src/permits.py pull --out DIR`, then `python3 src/boosted.py DIR --city sf --target a2i --test-year 2024 --seeds 3` per pair and `python3 src/summarise_m3.py`.

Paper: a first draft assembling milestones 1 to 8 is in [`paper/paper.md`](paper/paper.md) (numbers copied from the log, with limits and a to-do list; the proposal's other baselines are still unrun).
