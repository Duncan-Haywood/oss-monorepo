## 2026-11-13 · Step 11 outcome: localisation beats chance tenfold, H3 inconclusive (31% and 36%), and the nominal model adds nothing over the most-affected sensor
- **Ran as planned**, one run, no code change after it. 2,346 EPANET runs (782 junctions &times; q = 1, 5, 20 m³/h) plus the base, about 6 minutes on 4 cores. The plan's training window [d &minus; 21, d &minus; 7) was coded with a clip at day 0 and a 7-day minimum; no localisation hour needed it (the earliest is 2018-02-02, day 32).
- **Chance.** A random junction is within 300 m (along pipes) of a leak pipe for 4.3% of junctions at the median leak, so 0.57 of 13, 0.59 of 14 and 1.40 of 32 leaks are expected.
- **H3, detector-timed, 2019.** D1-F's 13 detected leaks: the model puts **4 within 300 m (31%, P = 0.002)**, median error 573 m. LILA-auto's 14: **5 (36%, P = 0.0002)**, median 468 m. Both sit in the proposal's inconclusive band (30 to 50%): H3 neither held nor refuted.
- **Oracle-timed, 32 leaks (2018 and 2019, at half-peak flow):** model 15 of 32 within 300 m (47%, chance 1.4, P &lt; 0.0001), median 436 m; cosine 13; the most-affected sensor (no model) 13, median 420 m. Paired: model only 6, sensor only 4, sign test P = 0.75; the model is closer on 19 leaks, the sensor on 12. Across all three sets the model and the model-free sensor are not separable.
- **Predictions.** (a) H3 not held for either detector: **held** (31%, 36%; inconclusive, not refuted). (b) Oracle-timed beats chance at P &lt; 0.05: **held**. (c) Model's median error below the sensor's on the 32: **failed** (436 against 420 m). (d) Same junction at q = 1, 5 and 20 for &ge; 80%: **failed**, 12% (q = 1 agrees with q = 5 for 62%, q = 20 for 19%). The largest signature is 0.04, 0.32 and 2.8 m for q = 1, 5 and 20: the response is not linear in q (head loss grows faster than flow near the leak), and neighbouring junctions have near-identical signatures at 33 sensors, so the argmax moves between neighbours.
- **What it changes.** For the paper: on L-Town the 300 m location rule is easy to beat by chance correction (both methods ~10 times chance) and hard to win by modelling: a nominal-model sensitivity matrix, without calibration, does no better than naming the sensor whose pressure fell most. The slow ramps (p193, p277, p455, p123) are lost by every method; of the 15 leaks that reach half their peak within an hour of onset, 10 are within 300 m. Next for H3: a calibrated model (demands from the AMR and inflow, leak size from the flow balance) and a time-resolved signature instead of a 24 h mean, both pre-registered against the most-affected sensor as the baseline to beat.

- **Sensitivity model (fixed now).** `L-TOWN.inp` as given (nominal demands, patterns, the three PRVs, tank T1), WNTR's EPANET simulator, 24 h from 00:00 at hourly reports. For each junction j a constant extra demand q at j; its signature s<sub>j</sub> is the 24 h mean of p<sub>leak</sub> &minus; p<sub>nominal</sub> at the 33 pressure-sensor nodes. **Primary q = 5 m³/h**; q = 1 and 20 as a linearity check.
- **Observed residual.** For a localisation time t (hour): per sensor, fit step 1's D1 regression without flows (48 hour-of-day &times; weekday/weekend columns plus tank level) on the 14 days [d &minus; 21, d &minus; 7) of hourly means, d = t's day; r = mean of (pressure &minus; prediction) over the 24 h from t, in metres. No threshold or setting is tuned.
- **Estimate.** The junction with the largest Pearson correlation between r and s<sub>j</sub> across the 33 sensors (primary; the standard of sensitivity-based isolation); cosine similarity as a secondary. Error: along-pipe distance from that junction to the leak pipe's midpoint (`network.pipe_dist`), the 300 m rule as BattLeDIM scored it.
- **Leaks and times.** (i) Detector-timed: the 2019 new leaks D1-F (step 1, h = 240) and LILA-auto (step 2, index ties, &delta; = 1, C<sub>thr</sub> = 20) detected under the ungated 30-day rule, at their alarm hour from those steps' `alarms.tsv` (H3 is about these). (ii) Oracle-timed: every scored new leak of both years (32), at the first hour its own flow reaches half its peak, to separate localisation error from detection timing.
- **Baselines and chance.** The model-free most-affected sensor (most negative r) and the detector's alarming sensor, each scored by the same 300 m rule. Chance: a junction drawn uniformly; for each leak the exact share p<sub>i</sub> of junctions within 300 m; the count's Poisson-binomial tail gives P(chance &ge; observed). Paired comparison of model-based against most-affected sensor by an exact sign test on discordant leaks.
- **H3 (proposal).** Held if at least half of the detector-timed leaks are within 300 m, refuted below 30%, inconclusive between; stated for D1-F and LILA-auto separately.
- **Predictions, to be reported held or failed.** (a) H3 is not held for either detector (under half within 300 m): the nominal model's demands differ from the real ones and 33 sensors are sparse. (b) Oracle-timed model-based localisation beats chance at P &lt; 0.05 on the 32 leaks. (c) Its median error is smaller than the most-affected sensor's on the same 32 leaks. (d) The estimate is stable in q: the same junction for q = 1, 5 and 20 for at least 80% of the 32 leaks.

## 2026-11-12 · Step 1 plan, written before any detector was run
- **Data.** BattLeDIM (Zenodo record 4017659, CC-BY-4.0), fetched through the session proxy for the first time; `infra-waterloss` had to fall back to Net3 because it could not. Ground truth is the onset of non-zero flow in `<year>_Leakages.csv` (it matches the onsets in Melnikov 2025's table; the configuration file's start times differ for incipient leaks). 2019 has 23 leaks: 19 that start in 2019 and 4 carried over from 2018 (p257, p427, p654, p810).
- **Protocol.** 2018 is the tuning year, 2019 the test year, scored once with the settings 2018 picked. 2018 labels are used for tuning; BattLeDIM's competitors had only 2018's repair times, so this is more information than they had, and it is disclosed wherever a comparison is made.
- **Detector (D1).** Hourly means. For each pressure sensor and each day, a least-squares model of pressure on hour-of-week, tank level and (variant F) the three inlet flows, fitted on a trailing window that ends `g` days before the day scored; residuals standardised by the window's residual sd; a one-sided CUSUM on the negative residuals per sensor; an alarm resets all CUSUMs and holds off for `g` days. The trailing refit absorbs leaks already running, so each alarm is a claim that a *new* leak started.
- **Score (detection only).** An alarm is a true positive if it falls while an unmatched leak is running and within 30 days of its onset (the latest such onset wins); every other alarm is a false positive. Delay is alarm minus onset. The distance from the alarming sensor to the leak pipe is reported as a first localisation figure (BattLeDIM counted a location within 300 m). Not the competition's score, which needed a reported pipe.
- **Hypothesis H1-step1.** Tuned on 2018 to at most 2 false alarms, D1 detects at least 9 of the 19 leaks that start in 2019 with at most 4 false alarms in 2019. *Refuted if* it detects 8 or fewer, or needs 5 or more false alarms. The published best entries reach 15 to 17 of 23 with 1 to 4 false alarms *with localisation* (Melnikov 2025, Table 2), so this is a floor, not a claim to match them.
- **Expected.** Abrupt leaks above ~10 m³/h found within a day; small incipient ones (p123, p455, p762, p193) missed, as every published entry mostly misses them.
