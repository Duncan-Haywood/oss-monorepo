# A radar twin without Doppler folding: the exact bias of a twin-tuned ego-velocity estimator, and the angular-diversity condition for unfolding

## Question
Radar simulators often produce radial speed as the ideal projection `v_r = v cosθ`. A real chirp-sequence radar measures radial speed from the phase advance between chirps, so it reports it modulo `2 v_u`, `v_u = λ/(4 T_pri)`. A robot that estimates its own speed from a stationary scene (radar odometry) and is tuned, validated and certified in the unfolded twin has never seen a fold. What does the folding do to the twin-tuned estimator, how exactly, and when can it be undone?

## Model
Ego speed `v`; stationary target `i` at azimuth `θ_i`, `c_i = cosθ_i`, radial speed `v c_i`. Real measurement `m_i = wrap(v c_i + n_i)`, `n_i ~ N(0, σ²)`, `wrap(x) = x − 2v_u⌊(x+v_u)/(2v_u)⌋ ∈ [−v_u, v_u)`. Twin: `m_i = v c_i + n_i`. Defaults: `v_u = 10 m/s` (e.g. 77 GHz, `T_pri ≈ 97 µs`), `σ = 0.1 m/s`, `n = 30`, azimuths uniform on ±60°. Naive estimator: least squares `v̂ = Σc m/Σc²`. Unfolding estimator: minimise the wrapped cost `J(v') = Σ wrap(m_i − v'c_i)²` on a grid (step 0.1 m/s on [0, 70]), unwrap all measurements around the minimiser, refit by least squares. Pure Python, seeded, no dependencies.

## Results
(all numbers from `experiments/results.txt`)
1. **Below `v_u` the twin and the real sensor are the same; just below it they are not.** The twin RMSE matches `σ/√Σc²` within sampling error (0.0218 predicted, 0.0199–0.0241 simulated over 300 trials per row; 3% in a 4000-trial unit test). The real naive estimator is identical to the twin up to 9.5 m/s, then 0.183 m/s at 9.8, 1.90 at 10.0, 8.31 at 10.5. The onset is `v_u − O(σ)` not `v_u`: noise pushes the `θ≈0` targets over the wrap boundary, and one flipped target moves the estimate by about `2v_u c/Σc²`. A twin-based certification of a 0.05 m/s accuracy fails on the real sensor from 9.8 m/s.
2. **Exact bias of the naive estimator.** For azimuths uniform on ±Θ and no noise, `bias = −2v_u E[ck]/E[c²]` with `E[ck] = Θ⁻¹ Σ_{j≥1} sin(min(Θ, acos((2j−1)v_u/v)))` over `j` with `(2j−1)v_u < v`, and `E[c²] = ½(1 + sin2Θ/(2Θ))`. It is 0 up to `v_u`, then −14.94 (12 m/s), −20.14 (15), −23.40 (20 and 30), −45.02 (50); simulation with 4000 random azimuths gives −14.92, −20.15, −23.41, −23.40, −45.02. The equal values at 20 and 30 m/s are not a coincidence: once every target has `k = 1` the estimate is `v − D`, `D = 2v_u Σc/Σc²` (23.48 here), independent of `v`. Beyond `2v_u` the naive estimate is not merely noisy but a confident wrong speed, and the RMSE (61.8 m/s) exceeds the true speed at 60 m/s.
3. **The wrapped-cost search undoes it.** From 2 to 60 m/s it reproduces the twin RMSE (0.0199–0.0241) with 0/300 mode errors per row, so the unfolding estimator costs nothing on the real sensor relative to the twin for this geometry.
4. **Unfolding is an angular-diversity question.** A wrong speed `v' = v + Δ` is indistinguishable from the truth when `Δ c_i ≡ 0 (mod 2v_u)` for all `i`. The best one-fold-off candidate fits the pattern `2v_u` against `c`: `D = 2v_u Σc/Σc²`, with residual energy `E_1 = 4v_u²(n − (Σc)²/Σc²)`, a function of the azimuths only (verified to 1e-8 against the wrapped cost in a test). The cost difference is `E_1 + 2√E_1 σ z`, so `P(confusion) ≈ Φ(−√E_1/(2σ))`. Compared with Monte Carlo of the two costs at `v = 25` (2000 scenes per row): ±3° 0.413 vs 0.399; ±5° 0.272 vs 0.265; ±8° 0.061 vs 0.054; ±12° 0.0005 vs 0.0010; ±20° and wider 0 vs 0. Mean `E_1` is 0.002, 0.015, 0.099, 0.50, 3.9, 20.5, 381 at ±3, 5, 8, 12, 20, 30, 60°. The full grid search errs in 218/300, 120/300, 21/300, 0/300 scenes at ±3, 5, 8, 12°, above the single-mode prediction at the narrowest FOVs (there are two neighbours `v ± D` and higher folds) and below it at ±8° (I did not isolate whether this is the grid step, 0.1 m/s, which adds residual energy of order 0.07 at this `E_1`). At ±20° the grid search errs in 7/300 scenes at `n = 5`, 0/300 from `n = 10`.

## Limitations
Stationary scene with known azimuths, known `v_u`, no outliers; real scenes contain moving targets (see `doppler-twin`) and ghost or multipath returns, and azimuth is itself estimated with error, which changes `c_i` and therefore `E_1`. The predictor is a pairwise (two-hypothesis) approximation; I did not derive a union bound for the full search and the comparison with the grid search is qualitative. Noise is white and Gaussian before wrapping; real phase noise near the wrap boundary is correlated with SNR. A fixed `v_u` is assumed; I did not model `T_pri` jitter, which shifts `v_u`. Only ego speed along the boresight is estimated (one parameter, not the full 2D velocity). The "real" radar is a model, not measured; no number here transfers to hardware.

## Next steps
