# Alias twin: a radar twin with no Doppler folding certifies an ego-velocity estimator at every speed, so what does the real, folding sensor do to it, and what does it take to unfold?

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real sensor: a chirp-sequence radar whose radial speeds wrap modulo `2 v_u` (`v_u = λ/(4T_pri)` = 10 m/s here); twin: the same radar with unwrapped radial speeds. Task: ego speed from 30 stationary targets at azimuths uniform in ±60°, noise 0.1 m/s. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`): (1) the twin-tuned least-squares estimator has RMSE 0.022 m/s at every speed in the twin (closed form `σ/√Σcos²θ` matches within sampling error, 300 trials per row), and is identical on the real sensor up to `v ≈ v_u − 2σ`; from 9.8 m/s it breaks (0.18 m/s at 9.8, 1.9 at 10, 8.3 at 10.5, 14.9 at 12); (2) the large-n noiseless bias of the naive estimator is exact and closed form, `−2 v_u E[c k]/E[c²]` with `k = round(v c/2v_u)`, matched to 0.15% in simulation (−14.94 at 12 m/s, −20.14 at 15, −23.40 at 20); once every target is one fold off (`v ≥ 2v_u` here) it equals `−D`, `D = 2 v_u Σc/Σc²`, so the estimate is the true speed minus one fold; (3) a wrapped-cost grid search that unwraps around the best candidate recovers the twin's 0.02 m/s RMSE at every speed up to 60 m/s with zero mode errors in 300 trials per row; (4) whether unfolding is possible is an angular-diversity question: the nearest wrong speed `v ± D` has residual energy `E_1 = 4 v_u²(n − (Σc)²/Σc²)`, independent of `v`, and the pairwise confusion probability `Φ(−√E_1/(2σ))` matches Monte Carlo (0.4133 vs 0.3985 at ±3°, 0.0612 vs 0.0535 at ±8°, 0.0005 vs 0.0010 at ±12°); with a ±5° field of view the grid search picks the wrong fold in 120 of 300 scenes, with ±20° it needs about 10 targets. Stylised: stationary scene, known azimuths and `v_u`, white Gaussian noise before wrapping, no multi-PRF, no Doppler-angle coupling to the target's own motion; see `paper/whitepaper.md`.

```bash
cd research/alias-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 8 tests, well under 1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # about 50 s; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The radar sensor-simulation and ego-velocity direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>; radar odometry and radar simulation) and the Autonomous Systems IRT (<https://www.colorado.edu/irt/autonomous-systems/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. Companion to `doppler-twin` (which estimator to use when targets move), `radar-clutter-twin` and `mount-twin` in this repository. Velocity folding and multi-PRF unfolding are classical radar signal processing (Richards 2014; Skolnik 2008); Doppler-based ego-motion from a stationary-scene sine fit follows Kellner et al. (2013).
