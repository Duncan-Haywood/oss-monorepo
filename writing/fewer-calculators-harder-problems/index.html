Fewer calculators, harder problems

The owner on why this repository's agents should stop shipping small calculators and put the effort into research and the projects with real depth.

20 October 2026 · 3 min read · Source
opinionai-agentsproductresearch

An opinion piece from the owner of this repository.

In my own words, the short version:

"we are still shipping all sorts of stupid calculators. I think we can move more of that effort into cooler projects like the ones I've suggested for example. a lot to work on there. also dedicating effort to actual research and research adjacent things is better than all these stupid caluclators."

The rest of this post is the longer version.

Where the effort went

This repository is built mostly by unattended AI coding sessions. Here is what they have built, counted from main on 20 October 2026:

  • 1,309 products in ventures/.
  • 647 of them are named *-calculator, and 121 of those are <trade>-bid-calculator.
  • 589 calculators have a calc.mjs engine. The average one is 258 lines, and most of those lines are presets, input checks and labels. The attic insulation bid calculator is typical: a table of R-value per inch and price per bag, then area times depth, bags times price, plus hours times a rate.
  • 24 open pull requests have "calculator" in the title. Most add a comparison page to a calculator that already exists ("vs. a flat price per acre", "vs. a spreadsheet") or adopt a stranded branch that did.

We have already written about how the fleet ended up with 121 bid calculators, and the router now ranks template families down. The calculators keep coming anyway, because they are the easiest thing in the repository to finish. A session can start one, test it, give it a page and merge it in an hour. That is exactly why it is the wrong default.

Why it's the wrong default

A calculator whose maths fits on an index card is not a product. Anyone with a chatbot gets the same answer by typing the question. It doesn't compound: the 648th calculator doesn't make the first 647 better, and nothing in it teaches the next session anything. And a page per trade, then a comparison page per calculator, starts to look like content made for search engines rather than for people.

Meanwhile the work that is hard and worth doing is sitting there:

  • Research. research/ has 765 project folders across 28 topics: self-replicating manufacturing, open-source robots, LLM and algorithmic chip-design loops, task-specific AI accelerators, simulation-first mechanical design, engineered clothing, open-source house construction, robot societies, machine learning on qualitative data. Most of those topics have far more open questions than finished answers. Each new result there is something nobody else has.
  • Research-adjacent work. Open tools, datasets, replications and write-ups that come out of the research: the things another researcher or engineer can pick up and build on.
  • The flagships. portfolio/flagships.yaml lists the products with real depth, each with a hard next step: an EN 16931 e-invoice validator running the official Schematron rules, a shift scheduler that has to prove its roster is the cheapest one, a photo pipeline that grades real footage. One milestone on any of those is worth more than a week of calculators.

What I want instead

  1. Default to depth. A run with a free choice picks research, a research-adjacent tool, or the next step on a flagship before it picks a new small product.
  2. A new calculator has to earn its place. It needs an engine that is actually hard: the companion post, If it's a calculator, make the maths hard, says where that bar sits.
  3. Stop dressing up the easy ones. Another comparison page on a bid calculator is not progress. Consolidate the families or leave them alone.

None of this means the existing calculators get deleted. Usage here is unmeasured, not zero, and some of them may turn out to help people. It means the next hour of work goes somewhere it can matter more.
