    The arithmetic, and what it leaves out

    • Owner, weather and other neutral delay earns a time extension, concurrent or not. Your own delay earns none.
    • Only owner delay with no concurrent delay of yours counts as compensable for money.
    • Field overhead is your daily rate times the compensable days. The home-office share uses the Eichleay formula: contract billings over total billings, times home-office overhead, divided by the days the job took, times the compensable days.
    • Liquidated damages are the daily rate times the days left after the adjusted completion date, held to the cap if you enter one.
    • Not modelled: notice and waiver clauses, no-damages-for-delay clauses, the critical path, acceleration, lost productivity, interest, and state or public-owner rules.

    Common questions

    How do you calculate a construction delay claim?

    Sort each delay day by cause. Owner-caused delay that did not overlap with your own delay earns a time extension and money. Weather and other neutral delay earns time only. Your own delay earns neither. Money is the daily field overhead times the compensable days, a home-office overhead share, and profit if the contract allows it.

    What is the Eichleay formula?

    A way to price the head-office overhead a contractor could not recover while a job sat idle. Contract billings divided by total billings for the contract period gives the job's share of home-office overhead. Divided by the days the job actually took it gives a daily rate, and the rate times the compensable delay days is the claim.

    What is a concurrent delay?

    Two delays that overlap in time, one on the owner and one on the contractor. Most contracts give the contractor extra time for the overlap but no extra money, so separating the days is often the whole dispute.

    How are liquidated damages computed?

    The contract's daily rate times the days the job finishes after its adjusted completion date, often limited by a cap. Getting time extensions granted lowers the late days before the rate is applied, which is why an extension can be worth more than the cost claim.

    Does a delay claim need written notice?

    Almost always. Many contracts require written notice of a delay within a short window of the event, sometimes a week or less, and a late notice can waive the claim. This tool does not know your notice terms; read the clause.

    What does this leave out?

    No-damages-for-delay clauses, notice and waiver terms, the critical path (it assumes each day entered really delayed completion), acceleration costs, loss of productivity, interest, state-specific rules and public-owner rules. Have a construction attorney check a real claim.
