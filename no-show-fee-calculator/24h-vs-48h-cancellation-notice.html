24 hours vs 48 hours: the notice window is a waitlist question

Trainers, salons, and tutors search this because a client who wants a shorter cancellation window will argue that 24 hours is “industry standard.” Booking software exposes both toggles. None of them tell you how much of the empty-chair hole a longer rule actually closes.

The fee is not the refill. Charging 100% of the session price still leaves the hour unsold unless someone from the waitlist takes it. A same-day cancel is almost never refillable. A 48-hour cancel often is — if you keep a list and actually call it.

On a typical $75 session, 20 bookings a week, and an 8% late-cancel rate, those openings are $6,240 of chair time a year. If you refill 10% same-day, 40% with 24 hours, and 65% with 48 hours:

The extra $3,432 is not a promise. It is what happens if you can actually hit 65% fill with two days of notice. If your waitlist is empty at every window, a 48-hour rule recovers nothing extra.

Common questions

Is a 48-hour cancellation policy better than 24 hours?

Only if you actually refill more cancelled slots with the extra day. A longer notice window that does not change your waitlist fill rate is just a longer argument with the client. Compare your own fill rates on the same booking volume.

Does a no-show fee replace a waitlist?

No. A collected fee compensates for holding the slot. It does not put another client in the chair. The empty-slot cash and the fee are separate numbers.
