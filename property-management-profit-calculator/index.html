    Questions people ask

    How many doors does a property management company need to be profitable?

    Fees scale with doors but people are hired in whole steps, so profit is not a straight line. The planner finds the door count from which profit stays at or above zero, and shows profit at a ladder of portfolio sizes, including the door just after you need another manager.

    What does it cost when I hire another property manager?

    A manager is paid in full from the door that needs them, but only brings in that door's fee. The planner shows profit at the last door your team covers, one door later, and how many extra doors it takes to earn the hire back.

    What management fee should I charge?

    Higher fees earn more per door and risk owners leaving. The planner searches fee percentages on a sensitivity you set (how many doors you lose for each 1% rise in the fee) and shows the profit at each, so you can see how flat the top is. That sensitivity is your assumption, not a measurement.

    Is winning a new owner worth what it costs?

    The planner turns your numbers into monthly contribution per door, multiplies by doors per owner, and shows payback on the cost to win the owner and the lifetime value at your owner churn.

    Is this legal, tax or financial advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type in plus a sensitivity you choose. Licensing, trust accounting, fair housing, state fee rules, tax and employment law are out of scope.

    Not decided
