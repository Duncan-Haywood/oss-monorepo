Consent Change Record vs. Cookiebot

Different jobs: Cookiebot crawls a whole domain on a schedule, auto-detects trackers, and manages the banner that blocks them. This page compares two trace snapshots you already captured of one consent sequence and reports what actually changed — free, local, no account.

What each tool is for

Cookiebot, from Usercentrics, is a consent management platform (CMP). Its scanner crawls a site's pages on a recurring schedule, auto-detects and categorizes the cookies and trackers it finds against its own database, and generates a cookie declaration page. Its banner blocks third-party scripts from firing until a visitor consents, supports IAB TCF, and covers a whole domain as an ongoing subscription tier scaled to page count. It is sold as recurring software; its own pricing is not something this session can verify, so no figure for it is stated here.

This tool does none of that. It does not crawl a site, does not maintain a tracker database, and does not render or manage a consent banner. It takes exactly two inputs — a before and after JSON trace you already captured around one consent choice (the requests fired and the storage keys written in each state) — and reports what changed between them: which requests appear only after consent, which fire regardless, and which storage keys were added. Comparison runs in your browser tab; nothing you paste is uploaded, and the tool never contacts the site itself. There is no account, no scheduled crawl, and no per-domain subscription.

Common questions

Should I use this tool or Cookiebot?

They do different jobs. Cookiebot (from Usercentrics) is a consent management platform: it crawls a site's pages on a recurring schedule, auto-detects and categorizes the cookies and trackers it finds, renders the consent banner itself, and blocks scripts from firing until a visitor consents, across a whole domain, sold as an ongoing subscription tier. This tool does not crawl anything, does not detect trackers on its own, and does not render or manage a banner. You paste two dated trace exports you already captured — one from before a consent choice, one from after — and it reports which requests and storage keys actually changed between them, entirely in your browser. A site that needs its consent banner built, its scripts auto-blocked, and its whole domain rescanned on a schedule is the case Cookiebot is built for. An agency or reviewer that already has two traces of one consent sequence and wants a fast, free, source-linked record of what that banner actually did is the case this tool is built for — including checking whether a Cookiebot-managed banner is blocking what it claims to.
