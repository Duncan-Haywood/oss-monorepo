Retainer vs hourly: unused hours pay you; unpaid extras do not.

Freelancers search this because a 20-hour block looks clean on a proposal and messy three months later. The question is not “is a retainer better.” It is “better at what usage.”

On the calculator’s default plan — $6,000 take-home, $1,800 costs, 160 available hours, 65% utilization, 20 included hours, 15% buffer — the floor is $75/hr and the retainer is $1,725. Full use is $86.25/hr. That is the buffer working.

The unpaid-overage break-even is $1,725 ÷ $75 = 23 hours. Past that, every extra hour without a billed overage line is a gift. Bill those 5 extra hours at the floor and 25 hours still pays $84/hr — the buffer, restored.

The 10-hour month is the other failure mode. You collected $975 more than hourly. The client paid for access they did not use. That is the usual reason a light-use retainer does not renew. Shorten the block or stay hourly if that is the real pattern.

Common questions

Is a retainer better than hourly for a freelancer?

It depends on how many hours the client actually uses. A retainer pays you more when usage is light and less when they exceed the unpaid break-even without billed overage. On a $1,725 / 20-hour block with a $75 floor, 10 hours of use is $172.50/hr for you and 25 hours with no overage is $69/hr.

How do I know when a retainer stops covering my hourly rate?

Divide the monthly price by your hourly floor. That many hours is the unpaid-overage break-even. Put billed overage at the floor rate in the agreement so months above that number still cover.
