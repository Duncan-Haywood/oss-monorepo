Poshmark vs Mercari vs eBay fees on the same jacket

Poshmark’s 20% looks like the worst deal of the three, and on one measure it is the best. On another it is the worst by a wide margin. Here is one jacket worked through all three, with every number visible, because the headline percentages hide where the money actually goes.

The item

A $60 jacket with $0 shipping charged to the buyer on eBay and Mercari (free shipping), $12 to source, $7.50 to ship, and $1 to pack. The same item and the same costs throughout; only the channel changes. Poshmark is the odd one out: the buyer pays Poshmark’s prepaid label at checkout, so on Poshmark the seller pays no postage at all.

The three, fee by fee

Poshmark takes 20% of the item price: $12.00. Mercari takes 10%: $6.00. eBay takes about 13.6% of the order plus $0.40: $8.56. Yet Poshmark leaves the most, because $7.50 of postage comes out of your pocket on the other two. The fee is not the whole cost of a sale; who pays for shipping can outweigh a ten-point difference in the fee.

After returns

Returns are handled differently: on Poshmark the illustrative return rate is low and the buyer carries the return label, while on Mercari and eBay it is higher and you pay to receive the item back. Counting that, the expected keep per sale is $34.26 on Poshmark, $28.56 on Mercari and $27.21 on eBay. Poshmark’s lead widens.

Per day and per month of shelf

The illustrative days to sell are 60 on Poshmark, 18 on Mercari and 21 on eBay, with sell-through of 30%, 55% and 65%. Waiting costs money when your cash is tied up in the next jacket:

The channel that pays most per sale is last on both. Which column to read depends on what you are short of:

  • Short of cash — you flip clothes and the money from this jacket buys the next one. Read the per-day and per-shelf rows. Mercari and eBay turn the same cash several times faster.
  • Short of closet space or patience — you are clearing pieces you own and nothing waits on the money. Read profit per sale, and Poshmark’s extra $1.50 over Mercari is the whole story.

The calculator can also tell you the size of the premium that would change the answer: Mercari would have to sell this jacket for about $67 and eBay for about $69 to leave what Poshmark leaves at $60. Whether your jacket can carry that is a judgement about its buyers, not arithmetic.

Common questions

Is Poshmark, Mercari or eBay cheaper for selling a $60 jacket?

On the worked example of a $60 jacket that cost $12 to source, Poshmark's fee is the highest at $12.00 against $8.56 on eBay and $6.00 on Mercari, yet Poshmark leaves the most on the sale ($35.00 against $33.50 on Mercari and $30.94 on eBay), because the buyer pays Poshmark's prepaid label while the seller pays $7.50 postage on the other two. Rates are illustrative inputs, so replace them with your own.

Does the channel that pays most per sale win?

Not on speed. With the illustrative days to sell in the calculator, Poshmark takes 60 days to Mercari's 18 and eBay's 21, so Mercari leaves $1.86 per day of waiting, eBay $1.47 and Poshmark $0.58. Per 30 days of shelf, which also counts sell-through, eBay is worth $11.75, Mercari $9.35 and Poshmark $3.81. Which column matters depends on whether your cash or your closet is the constraint.

How much would Mercari or eBay have to sell the jacket for to match Poshmark?

About $67 on Mercari and $69 on eBay, against $60 on Poshmark. The calculator solves for that price rather than choosing for you, because whether a listing can carry the premium is a judgement about your item and the buyers on each channel.
