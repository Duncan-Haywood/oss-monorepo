eBay vs Etsy fees on the same item

“Which is cheaper, eBay or Etsy?” has no single answer, because the two fee structures are shaped differently rather than one simply being lower. Here is one item worked through both, with every number visible.

The item

A $48 item with $6 shipping charged to the buyer. It cost $14 to source, $5 to post, and $1 to pack. Same item, same price, same costs — only the channel changes.

eBay

eBay charges a final value fee on the order total, item plus shipping, and that fee normally covers payment processing. On top of it there is a small fixed fee per order. Using an illustrative 13.6% and $0.40:

Illustrative rates. Yours depend on category and account — check a recent statement.
Order total ($48 + $6)$54.00
Final value fee, 13.6% of $54−$7.34
Fixed fee−$0.40
Item, postage, packaging−$20.00
You keep$26.26

Etsy

Etsy splits the same job across four line items: a per-listing fee charged whether or not the item sells, a transaction percentage, and a separate payment-processing percentage and fixed fee. Using an illustrative $0.20, 6.5%, 3% and $0.25:

Illustrative rates. Offsite Ads, when they apply, add another percentage on top.
Order total ($48 + $6)$54.00
Transaction fee, 6.5% of $54−$3.51
Payment processing, 3% of $54 + $0.25−$1.87
Listing fee−$0.20
Item, postage, packaging−$20.00
You keep$28.42

Why the gap moves

On this item Etsy leaves about $2.16 more, because 9.5% in percentage fees plus $0.45 in fixed fees is less than 13.6% plus $0.40. But that gap is not a constant:

  • On cheap items the fixed and per-listing fees dominate. At a $6 sale price, Etsy’s $0.45 of fixed fees plus the listing fee is a far larger share of the order than eBay’s $0.40.
  • Unsold listings only cost money on one side. Etsy’s listing fee is charged whether or not the item sells, so a slow-moving item pays it repeatedly. Model that by raising the per-listing fee to cover the relistings you expect.
  • Offsite Ads can invert the result. When an Etsy sale is attributed to Offsite Ads, an extra percentage lands on the same order total. Put it in the ads field and watch the ranking change.
  • The price you can get is not the same on both. If the same item lists for more on one channel, the fee gap can be irrelevant. That is the question the calculator’s last column answers: the price each channel needs to leave you the same money.

What fees do not tell you

A marketplace fee is partly a payment for demand you did not have to create. A channel that charges 13% and sells the item forty times a month can be worth more than one that charges 9% and sells it four times — and a channel that sells in a week can be worth more than one that sells in three months, because your cash is not sitting in unsold stock. Put those volumes and days-to-sell in each channel’s panel on the calculator — monthly keep and cash per day are the columns that answer that, and a monthly plan is spread over the same volume number. Fees are the part that is easy to calculate, not the whole decision — treat the comparison as one input. Returns are a separate input: a completed-sale winner can lose once each channel’s return rate is counted. Returns can flip the ranking.

Common questions

Is Etsy or eBay cheaper on the same item?

On the worked example of a $48 item with $6 shipping, Etsy leaves about $2.16 more, because 9.5% in percentage fees plus $0.45 in fixed fees is less than eBay's 13.6% plus $0.40. That gap is not a constant: on cheap items the fixed and per-listing fees dominate, Etsy's per-listing fee is charged whether or not the item sells, and Offsite Ads can invert the result.

Do lower marketplace fees mean more profit?

Not on their own. A marketplace fee is partly a payment for demand you did not have to create, so a channel that charges 13% and sells the item forty times a month can be worth more than one that charges 9% and sells it four times. Days to sell and return rates move the ranking too, which is why the calculator compares monthly keep and cash per day rather than fees alone.
