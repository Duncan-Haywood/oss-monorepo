    The model, and what it leaves out

    • Fee cost. Card sales x processing rate, plus card transactions (card sales / average ticket) x the per-transaction fee.
    • Surcharge. Card customers who stay pay the sale plus the surcharge; the processor also takes its rate on the surcharge. Some switch to cash or debit (no card fee, no surcharge) and some walk away (their margin is lost).
    • Cash discount. Card price stays the list price; cash pays less. Customers who switch to cash pay the discount, so it costs revenue but saves the fee. Walk-aways lose their margin.
    • Break-even customer loss. Profit is linear in the share who walk away, so the largest tolerable share is solved exactly from two points.
    • Rules matter. Card brand rules are commonly reported to cap surcharges near 3%, generally disallow them on debit cards and require disclosure; some states cap or ban them. Confirm current rules with your processor and state.
    • Not modelled: interchange tiers by card type, chargebacks, sales tax on the surcharge, tips, lost repeat business, effects on ticket size, and the cost of changing your point-of-sale set-up.

    Common questions

    Should I add a credit card surcharge or offer a cash discount?

    It depends on how customers react. A surcharge brings in extra money on every card sale that stays on card, but some customers switch to cash or leave. A cash discount gives up revenue on every sale that moves to cash. This calculator puts both next to absorbing the fee, on your own numbers, and shows how many customers you can lose before each stops paying off.

    What surcharge covers my card processing fees?

    With no change in customer behaviour, the surcharge that repays the fee is (rate + per-transaction fee / average ticket) / (1 - rate), because the processor also takes its rate on the surcharge itself. At 2.9% plus 30 cents on a $50 ticket that is about 3.6%, which is above the 3% cap card brand rules are commonly reported to set, so check the current rules.

    Is surcharging credit cards legal?

    It is restricted. Card brand rules commonly cap surcharges near 3%, generally disallow them on debit cards and require disclosure, and some states cap or ban them. A cash discount or dual pricing is treated differently. Confirm current rules with your processor and state regulator before charging anything.
