EPK Generator vs. assembling one by hand in a document editor

A word processor or slide deck can hold the same bio, links, tour dates, and press quotes this generator does — most press kits ever sent were built exactly that way. What it does not do on its own: flag a missing booking email, keep a plain-text copy in sync with the formatted one every time you edit it, or lay out the page. This tool does all three the moment the form is filled in.

What each approach actually does

A document editor — a word processor, a slide deck, a blank page in any general-purpose tool — will hold anything you type: bio, streaming and social links, tour dates, press quotes, a photo. Nothing about the blank page tells you what a press kit is expected to include, so a kit missing a booking contact, or with no way to actually hear the music, ships exactly as easily as a complete one. Producing a plain-text version for a submission form that rejects attachments means building a second copy and manually keeping it matching every time the formatted one changes. Layout — type sizes, spacing, where the photo sits relative to the bio — is a design decision you make yourself, each time you revise it.

This generator takes the same content through one form and builds both files at once: a self-contained, print-friendly HTML one-sheet and a plain-text edition, generated from the identical data so the two can never drift apart. Before it builds anything it requires an artist name and a bio; separately, without blocking generation, it warns when there is no booking contact email, no streaming or social link, or no photo link — the specific gaps a booker or blog notices first, not a spelling or grammar check. The form autosaves to your own browser as you type, so closing the tab mid-edit does not lose the draft.

The two are not mutually exclusive: a document editor is still the right tool for a kit built around custom design work a fixed template cannot express. For the common case — a complete, correctly-formatted one-pager and a plain-text edition to send tonight, with the obvious gaps flagged before a booker finds them — filling in a form is faster than laying out a page.

Common questions

Can I just build a press kit in a word processor or slide deck myself?

Yes — a document editor holds the same bio, links, tour dates, and quotes this generator does, and plenty of press kits in circulation were built exactly that way. What it does not do on its own is tell you the kit is missing something a booker expects, produce a plain-text copy alongside the formatted one, or lay out the page — all three are on you, every time you update it.

What does the generator check that a blank document does not?

A blank document accepts whatever you type and stays silent about what you left out. This generator requires an artist name and a bio before it will build anything, and separately warns — without blocking — when there is no booking contact email, no streaming or social link, or no photo link, because those are the specific gaps a booker or blog notices first.

Why does a plain-text version matter?

Plenty of festival submission forms and booking emails reject an attached file or strip its formatting, and a document built in a word processor or slide tool has no plain-text edition unless you build one separately and then keep it matching every time you update the original. This generator produces both from the same form in the same pass, so the two never drift apart.

Is a document editor ever the better choice?

Yes, for a layout this generator does not offer — a designed multi-page PDF with custom typography and photo placement, or a kit built around a visual identity a document or design tool can express and this one, deliberately kept to one plain template, cannot. This generator trades that design flexibility for a page that appears the moment the form is filled in, with no layout decisions to make.
