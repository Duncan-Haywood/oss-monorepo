What is an electronic press kit, and what belongs in it?

An electronic press kit (EPK) is a one-page summary a musician or band sends to the people who decide whether to book, cover, or playlist them — a venue talent buyer, a festival submission reviewer, a blog or playlist curator, a radio music director. It exists because that person is almost always looking at dozens or hundreds of submissions and deciding fast: who is this, what do they sound like, what have they done, and how do I reach them.

What belongs in it

  • A short bio — a few sentences to two short paragraphs. Lead with what you sound like and any concrete, checkable facts (years active, notable shows, releases) before adjectives.
  • One good photo — a live shot or a clean promo photo. It does not need to be a professional shoot to start.
  • Links to actually listen — Spotify, Bandcamp, YouTube, whatever is live. This is the section a reviewer clicks first.
  • Contact info — a booking or press email a reader can use without hunting through a website.
  • Upcoming shows, if any — proves you're active and gives a reader a reason to feature you now.
  • Press quotes, if you have any — even one line from a local blog is social proof. Skip this section entirely until you do; a placeholder quote reads worse than none.

What doesn't belong in it

Your whole discography, a mission statement, or anything a reader has to scroll far to get past. An EPK is a fast decision aid, not a website — if you want the deeper version, link to your actual site from it, not the other way around.

How it differs from a website or an Instagram bio

A website is built for fans and can be as deep as you like. An Instagram bio is built for discovery and has almost no room. An EPK sits between them: built for one specific reader making one specific decision, so it stays to a single page or screen and leads with exactly what that decision needs.

Frequently asked questions

Who actually reads an EPK?

Venue bookers, festival submission reviewers, blog and playlist curators, and radio music directors — almost always skimming dozens or hundreds of submissions, so a one-page, scannable format is the point, not a limitation.

How is an EPK different from a website or Instagram bio?

A website is built for fans and can be as deep as you like; an EPK is built for a gatekeeper making a fast yes/no decision, so it stays to one page or screen and leads with exactly what that decision needs: who you sound like, what you've done, and how to reach you.

Do I need professional photos and press quotes before making one?

No. A clean phone photo and no press coverage yet is a normal, complete first EPK — the generator simply omits any section you leave blank rather than showing a placeholder. Update it as you get better photos and your first write-up.
