This calculator vs. Crest Capital's lease calculator

One prices a lease in isolation. The other compares whatever rate you were offered — lease, discount, or subcontractor — against what you already pay, at the job volume you actually run.

What Crest Capital's calculator is

Crest Capital is a national equipment financing and leasing lender, founded in 1989 and headquartered in Alpharetta, Georgia, that finances equipment, vehicles, and software for small and mid-size businesses. Its site offers a free calculator that takes the cost of one piece of equipment and estimates the monthly payment for financing or leasing it through Crest Capital, typically as a first step toward submitting an application. It prices one thing — a new lease or loan on one asset — and is built to lead toward Crest Capital's own financing product.

What this calculator is

This page does not estimate a lease payment and is not tied to any lender. It starts from a rate you already have — a supplier's bulk-discount pricing, a subcontractor's day rate, or a lease payment quoted by Crest Capital or anyone else — and compares it against your current fixed and per-job costs at the number of jobs you actually run each month. It shows the monthly cost, cost per job, profit per job, and margin for each option side by side, plus the exact job volume where a new fixed fee pays for itself against the per-job savings it offers. Everything runs in the browser; nothing is uploaded, and there is no application to submit.

QuestionWhere to answer it
I want to finance or lease a new piece of equipment and need a monthly payment estimate before applying. Crest Capital's calculator or a comparable lender's — pricing new financing is exactly its job, and this calculator has no financing terms of its own to offer.
I already have a lease quote, a supplier discount, or a subcontractor rate and want to know if it actually beats what I pay today at my real job volume. This calculator — enter your current cost and the new rate's fixed and per-job figures, and it shows the break-even volume and the cost per job at your own numbers.
I want to compare up to three vendor offers against each other and against my current baseline in one table. This calculator — Crest Capital's tool prices one lease at a time and does not compare it to a baseline or to other vendors.
I don't want to enter my equipment cost into a lender's site before I know whether the deal is worth pursuing. This calculator — it needs only the fixed and per-job cost figures already on the rate card or lease quote, and sends nothing to Crest Capital or anyone else.

Neither tool replaces the other. A business financing a new asset for the first time still needs a lender's own payment estimate — that is what Crest Capital's calculator, or a comparable lender's, is for. Once a rate is on the table, from a lender, a supplier, or a subcontractor, this page is where you check whether it is actually cheaper at the volume you run, before signing anything.
