Do the two accessibility engines agree, and is the difference disclosed?

Study · 2026-08-24 · portfolio/research/a11y-engine-agreement/README.md

Built and measured 2026-08-21. Re-runnable, offline, no credentials:

Why this was built

This portfolio has two independent WCAG audit engines, both built for the same candidate — eaa-remediation-pipeline, the pipeline's highest-composite entry:

EngineBuilt forInput
a11y-evidence-record/audit.pythe measurement passesstorefront templates (Liquid, Twig, Blade, EJS, Vue, Svelte)
pipeline/probes/eaa-wcag-triage/the executable probeplain HTML

portfolio/research/README.md already names the risk:

audit.py and eaa-wcag-triage are two independent accessibility-audit engines built for the same candidate, with overlapping check IDs, and until this note neither referenced the other.

It named it and stopped there. Nobody had run them on the same input. This directory does, and the answer was not "they agree."

The reason this matters commercially rather than aesthetically: the thing the candidate sells is an evidence record, and its liability position is that coverage limits are stated in the product rather than buried in terms. A record carries two lists — what was found, and what was not checked. A reader can only distinguish "checked and passed" from "never looked at" by those two lists. A criterion in neither is invisible to the customer, and the candidate's own write-up says the customer who believes coverage is total is the one who gets hurt.

Finding 1 — each engine was silently blind to checks the other had

Measured against the hand-labeled fixture set in eaa-wcag-triage/fixtures/, whose 14 findings across three pages were labeled by hand and are the only ground truth either engine has.

audit.py missed two blocking Level A failures that the sibling engine caught on the same three pages, and — the actual defect — listed neither in its "Not tested by this method" boundary, which enumerated 17 other criteria:

MissedCriterionWhy it was invisible
<video> with no captions track1.2.2 CaptionsNo check, and 1.2.2 absent from the disclosure list entirely
focusable control with aria-hidden="true"4.1.2 Name, Role, Value4.1.2 was in the checked list (via button names), so a criterion-level comparison shows no gap — the missing case hides one level down

The reverse direction was also true. Two criteria audit.py checks were neither checked nor declared manual by the probe, so the probe's coverage statement was silent about them too:

Missed by the probeCriterion
autoplaying media with sound1.4.2 Audio Control
viewport meta that blocks zoom to 200%1.4.4 Resize Text

Neither engine was wrong about anything it reported. Both were quietly narrower than their own coverage statement implied, in opposite directions, and the two blind spots were disjoint — which is what you would expect from two engines written independently and never compared.

Finding 2 — the residual disagreement is attribution, not coverage

With those four gaps closed, both engines detect all 12 shared defect classes. Ten of the twelve cite the same success criterion. Two do not:

Defectaudit.py citeseaa-wcag-triage cites
form control with no label3.3.2 Labels or Instructions1.3.1 Info and Relationships
skipped heading level1.3.1 Info and Relationships2.4.6 Headings and Labels

This is not a coverage gap — the defect is reported either way, at the same severity, on the same element. It is a reproducibility problem, and it is narrow but real for this candidate specifically: the deliverable is a dated document a merchant hands to an enterprise buyer's procurement team or to their own counsel. Two records of the same page, produced by the same portfolio, citing different success criteria for the same defect, is the kind of detail that costs a document its authority.

It is deliberately left open. Deciding which citation is correct is a normative reading of WCAG, and both mappings have support in common practice (axe-core maps an unlabelled control to 1.3.1, 3.3.2 and 4.1.2; heading-order is classified there as best-practice rather than tied to one criterion). This repository's standing rule is that regulatory and normative claims are unverified against primary sources and must be checked against the actual specification before being acted on — and the W3C specification hosts are egress-denied from this environment (see egress-reachability/). Picking a winner here would be exactly the kind of confident-but-unsourced normative claim the pipeline's own status note warns against.

So it is recorded, pinned by a test (test_the_only_remaining_gaps_are_the_two_known_attribution_splits) so that a real coverage gap cannot hide behind a known attribution one, and left for whoever can read the specification directly.

What changed as a result

Both engines were changed; both suites pass.

a11y-evidence-record/audit.py — 61 → 75 tests

  • Added media-no-captions (1.2.2). <video> only: 1.2.2 is synchronized media, and audio-only content falls under 1.2.1 and needs a transcript this pass cannot see. A <track kind> that is a template expression withdraws the claim and is recorded as undecidable, matching the file's existing expression discipline.
  • Added interactive-aria-hidden (4.1.2), firing only when the element is still in the tab order — hidden, tabindex="-1", an <a> with no href and type="hidden" all make aria-hidden legitimate.
  • Added CoverageBoundaryIsComplete, two tests asserting that no criterion is both checked and declared untested, and that every statically reachable criterion appears in one list or the other. This is the invariant that would have caught 1.2.2 going missing, and it is the part of this change most likely to still be earning its keep in a year.

pipeline/probes/eaa-wcag-triage/ — 37 → 48 tests

  • Added rule_autoplay_unmuted (1.4.2) and rule_viewport_scaling (1.4.4), with their coverage-catalog rows.
  • The name rules (link, button, control) now skip elements that are aria-hidden, matching what audit.py has always done. An element AT never reaches is not a naming barrier; if it is also still focusable, rule_interactive_aria_hidden reports that instead, which is the accurate diagnosis — the fix is to stop hiding it, not to add a name. This removed a false-positive class where an intentionally hidden, non-focusable control was reported as unlabelled.
  • The aria-hidden rule gained the same focusability predicate as audit.py, so the two engines apply one definition rather than two.

The probe's committed fixture validation (validation-2026-08-20.json) is byte-identical after these changes — precision and recall stay 1.0, and none of the new rules fires on the fixtures. The changes add coverage without disturbing the existing regression guard.

What this does and does not do to the pipeline

Does not. No candidate is added, no score is changed, no ventures/ directory is created, nothing is published, no account is registered, no credential is used, nothing is spent beyond agent time. No claim here is a compliance claim.

No score moves, and the reasoning follows the rule rescore-2026-08-20.md set for itself. The eight scored dimensions ask about leverage, ops, revenue, margin, defensibility, distribution, liability and ethics. What is measured here is the correctness of the tooling, and no dimension asks about it. The nearest candidate is liability_containment, and the honest reading is that this change restores the record to the coverage it already claimed rather than improving on it — the score was never earned by the missing checks. Recording that explicitly is cheaper than a later pass moving a score by reflex.

Does. It establishes that the two engines disagreed, closes the four coverage gaps, leaves the two attribution splits open with the reason, and adds to each engine the invariant that makes a silent gap fail a test rather than ship in a customer-facing record.

What could not be measured here

  • The corpus is three hand-labeled fixture pages. They are the only ground truth either engine has, and they were written by the probe's author to exercise the probe's rules — so they are biased towards the probe and against audit.py, which is the direction that makes the 12-vs-14 result conservative rather than flattering. They say nothing about the rate at which either engine is wrong on real storefronts.
  • The template half is untested against the probe. audit.py handles six template syntaxes and the probe handles none, so the corpus comparison can only run on plain HTML. The template-specific paths in audit.py — expression-aware label matching, the undecidable taxonomy — have no second engine to disagree with them and remain checked only by their own tests.
  • No re-measurement of the theme corpus. a11y-evidence-record's headline numbers (286 findings, 63.2% undecidable over twelve npm storefront themes) were produced by the engine before 1.2.2 and the aria-hidden case existed, so the finding count is a lower bound by an unknown margin. Re-deriving it needs measure_themes.py to re-fetch the twelve tarballs from the npm registry; --recompute-from cannot do it, because the saved results hold per-package rows rather than the markup. That is a bounded, network-dependent follow-up, named here rather than silently left.
  • Neither engine is validated against a third-party tool. Both are this portfolio's own. Agreement between two engines written in the same repository is weaker evidence than agreement with axe-core would be, and axe-core's distribution hosts are egress-denied from here.
