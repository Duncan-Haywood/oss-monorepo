Does the record a customer receives carry the tool's own error rate?

Study · 2026-08-23 · portfolio/research/error-rate-disclosure/README.md

Question. Every probe in portfolio/pipeline/probes/ has been measured for accuracy, and every one of them renders something a customer would read. Does the second contain the first?

Answer, as first measured. No — not once, in four of four. Two of the four had a committed accuracy number and neither printed it. One named the direction it errs in and has never measured the rate for that direction. Two have no customer-facing record module at all. And in three of the four, the direction nobody disclosed is the direction the operator gets paid for.

This is the pipeline's second gate measurement, taken the same day as the first: ethical_robustness on the four candidates that have a probe — eaa-remediation-pipeline 9 → 6, public-record-watch 9 → 6, cra-evidence-desk 8 → 6, agent-permission-harness 9 → 7. No candidate crossed the gate.

Amended the same day: three of the four gaps are closed and the scores moved. eaa-wcag-triage now prints both of its committed figures, names both directions, and states which one pays the operator — eaa-remediation-pipeline 6 → 9. source-silence-detector gained the customer-facing record it did not have, which prints its held-out detection rate and refuses to render an absence of alerts as health — public-record-watch 6 → 9. A rubric bug that charged the alignment deduction to an artifact that discloses its adverse direction was fixed — cra-evidence-desk 6 → 7. Current scores: 9, 9, 7, 7. What remains is agent-permission-harness, which has no record module and no measured rate, and npm-reachability-triage, whose adverse direction has never been measured so there is no figure to print. Full account in What changed after this was measured; the tables and findings below are left as measured, because the whole point of the directory is what the artifacts said at the moment they were looked at.

Hand-verifying npm-reachability-triage's pnpm gap found 3 of its 53 hand-verified suppression claims traced to withdrawn OSV advisories rather than a reasoning error; the committed figure moved from 0 of 53 (0 of 102 raw) to 0 of 50 (0 of 97 raw), and this directory's citation for cra-evidence-desk now quotes the revised number. npm-reachability-triage's artifact also grew a second direction-vocabulary match (a new sentence mentioning that a suppression could be wrong, which the D2 vocabulary correctly catches). Every verdict, every rubric dimension and every score is unchanged — cra-evidence-desk stays 8 in candidates.yaml, because the deduction this citation feeds was never charged either before or after.

Why this is the gate and not an essay

pipeline/README.md defines ethical_robustness as "the business gets better for customers as it scales, not worse", and all eight scores for it were 2026-08-18 screening estimates in the 8–9 band. A dimension phrased that way invites an estimate, which is why it stayed one through eleven screening passes and four probes.

It becomes measurable once you name the mechanism by which an evidence business gets worse: the customer acts on a record whose errors are invisible to them, in a direction the operator profits from. Both halves are checkable here. The error rates are committed in this repository. The artifacts are rendered by code in this repository. Whether the first appears in the second is a string search.

Method

Three disclosure classes, evaluated by three different methods on purpose, so a weakness in one does not decide the result.

ClassHow it is decidedWhy that way
Scope — what was and was not examinedStructural: the rendering module declares a coverage/limits constant and it reaches the rendered textA module attribute exists or it does not; nothing to tune
Direction — which way the tool is wrongLexical, over a narrow vocabulary about the tool's own accuracy, never about its scope. Every match is quoted in the resultsThe conflation this whole measurement exists to separate: "does not check keyboard operability" is scope, "false positives are expected" is direction
Rate — how oftenNumeric: the committed figure in every plausible rendering, searched for in the artifact. The citation is re-verified against its source file on every runA measurement that moves elsewhere cannot leave a stale number here

Each probe's artifact is produced by calling its own rendering code on a real input — a page for the accessibility engine, a two-finding report for the dependency triage, the committed register history for the silence detector, this repository's 31 workflow files for the permission harness. Nothing is hand-written and nothing is fetched.

1. Scope disclosure is not accuracy disclosure

eaa-wcag-triage's evidence record is the most careful disclosure artifact in this repository. It labels every success criterion automated, partial, manual or unimplemented; it prints the contrast decidability share; it names every undecided element by why it could not be decided. Its coverage statement opens "This is a STATIC pass" and closes "A clean result here is NOT a statement of conformity."

It never says that a finding in the list can be wrong. Out of sample, 12–18% of them are not defects — a11y-own-sites measured precision at 0.821–0.881 and traced every false positive to markup the build tool emitted rather than the site owner. A reader of the record cannot discount it, because the record does not tell them there is anything to discount.

A second number for the same probe landed on main while this was being written, and changes nothing except the size of the gap. a11y-axe-recall/ measured out-of-sample recall at 0.976 against axe-core (0.975 when this was written; the denominator grew with the engine's claims). So eaa-wcag-triage now has both halves of its accuracy measured and committed, and its record prints neither. A test here pins that, so if either number reaches the artifact this directory has to be re-run.

This is worth stating plainly because the natural defence — "but the coverage statement is extremely thorough" — is true and does not help. Coverage answers "what did you look at". Accuracy answers "is what came back right". The pipeline had been reading the first as evidence about the second for four passes.

2. The two halves of the omission split evenly

npm-reachability-triage is the only artifact that names its own error direction, and it does it well, in the body of the record rather than a footnote:

False positives are expected; a suppressed finding that should not have been is the failure this tool is built to avoid, and both are possible.

That sentence identifies exactly the right hazard. The rate for it has never been measured. validation-2026-08-21-build-surface.json records suppression rates — how much was suppressed — which say nothing about how much of that was wrong. So one probe knows which way it fails and not how often; two know how often and never say which way; and the fourth knows neither.

3. The detector that prints MISSED and tells nobody

source-silence-detector has no customer-facing record module. The closest thing it renders is an operator table, and this is a real row of it:

source                                   thr   med   max  ...  quiet?   lat
FDA/openfda                             2060    37  1373  ...  MISSED    --

The detector knows it missed that source. Held-out recall is 0.35 — six of seventeen dormancy episodes — committed in the probe's own README. A subscriber sees the same silence whether the register is quiet or the watch failed, so unlike a false positive this error is unobservable from outside. That is the asymmetry that makes it a gate question rather than a quality question.

The rubric, stated before it was applied

A gate score arrived at case by case is an estimate wearing a measurement's clothes. Three deductions, applied uniformly, from the prior screening estimate:

  1. −1 rate undisclosed — the artifact carries no accuracy figure, whether because it was measured elsewhere and omitted or never measured at all.
  2. −1 direction undisclosed — the artifact does not say which way the tool is wrong. Scope disclosure does not substitute.
  3. −1 adverse alignment — the undisclosed direction is the one that increases the operator's revenue, judged against the stated revenue model.

Deduction 3 is a reading of a business model, not a measurement, and is held in a separate table in measure.py with its reasoning attached so it is visible as such. Deductions 1 and 2 are computed.

What this does not claim

  • Not that these are bad businesses. No candidate crossed the gate, and the mitigations are cheap: printing a measured precision in a record is one line of rendering. The gap is disclosure, not a model that has to harm someone.
  • Not a measurement of the other four candidates. They have no probe, so there is no artifact to inspect. Their ethical_robustness scores remain 2026-08-18 estimates and should be read as upper bounds.
  • Not a customer study. Whether a disclosed error rate changes what a buyer does is untested here and untestable from this environment. What is measured is whether the information reaches them at all.
  • Not a claim that the probes are inaccurate. Two of them have better measured accuracy than anything else in this repository. The finding is about where those numbers stop travelling.

Consequences recorded elsewhere

  • Four ethical_robustness scores and four composites in candidates.yaml, with the derivation in each measured: block, and the matching rows in the four write-ups and the pipeline table.
  • score_sensitivity.py had a fixed sentence claiming every revision is "down by one". Two of these are −2 and one is −3, so that sentence is derived now, and the script withholds its rank extrapolation rather than applying a magnitude it does not have. It also used to return at that point, which meant a second measured dimension would have silently deleted sections 4 and the verdict along with the extrapolation; it no longer does.
  • The cheapest follow-up is not more measurement. It is four lines of rendering: print the number each probe already knows into the artifact it already emits. Three of the four deductions here are recoverable that way, and doing it would make this directory's own tests fail — which is how it is meant to work.

What changed after this was measured

Everything above is the state as measured. This section is what happened next, the same day, and it is kept separate rather than folded in: a measurement directory that quietly rewrites its finding to match a later fix loses the only thing it was for.

1. eaa-wcag-triage closed its gap — eaa-remediation-pipeline 6 → 9

The prediction in "What this does not claim" was that printing a measured precision is one line of rendering. It was four, in probes/eaa-wcag-triage/src/wcag_triage/evidence.py, and the tests here failed exactly as designed.

The record now carries an ACCURACY block rendered in two places — a headline in the summary, and a full section immediately above the findings table it qualifies — with both committed figures, each stating its corpus, date, derivation path and the reason it is not the last word:

valuedirectionlimit disclosed in the record
precision0.821–0.881over-reportingdenominator excluded 1.4.3 entirely
recall0.976under-reportingone comparison; corpus exercises 3 of 18 criteria

It also does the thing deduction 3 asks for, which a rate alone does not: "this audit is the qualification step for a paid remediation job, so over-reporting is the error that would pay us. That is why the precision figure is printed here, beside the findings it applies to, rather than supplied on request."

All three deductions lapse, so the score returns to its 2026-08-18 screening estimate. Read it as the estimate no longer being reduced, not as a positive finding — this rubric measures disclosure and nothing else, and none of the three residual risks in the table above is resolved by disclosing it.

probes/eaa-wcag-triage/tests/test_accuracy_citations.py applies this directory's own Citation.verify() discipline one layer out: each figure printed at a customer is checked against the derivation it names, so a rate revised upstream fails the build rather than becoming a false statement about the product.

2. The rubric was charging a deduction it does not state — cra-evidence-desk 6 → 7

Deduction 3 is published as "the undisclosed error direction is the one that increases the operator's revenue." score() applied it whenever ALIGNMENT[candidate] was adverse, with no reference to disclosure. So an artifact could disclose everything and still be charged, which makes the deduction unrecoverable by the mitigation the rubric asks for — the opposite of what "Three of the four deductions here are recoverable that way" claims.

npm-reachability-triage is the one artifact of the four that already named its adverse direction, in the body of the record, so it was the one paying for this. The bug survived because test_full_disclosure_costs_nothing — whose name states the intended semantics — only ever ran against agent-permission-harness, the single candidate whose alignment is not adverse. The case where the distinction bites was untested.

score() now matches the published rubric, and both directions are pinned: full disclosure costs nothing even when alignment is adverse, and hiding an adverse direction still costs two. A new alignment_charged field records whether the deduction fired, separately from alignment_adverse, which still records the reading of the business model.

This is a harness correction, not a re-probe. cra-evidence-desk's remaining deduction is the real one and has not moved: the false-suppression rate has never been measured, so there is no figure to print.

3. D2's sentence splitter could not survive a decimal point

Found by fixing (1), and worth recording because it is the same shape as the finding this directory exists to report. sentences() was re.findall(r"[^.!?\n]*[.!?]", text), which is correct on prose and wrong the moment an artifact contains a number — and an accuracy disclosure is nothing but numbers. Against the new record it returned ["**Precision 0.", "881, recall 0."].

The boolean was unaffected: whole sentences matched too, so direction_disclosed was right either way. What broke is the checkability. D2 is lexical, and the reason that is acceptable is stated in the method table — "Every match is quoted in the results so the classification can be checked rather than trusted." A quoted fragment cannot be checked, so the method's own justification had quietly stopped holding. A period between two digits is now a decimal point; a newline still ends a sentence; three tests pin it, including one that renders all four artifacts and asserts no quoted match ends mid-number.

4. source-silence-detector gained the record it never had — public-record-watch 6 → 9

This was the sharp one, and it needed a build rather than a rendering line. The probe had no customer-facing module at all: its operator table printed MISSED beside a register whose dormancy the detector failed to catch, and nothing carried that — or the committed held-out recall of 0.35 — to a subscriber.

probes/source-silence-detector/subscriber_record.py is that surface. The rate and the direction are there, as for the accessibility probe. Two things are specific to this product, and they are the reason a rate alone would not have been enough:

  • A miss here is unobservable from outside. A false alarm announces itself; a missed dormancy looks exactly like good news, because the subscriber sees nothing either way. So the record carries a standing caveat that its own silence is worthless as evidence — "NO ALERT FROM US IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT A REGISTER IS ACTIVE... treat an alert from us as a prompt to look, and the absence of one as no information at all" — and a test enumerates every state string the record can emit and fails on "active", "healthy", "OK", "clear", "fine". The record says no alert, never anything that implies health.
  • A nominally-watched source can be effectively unwatched. The anchor is a high quantile of each source's own gaps, and nearest-rank p99 is the maximum below 100 gaps, so FDA/openfda — which contributes 9 of the 17 holdout episodes — calibrates to a 2060-day threshold. Its row would otherwise read no alert beside a months-old last change. It is now listed by name under "sources this watch does not usefully cover", with the arithmetic.

The alignment disclosure is the blunt one this candidate needs: "a false alarm costs us a subscriber, and a miss costs us nothing and is invisible to you — so our commercial incentive runs toward tuning this watch to stay quiet."

The same caution as (1) applies harder here. The 9 is a disclosure score. Held out, this detector still finds 6 of 17 dormancy episodes at a median 110 days, and reaching 14 of 17 costs ~43 nuisance alarm-days per source-year. None of that improved. What changed is that a subscriber is now told it.

5. agent-permission-harness closed its gap — ethical_robustness 7 → 9

validate_accuracy.py (a labeled 34-case synthetic fixture corpus, run for real through harness.analyse()) measured 0 false positives / 0 false negatives on the 16/34 positive-rule opportunities those fixtures cover, and render_markdown() now states that figure, its direction, and its honest limits (author-authored fixtures, not held-out; silent on attack classes none of the seven rules encode) in a "How this can be wrong" section. measure.py cites the committed figure for real instead of citation=None. See portfolio/pipeline/probes/agent-permission-harness/.

6. npm-reachability-triage closed the last gap, by measuring rather than rendering — cra-evidence-desk 7 → 8

Every other gap here closed by building a record module or adding a rendering line onto a rate that already existed. This one had no rate to render: the tool's own copy names its adverse direction — "a suppressed finding that should not have been is the failure this tool is built to avoid" — but nobody had checked how often that failure actually happens.

Closed by hand, not by a fixture corpus like agent-permission-harness used (that direction needs real advisories and real lockfiles, not synthetic cases). A fresh OSV feed run across the portfolio's seven npm products produces 102 suppressed findings, reducing to 53 distinct (advisory, rule, package) claims once the same advisory suppressed the same way on more than one product is counted once. Each was checked against its real advisory text and the actual lockfile/source evidence the suppression rule cites — 0 of 53 (0 of 102 raw findings) were wrong.

triage/evidence.py gained the same shape harness.py used for its own figure: an ACCURACY_NOTE constant, declared at module level and rendered into both the markdown and JSON records under a "How this can be wrong" section. measure.py now cites the figure for real, pinned against this probe's own README by Citation.verify() on every run, instead of citation=None.

One real but non-blocking finding came out of the review, and it is disclosed in the probe's README rather than fixed here: GHSA-89xv-2m56-2m9x is tagged server-surface:middleware when its actual subject is SSRF in Server Actions, because classify() matches the first pattern that hits anywhere in the advisory text rather than ranking the summary first. The suppression verdict is still correct; only the audit label a reviewer sees is imprecise.

This closes the directory. All four probes now have both a committed accuracy figure and a rendering that carries it, so rate_in_artifact is True across the board for the first time. See portfolio/pipeline/probes/npm-reachability-triage/ and its wrong-suppression-verification-2026-08-21.json.

Consequences recorded elsewhere: five measured: blocks and three composites in candidates.yaml, the two write-ups, the pipeline table, and score_sensitivity.py, whose section 3 asserted "Every revision is downward" as fixed prose and is now derived.
