    What it does, and what it leaves to you
    
    
      
      
    • Capacity: a tub can wash open minutes / (minutes in the tub + clean-up minutes) dogs a day. Full-service dogs are served first; self-serve gets the tub time left. A plan above capacity is rejected, and a busy month that goes over it turns the extra dogs away.
      • 
      
    • Sales: self-serve washes = tubs x washes per tub x open days, using the weekday and weekend figures, times the busy or slow season share and the ramp share. Sales = washes x price + the expected add-on + full-service dogs x their price.
      • 
      
    • Costs: card fees and the repairs reserve are shares of sales; each wash costs its water (gallons x cost per 1,000), heating, shampoo and towels; full-service labor = dogs x staff minutes x wage; attendant labor = hours x open days x wage x (1 + payroll share); rent, utilities, insurance, software, other fixed costs and owner pay are monthly. Operating profit = sales less all of these.
      • 
      
    • Break-even: what one self-serve wash earns after card fees, the reserve and its own supplies, divided into the fixed costs (less what full-service earns), and the same with the loan payments added. Shown per open day and per tub.
      • 
      
    • Return: start-up cost = tubs x cost per tub + square feet x build-out + the other lines. Yield on cost = full-traffic operating profit / start-up cost. Loan = share of start-up cost, level payments. Month by month from opening, traffic ramps in a straight line, and cumulative cash starts at minus your equity. Payback is the first month it reaches zero. If you enter a sale multiple, the shop sells at that many times the last twelve months of operating profit, less the loan balance; with it blank, no sale is assumed.
      • 
      
    • Not decided: how many dogs your location will bring or how fast; zoning, sewer and water connection fees, interceptor and drainage rules, animal-establishment and sales-tax rules; income tax and depreciation; build-out overruns and rent paid before opening; staff injuries, bites and damage claims; and what a lender will actually offer.

    Common questions
    
    
    How much does a self-serve dog wash make?
    
    
    It depends on your price, how many dogs each tub washes a day, your rent and what you pay for water and labor. Enter your own figures; the page works out sales, running costs, operating profit, yield on what you spend to open, the loan coverage and how long it takes to get your equity back.
    
    
    How many dogs can one tub wash in a day?
    
    
    Divide the open minutes by the minutes a dog spends in the tub plus the minutes to drain and clean it. A 30-minute wash with 10 minutes of clean-up in a 10-hour day is 15 washes at the very most. Real traffic is a fraction of that, and the page rejects a plan that needs more than the tub can do.
    
    
    How many washes a day do I need to break even?
    
    
    Two figures are shown: the washes per open day that cover running costs, and the higher number that also covers the loan payment, both per shop and per tub. They come from what each wash earns after card fees, the repairs reserve, water, heat and supplies, set against your fixed costs and attendant hours.
    
    
    Should I offer full-service as well as self-serve?
    
    
    Full-service dogs pay more per tub, but they need staff and they use tub time that self-serve customers would have used. The page serves full-service dogs first, caps self-serve at the tub time left, and shows what full-service adds after labor and supplies.
    
    
    Does this tell me what traffic to expect or whether I can get a permit?
    
    
    No. It works from your own figures. Wash traffic, local prices, zoning, sewer and water connection fees, interceptor rules and animal-establishment licensing all depend on your location, so check them before you sign a lease.
    
    
    Is my data uploaded anywhere?
    
    
    No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
