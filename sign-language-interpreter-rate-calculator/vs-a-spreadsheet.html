This calculator vs. a spreadsheet

A lot of freelance interpreters already track fees in a spreadsheet: one column for hours, one for rate, a formula that multiplies them. That arithmetic is trivial and a spreadsheet does it fine once you already know the billable hours and the right multiplier. What it does not do on its own is apply the booking minimum, the after-hours differential, and portal-to-portal travel together -- three formulas to write and keep in sync, before you even get to the team-interpreting question a spreadsheet has no way to ask.

Common questions

Isn't an interpreting fee just hours times a rate?

The multiplication itself is trivial and a spreadsheet does it fine once you already know the billable hours and the right multiplier. What a plain spreadsheet does not do on its own is apply the booking minimum (billing the greater of hours requested or the minimum, not just the hours typed in), the after-hours/weekend/holiday differential, and portal-to-portal travel together — each is its own formula to write and keep in sync with the others.

Will a spreadsheet tell me when a booking should be staffed with two interpreters?

No. That is a judgment call from RID's own Standard Practice Papers, not an arithmetic rule a spreadsheet formula can express on its own unless you build and maintain a lookup by setting and length. This calculator checks the booking's setting and billable hours against that guidance automatically and surfaces it as a finding — it never forces the team rate on, but it also never lets a six-hour legal deposition go quietly priced for one interpreter.

What happens if I apply the wrong differential multiplier in my own sheet?

Nothing catches it — a spreadsheet returns whatever the formula says, whether the 1.5x evening/weekend or 2x holiday multiplier you typed matches the booking you actually have or not. This calculator derives the multiplier from the dropdown you select, so it can't drift from what you picked, and it separately reports the booking minimum, the differential, travel, and mileage as their own line items rather than one collapsed number.

Does this replace an interpreter's own income-tracking spreadsheet?

No. A spreadsheet or ledger that tracks every booking, every invoice, and every month's total over a year is still the right tool for that ongoing record. This page answers one question at a time — what should this one assignment cost, and does its setting and length suggest a team — and nothing it computes is saved anywhere once you leave the page.
