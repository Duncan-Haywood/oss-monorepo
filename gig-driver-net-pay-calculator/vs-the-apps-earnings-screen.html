This calculator vs. the app's own earnings screen

Every gig platform's earnings screen does the same arithmetic: base pay plus tips plus promotions, added up and divided by the hours you were online, into one gross-per-hour figure. It is fast, and it is the authoritative record of what a session actually paid out.

What it has no field for is the car. Gas, tires, oil changes, and insurance are entirely the driver's own cost, and no platform subtracts them before showing a per-hour number -- it has no way to know your gas price, your MPG, or what your insurance actually costs. So "I made $33.85 an hour" on the earnings screen and "I kept $33.85 an hour" get treated as the same sentence when they usually are not.

Run one session's numbers now

A worked example, computed from this calculator's own arithmetic: a session pays $220 for 95 miles and 6.5 hours online. The earnings screen would show $33.85 an hour. Pricing the vehicle at this session's own gas price (28 MPG, $3.40/gallon), an 8-cent per-mile maintenance reserve, and $145 a month in insurance and other fixed costs prorated across 1,400 typical monthly miles brings the vehicle cost to $28.98 for this session, leaving $29.39 an hour before tax. A 25% self-employment tax reserve on that profit brings what's actually kept to $22.04 an hour -- $11.81 less than the $33.85 the earnings screen displayed, a 35% gap the screen never shows.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or the app's own earnings screen?

Both, for different numbers. The app's earnings screen is the fastest way to see what a session paid out -- base pay, tips, and promotions, added up and divided by the hours you were online. It is also the authoritative record of what was actually paid, and this calculator does not try to replace it. What the earnings screen has no field for is the vehicle: gas, wear on tires and the engine, and insurance are entirely the driver's own cost, and none of those platforms subtract them before showing a per-hour figure. This calculator takes that same session's gross pay, miles, and hours and prices the car on top of it, plus a self-employment tax reserve, so the number it shows is what actually stayed in the driver's pocket rather than what the app paid out.

Why doesn't the app's earnings screen subtract vehicle cost itself?

Because the platform has no way to know it. Gas price, fuel economy, how a driver's own car wears, and what insurance actually costs are figures no gig platform has access to -- they vary by driver, by car, and by week, and the app only knows what it paid out, not what a driver spent to earn it. A per-hour figure that never subtracts a cost the platform cannot see is not wrong, exactly; it is answering a narrower question than "what did I keep."

Does this calculator track earnings automatically across sessions?

No. It prices one session at a time, from the numbers you type in -- no account, no linked gig-platform login, and no background tracking. That is a real trade: an app that links your accounts and logs mileage automatically saves the typing, at the cost of an account and, on some platforms, a paid tier for the deeper breakdown. This calculator is the other trade -- nothing to sign up for, and an answer as soon as you have one session's numbers to type in.
