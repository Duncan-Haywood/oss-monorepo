This calculator vs. pricing tuition by hand or in a spreadsheet

Dividing a room's monthly cost by the number of children enrolled is one line of arithmetic — anyone can do it by hand or in a spreadsheet, and a correctly built spreadsheet gets that division exactly right. What it usually does not do on its own is stay fixed to the room's licensed ratio and overhead as occupancy changes, and recompute what tuition needs to be, and what an empty seat is actually costing you, at every occupancy level rather than just the one you happened to type in today.

That is the narrower job this calculator does. It takes the same inputs a spreadsheet would need — your child:staff ratio, staff scheduled, room capacity, wages, hours, overhead, and target margin — and holds staffing and overhead fixed while it walks occupancy from 50% to 100%, so the tuition needed and the profit at your current rate are visible at every level, not recalculated by hand each time enrollment changes.

QuestionWhere to answer it
What is the break-even tuition per child at today's enrolled count? Either — monthly cost ÷ enrolled children. A spreadsheet gets this exactly right too.
What does a full or half-empty room need to charge to clear a target margin, without rebuilding the sheet? This calculator's occupancy table — six occupancy levels at once, staffing and overhead held fixed. A spreadsheet has to be rebuilt each time you ask this by hand.
What is an empty seat actually costing me right now, in dollars per month and per year? This calculator solves it directly. By hand, it is easy to read an empty seat as a smaller bill rather than the lost revenue it actually is, since staffing does not shrink with enrollment.
Is the room's capacity set by the ratio or by the room's own licensed square footage? Either can check it, but a spreadsheet has to be told to take the smaller of the two — this calculator always does.
I want to try a different wage, overhead figure, or target margin quickly. Either — a well-built spreadsheet updates instantly too, once it is built correctly.
My situation has a wrinkle this calculator doesn't model (a second age group, a sliding tuition scale, a subsidy). A spreadsheet you build yourself — it can represent any rule you can describe.
Is anything I enter uploaded anywhere? No, either way — a local spreadsheet and this calculator both keep your numbers on your own device.

Most directors get different value from each: a spreadsheet for whatever wrinkle their own center has that a general tool will never model, and a calculator like this one for the standard ratio-and-occupancy question answered instantly, without rebuilding a formula every time a wage or an overhead line changes.

It needs no account and no sign-in: it is a static page, the arithmetic runs in your browser, and once it has loaded it keeps working with the connection off.

Common questions

Can't I just divide my monthly cost by the number of children enrolled?

Yes, and that division is exactly right as far as it goes — monthly cost divided by enrolled children is the break-even tuition per child, and a spreadsheet does that multiplication and division exactly as well as this calculator does. Where a hand calculation or a quick spreadsheet formula usually stops is at a single occupancy snapshot: today's enrolled count, today's tuition. It rarely gets rebuilt to show what the same room needs at a different occupancy rate, which is the question that actually decides whether to chase enrollment or raise the rate.

What does a spreadsheet not check that this calculator does?

Whether staffing cost is fixed to the room's ratio rather than to who shows up. A spreadsheet built to divide cost by headcount has no built-in reminder that two staff scheduled for a 10:1 room cost the same whether 14 or 20 children are enrolled -- so an empty seat is easy to model as a smaller bill instead of what it actually is: revenue that was never billed. This calculator holds staffing and overhead fixed and recomputes enrolled children, required tuition, and profit at six occupancy levels at once, so the empty-seat cost is a number on the page rather than a spreadsheet row nobody added.

Is the occupancy table something I could build myself in a spreadsheet?

With the right formulas, yes -- it is the same fixed-cost, ratio-bound-or-room-bound capacity check repeated at each occupancy percentage, with tuition solved backward from a target margin. Setting that up correctly the first time, and re-deriving it correctly every time a wage, an overhead line, or a target margin changes, is exactly the kind of arithmetic that is easy once and easy to get subtly wrong later -- a stale reference to last year's wage or a margin formula that forgot the overhead line does not announce itself the way a typo in a tuition figure does.

There is nothing to be affiliated with. This page compares against doing the arithmetic by hand or building your own spreadsheet -- a generic method every center director already has access to, not any specific company or product -- so it names no spreadsheet application, childcare management platform, or vendor at all.
