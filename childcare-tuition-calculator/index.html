      Why the number changes

      A classroom's biggest cost -- staff scheduled to meet its licensed ratio -- does not shrink when a seat sits empty. That is what makes occupancy, not the sticker price, the number that actually swings a classroom's margin. This calculator makes the empty-seat cost visible so you can decide whether to hold, market, or reprice a room before the next enrollment cycle.

      Common questions

      Does this tool tell me my state's legal child:staff ratio?

      No. Ratios vary by state and by age group and change over time, so this tool never asserts one — you type in the ratio from your own license. It only does the arithmetic once you supply that number, the same way it never checks your room's licensed square-footage capacity for you.

      Why is the tuition I need lower than what I actually charge?

      Because the required-tuition figure is only large enough to hit your stated target margin, not to match whatever you already charge. At the defaults — a 10:1 ratio, two staff, 85% occupancy, and a 10% target margin — the room only needs $669.02 per child to clear that 10% margin, well under the $900 actually being charged, which is why the current-rate margin comes out to about 33%.

      What does one empty seat actually cost me?

      Not a lower cost — lost revenue. A classroom is staffed to its licensed ratio, not to the exact number of children present on a given day, so payroll and overhead do not fall when a seat sits empty. At the defaults, three empty seats out of a 20-seat room at $900 tuition is $2,700 a month, $32,400 a year, that never appears on any expense line because it left as revenue that was never billed.

      How much does occupancy actually move the tuition I need to charge?

      More than price itself, in most rooms, because staffing cost is fixed once a classroom is opened. At the defaults, a full room only needs $568.67 per child to clear a 10% margin; the same room at half-full needs $1,137.33 per child to clear the same margin on the same fixed cost — and at the $900 rate actually being charged, that half-full room is losing $1,236 a month, not just earning less.

      No. Every figure is calculated in your browser and saved only to this browser's local storage so the form remembers your numbers next visit.
