    Questions people ask

    How much should a professional speaker charge?

    There is no single market rate: fees run from a few hundred dollars for a local talk to five or six figures for well-known keynoters. A defensible starting point is arithmetic: the income you want plus business overhead, divided by the bookings you realistically land in a year, grossed up for any bureau commission. This tool does that, then lets you compare the result with the fee you actually quote.

    What commission do speaker bureaus take?

    Bureaus and agents commonly keep a percentage of the fee, often around 20 to 30 percent, and the client pays the full fee. Because the commission comes out of the fee, your fee has to be grossed up to protect your net. Type your own rate and the share of your bookings that go through a bureau.

    Should I charge for travel separately?

    Many speakers bill travel at cost on top of the fee, which keeps the fee comparable between local and distant events. If you absorb travel instead, it must be recovered inside the fee. Switch the travel setting to see how each choice changes the fee floor and your net.

    How much less should a virtual keynote cost?

    Virtual events remove travel days and cost, but prep and delivery time remain, and the audience gets the same talk. Many speakers discount 25 to 50 percent. The tool shows the net per hour you keep at your chosen discount so you can see where it stops being worth it.

    What add-ons can a speaker charge for?

    Common ones are custom content built for the client, a workshop or breakout session, recording or reuse rights, and a rush fee for short notice. Each is a typed percentage or flat amount here, stacked on the base fee in a printable quote.

    Is this financial advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on figures you type. Fees depend on your reputation, audience and market; the example numbers are placeholders, not market rates.
