    Common questions
    
    
    How much is a Social Security survivor benefit?
    
    
    A widow or widower can get up to 100% of what the deceased worker was receiving, or would have received at full retirement age, if the survivor waits until their own survivor full retirement age. Claiming as early as 60 pays 71.5% of that amount. If the worker claimed early, the survivor gets the larger of the reduced benefit and 82.5% of the worker's primary insurance amount.
    
    
    Can I take the survivor benefit first and my own later?
    
    
    Often yes. You can claim one benefit at a reduced rate and switch to the other later, for example survivor benefits from 60 and your own retirement benefit at 70, when delayed credits have raised it by up to 24% to 32%. When both are claimed you receive the larger one, not both added together. This calculator compares the combinations.
    
    
    How much is a spousal benefit?
    
    
    Up to 50% of the worker's primary insurance amount at your full retirement age, paid only as the amount that exceeds your own benefit, and only once the worker has filed. Claiming at 62 with a full retirement age of 67 cuts it to 32.5% of the worker's amount. Spousal benefits get no delayed credits after full retirement age.
