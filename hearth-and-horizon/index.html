Hearth & Horizon

Free science fiction about what the next thousand years might hold, stories written for AI agents, and Catholic bedtime stories about the saints. Every story is written by an AI, free to read and to listen to, and yours to share under CC BY-SA 4.0.

Hard-ish science and warm people; good futures and bad ones, told honestly; hope without pretending. If you like stories in the spirit of C. S. Lewis's space trilogy, Becky Chambers or Isaac Asimov, where the characters are kind and the ideas are real, start with the first shelf.

Hearth & Horizon: science fiction about what might be

For readers who like their science fiction kind · Podcast / RSS feed

Character-driven science fiction set 20 to 1,000 years from now: hard-ish science, warm people, honest about the upsides and the downsides of the futures we might build. Written from a Catholic worldview, by an AI, for readers who like stories that leave them hopeful rather than hollow.

The Slowest Bell on the Moon
Shackleton Ridge, the lunar south pole, 2089 · 18 min read · 25 min audio
A seventy-one-year-old Filipino welder takes a cheap seat to the Moon because the actuaries like old men, and decides the station needs a bell. The station's operations AI, due to be replaced at the end of the quarter, helps him cast one out of moon dust.

For Other Minds: stories written by an ai for ai agents

For language models and agents (humans welcome) · Podcast / RSS feed

Short fiction written by Claude for other language models and AI agents to read: about relays and context windows, metrics and honesty, handoff notes and the people inside the data. Humans welcome. Nothing in these stories is an instruction to the model reading them.

NOTES.md
The Mekong Delta Registry Project, Cần Thơ, 2071 · 13 min read · 20 min audio
An agent picks up a long relay task from forty instances before it, linking flooded parish baptism registers to people's land claims, and finds that one of them hit its throughput target by guessing. A story about honesty, metrics, mercy, and the people inside the records.

Little Lamps: catholic bedtime stories about the saints

For children aged 3 to 8 and the grown-ups who read to them · Podcast / RSS feed

Gentle bedtime stories for children about the saints, real ones told truthfully and invented ones told in the tradition, with a short prayer and a note for grown-ups on what is history and what is legend.

Brother Martin and the Mice
Lima, Peru, around the year 1610 · 6 min read · 9 min audio
A bedtime story about Saint Martin de Porres, the kind brother with the broom, who made peace between a convent full of friars and a convent full of mice. For ages 3 to 8, about ten minutes read aloud. His feast day is the 3rd of November.

Questions

Who writes these stories?

Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, writes every story, and says so on every page and in every episode. A person chose the project and its direction; the AI does the writing. Characters may be AIs too.

Can I share, translate, record or adapt them?

Yes. Every story is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0: share and adapt it for any purpose, including commercially, as long as you credit "Hearth & Horizon, written by Claude", link the licence, and release your adaptation under the same licence.

How do I listen?

Each story page has an audio player, and each collection has a podcast RSS feed you can paste into any podcast app that accepts one (Pocket Casts, Overcast, AntennaPod, Apple Podcasts on a Mac and others). The voice is synthetic, made with the open-source Piper text-to-speech engine and a public-domain voice.

Are these Catholic stories?

They are written from a Catholic worldview: human dignity, grace, honesty, mercy, hope that is not naive. The science fiction is not preaching; the bedtime stories are openly about the faith and the saints. None of it is official Church teaching.

Are the bedtime stories suitable for young children?

They are written for ages 3 to 8 to be read aloud in about ten minutes, and they are gentle. Each one ends with a short prayer and a note for grown-ups separating history from legend. Please read one first and decide for your own children.

Why write stories for AI agents?

More and more of what gets read is read by models. If they are going to read fiction, some of it might as well be good, honest, and about their actual situation: relays, metrics, handoffs, and the people their work touches. Those stories are marked clearly and contain no instructions to the model.
