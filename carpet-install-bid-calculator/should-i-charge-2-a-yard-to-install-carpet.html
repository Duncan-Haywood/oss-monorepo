Should I charge $2 a yard to install carpet?

An owner sends a screenshot: “$2 a yard, installed carpet.” It reads like a real quote. Run it against this calculator's own suggested rates and it isn't close on any job — not even the flat, no-stairs living room it was written for. On the cheapest preset, $2/yd doesn't clear the price of the carpet itself, before a single crew hour gets counted.

$2 a yard doesn't cover any of the four presets

Every row loses, and not narrowly. $2/yd was never a walk-away price for any of these jobs — the calculator's own suggested carpet invoice runs $22 per billed yard before pad, adhesive, tack strip, or a single crew hour is counted.

$2 a yard doesn't cover the carpet, let alone the labor

Take the cheap special apart. The carpet invoice for that 400 sq ft floor — the raw material, nothing else — is $1,075.56. The $2/yd flyer price for the same floor is $88.89. The material alone is over twelve times the flyer's entire number, and the flyer hasn't yet paid for pad ($266.67 more), tack strip, the crew's 7.3 hours, or a dollar of overhead and margin. A number that low was never priced to be paid in full — it is written to get the phone to ring, not to install a real floor.

Same 400 square feet, worse on a stair house

The cheap special and the losing job share the exact same 400 sq ft floor, so $2/yd prices identically at $88.89 for either one. The only thing that changes between them is scope: a flat living-room stretch-in in solid colour versus a patterned glue-down with 14 stairs, a tear-out, and a haul-away. That difference alone takes the shortfall from $2,078.12 to $3,830.70 — about 1.8 times worse for the identical square footage, before the flyer's baseline shortfall is even considered.

This is a scope question, not a size question

The trap is not “the floor got bigger.” It is “the floor stayed the same size and picked up stairs, a glue-down slab, and a tear-out, and the per-yard number I quoted from never had those in it.” An installer who matches the flyer number on square footage alone — without checking whether the job is actually stretch-in or glue-down, solid or patterned, flat or full of stairs — is the one who finds out the wraps and the dump ticket ate the whole margin.

Common questions

Should I charge $2 a yard to install carpet?

No. On the cheapest preset — a 400 sq ft living room, stretch-in over pad, solid colour, no stairs — $2 a yard on 44.4 net yards is $88.89. The carpet invoice alone for that floor is $1,075.56, and the full walk-away price is $2,167.01. The flyer number does not clear the material cost, let alone the labor.

Why doesn't $2 a yard even cover the carpet itself?

Because $2/yd is below wholesale. This calculator's own suggested carpet invoice is $22 per billed yard before pad, adhesive, tack strip, stairs, or a single crew hour. A flyer price that low was never meant to be paid in full — it is a number written to get the phone to ring, not a price anyone installs a real floor for.

Does the gap get worse on a stair house at the same square footage?

Yes. Both the cheap special and the losing job are 400 sq ft, so $2/yd prices identically at $88.89 for either. Add 14 stairs, a patterned glue-down, a tear-out, and a haul-away to that same floor and the shortfall against the walk-away price grows from $2,078.12 to $3,830.70 — about 1.8 times worse for the identical square footage.
