This calculator vs. working out the 90% gambling loss cap by hand

The rule sounds simple — "you can only deduct 90% of your losses" — and that plain-English version is exactly the part that goes wrong. The 90% applies to winnings, not losses, and getting that backwards changes the answer whenever the two numbers differ.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Applying the 90% to the wrong figure. The cap is 90% of WINNINGS, not 90% of losses — the deduction is the lesser of your actual losses or 90% of your winnings. Taking 90% of a large loss figure and comparing that to winnings gives the wrong answer whenever losses exceed winnings by more than a little.

What's the second most common mistake?

Forgetting the pre-existing rule is still there underneath the new one. Losses have always been capped at winnings, full stop — OBBBA adds a SECOND, tighter cap (90% of winnings) on top of that first one. Only the tighter of the two ever actually binds.

What eligibility mistake gets made most often?

Assuming any deduction is available without itemizing. It is not, and this predates OBBBA entirely — a non-itemizer's full winnings are taxable with zero loss offset, cap or no cap.

What mistake do professional gamblers make most often?

Assuming a trade-or-business structure escapes the cap. It does not — OBBBA permanently folded a professional gambler's wagering-related business expenses into the same 90%-of-winnings limitation a casual gambler's losses face.
