What is a creator media kit, and what belongs in it?

A media kit is a one-page summary a content creator sends to a brand considering a sponsorship — the person deciding whether to book, cover, or playlist them, in the same role a venue booker or blog editor plays for a musician. It exists because that person is usually comparing several creators at once and deciding fast: who is this, what's their reach and audience, have they done this before, and what do they charge.

What belongs in it

  • A short bio — a few sentences to two short paragraphs. Lead with your niche and any concrete, checkable facts (years active, notable features, audience size) before adjectives.
  • One good photo — a clear headshot or on-brand content photo. It does not need a professional shoot to start.
  • Platform reach and engagement — follower count and engagement rate per platform. This is the section a brand's team reads first, and it is why a media kit exists at all.
  • Audience demographics, if you have them — age range, gender split, top location. Platform analytics show all three; brands use them to check fit against their own customer.
  • Past brand partnerships, if any — proves you've done sponsored work before and gives a brand a campaign to look at.
  • A rate card — even a first guess. Skipping it does not avoid the pricing conversation; it just delays it by one more email.

What doesn't belong in it

Your entire content history, a mission statement, or anything a reader has to scroll far to get past. A media kit is a fast decision aid, not a portfolio site — if you want the deeper version, link to your actual channel or site from it, not the other way around.

How it differs from a link-in-bio page

A link-in-bio page is built for a fan clicking through to your latest content and can be as shallow as you like. A media kit is built for one specific reader making one specific business decision, so it leads with exactly what that decision needs: reach, audience fit, proof, and a price.

Frequently asked questions

Who actually reads a media kit?

A brand's marketing or influencer-partnerships team, or an agency screening creators on a brand's behalf — almost always comparing several creators at once, so a scannable one-pager with numbers up front is the point, not a limitation.

How is a media kit different from a link-in-bio page?

A link-in-bio page is built for a fan clicking through to your content; a media kit is built for a brand deciding whether to pay you, so it leads with what that decision needs: reach, engagement, audience fit, and a rate — none of which a link-in-bio page is built to show.

Do I need a large following before making one?

No. A smaller, clearly-stated audience with a real niche and a genuine engagement rate is a normal, complete first media kit — many brands specifically seek out smaller creators for exactly that reason. The generator simply omits any section you leave blank rather than showing a placeholder.
