    Questions people ask

    What hourly rate should a contract pay to match a salaried offer?

    Not salary divided by 2,080. A contractor pays both halves of Social Security and Medicare tax, buys their own health cover, gets no paid vacation or holidays, loses weeks between contracts and replaces the retirement match. For a typical software salary that adds roughly 30% to 45% to the naive hourly figure. The tool shows each gap as its own step.

    Why is self-employment tax not simply 15.3%?

    It applies to 92.35% of net earnings, and the 12.4% Social Security part stops at the annual wage base while the 2.9% Medicare part does not. The tool nets this against the employee half of FICA you stop paying when you leave a W-2 job.

    Does the comparison include income tax?

    No. Income tax depends on state, filing status and deductions, and it is left out of both sides. This slightly favours the contractor, because half of self-employment tax is deductible and the qualified business income deduction may apply. Ask a CPA for the after-tax picture.

    Why count unpaid vacation and gaps between contracts?

    A salaried offer pays you through holidays and time off. A contract pays only for hours worked, so every unpaid week raises the rate you need on the weeks you do bill. Enter zero for any you do not expect.

    What about corp-to-corp or an S corporation?

    The tool models a sole proprietor or single-member LLC taxed as one. An S corporation can lower self-employment tax but adds payroll, reasonable-compensation rules and filing costs, so enter those as business expenses or ask a CPA.

    Is this tax or financial advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on numbers you type in. It does not know your state, your client's terms, worker classification rules or your own benefits. Have an advisor review a real offer before you sign.

    Not decided

    Income tax, state taxes, the qualified business income deduction, S corporation or corp-to-corp structures, whether the role would really be classified as contract work, the client's payment terms, liability insurance, unemployment eligibility, and benefits the tool does not list (tuition, pension, equity upside). The defaults are placeholders, not your offer: replace each with your own numbers and the current year's tax rates.
