This calculator vs. SaaSphalt

Two different shapes: SaaSphalt runs an asphalt contractor's estimating, scheduling, invoicing and books across every crew. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this one lot cost, given its stalls, fire lane, coats, and paint, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

SaaSphalt is estimating, scheduling and invoicing software for asphalt contractors, with a module aimed at line-striping work and an integration with QuickBooks, described as customisable to the way a given contractor works. It is sold as an ongoing account for a business, covering the paving, sealcoating and striping sides of a company rather than one trade in isolation. SaaSphalt's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's plan names or dollar figures, so none is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — asphalt-contractor business software, sold as an ongoing account — are stated, and no dollar amount for SaaSphalt appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what SaaSphalt does. It does not schedule a crew, raise an invoice, sync to your books, or keep a customer record on a server. It takes one lot's standard and ADA stall counts, handicap symbols, fire lane and curb paint footage, crosswalks, stop bars, arrows and signs, the number of coats, paint coverage and gallons, crew hours, loaded wage and payroll burden, mobilisation and travel, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into the per-job price and the walk-away price below which the lot loses money — plus side-by-side comparisons of coats, layout and ADA count, a check of whether a price already on the table clears the floor, and a printable customer proposal for that one job. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or SaaSphalt?

They do different jobs, and a striping business of any size may end up using both. SaaSphalt is estimating, scheduling and invoicing software for asphalt contractors, with a module aimed at line-striping work and an integration with QuickBooks, and it is described as customisable to the way a given contractor works. It is sold as an ongoing account for a company. This calculator does one narrower thing: starting from standard and ADA stall counts, handicap symbols, fire lane and curb paint footage, crosswalks, stop bars, arrows, signs, the number of coats, paint coverage and gallons, crew hours, loaded wage and payroll burden, mobilisation and travel, overhead, and target margin, it turns that into the per-job price and the walk-away price below which the lot loses money. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A contractor already running estimates, schedules, invoices and books across paving, sealcoating and striping crews is better served by SaaSphalt or a comparable asphalt platform; a striper who wants an instant, private gut-check on what one lot should cost before opening a business system is in the right place here.

Can it compare one coat against two before I bid?

Yes, and that comparison is the reason the coat count is its own input rather than folded into a rate. A second coat roughly doubles the paint and adds crew hours without adding a single stall, so the two bids diverge by an amount that depends on the lot's own mix of stalls, fire lane, and curb. The calculator prices both from the same measurements, alongside comparisons of layout and ADA stall count, so the question a property manager asks — what does the better-looking job actually cost me — has a number rather than a guess. Each variant is checked against the same walk-away price, so a discount offered on the two-coat job cannot quietly fall below cost.
