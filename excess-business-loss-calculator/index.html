    Common questions

    What is the excess business loss limitation?

    IRC Section 461(l) limits how much of a noncorporate taxpayer's net business loss can offset OTHER income — wages, interest, dividends — in the current year. It was scheduled to expire after 2028; the One Big Beautiful Bill Act made it permanent starting with the 2025 tax year.

    What happens to the loss above the threshold?

    It is not lost — the excess is added to your net operating loss (NOL) carryforward to next year, where it can offset up to 80% of that year's taxable income under current law, with no carryback for most businesses.

    Why is the 2026 threshold LOWER than 2025's?

    OBBBA rebased how this threshold is indexed for inflation, resetting it closer to its original 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act base instead of continuing to compound. The result is counterintuitive: $313,000 single/HOH/MFS in 2025 falls to $256,000 in 2026; $626,000 MFJ falls to $512,000. Most OBBBA changes raise a threshold — this one is a rare exception.

    Does married filing jointly get double the single threshold?

    Yes, exactly double. Single, head-of-household, and married-filing-separately filers all share the same threshold; married filing jointly gets precisely twice that figure, not a separate number of its own.

    What counts as the net business loss?

    Aggregate income or gain from all your trades or businesses, minus aggregate deductions attributable to those same trades or businesses. Wages, interest, dividends, and capital gains outside a business are not part of this figure — they are what the ALLOWED loss then offsets.
