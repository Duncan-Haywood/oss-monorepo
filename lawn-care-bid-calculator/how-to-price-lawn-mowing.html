How to price lawn mowing without a per-acre guess

Operators search for “how to price lawn mowing” because the numbers they find — $40 a half-acre, $50 a front yard — cannot see the stop they are actually running. Two lots of the same size are not the same job.

Split the stop into services

Open turf mows fast. Beds, hedges, and a careful edge do not. Time each service from your own crew: square feet of turf per hour, minutes of edging and blowing, square feet of beds. Hours on the property are those quantities divided by those rates. A 10,000 sq ft lawn at 20,000 sq ft per hour is a 30-minute mow. The same lot with 350 sq ft of beds at 400 sq ft per hour is another 50 minutes before anyone starts the truck.

Count windshield time as labor

Round-trip drive time is paid time. On a suburban route an 18-minute one-way hop is 36 minutes on every visit — often more than the mow. A price-per-acre rule never adds it. Fuel and dump fees sit on top of the same visit.

Do not invent winter

In-season monthly is weekly visits times 52/12 weeks, not a flat four. Annual is the per-visit price times the growing-season weeks you actually bill — commonly the high 20s to mid 30s in temperate US climates, not twelve months. Quoting monthly × 12 either overcharges a homeowner who wants a year-round number or undercharges you if you skip winter.
