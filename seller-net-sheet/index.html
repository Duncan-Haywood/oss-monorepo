    Common questions

    How do I calculate how much I will net from selling my house?

    Start with the offer price. Subtract the loan payoff, the listing broker fee, any buyer-broker compensation the seller agrees to pay, other concessions, title and escrow, transfer tax, prorations, and remaining closing costs. What remains is an estimate of cash to the seller. This page does that arithmetic from the numbers you enter; it is not a closing disclosure.

    Is the higher offer always more money for the seller after NAR?

    No. Buyer-broker compensation is now a negotiated amount, not an automatic split of a 5% or 6% commission. A higher offer that asks the seller to pay the buyer’s broker and closing costs can leave less cash than a cleaner, lower offer. Compare both on a net sheet before deciding.

    What sale price do I need to walk away with a target amount?

    Enter the net you want, the loan payoff, and the same cost rates. Percentage fees scale with price; flat fees and the payoff do not. The calculator solves for the sale price that produces that net on those assumptions. Confirm the figure with title or escrow before listing or countering.

    How do I estimate the Tax / HOA proration figure above?

    Take the annual property tax bill and divide by 365 for a daily rate, then multiply by the number of days in the tax period the seller is responsible for through closing. A $3,650 annual bill is $10/day; 80 days charged to the seller comes to $800 — which is why that field defaults to $800 above. The Estimate it box under the field does this arithmetic for you: enter the annual tax and the days charged, then Use this estimate fills the proration field for you. Whether the seller owes days before or after closing depends on whether your local jurisdiction bills taxes in advance or in arrears, so use the title company's proration once you have it; this is a starting estimate, not the number on the closing statement.
