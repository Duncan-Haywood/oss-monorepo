Czech Republic e-invoicing: required for public bodies, not yet for B2B

Czech public contracting authorities may not refuse an e-invoice that follows the European standard, but no law yet makes businesses issue e-invoices to each other. Below: the dates from the procurement act itself, what advisory sources report about plans, and how to test a UBL or CII file before it goes out.

The timeline (the procurement act read directly; the rest from advisory sources)

DateWhat changes
1 Oct 2016Act No. 134/2016 on public procurement takes effect, but its e-invoice section, § 221, does not yet: advisory guides that date B2G e-invoicing to this day are reading the Act's start date.
17 Oct 2017The reference to the European e-invoice standard (EN 16931-1:2017) is published in the EU Official Journal (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2017/1870, OJ L 266). § 279(5) counts from this day.
1 Apr 2019§ 221 applies to the state (§ 4(1)(a)) and the Czech National Bank: a contracting authority may not refuse an e-invoice for a public contract because of its format if the format follows the European standard (first day of the 18th month after publication, by our count).
1 Apr 2020§ 221 applies to every other contracting authority (first day of the 30th month, by our count).
Mid-2026A VAT Update country booklet with a 2 July 2026 cut-off says no domestic mandatory B2B e-invoicing or digital-reporting mandate had been enacted.
PlannedAn undated third-party summary says the Ministry of Finance plans to receive e-invoices first and require issuing in B2B later, in 2026 to 2027. No enacted date found.
Jun to Sep 2026A tracker (lookuptax) says the first ViDA VAT bill (tisk 218) went to the Chamber of Deputies on 5 June 2026, passed first reading on 14 July and got a Budget Committee approval recommendation on 17 September, for 1 January 2027. It says the bill holds no domestic e-invoicing rule. Not checked against the Chamber's own tracker.

§ 221 and § 279 were read in Czech on zakonyprolidi.cz (consolidated text of Act No. 134/2016 Coll.) on 2026-11-14. § 221 is a duty to accept, not to issue: it sets no format of its own, names no network and no penalty. The April dates are our count of "the first day of the eighteenth (thirtieth) month after publication"; they line up with the EU directive's deadlines of 18 April 2019 and 18 April 2020. The rows from mid-2026 on are advisory sources, not the Ministry of Finance; the Chamber of Deputies' tracker (psp.cz) refused the connection from here, so tisk 218 is unchecked. Which file, and what to do now

Czech businesses commonly use ISDOC, a national XML format, while buyers abroad and public bodies reached over Peppol use EN 16931 UBL or CII. Because B2B issuing is not yet mandatory, an exporter to Germany, France or Poland usually meets an e-invoice rule on the buyer's side first. Fixing arithmetic, tax reasons and identifiers now saves rework when a domestic rule lands.

Common questions

Is B2B e-invoicing mandatory in the Czech Republic?

Not as of the latest source found, a July 2026 advisory booklet that reports no enacted domestic B2B mandate. Public contracting authorities may not refuse an e-invoice that follows the European standard (Act No. 134/2016, § 221): by the Act's own timing rule (§ 279(5)), counted from the standard's publication in the Official Journal on 17 October 2017, that applies from 1 April 2019 for the state and the Czech National Bank and from 1 April 2020 for other contracting authorities. The Act took effect on 1 October 2016, but not § 221. The Ministry of Finance is reported to plan a B2B rollout in 2026 to 2027, but no enacted date was found, so confirm with the Ministry of Finance. This is a summary, not advice.

Is EET 2.0 an e-invoicing mandate?

No. It is a planned sales-reporting reform reported for 1 January 2027, a different obligation from exchanging structured invoices between businesses.

Which invoice format do Czech businesses use?

ISDOC is the common national XML format, and EN 16931 UBL or CII is used over Peppol. This checker reads UBL and CII and applies EN 16931 and optional Peppol rules. It does not read ISDOC or apply Czech national rules.

How do I check an invoice before sending it to a Czech or foreign buyer?

Drop the UBL or CII file into the checker. It tests totals and tax breakdown arithmetic, missing tax reasons on zero or exempt lines, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page. It is an offline observation, not an authority's acceptance and not legal advice.
