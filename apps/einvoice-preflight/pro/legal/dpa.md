# Data Processing Agreement

**Version 2026-11-26** · Maris Stella Labs LLC

This Data Processing Agreement (the "**DPA**") forms part of the Terms of Service (the "**Agreement**") between **Maris Stella Labs LLC**, a Colorado limited liability company ("**Provider**"), and the firm or company that holds an account for the e-invoice workspace and API (the "**Service**") ("**Customer**"). It applies automatically, without a signature, whenever Provider processes Customer Personal Data on Customer's behalf and that processing is subject to the EU GDPR, the UK GDPR or the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection. A Customer that wants a copy signed by both parties can ask at hello@marisstella.app; the signed copy has the same terms.

## 1. Definitions and roles

1.1 "**Data Protection Law**" means Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the "**GDPR**"), the GDPR as it applies in the United Kingdom with the Data Protection Act 2018 (the "**UK GDPR**"), the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, and any other data protection law that applies to the processing. "Controller", "processor", "personal data", "processing", "data subject", "personal data breach" and "supervisory authority" have the meanings Data Protection Law gives them.

1.2 "**Customer Personal Data**" means the personal data Provider processes for Customer in providing the Service, as described in Annex I.B. It includes personal data in the invoices Customer sends to be checked and in the client records Customer keeps in the workspace.

1.3 For Customer Personal Data, Customer is the controller, or a processor acting for its own clients (for example an accounting firm checking its clients' invoices), and Provider is a processor, or a sub-processor. Where Customer acts for its clients, Customer confirms that its instructions, including its use of Provider, are authorized by those clients.

1.4 Provider is a controller, not a processor, for the account, sign-in, billing and usage data it needs to run and bill the Service, as the Privacy Policy describes. This DPA does not cover that data.

## 2. Instructions

2.1 Provider processes Customer Personal Data only on Customer's documented instructions, including for transfers to a third country, unless Union or Member State law to which Provider is subject requires otherwise; in that case Provider tells Customer first, unless that law prohibits it.

2.2 The Agreement, this DPA and Customer's use of the Service (the invoices it sends, the language and rule set it chooses, the fixes it asks for, the client records it creates and deletes) are Customer's complete instructions. Provider will tell Customer promptly if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes Data Protection Law.

2.3 Provider does not sell Customer Personal Data, use it for advertising, use it to train AI models, or combine it with other customers' data.

## 3. Provider's obligations (GDPR Article 28(3))

Provider shall:

(a) ensure that everyone it authorizes to process Customer Personal Data is bound by confidentiality;

(b) implement the technical and organizational measures in Annex II, which Provider may update as long as the overall level of protection does not fall;

(c) engage sub-processors only as set out in section 4;

(d) assist Customer, taking into account the nature of the processing and by the measures in Annex II, in responding to requests from data subjects exercising their rights (section 6);

(e) assist Customer with security, notification of personal data breaches, data protection impact assessments and prior consultation of a supervisory authority (GDPR Articles 32 to 36), taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to Provider;

(f) when the Agreement ends, delete Customer Personal Data as set out in section 7;

(g) make available to Customer the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 28 GDPR and this DPA, and allow for and contribute to audits as set out in section 8.

## 4. Sub-processors

4.1 Customer gives Provider general written authorization to engage the sub-processors listed in Annex III.

4.2 Provider will tell Customer of any intended addition or replacement of a sub-processor at least **30 days** in advance, by updating Annex III and the Privacy Policy and by a notice in the workspace (and by email to the account's confirmed sign-in address, if it has one). Customer may object on reasonable data protection grounds within that period. The parties will discuss the objection in good faith; if they cannot resolve it, Customer may cancel the Service and receive a pro-rata refund of fees prepaid for the period after cancellation.

4.3 Provider imposes on each sub-processor, by contract, data protection obligations that offer the same level of protection as this DPA, and remains liable to Customer for each sub-processor's performance of those obligations.

## 5. Personal data breaches

5.1 Provider will notify Customer of a personal data breach affecting Customer Personal Data **without undue delay** after becoming aware of it, through the workspace and by email to the account's confirmed sign-in address if it has one, or otherwise through the contact Customer gives.

5.2 The notice will say, as far as Provider then knows: what happened; the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned; the likely consequences; the measures taken or proposed to address the breach and limit its effects; and a contact for more information. Provider supplements the notice as it learns more.

5.3 A notice is not an acknowledgment of fault or liability.

## 6. Requests from data subjects and authorities

6.1 If a data subject contacts Provider about Customer Personal Data, Provider will refer them to Customer, unless the law prohibits it, and will not respond on the merits without Customer's instruction. Customer can delete client records and their history in the workspace at any time.

6.2 If a public authority asks Provider to disclose Customer Personal Data, Provider will tell Customer, unless the law prohibits it, examine whether the request is lawful, challenge it where there are reasonable grounds, and disclose only the minimum the request lawfully requires.

## 7. Return and deletion

7.1 Invoice contents are not stored: each invoice is read in memory to produce the result, and the corrected file and diff are returned to Customer, not kept. There is therefore no invoice to return or delete.

7.2 During the Agreement, Customer can delete each client record and its history in the workspace. When Customer asks Provider to close its account, or within **30 days** after the Agreement ends, Provider deletes the remaining Customer Personal Data (client records and check history) from the Service's database. Earlier states of the database kept in its point-in-time history (Annex II, "Availability and backups") expire within 30 days and are not restored except to recover from an incident. Before the deletion, Customer may ask for an export of its client records and history.

7.3 Provider may keep Customer Personal Data longer only where Union or Member State law requires it, and then keeps it confidential and processes it only for that purpose.

## 8. Information and audits

8.1 Provider holds no third-party security attestation or audit report. On request, no more than once a year or after a personal data breach, Provider will answer a reasonable written security questionnaire and provide its current security documentation (this DPA's Annexes, its policies and its list of sub-processors).

8.2 If that information is not enough to demonstrate compliance, Customer, or an independent auditor it appoints who is bound by confidentiality, may audit Provider's compliance with this DPA, at Customer's cost, with at least 30 days' written notice, during business hours, without disrupting the Service and without access to other customers' data. A supervisory authority may audit as the law allows. Where the Standard Contractual Clauses apply, this section is how Provider meets their audit clause and does not limit it.

## 9. Transfers outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

9.1 Provider is established in the United States. To the extent Customer transfers Customer Personal Data subject to the GDPR to Provider, the standard contractual clauses annexed to Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 of 4 June 2021 (the "**SCCs**") are incorporated into this DPA by reference, with Customer as data exporter and Provider as data importer, completed as follows:

| SCC item | Completion |

| Docking clause (Clause 7) | Not used |

| Redress (Clause 11) | The optional wording is not used |

9.2 For transfers subject to the UK GDPR, the International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Commission Standard Contractual Clauses issued by the UK Information Commissioner applies, completed with the information in this DPA's Annexes; either party may end it as the Addendum allows when it is revised. For transfers subject to Swiss law, the SCCs apply with these changes: the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner is the competent supervisory authority for those transfers, references to the GDPR include the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, and data subjects in Switzerland may bring claims there.

9.3 Provider does not rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework for these transfers.

9.4 If the SCCs conflict with this DPA or the Agreement, the SCCs prevail.

## 10. General

10.1 This DPA lasts as long as Provider processes Customer Personal Data.

10.2 If this DPA conflicts with the Agreement on the processing of Customer Personal Data, this DPA prevails.

10.3 Each party's liability under this DPA is subject to the limits in the Agreement, except where the SCCs or Data Protection Law do not allow a limit.

10.4 Provider may update this DPA with a new version date, for example when a sub-processor changes or the law requires it. A change that lowers the protection of Customer Personal Data needs Customer's agreement; other changes are announced in the workspace in advance.

## Annex II: Technical and organizational measures

Each measure is what the Service's code does today, except where marked "not in place yet".

| Measure | What the Service does |
| --- | --- |
| Data minimization | Invoices are read in memory and never written to the database, logs or disk; the history keeps file names, hashes and rule ids, not contents. Usage is counted by the SHA-256 hash of each invoice |
| Encryption in transit | Served only over HTTPS, terminated by Cloudflare |
| Encryption at rest | The database is Cloudflare D1, which encrypts everything it stores, the live database and its history included, with AES-256 (GCM mode), under keys Cloudflare manages (developers.cloudflare.com/d1/reference/data-security) |
| Access control for customers | Every request is authenticated with an API key or a one-time sign-in link, and every database read and write is limited to the account that owns the data. API keys and sign-in links are stored only as SHA-256 hashes |
| Access control for the operator | The operator console shows account names, plans, sign-up dates, subscription status and usage counts, not client records, history or sign-in emails; each view is logged |
| Abuse limits | Per-address and daily caps on sign-up and sign-in-link requests, held in memory only; plan limits checked atomically in the database |
| Logging | Error logs record a short error message, never an API key or an invoice |
| Use of an AI model | Most findings have a built-in explanation and nothing is sent. For a finding without one, the rule id, the finding's message (which can quote one value from the invoice), the proposed fix text and the language are sent to Anthropic, PBC; never the invoice file, the client or the account. Explanations never change a result |
| Corrections | A fix is applied only from a fixed list of formal corrections, only when the corrected file passes the targeted rule without a new finding; totals, identifiers and missing business data are never changed |
| Availability and backups | Cloudflare D1 Time Travel, always on, lets the database be restored to any minute of the last 30 days (developers.cloudflare.com/d1/reference/time-travel); no other copy of the database is made |
| Deletion | Deleting a client in the workspace deletes its history; account closure as in section 7 |
| Development | Changes go through pull requests with automated tests, including tests that one account cannot read another's data |
| Assistance to Customer | Self-service deletion of clients and history; questionnaire answers and this DPA's Annexes on request |
