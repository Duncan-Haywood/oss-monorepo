How much should I charge for flooring installation?

A blended dollar-per-square-foot number is the flooring version of a postcard special: it prices the finished floor and ignores the two things that actually move the job — how much material waste the pattern creates, and how much of the room is tear-out instead of install. A straight-lay LVP room and a diagonal-pattern room the same size are not the same material order. A room with ceramic tile to demo first is not the same labor hours as one that's already down to subfloor.

What a blended $/sq ft price cannot see

Waste. Straight-lay LVP commonly starts around 8% waste; diagonal or herringbone often needs 12% or more. That is material you buy and pay for, not extra floor the customer sees — and a quote that ignores it is quietly eating margin on every box.

Tear-out. Demoing ceramic tile can take more hours than laying new LVP in the same room. A quote that treats every room as "install only" underbids the ones that aren't.

Stairs. Stairs are priced per tread, not as hallway square footage folded into the total. Each tread is its own cut, its own nosing, its own labor.

A bid you can defend

  1. Price purchased material per room after your waste percent, not the finished square footage.
  2. Add demo hours for rooms that need tear-out, separately from install hours.
  3. Price stairs per tread.
  4. Load the wage (payroll burden), then add overhead, and price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin).

The calculator does that arithmetic in your browser. It is not a quote you can send as-is — it is a draft to check against your own invoices, payroll, and a walk-through before you send a price.

Worked example

A great room in straight-lay LVP at 8% waste and a stairway with a diagonal accent room at 12% waste are not one blended square-foot number — they are two different material orders. Add a kitchen where the existing tile has to come out first, and the labor hours split three ways before a single plank goes down. The calculator prices each of those separately, then rolls them into one job price and one price per installed square foot so you can see what the blended number was hiding.

Common questions

How much waste should I add to an LVP flooring bid?

Straight-lay LVP commonly starts around 8% waste; diagonal or herringbone often needs 12% or more. Waste is extra material you buy, not extra floor the customer sees. This calculator applies your waste percent to purchased square footage and underlayment, then shows the dollars that waste actually costs.

Why does a price per square foot underbid a kitchen or stairs?

Tear-out is a different job from the install. Ceramic tile demo can take more hours than laying new LVP in the same room. Stairs are priced per tread, not as hallway square footage. A blended $/sq ft on the finished floor cannot see either one.
