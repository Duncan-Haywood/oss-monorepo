    What this does not cover

    • Income tax. Everything is before income tax; take-home means the pay you want after the costs listed.
    • Equity and bonuses from the old job. Enter the cash you want to replace; equity upside is not valued.
    • Slow or missed payment and gaps between engagements beyond the sold share you enter.
    • Market rates. The rate is what your own numbers need, not what a client will pay. Check it against what comparable people charge.
    • Contracts. Scope, termination and liability belong to a written agreement reviewed by a lawyer.

    Common questions

    How do I price a fractional executive engagement?

    Work out the revenue that replaces the full-time package: take-home pay, the benefits you now buy yourself and your business costs, grossed up for the payroll tax you now pay. Divide by the days you will actually sell in a year, not the days in the year. That is the day rate; a retainer is some number of days a month at that rate.

    How much should a fractional CFO charge per day?

    There is no single number. A useful check is the multiple of an employee's daily pay: fractional work carries unsold days, admin, benefits and tax the employer used to carry, so a day rate under about 1.5 times a full-time employee's daily pay usually fails to cover them. The calculator shows your multiple.

    What share of my time is billable?

    Most fractional executives lose a fifth or more of their working days to admin, proposals and finding the next client, plus time off. Enter your own figure; the calculator charges the cost of those days to the days you do sell.

    Should I discount a monthly retainer?

    A discount for committed months can be worth it if it replaces gaps between clients, but it lowers your effective day rate. Enter a discount and the calculator shows the effective rate and what the client mix then earns.
