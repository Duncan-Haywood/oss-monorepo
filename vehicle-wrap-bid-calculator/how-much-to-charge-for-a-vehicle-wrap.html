How much to charge for a vehicle wrap

A wrap coverage chart or a customer's own tape measure gives you the vehicle's bare surface area — say, 250 square feet for a mid-size sedan full wrap. That number is real, and it is also not what you actually buy. Vinyl film comes off a 60″ roll, and cutting around mirrors, door handles, pillars, window trim, and every compound curve wastes material that a flat "surface area × cost per square foot" quote never sees. A shop that quotes at the bare area is quoting the vehicle, not the roll — and paying the difference out of its own margin on every job.

Worked example

Take a typical sedan full wrap: 250 sq ft of vehicle, a 20% waste factor (mirrors, handles, curves), cast vinyl at $2.75/sq ft, laminate at $0.75/sq ft, a 12 sq ft/hour flat-panel install rate with a 1.25× complexity multiplier for the curves, a $35/hr installer, a $150 design fee, 12% overhead, and a 35% target margin.

BasisMaterial square footagePrice to quote
Bare surface area (waste ignored)250 sq ft$3,336.67
Waste-inclusive (what the roll actually gives up)300 sq ft$3,638.21

The gap is $196.00 per job — the calculator reports this as the waste-blind loss. Quote at the bare-area price and you are $196.00 short of what covering this specific vehicle actually costs, before you have even opened the roll.

Why the complexity multiplier matters too

A flat panel — a box truck side, a straight fleet van door — installs close to your shop's baseline square-foot-per-hour rate. A sedan or SUV with mirrors, handles, and compound curves does not: the multiplier keeps that difference visible in the labor line instead of quietly averaging it into a single "installed rate" that overprices easy vehicles and underprices hard ones.
