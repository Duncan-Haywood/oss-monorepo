This calculator vs. Oblyss

Both end with a price you can hand a customer, built on opposite assumptions about what you are willing to install and pay for.

What Oblyss is

Oblyss is a paid CRM and estimating platform built specifically for foundation repair and waterproofing companies: pre-loaded product categories for structural repair, waterproofing, crawl space, and drainage work, plus scheduling, warranty tracking, and job costing across every job the company runs, on an ongoing subscription. It is built to run day-to-day operations for a company doing many jobs, not to price a single basement once.

Getting there means creating an account and paying an ongoing subscription priced by users and features.

What this calculator is

This page does not schedule anything and keeps no account. You open it in a browser, type wall length and height, window wells, crack injection, whether the sump is new or existing, backup pump, and discharge line, and it shows a price for that one job. Close the tab and it is gone unless you save a share link or print a proposal — there is no CRM, no warranty tracker, and no subscription.

QuestionWhere to answer it
I want CRM, scheduling, and warranty tracking for every job my waterproofing company runs. Oblyss or a comparable paid platform — ongoing CRM and warranty tracking is exactly its job, and this calculator keeps no job history.
I have one basement to price right now and want a number before the bid goes out. This calculator — type the wall, drain, and sump details, get a price, no account.
I do not want to create an account or start a subscription to price a single job. This calculator — nothing to sign up for.
I want the tool to track warranty claims and remember every job I have bid over time. Oblyss or a comparable paid platform — this calculator has no job list and no memory between visits beyond what your own browser keeps.

Neither tool is the wrong choice in general. A waterproofing company running CRM, scheduling, and warranty claims across many jobs a season is the case a paid platform is built for. A single basement, priced once before the bid goes out, is the case this page is built for.
