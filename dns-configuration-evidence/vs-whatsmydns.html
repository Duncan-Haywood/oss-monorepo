This tool vs. whatsmydns.net

whatsmydns.net shows one worldwide snapshot right now: what a network of resolvers in many countries currently answer for a domain. This tool captures two dated snapshots — before your change and after it — and reports what moved, what did not, and what could not be observed at all.

What each tool is for

whatsmydns.net is a free, no-account web tool built for one question: has a DNS change reached different parts of the world yet? Enter a domain and a record type, and it queries a network of DNS resolvers located in many different countries at once, showing what each currently answers. That single live view is exactly what it is for, and it does it well. It keeps no history between visits — there is nothing to compare a later look against, and nothing you can hand someone as a dated record of what a resolver answered an hour ago.

This tool does the other half. It captures what named resolvers answer for a domain now, lets you make your change, captures again afterward, and diffs the two captures into a report that keeps changed, unchanged, and inconclusive separate — a timeout or a refused query is never folded into "no change". That report is a file you keep or hand to a customer, source-linked to the exact resolvers and timestamps behind it. It runs free in your browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type; a matching Python CLI lets you name a specific resolver, such as a customer's internal one, that a browser cannot reach.

Common questions

Should I use this tool or whatsmydns.net?

They answer different questions, and a DNS cutover usually needs both. whatsmydns.net is a free, no-account web tool: enter a domain and a record type, and it queries a network of DNS resolvers located in many different countries at once, showing what each one currently answers right now. It is built for one question — has a change reached different parts of the world yet — and it answers that well as a single live snapshot. It keeps no history between visits and produces nothing you can save as a dated record: refresh the page tomorrow and today's view is gone. This tool does the opposite half of the job. It captures what named resolvers answer now, lets you make your change, captures again afterward, and diffs the two into a report that states changed, unchanged, and inconclusive separately rather than folding a timeout into 'no change'. That report is a file you keep or hand to a customer, not a page you have to remember to screenshot. It runs free in your browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A cutover that needs to know how far a change has spread geographically is a job for whatsmydns.net or a comparable global checker; a cutover that needs a dated, source-linked record of what actually moved is a job for this tool.

Does this tool check the same worldwide network of locations as whatsmydns.net?

No, and it is not trying to. The browser surface here queries three named public resolvers — Cloudflare, Google, and Quad9 — over DNS-over-HTTPS, and the command-line version can query any resolver you name yourself, such as a customer's internal one. Neither is a stand-in for a network of resolvers spread across many countries, which is what a global propagation checker like whatsmydns.net is built for. If what you need to know is whether a change has reached many different regions of the world, use a global checker for that view. If what you need is a comparable, dated answer from a specific handful of resolvers — before the change and after it — that is what this tool records.
