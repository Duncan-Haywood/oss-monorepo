This calculator vs. a flat day rate and a per-pound hazmat estimate

A common way to price a biohazard or trauma-scene job by hand: a flat day rate for the crew, plus a per-pound hazmat fee tacked on for the waste. It's fast, it needs no tool, and it gets the rough size of the job right.

It gets two real costs wrong, and both of them move the price by a meaningful amount. PPE doesn't cost one set per technician — it costs one set per time a technician re-enters the contaminated area, and a job with a break or a shift change burns through more sets than headcount alone predicts. And regulated-waste disposal isn't billed by the pound — it's billed by the container, and a container that's mostly empty still costs the full per-container charge. This page prices both directly instead of estimating around them.

Common questions

Is a flat day rate wrong?

Not as a starting point — it is a reasonable way to price the crew's time for a job of roughly known size. It just doesn't carry the two costs that swing a biohazard job the most: how many fresh PPE sets the crew actually burns through, and what the regulated-waste hauler's invoice actually charges once the waste is picked up. A day rate answers roughly how long a job will take, not what it will cost.

Why is pounds times a per-pound hazmat rate wrong?

Because that is not how a licensed regulated-waste hauler actually bills. Haulers charge per container picked up, not per pound, and a container that is 10% full costs the same as one that is completely full, up to its rated capacity. A 55-pound job with a 40-pound container still needs two containers, not 1.375 — and a per-pound estimate that assumes a fractional container undercounts the real invoice every time the waste doesn't divide evenly.

Why does headcount undercount PPE?

Because PPE is not a one-time cost per technician on the job — it is a cost per time a technician enters the contaminated area. A technician who leaves for a break, to run equipment out, or across a shift change must doff the suit they were wearing (used PPE cannot be re-donned) and don a fresh set to go back in. A crew of two who each re-enter twice needs four sets, not two.
