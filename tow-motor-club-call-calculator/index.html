    The model, and what it leaves out

    • Cost per billable hour is (payment + insurance + overhead) divided by billable hours per month, plus the driver wage with burden. Fuel (price over mpg) and maintenance are per mile.
    • Truck-hours are (deadhead + loaded + return miles) over your average speed, plus on-scene minutes, stretched by any weather slowdown. Your time back to the yard counts only if you type return miles.
    • Payout is the flat amount plus the per-mile rate for loaded miles beyond the included miles. Kept is payout times (1 minus fee, chargebacks and the carrying cost of waiting for pay) minus any fixed fee.
    • Profit is kept minus job cost. Break-even payout is the payout at which profit is zero. Minimum acceptable payout is the larger of the payout that meets your margin threshold and the payout that matches what a cash call would earn over the same truck-hours.
    • Decline means a loss, a margin under your threshold, or profit per truck-hour under your cash-call figure. The thresholds are yours; none is a recommendation.
    • Reconciles. Break-even payout gives zero profit exactly, and the batch numbers equal the single-call numbers for the same inputs.
    • Not modelled: state or municipal rate rules, storage and lien fees, tolls, equipment wear beyond your per-mile figure, tax, whether a club penalises declines or drops your rating, and the chance a cash call actually turns up.

    Common questions

    Should I accept a motor club dispatch or wait for a cash call?

    Compare what the dispatch leaves you per truck-hour, after the club's deductions and the cost of running the truck, with what you expect a cash call to leave you per truck-hour. This calculator does that arithmetic on numbers you type: the cost of the job, profit, profit per truck-hour, the break-even payout and the minimum payout that meets both your margin threshold and your cash-call alternative.

    What is my cost per hour for a tow truck?

    Add the truck payment, insurance and overhead you pay per month, divide by the hours the truck is actually billable, and add the loaded driver wage. Fuel and maintenance are counted per mile. The helper on this page does exactly that with your own figures; it assumes nothing about your costs.

    Why does deadhead matter so much?

    Deadhead miles and wait time are truck-hours and fuel you spend before and after the paid miles. A payout that covers a short tow can lose money once a long drive to the pickup and an hour on scene are counted. The sensitivity table shows profit for each combination of deadhead miles and wait minutes.

    How do I compare several calls at once?

    Paste one call per line as label, deadhead miles, loaded miles, wait minutes, total payout and an optional after-hours flag. The batch mode uses the truck and club settings above and ranks the calls by profit per truck-hour.

    Does this tell me what rates I may charge?

    No. Towing and roadside rates, storage and lien rules and motor-club contract terms differ by state, municipality and contract. This tool is arithmetic on numbers you type. It is not legal, rate-setting or financial advice, and it does not know any club's actual terms.
