When to follow up on a job application

Most applications get no reply, and it is hard to tell silence from "still reviewing". A simple schedule removes the guesswork: decide the gaps once, write the dates down, and stop re-checking your inbox. These are common conventions, not rules, and no employer is bound by them.

A default schedule you can change
Where you areFollow up afterWhat to send
Applied, no replyabout 7 days (or after the posting's stated review date)Two or three lines to the recruiter or named contact restating interest in the role.
Phone screen done1 day, then 3 days if a next step was promisedA thank-you, then a short check-in on timing.
Interviewed1 day, then a few days past their stated decision dateA thank-you referencing one specific thing discussed, then a polite timing question.
Applied, 30 days silentClose it outMark it ghosted, keep the contact, put the effort elsewhere.

Why track it at all

Once you have more than a handful of applications open, the failure mode is not a bad follow-up; it is no follow-up because nobody remembered the date. A dated list also shows which sources actually answer, which is worth knowing before you spend another week on the one that never does.

Keep it short and optional

A follow-up should be easy to answer in one line and should never pressure. If a company has told you not to contact them, respect that.
