  • Self-employed vehicle costs only. This is for a sole proprietor or single-member LLC deducting car expenses on Schedule C. It does not model an employee's unreimbursed expenses.

Common questions

What is the 2026 IRS standard mileage rate for business?

It is 72.5 cents per mile for business miles driven January 1 through June 30, 2026, and 76 cents per mile for business miles driven July 1 through December 31, 2026 (IRS Notice 2026-10, raised mid-year by Announcement 2026-11). Each mile is deducted at the rate for the half of the year it was driven in.

Is it better to take the standard mileage rate or actual expenses?

Whichever gives the larger deduction, if you qualify for both. Actual expenses win for a car that costs a lot to run per business mile (high depreciation, fuel, insurance and repairs), and the standard rate wins for a cheap-to-run car driven a lot for business. Compare the two using your own numbers before you choose.

Can I deduct parking and tolls with the standard mileage rate?

Yes. Business parking fees and tolls are deductible in addition to the standard mileage rate, and they are also deductible under the actual-expense method, so they add the same amount to either method.

Can I switch between the standard mileage rate and actual expenses?

For a car you own, you must choose the standard mileage rate in the first year the car is available for business use. You can switch from the standard rate to actual expenses in a later year, but if you start with actual expenses on a car you cannot use the standard rate for that car afterwards.

When can I not use the standard mileage rate?

You cannot use it if you operate five or more cars at the same time, or if you have claimed accelerated (MACRS), Section 179 or special depreciation on the car, or if you started with actual expenses on a car you own.
