The model, and what it leaves out

  • Client estimate = cleaning + anesthesia hours × hourly charge + bloodwork + IV + radiographs + (blocks + extraction fees per tooth) + medications.
  • Anesthesia time = base minutes + added minutes per simple and per surgical extraction.
  • Clinic cost = supplies + extraction supplies + technician, veterinarian and overhead cost for the hours worked.
  • Price for target margin = cost ÷ (1 − target margin).
  • Not modelled: specialist referral, dental recheck visits, complications, discounts, client financing and payment fees, and taxes. Grade definitions and extraction counts are yours to set from your own records.

Common questions

How much should a dog dental cleaning cost?

It depends on the grade of disease found once the patient is under anesthesia. A grade 1 mouth needs scaling and polishing; a grade 4 mouth can need ten extractions, nerve blocks and an hour or more of added anesthesia. This calculator itemises each grade so the estimate you give reflects the work.

Why does a flat dental quote lose money?

A single price has to be set for the mix of cases you see. If it matches grade 1 or 2 cases it under-recovers on grade 3 and 4 mouths, where extractions, blocks, supplies and extra anesthesia time all add cost.

What margin should a veterinary dental target?

That is your decision. The page lets you set a target and shows the price each grade needs to reach it, and the profit per anesthesia hour for each grade.

Does this replace my practice management system?

No. It is a pricing worksheet. Enter your own fees and costs; nothing is stored or sent anywhere.
