    Common questions

    What does this quote actually add up?

    The price adds up extraction hours per room from the square footage and production rate you enter, an extra extraction pass on any room marked pet treatment, stairs by flight, upholstery piece by piece, furniture-moving minutes, and the round-trip drive — plus chemistry per square foot and per pet room, other job costs, overhead, and your target margin, then compares that to your posted minimum charge and quotes whichever is higher.

    What is the “hours that are not extraction” number, and why does it matter?

    It is every hour on the ticket that is not carpet extraction — stairs, pet extra passes, upholstery, furniture moving, and the drive. A posted per-room special is built on light-soil, vacant-room extraction time; stairs, a sofa, a pet room, and the windshield time to get there are exactly the hours that special leaves off, which is why a “3 bedroom special” that looked full on the schedule can still lose money once they are added back in.

    Why does a pet-treatment room cost more than the same square footage without it?

    A pet-treated room gets an extra extraction pass — by default a full second pass at the same production rate — on top of the normal extraction hours for that room’s square footage, plus its own line of pet chemistry cost. Two rooms of identical size price differently once one of them is marked for pet treatment.

    Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    No account and no network requests — the calculator runs entirely in your browser, and nothing about the job is sent anywhere. A share link encodes only the rooms, upholstery, and rates you choose into the address bar and is never sent to us; only your business details for the printed proposal are kept in this browser’s local storage so you do not retype them, and the customer’s details are never saved.

    What does “minimum applied” mean?

    It shows whether your posted minimum charge ended up higher than the price the job’s own hours and costs would have produced. On a small stop — one light room and a short drive — the minimum is usually what you actually quote; on a house with stairs, pets, and upholstery, the job price passes the minimum on its own.

    Does this check drying time, mold risk, or whether a carpet is worth cleaning?

    No. Production rates and chemistry costs are ballpark shop figures you can edit, not a moisture-control assessment, a mold-risk determination, or a guarantee that a given stain, pet odor, or worn carpet will come clean. Whether a carpet is salvageable, how long it needs to dry, and what soil level a specific room really is are still a judgment call from the walkthrough — this tool prices the job you describe, it does not inspect it.
