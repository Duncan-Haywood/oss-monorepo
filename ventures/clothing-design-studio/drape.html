Sew the pattern onto a body and see where it pulls

A size chart compares two numbers: the garment's chest and yours. Cloth does more than that. It hangs from the shoulders, takes length going round the chest, and stretches where the body is bigger than the cut. This page takes the drafted front and back pattern pieces, sews them at the shoulders and side seams around a body built from your measurements, sets the sleeves into the armholes around the arms, lets gravity settle them, and colours each part of the cloth by how much it stretches around the body.

A simulation from typical drafting ratios, a smooth body made of ellipses, and fabric stiffness you type or guess. Body and sleeves on straight arms held out 30°; no hood or rib bands yet. It shows ease and tension; it is not a promise of fit. A sample garment on a real person always wins.

How it works, and what was checked

The front and back pieces are meshed into triangles on a grid inside their stitch lines. Every grid edge is a spring set from the fabric's stiffness, solved with extended position-based dynamics (XPBD) in many small time steps; the springs on the outline carry half a cell, so the panel's stiffness per unit width is the number you enter. The panels start on a loose tube around the body and the sleeves on a tube around each arm; the shoulder, side and underarm seams are pulled shut and each sleeve cap is sewn into its armhole, joining points at the same fraction of each curve's length so the cap's ease is gathered in, all with gravity off; then gravity is switched on and the garment settles. The arms are straight, held 30° out from the sides (the pose virtual fitting avatars use, so the underarm has room), and round, tapering from your upper arm girth to your wrist. The body is a stack of elliptical sections whose girths are your chest, waist and hip, with height proportions from the same body model as the size finder's try-on.

Before any garment, the solver is checked against answers known in closed form (drape.test.mjs): a strip hanging under its own weight stretches by ρgL²/2K (within 2%), a nearly unstretchable strip between two pins sags like a catenary (within 3%), and a tube pulled over a cylinder takes the stretch the two girths dictate, with a contact force that approaches Laplace's law, pressure = tension ÷ radius, as the time step shrinks. The pressure shown here is that law applied to the simulated stretch.

Putting arms in found a drafting error (fixed 2026-11-13): the sleeve piece had been drawn with the flat, laid-down bicep as its full width, so at M it was 24 cm round, and the drape stretched it 37% round a 31 cm upper arm. Its length also ran from the underarm instead of the shoulder point, so sleeves were a cap height too long. The pattern now draws the whole sleeve (about 38 cm round at M, a 10 cm cap) measured from the shoulder point, as the spec sheet does. You can enter your own flat bicep above to see a narrower or wider sleeve.

One thing the flat numbers miss: on the default body every drafted tee, sweatshirt and hoodie that is not stretched ends about 5.5 to 8 cm higher than its body length measured straight down from the shoulder, because that length goes over the shoulder and round the chest and back. It is a model result, not yet checked against a real garment on a real person.
