Clothing Design Studio

You have the designs. This turns them into something a factory can make and a price you can live with.

For designers starting their own line: people who know what the shirt should look like and what fabric should feel like, and would rather not learn a 3D pattern-making suite or a spreadsheet to find out what it costs.

The problem, in a designer's words

This tool started from an interview with a designer planning a unisex line of shirts with classical and devotional artwork, on fabric that looks expensive even if it does not cost much. Three things they said shaped it:

"Of course I need to know the prices of the fabrics … and how much a shirt is going to cost."
"I'm not good at marketing."
Paintings of saints and angels on shirts, "like 3D paintings".

What it does

Talk to it

"Make it cream, add a halo, embroider it in 3D and move it up a bit" is four changes, each listed with the words that made it and an Undo. Typing works the same. No AI is needed for this; an optional model of your own handles freer speech.

Or drag it

Move, resize and rotate artwork on a tee, long-sleeve, sweatshirt or hoodie, front and back, inside the real print areas.

Compare three versions

One click (or "show me three versions") gives a different colourway, a different size and placement, and a different decoration method, side by side. "Go with the second one" picks it.

Fabric and method that work together

Nine fabrics with weights, eight decoration methods including raised high-density ink, puff print and 3D puff embroidery, and plain-language notes when a pairing usually fails (sublimation on cotton, dense embroidery on a light knit).

What it costs and what is left

Landed cost per garment from your manufacturer's prices, the margin after VAT and fees, cash needed up front, sales to break even, and the price a target margin needs.

Ready for the factory

A printable tech pack with both sides drawn and every placement measured from the shoulder, and a quote request you edit and send.

A start on marketing

A product description, three captions and hashtags drawn only from the design's facts, plus a store import file. Drafts to edit; nothing is posted for you.

Yours, on your device

Free and open source. Designs, images and prices stay in your browser.

A worked example

100 black 260 gsm tees with a raised halo on the front and lettering on the back, using the studio's example prices: about €21.27 each landed, 38.9% margin at €45 including 20% VAT, paid back after 74 sales. The full sum, line by line. The example prices are placeholders, not quotes.

When to use something else

  • You need patterns, grading and fit on a 3D avatar. Professional 3D garment design suites do this; this studio draws flat front and back views and does not make patterns.
  • You want someone else to print and ship each order as it sells. A print-on-demand service does that with its own blanks and prices; this studio is for planning a run with your own manufacturer.
  • You need a final production spec. The tech pack here is a strong first draft for a quote and a sample. The sample is the real spec.
