Questions

What must be on a clothing label in the US?

Fibre content by generic name in descending order of weight (spandex, not Lycra), the country of origin, the maker's name or an RN number from the FTC, and a permanent care label. See 16 CFR 303 and 16 CFR 423.

What must be on a clothing label in the EU?

Fibre content in the language of the country of sale, with the approved fibre names (Regulation 1007/2011). The general product safety regulation (2023/988) also asks for the maker's name and postal address and a product identifier, plus an EU responsible person when the maker is outside the EU.

What must be on a clothing label in the UK?

For Great Britain: fibre content in English with the approved fibre names and in descending order (Textile Products Regulations 2012), plus the maker's or importer's name and address and a product reference under the General Product Safety Regulations 2005. Care labels are not required by law. Northern Ireland follows the EU rules.

What must be on a clothing label in Canada?

Fibre content by generic name in descending order, in English and French, and the dealer's name and postal address or a CA number from the Competition Bureau (Textile Labelling Act). Care labels are voluntary; when used they follow the CAN/CGSB-86.1 symbols.

Can I write "Lycra" or "bamboo"?

Not as the fibre name. Lycra is a brand of elastane (spandex in the US). Bamboo fabric is usually viscose or lyocell, and the label carries that name.

How exact must the percentages be?

Both markets allow about 3 percentage points between the stated and tested share. More than that is a wrong label, so state what the fabric supplier's test says.
