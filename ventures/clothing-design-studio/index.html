Free clothing design studio

Say what you want on the shirt. See it, change it, know what it costs.

For independent designers starting a clothing line: design a tee, long-sleeve, sweatshirt or hoodie by talking, typing or dragging, compare three versions side by side, cost every piece from your manufacturer's prices, and download the tech pack and quote request to send them.         Questions
        
    
        Do I need to pay or sign up?
        
    
        No. The studio is free and open source (MIT) and runs in your browser. The command box understands a fixed set of design commands with no AI at all. If you want it to understand freer speech, you can add your own AI key or point it at a model running on your own computer; that is optional and never required.
        
    
        Where do the prices come from?
        
    
        From your manufacturer. The studio ships an example sheet with round placeholder numbers so the page has something to show, and it says so next to every figure. Type your manufacturer's quote in, or import it as a CSV, and every cost on the page uses it.
        
    
        Can it print a 3D, raised design?
        
    
        It lets you choose raised methods (high-density screen print, puff print and 3D puff embroidery) and draws them with a shadow so you can see the effect. It also notes where a raised method suits the artwork poorly, for example fine painted detail, which usually reproduces better flat with the raised ink on an outline or halo.
        
    
        Can I put a famous old painting on a shirt?
        
    
        Often, if the painting is in the public domain and the photograph you use is released for reuse. Several museums publish open-access images; the studio lists some. Check each image's terms yourself: the studio does not check rights.
        
    
        Is my design uploaded?
        
    
        No. Designs and uploaded images stay in your browser. A share link carries the design inside the link, without uploaded images. If you add an AI key, your command text and a short description of the design go to the provider you chose; with a local model, nothing leaves your computer.
