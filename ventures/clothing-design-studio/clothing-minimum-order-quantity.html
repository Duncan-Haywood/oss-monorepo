Can pre-orders reach a factory's minimum order quantity?

A new brand with no cash for a first run can sell before it makes: take pre-orders, place the factory order only if the minimum is reached, and refund everyone if not. Whether that works is arithmetic you can do before spending anything. Here it is for one example.

Every figure below is an assumption typed for illustration, not a benchmark: no real conversion rate, traffic or factory quote. It is not financial advice. Pre-orders also carry consumer-protection duties (delivery promises, refund times) that differ by country; check yours. The pre-order planner lets you type your own numbers.

The example

A tee sells for 35.00 including 20% VAT. Landed cost at the factory's 300-piece MOQ is 12.00. Payment fees 3% plus 0.30, 5% of orders cancel or fail payment, 800 already spent on samples and photos, 2% of visitors order, a 30-day campaign.

What the MOQ really asks for

  • 316 orders, not 300: 5% fall away, so you need 300 / 0.95.
  • 15,800 visitors at a 2% conversion rate. Most new shops get far fewer than this in a month.

Two things stand out. First, at these numbers bought traffic loses money even when the run is funded: 15,800 visits at 0.40 is 6,320, more than the margin on 300 tees. The price would have to be at least 44.85 for that run to break even. Second, even 400 unpaid visitors a day misses a 30-day window: the chance of funding is effectively zero, so the designer either needs a longer window, a higher conversion rate (test it first on a small group) or a factory with a lower MOQ.

What to do with this

  • Ask the factory what the price is at half the MOQ. A dearer first run you can fill beats a cheaper one you cannot.
  • Run a small test to bound your conversion rate before promising a campaign; the planner turns a test into a range.
  • Take a deposit rather than the full price if the law where you sell allows it, so less money is held for refunds.
  • Compare factory quotes at your realistic run size on the quotes page.

Questions

What is a minimum order quantity (MOQ) in clothing manufacturing?

The smallest number of pieces a factory will make per style, and often per colour. Below it the factory either refuses or charges a higher price per piece, because set-up cost (pattern, cutting, printing screens, line changeover) is spread over fewer garments.

How many orders do I need to take before I can place a 300-piece MOQ?

More than 300. With 5% of pre-orders cancelling or failing payment, you need 316 orders to keep 300. At a 2% conversion rate that is about 15,800 visitors.

Can I negotiate an MOQ down?

Often, for a higher price per piece, a stock fabric instead of a custom one, or a single colour. The quotes page compares your own factory quotes at your run size.
