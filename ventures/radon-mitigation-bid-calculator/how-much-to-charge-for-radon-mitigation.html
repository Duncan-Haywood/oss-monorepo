How much to charge for radon mitigation

The number a homeowner wants is a published “$1,195 radon special.” That number is not a takeoff. A 1,200-square-foot walk-out basement with one suction point, a standard fan, and a sidewall discharge is not the same job as a 2,400-square-foot slab-and-crawl combo with two pits, a high-suction fan, a roof stack, a crawl membrane, and a new circuit. The word “radon system” is the same. The hours are not.

Count the suction and the membrane, not just the special

Basement-waterproofing calculators price wells, a pit, and discharge. Foundation-repair calculators price access, extra shaft, and straps. Attic-insulation calculators price blown-in bags. A radon install is a depressurization system: the included footprint, the extra suction, the crawl poly the special never mentions, the roof penetration, and the circuit. Quoting all of that from a published special is how the first extra suction pit eats the field margin.

A job with a similar first suction can cost much more

Each extra suction is a pit, a run, and a seal that never appears on the brochure. Extra square feet over the included system are billed as their own hours. A crawl membrane, a high-suction fan, a new 20A circuit, firestops, a permit, and a post-mitigation test are adders that get forgotten on a walk-out special. On the sample rates a 2,400 sq ft mixed slab-and-crawl job with two suctions, a high-suction fan, an 800 sq ft membrane, a new circuit, two firestops, a permit, and a post-test prices about $4,203 ($1.75 per square foot of footprint, 21.6 crew-hours). The same shop’s 1,200 sq ft basement SSD prices about $1,122 ($0.93 per square foot, 5.0 hours, walk-away $808). Matching the brochure job’s per-square-foot number — or a $1,195 special — on the mixed stop is how that job becomes a loss of about $1,831.

A tight slab is not automatically the expensive one

A 2,000 sq ft tight slab-on-grade with two suctions, a high-suction fan, a roof stack, a new circuit, two firestops, and a post-test is not the mixed job plus 20%. On the sample rates that slab prices about $3,050 ($1.53 per square foot, 16.9 crew-hours) — more than the brochure special, less than the mixed takeoff, because the crawl membrane and permit do not live in a published slab adder. A typical 1,800 sq ft basement with a roof stack and a post-test prices about $1,507 (8.4 hours). Treating either as a blended add-on is how the special that looked fine turns into a loss after the first extra pit.

Load the wage, then keep a walk-away number

A $42/h tech loaded at 34% payroll burden is $56.28. Margin is a share of the price you send, not a markup on cost. The walk-away price is total cost — below that the job makes nothing before you take a dollar home. On the sample rates even the brochure basement walk-away is $808, which is why a $795 Facebook special is already a loss before the first extra suction. Replace every rate with your fan invoices before you send either number.

What this is not

This is not a radon design, a health determination, a permit, a guarantee that any building will test below an action level, or a recommended market price. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type. Foundation type, footprint, suction points, fan, discharge, and whether the house needs a new circuit are yours to confirm.

Common questions

How much to charge for radon mitigation?

Charge from the included footprint, extra square feet over that include, extra suction points, crawl membrane, roof versus sidewall discharge, high-suction fan, new dedicated circuit, firestops, permit, post-mitigation test, loaded labor, overhead, and a margin that is a share of price. A default 1,200 sq ft basement SSD at the sample rates in this calculator prices around $1,122 — about $0.93 per square foot of footprint.

Why is a flat radon-special quote dangerous?

Homeowners count 'a radon system is a radon system.' Extra suction, a crawl membrane, a roof stack, a high-suction fan, a new circuit, firestops, and a post-mitigation test do not live in a $1,195 special. Matching a cheaper basement quote written for a brochure walk-out is how a mixed slab-and-crawl job becomes a loss.

Does a larger house always cost more to mitigate?

Not in proportion. On the sample rates a 2,400 sq ft mixed slab-and-crawl job with two suction points, a high-suction fan, an 800 sq ft membrane, a new circuit, two firestops, a permit, and a post-test prices around $4,203 ($1.75 per square foot), while a 1,200 sq ft basement SSD prices around $1,122 ($0.93 per square foot).
