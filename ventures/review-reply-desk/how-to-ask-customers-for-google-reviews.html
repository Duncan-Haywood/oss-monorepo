How to ask customers for Google reviews

Most happy customers never leave a review unless someone asks. Asking is allowed and expected. The way you ask decides whether the reviews stay up, so this page covers the ask, the link, the timing and the three mistakes that get reviews removed.

Write your requests free, in your browser

The three mistakes

  1. Offering something for the review. A discount, a free item or an entry into a draw, in return for a review, is against Google's policy. Say thank you instead.
  2. Review gating. Asking everyone how they felt and only sending the happy ones to the public page is selectively soliciting positive reviews, which Google prohibits. Send every customer the same request.
  3. Asking for a rating. “Please leave us five stars” turns feedback into a favour. Ask for honest feedback, good or bad.

Make it one tap

Every extra step costs responses. Use your direct review link, so the customer lands on the review box, not your listing. Review Reply Desk builds the link from your Google Place ID or accepts the g.page link from your Business Profile.

When to ask

Within a day or two of the visit or job, at a calm moment: after the invoice is paid or the work is signed off, not mid-job. Add a line to receipts and invoices so customers who skip the message still have the link.

Questions

Is it allowed to ask customers for Google reviews?

Yes. Google encourages businesses to ask customers to leave reviews and lets you share a direct link to your review form. What is not allowed is offering something in return, asking only the customers you expect to be happy, or discouraging negative reviews.

What is review gating?

Review gating means screening customers first, for example asking how their experience was and only sending the happy ones to the public review page. Google's policy prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews, so send the same request to every customer.

Can I give a discount for a review?

No. Offering a discount, gift or entry into a draw in exchange for a review breaks Google's policy, and rules on bought reviews, such as the FTC's, point the same way. Say thank you instead. This is not legal advice.

When is the best time to ask for a review?

Soon after the job or visit, while it is fresh, and at a moment the customer is not rushed: after the invoice is paid or the work is signed off. A text or email within a day or two works better than a request weeks later.

How do I get my Google review link?

Open your Business Profile on Google, choose Ask for reviews, and copy the link, or use your Place ID with Google's write-review format. Paste either into Review Reply Desk and it builds the link for you.

Do I need permission to text customers?

Texting people without their agreement can break telephone and carrier rules in many places. Only text customers who gave you their number and agreed to be contacted, and include a way to opt out. Ask your own adviser what applies to you.
