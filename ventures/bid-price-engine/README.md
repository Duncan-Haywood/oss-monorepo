# Bid Price Engine

After using this, you know the price that pays you the margin you need on a
job, the walk-away price below which the job loses money, and how far labor can
run over before it stops paying. One calculator covers every trade.

**Live tool:** https://duncan-haywood.github.io/monorepo-projects/bid-price-engine/

Free, no account, runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded; a share link keeps the inputs in the address after `#`.

**What it does:** labor (hours at wage plus payroll burden), materials (plus the waste you buy), subcontractors, equipment and travel, then contingency and overhead, then `price = full cost / (1 - margin)`. It shows the walk-away and cash-floor prices, the margin a rival's bid would leave you, a price ladder, and the markup trap (a 25% markup is only a 20% margin). A helper works your overhead % out from last year's books.

**Why use it instead of what you have:** a spreadsheet markup (the usual way, and wrong whenever markup gets typed as margin), a paid field-service app at $10-60 a month, or one of the ~120 single-trade pages in this repo. The trade picker loads starting inputs for that kind of work and links to the specialist calculator for its particulars (`../<trade>-bid-calculator/`), so those pages are the depth and this is the shared engine.

**Learn which margin wins:** log each quote as won or lost (or paste past bids as `price,cost,won`). From eight bids with two wins and two losses, `winrate.mjs` fits a logistic win-chance curve against margin (ridge-penalised Newton's method, so a clean split of wins and losses stays finite), finds the margin with the best expected profit per bid (win chance times markup) inside the range you have actually quoted, and gives a seeded bootstrap range for it. If your win rate does not fall as margin rises, it says so and recommends nothing. It sees price only, not customer, trade or season. The log is stored in this browser and never uploaded.

**Close out the job:** after the work, enter actual hours, materials, subs and revenue. The page shows what the job really left against the bid (realized margin, labor and material overrun, profit per labor hour). Closed jobs stay in your browser's local storage; after three, it shows how far your jobs run over and the contingency that would have covered it, with one click to apply it. Most bidding tools stop at the quote; this is the feedback loop that fixes the next one.

Files: `calc.mjs` (pure arithmetic), `closeout.mjs` (closeout and track-record maths), `winrate.mjs` (win-chance curve and best margin), `trades.mjs` (generated by `node build-trades.mjs` from the `*-bid-calculator` directories), `index.html`, `markup-vs-margin.html`. Run the tests with `node --test`.

## Host your own copy

You do not need the author's site. Fork the repository and run your own:

- **Docker:** `docker compose up -d`, then open <http://localhost:8080> (`Dockerfile` is nginx plus the site's files and PWA assets).
- **Fly.io:** `fly launch --copy-config --no-deploy --dockerfile Dockerfile`, then `fly deploy` (`fly.toml`).
- **Render:** deploy from your fork with `render.yaml` (static site, no paid plan needed).
- Nothing runs on the author's accounts, and nothing you type reaches a server. The Docker image has not been built in the session that added these files, so build it once and open the page before relying on it. Tests: `node --test deploy.test.mjs`.
