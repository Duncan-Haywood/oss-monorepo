Common questions

    
How is statutory redundancy pay worked out?

    
For each complete year of service, up to 20 years counted back from the dismissal date, you get half a week's pay for each year you were under 22, one week's pay for each year aged 22 to 40 and one and a half weeks' pay for each year aged 41 or over.

    
Who qualifies?

    
Employees with at least 2 years of continuous service who are dismissed because of redundancy. Northern Ireland has its own rules and figures.

    
Is there a limit on a week's pay?

    
Yes. A week's pay is capped: £719 for dismissals from 6 April 2025 and £751 from 6 April 2026. With the 30-week maximum that makes the most you can get £21,570 or £22,530.

    
Is redundancy pay taxed?

    
The first £30,000 of redundancy pay is free of income tax. Anything above that, and any pay in lieu of notice, is taxed as earnings.

    
Can my employer pay more than the statutory amount?

    
Yes. Contracts and company schemes often pay more. They can never pay less than the statutory minimum if you qualify.
