The arithmetic, and what it leaves out

  • LCL is billed on the greater of volume (CBM) and weight (tonnes), with a minimum, plus your fixed per-shipment charges.
  • A container is a flat price. Capacity used is about 28, 58 and 68 CBM for a 20', 40' and 40' high cube, and about 26 to 26.5 tonnes of payload; weight or volume can force a second box.
  • The break-even assumes your cargo keeps the same density as you entered, and is searched in half-CBM steps up to 200.
  • Carrying cost is goods value × annual cost of money × the extra LCL days ÷ 365.
  • Not modelled: peak-season and currency surcharges, port-specific fees, duty, insurance, drayage, free time and demurrage, and each carrier's own rounding. Confirm every figure with your forwarder.

Common questions

When is FCL cheaper than LCL?

When the cheapest full container costs no more than the LCL charge for your volume. LCL is billed per cubic metre (or per tonne if the cargo is dense), so its cost rises with every CBM, while a container is a flat price. Past roughly half a 20-foot box, many lanes already favour the container; this tool finds the line from your own quotes.

What is the LCL minimum and the W/M rule?

Most consolidators charge the greater of volume in CBM and weight in tonnes (W/M, 'weight or measure'), with a minimum of about 1 CBM. A dense pallet of 5 CBM weighing 9 tonnes is billed as 9.

How many CBM fit in a 20ft, 40ft and 40ft high-cube container?

Practical loadable space is about 28 CBM, 58 CBM and 68 CBM. Payload is roughly 26 tonnes for a 20-foot and 26.5 tonnes for a 40-foot. The tool uses these and lets weight force a second box.

Why does a half-empty container sometimes still win?

Because LCL carries per-CBM rates plus consolidation, handling and documentation charges, and adds days at the origin and destination warehouses. Add the cost of stock sitting in transit and the container can come out ahead.

What does this leave out?

Port-specific surcharges, peak-season and currency adjustments, customs duty, insurance, drayage, free-time and demurrage, and the carrier's own definition of chargeable weight. Use it to compare quotes, then confirm with your forwarder.
