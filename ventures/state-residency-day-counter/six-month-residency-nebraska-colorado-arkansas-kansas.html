Nebraska, Colorado, Arkansas and Kansas: the six-month tests

These four states do not name a day count. Nebraska (Neb. Rev. Stat. 77-2714.01), Colorado (1 CCR 201-2), Arkansas (Ark. Code 26-51-102) and Kansas (K.S.A. 79-32,109) look at more than six months of the tax year. The counter treats 183 days as crossing it, so the safe maximum is 182 days in each. Nebraska, Colorado and Arkansas apply it to someone who also keeps a permanent place of abode in the state; Kansas presumes residency from presence alone, and the presumption can be rebutted.

Questions

Why is the limit 182 days in Nebraska, Colorado, Arkansas and Kansas?

The rules say more than six months, not a number of days. The counter treats 183 days as crossing it, so the most you can spend is 182. Exactly six months does not cross it. Confirm the state's counting method if your year is close.

Which of the four need a home in the state?

Nebraska, Colorado and Arkansas apply the six-month test to someone who is not domiciled there only if they also keep a permanent place of abode in the state. Kansas does not ask for an abode: it presumes residency from more than six months of presence, and the presumption can be rebutted.

Does domicile change this?

Yes. Domicile in any of the four makes you a resident at any day count. The counter measures days only, so it cannot tell you whether you are domiciled there.

Does the day count have to be at the home?

No. In Nebraska presence anywhere in the state counts, and in Kansas presence anywhere in the state counts, not just time at a house.
