    Common questions

    How do I bid a kitchen tile backsplash so the price reflects the wall and the pattern?

    Enter the wall’s square footage and edge length, pick the scope (tile-only, new prep, or demo-and-replace) and the pattern (subway running-bond, herringbone, mosaic, or large-format), then replace the starting hours and material rates with your own crew’s numbers. The calculator prices the job from wall size, pattern waste, extras like outlets, a cooktop cutout, and Schluter, and your loaded labor rate — not from a flat per-square-foot number that cannot see any of that.

    Why does a published $35 per square foot special lose money on a herringbone demo job?

    Because a flat rate assumes every splash is the same job. Herringbone burns more tile to waste and more hours to cut and align than a subway running-bond stack, and demo-and-replace adds a pull on top of the same prep as new prep. The three-scope and four-pattern comparisons put the herringbone-demo price against the subway-tile-only price on the identical wall, so the gap a flat rate hides becomes a number.

    Should I install tile the customer already bought instead of tile I supply?

    It depends on whether zeroing the tile invoice actually saves you money once outlets, the cooktop cutout, the trip, and a short or wrong-lot buy are still on you. The who-supplies comparison prices you-supply, owner-covers, and short-or-wrong-lot on the same wall so you can see whether the customer’s number still covers your labor once their tile choice risks a second trip.

    Do leftover tile boxes from the kitchen cover a laundry or pantry wall too?

    Only if what is left over after the kitchen buy, rounded up to whole boxes, still carries the waste rate you assumed for that job. Tile sells by the box, not by the square foot, so a 33-square-foot buy at 10 square feet a box is four boxes, not 3.3 — and the leftover credit only applies if you decide that stock already on the van is already paid for.

    Does anything I type get uploaded when I use this calculator?

    No. The whole calculator runs in your browser: it makes no network requests, your wall size, wages, costs, and prices stay in your own browser’s local storage so the page remembers your bid on the next visit, and a bid link carries those numbers in the URL fragment itself, which browsers never send to a server. Only an anonymous local tally of which features were used is kept, and it is opt-out from the footer.

    Should I tile a backsplash after hours or on a weekend if the customer asks for it?

    It depends on whether the extra rate covers time-and-a-half plus the second evening trip you need because mud cannot be left overnight. The when-you-can-work comparison prices an empty weekday, a job where they cook around you, and after-hours or weekend access on the same wall, so you can see whether their preferred schedule still clears your target margin.

    What does payroll burden change about the bid?

    Payroll burden is the employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off layered onto the crew’s hourly wage before it becomes loaded labor cost. Underestimating it is a common way a job that looked profitable on the wage alone was not — the calculator applies your burden percentage to every hour in the job, not just the base wage.

    Is this a published tile price guide or industry standard?

    No. It is an estimate built from the scope, pattern, and rates you enter, not a catalog or a benchmark. The starting hours and material figures are common ballpark numbers meant to be replaced with your own timed crew and your own supplier invoices; the calculator does the arithmetic, not the pricing judgment.
