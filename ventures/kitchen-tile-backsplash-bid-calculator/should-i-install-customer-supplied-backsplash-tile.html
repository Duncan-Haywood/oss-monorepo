Should I install customer-supplied backsplash tile?

Owners call with boxes in the garage: “I already bought the tile. Can you just set it for $35 a square foot?” That sounds like free margin because the tile invoice left the bid. It did not leave the trip, the outlet cuts, the cooktop, or the chance that they bought the measured wall and not the waste — or leftover from another room with a different dye lot.

Zeroing the tile is not a free install

A you-supply herringbone still carries the tile invoice. An owner-supplied buy that actually covers zeros that line and keeps the same set hours. On a typical 42 SF herringbone demo — $38 wage, 30% burden, 15% overhead, 20% target margin, 16 miles, six outlets, cooktop, Schluter — the you-supply bid is $3,858.00. Zeroing the tile drops it, but the owner-supplied walk-away stays at $2,698.84. Taking $1,470 ($35 / SF) to “just set what they bought” still loses money.

Short boxes and a wrong dye lot are a second trip

Store shelves and leftover from another room do not include pattern waste. Setting a short or mismatched lot means a callback plus rush tile. Price that on the same splash before you agree. The calculator adds a starting second trip and buys the waste they skipped at a rush multiplier on top of the rates you already typed.

This is not leftover on your van

Leftover boxes already on the van are your stock after a kitchen you bought. That is a different question — see do leftover boxes cover the laundry wall. Customer-supplied tile is their boxes, their lot, and an install-only number. A walk-in shower is a wet area, a slope, and glass. This prices a dry kitchen splash.

Common questions

Should I install kitchen backsplash tile the customer bought at a store?

Only after you price the set without the tile invoice. Pulling their boxes is still a trip, outlet cuts, and crew hours. A $35 / SF install-only special on a herringbone demo often sits under that walk-away number even when the tile is free to you.

Does customer-supplied tile make the $35 / SF special profitable?

Usually not on the job they actually want. Zeroing a herringbone invoice drops the bid, but loaded labor, travel, outlets, cooktop, and overhead stay. On a typical 42 SF herringbone demo the owner-supplied walk-away is $2,698.84. $1,470 ($35 / SF) still loses money.

What if the store-bought boxes are short or the wrong dye lot?

That is a second dispatch plus rush tile for the waste they skipped. Homeowners buy the measured wall, not the waste, or leftover from another room. Price that callback on the same splash before you agree to set whatever they rolled into the kitchen.
