Should I use Amazon Remote Fulfillment?

Sellers search this after a US FBA pile is already sitting and Amazon offers Remote Fulfillment with North America. Skipping Canada leaves that demand on the table. Remote Fulfillment ships the US unit across the border for a surcharge. A dedicated Canada pile buys local Prime take — and a second inbound. The main calculator’s inbound / unit line is a blended US number, so it cannot show whether the remote fee is cheaper than a first CA shipment.

On the default $29.99 large-standard unit already in US FBA, Remote Fulfillment keeps $5.12 more per Canada session than skipping ($5.12 vs $0.00), $204.86 on 40 sessions. The $1.25 remote surcharge on a unit you already inbounded still beats a $5.20 first Canada inbound even though local Prime converts 65% against Remote Fulfillment’s 50%. A dedicated Canada pile is $4.87, $0.25 behind. The remote fee can rise to about $1.75 before the pile matches. A $2.40 Canada inbound flips the pile ahead by $1.57 ($6.69 vs $5.12).

Is Remote Fulfillment the same as Amazon AWD or inbound placement?

No. AWD is where a fresh US purchase order parks before it replenishes FBA. Inbound placement is which US fulfillment centers a shipment goes to. Remote Fulfillment is whether Canada or Mexico demand uses the US pile you already sent, for a per-unit surcharge, or whether you send a second pile across the border. This page holds US placement at the listing inbound line so the table stays a Canada-expansion question. It does not recommend Remote Fulfillment and it does not publish Amazon's live NARF schedule.

When does a dedicated Canada pile beat Remote Fulfillment?

When Canada inbound plus the slower Remote Fulfillment take costs more than sending working stock for local Prime. On these defaults a $2.40 Canada inbound flips the pile ahead by $1.57 per session ($6.69 vs $5.12). Matched take rates usually favor whichever inbound is cheaper.
