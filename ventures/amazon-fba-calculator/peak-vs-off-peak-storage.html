Should you send FBA inventory before Q4 peak storage?

Sellers search for “Amazon peak storage fees” in late summer because the referral fee is not the decision. Both a September inbound and a January inbound usually pay the same category referral and the same fulfillment fee. The gap is what the unit pays to sit through October, November, and December.

Amazon bills FBA storage by cubic foot per month. January through September is the off-peak rate. October through December is the peak rate — several times higher on the same box. A shipment that arrives in late August and sells through slowly pays one off-peak month and then three peak months. The same units sent in January pay the lower rate for the whole stay.

On a one-cubic-foot example held three months, with two of those months in peak — $0.87 off-peak and $2.40 peak, 200 units — waiting until January keeps $3.06 more per unit, $612 on the shipment. That is only storage. Referral, fulfillment, inbound freight, and cost of goods stay where they are.

Peak storage can also wipe a thin listing by itself. Divide the unit’s FBA profit before storage by the peak cubic-foot charge: that is how many October–December months the unit can sit before storage alone puts it underwater.

Rates change by size, account, and year. This page does not publish Amazon’s live schedule. Open the Amazon FBA calculator, enter the months of the stay that fall in October–December and the rates from your fee preview, and compare waiting until January against sending now.

The calculator is arithmetic, not advice. It does not tell you to inbound or to wait, and it does not claim a shipment is profitable after you change the month.
