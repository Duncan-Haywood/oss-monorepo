Low-inventory-level fee: is restocking to 28 days cheaper than staying thin?

Amazon sellers search for the low-inventory-level fee because running FBA stock close to demand is no longer free. When both the 30-day and the 90-day historical days of supply sit below 28, FBA shipments can pick up a per-unit surcharge. If either window is 28 days or more, this model adds nothing.

Days of supply is average on-hand inventory divided by average daily units shipped. The surcharge band then depends on how thin the thinner window is, plus size tier and weight. Merchant-fulfilled orders do not pay it. Illustrative grocery and new-FNSKU exemptions are switches you can turn on — they are not a claim that your account qualifies.

Restocking is not free either. Sending enough units to cover 28 days at the faster of those two sales windows adds inbound now and monthly storage on the extra cube. On a small, fast SKU the surcharge is often larger than that storage. On a bulky SKU in peak season, paying the fee and staying thin can leave more cash.

Open the Amazon FBA vs FBM calculator, enter on-hand units and the last 30 / 90 days of sales from Seller Central, and compare stay thin against restock to 28 days on the same listing. The numbers are arithmetic from your inputs, not Amazon’s live fee schedule.

The calculator does not recommend a restock quantity, and it does not claim a listing is profitable after you send more inventory.
