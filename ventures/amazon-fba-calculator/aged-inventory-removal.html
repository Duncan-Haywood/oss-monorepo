Should I keep leftover FBA inventory or pay to remove it?

Sellers search for “aged inventory surcharge” and “FBA removal fee” because a unit that still shows a profit on a fee calculator can lose that leftover sale while it sits. Monthly storage continues. After a few months Amazon may also charge an aged-inventory surcharge on the cubic feet still in the warehouse. Removal or disposal is a separate fee — and sometimes cheaper than hoping the last units sell.

What you already paid to source and inbound the units does not change this decision. Those costs are sunk either way. The comparison is forward-looking:

Hold assumes the remaining units sell at your current listing price after more months in FBA. You still pay referral, fulfillment, ads, future storage, and any surcharge that applies after your aging threshold. Remove pays the removal or disposal fee now and recovers whatever you would get from liquidation, selling the units elsewhere, or nothing if you dispose of them.

On a typical leftover example — twenty $29.99 home-and-kitchen units, already stored five months, expected to sell in three more, a $1.50 aged surcharge after six months, and a $1.04 disposal fee with no salvage — holding still leaves more than paying to throw them away. Change the size, the wait, or the surcharge and the answer flips. A bulky unit already past the aging threshold, held six more peak-season months, can lose more in warehouse cost than a $4 liquidation minus the removal fee would recover.

Rates change by size, age band, season, and account. This page does not publish Amazon’s live aged-inventory or removal schedule.
