Is Amazon inbound placement cheaper than splitting the shipment?

Sellers search for “Amazon inbound placement fee” because the listing calculator’s inbound field hides the real trade. That number is usually freight plus placement blended into one unit cost. The decision Amazon actually offers on a create-shipment flow is three splits:

Send to one warehouse — cheapest freight, highest per-unit inbound placement fee. Partial split — more destinations, a mid placement fee. Amazon-optimized split — the destinations Amazon asks for, usually $0 placement, more freight and more labels.

On a 200-unit large-standard example — $85 to one warehouse, $140 for a partial split, $210 to follow Amazon’s split, and illustrative placement of $0.43 / $0.27 / $0 — one warehouse totals $171.00, the partial split $194.00, and the optimized split $210.00. Paying placement is $23.00 cheaper than the next option. The extra freight the optimized split can absorb before one warehouse wins is $86.00. Flip the freight quotes to $90 / $105 / $120 and a $0.55 minimal placement fee, and the optimized split wins by $80.00.

Common questions

What is the Amazon inbound placement service fee?

It is a per-unit fee Amazon charges when a shipment is sent to fewer fulfillment centers than the Amazon-optimized split. Sending to one warehouse usually costs the highest placement fee. Following Amazon's split usually costs $0 placement and more freight.

Is it cheaper to pay inbound placement or split the shipment?

Compare three totals at the same unit count: freight to one warehouse plus the minimal placement fee; freight for a partial split plus the partial placement fee; and freight for the Amazon-optimized split plus its placement fee (often $0). The cheaper total inbound is the one to send.
