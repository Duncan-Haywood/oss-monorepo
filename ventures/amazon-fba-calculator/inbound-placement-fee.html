Is Amazon inbound placement cheaper than splitting the shipment?

Sellers search for “Amazon inbound placement fee” because the listing calculator’s inbound field hides the real trade. That number is usually freight plus placement blended into one unit cost. The decision Amazon actually offers on a create-shipment flow is three splits:

Send to one warehouse — cheapest freight, highest per-unit inbound placement fee. Partial split — more destinations, a mid placement fee. Amazon-optimized split — the destinations Amazon asks for, usually $0 placement, more freight and more labels.

On a 200-unit large-standard example — $85 to one warehouse, $140 for a partial split, $210 to follow Amazon’s split, and illustrative placement of $0.43 / $0.27 / $0 — one warehouse totals $171, the partial split $194, and the optimized split $210. Paying placement is $23 cheaper than the next option. Flip the freight quotes to $90 / $105 / $120 and a $0.55 minimal placement fee, and the optimized split wins by $80.

The extra freight the optimized split can absorb before one warehouse wins is the placement fee you avoid: units × (minimal − optimized placement). If your extra freight is under that number, follow the split. If it is over, send to one warehouse and pay placement.

Rates change by size tier, weight, and account. This page does not publish Amazon’s live inbound-placement schedule. Open the inbound placement comparison, paste the per-unit fees from your current inbound preview and the freight quotes you actually have, and send the cheaper total.

The calculator is arithmetic, not advice. It does not recommend a split, and it does not claim a shipment is cheaper after Amazon reprices freight or placement.
