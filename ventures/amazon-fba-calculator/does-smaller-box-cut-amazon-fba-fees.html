Does a smaller box cut Amazon FBA fees?

Sellers search for “Amazon FBA size tier” after a fee preview jumps overnight. The referral rate did not change. The carton crossed a gate. Large standard starts when the shortest side is over 0.75 inch or the weight is over 1 lb. Extra-large starts when the longest side clears 59 inches or the girth math fails.

Open the Amazon FBA calculator and keep the default 8×6×2 inch, 0.75 lb home-and-kitchen example. That pack is large standard. Flatten the shortest side to 0.75 inch and it becomes small standard — if the unit still fits.

Current 8×6×2 in (large standard) $4.15 $10.99 —
Flattened to 0.75 in (small standard) $3.38 $11.79 +$0.80

That $0.80 is fulfillment plus the storage the smaller cube no longer occupies. At 80 units a month it is $64.02 before extra prep. A mailer that costs $5.00 more than the current carton erases the cliff and the smaller pack loses.

A weight-band drop is different: the same 8×6×2 carton at 8 oz instead of 12 oz stays large standard and keeps +$0.40 more. The calculator shows both rows so you do not confuse a cheaper ounce band with a cheaper size tier.

Rates change by size, season, category, and account. This page does not publish Amazon’s live schedule. Enter the numbers from your fee preview, add any extra prep, and verify the result before you change packaging.

The calculator is arithmetic, not advice. It does not claim the product fits the smaller box, and it does not recommend a pack-out.
