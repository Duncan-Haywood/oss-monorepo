Common questions

What is the Section 174A catch-up deduction?

A one-time deduction created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) for an eligible small business that had capitalized and was amortizing domestic research and experimental (R&E) costs from 2022-2024 under the old Section 174 rules. It covers whatever portion of those costs was not yet amortized by the start of the business's first tax year beginning after December 31, 2024 -- 2025, for a calendar-year filer -- and is claimed on that 2025 return.

Who counts as an "eligible small business" for this catch-up?

A business whose average annual gross receipts for 2022, 2023, and 2024 (including a controlled group's) were $31 million or less -- the same Section 448(c) gross-receipts test used elsewhere in the tax code. This calculator does not check your gross receipts against that threshold; confirm your own eligibility before relying on either election.

What is the difference between the 1-year and 2-year election?

Both are elected on the 2025 return under Rev. Proc. 2025-28. The 1-year option deducts the entire remaining unamortized balance in 2025. The 2-year option spreads it ratably over two years -- half in 2025, half in 2026. Taking it all in 2025 front-loads the tax savings, which is better if 2025's rate or income is expected to be higher than 2026's. Spreading it out is better if 2025's rate or income is expected to be lower -- for example a loss or near-breakeven year -- or if the rate is expected to rise in 2026.

What happens if the deduction is bigger than my taxable income for that year?

The excess does not disappear, but it also does not produce an immediate cash tax saving that year -- it can create or increase a net operating loss (NOL) instead. This calculator flags that clearly and caps the current-year cash saving at that year's taxable income before the deduction. It does not model NOL carryforward value, the 80%-of-taxable-income limitation on using a post-2017 NOL in a later year, or state conformity -- that is a case to bring to a tax professional.

Is this the same as amending my 2022-2024 returns directly?

No. A separate, fully retroactive election existed under the same Rev. Proc. to amend 2022-2024 returns directly, but its election window closed July 6, 2026. This calculator only compares the two elections still available on the 2025 return -- the full-balance-in-2025 option and the split-over-2025-2026 option -- which is the live choice a small business faces right now.
