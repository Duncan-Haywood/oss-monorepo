Common questions

    
How much of my salary will I take home in the UK?

    
For 2025/26 you pay no income tax on the first £12,570, then 20% up to £50,270, 40% up to £125,140 and 45% above, plus employee National Insurance of 8% between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above. On £30,000 that leaves about £25,120 a year, or £2,093 a month, before any pension or student loan.

    
What is the 60% tax trap?

    
Between £100,000 and £125,140 of adjusted net income the personal allowance is withdrawn at £1 for every £2 earned. Together with the 40% rate that is an effective 60% tax rate on that slice. Pension contributions reduce adjusted net income and can bring it back down.

    
What is the difference between salary sacrifice, net pay and relief at source?

    
Salary sacrifice cuts your pay before tax and National Insurance, so it saves both. A net pay arrangement takes the contribution before tax but after National Insurance. Relief at source takes it from take-home pay and the provider adds 20% back; higher rate relief is claimed separately. For the same percentage, salary sacrifice usually costs you least.

    
Do student loan repayments reduce my take-home pay?

    
Yes. Plan 1, 2, 4 and 5 loans take 9% of pay over the plan threshold and a Postgraduate Loan takes 6% over £21,000, both through payroll. They are not tax but they come off take-home pay in the same way.

    
Does this work for Scotland?

    
No. Scotland has its own income tax bands. This calculator covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland only.
