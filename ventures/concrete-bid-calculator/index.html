FOR CONCRETE CONTRACTORS

Bid the yards and the forms, not the square footage.

Price a driveway, patio, or slab the way the trade actually does: area times thickness for cubic yards, waste and the plant’s quarter-yard ticket, forms by the edge, finish by the surface, and a separate line for chute vs wheelbarrow vs pump. You get the bid, what it works out to per yard and per square foot, and the number below which the pour loses money.

The pour

One row per slab. Thickness is what turns a cheap square-foot quote into a losing job — a 6-inch RV pad is 50% more concrete than a 4-inch driveway of the same size. Pick a finish to start from a common labor figure, then replace it with a time from your own crew.

Material, access, and demo

Most plants add a short-load fee under about five yards. A 5×5 stoop that looked cheap per square foot often loses the job on that fee plus the truck minimum.
