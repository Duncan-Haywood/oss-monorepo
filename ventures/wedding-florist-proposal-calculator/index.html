Common questions

Why does buying in bunches change the price so much?

Because a recipe is written in stems and a wholesaler sells bunches. A recipe calling for 85 garden roses from a variety that ships in tens means you buy 100 and pay for 100 — the other 15 are yours whether the design uses them or not. Multiply that across six varieties and the difference between the recipe's stem count and the invoice is real money. A per-stem markup never recovers it, because the markup was applied to a cost you never actually paid. This calculator buys whole bunches on every line and reports the gap as its own figure.

Should the shrink allowance be a percentage on the flower cost?

No, and it is wrong in both directions. Adding the percentage to the line's cost at the end buys a fraction of a bunch, which no wholesaler sells. On 100 stems of a ten-stem variety the allowance genuinely needs a whole eleventh bunch, and a 5% cost bump does not pay for it — you arrive short. On 95 stems the allowance already fits inside the ten bunches the recipe forced, and the same 5% pads the quote for a bunch nobody buys. This calculator adds shrink to the stem count first and rounds bunches afterwards, so the allowance is always something you can actually purchase.

Is labour really a third of the flower total?

It is a reasonable rule for a bouquets-and-centrepieces wedding, where the hours track the stems. It breaks on installations. An arch, a hanging piece, or a late strike takes hours and comparatively few stems, so a percentage of flowers under-prices exactly the events that cost you the most time. This calculator prices design and on-site labour by the hour and shows what the percentage rule would have quoted beside it, so the gap is a number you can look at. The rule never changes the total — it is reported for comparison only.

Why is the studio's own design time counted as revenue but not as a cost?

Because it is billed, not paid out. The gross profit line subtracts the cash that actually leaves the studio for this event — flowers at purchase cost, hard goods, and anything paid to freelance designers or install crew. Your own hours are what the proposal is charging for, so counting them again as a cost would show every event you work yourself as less profitable than the identical event you hired out. If you want to see your own time as a cost, put it in the freelance crew payout field.

Does anything about my costs or my clients get uploaded?

No. Every field on this page runs in your browser only, there is no account, and nothing you type is uploaded. Nothing leaves this device unless you choose to send it: the Copy shareable link button puts the figures on the form into the page's URL fragment, which a browser never sends to a server or into a Referer header, so it reaches only the person you hand the link to.
