Nest your DXF parts on a sheet, free

Drop in the DXF files for a job, set how many of each, the sheet size, the gap between parts and the edge margin. You get nested sheets as DXF for your CAM, how much of each sheet is used, the cut length and the pierce count, with every gap checked. Small parts go inside the holes of big ones.

For shops quoting or cutting small batches that do not have a nesting module, and for anyone checking how many sheets a job needs before buying them. A greedy nester, not an optimiser: see what it does not do.

Worked example

The example job is 72 mild steel parts in millimetres: 12 square flanges (200 mm, 110 mm bore, four bolt holes), 16 gussets, 10 L brackets, 30 washers (80 mm) and 4 base plates (600 × 140), on a 2500 × 1250 sheet with a 5 mm gap and a 10 mm margin. It nests on one sheet using about 1,912 mm of its length (about 80% of that length is parts), with washers placed inside the flange bores and gussets turned to pair up. Cut length is about 59.5 m and there are 206 pierces, which is what a laser or plasma quote is priced on.

What it does and does not do

Does: true-shape nesting of closed outlines with their holes on rectangular sheets, parts inside holes, rotation in steps you choose, a minimum gap and edge margin, several sheets, offcuts of any shape (with cut-outs) and a list of mixed sheet sizes used first, the remnant left at the far end, an independent gap check, DXF out on separate cut and mark layers, utilisation, cut length and pierce counts, and the area bound (no nester can use fewer sheets than the parts' area divided by a sheet's usable area).

Not modelled: common-line cutting, lead-ins and lead-outs, kerf compensation (your CAM does that; set the gap at least to the kerf plus what your material needs), cut order and heat, micro-joints and tabs, grain beyond 0/180° turns, mirroring parts, choosing which stock is cheapest (sheets are used in the order you list them), saving the new offcut back to a remnant list, and the squeeze on a remnant's outline (it runs on rectangular last sheets only). Placement is greedy on a grid with a short search and an optional squeeze of the last sheet: on 13 standard benchmarks its layouts are about 17 to 20% longer than the best published ones, so treat the sheet count as a cautious estimate.

When to use something else: production runs where the material a stronger nester saves is worth a licence (SigmaNEST, ProNest, Lantek, your machine's own software), or when you need lead-ins and cut order in the same step.

Questions

How do I nest DXF parts for free?

Open this page, drop in one or more ASCII DXF files, give each part a quantity, enter the sheet size, the gap between parts and the edge margin, and press Nest. You get each sheet as a picture and as a DXF file for your CAM, with utilisation, cut length and pierces per sheet. Nothing is uploaded.

Will it put small parts inside the holes of big ones?

Yes. Holes in a part stay free, so washers, slugs and small brackets are placed inside large cut-outs when they fit with the gap you set.

How is the gap between parts guaranteed?

Parts are placed on a fine grid that always covers the whole shape, and each placed part reserves extra cells for the gap plus the arc flattening error. After nesting, a separate check measures the distance between every pair of placed outlines from their edges and reports any pair closer than the gap.

Is it as good as SigmaNEST, ProNest or Deepnest?

No. On 13 standard nesting benchmarks its layouts come out about 20% longer than the best published ones with the default search (24% with it off, 17% with the search at 100 orders and the last sheet squeezed), and the gap is widest on interlocking, concave parts; a commercial true-shape nester with a long search gets closer to those. It is free, quick, private and its sheets come out as plain DXF; the page also shows the area bound, the fewest sheets any nester could use.

Can it nest into my offcuts and remnants first?

Yes. Under "Remnants and other sheets on hand", load a DXF of each offcut (any closed outline, with holes where parts were already cut) or type the sizes of rectangular sheets you have, with quantities. They are filled in the order listed before a new sheet is opened, and the gap check measures every part's distance from the offcut's real edge and its cut-outs. On the worked example, an L-shaped offcut takes 35 of the 72 parts and the new sheet's used length falls from 1,912 to 1,124 mm.

Which DXF entities are read?

LINE, ARC, CIRCLE, LWPOLYLINE and POLYLINE with bulges, ELLIPSE, SPLINE and INSERT of blocks (with scale, rotation, mirroring and arrays). Text, dimensions and hatches are skipped and counted. Binary DXF is not read; save as ASCII DXF.

Are my drawings uploaded?

No. The files are read and nested in your browser tab and never leave your computer.
