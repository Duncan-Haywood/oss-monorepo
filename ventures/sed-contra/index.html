Sed Contra · Thomas Aquinas, told as stories

The Summa, one argument at a time, in ordinary rooms

Free stories, podcast episodes and videos that dramatise one article of Thomas Aquinas's Summa Theologiae at a time. Invented people argue it out in ordinary places, and every line read as Thomas is his own text in the public-domain 1920 Dominican translation, checked word for word. Both sides get their strongest case.

How each episode works

Thomas wrote every article of the Summa the same way: the objections first, at full strength; then sed contra, “on the contrary”; then his answer; then a reply to each objection. Each episode follows that shape as a story. The people are invented; Thomas is not, and you hear his words read as he wrote them (in English), cut only where marked.

  • Long-form video with chapters and captions, for YouTube or a classroom screen.
  • A podcast episode of the same story, with its own RSS feed.
  • Vertical shorts under a minute, cut around a single objection or idea.
  • The transcript, with every quotation cited to the question and article.

What it is not

It is not a substitute for reading Thomas, for a teacher, or for the Church's own teaching. Each episode ends with the strongest objections modern critics make and where to read more.

Written by an AI, around a real text. The stories, characters and narration are by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, and read by synthetic voices. Every line read as Thomas Aquinas is his own words in the 1920 English Dominican translation (public domain), checked word for word against the source by a script. The series is written from a Catholic point of view; it is not official Church teaching and no Church authority has reviewed it.
