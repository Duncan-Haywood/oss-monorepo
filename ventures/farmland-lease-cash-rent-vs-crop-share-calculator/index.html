Questions people ask

Is cash rent or a crop share better for the landlord?

Neither always wins. Cash rent is a fixed amount whatever the crop does. A crop share pays more in a good year and less in a bad one, and the landlord also pays a share of inputs. The tool finds the yield above which the share pays the landlord more than the cash rent, at the price and costs you enter.

Why would an operator prefer a crop share?

A share shifts part of the yield and price risk to the landlord and cuts the cash the operator needs up front, because the landlord pays a share of seed, fertilizer and chemicals. The operator gives up upside in a good year. The grid shows the operator's worst and best result under each lease.

What is a flexible cash lease?

A base rent plus a bonus when revenue rises above a threshold, often based on yield times a price. It sits between cash rent and a crop share. The tool takes the base, the bonus percentage and the revenue threshold you negotiate.

What do the negotiation numbers mean?

The first is the highest cash rent the operator can pay and still keep the profit you set per acre at the expected yield. The second is the cash a landlord would need to receive to match the crop share at the same yield. If the first is lower, no cash rent satisfies both sides at these numbers.

Is this legal, tax or farm-management advice?

No. It is arithmetic on numbers you type in. It does not know your county rents, your insurance, government payments, tax treatment or state lease law. Use extension-office rent surveys for local figures and talk to an attorney before signing a lease.
