How much to charge to replace a pool liner

A published “$2,999 for a 16×32” special prices the water. A residential vinyl liner is not the water. It is wall vinyl that does not scale with square feet, a hopper the coupon never counted, stairs that wrinkle, and often a new bead track. Matching the coupon on an 18×36 hopper oval is how a sold job becomes a two-day loss after the first wrinkle in the deep end.

The number a water-surface special cannot see

Take a 16×32 rectangle hopper, 20 mil solid, standard bead, no stairs. That is 96 lineal feet of wall and about 961 square feet of liner — on a 512 square-foot floor. The coupon says $2,999. Raise the same shop to an 18×36 oval with wedding-cake stairs and a patterned 27-mil sheet. The water is still “about an 18×36,” so the coupon barely moves. The liner did not scale that way: more wall, hopper extra, 85 extra square feet of stairs, and 14% pattern waste the 16×32 never had.

Put a new bead track on that oval. Track is lineal feet, not water surface. Overlap bead is coping work. Over-the-fence access is not “the same job plus a ladder.”

How to price it

  1. Measure length and width. Rectangle is the product. Oval is the ellipse.
  2. Count wall vinyl: perimeter times wall height (usually 42 inches). Add hopper extra. Add stairs. That sum is liner square feet.
  3. Order square feet is liner times one plus waste. Patterned prints waste more.
  4. Price mil (20 / 27 / 30), bead, track, cove, foam, a cover, pump-down, access, and the permit allowance.
  5. Load the wage, add materials and mobilization, mark overhead on direct cost, then divide by one minus the target margin. The walk-away price is total cost.
