This calculator vs. Paved

Paved is a newsletter advertising marketplace. A publisher lists their newsletter, advertisers browse and book placements against it, and the platform handles the matching, the booking workflow, and the payment — earning a share of what the sponsor pays. It is a sales channel: its job is to find you a sponsor and get the money to you.

This page is not a sales channel and never will be. It answers the question that comes before a sales channel: what is a defensible price range for your own list, right now — typed in your browser, with nothing signed up for and no inventory listed anywhere.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Paved?

They do different jobs, and most newsletter operators end up wanting both at different moments. Paved is a newsletter advertising marketplace: a publisher lists their newsletter, advertisers browse and book placements, and the platform handles the matching, the booking workflow, and the payment, earning a share of what the sponsor pays. This page is a single free form that does none of that. You type your subscriber count, average open rate, and niche, and it applies commonly cited CPM-per-1000-opens benchmark ranges to give a starting price range for a dedicated send, a primary in-issue placement, a secondary placement, and a classified or text link. No listing, no account, no inventory handed to anyone.

Do I have to list my newsletter on a marketplace just to find out what to charge?

No, and that is the specific gap this page exists to close. Marketplaces price newsletter inventory against opens rather than raw subscriber count, because a sponsor is paying for eyes on the ad rather than names on a list — but seeing those benchmark numbers applied to your own newsletter has usually meant signing up and listing first. This calculator applies the same opens-based logic to numbers you already have, in your browser, with nothing to sign up for and nothing listed. If you then decide a marketplace is the right sales channel for you, you go in knowing roughly what your inventory is worth.

Does this replace a marketplace's ad sales?

No. Finding sponsors, negotiating, sending the insertion order, and collecting the money are real work, and a marketplace's core value is doing that work for you in exchange for a share of the revenue. This page sells nothing and introduces you to nobody. It answers the pricing question that comes before all of it — the one that otherwise gets answered by guessing from a competitor's public rate card, or by quoting low because you have no reference point when a first sponsor inquiry lands in your inbox.
