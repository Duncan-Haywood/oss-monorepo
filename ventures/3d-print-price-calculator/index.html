Common questions

Why does the calculator inflate cost for a failure rate instead of just adding a flat buffer?

Because a failed print still burns filament, electricity, and printer hours before it gets scrapped, and the eventual successful print has to earn that back. On average, 1/(1 − failure rate) attempts are needed to land one sellable part, so material, electricity, and machine wear are multiplied by that factor rather than by a flat percentage guess.

Why is the marketplace fee subtracted from the price instead of added to the cost?

Because the fee is a percentage of whatever you charge, not a fixed dollar amount, so it cannot be added as a cost line the way filament or electricity can. The calculator solves price×(1 − fee%) − cost = price×(margin%) for price directly, so the number you see already clears the fee at your target margin.

Does post-processing labor or packaging get inflated by the failure rate too?

No. Post-processing time and packaging only ever happen once, on the part that actually sells — a failed print never reaches sanding or a shipping box. Only filament, electricity, and printer depreciation are multiplied, since those are what a failed attempt actually spends.
