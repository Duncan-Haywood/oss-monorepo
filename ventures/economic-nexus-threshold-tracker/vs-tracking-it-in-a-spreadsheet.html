This tracker vs. tracking thresholds in a spreadsheet

Every figure this tracker uses is public: each state's dollar threshold, its transaction-count test where one exists, and whether that test is combined with an AND or an OR. A spreadsheet can hold the same 51 rows. Transcribing them correctly, keeping the AND/OR logic right for the states that pair the two tests, and noticing when a state changes its rule is the part that gets skipped when you need an answer today.

Common questions

Can I just build a spreadsheet to track this instead?

Yes. Every figure this tracker uses — each state's dollar threshold, its transaction-count test where one exists, and whether that test is combined with an AND or an OR — is public information you can type into a spreadsheet yourself. Building it means transcribing all 51 jurisdictions correctly, including the roughly sixteen that pair a transaction count onto the dollar figure, and updating any cell a state changes (Illinois dropped its 200-transaction alternative entirely as of 2026-01-01).

What does a spreadsheet not do that this tracker does?

Keep the AND/OR logic right without you re-deriving it per state. Connecticut and New York require both the dollar figure and a transaction count together (AND) — most other transaction-count states need only one or the other (OR). A spreadsheet formula that treats every state the same way will misjudge Connecticut or New York specifically. This tracker also states outright when it needs a transaction count it doesn't have, rather than defaulting to a silent guess, and it already knows which states levy no sales tax at all so a large number there never reads as crossed.

Is a spreadsheet ever the better choice?

Yes, if you want to do more with the numbers than check a threshold — combine nexus tracking with other tax reporting, chart your trailing sales over time, or keep a running history from filing period to filing period. This tracker holds only what you've entered right now; a spreadsheet you build and save yourself can be extended to do anything you can formula your way into.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No account and no network requests — the tracker runs entirely in your browser. Whatever you enter is saved to this browser's local storage between visits and never sent to a server, the same as a spreadsheet file that never leaves your own computer.
