This calculator vs. a flat usage fee

"Just add 25% for usage" is not a price for a license — it is a price for one narrow scope, quietly applied to a worldwide, fully exclusive, multi-channel license.

What a flat usage-fee percentage is

A flat usage add-on — 20%, 25%, sometimes "double the day rate" — is the fastest way to quote a client without opening a spreadsheet: one round number, tacked onto the creative fee, easy to say out loud on a call.

The number behind it was set with one scope in mind — usually a short term, one city, a single low-value channel, and no exclusivity — and then reused for every license afterward, whether or not the next client wants a year of paid advertising across the whole country or a two-year worldwide exclusive.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. Common questions

Why does a flat usage-fee percentage get the price wrong?

Because usage-rights value scales with the channels a client can use the images in, the license term, the territory, exclusivity, and any rush need — not with the creative fee alone. A one-month, local, non-exclusive, single-channel license is a different scope from a two-year, worldwide, fully exclusive license across four channels, even when the creative fee on the shoot is identical. A flat percentage prices both the same and is wrong on one of them every time.

What does a flat usage-fee percentage usually leave out entirely?

Which channels the client can use the images in (web is worth less than paid advertising or outdoor/broadcast), how long the license runs, whether it covers one city or the whole world, whether it locks out competitors or everyone else entirely, and any rush turnaround. A round flat percentage — 20%, 25%, "double the day rate" — is set with one scope in mind, usually a narrow one, and has no separate line for any of those.

How much do channels, term, territory, and exclusivity actually add over a flat rate?

On this calculator's own worked examples, at a $1,200 creative fee: a narrow one-month, local, non-exclusive, web-only license walks away at $1,371.46, comfortably under a flat 25% usage add-on ($1,500.00). This calculator's own shipped default job — the same $1,200 creative fee, a 6-month national web license, non-exclusive — walks away at $1,725.00, $225.00 over that same flat 25% number. A 24-month worldwide, fully exclusive license across all four channels with a 20% rush walks away at $7,261.10, $5,761.10 over a flat 25% add-on on the same $1,200 creative fee.

Is a flat usage-fee percentage ever the right way to quote?

For something close to the narrow scope it was probably set on — a short term, one city, non-exclusive, a single low-value channel — a flat percentage can land close or leave room to spare, and on this calculator's own numbers that easy license clears a flat 25% add-on with over a hundred dollars to spare. The risk is a longer term, a wider territory, exclusivity, or extra channels stacking on top of each other, because a flat percentage has no way to see any of them until the client has already been quoted.

Does this calculator know what to charge in my market?

No. It has no data on going rates for photography or licensing in any market. It takes the creative fee, channels, term, territory, exclusivity, and rush need you enter and turns them into a usage-rights breakdown and total for the license you describe — the arithmetic a flat percentage skips, not a market survey.

Nothing here says or implies that quoting a flat usage-fee percentage is a bad-faith practice — it is a common, honest shortcut that works until the scope stops matching the license it was set on. This page describes where that shortcut breaks down, not a fault in anyone who uses it.
