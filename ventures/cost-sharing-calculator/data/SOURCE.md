# Data sources

## `talmud-ketubot-claims.csv` (real: the page's first example)

- **What it is:** the three marriage contracts of the Mishnah's estate
  problem: a man dies owing his three wives 100, 200 and 300 zuz, and the
  estate is worth 100, 200 or 300. The Mishnah's divisions are 33⅓ each;
  50, 75, 75; and 50, 100, 150.
- **Source:** Mishnah, Ketubot 10:4 (Babylonian Talmud, Ketubot 93a), a text
  of the 2nd century CE, in the public domain. The figures are as given and
  analysed by Aumann & Maschler (1985), "Game theoretic analysis of a
  bankruptcy problem from the Talmud", Journal of Economic Theory 36(2),
  195-213, doi:10.1016/0022-0531(85)90102-4, whose theorem is that each of
  these divisions is the nucleolus of the claims game.
- **Licence:** public domain (ancient text); the three numbers are facts.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-17; the paper's metadata were checked against
  Crossref (api.crossref.org) on that date. The tests assert the engine
  reproduces all three divisions.

## `shared-road.csv` and `three-towns-water.csv` (illustrative)

Made-up numbers, written for this tool on 2026-11-17 to show the road mode
and the coalition table. They describe no real road, household or town, and
the page labels them as made up.
