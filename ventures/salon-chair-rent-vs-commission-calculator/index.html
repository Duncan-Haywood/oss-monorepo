How it is calculated

Yearly services = weekly services times weeks worked; retail likewise; tips = the tip percentage of services. You keep the entered share of services and retail on commission, and all of them on chair rent. Take-home before tax = what you keep + tips, less supplies (percent of services), card fees (percent of services and retail), rent (weekly rent times weeks charged) and fixed costs (monthly times twelve). Tax = the self-employment rate you enter times positive income. Hourly = take-home over hours per week times weeks. The crossover is any weekly revenue where the two yearly take-homes are equal.

Not modelled: income tax, paid time off or benefits an employer may provide, unemployment and workers' compensation coverage, client ownership and the cost of building a book, and how the arrangement is classified for tax or labour law. Unverified: checked by hand calculation and unit tests only.

Common questions

What is the difference between chair rent and commission?

On commission the shop takes a share of each service (and usually supplies the product, booking and card processing) and you keep the rest. On chair or booth rent you pay the shop a fixed amount, usually weekly, and keep what you earn, paying your own supplies, card fees and insurance. Some shops mix the two: a lower rent plus a smaller commission.

Which pays more?

It depends on how full your book is. Rent costs the same in a slow week and a busy one, so it wins when you are consistently booked and loses when you are not. Enter your expected weekly services and the tool shows each arrangement's yearly take-home and the weekly revenue where the two pay the same.

Why does rent cost more than the weekly figure suggests?

Most chair rent runs fifty-two weeks a year, including vacations and sick weeks. If you work forty-eight weeks, four weeks of rent buy nothing. Enter the weeks of rent you are charged and the tool shows that cost.

What costs does the tool include?

What you keep of services, retail and tips, less supplies (as a percentage of services), card fees, weekly rent, fixed monthly costs such as insurance, licence and software, and a self-employment tax rate you supply. Commission arrangements usually have none of the supplies, card or rent costs; enter zero for what the shop pays.

Is chair rent the same as being an independent contractor?

Not automatically. How a worker is classified for tax and labour law depends on the facts of the working relationship, not on what the arrangement is called. This tool makes no classification determination; ask a tax professional or your state labour agency.

Does the tool say what a fair rent or split is?

No. It contains no benchmark data and states no market rate. Enter the numbers you were actually offered and compare them with what you know of your local market.
