      Common questions

      Why does a toilet pull or new cleanout cost more than an existing cleanout job?

      Access is priced as its own line because getting into the line changes the labor before the machine ever starts running: pulling and resetting a toilet, or cutting in a new cleanout, adds real hours a job that already has a cleanout doesn't need. Match the access option to what's actually on site rather than assuming every job starts the same way.

      Why does a “$149 drain special” price so much lower than this tool's number?

      A flat brochure price usually covers a short run from an existing cleanout with no roots, no grease, and no camera pass — extra footage past the included run, roots, grease, hose distance from the truck, and a camera warranty pass all add hours the flat price never counted. Enter your actual line feet and conditions to see what the special would need to charge to still be profitable.

      Does the camera pass replace a real sewer inspection or a home-sale scope report?

      No. This prices your labor and equipment time for running a camera, not a certified inspection report, a real-estate condition disclosure, or a code determination about the line's condition. Whatever the camera finds is still something you (or a licensed inspector) has to interpret and document separately.

      Is the footage-included assumption accurate for my trucks?

      The included footage and hose-run figures are common ballpark starting points, not a manufacturer spec for your specific jet unit or cable machine. Replace them with what your actual equipment carries so the “extra footage” charge kicks in at the right point.
