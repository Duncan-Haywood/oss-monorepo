How much commission to pay a location for arcade machines

Commission splits on a coin-op route are usually set once, by habit, and never looked at again. But what a location can afford to be paid depends on what its machines take, how often you visit and how far you drive. The same 50 percent can be a good deal at one stop and a loss at another.

The method

For each location, take the monthly gross, then subtract prizes, card fees, repairs, licences, machine wear and driving. Divide what remains, after the commission, by the hours the stop takes. That is profit per route hour. Set it equal to the hourly you want and solve for the commission: that is the most you can pay.

Example assumptions: a target of $35 an hour, $0.60 per mile, prizes at 8% of takings, 40% of takings by card at 3.5%, $18 a month repairs and $120 a year licence per machine, machines costing $3,500 over 7 years.

Common questions

What is a fair commission split for amusement machines?

There is no single fair split. Splits of 40 to 60 percent are common, but the right one for a given location is the highest commission that still earns you your target hourly, which depends on take, visits and miles. The worked example below shows how far apart those ceilings can be.

Why do some locations lose money even when they pay?

Because a visit costs the same whether the machines took much or little: the drive, the time on site, and per-machine repairs and licences. A low take spread over those fixed costs can go negative once the commission comes off.

When should I pull machines from a location?

When even a zero commission cannot reach your target hourly at the current take, visits and miles. Otherwise the calculator gives the commission to ask for.
