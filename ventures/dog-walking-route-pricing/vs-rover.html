This calculator vs. Rover's marketplace pricing and fees

Rover is a booking marketplace: you list your dog walking or pet sitting service, set your own price, and Rover surfaces you to pet owners searching in your area. It also publishes a national dog walking rates guide with typical price ranges by service and region as a starting reference. What it does not do is compute a price from your numbers — your drive time between stops, how many pets are on a visit, overnight on-call hours, your overhead, or a target margin — and every booking that goes through Rover is reduced by its own published service fee taken from the sitter's earnings, plus a separate booking fee Rover adds on top of what the owner pays.

That makes Rover the right tool for getting found by pet owners and seeing a rough market range. This calculator answers a different question: for your own time, route, and costs, what should one visit cost, and does your whole client list clear your income goal — whether those clients came from Rover, a referral, or your own website.

Common questions

Should I price on Rover or use this calculator?

Both answer different questions. Rover is a booking marketplace: it lists you to pet owners searching in your area, publishes national/regional rate averages as a starting point, and lets you set your own listed price on your profile. This calculator does not list you to anyone — it takes the numbers for a specific visit or route (your time, drive, mileage, extra pets, overnight on-call hours, and overhead) and computes the price that hits a target margin, then rolls a whole client list into a monthly revenue figure and the gap to an income goal. Use Rover to find clients; use this calculator to decide what to charge them, on or off the platform.

Does this calculator account for Rover's service fee?

Not by default, because it is built for pricing any client relationship, not one platform's terms. If you book through Rover, its published fee structure takes a service fee out of the sitter's earnings and adds a separate booking fee on top of the owner's payment, so a rate that looks fine on paper nets less once it clears Rover's cut. This calculator's optional "Payment processing fee" field grosses the price up for exactly that kind of deduction — set it to the percentage a platform or payment processor takes so the margin you type in is the margin you actually keep.

Does Rover have a tool like this one?

Not that we could find. Rover publishes a national dog-walking-rates guide with typical price ranges by service and region, which is useful as a market reference but is not computed from your own drive time, pet count, overnight on-call hours, or overhead, and it has no equivalent to rolling a client list up into a monthly income projection against a goal.
