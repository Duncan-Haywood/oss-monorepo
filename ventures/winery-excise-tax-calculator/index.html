Common questions

How much is the federal excise tax on wine?

Federal law taxes still wine up to 16% alcohol by volume at $1.07 per wine gallon, wine over 16% up to 21% at $1.57, over 21% up to 24% at $3.15, naturally sparkling wine at $3.40, artificially carbonated wine at $3.30 and hard cider at $0.226. The Craft Beverage Modernization Act then gives a credit against that tax: $1.00 per wine gallon on the first 30,000 wine gallons removed in a calendar year, $0.90 on the next 100,000 and $0.535 on the next 620,000.

How much federal tax is in a bottle of wine?

A 750 mL bottle holds about 0.198 wine gallons, so it carries about 21 cents of federal tax at the statutory $1.07 rate for still wine. After the $1.00 credit on a producer's first 30,000 gallons it carries about 1.4 cents. Sparkling wine carries about 67 cents before the credit.

What is the CBMA wine tax credit?

A credit against federal wine excise tax: $1.00 per wine gallon on the first 30,000 wine gallons removed in a calendar year, $0.90 on the next 100,000 and $0.535 on the next 620,000. Hard cider gets smaller credits of 6.2, 5.6 and 3.3 cents. The credit cannot be more than the tax, so it takes still wine from $1.07 to $0.07 on those first gallons.

Does a winery pay tax when it makes the wine?

No. Tax attaches when wine is removed from bonded premises for consumption or sale, including tasting-room and wine-club sales. Enter what you remove in a calendar year, not what you produce or have in tank.

What counts as hard cider?

For the lower $0.226 rate, a wine made from apples or pears with at least 0.5% and under 8.5% alcohol by volume and no more than 0.64 grams of carbon dioxide per 100 mL. Cider that falls outside these limits is taxed as wine.

Do wineries under common ownership each get the full credit?

No. Wineries and cideries under common control share one set of credit tiers. Enter your share as a percentage if you divide them.
