This calculator vs. AutoLeap's labor rate calculator

AutoLeap, a cloud-based shop management platform for independent auto repair shops, publishes its own free labor rate calculator as a lead-generation tool. Based on AutoLeap's own description of it, that calculator asks for three numbers — your effective labor rate (labor dollars collected last month, divided by labor hours sold), your highest-paid technician's hourly rate, and your shop's technician productivity percentage — and turns them into a recommended rate to charge and an affordable technician wage. It shows the result after you submit your name, email, phone number and business name.

That makes it a tool for a different step than this page: AutoLeap's calculator assumes you already know your effective labor rate and helps you decide what to charge from there; this page computes the effective labor rate itself, from twelve figures a shop already has on a P&L or a management-system report, with no contact form and no account, and splits the gap between your posted door rate and your collected rate into three pieces with different fixes: customer-pay discounting, warranty shortfall, and comeback or internal hours priced as forgone capacity.

AutoLeap also sells a paid shop management platform — repair orders, digital vehicle inspections, parts ordering, technician time clocks, scheduling and customer communication all run through it — and its own reporting separately surfaces an effective labor rate figure once a shop's activity lives inside the platform, the same category of comparison as a full shop management system generally.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or AutoLeap's labor rate calculator?

They start from different places. AutoLeap's free tool asks you to type in your effective labor rate as an input — alongside your top technician's hourly pay and your shop's productivity percentage — and it computes the posted rate you would need to charge, showing the result only after you submit your name, email, phone number and business name. This page computes the effective labor rate itself, from twelve figures most shops already have on a P&L or a management-system report, with no contact form and no account, and it also works out the posted rate a target gross profit would really require.

Does AutoLeap's tool split the gap the way this page does?

No. Based on AutoLeap's own published description of the tool, it takes your effective labor rate as a given and turns it into an affordable technician wage and a recommended rate to charge — it does not break the gap between your door rate and your collected rate into discounting, warranty shortfall, and comeback or internal hours the way this page does. AutoLeap's paid shop management platform separately reports an effective labor rate figure as part of its own dashboard, once a shop's repair orders run through it; that is a different comparison from the free calculator, covered above.
