Delivery App Margin Calculator vs. a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet multiplies a ticket by a commission rate fine — once you have written a formula for every channel's fee shape, and a second one, solved backwards, for the price or commission rate that matches dine-in. This page has both built in.

What each does

A restaurant's own spreadsheet can absolutely hold a ticket price, a commission rate, and a formula that multiplies them. That arithmetic is trivial and correct once every cell already has the right formula in it. What it does not do on its own is apply a different fee shape per channel: dine-in and pickup pay card processing only, a marketplace's commission is often a percentage that bundles payment processing while some plans split the two into separate rates, and packaging plus extra bagging minutes at your hourly wage are cash the restaurant spends on every delivery ticket whether or not the app takes a cut. Six channels, six fee shapes, six formulas to keep in sync as any one rate changes.

This calculator carries those six fee shapes built in. Enter one ticket's price, food cost, packaging cost, hourly wage, and the commission and processing rates from your own statements, and it reports what dine-in, pickup, each delivery app, and your own driver keep after fees — plus, solved for you rather than by hand, the delivery-menu price that would match dine-in profit on that ticket, the commission rate a channel would need at its current price to match it, and the monthly cash gap if you change neither. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Isn't a delivery app's cut just the ticket times a commission percentage?

The multiplication itself is trivial and a spreadsheet does it fine once you already know the commission rate. What a plain spreadsheet does not do on its own is apply a different fee shape per channel — dine-in and pickup pay card processing only, a marketplace commission is often bundled with payment processing while some plans split them, and packaging cost plus extra bagging minutes at your hourly wage are cash the restaurant spends whether or not the app takes a cut. Six channels, six formulas, kept in sync as any one rate changes.

Will a spreadsheet tell me the delivery-menu price that matches dine-in profit?

Not without solving the fee formula backwards, which most spreadsheets used for this are not set up to do. Profit is price minus commission minus processing minus packaging minus labor minus food cost; the price that reproduces a target profit is that equation rearranged for price, and the algebra changes depending on whether the fee is a straight percentage or has a fixed component too. This calculator solves it directly for every channel and also reports the commission rate a channel would need, at its current listed price, to match dine-in — the number worth having ready when a plan renewal comes up.

What happens if I mix up which fees apply to the listed price versus the payout?

Nothing catches it — a spreadsheet returns whatever the formula says, whether the commission and processing rates you wired in apply to the right base or not. This calculator applies each channel's commission, payment rate, and fixed fee to the same defined base for every channel, so a rate typed into the wrong cell in a spreadsheet cannot silently understate or overstate what one channel keeps relative to another.

Does this replace a restaurant's own P&L or accounting spreadsheet?

No. A spreadsheet or accounting system that tracks every day's sales, costs, and margin across a full month or year is still the right tool for that ongoing record. This page answers one narrower question at a time — what does today's ticket actually keep on each channel, and what price or commission rate would match dine-in — and nothing it computes is saved anywhere once you leave the page.
