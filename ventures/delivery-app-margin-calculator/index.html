What this does, and what it does not

The headline commission is not the decision

A 27% DoorDash plan on a $32 ticket is $8.64. Packaging and eight extra bagging minutes at $18/hour add another $3.65 the percentage comparison never shows. The decision number is the listed price that leaves you the same cash as dine-in — often around 45% higher on that ticket — and the monthly gap if you do not raise it. Since you cannot always set that price yourself, the table also solves the other side: the commission rate this path would need, at its current listed price, to match dine-in — the number worth having ready when a plan renewal or a lower-commission tier comes up. DoorDash fees on one restaurant ticket works the default example through in full, and DoorDash vs Uber Eats fees lines the three delivery apps up against each other on the same ticket.

What it deliberately leaves out

Fees are not the whole decision. An app fee is partly a payment for demand you would otherwise have to create, and this page has no way to know how many of those tickets you would have sold as dine-in or pickup. It also does not model refunds, marketing credits, DashPass discounts you absorb, or occupancy you lose when the kitchen is full of delivery tickets. Read the gap as one input, not the decision. It does not recommend a price, a contract, or a business model, and it is not tax, financial, or business advice.

Common questions

How much does DoorDash take from restaurants?

It depends on the plan and market. Marketplace commissions commonly land between about 15% and 30% of the order total, and some plans add a separate processing fee. The number that matters is not the headline percentage: packaging, extra bagging minutes, and the price you list on the app all change what you keep. Enter the rate on your own statement above — this page does the arithmetic on the numbers you give it.

Is DoorDash worth it for a restaurant?

On fees alone, dine-in almost always leaves more than a marketplace order at the same menu price, because the app takes a commission dine-in does not. Whether that is worth it depends on whether the app brings tickets you would not have sold otherwise, and on whether you mark the delivery menu up enough to keep the same cash. This calculator shows the dine-in-matching price and the monthly gap; it does not know your incremental demand.

How do I price a DoorDash menu to keep dine-in profit?

Take the profit you keep on the dine-in ticket, add packaging, extra labor, and any separate processing fee, then divide by one minus the commission rate. The last columns of the table do that from the rates you entered. A worked example is on the DoorDash fees page linked below.

How much does Uber Eats take from restaurants?

Like DoorDash, Uber Eats commission depends on the plan and market, and commonly lands in a similar rough range of the order total, sometimes with a separate marketing or processing fee depending on the tier. The Uber Eats preset above starts at 30% as an illustrative default — replace it with the rate on your own statement, then this page shows the same dine-in-matching price and monthly gap it computes for DoorDash.

How much commission does Grubhub charge restaurants?

Grubhub's commission also varies by plan and market, and is often bundled with marketing or delivery fees rather than one flat number. The Grubhub preset above starts at 25% as an illustrative default.
