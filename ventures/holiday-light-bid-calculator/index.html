    Common questions

    Why can a flyer advertise “$3 a foot, hang-only”?

    Because that is a real number for a real, narrow job: the first preset here, a one-story ranch, 140 feet of C9 on clips, customer-owned lights, no take-down. Hang-only skips the 45% take-down ratio and the full-service 50% ratio entirely — there is simply no second trip to price. Add a January take-down, a second story, or icicle lights and the flyer’s number stops describing your job; the gap is not padding, it is the trip the flyer left out.

    Why does hang-and-takedown cost more than twice a hang-only special?

    Because take-down is not half the hang labor, it is 45–50% of the hang-and-access hours billed as a second visit — a separate trip, separate travel, separate ladder-setting — on top of the original hang. Full service adds a third cost on top of that: contractor-owned lights at $2.60 a foot and a flat $95 off-season storage invoice, since nothing goes back to the customer’s garage.

    What does a second story actually add?

    1.6 access hours and $0.014 per foot before a single extra light is hung, plus a $165 lift invoice the calculator bills as a flat material cost. Three-story or a lift-required roofline doubles the access hours to 3.2, the per-foot premium to $0.028, and the lift invoice to $390. That is ladder-setting and working off height, not lineal footage — which is why the same 180 feet costs more on a colonial than a ranch.

    What changes between C9, icicle, and commercial-grade lights?

    C9 on clips is the base case: zero extra hours, zero extra material, because it is what the hang-hours-per-foot rate already assumes. Icicle adds 0.02 hours and $1.40 of material per foot for the denser clip-and-hook run. Commercial-grade LED C9 adds less labor — 0.008 hours per foot — but more material, $3.20 a foot, because the fixture itself costs more than the extra clipping time.

    How are peaks, trees, bushes, and columns priced?

    Each is its own line, not folded into the per-foot rate: a peak or gable adds 0.35 hours and $18 of material, a tree wrap adds 0.9 hours and $42, a bush or shrub net adds 0.25 hours and $14, and a column wrap adds 0.2 hours and $12. A timer or photocell adds 0.35 hours and a $28 invoice, and a steep-pitch roof adds a flat 0.8 access hours on top of the story premium. None of these move with eave footage, so a small house with six trees can cost more than a long, plain roofline.
