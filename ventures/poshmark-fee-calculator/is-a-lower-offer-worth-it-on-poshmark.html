Is a lower offer worth it on Poshmark?

Whenever you accept a lower offer or set a bundle discount, part of that discount comes out of Poshmark's commission and part comes out of your own payout — and how the split works depends entirely on which side of $15.00 you land on.

Above $15.00: Poshmark shares in every discount

In the 20% tier, commission is 20% of whatever the buyer actually pays. Drop the price and the commission drops with it — automatically, proportionally.

LineListed at $40Offer at $35
Commission (20%)$8.00$7.00
Payout$32.00$28.00

The $5 discount only costs $4.00 in payout — Poshmark absorbed the other dollar. Any discount that keeps both prices at $15 or more works this way: you only ever absorb 80% of it.

Below $15.00: the fee is fixed, so you absorb the whole discount

The flat $2.95 fee does not move once you are under $15.00, no matter how much lower the price goes. A discount that keeps both prices under $15 comes entirely out of your own pocket.

LineListed at $12Offer at $8
Commission (flat)$2.95$2.95
Payout$9.05$5.05

The full $4 discount lands in your payout, dollar for dollar — Poshmark's cut never moves.

Crossing the line: usually a one-time break, not a penalty

It is tempting to assume crossing under $15.00 makes things worse, since it feels like leaving one tier for a "cheaper item" tier. It is the opposite: twenty percent of anything at or above $14.75 is already at least $2.95, so the flat fee is never a bigger bite than the percent rate would have been at that price. Crossing the line is a one-time reduction in commission.

LineListed at $20Offer at $14
Commission$4.00 (20%)$2.95 (flat)
Payout$16.00$11.05

The sticker discount is $6.00. The actual payout drop is $4.95 — never more than the discount, because the commission itself fell by $1.05 on the way down.

Worked example: one cent can raise your payout

The most surprising case is right at the boundary. A $15.00 sale pays 20% commission. A $14.99 sale — one cent lower — pays the flat fee instead.

The one-cent-lower sale pays four cents more. If a buyer's offer lands one cent under $15.00, taking it can put more in your pocket than holding firm at $15.00 — the opposite of what most sellers assume.

Common questions

Does Poshmark share in a discount I offer?

Yes, but only above $15.00. In the 20% tier, commission is 20% of whatever the buyer pays, so a $5 discount only costs you $4 — Poshmark's cut shrinks by $1 automatically. Under $15.00 the fee is a fixed $2.95 no matter the price, so any discount there comes entirely out of your own payout.

Does accepting an offer that drops below $15 cost me more?

Usually less, not more. Twenty percent of anything at or above $14.75 is at least $2.95, so switching to the flat fee is a one-time reduction in commission. A $20 listing dropping to a $14 offer loses $6 in sale price but only $4.95 in payout, because the fee itself dropped by $1.05.

Can a lower price ever pay me more?

Yes, right at the boundary. A $15.00 sale pays 20% ($3.00 commission, $12.00 payout). A $14.99 sale — one cent lower — pays the flat $2.95 fee ($12.04 payout). The one-cent-lower sale nets four cents more.
