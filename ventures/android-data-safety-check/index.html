Android Data Safety Checker

Scans your AndroidManifest.xml permissions and Gradle dependencies for known analytics/ads/crash SDKs, maps each one to a Google Play Data safety category, and checks it against a plain list of the categories you've already declared in Play Console's App content > Data safety form -- so a missing declaration doesn't cost you a policy warning or a listing taken down for an inaccurate disclosure. Everything runs in this tab; nothing is uploaded. A command-line version for CI is in the same repository (node check.mjs . --declared declared.txt).

Common questions

Why does Google Play flag my app's Data safety declaration?

Most often because a permission your app requests, or an SDK it embeds (analytics, ads, crash reporting), collects a category of data your Play Console Data safety form doesn't declare. This tool scans your AndroidManifest.xml and Gradle files for the permissions and known SDKs that imply a category, and tells you which one is missing.

Does this tool read or fill in the Play Console form for me?

No. Play Console has no public file format this tool reads from or writes to. You tell it what you've already declared (a plain list of categories, or ticking the checkboxes in the browser tool), and it compares that against what your manifest and Gradle files suggest you collect. You still make every declaration decision in Play Console yourself.

Is my project uploaded anywhere?

No. The browser tool reads the files you select using the File API and never sends them anywhere -- everything happens in memory in your tab. The CLI never makes a network call either.

Does a clean report mean Google Play will accept my declaration?

No. This is pattern-matching against known permission names and SDK dependency coordinates -- it cannot see what a permission is actually used for, dynamically loaded code, or a compiled third-party library's real behaviour, and Google can change its Data safety categories or a given SDK's practices at any time. Always confirm against Play Console's current Data safety form and each SDK's own privacy documentation before you submit.

Evidence, not a determination. This tool reports what pattern-matching found in the files you selected and what you ticked as already declared. It cannot see what a permission is actually used for, dynamically loaded code, or a third-party SDK's real runtime behaviour, and Google can add categories or change a listed SDK's practices at any time. A clean report is not a guarantee Play Console will accept your submission -- verify against Google Play's current Data safety documentation before you submit, and decide the actual collection purpose and sharing status yourself -- this tool never picks on your behalf.
