The rules, and what they leave out

  • Creditor identifier: shape, SEPA country and the mod-97 check digits, ignoring the three-character business code as the scheme specifies.
  • IBANs and amounts: ISO 13616 check digits and country length; amounts as exact cents with no floating point. 1.250,00 and 1,250.00 are read; an ambiguous 1,234 is refused.
  • Mandates: each row needs a reference (quoted exactly as signed, never rewritten, 35 characters at most) and a signature date that is not in the future or after the collection date. One mandate on two IBANs is an error; a mandate over 36 months old is flagged, since an idle mandate lapses.
  • Sequence types: FRST, RCUR, OOFF and FNAL, also read as first, recurring, one-off and final. One payment block is written per type, each with its own count and control sum, and the group header carries the grand total.
  • Dates: a weekend, past or next-business-day collection date is flagged. Slash dates that could swap day and month are refused, not guessed.
  • Text: names and references are transliterated to the SEPA character set, cut to 70 and 140 characters, and XML-escaped, each change reported.
  • Not done: collecting money, bank connections, whether a mandate exists, was signed or was registered for B2B, pre-notification to debtors, returns, refunds and mandate amendments, pain.008.001.08, non-euro payments, bank-specific limits and cut-off times. Reference data is read from memory of public sources; confirm against your bank's file specification.

Common questions

How do I turn a member or tenant list into a SEPA direct debit file?

Export the list as CSV with the debtor's name, IBAN, amount, the mandate reference and the date the debtor signed the mandate. Enter your own name, collection IBAN, creditor identifier and the collection date, then download the pain.008 XML and upload it in your business banking portal's direct debit section.

What do I need before I can collect by SEPA direct debit?

A SEPA creditor identifier from your country's issuer (in Germany the Bundesbank, elsewhere usually the central bank or your bank), a business account that your bank has enabled for direct debits, and a signed mandate from every debtor with a unique mandate reference. This tool makes the file; it does not get you any of those.

What are FRST, RCUR, OOFF and FNAL?

The sequence type says where a collection sits on its mandate: FRST is the first of a series, RCUR a later one, OOFF a one-off with no series, and FNAL the last. A payment block can hold only one type, so the tool writes one block per type and totals each block separately.

CORE or B2B: which scheme should I choose?

CORE works on consumers' and businesses' accounts and gives consumers a right to a refund for eight weeks. B2B works only on business accounts, has no refund right, and needs each debtor to have registered the mandate with their own bank first. If you are unsure, your debtors are consumers and you want CORE.

What happens if a row has an error?

No file is written at all. Every problem is listed with its line number: a bad IBAN or amount, a missing mandate reference or signature date, an unknown sequence type, one mandate on two accounts. Warnings, such as a character the SEPA set does not allow, are fixed and reported but do not block the file.

Is the debtor list uploaded anywhere?

No. The conversion runs in your browser tab and a test in the code asserts there are no network calls. Your customers' bank details and your own IBAN never leave your device.

What does this not do?

It does not collect money, connect to your bank, check that a mandate exists or is valid, handle mandate amendments, pre-notification, returns or refunds, or produce pain.008.001.08 or non-euro files. Your bank remains the final validator.
