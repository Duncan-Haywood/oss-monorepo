Email Authentication Checker vs. checking DNS records by hand with dig

Every developer and admin already has this move: run dig txt against a domain and read the record back. For a single top-level SPF or DMARC record, that is enough. This checker exists for the three things one dig call cannot show: the true recursive cost of an SPF include chain, which of many possible DKIM selector names your provider actually uses, and whether DMARC enforcement is strict enough for BIMI to display.

A single well-aimed dig txt call is still the fastest way to read a record you already know the name of, and this checker's own lookups are not doing anything dig couldn't -- they are the same kind of DNS queries, just walked recursively through every SPF include, tried against nine DKIM selector names, and cross-checked between BIMI and DMARC, instead of leaving that walking and cross-checking to be done, and remembered, by hand.

Common questions

Can't I just run dig txt myself?

Yes, for the most obvious case: dig txt yourdomain.com will show your top-level SPF record, and dig txt _dmarc.yourdomain.com will show your DMARC policy. For that specific, most-obvious read, one command each is enough. The problem is the other three checks this tool does are not visible in any single dig response -- they only show up when you compare several DNS queries against each other, which is where running dig by hand stops being enough evidence.

How do I know which DKIM selector to check?

You don't, from DNS alone -- the selector is chosen by whichever provider sends your mail, and there is no single fixed name to dig for. By hand, that means guessing a selector, running dig txt selector._domainkey.yourdomain.com, getting NXDOMAIN, and trying another -- and NXDOMAIN on a guess you invented yourself proves nothing about whether DKIM is actually configured, only that you guessed a name nobody published a key at.

Why doesn't a clean top-level SPF record mean SPF actually passes?

Because RFC 7208 counts the whole recursive chain, not just your own record. A record with three include: mechanisms looks short in a single dig txt call, but each of those includes can itself contain further include:/redirect= mechanisms, and the 10-DNS-lookup budget is spent across the entire chain. Confirming you're under the limit means recursively dig-ing every nested include and keeping a running total by hand -- skip one level and you can be well over 10 while a single top-level read looks fine.

Can I tell from DNS alone whether BIMI will actually display?

Not from the BIMI record by itself. A dig txt default._bimi.yourdomain.com can return a perfectly valid-looking record while BIMI stays invisible in Gmail or Yahoo, because both require DMARC enforcement to be strict enough first -- p=quarantine or p=reject at pct=100. Answering "will this display" means reading the BIMI record and the DMARC record separately and cross-checking one against the other, not reading either alone.
