Does married filing separately get the auto loan interest deduction?

Yes. Married filing separately (MFS) is not excluded from this deduction.

That surprises a lot of filers, because two other new 2025 deductions this portfolio has calculators for -- the "no tax on tips" / "no tax on overtime" deduction and the senior deduction -- both exclude MFS entirely. The auto loan interest deduction runs the opposite way: each spouse who files separately gets their own independent $10,000 cap.

How an MFS spouse's cap works

Each MFS spouse tests their own modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) against the single-filer thresholds -- not the higher joint thresholds -- and claims the deduction on their own separate return:

ItemMFS (per spouse)Married filing jointly (combined)
Cap before phase-out$10,000, on each spouse's own return$10,000 total, shared by both spouses
Phase-out starts at MAGI$100,000 (each spouse's own MAGI)$200,000 (combined household MAGI)
Fully phased out at MAGI$150,000 (each spouse's own MAGI)$250,000 (combined household MAGI)
Reduction rate$200 per $1,000 (or part) over the thresholdSame $200 per $1,000 (or part) over the threshold

Worked example: same household income, two filing statuses, very different result

Two spouses, Spouse A and Spouse B, each finance their own eligible vehicle and each pay $8,000 of qualifying interest in the year. Their combined household MAGI is $240,000 either way -- only the filing status changes.

ScenarioMAGI testedCap after phase-outDeduction
Spouse A, filing MFS, MAGI $120,000$120,000 vs. single-filer $100k-$150k range$10,000 − $4,000 = $6,000$6,000
Spouse B, filing MFS, MAGI $120,000$120,000 vs. single-filer $100k-$150k range$10,000 − $4,000 = $6,000$6,000
MFS combined household deduction$240,000 combined—$12,000
Same couple, filing jointly, combined MAGI $240,000$240,000 vs. joint $200k-$250k range$10,000 − $8,000 = $2,000 (one shared cap)$2,000

At this specific combined income, filing separately produces a combined deduction six times larger than filing jointly -- because each MFS spouse's MAGI is tested against the lower, single-filer phase-out range independently, rather than the household's full combined MAGI being tested against the higher joint range all at once. Reverse the numbers -- one spouse earning most of the household income -- and the comparison can flip. There is no substitute for running your own numbers both ways.

Common questions

Does married filing separately (MFS) qualify for the auto loan interest deduction?

Yes. Unlike the separate 'no tax on tips' and 'no tax on overtime' deductions, which exclude MFS entirely, the auto loan interest deduction does not exclude MFS. Each spouse who files separately gets their own $10,000 cap on their own return.

What income threshold applies to an MFS return?

The same $100,000-$150,000 MAGI range used for single filers and heads of household -- not the $200,000-$250,000 joint range. Each MFS spouse's own MAGI is tested against the single-filer thresholds independently.

Can filing separately produce a bigger combined deduction than filing jointly?

Yes, in some cases. Two spouses each earning $120,000 MAGI (a combined $240,000) and each paying $8,000 of qualifying interest on their own vehicle loan can deduct $6,000 apiece filing MFS -- $12,000 combined. The same couple filing jointly, at the same $240,000 combined MAGI, shares one cap reduced to $2,000 total for the household -- a sixth of the MFS combined result.

Why is the MFS rule opposite to this portfolio's other 2025 deduction calculators?

Congress wrote each OBBBA deduction with its own filing-status rules; there is no single consistent MFS treatment across them. The 'no tax on tips' and 'no tax on overtime' deductions exclude MFS filers entirely. The senior deduction also excludes MFS. The auto loan interest deduction does not -- it is the one sibling calculator in this portfolio where an MFS filer is not automatically shut out.

Does this mean married couples should always file separately for this deduction?

Not necessarily. Filing status affects your entire return, not just this one deduction -- other credits, deductions, and tax brackets change with it too. This page and its calculator only show the auto loan interest math for each scenario; compare your full return both ways, or ask a qualified tax professional, before choosing a filing status because of this deduction alone.
