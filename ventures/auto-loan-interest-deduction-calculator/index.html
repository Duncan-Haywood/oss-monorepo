What's the new car loan interest deduction worth?

A new federal deduction applies to tax years 2025 through 2028: up to $10,000/year of interest paid on a loan for a new, US-assembled, personal-use vehicle — but only if eight eligibility conditions are all met, and it shrinks once your income clears $100,000 (single/HoH/MFS) or $200,000 (joint). This checks every condition and estimates the deduction, including the married-filing-separately rule most people assume works the opposite way it actually does.

1. Filing status & income

MAGI here is modified adjusted gross income — for most filers with no foreign income or certain exclusions, this is close to the AGI on last year's return, adjusted for this year's income. Married filing separately uses the same $100,000 threshold as a single filer, applied to each spouse's own return.

2. Loan interest

This is the interest portion of your loan payments this year — not your monthly payment and not the vehicle's price. Your lender's year-end statement (Form 1098, where applicable) or loan amortization schedule shows this figure.

3. Eligibility checklist

All eight must be true. If even one is not, none of the interest above qualifies for this deduction — the deduction is not prorated for a partially-eligible vehicle or loan. The cap itself does not double for a joint return even when every item below is met — see the results below and the FAQ.

Common questions

How much car loan interest can I deduct?

Up to $10,000 per year of qualified interest paid on a loan for a new, personal-use passenger vehicle finally assembled in the United States, for tax years 2025 through 2028. This is a deduction on the INTEREST you pay, not the price of the vehicle or the loan amount, and it phases out for higher earners -- see the phase-out question below.

Does the $10,000 cap double for married filing jointly?

No. Unlike some other new deductions, a married couple filing jointly shares ONE $10,000 cap between them, not $10,000 each. Married filing separately works differently -- see the next question.

Can I claim this if I file married filing separately?

Yes -- and this is the detail most people assume is wrong, because this portfolio's own separate 'no tax on tips and overtime' deduction calculator excludes MFS entirely. This auto loan interest deduction does NOT exclude MFS: each spouse gets their own $10,000 cap on their own separate return, phased out using the same $100,000-$150,000 MAGI thresholds a single filer uses.

What is the income phase-out?

The deduction is reduced by $200 for every $1,000 (or part of $1,000) that your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) exceeds $100,000 (single, head of household, or married filing separately) or $200,000 (married filing jointly). It reaches exactly $0 at $150,000 MAGI (single/HoH/MFS) or $250,000 MAGI (joint).

What vehicles qualify?

A NEW car, minivan, van, SUV, pickup truck, or motorcycle with a gross vehicle weight rating under 14,000 lbs, with final assembly in the United States, bought (not leased) for personal use with a loan secured by a first lien on the vehicle. RVs and campers do not qualify even when used as a primary residence. A salvage-titled vehicle, a vehicle bought for scrap or parts, a fleet purchase, and a vehicle used in a trade or business are all excluded.
