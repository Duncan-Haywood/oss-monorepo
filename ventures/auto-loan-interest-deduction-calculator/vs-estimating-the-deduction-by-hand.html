This calculator vs. doing it by hand

The auto loan interest deduction looks like simple arithmetic — a cap, a phase-out, a checklist. The problem is that two of its rules run opposite to what people assume after reading about a similar 2025 deduction, and a quick by-hand estimate tends to copy the wrong assumption over.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake estimating this deduction by hand?

Assuming married filing separately is excluded, because this portfolio's own 'no tax on tips and overtime' deduction excludes MFS and people generalize that rule to every new 2025 deduction. This one does not exclude MFS -- each spouse gets their own $10,000 cap on their own return.

What's the second most common mistake?

Assuming the $10,000 cap doubles for a joint return, the way some other per-filer caps do. It does not -- a married couple filing jointly shares one $10,000 limit between them, not $10,000 each.

What eligibility condition gets missed most often?

Final assembly location. A vehicle being 'made in America' by brand reputation is not the same as its specific VIN showing US final assembly -- many models built by US and foreign brands alike are assembled in Mexico, Canada, or elsewhere, and a by-hand estimate often assumes eligibility from the brand name instead of checking the window sticker or VIN.

What about the MAGI phase-out?

A by-hand estimate often forgets the excess MAGI rounds UP to the next $1,000, not down -- so $100,001 of MAGI already costs the first $200 of the deduction, the same as $100,999 does.

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page knows your full return -- other deductions, credits, or state conformity.
