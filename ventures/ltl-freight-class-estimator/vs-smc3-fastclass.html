This calculator vs. SMC³ FastClass

Two different starting points: FastClass looks up the exact NMFC item for a commodity you can already name. This page estimates the density-based class band for a pallet you have not classified yet — the input FastClass needs before it can answer anything.

What each tool is for

FastClass, from SMC³, is a licensed software product — installed or accessed online — built on the official National Motor Freight Classification (NMFC) data. Its job is exact-item lookup: given a commodity, it returns the NMFC item number, the class or classes that apply, and the packaging, sub-group, and rule notes that go with it, described on SMC³'s own FastClass product page. It is sold as an ongoing licence to carriers, logistics providers, and shippers, and it is the closest thing the LTL industry has to an authoritative classification lookup for a commodity you can already name.

This calculator does not have a commodity database and does not look up an NMFC item — that is not the job it does. It estimates the density-based class band from the outside dimensions, weight, and piece count of a pallet or crate, which is the planning question that comes before a commodity has an NMFC item decided, or before a shipper wants to sanity-check what a carrier's own answer should land near. It runs free in any browser, needs no licence and no login, and nothing typed in is uploaded anywhere.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or SMC³ FastClass?

They answer different questions at different points in the shipment. FastClass, from SMC³, is a licensed software product built on the official National Motor Freight Classification (NMFC) data — its job is to look up the exact NMFC item, class, and packaging rules for a known commodity, and it is sold to carriers, brokers, and shippers as an ongoing subscription. This page has no commodity database and does not look up an NMFC item at all. It estimates the density-based class band from dimensions, weight, and piece count you type in — the input FastClass needs before it can look anything up — for free, instantly, in the browser, with no login and no licence. A shipper who already knows the NMFC item for their commodity should look it up in FastClass or ask their carrier; a shipper sizing a pallet before that item is decided, or sanity-checking what a carrier's answer should land near, is in the right place here.
