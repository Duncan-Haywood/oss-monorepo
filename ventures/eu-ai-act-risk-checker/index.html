What it does, and what it leaves to you

    
    
      
  • Tiers: Article 5 prohibited practices; Annex III and Annex I high-risk systems; Article 50 transparency duties for chatbots, synthetic content and deepfakes; everything else minimal. General-purpose model duties (Articles 53 to 55) are added when you say you provide one.
    • 
      
  • Article 6(3): an Annex III system is treated as not high-risk only when it does a narrow or preparatory task and does not profile people. That is the provider's documented call and authorities can challenge it.
    • 
      
  • Not decided: whether your system fits an Annex III entry as the Commission's guidelines read it, harmonised standards, national rules on enforcement, whether you become a provider by modifying a system (Art 25), sector law and GDPR, and any amendment adopted after this page was written, including the proposed Digital Omnibus delay of high-risk dates.

Common questions

    
Is my AI system high-risk under the EU AI Act?

    
It is high-risk if it is a safety component of a product under the Annex I harmonisation laws, or if its use is listed in Annex III: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services such as credit scoring and insurance, law enforcement, migration, and justice and democratic processes. A provider can show an Annex III system is not high-risk under Article 6(3) if it only does a narrow or preparatory task and does not profile people.

    
What AI practices are banned?

    
Article 5 bans manipulative techniques, exploiting vulnerabilities, social scoring, crime prediction based only on profiling, untargeted facial-image scraping, emotion recognition at work and in schools, biometric categorisation by sensitive attributes, and real-time remote biometric identification in public for law enforcement apart from narrow exceptions. These have applied since 2 February 2025.

    
Does the AI Act apply to me if I am outside the EU?

    
Yes if you place a system on the EU market, or if the output of your system is used in the EU. Deployers established in the EU are covered wherever the provider is based.

    
What is the difference between a provider and a deployer?

    
A provider develops an AI system, or has it developed, and puts it on the market or into service under its own name. A deployer uses a system under its authority in a professional setting. Most of the heavy duties for high-risk systems fall on providers; deployers have oversight, logging, worker information and in some cases fundamental rights assessment duties.
