What it does

Track the waiver each payment needs (conditional or unconditional, progress or final), prefilled from the job and the amount, and marked sent. Crew plan and up.

  • Prints the state's statutory form where we have checked the text against the statute: Arizona, California, Florida, Georgia, Michigan, Mississippi, Nevada, Texas, Utah and Wyoming, plus Missouri's residential final waiver and Massachusetts' partial waiver and subordination of lien (its pay-app lines filled from the application); a general form in states with no statutory form
  • Once a payment arrives, a reminder for the unconditional waiver you now owe, for the amount actually paid
  • The lien deadline on every job (every plan): the last day to record a mechanics lien in Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oregon, Texas, Utah, Virginia or Washington, from the property's state and your last day on site, with the statute cited, and a warning on the cash page and in the weekly email 45 days ahead on any job that still owes you money
  • Public jobs (every plan): on a federal job, the Miller Act payment bond dates in place of a lien date (the 90-day notice to the prime and the one-year limit on the suit, 40 U.S.C. § 3133(b)), and in Crew and up the 90-day notice itself drafted from the job (the unpaid amount with retainage, the sub you worked for, your last day) and printed as a PDF; on a state or local job, the state's payment bond claim steps (the notices and the suit limit) dated from your last day and the public body's acceptance in Texas, Florida, New York, Virginia, Washington, Arizona, North Carolina and Ohio, and your retainage checked against the state's cap on public work in 16 states, with the statute linked
  • Preliminary notices from the job page where the statute sets the wording or contents: California (private works), Florida's Notice to Owner, Texas' notices of claim and Wyoming so far, prefilled from the job
  • Texas: the § 53.056 notice of claim for unpaid labor or materials and the § 53.057 notice of claim for unpaid retainage, word for word, with the 15th-of-the-month date for each unpaid month (rolled past weekends and Texas legal holidays), the retainage notice's 30 days and the lien affidavit dates; the months read from the job's unpaid pay apps, a log of each notice sent, and a reminder on the cash page and in the weekly email before each month's date; save the day your contract (or the GC's) ended and the retainage notice and retainage lien affidavit get their own reminder while retainage is held
  • Florida: the § 713.06 Notice to Owner word for word, with the 45-day last day to serve it and the 40-day last day to mail it so service counts on mailing (§ 713.18), from the day you first furnished work; record each notice and copy served (owner, contractor, the notice of commencement's designee) with how and when it went, see when each counts as served, and get a reminder on the cash page and in the weekly email until the owner has one
  • California: record each preliminary notice you give, see the 20-day deadline from the day you first furnished work, get a reminder until the owner and GC have one, print the § 8118 proof of notice declaration, and keep the mailing receipt and return receipt with each notice
  • We don't tell you which waiver to sign or what it means for your rights: that is a question for your attorney
