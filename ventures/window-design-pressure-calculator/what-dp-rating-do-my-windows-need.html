What DP rating do my windows need?

A plan reviewer asks for "design pressures for all exterior windows and doors", a dealer asks what DP to quote, and the answer is not one number for the house. This page works one house through ASCE 7-16, the edition the 2018 and 2021 IBC and IRC adopt, the way the calculator does it.

The house

Two storeys near the coast, basic wind speed V = 140 mph, exposure C (open terrain), mean roof height 22 ft, a 6/12 roof, 32 ft deep, enclosed, flat site. Seven lines of openings, from a 30 × 18 in stair transom to a 16 ft garage door.

1. Velocity pressure at the roof height

For exposure C, Table 26.10-1 gives Kh = 2.01 (22/900)^(2/9.5) = 0.920. With Kzt = 1 (flat site), Kd = 0.85 for components and cladding and Ke = 1:

qh = 0.00256 × 0.920 × 1 × 0.85 × 1 × 140² = 39.24 psf.

The same house in a suburb (exposure B) has Kh = 0.641 and qh = 27.35 psf: exposure alone changes every pressure on this page by 30%.

2. Which openings are near a corner

Figure 30.3-1 calls a strip of width a at each wall corner zone 5. a is the smaller of 10% of the least horizontal dimension (3.2 ft) and 40% of h (8.8 ft), but at least 3 ft: a = 3.2 ft. The bedroom window 1.5 ft from the corner and the garage door 2 ft from it are zone 5; the rest are zone 4.

3. Pressure coefficients by size

The wall coefficient falls with the logarithm of the opening's area between 10 and 500 ft². The 3 × 5 ft bedroom window (15 ft², zone 5) gets (GCp) = +0.969 and −1.338; the 3.75 ft² transom keeps the full +1.0 and −1.4; the 112 ft² garage door drops to +0.815 and −1.029. The roof is steeper than 10°, so there is no 10% reduction.

4. Net pressure and the DP

An enclosed building takes (GCpi) = ±0.18, in whichever direction adds. For the bedroom window:

p− = 39.24 × (−1.338 − 0.18) = −59.6 psf strength level, and 0.6 × that = 35.74 psf allowable-stress. Window ratings are compared with the allowable-stress number, so the window needs DP 35.8 (rounded up). Suction governs: the positive side needs only 27.1.

5. The debris region changes the enclosure

At 140 mph the site is in a wind-borne debris region (Section 26.12.3.1). If the glazing is impact-rated or shuttered the building stays enclosed and the middle column applies. If not, it is designed as partially enclosed, (GCpi) = ±0.55, and every opening needs about 9 psf more, the last column. Either way the debris region adds a cost: impact glass or shutters, or higher-rated units everywhere.

Common questions

Do all the windows on a house need the same DP rating?

No. Openings near a corner (zone 5) and small openings take higher suction. In the worked example the highest need is DP 37.3 for a small transom near a corner and the lowest DP 27.2 for a sliding door mid-wall, on the same house.

How much does a partially enclosed building raise the DP?

A lot. Moving the internal pressure coefficient from ±0.18 to ±0.55 adds 0.37 qh to every opening's net pressure. In the worked example the bedroom window goes from DP 35.8 to DP 44.5.
