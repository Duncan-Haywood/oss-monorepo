How it is calculated

Everything follows ASCE/SEI 7-16, the edition adopted by the 2018 and 2021 International Building Code and International Residential Code, using Chapter 30 Part 1 for buildings with a mean roof height of 60 ft or less. The velocity pressure at the mean roof height is qh = 0.00256 Kh Kzt Kd Ke V² (Eq. 26.10-1), with Kh from the Table 26.10-1 formula (never below its 15 ft value), Kd = 0.85 for components and cladding (Table 26.6-1), Ke from the ground elevation (Table 26.9-1, 1.0 if left blank) and Kzt from Figure 26.8-1 for a ridge, escarpment or hill that meets the height and steepness conditions of Section 26.8.1.

Each opening's effective wind area is its width times its height. Figure 30.3-1 gives the wall pressure coefficient (GCp) for zone 4 and zone 5 at 10 ft² and 500 ft², varying with the logarithm of the area between them, reduced by 10% when the roof slope is 10° or less. An opening closer to a corner than a is zone 5. The net pressure is p = qh [(GCp) − (GCpi)] (Eq. 30.3-1), with (GCpi) = ±0.18 for an enclosed or partially open building and ±0.55 for a partially enclosed one (Table 26.13-1), taken in the direction that adds, and never below the 16 psf minimum of Section 30.2.2. The DP a unit needs is the larger of 0.6 p in each direction, the allowable-stress level that window and door ratings are compared with, rounded up to 0.1 psf.

Not modelled: the wind speed itself (you enter it), ASCE 7-22 (IBC and IRC 2024), buildings over 60 ft (Part 3), open buildings, the effective-width rule that can enlarge the area of a long, narrow unit (this tool uses the plain area, which is conservative), roof openings such as skylights, parapets and overhangs, the IRC's tabulated alternative (Table R301.2.1(1)), the impact-test requirements themselves, and the glass, frame and anchorage checks a product approval covers. Unverified: checked against hand calculations of each equation and against the rounded Kz values of Table 26.10-1 in its unit tests, not against a commercial program or a sealed calculation.

Common questions

What DP rating do my windows need?

One at least as large as the allowable-stress design pressure on that window, in both directions. ASCE 7-16 gives the strength-level pressure p = qh [(GCp) − (GCpi)]; the window's rating is compared with 0.6 p. In the example on this page, a 140 mph exposure C house, a 3 × 5 ft bedroom window near a corner needs DP 35.8, and the same house's kitchen window away from the corners needs DP 29.9.

What are wall zones 4 and 5?

Wind pulls hardest on a wall near its corners. ASCE 7-16 Figure 30.3-1 calls the strip of width a at each corner zone 5 and the rest of the wall zone 4. a is 10% of the building's least horizontal dimension or 40% of the mean roof height, whichever is smaller, but not under 4% of the least dimension or 3 ft. In the example a is 3.2 ft.

Why does a bigger window get a lower pressure?

Gusts are small. The peak pressure averaged over a large area is lower than over a small one, so Figure 30.3-1 lowers (GCp) as the effective wind area grows from 10 to 500 square feet. A garage door therefore has a lower pressure per square foot than a transom on the same wall, though its total load is far higher.

What is a wind-borne debris region?

Where hurricanes throw debris into windows. Under ASCE 7-16 Section 26.12.3.1 it covers sites with a basic wind speed of 140 mph or more, and sites at 130 mph or more within 1 mile of the coastal mean high water line. Glazing there must be impact-resistant or shuttered, or the building is designed as partially enclosed, which raises the internal pressure coefficient from ±0.18 to ±0.55.

Is the DP rating the same as the strength-level pressure?

No. ASCE 7-16 wind speeds are ultimate (strength-level) speeds, and window and door ratings under AAMA/WDMA/CSA 101/I.S.2/A440 are compared with allowable-stress design pressures, 0.6 times the strength-level number. This tool shows both.

Does this replace an engineer or the building official?

No. It does the Chapter 26 and 30 arithmetic on the numbers you enter, so you can order windows and check a quote or a submittal. The building official or the engineer of record sets the design pressures; check which edition your jurisdiction adopted, since IBC and IRC 2024 move to ASCE 7-22.
