How to quote CNC turning and lathe parts

A turned part's quote comes down to three numbers: the cycle time, the setup, and how much bar the order really uses, which is the easiest of the three to undercount. Here is the method, a worked example at 10, 100 and 500 pins, and the mistakes that lose money on jobs you win.

The method in six steps

  1. Read the drawing for what changes the price. Material and bar size, the diameters and their tolerances, surface finish, threads, cross holes and flats (a second operation or live tooling), concentricity and runout callouts, and the finish or plating. A missing material grade or finish is a question to the customer, not a guess.
  2. Pick the bar and the cut length. The smallest standard bar that cleans up the largest diameter. Each part takes its finished length plus the part-off tool's width and a facing allowance off the bar.
  3. Count parts per bar. Parts per bar = (bar length − the remnant the chuck or bar feeder leaves) ÷ cut length, rounded down. Bars for the order = parts to start ÷ parts per bar, rounded up.
  4. Time each cut. rpm = surface speed (sfm) × 12 ÷ (π × diameter); minutes = length of cut ÷ (feed per rev × rpm), times the passes. Roughing passes = radial stock to remove, less what you leave to finish, ÷ depth of cut, rounded up. Add facing, finishing, drilling, threading and the part-off, then the time between cuts: tool indexes, rapids and the bar pull, timed on your own machines.
  5. Add setup, secondary operations and outside processes. Loading the program, setting tools and proving out the first part once per job; deburring, inspection, any milling after the lathe; plating, passivation or heat treat with their lot minimums.
  6. Add fallout and markup, then quote every quantity. Start enough parts to ship the good ones, and repeat the sum at each break, since the setup, the first bar and the vendor's minimum spread differently at each.

A worked example

A stainless pin: 303 stainless round bar, 1-1/4 in, faced, turned from 1.25 in to 1 in over 2.5 in, parted off at 2.875 in long, deburred, inspected and passivated by a vendor. Every number here is made up to show the arithmetic; they are not recommended speeds, feeds, prices or rates for any material, machine or region. Use your own tooling maker's data and your own timing.

The cycle time

  • Face the end: 400 sfm on 1.25 in is 1222 rpm; 0.625 in radial at 0.006 in/rev = 0.085 min.
  • Roughing passes: (1.25 − 1) ÷ 2 = 0.125 in of radial stock, less 0.01 in left to finish, is 0.115 in at 0.06 in a pass: 2 passes.
  • Rough the diameter: 400 sfm on 1.25 in is 1222 rpm; 2 passes × 2.5 in long at 0.008 in/rev = 0.511 min.
  • Finish the diameter: 500 sfm on 1 in is 1910 rpm; 2.5 in long at 0.004 in/rev = 0.327 min.
  • Part off: 250 sfm on 1.25 in is 764 rpm; 0.625 in radial at 0.002 in/rev = 0.409 min.
  • Between cuts: 0.5 min a part for tool indexes, rapids and the bar pull. Cycle = 1.333 min cutting + 0.5 = 1.833 min, rounded up to 1.84 min a part.

The facing and part-off times are worked on the bar's diameter: on a lathe holding constant surface speed the spindle speeds up as the tool moves to the centre, up to the machine's limit, so a cut timed at the starting rpm errs long.

The bar

  • Cut length: 2.875 in + 0.125 in for the part-off tool and facing = 3 in a part.
  • Parts per bar: 144 in bars (12 ft) less a 6 in remnant is 138 in usable ÷ 3 in = 46 parts a bar.
  • Price: $9.80 a foot, $117.60 a bar. With 2% fallout, 10 pins start 11 (1 bar), 100 start 103 (3 bars) and 500 start 511 (12 bars).

The rest

  • CNC lathe with bar feeder at $85.00 an hour: 75 min setup, 1.84 min a part.
  • Bench at $55.00 an hour: deburr and inspect, 0.75 min a part; a 30 min first-article check once per job.
  • Passivation (outside vendor): $0.45 a part, minimum $125.00 a lot.
  • Markup: 20% on material, 15% on the outside service, none on labor (the shop rates are taken to hold the margin already); a $250.00 minimum per line.

What the table shows:

  • On a short run the bar decides more than the metal in the part. 10 pins use 11 × 3 in = 33 in of a 144 in bar. Charged by the length used, the material is $26.95 and the price each $34.61; charged the whole bar, it is $117.60 and $45.49. Which is right depends on whether the rest of that bar will be used on another job soon; decide it per material, not per quote.
  • Setup is the price of a small order. The $133.75 of setup and first article and the $125.00 passivation minimum are 63% of the cost at 10 pins; at 500 the same setup is $0.27 a pin. That is why the price each falls from $45.49 to $10.41 to $7.55.
  • At volume it is cycle time and bar. At 500 pins, run time ($1,683.32) and bar ($1,411.20) are 89% of the cost. Each second off the cycle is worth $12.07 on this order, and whole bars matter less: priced by the length used, the 500 would be $7.17 each instead of $7.55.

Mistakes that lose money on jobs you win

  • Pricing the metal in the part. The part-off tool, the facing stock and the remnant at the end of every bar are bought too. On a short part they can be a large share of the bar.
  • Counting only cutting time. Tool indexes, rapids, the bar pull and the spindle coming up to speed are time on the machine on every part; time a real cycle and add them.
  • One roughing pass where the stock needs three. Work the passes out from the stock to remove and the depth your tooling takes in that material.
  • Forgetting the second operation. Cross holes, flats, hex features and back-side work need live tooling, a sub-spindle or a trip to the mill, each with its own setup and cycle.
  • Leaving out deburring and inspection. Hand deburring threads and cross holes and checking tight diameters take time on every part.
  • Missing the outside minimum. Plating, passivation and heat treat have lot charges; on a small order the minimum is the whole charge.

Doing it in Job Shop Quote

Job Shop Quote does not compute lathe cycle times: you work out the times above and type them in. What it does do is the bar. A rate-card material can be priced by the foot, with the cut allowance, the bar length the shop buys and the remnant each bar leaves. A quote line with a round blank then counts parts per bar, charges whole bars for the parts started (or, with no bar length, the length used plus the row's scrap), prints the arithmetic ("2.875 in blank + 0.125 in cut allowance = 3 in per part ... 46 parts per bar"), and puts the cut list and the bars to pull on the job traveler once the quote is won. Fallout, the lathe and bench at their own rates, the passivation with its lot minimum and every quantity break follow, and a line missing a time or a material is blocked rather than priced as if it were free.
