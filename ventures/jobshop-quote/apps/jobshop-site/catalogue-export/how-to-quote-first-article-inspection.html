How to quote first article inspection (AS9102)

An aerospace PO that calls for a first article inspection report asks for hours of work that never touch a machine: ballooning the drawing, measuring every characteristic on the first part and filling in the forms. On a small order that work can cost more than the machining, and it is the line shops most often leave off. Here is what the work is, when a partial first article is due, and one part priced with and without it at 1, 10 and 100.

What a first article is, and what it is not

AS9102 is the aerospace standard for first article inspection (FAI): a documented check, on a part from the first production run, that every design characteristic on the drawing and every requirement on the PO was met. It is recorded on three forms:

  • Form 1, part number accountability: the part, its drawing and revision, and any sub-assemblies.
  • Form 2, product accountability: the raw material, the specifications, the special processes (plating, anodizing, heat treat and the like) and any functional tests, each with the certificate that proves it.
  • Form 3, characteristic accountability: every dimension, tolerance and note on the drawing, numbered (ballooned) on the drawing and listed with its measured result.

A first-piece check is not a first article. Measuring the critical features of the first part off the machine before running the rest is part of the setup, and the CNC quoting guide prices it as such (60 minutes on the mill). An AS9102 report is a separate job with a separate deliverable, and it stays on the routing in every variant below. Which revision of AS9102 applies, and whether the customer accepts their own form or software output, is set by the PO and its quality clauses: read them before you price.

What the work takes

  1. Balloon the drawing. Number every characteristic: each dimension, tolerance, thread, surface finish, note and flag note. A busy drawing can carry well over a hundred.
  2. Plan the inspection. Decide which gauge or CMM program checks each one; a CMM program written for the first article is time too.
  3. Measure the first part. Every characteristic, with the result written down, not just a pass.
  4. Collect the certificates. Mill certs for the material and a certificate from every special-process vendor, some of whom charge for one.
  5. Fill in the forms and file them. And answer the customer's questions when their quality team reviews the package.

None of that scales with quantity: it is the same for one part as for a thousand, so it belongs in the quote as a charge per line or per order, not per part.

A worked example

The anodized 6061 mounting bracket from the CNC quoting guide, now for a customer whose PO calls for an AS9102 first article. Inspection time is charged at $95.00 an hour (a CMM and bench rate of its own, lower than the mill's $123.85). Every time, rate and price here is made up to show the arithmetic; none of it is a benchmark for what a first article takes or costs, and AS9102 sets no price.

  • Full AS9102 first article: balloon the drawing and plan the inspection (45 min), measure every characteristic on the first part (90 min), fill in Forms 1 to 3 and collect the certs (60 min), plus the anodizer's certificate for Form 2 ($25.00): 195 minutes, $333.75 before markup.
  • Partial (delta) first article after a revision: re-balloon the changed characteristics (15 min), measure the changed characteristics (30 min), update Forms 1 to 3 (30 min): 75 minutes, $118.75 before markup.

What the table shows:

  • On one part, the first article is 36% of the price. The full report adds $337.50 to a $587.26 part ($333.75 of inspection and certificate, and $3.75 of markup on the certificate). Quote it without that line and the shop does that work for nothing.
  • Spread over the order, it fades. At 10 parts it adds $33.75 a part; at 100, $3.38. The charge is the same; only the share each part carries changes.
  • A partial first article is a smaller charge, not none. After a revision that changes a few characteristics, re-ballooning and measuring only those, and updating the forms, adds $118.75 to the line here, against $337.50 for a full one.

When a new or partial first article is due

Once a part has a complete first article, repeat orders do not need another, until something changes. The events AS9102 lists include a change to the design that affects the part's characteristics, a change in the manufacturing source, process, inspection method, location, tooling or materials, and a lapse in production (two years in the standard). The customer can ask for more. A partial ("delta") first article covers only what the change affected. So check, on every repeat RFQ: has the revision changed, has anything about how you make it changed, and when did you last ship it? Your own records answer the last two; the drawing answers the first.

How to put it on the quote

  • Price it as time, at an inspection rate. Minutes on the inspection bench or the CMM, charged once per line, the same way you charge a setup.
  • Say it on the quote. "Includes AS9102 first article report" or "first article report not included" in the notes settles the question before the PO arrives, not after. When a customer wants it priced separately, give it a line of its own at quantity one.
  • Carry the vendors' certificate charges. They are small and easy to forget.
  • Do not charge it again on a repeat order that does not need it, and do not forget the partial one when the revision changes.

Doing it in Job Shop Quote

Add your inspection bench or CMM to the rate card as a machine with its own rate. On a quote line, add the first article as operations on that machine with setup minutes and a cycle time of zero (the app accepts zero for setup-only work such as programming or a first article), and a certificate charge as an extra with a lot amount. The app then charges it once for the line, spreads it across each quantity break, and prints the per-part prices. The prices in the table are the ones the app gives for the same inputs, checked by a test in its code. Once the RFQ's drawing PDF is in, the RFQ page also has a "First article list" download: a draft of Form 3's characteristic list read from the drawing's text (toleranced dimensions with their limits, geometric tolerances, threads, finishes, numbered notes, material and finish), numbered so the numbers can be the balloon numbers. It is a draft to check against the drawing: a dimension with no text on the PDF, or a scanned drawing, is not in it. Beside it, "Ballooned drawing" downloads the same PDF with a numbered balloon beside each listed characteristic it found in the drawing's text, with the CSV's row numbers; the drawing itself is left as it was, and a characteristic whose text was not found gets no balloon rather than a guessed one. Job Shop Quote does not fill in Forms 1 and 2 or record results. The RFQ review checklist has the question to ask before you price: is a first article required, and to which standard?
