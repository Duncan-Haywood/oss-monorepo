How to quote a CNC machined part

A machined part's price is material, setup, run time, finishing and a markup, worked out again at every quantity the customer asks about. Here is the method step by step, a worked example at four quantity breaks, and the places quotes most often go wrong.

The method in six steps

  1. Material. Size the blank: the finished part plus stock to face and clean up on every side, rounded up to a size you can buy or saw. Weight is length × width × thickness × the material's density; price it per pound (or per bar, sheet or foot, however your supplier sells it) and add saw kerf, facing and drop. A supplier's cut charge or minimum is a floor, not a rounding error.
  2. Operations and setup. List every time the part goes on a machine: first side, flip, a second machine, deburr. Each operation has a setup (fixturing, indicating, tool loading, first-article check) that is paid once per lot, whatever the quantity.
  3. Cycle time. Estimate the minutes per part for each operation, from CAM, a similar job or a time study, including load and unload. Multiply by the machine's hourly rate and the number of parts started.
  4. Fallout. If you expect to scrap some parts, start more than the customer orders: starts = quantity ÷ (1 − fallout), rounded up. Material, run time and per-part finishing are priced on the starts.
  5. Finishing and outside services. Anodize, plating and heat treat usually cost a price per part with a vendor lot minimum. On a small order the minimum is the price.
  6. Markup and minimums. Add your markup to each component (many shops mark up labor and purchased items at different percentages), then check the line against your minimum order. Divide by the quantity for the price each, rounded up to the cent.

A worked example: one bracket at four quantities

A 6061 aluminum bracket, milled in two setups on a 3-axis mill and sent out for anodize. Every number here is made up to show the arithmetic; they are not typical prices, rates or times for any shop or region. Use your own.

  • Blank 4.25 × 3.25 × 1 in at 0.0975 lb/in³: 1.347 lb, at $4.50 a pound plus 10% for kerf and facing, with a $25.00 material minimum.
  • Op 10: first side: 60 min setup, 7 min a part.
  • Op 20: flip, second side: 30 min setup, 4 min a part.
  • Both at $100 an hour (the cost-based rate from the hourly rate guide's example, rounded), for setup and run alike.
  • Anodize, clear (outside vendor): $3.00 a part, $125.00 lot minimum.
  • 2% fallout; markup 20% on labor, 15% on material and 15% on the outside finish; $250.00 line minimum.

Run time is 11 min × 11 parts, $201.66. The anodize lot minimum ($125.00) is more than 11 × $3.00, so it applies. That is $549.99 of cost; markup adds $100.08, and the price is $65.01 each.

Two things the table shows:

  • Setup and lot minimums dominate small orders. At one piece, setup alone is $150.00 of the part's cost; at 250 it is $0.60. That is why one price each for every quantity loses money on the small orders or loses the big ones.
  • Fallout bites hardest at quantity one. 2% fallout on one part still means starting 2. Whether you price a spare blank, or quote a single prototype with no fallout and accept the risk, is a decision to make on purpose.

Where quotes go wrong

  • One price at every quantity. Quote breaks (1, 10, 50, 250, or the quantities the customer named) so the setup is spread honestly.
  • Forgetting the second setup. Every flip, second machine and secondary operation has its own setup. A part with three setups at one piece costs three setups.
  • Cycle time without load and unload. CAM run time is cutting time; the machine is not earning while the operator swaps parts, deburrs or measures.
  • Vendor minimums priced per part. $3.00 a part anodize is $30.00 on ten parts; the vendor will bill $125.00.
  • Material at the finished size. The blank is bigger than the part, and the bar or plate you buy is bigger than the blank.
  • No record of why. Six months later the repeat order comes in, and nobody knows which rate, time or vendor price the old quote used.
