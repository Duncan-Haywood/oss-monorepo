Machine shop quoting software, compared honestly

Most CNC and fabrication shops quote in one of three ways: a spreadsheet, the quoting module of their ERP, or dedicated quoting software. Here is what each costs where a price is published, what it is good at, and where Job Shop Quote fits, including what it does not do.

What Job Shop Quote does today

  • Prices each part from the machines, rates, materials, minimums and markups on your rate card. The math is plain arithmetic you can check line by line; no model sets a price.
  • Measures CAD files: a STEP or IGES solid's blank size and hole locations (for roughing and drilling time at your machine's rates), an STL's size and volume, and a DXF flat pattern's cut length, pierces and bend lines (for laser, waterjet, plasma and press-brake time, and parts per sheet).
  • Imports your rate card and your supplier's price list from a spreadsheet.
  • Builds the quote PDF, emails it when your estimator sends it, keeps revisions, and logs each quote as won or lost so you can see your win rate by estimator, routing, part family and month.

What it does not do

  • It is not an ERP: no scheduling, work orders, inventory, shop-floor tracking or quality records. If you need those, JobBOSS² or ProShop are built for it.
  • Reading RFQ emails and PDF drawings with AI is built but not switched on in the hosted app yet; until it is, you enter those values yourself.
  • No connection to an ERP yet. A QuickBooks Online connection is built and switches on once Intuit approves it.
  • Not for CUI or export-controlled drawings during early access (why).
  • It is new. The products above have years of shops behind them; Job Shop Quote does not yet, and we won't pretend otherwise.

How to choose

  1. Count your RFQs. A few a week rarely justifies more than a good spreadsheet. Dozens a week, with parts that repeat, is where quoting software pays for the estimator's time.
  2. Decide whether you need an ERP. If you already run one, try its quoting module first. If you don't, buying an ERP just to quote is a large project; quoting-only tools are smaller.
  3. Know your rates first. Every tool prices from your hourly rates, so a rate that is too low gives a wrong quote in all of them (how to work one out).
  4. Price it on your own work. Quote last month's RFQs in any tool you trial and compare the numbers with what you actually charged and what the jobs cost.
  5. Check who owns your data. Ask whether your drawings are used to train AI models and whether you can export your rate card and quote history. (Job Shop Quote: never used for training, and your rate card exports as a spreadsheet.)
