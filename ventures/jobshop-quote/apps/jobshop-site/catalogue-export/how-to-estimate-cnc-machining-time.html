How to estimate CNC machining time for a quote

Cycle time is the number a machining quote rests on: every minute of it is billed on every part. Here is how to build it up from the cut, the holes and the time the spindle isn't cutting, a worked example, and the mistakes that make a won job lose money.

The method in five steps

  1. Work out the feed from the tool. Spindle speed (rpm) = surface speed (SFM) × 12 ÷ (π × tool diameter), the familiar SFM × 3.82 ÷ D. Feed (in/min) = rpm × flutes × chip load. Take surface speed and chip load from your tooling supplier's data for the material, and cap rpm at your spindle's limit.
  2. Turn the feed into a removal rate. Material removal rate (in³/min) = width of cut × depth of cut × feed. That is the rate while the tool is in the cut. A real roughing path also ramps, steps over, lifts and moves between pockets, so keep the share of it you see on your own jobs: time a few and divide the volume removed by the minutes.
  3. Rough time = volume removed ÷ removal rate. The blank's volume less the finished part's (your CAD system measures the part), at the rate for that machine and material.
  4. Time the holes one by one. Each hole costs a fixed time (position, spot, rapid in and out) plus its depth at the drill's feed (rpm × feed per revolution). Holes deeper than about 3 diameters are usually pecked: count the extra retracts and time each. Taps add a fixed time per tapped hole.
  5. Add what isn't roughing or drilling. Finishing passes (toolpath length ÷ finish feed), tool changes (count × chip-to-chip time), and loading, unloading and cleaning the part. On small parts these can be as large as the cutting.

A worked example

A manifold block in 6061-T6 aluminum plate, roughed from a 4.25 × 3.25 × 1.5 in blank on a 3-axis mill at $123.85 an hour (the rate the hourly rate guide works out). Every number here is made up to show the arithmetic; they are not recommended feeds, speeds, chip loads or times for any tool, material or machine. Use your tooling data and your own timings.

  • Feed: a 1/2 in 3-flute carbide end mill at 800 SFM is 800 × 12 ÷ (π × 0.5) = 6,112 rpm; at 0.003 in a tooth on 3 flutes that is 55.0 in/min.
  • Removal rate: 0.2 in wide × 0.5 in deep × 55.0 in/min = 5.50 in³/min in the cut. The shop's timed jobs keep 50% of that over a whole roughing path, so 2.75 in³/min goes on the rate card for this mill and material.
  • Roughing: the blank is 20.72 in³ and the model 12.4 in³, so 8.32 in³ comes out: 8.32 ÷ 2.75 = 3.0 min.
  • Holes: 6 × 1/4-20 tapped, #7 (0.201 in) tap drill, 0.75 in deep; 2 × 1/4 in through, 1.5 in deep. The drill runs at 3,000 rpm × 0.004 in/rev = 12.00 in/min. All 8 holes are deeper than 3 diameters, so they are pecked every 1.5 × D: 18 extra retracts at 1.5 s. Drilling comes to 1.61 min and tapping to 0.5 min.

What the table shows:

  • Roughing is the largest single line, not the whole cycle. It is 32% of the 9.36 minutes; tool changes and loading alone add 2.25 min a part that no feeds-and-speeds formula shows.
  • A small miss is a big one at volume. Underestimate this cycle by 10% and the 100-part order gives away $193.21 of mill time.
  • Setup is a separate line. The 45 min setup ($92.89) is $9.29 a part at 10 and $0.93 at 100; keep it out of the cycle time so it spreads over the lot.

Mistakes that make a won job lose money

  • Using the theoretical removal rate. The rate in the cut is not the rate over the path. Measure the share your own toolpaths keep, per machine and material.
  • One rate for every material. Stainless and titanium rough at a fraction of aluminum's rate; a rate measured in 6061 badly underprices 304.
  • Timing holes by depth alone. Positioning and peck retracts can be most of a drilled hole's time: in the example they are 61% of it.
  • Leaving out what isn't cutting. Tool changes, probing, loading, blowing off chips and in-process checks are billed time too.
  • Trusting CAM's cycle time as the whole answer. A CAM estimate is good for the cut itself, but it leaves out loading and often your machine's real rapid and tool-change times; compare it with a timed run.

Doing it in Job Shop Quote

Put each machine's removal rate per material, its seconds per hole, drill feed per material, peck depth and seconds per peck, and its seconds per tapped hole on the rate card once. When an RFQ comes with a STEP model, Job Shop Quote measures the part's volume and its holes and suggests the roughing, drilling and tapping operations above, each labelled with the figures it came from; you mark which hole sizes are threaded, since a model rarely says. It does not work out speeds and feeds, finishing passes, tool changes or loading: you type those, and a line missing a time is blocked rather than priced as if it were free. The suggested minutes in the example are the ones the app gives for the same inputs, checked by a test in its code. You can see how a quote is built.

For the rest of the price (material, setups, outside processes and quantity breaks), see how to quote a CNC machining job; for the rate the minutes are billed at, how to work out a machine shop hourly rate.
