    • Version ranges. An advisory saying "below 1.4.2" is reported as not evaluated, never resolved.
      • 
    
    • Reachability and exploitability. A matched component may not be reachable in your build at all.
      •

      •

    Questions
    
  
    When do CRA Article 14 reporting obligations start?
    
  
    
    11 September 2026, under Article 14(1) and (3): a manufacturer that
    becomes aware of an actively exploited vulnerability or a severe incident
    in a product with digital elements already on the EU market must notify
    ENISA — an early warning within 24 hours, a fuller notification within 72
    hours, and a final report within 14 days (actively exploited
    vulnerability) or one month (severe incident) after the 72-hour
    notification. It applies to products already on the market, not only new
    releases. This tool does not determine whether an obligation applies to
    your situation; it helps you find the matching components and draft the
    notification once you or a qualified professional decide it does.
  
    
  
    What SBOM formats does this accept?
    
  
    
    CycloneDX JSON (what npm sbom, syft, cdxgen and trivy emit),
    SPDX JSON, and a plain component list of the form
    {"components": [{"ecosystem", "name", "version"}]}. Package
    URLs are read where present, so an npm scope, a Maven group and a Go module
    path all resolve to the coordinates an advisory names them by. Nested
    CycloneDX sub-components are walked too.
  
    
  
    Why does it only match exact versions?
    
  
    
    Because resolving a version range is a judgement about your product, not a
    lookup. An advisory saying "all versions below 1.4.2" requires deciding how
    your ecosystem orders versions, whether a backport applies, and whether the
    vulnerable path is reachable in your build. This tool refuses to make that
    call on your behalf and instead names every range-only advisory in the
    report so you review it yourself. That refusal is the point: an exact match
    is evidence, an inferred one is an opinion.
  
    
  
    Does a match mean I have to file a CRA notification?
    
  
    
    No. A match means one component in your SBOM has the same name, ecosystem
    and version as an entry in the advisory export you supplied. Whether it is
    exploitable in your product, whether it is an actively exploited
    vulnerability or a severe incident under Article 14, and whether a
    reporting deadline applies are all determinations for you and a qualified
    professional. The tool produces a draft to review, never a filing.
  
    
  
    Is my SBOM uploaded anywhere?
    
  
    
    No. The files are read by your own browser and nothing is transmitted.
    There is no server, no account, and no upload step — you can disconnect
    from the network and the tool still works. This matters more than usual
    here: an SBOM is a complete map of your product's dependencies, and an
    advisory match list is a map of its exposure.
  
    
  
    What does a clean report actually prove?
    
  
    
    Only that no component in the SBOM you supplied exactly matched an entry in
    the advisory export you supplied, on the day you ran it. It is not a
    vulnerability scan, not conformity assessment, and not coverage of
    advisories you did not export. If part of your export stated versions as
    ranges, the report says so explicitly, because a clean result read without
    that caveat would look like far more assurance than it is.
  
    
  
    My SBOM and my advisory export spell packages differently. Now what?
    
  
    
    That is the normal case, not a broken export — the two formats name the
    same registry two ways, and this tool folds them onto one spelling before
    matching. Why
    don't my SBOM and advisory package names match? walks through where
    the mismatches come from and which ones can be resolved without guessing.
  
    
  
    How is this different from OWASP Dependency-Track?
    
  
    
    Dependency-Track is a continuous, self-hosted SBOM-analysis server; this
    is a free, one-shot page with nothing to deploy. See
    CRA Evidence Desk vs. OWASP
    Dependency-Track for the full comparison.
  
    
  
    Can't I just match my SBOM to the advisory list by eye?
    
  
    
    For a handful of components, yes. It stops being reliable once ecosystem
    names are spelled two different ways across the two files, a component is
    nested rather than top-level, or an advisory names a version range instead
    of a list. See
    CRA Evidence Desk vs. matching an SBOM
    to advisories by hand for where a manual read breaks down.
