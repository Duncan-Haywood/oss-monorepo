# ADA Title II Website Accessibility Evidence

Small-city and special-district webmasters and IT staff can look up their exact ADA Title II website compliance date and turn an `axe-core` JSON scan into a dated evidence record, split into what WCAG 2.1 AA actually requires versus best-practice extras. The tool reports what the supplied automated test found and what date applies to the entity described; it does not claim that a site is compliant.

## Run it

Open `deadline.html`, pick your entity type (general-purpose government, or any special district — school, water, fire, transit) and, if it's not a special district, enter total population. It shows the exact date — April 26, 2027 for entities at or above 50,000 population, April 26, 2028 for everyone else — sourced to the Department of Justice's 2026 rule extension, with a link to the Federal Register notice and ada.gov's own fact sheet. **Copy result** hands the reading to an email or a council memo.

Open `index.html`, paste the JSON exported by axe-core against your site, and choose **Generate evidence record**. Findings are split into rules tagged `wcag2a`/`wcag2aa`/`wcag21a`/`wcag21aa` — the WCAG 2.1 AA set the DOJ rule actually adopts — and everything else, so a reader isn't left guessing which findings are the legal minimum and which are extra credit (WCAG 2.2, AAA, or axe's own best-practice rules). Use **Copy record** for a council memo or contractor handoff, or **Download .txt** for a dated file. No account, network call, or API key is required.

Everything runs in the browser. The JSON and the population figure you enter are never uploaded, and there are no cookies or analytics vendors. Both pages keep anonymous aggregate counts in localStorage (`report_generated`, `report_copied`, `report_downloaded` on the evidence page; `deadline_looked_up`, `deadline_copied` on the lookup), each with a visible opt-out. GitHub Sponsors is optional and does not change what the tool does.

## Opportunity assessment

The target user is a webmaster, IT lead, or accessibility coordinator at a small city, county, school district, water/fire/transit special district, or similar public entity — someone who searches "ADA title II deadline for my city" or "WCAG 2.1 AA government website checker" because the DOJ's April 2026 rule extension (pushing dates to April 2027/2028) is recent news most of this audience has not yet fully absorbed. That deadline is the distribution channel: it drives active search volume right now, and it recurs (state municipal leagues, ICMA, and school-IT mailing lists cover it), unlike a generic accessibility pitch with no forcing date. This niche is distinct from `shopify-a11y-evidence`: different regulation (ADA Title II vs. private commercial sites), different buyer (government IT vs. e-commerce merchants/agencies), and a different standard framing (WCAG 2.1 AA specifically, not axe's default rule set) — confirmed against `portfolio/pipeline/duplicate_check.py` before writing this up (no match above 0.14).

Positioning stays in the evidence lane this portfolio already uses successfully (`cra-evidence-desk`, `backup-verification-evidence`, `dns-configuration-evidence`): report what was tested and when, split by legal relevance, never a determination that the entity is or isn't compliant. The main risks are a reader treating either page as legal advice or the date as fixed forever (both pages state the caveat and link the primary source so the reader can re-verify), and axe-core's own coverage gap (most WCAG failures need manual/assistive-technology testing, which every mention of "automated testing typically finds a minority of issues" exists to flag). Low variable cost: static pages, no server, no per-user cost. Future revenue path, if this finds a buyer, would be a hosted multi-page crawl or a scheduled re-check tier — not needed for the free core to be useful.
