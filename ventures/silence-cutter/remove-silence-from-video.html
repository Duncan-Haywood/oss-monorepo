How to remove silence from a video automatically

A ten-minute talking-head recording usually holds one to two minutes of pauses: the breath before a sentence, the moment spent finding the next point, the gap after a stumble. Cutting them by hand means scrubbing the waveform and making a blade cut on each side of every pause, often 100 or more for a ten-minute video. There are three ways to avoid that.

1. Cut by hand on the waveform

Zoom into the audio track, find each flat stretch, blade both ends and ripple-delete. It gives full control and is fine for a two-minute clip. For anything longer it is the slowest option by far, and it is the work the other two methods automate.

2. Your editor's built-in tools

Some editors and subscription apps now detect silence for you. They work well inside their own app; check which version or plan includes it. If your editor has none, or you want the same settings across several apps, use a cut list.

3. Import a cut list (works in Resolve, Premiere and Final Cut)

Every major editor can import a timeline from a file. Silence Cutter finds the pauses in your browser, with nothing uploaded, and writes that file:

Settings that keep words intact

  • Shortest pause to cut, 0.5 s. Shorter than about 0.3 s and you start cutting the natural gaps inside sentences, which sounds rushed.
  • Keep before speech, 0.12 s; keep after, 0.18 s. Words start sharply and fade out slowly, so more is kept after speech than before it. Raise "before" if starts sound clipped.
  • Leave of each pause, 0 to 0.2 s. Leaving a short beat keeps an interview from sounding breathless.
  • Threshold: leave it on auto. It is set from your recording's own noise floor and voice level, so a quiet lapel mic and a loud USB mic both work without tuning.

Worked example

A 60-second test clip at 30 fps (the public test recording from the Silero VAD project, laid over colour bars) loses seven pauses: 4.8 seconds, 8%. The first two events of the EDL read:

001  AX       AA/V  C        00:00:00:00 00:00:02:09 00:00:00:00 00:00:02:09
002  AX       AA/V  C        00:00:02:17 00:00:07:01 00:00:02:09 00:00:06:23

Source 00:00:02:09 to 00:00:02:17 (8 frames) was a pause, so the second event starts on the record side where the first ended. The ffmpeg render of the same cut list came out at 55.27 s, the length of the kept frames.

Questions

What minimum pause length should I use?

0.5 s is a good start for a talking-head video: it removes the dead air between sentences and leaves the short breaths inside them. Tutorials and lectures often go to 0.35 s for a tighter pace; interviews and podcasts read more naturally at 0.7 to 1 s, or with a short part of each pause left in.

Why import a cut list instead of rendering the cut video?

A rendered file is final: a cut you disagree with means doing it again. An imported EDL or XML gives you a timeline of ordinary clips from your original footage, so you can drag one edge to restore a word, add B-roll over a jump cut, and grade and export once at full quality.

Do the frame rates have to match?

Yes. Choose the frame rate of your footage before you export. Cut points are rounded to whole frames at that rate, and an EDL read at the wrong rate drifts by a little more at every cut.
