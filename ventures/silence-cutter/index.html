How it decides what is silence

    
    
      
  1. The audio is mixed to mono and filtered to the voice band (high-pass at 90 Hz, low-pass at 4 kHz), so mains hum, air conditioning and hiss count for less.
    2. 
      
  2. The level of every 10 ms is measured. The quietest tenth gives the room's noise floor; the loudest twentieth gives your voice.
    3. 
      
  3. The threshold sits 35% of the way from the floor to the voice, and never closer than 6 dB to the floor. That is why one setting works on a whispery lapel mic and a hot USB mic alike.
    4. 
      
  4. Speech starts above the threshold and ends 4 dB below it, so a word does not flicker on and off. A burst shorter than 60 ms (a click, a bumped desk) is not speech. Each word's edges then grow outward while the sound stays 4 dB over the floor, which keeps soft starts like "s" and "f".
    5. 
      
  5. Every gap of at least the shortest pause becomes a cut, less the audio kept before and after speech.
    6. 
    

    
How well it works

    
Measured on 2,496 real spoken words (the Free Spoken Digit Dataset, six speakers) laid into takes with known pauses and put through six recording conditions: clean, white noise at 20 and 12 dB signal-to-noise, 60 Hz hum, a recording 20 dB too quiet and one 12 dB too hot. It removed 95.8% of the removable pause time (91.6% in its worst condition) and cut into 25 words, all of them in the noise and hum conditions. A fixed -30 dBFS threshold cut into 499 words, nearly all in the quiet recording; fixed -40 and -50 dBFS thresholds removed none of the pauses under noise or hum. On a real two-person conversation with hand-marked speech turns it removed 90.6% of the pause time and cut into no turn. The benchmark script and full tables are in the source.

  


  

    
Questions

    
Is my video uploaded?

    
No. The browser decodes the audio track on your device and the analysis runs in this page. Nothing is sent to a server. The exports are generated in the browser too.

    
How does it decide what is silence?

    
It filters the audio to the voice band, measures the level of every 10 ms, and reads two numbers off the recording itself: the noise floor of the room and the level of your voice. The threshold sits between them, at least 6 dB above the floor, so the same settings work for a quiet lavalier recording and a loud USB microphone. A second, lower threshold keeps the soft start and end of each word.

    
Which editors can open the export?

    
The EDL imports into DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro and Avid Media Composer. The FCPXML imports into Final Cut Pro and DaVinci Resolve. The XML imports into Premiere Pro through File, Import. Each one points at your original file by name, so keep the video next to the export or relink it once.

    
Will it cut off the start of my words?

    
It is built not to. On a benchmark of 2,496 spoken words in six recording conditions it removed 95.8% of the removable pause time and cut into 25 words, mostly soft starts under white noise; with no noise or hum added it cut into none. Pad before speech keeps extra audio in front of each word if you hear a clipped start; raise it to 0.2 s for a breathy speaker.

    
How long a file can it take?

    
The browser holds the decoded audio in memory at 16 kHz mono for the analysis: about 230 MB per hour. An hour-long podcast is fine on a laptop; for longer files, or to batch a folder, use the command-line version: node cutter.mjs talk.mp4 --edl talk.edl.
