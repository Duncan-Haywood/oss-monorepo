Questions restaurant owners ask

How many 2-tops and 4-tops should a restaurant have?

It depends on the sizes of the parties that actually come, how long each size stays, and how much floor you have. Enter your floor area, each table type's seats and footprint, and your party-size mix. The tool runs hundreds of simulated services, seating each party at the smallest free table (or two pushed together), and searches for the mix that seats the most guests in the floor you have.

Why does a room full of four-tops lose guests?

A couple at a four-top wastes two seats for the whole meal. If most of your parties are twos, those seats are empty while a pair waits at the door. Swapping some four-tops for two-tops (which take less floor) often seats more guests; the tool measures how many more on identical simulated nights.

How accurate is the result?

It is a simulation of one busy service from the figures you type, not a forecast. Parties arrive at random at the average rate you enter, stay for a random time around the average, and leave if the wait passes your tolerance. The gain is shown with its standard error so you can see whether it is clearly more than noise. Check the party mix against a week of your own reservation or POS data.

Does it design the floor plan?

No. It chooses how many of each table type, using the square footage you give each. Aisles, ADA clearances, exits, fire-code occupancy, service stations and the bar are yours to decide and to check with your local authority.

Is my data uploaded?

No. It runs in your browser; nothing you type leaves your device.

Not decided

Where tables go, aisle widths, accessible seating, exits and occupancy limits under your local fire and building codes, the bar and booths, table and chair cost, reservations versus walk-ins policy, check size by party size, and seasonal or day-of-week swings. The simulation uses one average busy service and one stay length per party size, with random variation around both, and seats parties first-come among those who fit. Real guests linger, split, and arrive in waves.
