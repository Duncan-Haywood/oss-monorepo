FOR DECK STAINING CREWS

Bid the rails, the stairs, and the coats.

Price a stain or seal the way the trade actually loses money: stainable surface from picket rails and stairs, strip versus wash, oil two-coat versus water, and second-story carry as their own hours. You get the bid, the hours the first picket rail actually consumes, and the walk-away number below which the job loses money.

The deck

Start from a common job, then replace every figure with your takeoff. Changing prep, finish, or access never overwrites a rate you already typed. This prices a stain — not a new deck build, and not a house paint job.

Your crew rates

A $38/h painter loaded at 30% is $49.40. Quoting the raw wage on a published $2.50/sf special is how a 12x16 with picket rails that looked fine turns into a two-day loss after the first stair. Zero walking feet still prices — stain hours are the surface, not an error. Picket rail defaults to 9 stainable sf per lineal foot (both sides of the pickets plus the rails).
