Common questions

How many sales reps do I need to hire to hit my bookings target?

Divide the target by what one fully ramped rep is expected to book (quota times your real average attainment), then correct for the three things that make that number too low: new hires take months to ramp, some reps leave before they are productive, and recruiting takes time before a rep can even start. This planner does that correction month by month: it counts existing reps by tenure, applies the ramp and attrition, and finds the fewest hires, starting as early as your hiring lead time allows, that close the gap.

How do I work out the latest date I can start a hire?

Work backwards from the target. A rep who starts later contributes fewer ramped months inside the year, so each start month has a known contribution. The planner delays each needed hire as far as the target still holds, given the others, and then subtracts your hiring lead time to give the date by which the requisition must be open. Any slack you have is spent on the latest start dates; with no slack, every hire has to start at the earliest month the lead time allows.

Why plan on average attainment instead of quota?

Because most teams do not average 100% of quota. If 55% of reps hit quota and the rest average 70%, the team averages about 86.5%, and capacity planned at 100% is roughly 15% short before any ramp or attrition. The planner takes the share of reps at or over quota and the average attainment of the rest and blends them. It counts reps at or over quota at 100% unless you enter a higher figure, which is deliberately conservative.

Should I hire early or hire late?

Hiring early costs salary and benefits for months of partial output; hiring late saves that cost but leaves no margin if attainment slips or reps leave. The planner prices both from your fully loaded cost per rep: the extra cost of starting every hire at the earliest month, the extra bookings it buys, and the shortfall each plan shows if attainment is ten percent lower. That turns an argument into a cost per extra dollar of bookings.

Does this model pipeline, seasonality or territories?

Only partly. Pipeline coverage and win rate give a ceiling on what the pipeline can support, and the planner warns when more reps would not help because there is not enough pipeline. It does not model seasonality, territory splits, product mix or quota changes mid-year: bookings are spread evenly across months and the target is a single annual number.

Is my sales data uploaded anywhere?

No. The planner runs entirely in your browser. Your target, quota and headcount are never sent anywhere. The share link stores your inputs in the link itself, so anyone you send it to sees those numbers, and the optional usage counter records only which features were used and the day.
