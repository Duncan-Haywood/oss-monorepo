This calculator vs. a flat price per square foot

Drywall crews are shopped by the square foot. That rate cannot see the job — hanging is the cheap half, and finish level, texture, height, and openings decide whether the same footage pays, and two rooms of identical area can differ by more than three times in labor.

What a flat price per square foot is

A price per square foot is the fastest number to quote over the phone: pace off the room, multiply by a rate, read back a total. It is also the unit customers already compare between competing bids.

The rate behind it was learned from a specific job — a given finish level, texture, and ceiling height — and then reused for the next square footage that calls in, whether or not that next job shares any of those.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter each room's length, width, and height, openings, waste, board type, finish level, texture, and whether you are tearing out existing board, plus your own wage, payroll burden, travel, dumpster, overhead, and target margin. It nets wall and ceiling area against openings, applies a height multiplier room by room, and prices hang, finish, and texture hours separately — for this job, not a rate carried over from the last one.

Bills openings inside the same blended per-foot number, if at all. Nets opening square footage out of billable wall area and prices opening labor and tape separately.
Gives the crew a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the price per square foot and the walk-away price alongside the total, so a shopped rate can be scored against real cost before you match it.

Same 1,954 square feet, two different jobs

Using this calculator's own shipped cost inputs — $34 hourly wage, 32% burden, a 1/2" regular board, 10% waste, 86 sq ft of openings across 7 openings, $75 trip fee, 16% overhead, 26% target margin:

A 1,954 sq ft hang-and-finish job at Level 1 (fire-tape, joints covered) takes 46.4 hours and prices at $5,473.07 for the job — $2.80 per square foot.

Finish the identical 1,954 sq ft to Level 5 instead — a full skim for critical lighting, same rooms, same waste, same openings — and it takes 152.8 hours, costs $10,536.55 to run before a dollar of profit, and this calculator prices it at $14,238.58 — $7.29 per square foot. Quoting the Level 5 job at the Level 1 job's learned rate of $2.80 per square foot reproduces the Level 1 price, $5,473.07, which is $5,063.48 below the Level 5 job's own break-even cost — before counting the $8,765.51 it falls short of this calculator's own price at a 26% target margin. Common questions

Why does a flat price per square foot get a drywall bid wrong?

Because square footage only measures the area, not what it takes to finish it. A 1,954 sq ft hang-and-finish job at Level 1 (fire-tape, joints covered) takes about 46.4 hours. The identical 1,954 sq ft finished to Level 5 (a full skim for critical lighting) takes about 152.8 hours -- more than three times the labor on the same area, because hanging is the cheap half and finish level is where the hours actually go.

What does a flat per-square-foot rate usually miss entirely?

Finish level, texture, ceiling height, and openings. A flat rate has no line for the difference between a hang-only wall and a full Level 5 skim, no line for a textured ceiling versus a smooth one, no line for a 12-ft great room taking longer per foot than an 8-ft closet, and no line for a room dense with doors and windows netting out less billable board than an open wall of the same footage.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

Using this calculator's own shipped cost inputs on a 1,954 sq ft hang-and-finish job: Level 1 (fire-tape) takes 46.4 hours and prices at $5,473.07 for the job -- $2.80 per square foot. The identical 1,954 sq ft finished to Level 5 (full skim) takes 152.8 hours, costs $10,536.55 to run before a dollar of profit, and this calculator prices it at $14,238.58 -- $7.29 per square foot. Quoting the Level 5 job at the Level 1 job's learned rate of $2.80 per square foot reproduces the Level 1 price, $5,473.07, which is $5,063.48 below the Level 5 job's own break-even cost -- before counting the $8,765.51 it falls short of this calculator's price at a 26% target margin.

Is a flat price per square foot ever a reasonable shorthand?

For a narrow band of similar jobs -- the same finish level, texture, and ceiling height as the job the rate was learned on -- a per-foot number can land close. The risk is quoting a Level 5 skim, a textured ceiling, or a run of 12-ft walls the same way sight unseen, because the rate does not know the finish level, the texture, or the height until the crew is on site, and by then the per-foot number has often already been quoted.

Does this calculator know my local labor rates or material costs?

No. It has no data on your local labor market or your supplier's board and mud pricing. It takes each room's dimensions, finish level, texture, and openings, and your own wage, payroll burden, board and tape material, waste, travel, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into hang and finish hours and a price for this job -- the arithmetic a flat per-square-foot rate skips, not a market survey.
