How do I stay under the ACA subsidy cliff in 2026?

The short answer: keep your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) at or below 400% of the federal poverty line for your household, and plan for it before the year ends, because one dollar over removes the whole credit. The line is $62,600 for one person and $84,600 for two in the 48 states and DC.

Where the line is

The enhanced credits that had removed the cap expired after 2025. The 2025 HHS poverty guidelines govern 2026 coverage, and 400% of them is the cutoff (48 states and DC; Alaska and Hawaii are higher).

What one dollar costs

Take a single filer whose benchmark plan costs $600 a month ($7,200 a year). At $55,000 of MAGI the calculator gives a credit of about $1,722 a year. At exactly $62,600 the credit is about $965. At $62,601 it is $0. Crossing the line by a dollar costs the whole remaining credit, and it costs more for people with a higher benchmark premium. Below the line the credit shrinks smoothly as income rises, because you are expected to pay a share of income (9.96% from 300% to 400% of the line) toward the benchmark plan.

What lowers MAGI

For a self-employed person, contributions to an HSA, a solo 401(k) or a SEP-IRA, and half of self-employment tax, reduce adjusted gross income and so MAGI. If you are $4,000 over, $4,000 of deductions you can still make before the deadline for that contribution puts you back at the line. Check the limits first with the HSA contribution limit calculator and the solo 401(k) contribution calculator.

The repayment trap

If you take the credit monthly on an income estimate and your final MAGI ends above the line, the full amount is owed back on Form 8962. From tax year 2026 the repayment caps no longer apply. Underestimating income is the expensive mistake, so update your marketplace estimate when income changes.

Common questions

What is the ACA subsidy cliff in 2026?

The enhanced premium tax credits expired after 2025, so for 2026 coverage the credit ends entirely above 400% of the federal poverty line. At or below 400% you still get a credit, set so you pay a share of income toward the benchmark plan. One dollar of income over the line removes all of it.

How much income is 400% of the poverty line in 2026?

The 2025 HHS poverty guidelines govern 2026 coverage. In the 48 states and DC the line is $15,650 for one person plus $5,500 for each extra person, so 400% is $62,600 for one person and $84,600 for two. Alaska and Hawaii use higher lines (this calculator uses $19,550 plus $6,880 per extra person for Alaska and $17,990 plus $6,320 for Hawaii).

What income counts toward the cliff?

Modified adjusted gross income (MAGI): adjusted gross income plus tax-exempt interest, non-taxable Social Security benefits and excluded foreign income. Deductions that reduce AGI, such as HSA contributions, solo 401(k) or SEP-IRA contributions and half of self-employment tax, lower it.

Can I get back under the cliff by making deductible contributions?

Often, if the contribution reduces MAGI and you still have room to make it. The calculator takes the deductions you say you could still make and tells you whether they cover the amount you are over. It does not check contribution limits; use the HSA and solo 401(k) calculators for that.

What happens if I take the credit monthly and my income ends up over the cliff?

You repay it. From tax year 2026 there is no cap on repaying excess advance credit, so if final MAGI is above 400% of the poverty line, every dollar of advance credit you received for the year is owed back when you file Form 8962.
