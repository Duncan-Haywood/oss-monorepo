This calculator vs. subtracting a flat percentage

"Airbnb takes about 15%, Vrbo takes about 8%" is the mental math most hosts start from — two round numbers subtracted from a nightly rate to guess a payout. It costs nothing and takes no time. It is also built on an assumption that only sometimes holds: that a channel's whole fee is one rate charged on one number.

The flat-percentage guess takes a headline rate and applies it to the booking subtotal, done. This calculator instead runs the same stay through each channel's actual fee structure — a commission on one base, processing on another, a flat site cost with no percentage at all — because that is what a payout statement actually charges, not what a single blended number can represent.

On this calculator's own shipped example — a $225 nightly rate, 2 nights, a $95 cleaning fee, 10% occupancy tax, and a $200 security deposit, for a $545 subtotal — an 8% flat guess prices Vrbo's pay-per-booking payout at $501.40. This calculator's own arithmetic, splitting the 5% commission on the subtotal from the 3% processing fee on the guest's full payment (subtotal plus tax plus deposit), comes to $493.77. That is $7.63 overstated on one stay — about $275 a year across the calculator's own default of 36 marketplace bookings.

Common questions

Why doesn't subtracting a flat percentage give the same answer as this calculator?

Because a flat percentage assumes every fee is a share of the same number, and on most channels it isn't. Vrbo's pay-per-booking plan charges a 5% commission on the rental subtotal and a separate 3% processing fee on the guest's full payment — subtotal plus occupancy tax plus any refundable security deposit. Blending those two rates into one "about 8%" and applying it to the subtotal alone leaves out the tax and deposit that the processing leg is actually charged on, so the guess and the real payout diverge by exactly that gap.

How much difference does it actually make?

On this calculator's own shipped example stay — a $225 nightly rate, 2 nights, a $95 cleaning fee, 10% occupancy tax, and a $200 security deposit, for a $545 subtotal — an 8% flat guess prices the Vrbo payout at $501.40. The same stay run through this calculator's actual commission-plus-processing arithmetic comes to $493.77. That is $7.63 overstated on one stay, or roughly $275 a year across the calculator's own default of 36 marketplace bookings — money the guess says is in the deposit that the real fee schedule already took.

Why does the flat-percentage guess work fine for Airbnb but not Vrbo?

Because Airbnb's 2026 host-only fee is, unlike Vrbo's, already a single rate on a single base: 15.5% of the same booking subtotal, with no separate processing charge on a different number. Applying 15.5% straight to the $545 subtotal gives $460.53 against this calculator's own $460.52 — close enough to round to the same cent. The flat-percentage shortcut is not wrong in general; it is wrong exactly when a channel's published rate is actually two rates on two different bases, which is Vrbo's case and not Airbnb's.

What about a direct-booking site's monthly cost — where does that fit into a percentage?

It doesn't, which is the sharper version of the same problem. A direct-booking site or channel manager typically bills a flat monthly or annual fee — this calculator's own default is $29 a month — on top of card processing. That cost has no percentage at all until it's divided by the bookings a host actually keeps after leaving the marketplace, and a host estimating "direct is basically free besides card fees" has no way to fold a flat monthly bill into any percentage guess. This calculator amortizes it per stay automatically; a flat-percentage mental model has nowhere to put it.

Is this the same as checking my own payout statement?

No, and it does not replace one. A payout statement reports what a platform actually paid on a booking that already happened. This calculator answers a forward-looking question — what would this stay pay on each channel, before it's booked — using the fee structure a host enters or accepts as a 2026 starting point. Reconcile against an actual statement once a stay closes; use this calculator to compare channels before choosing one.
