This workbench vs. Synder

Two tools that both end with a matched payout and a matched bank deposit, built on opposite assumptions about what you are willing to connect.

What Synder is

Synder, from CloudBusiness LLC, is a well-known paid subscription that connects directly to a payment processor account (Stripe, Square, PayPal, and dozens more) and to a QuickBooks or Xero account. Once connected, it posts every individual transaction — sales, fees, refunds, tips — into a clearing account for that channel and keeps that clearing account in sync on an ongoing, automated basis, so that when a payout later lands in the bank feed inside QuickBooks or Xero, matching it is a single click. That is a real, useful job: continuous, itemized bookkeeping across many sales channels without typing each transaction by hand.

Getting there means granting Synder API access to your payment processor account and connecting it to your accounting software account, and paying an ongoing subscription priced by transaction volume and how many accounts you connect.

What this workbench is

This page does not connect to anything. You export a payouts CSV from your processor and a deposits CSV from your bank — files you already have, using logins you already have — and upload both here. The comparison runs once, in your browser, and produces a review queue for whatever did not match. Nothing is granted access to your accounts, nothing keeps running after you close the tab, and there is no account to make, cancel, or pay for.

QuestionWhere to answer it
I want every processor transaction posted into QuickBooks or Xero automatically, every day, across several sales channels. Synder or a comparable paid sync tool — continuous, itemized posting is exactly its job, and this workbench does not touch your books.
I have a payouts export and a bank export and want to know which ones do not line up, right now. This workbench — upload both CSVs, get a review queue, no account to connect.
I do not want to grant a third party API access to my payment processor or accounting software account. This workbench — it reads two files locally and connects to nothing. Synder's whole job requires that access.
I want the tool to keep matching new payouts as they arrive without me doing anything. Synder or a comparable paid sync tool — this workbench has no memory between visits and no background process; you re-run it on a fresh export.

Neither tool is the wrong choice in general — they answer different questions. A business already running QuickBooks or Xero and reconciling many channels every day is the case a paid sync tool is built for. A one-off check, a periodic spot review, or a business that does not want to connect its bank and processor logins to a third service is the case this page is built for.

Common questions

Should I use this workbench or a paid sync tool like Synder?

They do different jobs. Synder is a paid subscription that connects to your payment processor account and your QuickBooks or Xero account, then posts every individual transaction to a clearing account and keeps syncing on an ongoing basis so daily bookkeeping stays current. This workbench does none of that: it is a free, browser-only page that reads two CSV files you already exported and matches them once, for a one-off check or a periodic spot review. If you want continuous, connected bookkeeping across many sales channels, a paid sync tool is built for that job. If you want to check a batch of payouts against the bank without connecting anything, this page does that with nothing to sign up for.
