What this does and doesn't do

This applies Turo's documented plan x booking-window host-share matrix to the numbers you enter, using commonly cited default rates you can adjust by choosing a different plan and window. It does not know your specific listing's real booking history, promotions or delivery discounts that can lower your effective rate, or whatever Turo's structure is on the day you read this — Turo has changed this exact structure more than once within 2026. Common questions

How much does Turo actually take from hosts in 2026?

There is no single answer — Turo restructured host earnings around March 2026 into three named earnings plans (Peace of Mind, Balanced, More Earnings), and within each plan your host share also depends on how far ahead of the trip the guest booked (0-2, 3-13, 14-27, or 28+ days). The same trip can pay a host anywhere from roughly 65% to 100% of the trip price depending on which cell of that matrix it falls into. This calculator models the whole matrix rather than assuming one flat percentage.

Why does the booking window change my payout for the exact same trip?

Turo's host share locks in at the moment the guest books, and it rewards trips booked further in advance — a trip booked 28+ days out pays a higher host share than the identical trip booked 0-2 days before it starts, under every earnings plan. That is a real, sizeable dollar difference on the same listing, not a rounding effect, which is what the booking-window comparison on this page is for.

What is the trade-off between the three earnings plans, besides the percentage?

Each plan pairs a host share with a damage-responsibility cap — the dollar amount you can be held responsible for if the vehicle is damaged on a trip. Peace of Mind pays the lowest share but caps your exposure lowest (commonly cited around $250); More Earnings pays the highest share but raises your exposure cap (commonly cited around $2,750). A higher host share is not free money — it comes with more risk on your own vehicle.
