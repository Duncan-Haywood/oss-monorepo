What is a good effective rate for card processing?

It is the wrong question, and asking it is how most small businesses end up comparing two quotes on the one number that cannot tell them apart. The effective rate is total fees divided by total card sales, so it moves with your average ticket and your card mix at least as much as it moves with your pricing.

The three components, and which one is yours to argue about

Interchange is the portion the card-issuing bank keeps. The card networks publish the schedules; they apply identically to every merchant in a category, and no processor sells them to you cheaper.

Assessments are a smaller slice the networks themselves keep — roughly a tenth of a percent plus a couple of cents. Also pass-through, also not negotiable.

Processor markup is everything left over: the percentage and per-transaction fee the processor adds, plus every recurring monthly fee. This is the entire negotiable surface, and on a bundled statement it is the one number nobody prints.

What to do with one statement

  1. Take total card sales, transaction count and total fees off the summary page.
  2. Estimate what share of sales were taken in person rather than keyed or online — card-not-present interchange is genuinely higher, and that part is not your processor’s to give back.
  3. Add up the flat monthly lines: statement, PCI compliance, gateway, batch, monthly minimum.
  4. Split the bill into pass-through and markup, then price the same month under any quote you have been offered. A quote that will not state its markup and monthly fees as separate line items cannot be compared to anything.

Common questions

What is a good effective rate for credit card processing?

There is no single good number, for the reason the table above shows. The comparable figures are the processor’s markup over interchange and the flat monthly fees.

How do I work out my effective rate?

Divide one month’s total fees by that month’s total card sales. $1,300 on $42,000 is 3.10%. Include the flat monthly lines in the fee total — leaving them out is how a rate that looks competitive turns out not to be.

What part of my processing bill can actually be negotiated?

The processor’s markup and the recurring monthly fees. Interchange and assessments are pass-through, collected on behalf of the issuing bank and the network.

Is flat-rate pricing more expensive than interchange-plus?

Usually, and the gap grows with volume — but not always. Flat-rate charges one number for every card, so a business whose customers mostly pay by debit is subsidising one whose customers mostly pay by rewards card. Price your own statement both ways before switching.

What are the monthly fees on a merchant statement for?

They are processor revenue, and they do not fall when your rate does. At $45 a month they are $540 a year, which is worth pricing as its own line rather than rounding away.
