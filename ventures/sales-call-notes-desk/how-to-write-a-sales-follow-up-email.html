How to write a sales follow-up email after a call

The follow-up email decides whether the next call happens. It should take two minutes to write and be impossible to misread: what you heard, who does what by when, and the one thing you still need. Here is the method, with a worked example produced from the call notes below by the free Sales Call Notes Desk.

The five rules

  1. Send it the same day. A recap three days later reads as an afterthought.
  2. Recap their problem in their words. A quoted sentence proves you listened better than any adjective.
  3. Date every next step, yours and theirs. An undated step is a hope. The tool flags a call with no dated next step.
  4. Ask for at most two things. Pick from decision maker, timeline and budget, whichever the call left blank.
  5. Add nothing you did not hear. No new price, discount or promise. A number first appearing in the recap reopens the call in writing.

Worked example

The notes (invented):

Call with Dana Reyes (Ops Director), Brightline Logistics, 45 min.
Dana: Right now dispatch builds the weekly routes by hand in spreadsheets. It takes two people about 12 hours a week and we still get errors, maybe 8% of stops are wrong.
Dana: We looked at RouteWorks last year but it was too expensive for our size.
Dana: Budget is roughly $20k a year, I'd have to check with Marcus (CFO) because he signs off on anything over $15k.
Dana: We have to be live before peak season starts on January 15.
Dana: Security review with their IT team takes about three weeks. Must have a QuickBooks integration.
Me: I'll send the sample route comparison by Tuesday.
Dana: I'll loop in Marcus and get you thirty minutes with him.
Me: I'll book a follow-up with Marcus for next Thursday.

What I heard:

- It takes two people about 12 hours a week and we still get errors, maybe 8% of stops are wrong.

What we agreed:
- I will send the sample route comparison by Tuesday (Tue, Oct 20)

On your side, as I noted it:
- “I'll loop in Marcus and get you thirty minutes with him”

Let me know if I got anything wrong.

Sam

Questions

How long should a sales follow-up email be?

Under 200 words. It is read on a phone between meetings, so the recap, the agreed steps with dates and one question fit and nothing else needs to.

When should I send the follow-up email after a call?

The same day, while both sides remember the call. A recap sent three days later reads as an afterthought and gives the other side time to cool off.

Should the follow-up email include pricing?

Only a number the contact said or you both agreed on. A price that first appears in the recap reopens the call in writing. If a quote is the agreed next step, say it is coming by a date and send it separately.

What if the contact said send me some info?

Treat it as a request for a meeting, not a document. Ask what they would want to see in it and book fifteen minutes to go through it together, because a deck sent into silence is rarely read.
