New Jersey certified payroll: the MW-562 for the NJ Wage Hub

New Jersey's P.L. 2023, c.138 was enacted on 16 August 2023 and took effect 365 days later, in August 2024. Since then, contractors and subcontractors on New Jersey public works submit each week's certified payroll online, through the NJ Wage Hub run by the Department of Labor and Workforce Development (NJDOL), and still send it to the public body as well. The form behind it is NJDOL's MW-562, "Payroll Certification for Public Works Projects".

Where it goesThe NJ Wage Hub (njwages.nj.gov), and directly to the public body
SinceAugust 2024 (P.L. 2023, c.138; N.J.S.A. 34:11-56.29)
How oftenWeekly, one certified payroll per week ending date; the Hub emails a reminder every Monday at 10 AM Eastern for each project you are named on
HowTyped in (or a previous week duplicated and edited), an electronically filled MW-562 PDF, the Hub's two-file CSV template, or a Quantum Project Manager export

The four ways in

  • Type it in. The Hub's form has four steps: company details, employee hours worked and deductions, fringe benefits, and the certification. Duplicate & Edit copies a previous week when the same crew keeps working the job.
  • Upload a filled MW-562. Fill NJDOL's fillable PDF on a computer, save it, click "Add Certified Payroll" and drop the file in. The Hub reads it into the four steps and lists invalid inputs and missing fields per step for you to fix before you submit. This route needs no special software.
  • Upload the CSV template. NJDOL's zip holds two CSVs (hours and deductions for step 2, fringe benefits for step 3) and a file describing their headers; one row per employee, and only those two files can be uploaded.
  • Upload a Quantum Project Manager export, if you already use that program.

NJDOL's user guide warns that the Hub's step numbers "don't exactly match up" with the sections of the paper MW-562, so check each step against your form after an upload.

What the MW-562 asks for that the WH-347 does not

The hours, rates, gross, deductions and net are the same numbers as on the federal WH-347. New Jersey's form adds:

  • Your nine-digit FEIN and your NJ Public Works Contractor Registration certificate number. Every contractor and subcontractor on NJ public work has to be registered first.
  • The date the week's wages were due and paid, and the dates over fixed Sunday-to-Saturday day columns, so the week has to end on a Saturday to fit the form.
  • Each worker's address, sex (M, F or N), race (the form's key: W, B, A, N, I, M) and ethnicity (H or N).
  • A job title next to the work classification: apprentice, journeyman, foreman.
  • Federal and state income tax in separate columns, where the WH-347 has one withholding column, plus two labelled "other" deduction columns.
  • Fringe benefits per hour by type in section 4(c): health and welfare, vacation and holiday, apprenticeship and training, pension, and any other by name, with the plan administrator; boxes 4(a) (paid to plans) and 4(b) (paid in cash).
  • A "No Work Week" box and a "Final Certification" box for the last payroll on the project.
  • An electronic signature: a box acknowledging it has the same legal effect as a written one, your full name and the date.

The form has 10 worker rows. A bigger crew in one week needs the CSV template or typing it into the Hub.

Mistakes to check before you send

  • Resubmitting to fix one number. A submitted payroll cannot be edited. A corrected one goes in with the same week ending date, and the Hub keeps the last one for that week as the correct version, so the correction has to carry the whole week, not just the changed line.
  • A no-work week left blank. Tick "No Work Week" rather than leaving the employee fields empty; the Hub flags empty fields as errors.
  • Skipping the public body. The Hub does not replace the copy the public body is owed.
  • Waiting for a project that is not there. A subcontractor does not add the project: the prime or the public body does, with your FEIN and registration number. If the prime never registers it, NJDOL says to send your payrolls to the public body as you did before the Hub.
  • An old form. Use the MW-562 the Wage Hub FAQ links as the latest version.

Keep one set of numbers

The usual mistake is retyping the week: once on the WH-347 a federal job or the GC wants, once into the MW-562 or the Hub. Two copies drift. Compute the week once, then produce each form from it, so gross, deductions and net agree everywhere.

Questions

Do I still send certified payroll to the public body?

Yes. NJDOL's Wage Hub FAQ says the statute, P.L. 2023, c.138 (N.J.S.A. 34:11-56.29), requires certified payrolls both in the Wage Hub and directly to the public body.

Can I fix a certified payroll after I submit it?

A submitted payroll cannot be edited. Submit a corrected one with the same week ending date: the Hub time-stamps each submission and treats the last one for that week as the correct version.

As a subcontractor, do I add the project to the Wage Hub?

No. The general contractor or the public body enters the project and adds subcontractors by FEIN and registration certificate number. If the prime never registers the project, NJDOL says to send your certified payrolls to the public body as before.

What do I need to register for the Wage Hub?

A myNJ account, the nine-digit FEIN, your Public Works Contractor Registration number and the 10-digit Contractor Registration Reference ID that came by email when you applied. An NJ-927 is no longer needed.
