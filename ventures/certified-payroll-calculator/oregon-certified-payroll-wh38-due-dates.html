Oregon certified payroll: WH-38 due dates and the 25% hold

On an Oregon public works job, every contractor and subcontractor files a certified statement for each week it has a worker on the job, and files them once a month, by the fifth business day of the following month, with the public agency (ORS 279C.845(4)). Miss that date and money stops: the next tier up must hold back 25 percent of what you earned until you file. The form is the Bureau of Labor and Industries' (BOLI) Payroll and Certified Statement, the WH-38.

Where it goesThe public agency on the contract; on a funded project with no agency contract, the agency that funds or will occupy it (OAR 839-025-0010(7), (8))
How oftenA statement for each week with a worker on the job, submitted monthly by the fifth business day of the next month
FormBOLI's WH-38; the Enhanced WH-38 on a qualifying agency's public improvement contract over $3 million (OAR 839-025-0010(1)(a), (2)). Your own form is allowed if it has every WH-38 element and the WH-38's certification statement
If late25% of the unpaid amount earned since the last filing is held, on top of other retainage, and paid within 14 days of filing (ORS 279C.845(7), (8); OAR 839-025-0010(4)-(6))
Keep themThree years from completion of the contract (ORS 279C.845(5))

Due dates for 2027

Business days counted Monday to Friday, less Oregon's legal holidays (ORS 187.010, with a Saturday holiday kept on the Friday and a Sunday one on the Monday). Neither the statute nor BOLI's rule defines "business day", and a day the Governor adds as a holiday (ORS 187.020) cannot be known ahead, so treat these as the latest safe dates and file earlier when you can.

A week that runs across two months (Sunday 29 November to Saturday 5 December 2026, say) is not placed by the statute. Filing it with the earlier month is the safe reading: it is then due 7 December, not 8 January. Ask the public agency which it expects.

What the 25% hold costs

ORS 279C.845(7) has the public agency retain 25 percent of any amount the prime contractor earned until the prime has filed; (8) has the prime retain 25 percent of any amount a first-tier subcontractor earned until that sub has filed, and the prime has to verify the filing before it releases the money. BOLI's rule works the amount: 25 percent "of the unpaid amount earned ... at the time each payroll and certified statement are due" since the last statements were filed. Its own example: a first-tier sub that misses the fifth after earning $100,000 since its last filing has $25,000 held, until it files. Earlier holds are left out of the next one, and the hold comes on top of the contract's ordinary retainage (OAR 839-025-0010(5)(a)-(b), (6)). Once filed, it is paid within 14 days.

The statute's hold names the prime and first-tier subs only. A second-tier sub's statements are still due on the same date, and its subcontract can set its own terms.

A WH-347 is not enough

BOLI's handbook is plain that a federal WH-347 alone does not satisfy Oregon's filing rule. If you keep one payroll for both, attach the signed certified statement from the WH-38 (OAR 839-025-0010(3)(a)) and make sure the payroll carries what ORS 279C.845(3) and the handbook ask for:

  • each worker's name and address (PayApps' WH-347 PDF does not print it), work classification, rate of pay, daily and weekly hours and gross wages for the week;
  • deductions and net pay;
  • fringe benefits for each worker by plan name, with the hourly credit taken for each plan, or fringe paid as wages;
  • hours split by classification when a worker works in more than one in a day;
  • owners, managers and supervisors who earned prevailing wage that week;
  • a signature: an unsigned statement does not meet the filing requirement.

On an enhanced reporting project the Enhanced WH-38 also asks each worker's journey or apprentice hours, race and ethnicity, gender and veteran status (ORS 279C.533(5)).

Questions

Is a WH-347 enough on an Oregon public works job?

No. BOLI's handbook says a WH-347 alone does not satisfy its filing rule. A payroll on another form must carry every element of BOLI's WH-38 and its certification statement (OAR 839-025-0010(3)(a)), signed.

When are Oregon certified payroll statements due?

Once a month, by the fifth business day of the following month, with the public agency, covering each week you had a worker on the job (ORS 279C.845(4)).

What happens if I file certified payroll late in Oregon?

The public agency must retain 25 percent of any amount the prime earned until the prime files (ORS 279C.845(7)), and the prime must retain 25 percent of any amount a first-tier subcontractor earned until that sub files (ORS 279C.845(8)). The amount is 25 percent of the unpaid amount earned since the last statements were filed, on top of other retainage, and is paid within 14 days of filing (OAR 839-025-0010(4)-(6)).

Do I file with BOLI or with the public agency?

With the public agency (ORS 279C.845(4)). On a project with no public agency contract, such as one a public agency funds, the statements go to the agency that provides the funds or will occupy the project (OAR 839-025-0010(7), (8)).
