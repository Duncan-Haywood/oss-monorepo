This calculator vs. doing it by hand

Certified payroll overtime is arithmetic anyone can do with a calculator or a spreadsheet. The problem is one non-obvious rule: cash paid directly to a worker in lieu of fringe counts toward the overtime "regular rate" and must itself carry the time-and-a-half premium, while a bona fide plan contribution never does — and a hand calculation built for ordinary hourly overtime has no reason to know the difference exists.

Common questions

What's the most common mistake when calculating certified payroll overtime by hand?

Applying the overtime premium to the base cash rate only, and paying any fringe paid directly to the worker in cash at straight time even during overtime hours. Under 29 CFR 5.32 and 29 CFR 778.7, cash-in-lieu-of-fringe is part of the overtime "regular rate" and must itself get the time-and-a-half premium. A bona fide plan contribution is excluded and never gets the premium — the two figures are easy to swap by hand.

Why do payroll systems and spreadsheets get this wrong by default?

Most general-purpose payroll software and hand-built spreadsheets compute overtime as 1.5x the base hourly rate, because that is correct for a plain hourly job with no fringe. Certified payroll on a Davis-Bacon or prevailing-wage job adds a second required figure, the fringe rate, and the rule for which part of it counts toward the regular rate is specific to government-contract wage law — not something a general payroll setup applies unless it was built for this.

Is a spreadsheet formula good enough once it's built correctly?

Only until the mix changes. A formula built for one classification with a fixed split between bona fide and cash-in-lieu fringe silently gives the wrong overtime rate the week a worker's cash-fringe portion changes, or the week someone copies last week's blended rate forward instead of recomputing it from this week's actual pay.

Does this calculator check whether my contract is covered by Davis-Bacon?

No. Whether a contract is covered, which wage determination applies, and a worker's approved apprentice rate all come from the contracting officer's wage determination and the registered apprenticeship agreement. Both a hand calculation and this calculator take those figures as inputs already looked up; neither one guesses them.

Does this generate or submit my WH-347 certified payroll report?

No, and neither does a spreadsheet on its own. This calculator checks the underlying arithmetic — required vs. actual compensation, the correctly computed overtime rate, and the apprentice ratio — for one classification, one week, so you can carry the right numbers into a certified payroll report. It does not know your project name, contract number, or DOL's exact form layout, and it does not submit or sign anything.
