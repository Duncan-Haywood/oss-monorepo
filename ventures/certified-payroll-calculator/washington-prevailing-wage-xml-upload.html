Washington certified payroll: the L&I PWIA XML upload

Every contractor on a Washington public works project files certified payroll online with the Department of Labor & Industries at least once a month (RCW 39.12.120), in L&I's Prevailing Wage Intents and Affidavits (PWIA) system. Weekly payrolls can be typed in, or sent as an XML file ("Submit Payroll in XML File") that takes several weeks at once.

Since1 January 2020, all public works
How oftenAt least monthly
HowOnline entry, or an XML file on L&I's schema

What the L&I PWIA file holds that the WH-347 does not

The hours, rates, fringes and deductions are the same numbers as on the federal WH-347. The state's file wraps them with more:

  • The project's Intent ID (from the intent to pay prevailing wages you filed).
  • Each worker's full SSN and home address (the WH-347 asks only for the last four digits).
  • For every row, a four-letter L&I trade code (99 codes) and the county the work was in, plus L&I's job classification.
  • For apprentices: the ARTS ID, the registering state (WA, OR, MT or AK), occupation, step and step hours.
  • Fringe credits in L&I's five fixed benefit fields, by type, with other plans named.
  • Double-time hours and rate where overtime is paid at double time.
  • A no-work week, sent as a file with the no-work flag and no employees.

Upload errors to check before you send

  • Trade codes in capitals and the county in Title Case, as the upload guide asks.
  • SSNs checked against the patterns the SSA never issues; no duplicates.
  • The apprentice's state one of WA, OR, MT or AK; apprentice rows carry a step, not a job class.
  • Obsolete elements (WageID, the apprentice program IDs) left out.
  • L&I's own upload step compares your rates with the project's journey-level and apprentice wages: a rate below them is caught there, after you have built the file.

Keep one set of numbers

The usual mistake is retyping the week: once on the WH-347 for the GC, once into the state portal. Two copies drift. Compute the week once, then produce the WH-347 and the state's file from it, so gross, deductions and net agree on both.

Questions

How often do I file?

At least once a month, on every public works project, under RCW 39.12.120. Several weekly payrolls can go in one XML upload.

Do I report weeks with no work?

The schema has a no-work flag for a week with no employees on the job.

Is the WH-347 enough in Washington?

Not on state public works: L&I wants the payroll in its own system. A federal job may also need a WH-347 for the GC; the contract says.
