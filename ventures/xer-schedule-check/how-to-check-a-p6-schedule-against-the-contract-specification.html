How to check a P6 schedule against the contract specification

A contractor's baseline schedule, and every monthly update after it, is a submittal. The contract's scheduling section (CSI 01 32 16 on many building jobs, UFGS 01 32 01.00 10 on US federal work, a progress-schedule article in a state DOT's standard specifications) says what it must look like, and the owner's reviewer checks it against that list before accepting it. Without tooling that means filtering a P6 layout once per requirement, or reading a PDF by eye. This page does the list in one pass from the XER, with your contract's numbers.

Open the free schedule check (open the XER, then open Rules from your contract)

Steps

  1. Export the schedule from P6 as XER (File, Export, Primavera PM XER), or save a Microsoft Project schedule as XML.
  2. Open it on the tool page and go to Contract specification check.
  3. Open Rules from your contract and type the limits your specification sets. Leave a rule blank to skip it. Nothing is filled in for you, because every contract differs.
  4. Press Check against the specification. Each requirement shows met or not met, with what the file shows; open a missed one to see every activity or relationship behind it.
  5. Press Save rules (JSON) to keep the rules for next month, and Download results (CSV) for the full list to attach to review comments.

What it found on public schedules

With one invented rule set (20-day durations, 10-day lags, no leads), on two public P6 files (the project's eval/spec-check-2026-11-24.md names them and gives all six): the hotel schedule had 277 open activities over 20 working days, the longest being 330-day design submission and approval rows that most specifications would exempt or ask to split, 547 lags over 10 days and one lead; the airport terminal file had 21 activities over 20 days, 7 long lags and one start-on-or-before constraint. These files were never submitted against those rules; the numbers show what the list looks like, not how good the schedules are.

How it was tested

On six public XER files (52 to 16,146 activities), each rule's count of failures was recounted straight from the raw XER tables without the scheduling model, and all 64 counts agreed. The check takes about half a second on the 16,146-activity file.

Things to know

  • Read the specification, not a summary of it. Many sections carry exceptions (procurement, owner activities, the first and last milestones) and definitions this list cannot know. Type the exceptions in as exempt words where they are by name.
  • Met is not accepted. The check says whether the file meets the numbers you typed. Whether a submittal is acceptable is the contract reviewer's call.
  • Run the 14-point check too. DCMA's metrics (missing logic, high float, negative float, the critical path test) sit alongside most specifications and are on the same page.

Questions

What does a construction scheduling specification require?

It depends on the contract. Common requirements are a cap on activity length (often 15 to 20 working days for construction work, with procurement and submittals exempt), a cap on lags, no leads, limits on which date constraints may be used, activity codes for responsibility and area on every activity, the schedule cost-loaded to the contract value, the contract milestones shown, and retained logic. Read your own contract's section: this tool has no rules built in.

Is this an official USACE, DOT or CSI checker?

No. Nothing here is any agency's specification. You type the limits from your own contract, and the tool checks the file against what you typed. The result is a review aid, not acceptance or rejection of a submittal, which stays with the contract's reviewer.

Can I reuse the same rules every month?

Yes. Press Save rules (JSON) once and Load rules for each update. The page also remembers the last rules you ran on this device. From the command line, node cli.mjs update.xer --spec rules.json exits 1 when a rule is not met, so a folder of updates can be screened in a script.

How are durations and lags counted?

In working days: an activity's original duration in hours divided by the hours per day of its own calendar, and a lag in hours divided by the hours per day of its predecessor's calendar. Milestones, level of effort, WBS summaries and finished activities are left out of the duration rule.

Is the file uploaded?

No. The schedule and the rules are read in your browser tab. There is no server, account or upload.
