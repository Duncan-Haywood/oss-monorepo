# The engine against P6 23.12 on 338 public probe files, 2026-11-24

**Question.** The open item was relationship dates (TASKPRED `aref`/`arls`)
on start-to-finish links and on SS links whose lag runs on a calendar other
than the predecessor's (`eval/relationship-dates-started-2026-11-24.md`, Not
checked). The six P6 files hold three SF links and no such SS link. Is there
public P6 output that covers them, and what does it say about the engine?

**Data.** A code search for `TASKPRED aref arls extension:xer` (808 hits)
turned up `danafitkowski/cpp-cpm-engine` (MIT), commit `f4554cb`,
`validation/p6-probes/`: 338 synthetic XERs in 35 folders, each imported into
Primavera P6 Professional 23.12.1 and scheduled once (F9) by the repository's
author, who read P6's early start and finish, late start and finish, total
and free float for every unfinished activity back from P6's database into the
folder's `p6-values.json`. Each folder's README says what it probes (As Late
As Possible work under way, SF links into started work, progress override,
mandatory constraints, resource-dependent work, lag calendars). Fetch: `bash
eval/corpus-fetch.sh DIR --probes`. Not committed, like the rest of the
corpus.

The probe XERs are P6's *inputs*: their `aref`/`arls` are empty. So they
cannot decode relationship dates. They are a second P6 oracle for the engine
itself, with 80 files holding SF links and 43 holding an SS link off the
predecessor's calendar.

**Method.** `node eval/p6-probes.mjs DIR/p6-probes --json
eval/p6-probes-2026-11-24.json` (0.5 s). An activity matches when all six of
P6's values agree: dates to the minute, floats to 0.01 h. Work under way is
compared on its remaining early start and remaining late start. The probes
were written to isolate hard cases, so the share is not a measure of how the
engine does on a real schedule. The six real files are that measure
(`eval/cpm-vs-p6.mjs`: 19,908 of 19,935 before and after this change).

**The biggest gap is resource-dependent work.** The engine schedules a
`TT_Rsrc` activity on its own calendar (`cpm.mjs`, header); P6 schedules it on
its resources' calendars and assignment lags (the probe READMEs, h9 to h17,
h23 to h28). It misses 780 of 936 such rows, and their successors follow. The
six real files barely show it: their resource-dependent work shares one
calendar with its resources. A real file whose crews work other hours than
the activity calendar would. That is the next item (NEXT.md).

## One rule: an SF link into a started successor

Probes `h6s-an`, `h6s-ay`, `h6s-as` (retained logic) and `h4s-a`, `h4s-ar`.
As Late As Possible work slides by its free float. With an SF link into a
successor already under way, the engine skipped the link ("an SF link is
met") and slid the work to the project's end (total float 0). P6 holds it:

    remaining start <= successor's early finish - unspent SF lag
    unspent = max(0, lag - working time from the predecessor's actual start
              to the data date)      (all of it when not started)

and the same in the backward pass with the successor's late finish. For a
2026-03-02 start and a 03-11 data date (56 h spent), the successor finishing
03-20 17:00:

| Probe row | Lag | Unspent | P6 remaining start | Engine before | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1X, A2X, A3X | 0, 16, 56 | 0 | 03-23 08:00 | 05-19 08:00 | 03-23 08:00 |
| A4X | 64 | 8 | 03-20 08:00 | 05-19 08:00 | 03-20 08:00 |
| A5X | 80 | 24 | 03-18 08:00 (late start 05-15) | 05-19 (05-19) | 03-18 (05-15) |
| A9X (not started) | 40 | 40 | 03-16 08:00 (late start 05-13) | 05-19 (05-19) | 03-16 (05-13) |
| A10X (started 02-24) | 40 | 0 | 03-18 13:00, after lunch | 05-06 08:00 | 03-18 13:00 |

**Under progress override** an SF link out of work already under way does not
hold it (h4s-a A11X: P6 slides it to 05-19 13:00, total float 0), while one
out of work not started does (A8X). The probe's README lists A11 with the
links that "still bind"; P6's own values say otherwise, and the engine
follows the values. Fixed: 24 rows (21 on h6s, 3 on h4s), 0 made worse.

`cpm.mjs` `sfActiveSucc` (on; `variant: { sfActiveSucc: false }` restores the
old reading); test in `engine.test.mjs` on the same shape. These rows are
fitted: the rule was read off them, so they are not a held-out test. The
other 2,929 rows were not used to fit anything here.

## SF relationship dates

The three SF links in the seven P6 files, read with the same shapes as the
other types:

    aref = (pred early start + lag) less the successor's duration
    arls = succ late finish - lag - the predecessor's duration

| File | Link | Lag | `aref` | `arls` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CCA (P6 6.0) | PP3DP21113C20 -> PP3EM21106C0, both not started | 0 | pred ES - 144 h (succ duration) | succ LF - 96 h (pred duration) |
| hotel (P6 6.0) | HCSWSSZ1010 -> HMMOAZ020, both not started | 8 | pred ES + 8 - 152 h | succ LF - 8 - 88 h |
| Harbour Pointe (P6 17.12) | EC1150 (finished) -> EC2020 | 0 | (not read: finished) | succ LF |

`reldates.mjs` now decodes SF (`impliedLags`; started links count an SF lag
from the predecessor's actual start, as SS). `node eval/relationship-dates.mjs
DIR/xer`: `arls` **40,478 of 40,478** unstarted links (the two SF added),
`aref` 40,385; started links **11,611 of 11,611** (Harbour Pointe's SF
added); planted edits unchanged on CCA (200: 114 exact, 7 at most, 0 wrong,
0 missed), and Harbour Pointe's 190 (now including its SF link): 60 exact, 4
at most, 126 invisible, 0 wrong, 0 missed. Three links is a small sample: the
formulas are the only ones that fit both unstarted links on both dates, not a
large-sample result.
