# Windows analysis against planted delays, 2026-11-19

**Question.** Given a series of P6 updates, does `windows.mjs` put them in
order, split each window's finish movement correctly into progress and
revisions, name the activity or link behind a planted delay, and add the
windows up to the whole movement?

**Data.** No public series of real consecutive P6 updates was found (see
`compare-check-2026-11-19.md`). So each of the six public XER files of
`cpm-vs-p6-2026-11-19.md` (not committed) is the first update of a series
made by `status.mjs`: statused as planned four times, 20 working days of the
project calendar apart, with known changes planted.

**Method.** `node eval/windows-check.mjs <dir> --seeds 6 --json eval/windows-check-2026-11-19.json`.
Per file and seed, in the file's largest project:

| Window | Planted | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | nothing (as planned) | finish moved 0, no drivers named |
| 2 | 1-5 days added to the remaining duration of a driving-path activity under way or due in the window | revisions 0 |
| 3 | 1-5 days of lag on an FS link between two unstarted driving-path activities (odd seeds), or that much more duration on the predecessor (even seeds) | progress 0 |
| 4 | both, on other activities | both sides |

The planted number of days is what was typed. It is not always what the
finish moves by, so the expected split is measured from counterfactual files:
the progress effect is the finish of the window's file built with the slip and
without the revision, against the previous update; the revision effect is the
rest. Checks: the split equals that exactly; the side is right (a slip-only
window shows revisions 0, a revision-only window progress 0); the planted
activity or link is among the window's drivers; the windows' sum equals the
first-to-last movement; the updates come back in data-date order after being
handed over shuffled.

**Where nominal days differ from the effect (22 windows), and why.**

- CCA: its driving path runs from a 6-day calendar with holidays onto a
  calendar-day one before the finish, so a slip of 2 working days on the first
  can move the finish 6.4 days on the project calendar. A slip on
  T12CO04405C0 moved nothing: other work keeps the same finish.
- Expressway: BEW/MO/NC/1030 drives its successor through an SS link, so
  lengthening its finish moves nothing (4 windows); BEW/PR/FC/1040 is on a 7-day
  calendar and its successor on a 5-day one, so a lag lands on a weekend and
  rounds up a day.
- example and terminal: in window 4 the lag moved the path, so the slipped
  activity was no longer driving and its slip moved nothing (progress 0).
- hotel: its driving path as walked from the finish is one as-late-as-possible
  milestone with no driving predecessor, so no slip or lag candidate was
  found and nothing was planted. Its windows only check as-planned (0) and
  adding up.

**Found on the way and fixed.** `halfStepXer` (compare.mjs) took the current
update's remaining duration for every activity, started or not. On an
activity still unstarted in both updates that is a re-estimate, a revision,
so a re-estimate on the driving path was reported as progress. The example
series' May window (paint 8 to 11 days, unstarted) read progress +3,
revisions 0; it now reads progress 0, revisions +3. The example pair's split
went from +6 / +9 to +8 / +7 (paint shortened 10 to 8 days in that update).
`compare-check.mjs` after the fix, five of the six files (CCA not rerun):
600 of 600 planted edits, 0 extra, progress-only 100 of 100, unchanged.

**What this does not show.** That the split matches a real job's updates or
P6's own half-step: the series are generated by statusing as planned with this
tool's CPM engine, and the expected split is measured with the same engine.
What it does show is that the windows are put in order, that progress and
revision changes land on the right side, and that a planted change is named
in its window. Wanted: a real series of updates of one project.
