How to assess a schedule against the GAO Schedule Assessment Guide

The GAO Schedule Assessment Guide (GAO-16-89G) is what US federal auditors read a programme's schedule against, and what agencies and their contractors prepare for. It sets out ten best practices under four characteristics: a reliable schedule is comprehensive, well-constructed, credible and controlled. Scoring a schedule against it by hand means filtering the same P6 layouts again and again; paid analytics products sell a GAO metric library per seat. The free tool here scores the ten practices straight from the XER (or a Microsoft Project XML file), lists every test with its threshold, and names the activities behind every failure.

Open the free schedule check (open the XER, then press Score against the GAO guide)

Steps

  1. Export the update from P6 as XER (File, Export, Primavera PM XER), or save the Microsoft Project file as XML.
  2. Open it on the tool page. If the file holds several projects, pick one.
  3. Under GAO Schedule Assessment Guide, optionally add the baseline project's XER, so practice 10 is scored against the real baseline, and press Score against the GAO guide.
  4. Read the four characteristics, then open each practice: every failed test lists its activities. Download the CSV for the full lists, or the printable scorecard to attach to a review.

What is tested, practice by practice

PracticeCharacteristicTests from the file
1. Capturing all activitiesComprehensiveOpen work filed under a WBS element, not the project node; activity names that stand alone; a finish milestone
2. Sequencing all activitiesWell-constructedDCMA logic, leads, lags, relationship types and hard constraints; logic loops
3. Assigning resourcesComprehensiveOpen work with a resource or cost assignment (DCMA 10); every resource used has a maximum units per time to level against
4. Establishing durationsComprehensiveDCMA high duration; unstarted zero-duration tasks that should be milestones; tasks under half a working day
5. Horizontal and vertical traceabilityCredibleDCMA critical path test; open finishes; stored dates that follow the logic; milestones driven by the work they summarise
6. A valid critical pathWell-constructedA driving path of work from the data date to the finish; no date constraints or lags on it; CPLI; P6's own longest-path flags agree
7. Reasonable total floatWell-constructedDCMA high float and negative float
8. Schedule risk analysisCredibleRated only when the file carries three-point duration ranges
9. Updating with progress and logicControlledA data date; no invalid dates; no out-of-sequence progress; rescheduled after the last edits
10. Maintaining a baselineControlledWith the baseline file: activities traced to it by ID, missed tasks and BEI against it. Without: planned dates, DCMA missed tasks and BEI

GAO rates each practice on five levels, and its reports roll practices up to a characteristic by averaging. The tool does the same with one stated rule: a practice is fully met when all its tests pass, substantially at three quarters, partially at half, minimally when any pass, and not met when none do.

How it was tested

On six public P6 files, one fault of each kind GAO asks about and the DCMA points miss was planted at random (an activity moved onto the project node, a duplicated name, a zero-duration task, a milestone cut loose from its work, a lag or a start-on constraint on the driving path), and the scorecard had to name the planted activity under its own test. The results are in the project's eval/ notes.

Things to know

  • A rating is not an audit opinion. GAO's auditors weigh evidence beyond the file: the basis of estimate, the schedule management plan, interviews. The tests are what the file can show.
  • The thresholds are the tool's. The guide gives practices, not numeric limits; each test shows the threshold it used.
  • Without a baseline file, practice 10 uses the planned dates the file keeps, which P6 moves for work not yet started, so it reads better than it should.

Questions

What is the GAO Schedule Assessment Guide?

A guide published by the US Government Accountability Office in December 2015 (GAO-16-89G) setting out ten best practices for a reliable project schedule, grouped under four characteristics: comprehensive, well-constructed, credible and controlled. GAO auditors use it when they review federal programmes' schedules, and agencies and their contractors use it to prepare for those reviews.

How is it different from the DCMA 14-point assessment?

The DCMA 14 points are numeric screening metrics with fixed thresholds, aimed at the network's mechanics. The GAO guide is broader: it asks whether every activity is captured under the WBS, resourced and given a sensible duration, whether the schedule traces from the lowest activity to the milestones, whether the critical path is driven by work rather than constraints and lags, whether a risk analysis was done, and whether a baseline is kept. Several DCMA points answer parts of GAO practices, and this tool uses them there.

Does the guide set numeric thresholds?

Mostly no: it describes practices and gives judgment-based criteria, and GAO's auditors rate each practice on five levels from not met to fully met after weighing the evidence. The tests here use the DCMA's published thresholds where a DCMA point measures the same thing (44 working days, 5 percent) and say so beside each test, so a reviewer can disagree with a threshold and see exactly what changes.

Why is the risk analysis practice not rated?

Because a schedule risk analysis is evidence outside the schedule file: the agreed duration ranges, the simulation and its reported result. The tool rates that practice only when the file itself carries three-point duration ranges in activity user-defined fields, and then only on whether the ranges cover the open work. The tool's own Monte Carlo can run the analysis; keep its report with the schedule.

Is the file uploaded?

No. The schedule is read and scored in your browser tab. There is no server, account or upload.
