Count ranked-choice ballots and show every round

For club, association, board, union and student elections that use ranked ballots. Paste the ballots or open the CSV your form exported. You get the instant-runoff winner with each elimination, a single transferable vote (STV) count when you fill several seats, the Schulze ranking and whether a candidate beats every other head to head, and the full head-to-head table, so anyone on the committee can check the result by hand.

Nothing you paste leaves this browser tab. The three published worked examples in the README count to their stated results, and a separately written Python count agrees on 3,000 random elections.

Who it is for, and when to use something else

It suits a secretary or returning officer who has ranked ballots in a spreadsheet and needs a count the members can follow: a club choosing a chair, a co-op electing three directors, a student union, a staff vote on a name. Each method shows its working, so a disputed result can be checked one round at a time.

Use something else for a public election run under a statute: those need the certified software and the exact rules your election authority sets. If you only need to collect votes, any form tool will do; this page counts them once you have them.

Method and citation

Instant runoff. Each ballot counts for its highest-ranked candidate still in the race. While nobody holds more than half of the ballots still in play, the last-placed candidate is eliminated. A ballot with no candidates left, or whose next live rank is shared by two candidates, is exhausted and stays exhausted.

Single transferable vote. The quota is the Droop quota, floor(valid ballots ÷ (seats + 1)) + 1. A candidate who reaches it is elected; their surplus moves on by the weighted inclusive Gregory method, every paper they hold passing to its next hopeful choice at value surplus ÷ total, truncated to 5 decimal places (what truncation drops is shown as rounding). With no surplus to move, the lowest hopeful is excluded and their papers move on at their current value. When the hopefuls left equal the seats left, they are all elected. With one seat this gives the instant-runoff winner.

Schulze. From the head-to-head table, each win's strength is the number of ballots that preferred the winner; a path's strength is its weakest link, and a candidate ranks above another when its strongest path to them is stronger than theirs back. A candidate who beats every other head to head always comes first. A candidate a ballot leaves unranked counts as below every ranked one.

Ties for elimination or for the largest surplus go to the most recent earlier round where the tied candidates differed, then to a lot drawn from the seed, so the same seed always gives the same count.

Questions

How do I count ranked-choice votes from a Google Form?

Ask the question as a multiple choice grid with the candidates as rows and 1st choice, 2nd choice and so on as columns, then download the responses as CSV from the linked sheet and paste or open the file here. The tool reads each candidate column, ignores the timestamp, and counts the ballots.

What is the difference between instant runoff and STV?

Instant runoff fills one seat: the last-placed candidate is eliminated and their ballots move to the next choice until someone has a majority of the ballots still in play. STV fills several seats: a candidate who reaches the quota is elected and the votes they do not need move on at a reduced value, so each group of voters elects about its share of the seats. With one seat, the two give the same winner.

Why can instant runoff and Schulze pick different winners?

Schulze looks at every head-to-head contest and picks a candidate who beats each other one (the Condorcet winner) when there is one. Instant runoff can eliminate that candidate early for having few first choices. In the four-city example loaded by the button, Nashville beats every other city head to head but instant runoff elects Knoxville.

How are ties broken?

When two candidates are tied for elimination, the one with fewer votes in the most recent earlier round where they differed goes. If they were tied in every round, a lot is drawn from the seed you set, so the same seed always gives the same draw. Many bylaws say how a tie is settled; follow them, and use the count to see exactly where the tie arose.

Can I use this for an official public election?

No. It is not certified for any public election and does not implement any jurisdiction's statute word for word. It suits clubs, associations, boards, unions, student bodies and polls, where you can check the result against the rounds it shows.

Are the ballots uploaded?

No. The count runs in this browser tab. There are no accounts, cookies or analytics vendors, and the command-line version makes no network calls.
