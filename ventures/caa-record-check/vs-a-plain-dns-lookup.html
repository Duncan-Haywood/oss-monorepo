CAA Record Checker vs. a Plain DNS Lookup

dig CAA yourdomain.com, or pasting a hostname into a generic online DNS-record viewer, is the first thing most people reach for when a certificate authority reports "CAA record prevents issuance." It's the right instinct, and it isn't wrong — it's incomplete, in a way that matters specifically for this record type.

What a plain lookup shows you

The raw CAA record text at exactly the name you queried. If that name genuinely has no CAA records, most plain lookup tools report "no CAA record found" and stop there — a perfectly correct answer to the question they asked, which is narrower than the question you actually have.

Common questions

What does a plain DNS lookup (dig CAA, or a generic online CAA viewer) show me?

The raw CAA record text at exactly the hostname you queried — nothing more. If that name has no records, most plain lookup tools report "no CAA record found" and stop there, even when a parent domain actually restricts issuance for it.

Why isn't that enough to know whether a certificate will be blocked?

Because RFC 8659 does not evaluate CAA at only the exact name. A certificate authority walks up the DNS tree to the first ancestor domain that publishes a CAA record set, applies the issuewild-overrides-issue fallback for wildcard certificates, and must refuse issuance entirely if any record carries the critical flag on a tag it doesn't recognize. A raw record dump tells you what's published; it doesn't tell you what those rules mean for a specific certificate authority you're trying to use.

Does this tool replace dig or a plain lookup?

No — both are useful, for different questions. A plain lookup answers "what CAA record is published at this exact name?" quickly, with no interpretation. This tool answers the question that actually matters when a certificate fails to issue: "which certificate authorities are authorized, once the real tree-walk and fallback rules are applied?" It is still the right tool for simply confirming a record you just added is visible. See why a CAA record blocks issuance for the full walkthrough of all four causes.
