This calculator vs. a flat pump-swap special

“$X, any sump pump, done” is not a price for a sump-pump job — it is a price for an existing pit and the primary pump alone, quietly applied to a new basin cut through a slab with a battery backup too.

What a flat pump-swap special is

A flat swap special is the fastest way to quote a sump-pump call over the phone: name a number and go. Customers like it because it is easy to compare against a competitor's coupon, and it is the number a homeowner holding one will ask you to match.

The number behind it was usually set for the simplest version of the job — a pump that has already failed in a pit that already exists, swapped for a like-for-like replacement — and then reused for every call afterward, whether or not the next one needs a new pit cut through a slab, forty feet of discharge, or a battery backup the homeowner asks for once the tech is already there.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You pick a pump size, a pit type — existing pit, new pit in dirt, or a new pit cut through a slab — discharge footage past the included first fifteen feet, a backup (none, battery, or water-powered), whether to add an alarm or freeze protection, and your own wage, payroll burden, travel, overhead, and target margin. It prices the pit, the extra discharge, and the backup as their own lines, on top of the swap itself, and returns the price, the crew-hour split, the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price — for this job, not a flat number for every sump pump.

Common questions

Why does a flat pump-swap special get a new-pit job wrong?

Because the special was priced for an existing pit and the primary pump alone, and the cost drivers for a new-pit, battery-backup job are not in that price at all. Using this calculator's own suggested figures, an existing-pit 1/3 HP swap costs $517.23 to run, and a new pit cut through a slab with a 1/2 HP pump, 40 ft of discharge, and a battery backup costs $2,418.52 to run -- 4.68 times as much, on the same trip and the same crew wage.

What does a flat pump-swap special usually leave out entirely?

The pit and the backup. A pump-swap special has no line for cutting a new basin through a slab or dirt floor, no line for discharge past the first included feet, and no line for a battery or water-powered backup, which this calculator's own suggested kit prices at more than the primary pump itself. It also has no line for a high-water alarm or freeze protection, which are commonly sold alongside a backup.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own suggested figures: the existing-pit swap prices at $738.90, on a job that costs $517.23 to run, holding a 30% target margin. The new pit, 1/2 HP pump, 40 ft of discharge, and battery backup costs $2,418.52 to run, and this calculator prices it at $3,455.03, still the same 30% margin. Quoting that job at the swap special's $738.90 price leaves it $1,679.62 below its own break-even cost -- before counting the $2,716.13 shortfall against what this calculator prices the same job at.

Is a flat pump-swap special ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of jobs -- an existing pit, the primary pump only, no backup, and discharge within the included footage -- a flat swap special can land close, because that is probably the job the price was set on. The risk is quoting a new pit, a battery or water-powered backup, or extra discharge sight unseen, because the special does not know what is under the floor until someone opens it up, and by then the flat number has often already been quoted over the phone.

Does this calculator know my local labor rates or parts costs?

No. It has no data on your local labor market, your supplier's parts pricing, or what other plumbers in your area charge. It takes pump size, pit type, discharge footage past the included first fifteen feet, backup type, alarm, freeze protection, and your own wage, burden, travel, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a price for this job -- the arithmetic a flat swap special skips, not a market survey.
