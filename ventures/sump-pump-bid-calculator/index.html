      Common questions

      Why does a two-and-a-half hour job price at $739?

      Because hours are the smallest part of it. The default swap runs 2.45 hours including a 0.85-hour trip and 0.5 hours of site time, which is $137.89 of loaded labor — but the pump and check valve add $223, travel and equipment $85, and overhead $71.34, for a $517.23 cost before margin. Labor is 26.7% of direct cost here; material is 43.1%. Short jobs are priced by what it costs to show up, not by the clock.

      Why does the trip count as billable hours?

      Because it consumes the same crew hour a wrench does. The 0.85-hour trip allowance is loaded at the same $56.28 rate as the work itself ($42 wage plus 34% burden), which is what stops a half-hour job across town from quietly costing you money. The separate travel-and-equipment line covers the vehicle rather than the person.

      Why is the discharge pipe $0 when I entered 15 feet?

      Because 15 feet is exactly the included allowance. The base price carries the first 15 ft of discharge run, so extra footage is zero and nothing bills at the $6.50 per-foot rate. Enter 30 feet and 15 of them become chargeable. The allowance is editable for the same reason as every other rate on this page — it should match how you actually bundle it.

      Does a backup pump change the answer much?

      Substantially, and it is off by default. The defaults price a primary swap only, with no battery or water-powered backup, no high-water alarm, and no freeze protection on the discharge. Those are the lines that separate a $739 swap from a system a homeowner would actually call protected, so if you are being compared against a quote that includes them, turn them on before comparing.
