Common questions

    
Does the new EU Product Liability Directive cover software and AI?

    
Yes. Directive (EU) 2024/2853 expressly treats software, including apps, firmware and AI systems, and digital manufacturing files as products, whether installed, embedded or delivered through the cloud. It applies to products placed on the market from 9 December 2026.

    
Who is liable if my product is defective?

    
The manufacturer first. Then, in turn, a component maker (jointly), an importer, an authorised representative, a fulfilment provider, and a distributor or online platform in the cases the Directive lists, so that an injured person always has an EU business to claim against.

    
Is open-source software covered?

    
Free and open-source software developed or supplied outside the course of a commercial activity is excluded. If it is supplied commercially, or built into a product someone sells, the business that does so carries the liability.

    
What damage can be claimed and is there a cap?

    
Death, personal injury including recognised psychological harm, damage to property not used exclusively for professional purposes, and destruction or corruption of data not used for professional purposes. The old EUR 500 threshold and the overall cap are gone.

    
How long can claims be brought?

    
Three years from when the claimant knew or should have known, with a 10-year long-stop from placing the product on the market, extended to 25 years for latent personal injury.
