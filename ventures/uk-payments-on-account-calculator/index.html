Common questions

    
Do I have to make payments on account?

    
Only if your last Self Assessment bill was over £1,000 and less than 80% of your total tax was collected at source (for example through PAYE). Below either test, you simply pay the balancing payment each January.

    
How much are the payments on account?

    
Each of the two payments is 50% of last year's Self Assessment bill, the income tax and Class 4 National Insurance not already collected at source. They fall due on 31 January during the tax year and 31 July after it.

    
Why is my first Self Assessment bill so much bigger than I expected?

    
In your first year you pay the full balancing payment on 31 January and the first payment on account for the following year at the same time, then the second payment on account on 31 July. That is about one and a half times a normal year's bill, spread across seven months.

    
Can I reduce my payments on account?

    
Yes, if you expect a lower bill, claim a reduction online or with form SA303. If you reduce them by more than is justified, HMRC charges interest on the shortfall, and can charge a penalty if the claim is careless or false.

    
What if I paid too much on account?

    
HMRC credits the excess against your next payment or refunds it on request after you file.
