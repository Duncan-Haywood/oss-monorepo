Common questions

    
What are payments on account?

    
Advance payments towards next year's Self Assessment bill. Each is 50% of your latest bill (income tax plus Class 4 NIC, after tax already collected at source), due on 31 January and 31 July.

    
Why is my January bill so much bigger than my tax?

    
In January you pay the balancing payment for the year just ended and the first payment on account for the next one. In your first year of self-employment nothing was paid on account, so January asks for about 150% of the year's bill.

    
Do I always have to make payments on account?

    
No. They are not required if your last bill was under £1,000 after tax collected at source, or if 80% or more of your liability was collected at source, for example through PAYE.

    
Can I reduce my payments on account?

    
Yes, if you expect a lower bill next year you can claim to reduce them online or on form SA303. If you reduce too far you pay interest on the shortfall from the original due dates, and a careless or dishonest claim can bring a penalty.

    
What happens if I miss a payment date?

    
HMRC charges late payment interest from the due date, and penalties can follow on a balancing payment that stays unpaid. Contact HMRC about a time-to-pay arrangement if you cannot pay.
