FOR TWITCH STREAMERS

The dashboard number isn't your payout. Take-home is.

"$414 in sub revenue this month" is not what lands in your bank account — your revenue split (50% by default, or 60%/70% under the Plus Program) cuts subs, Bits pay a flat $0.01 each, and mobile subs pay exactly the same as desktop ones despite what the app store fee headlines suggest. This free calculator finds your real monthly take-home and checks it against an income goal.

Plus Points (optional)

Plus Points are 1 per Tier 1 sub, 2 per Tier 2 and 6 per Tier 3; Prime subs earn none. Sources disagree on whether gifted subs count, so lower these counts to what your own Twitch dashboard credits. Levels used here: 100 points for 60%, 300 for 70%. Twitch also requires the level to be sustained, so treat the result as a planning view and confirm on your dashboard.

Split & other income

50% is the default split. Sustaining 100 Plus Points for three consecutive months moves you to 60% under Twitch's Plus Program; 300 Plus Points moves you to 70%. Read yours off your own Twitch dashboard. "Other monthly income" is ads, sponsorships, or donations elsewhere — a figure you supply, since Twitch publishes no ad rate this tool could model honestly.

Common questions

Does a subscription bought on mobile pay me less because of Apple or Google's cut?

No, as of Twitch's October 2024 pricing change. Twitch charges more for a mobile subscription specifically to cover the app store's in-app-purchase fee, so your payout per sub is the same whether the viewer bought it on desktop or in the iOS/Android app. Enter your web tier price here either way — see the worked explainer.

Why is the revenue split 50% for some streamers and 60% or 70% for others?

Everyone starts at 50% of subscription revenue. Affiliates and Partners who sustain 100 Plus Points for three consecutive months move to 60% under Twitch's Plus Program, and those who reach 300 Plus Points move to 70%. Plus Points come only from subs — 1 point per Tier 1, 2 per Tier 2, 6 per Tier 3; Prime subs earn none, and sources disagree on gifted subs, so check what your dashboard credits. This is the one number that actually changes your payout per sub — read your own current split off your Twitch dashboard rather than assuming one.

Why don't Bits follow the same split as subs?

A cheered Bit is worth a flat $0.01 to the streamer no matter what the viewer paid for the Bits bundle or what the streamer's subscription split is — Twitch's own cut is already built into what a viewer pays for Bits before the streamer ever sees the cheer. So Bits income is not reduced by a 50% or 70% split the way sub revenue is.

Does this tool estimate ad revenue?

No. Twitch publishes no standard ad CPM a static calculator could model honestly, and ad payouts vary by campaign and channel. Enter any ad revenue, sponsorship, or other income as the single "other monthly income" figure instead — this tool only computes the parts with documented, deterministic mechanics: subs and Bits.

Does anything about my numbers get uploaded?

No. Every field on this page runs in your browser only. Nothing is sent to a server and there is no account. Your inputs and target are saved locally on this device so you don't retype them, and a shareable link can carry them to another device. Usage counting only tallies anonymous feature events, and you can turn it off with the button above.
