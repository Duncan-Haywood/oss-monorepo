FOR CARPET INSTALLERS

Bid the floor, the stairs, and the extras.

Price a carpet install the way the trade actually loses money: a flat living-room stretch versus a patterned glue-down with fourteen stairs, a tear-out, and a furniture move — not the published $2-a-yard special. Stairs are not yards. You get the job price, what it works out to per yard, and the number below which the install loses money.

The floor

Pick a common job to start, then replace every rate with a mill invoice and a crew you have timed. Changing scope or pattern fills starting hours and material only when you have not already edited that field.

Hours and material rates

These start from the scope and pattern you picked. They are ballpark crew figures, not a mill catalog or a guarantee.

Your costs

Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off. Underestimating it is how a $2-a-yard stretch that looked profitable was not.
