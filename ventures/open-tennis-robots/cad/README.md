# Parametric CAD and manufacturing packages

Code-CAD for Open Tennis Robots, built on [build123d](https://github.com/gumyr/build123d)
(OpenCascade). One command turns the parameters into everything a fabricator
needs:

```
pip install -r requirements.txt        # build123d, pinned
python -m otr_cad collector --out ../manufacturing/collector
python -m otr_cad feeder --out ../manufacturing/feeder
```

| Output | For |
| --- | --- |
| `laser/*.dxf` | Laser or waterjet cutting service. Flat aluminium 5052, true mm, no bends, no tapped holes |
| `print/*.stl` | Any FDM printer or print service, PETG |
| `step/*.step`, `step/assembly.step` | Any CAD package (Fusion, FreeCAD, Onshape, SolidWorks), CNC/CAM |
| `cut_list.csv` | Wire, tube and bar cut to length |
| `bom.csv` | Every part: made, cut, bought, and the fasteners |
| `drawings/*.svg` | Top, side and front views of the assembly |
| `manifest.json` | Parameters, derived numbers, check results, SHA-256 of every file |
| `collector-package.zip` | All of the above, ready to send |

The ready-built package is committed at
[`../manufacturing/`](../manufacturing/) and served from the project page.
You only need to run the generator if you change a parameter.

## What the build checks

Every build runs these checks, and the generator exits non-zero if one fails:

- **Design rules** (`params.Collector.check`): the rod gap squeezes a ball by
  15-30%, there is edge distance around holes, the drum clears the base, the
  motors fit, and the electronics tray fits.
- **Collisions**: every pair of parts, bought-in parts included as
  envelopes, intersects with zero volume.
- **Ground contact**: wheels, caster and drum all touch z = 0.

`tests/test_cad.py` adds hole alignment: every pilot hole in a printed part
that sits on the base plate must land on a hole in the plate. It also checks
that the DXFs read back in millimetres, that two builds are byte-identical,
and that the committed package matches a fresh build.

## Assembly guide (2026-11-20)

`python -m otr_cad collector` also writes `guide/ASSEMBLY.md` and one drawing a
step (`guide/step_NN.svg`, the step's parts in black over what is built in
grey), from `otr_cad/guide.py`. The build fails if the guide and the package
disagree:

- **Fastener stacks.** For each bolted joint, a ring around the bolt's axis is
  intersected with the placed parts to measure what lies under the head. A
  nyloc needs its height plus two threads past the stack; a screw into a
  heat-set insert must reach the insert's whole 5.7 mm and not bottom out in
  its pocket; a shoulder bolt must be 0-1 mm longer than what swings on it.
  The table is in the guide.
- **Tally.** Every placed part goes on in exactly one step, and the steps use
  every bought line of `bom.csv` to the unit.
- **Order.** Each part a step adds touches something already built (wheels may
  sit off the gearmotor face by the shaft's length, which is not modelled).
- **Hatch.** With the drum hung, the emptying hatch, turned slot-down, lifts
  3.3 mm out of its seat and rises off the axle spacer without touching
  anything.

Writing the guide against the model found six things a builder of the v0.2
package as committed before 2026-11-20 would have hit, all fixed in the same change:

| Found | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat-set insert holes | 4.0-4.4 mm pilots; an M4 insert wants about 5.6 mm | 5.6 x 6.5 mm pockets (`insert_hole_d`, `insert_depth`), M4 clearance past them |
| Mast socket and caster adapter | each one's M4 heads landed under the other part, on the same patch of plate | one set of four M4 x 20 from below through adapter (heads in counterbores) and plate into the socket's inserts; `mast_x` = `caster_x` is a design rule |
| Motor clamp cap bolts | 1.1 mm of plastic between the bolt hole and the motor bore (0.5 mm with an insert) | clamp 64 mm wide, bolts at x = +/-25.5: 4 mm to the bore, 3.7 mm to the side |
| Yoke pivot | an "M8 x 25" shoulder bolt through a 5 mm arm and a 30 mm block | an 8 mm x 40 mm shoulder bolt (ISO 7379, M6 thread); the block is 34.5 mm deep, so the arm swings with 0.5 mm float |
| Drum axle | the same 25 mm shoulder bolt with a 3 mm M8 washer stack between arm and cover: the washers (16 mm OD) bore on the turning cover, and nothing clamped the bearing's inner race | an M8 x 30 bolt clamping arm, a 12 mm OD x 7 mm spacer (through the cover's 14 mm hole) and the 608's inner race |
| Emptying hatch | the axle bolt passed through it, so emptying the drum meant unhanging it | a 14 mm slot from the axle to the rim, and a 4 mm arm gap: turn the drum slot-down, undo six thumb screws, lift the hatch out and up |

Smaller ones: the clamp-ring screws were M4 x 12 with nylocs over an 8 mm stack
(now M4 x 16); the disc holes for the M5 rivet nuts were 5.5 mm (now 7.0 mm,
`rivet_nut_hole_d`); the standoffs' screws from below reached 3 mm into the
standoff (now M3 x 10, 5 mm). The yoke-lift microswitches have no mount in the
CAD yet; the guide says so.

Caveats: the bought-in parts are envelopes drawn from `params.py`, so the stack
check is only as good as those numbers (nut heights are DIN 985; a rivet nut's
flange is taken as 1 mm). The guide has not been followed by anyone building one.

## Feeder assembly guide (2026-11-20)

`python -m otr_cad feeder` now writes `guide/ASSEMBLY.md` too: 25 steps (base
frame, feet, wheels, mast, turntable, cheeks, the head on the bench, the hopper,
the net and trough, the lift and the wiring), one drawing for each step that adds
parts. The steps and joints are in `otr_cad/feeder_guide.py`; the checks are the
collector's, shared in `guide.py`, with three more:

- **Placements.** A step can fit one placement of a part (`"lift_pulley[0]"`), so
  the lift's bottom pulley goes on with the boot and its top one with the head;
  the tally counts every placement of every placed part once.
- **T-nuts, tapped holes, through-bolts.** A screw into a T-nut must stand
  4-8 mm past the extrusion's face (catch the nut, stop short of the slot floor:
  an assumption, check your profile); a screw into a tapped hole must reach at
  least its diameter and not pass the thread (`room`); a through-bolt across
  the open head measures from cheek to cheek through the gap.
- **Head access.** For the joints marked `access`, a cylinder the head's size
  running 60 mm back from its seat must miss the parts it clamps: a
  counterbore closed at the far end fails.
- **Extrusion end bores.** Every hole over a 2040's end (mast plate, deck,
  shelf) must sit on one of its two core bores, 10 mm either side of the end's
  centre; the centre is the web between the cells.

24 joints are measured. Writing the guide found the package as committed on
2026-11-20 could not be built as drawn; all fixed in the same change:

| Found | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holes over the 2040 posts' ends | the mast plate, deck and shelf had one hole at each post's end centre, which is the web between the profile's two core bores: nothing to screw into | two holes per end, over the core bores (`_cores`); 8 M5 x 12 into the mast posts, 16 M5 x 16 into the hopper posts |
| Mast posts to the mast rails | "M5 x 16, posts to rails": a head inside the rail's slot cannot be reached by a key | M5 x 50 up through the rail (drilled 5.5 mm) into the post's core bores, 10 mm of thread |
| Head pivot | M8 x 110 over a 105 mm cheek-to-cheek stack plus 3.2 mm of washers: 1.8 mm left for a nyloc that needs 10.5 | M8 x 120 |
| Servo bracket legs | M4 x 30 into a 10 mm insert pocket: bottoms out 15 mm before it clamps | M4 x 12 (legs), M4 x 10 (tabs) |
| Trough hangers | M5 x 35 through a 6 mm web: 29 mm past the beam's face into a slot about 6 mm deep; and the counterbore stopped 30 mm in, closed behind on the hangers whose saddle sits high | M5 x 12; the counterbore runs out through the hanger |
| Pole feet | the M5 hole ran across the foot 10 mm above the beam's top, reaching nothing; the pole stands at the beam's end | a tongue inboard of the pole with two counterbored M5 x 12 down into T-nuts in the beam's top slot |
| Cable clamp | its M5 hole ran blind from the crossbar's face: no screw could be put in | a 6 mm web against the bar and a pocket open on top for the head (M5 x 12 and a T-nut) |
| Levelling feet | an M10 stud and "M10 T-nut" in a slot-6 profile: an M10 cannot pass a 6.2 mm slot opening | M6 foot, M6 slot-6 T-nut and a jam nut |
| Heat-set insert pockets | 4.0 mm in the cheek feet, gear sector, feed floor and roof, sleeve socket and agitator mount (the collector's fault of the same day) | 5.6 x 6.5 mm pockets with 4 mm pilots past them (`_insert_cut`) |
| Azimuth stop blocks | M4 x 10 through the 5 mm plate reached 5 of the insert's 5.7 mm (pocket 6 mm) | pocket and pilot 7.5 mm, M4 x 12 |
| Sleeve socket | a 5 mm flange cannot hold a 5.7 mm insert; M4 x 10 | 9 mm flange, 8 mm pocket, M4 x 12 |
| Agitator mount, home switch, azimuth stepper, deck saddles | M4 x 10 (5 mm into a 5.7 mm insert); M3 x 8 with washer and nut over a 7.5 mm stack; M3 x 10 into a NEMA 17's ~4.5 mm threads; M5 x 12 with a nyloc over 6.5 mm | M4 x 12; M3 x 12; M3 x 8; M5 x 16 |

The BOM had its own faults, found by the tally: the 80 mm standoffs were
listed twice (as the placed `head_standoff` and as a fastener line), so were the
tub; the standoffs had 4 screws for 8 ends; nothing joined the frame's rails
(16 M5 x 30 through end-tapped joints now), the cable crossbar's two corner
brackets, the battery's strap, the camera clamps' pinch screws and nuts for an
untapped lazy-susan were missing; 8 T-nuts and the "T-nut or tapped end" line
had no use. All are corrected in `feeder.fasteners` and `COTS_EXTRA`.

Caveats: as for the collector, bought-in parts are envelopes, so the stacks are
only as good as `params.py`; the T-nut window (4-8 mm), the standoffs' 8 mm of
thread, a NEMA 17's 4.5 mm and an extrusion's 15 mm end tap are assumptions the
guide states. Not checked: the elevation actuator's and shuttle drive's shoulder
bolts, the launcher motors' screws (check them against your motor), the lift's
small screws. The take-up spring's bracket is not in the CAD yet; the guide says
so. Nobody has built a feeder.

## Changing the design

Edit `otr_cad/params.py`, or override from the command line:

```
python -m otr_cad collector --out /tmp/wide --set drum_len=550 --set base_half_w=300
```

Bought-in parts (motor, wheel, bearing, caster, Raspberry Pi) are drawn
from the dimensions in `params.py`. Check them against the datasheet of
the part you actually buy before cutting metal.

  **Azimuth drive (2026-11-10, `Feeder.azimuth_drive`, `check_azimuth`, `feeder.az_gear_sector`, `package.azimuth_sweep`, `feeder.turning_inertia`):** a NEMA 17 stepper hangs flange-up under the mast plate on +y, 123.75 mm from the turntable axis, and turns a bought steel pinion (module 1.5, 15 teeth, 5 mm bore) meshing with a printed PETG gear sector (150 teeth on the full circle, pitch radius 112.5 mm, cut over +-38 deg) bolted under the turntable plate's +y edge with three M4 screws into heat-set inserts. The turntable plate is trimmed to a 108 mm radius so the pinion's tip circle (111 mm from the axis at its nearest) clears it at every angle; the pinion is drawn at its root circle so the mesh is not reported as a clash. One full step is 0.18 deg, 75 mm sideways at 24 m (microstepping divides it, with less accuracy under load); 0.1 mm of backlash is 0.05 deg, 21 mm. The hose term is a taut string pulling along its chord from the socket to the sleeve's lower end: zero with the head straight ahead, 0.40 N m at worst; the hose's own bending and twist are not modelled. The firmware drives it as a homed step/dir axis with the same 60 deg/s and 120 deg/s^2 (`firmware/esp32/controller.py`, `StepperAxis`; home switch on GPIO 22 at about -32 deg, its bracket not modelled yet). With 100 nF and one reading per tick the head stopped 0.37 deg off the goal (the deadband is 0.25 deg); the jam still faulted in 0.8 s at every gap tried (1 to 10 mm), so the stall rule is conservative: it fails on the worst swing over every duty, which random readings rarely straddle. **Wiring:** a 1 uF X7R from GPIO 39 to GND at the ESP32 (hardware/electronics.md pin table, BOM). **Not modelled or measured:** every capacitance above and the pot's resistance (read them with a meter: wiper to the toggling motor wire over the built lead), the real edge rate (a slower edge only helps), the ESP32 ADC's own noise and non-linearity (the trimmed mean helps both), how long `read_u16()` takes and so where the eight readings fall in the PWM period, the pickup inside the actuator's own cable, and a scope on GPIO 39 with the head moving at a part duty: that trace is the check.
  **Power distribution (2026-11-14, `Feeder.power_distribution`, `check_power`, `launch_wheel_kgm2`, `spinup_s`, `AWG_AMPS`, `fuse_for`, `gauge_for`; manifest "power" line; firmware `controller.SpinupRamp`):** one fuse per branch after the e-stop contactor, the main fuse and contactor sized from them, and every load drawing at once checked against the battery BMS. **Rules:** a branch fuse is the smallest standard blade rating at or above 1.25 times the branch's running current and at or above half its stall current (a motor starts at stall for a few ms, and a blade fuse at twice its rating takes 0.15 s or more to open); its wire is the thinnest gauge whose assumed bundled rating carries the fuse, never under 20 AWG. The main fuse is the smallest rating at or above 1.25 times the total, and the contactor is rated at least that. The Pi's buck is fed between the main fuse and the contactor, so the Pi and ESP32 report an e-stop instead of losing power. **Numbers (24 V low-battery cut; every current assumed from the BOM's part classes):** ESCs 6.4 A each (15 A fuse, 16 AWG), H-bridge 6.0 A for both base gearmotors and the actuator (7.5 A, 20 AWG; peak 14 A), lift 2.0 A (3 A), agitator 0.8 A (1 A), stepper driver 0.3 A (1 A), servo buck 0.8 A (2 A), Pi buck 0.6 A (2 A). Main fuse 30 A on a 12 AWG feed that drops 0.15 V; the old wiring note's "about 30 A" now comes out of the model. **What it found:** the speed loop's feedforward stepped each ESC's throttle the moment a new speed arrived. So a wheel starting from rest could draw the motor's 1 kW peak, 49 A per ESC and 98 A for the pair, more than three times the BMS's limit. A BMS trip takes the Pi down too. The firmware now ramps each wheel's target: 1,500 rpm/s at low speed, then 100 W of shaft power (rpm^2 grows at 2P/I, with I = 0.00155 kg m^2 from an assumed 0.40 kg wheel with its tread at the rim, plus the rotor). 107 W per wheel would still fit the budget. From rest to the drill's fastest 6,425 rpm takes 4.8 s by the ramp's integral. On the simulated feeder (`tests/spinup_ramp_sweep.py`, 27 cases) the worst per-wheel peak is 110 W, against a median of 871 W with no ramp. A 6,000 rpm/s start still peaked at 436 W, because the once-per-rev tach reads 0 for the first revolutions and the loop pushes past the ramp. **Checks:** the total against the BMS (the message names the `wheel_spinup_w` that fits), the main fuse against its feed, the feed's drop, the lift lead, stepper coils and servo supply against their fuse or current limit, both H-bridge channels against 10 A continuous and 30 A peak. A firmware test fails if `config.py`'s ramp numbers or inertia disagree with these, and the BOM's fuse list is checked against the model. **Not modelled or measured:** every real current, regenerative current from braking ESCs, and the speed loop's recovery after each ball. The ESC fuses are twice the ramped draw for that last one.
  **Contactor inrush and precharge (2026-11-14, `Feeder.contactor_inrush`, `check_inrush`, `rlc_step`, `time_above`; manifest "inrush" line; `tests/inrush_sweep.py`):** closing the contactor joins the pack to the ESCs' empty input capacitors through nothing but the loop. **Model:** a series R-L-C from the pack's resistance and inductance, the 12 AWG feed out and back, the main fuse, the contactor, and the two ESC branches in parallel (each its 15 A fuse, its 16 AWG lead and its capacitors' ESR) into every switched capacitor at once (2 x 1,000 uF for the ESCs plus 300 uF for the rest). The step response is solved in closed form for each damping; tests check it against an RK4 integration in all three cases, and the fuse's integral of i^2 dt against C V^2 / 2R, which holds for any damping. **Numbers (at the charger's 29.2 V; every other figure assumed and listed in `params.py`):** 64 mOhm, 0.68 uH, 2,300 uF, damping 1.86. A direct close peaks at 394 A after 31 us and stays over an assumed 150 A BMS short-circuit trip for 170 us, against an assumed 200 us delay. The sweep (27 cases: pack 20/40/80 mOhm, 470/1,000/2,000 uF per ESC, 0.3/0.6/1.0 uH per metre) gives 242 to 589 A and 84 to 315 us, past 200 us in 9 cases, all of them the 2,000 uF ESCs. The lightest damped case (20 mOhm, 470 uF, 1.0 uH/m, damping 0.77) rings the bus to 29.8 V. I^2 t is 15 A^2 s at the defaults, under 4 in each ESC fuse, so the fuses are not the problem; the contactor's contacts (13 times their 30 A rating) and the BMS's trip are. **Design change:** a 4.7 ohm precharge resistor and a 10 A relay across the contactor, the relay's coil and a 0.5 s on-delay timer for the contactor's coil both fed through the e-stop. The relay makes 6.1 A; the bus time constant is 11 ms, so after 0.5 s what is left is the idle loads' assumed 0.3 A across the resistor, 1.43 V, and the contactor makes 19.6 A (13 to 29 A across the sweep). The resistor takes 0.98 J per closing, and 181 W for 0.5 s (91 J) if the bus is shorted. **Checks:** the contactor's make current against an assumed 60 A (twice its rating; few small DC contactors state a capacitive make rating), the time over the BMS's trip against its delay, the precharge relay's current against its rating and the precharge current against the BMS's trip. Each fires in a test on a bad value (no precharge, 2,000 uF without it, a 20 ms delay, 3 A of idle load, a 2 ohm resistor). **Not modelled or measured:** every figure but the charger's voltage, the ESCs' own soft-start if they have one, contact bounce (each bounce re-closes onto a part-charged bus), and the bucks' start-up current while the bus rises through the resistor.

  **Start-up loads during the precharge (2026-11-14, `Feeder.precharge_bus`, `startup_loads`; the manifest's "inrush" line; `tests/inrush_sweep.py`, second table):** the precharge above assumed the switched loads idle at a constant 0.3 A while the bus charges. Two do not. A buck or a chopper drive draws constant power once its input passes its start threshold (assumed 8 V on, 7 V off), so it takes more current the lower the bus, and through 4.7 ohm the most the resistor can pass into the bus is V^2 / 4R: 45 W at 29.2 V, 30 W at 24 V. The azimuth stepper driver's ENABLE is not wired, so it holds its phase current (about 8 W at the input) from the moment it has power. And until this change the firmware held the shuttle servo's pulse at `GATE_LO_US` from power-up, so a servo left anywhere but home (an e-stop mid-stroke) sought home at stall the moment its 6 V buck started: 20 W at the input for the loaded back stroke's 0.68 s, longer than the 0.5 s delay. **Model:** C dV/dt = (Vpack - V) / R - idle - the sum of P / V over the loads that have started, stepped at a 400th of the time constant to the contactor's closing; with no loads it equals the closed form to 1e-4 of the pack's voltage, and with one load and a long delay it settles on the quadratic's upper root (both are tests). **Numbers (assumed figures):** with the servo seeking, at 29.2 V the bus is 7.6 V short when the timer closes the contactor, which makes 104 A; at the 24 V low-battery cut the 28 W asked is more than the 30 W the resistor can pass less the idle load, so the bus never clears the loads' threshold: it chatters at 8 V (101 restarts in 0.5 s), is 16.2 V short at the closing, and the contactor makes 223 A, 89 us over the BMS's assumed trip. Across the 27 swept cases it makes 136 to 328 A, every one past the assumed 60 A. **Design change (firmware):** the shuttle servo gets no pulse until a FEED (`FeederHw.gate_back`): the first FEED after power-up or a stop runs a back stroke (where it stopped after a power cut is unknown), then the forward stroke, and after each back stroke the pulse is cut, since both ends of the stroke are the crank's dead centres and the shuttle rests unpowered. `stop_all` cuts it too. With only the stepper holding, the bus is 2.9 V short at 29.2 V and 3.3 V at 24 V, and the contactor makes 39 and 45 A (20 A with nothing starting). The sweep gives 28 to 66 A; four cases, all with a 20 mOhm pack, pass the assumed 60 A, so the stepper's ENABLE is next: a pin that keeps the driver off until the contactor is closed (landed the same day, next item). **Not modelled or measured:** the real thresholds, hysteresis and soft-start of the bucks and the driver; the ESCs' own start-up draw (in the 0.3 A idle); a wireless e-stop fob that cuts the coils without the ESP32 seeing it, after which a queued FEED could still start the servo during the next precharge (sensing the switched bus would close that); every figure but the charger's voltage.
  **Stepper ENABLE after the contactor, and contact bounce (2026-11-14, `Feeder.contactor_bounce`, `stepper_enable_after_ms`, `stepper_holds_at_power_up`; firmware `controller.PowerGate`, `FeederHw.bus_tick`, `config.CONTACTOR_SETTLE_MS`; `tests/inrush_sweep.py`, third table):** the item above left the azimuth stepper driver holding its phase current from power-up, because its ENABLE was tied to GND, and its 8 W through the precharge resistor made the contactor take 28 to 66 A across the sweep, over the assumed 60 A in four 20 mOhm cases. **Wiring:** the driver's EN (active low on A4988 and TMC2209 StepSticks) goes to GPIO 5 with a 4.7k pull-up to 3.3 V, so it is off while the ESP32 boots (GPIO 5 is a strapping pin that wants to be high at reset, which the pull-up also gives; a board with its own pull-down on EN needs that resistor removed or the pull-up sized against it). A PC817 optocoupler's LED sits across the contactor's coil through 10k (about 2.5 mA at 29 V; the coil's flyback diode clamps the LED's reverse voltage); its transistor pulls GPIO 15 low while the coil is energised. GPIO 15 low at reset only silences the boot log, and the coil cannot be on that early after a power-up (the timer's 0.5 s runs from the e-stop's release). The opto sees the coil, not the contacts, so the firmware waits: ENABLE goes low only once the coil has read on continuously for `CONTACTOR_SETTLE_MS` = 150 ms, against an assumed 50 ms pull-in and 10 ms bounce. When the coil drops, for any reason, ENABLE goes high at once, the azimuth forgets its position (`StepperAxis.unpowered`: a stepper without current holds nothing), the controller halts (the FEED queue included), and `FeederHw.update` drives nothing until the bus is back and settled. Telemetry carries `FLAG_BUS_OFF` (1024) meanwhile, and the Pi's drill runner waits for it at connect and aborts on it mid-drill. This also closes the gap the item above named: a wireless e-stop fob that cuts the coils without the board seeing its switch can no longer leave a queued FEED to start the servo during the next precharge (a test on the simulated feeder cuts the fob after two balls: the drill aborts, the queue is empty, and no ball leaves when the fob is released). **Numbers (assumed figures):** with nothing starting during the precharge the contactor makes 19.6 A at the defaults (was 44.6 A with ENABLE tied), 12 to 29 A across the 27 swept cases, none over 60 A (was 4 of 27). **Bounce:** while the contacts are open the precharge relay is still closed, so the bus does not collapse; it relaxes toward the pack less I x 4.76 ohm with the 11 ms time constant, and each re-close is an R-L-C step across what has opened up. One whole 10 ms bounce open leaves 0.86 V with the idle loads (the re-close makes 11.8 A; 5 to 21 A across the sweep), and 1.80 V if the driver were already enabled (24.9 A; 11 to 44 A across the sweep). So bounce is not an inrush problem with the precharge relay in place, but enabling the driver on the coil's first edge would roughly double each re-close, which the settle time avoids. **Checks:** `check_inrush` fails a `stepper_enable_after_ms` shorter than pull-in plus bounce (it names `CONTACTOR_SETTLE_MS`), and a bounce re-close over the contactor's make rating. A firmware test fails if `config.CONTACTOR_SETTLE_MS` and the model's figure disagree; the pin map test checks GPIO 5 starts high and GPIO 15 is an input. **Not modelled or measured:** the real pull-in and bounce of the contactor you buy (scope its coil and contacts on the first build), several bounces in a row, a contactor that welds or fails to close (the coil sense cannot see the contacts: a contactor that never closes leaves the stepper enabled on the precharged bus, about 0.3 A through the resistor, harmless but weak), and regenerative current from braking ESCs.

  **Regenerative braking (2026-11-14, `Feeder.regen_braking`, `check_regen`, `coast_s`; manifest "regen" line; firmware `SpinupRamp(brake=)`, `config.REGEN_BRAKE_MAX_MV`, `config.WHEEL_BRAKE`):** the two items above left "regenerative current from braking ESCs" unmodelled. The firmware lowers a wheel's target at the same capped 100 W of shaft power it spins up with, and an ESC that follows a lower throttle by braking (synchronous rectification, "damped light", a duty-cycle mode: most e-skate and BLHeli-class ESCs) sends that energy back into the bus: at most 2 x 100 W x an assumed 85% = 170 W (drag ignored, which only takes some of it). **Three cases.** (1) Into the pack with the contactor closed: 6.2 A on the bus at 27.6 V, 5.4 A of charge after the Pi and idle loads, under an assumed 15 A BMS charge limit. But at a full 29.2 V pack the 0.048 ohm pack-side resistance lifts the terminal to 29.44 V, and with an assumed 0.10 V cell spread the highest cell reaches 3.78 V, past a 3.65 V over-voltage cut: the BMS stops charging and the braking energy has nowhere to go. (2) The e-stop opens the contactor mid-brake. The firmware sees the coil drop within a tick, but the ESC keeps braking until its next 50 Hz pulse and its own input filter (an assumed 40 ms in all, less an assumed 5 ms contact drop-out): about 6 J into the bus's 2,300 uF alone. C V dV/dt = P lifts it past 30 V, the MDD10A-class H-bridge's input rating (the lowest on the bus), in 0.98 ms from 27.6 V (0.34 ms from 29.2 V, 2.3 ms from 24 V), with energy enough for 75 to 76 V. (3) The pack refuses charge with the contactor closed: the same 0.9 ms. **Changes.** A Schottky across the contactor and the precharge relay, anode on the switched bus and cathode between the main fuse and the contactor (45 V 20 A class, BOM): with the contacts open it returns the braking current to the pack and holds the bus at 28.4 V, and it conducts only from the bus to the pack, so the e-stop still isolates every motor and the precharge is unchanged. The firmware brakes only while the battery reading (GPIO 36, pack side) is at or under `REGEN_BRAKE_MAX_MV` = 27,600 mV (3.45 V a cell): there the highest cell reaches 3.58 V. Above it, and always with `WHEEL_BRAKE = False`, a lowered target coasts: `SpinupRamp` sets `coasting` and follows the wheel's measured speed down, and the board sends zero throttle until the wheel reaches its target (a throttle that matches the wheel's speed holds it against drag; anything lower is what such an ESC brakes on), then the loop takes over. Set the ESCs' brake-at-zero-throttle off. **What braking buys:** slowing a wheel 20% from the drill's top 6,425 rpm takes 1.3 s on the ramp and at least 4.7 s coasting; to rest, at least 23 s (351 J a wheel). `coast_s` is a lower bound: `launch_drag_w` holds the wheel at the top speed, and drag torque that rises with speed (bearings and air) is at most that torque below it, so constant-torque coasting is the fastest (a test integrates bearing-plus-air mixes against it). A stop and an e-stop coast too (zero throttle, as before), so the head's wheels turn for 20 s or more after either; `hardware/safety.md` now says so. **Checks:** the braking charge against the BMS's charge limit, the highest cell against its cut, the bus against `switched_v_max` with the pack and with the diode, the isolated bus without the diode, and the diode's reverse and forward ratings; a firmware test fails if `REGEN_BRAKE_MAX_MV` and `regen_brake_max_v` disagree, and two tests run the simulated board at 26.6 and 28.2 V (it brakes on the first and sends zero throttle on the second). **Not modelled or measured:** case (3) for any reason but a full cell (a BMS that blocks charging below 0 deg C, or a fault) is not covered: nothing in the design acts inside 0.9 ms, so set `WHEEL_BRAKE = False` with such a BMS; how hard your ESC really brakes on a lowered throttle (scope the bus and log the battery current on a 20% slow-down), its brake setting at zero throttle, the real drag curve, contact drop-out and the ESC's response to a pulse.

  **Reset at the machine (2026-11-18, `Feeder.estop_reset_chain`, `estop_reset`, `check_estop_reset`, `estop_reset_latch`; manifest "estop reset" line; `tests/feeder_sim.py` `press_reset`):** the 2026-11-08 quality audit left one safety gap open: releasing the e-stop or the fob re-powered every motor at once, and the only other step was a `CLEAR_ESTOP`, which a phone or script anywhere on the network can send. **Wiring:** a latch relay K0 (24 V, 10 A class) after the fob and the e-stop, its coil through a RESET push button's NO contact in parallel with its own NO hold contact; the precharge relay and the on-delay timer now take their feed from K0's node through RESET's NC contact. So K0 drops on any cut and only RESET sets it, and the bus starts its precharge as RESET is let go (the NC recloses), not while it is held: a jammed or taped button never powers the motors. No ESP32 pin is used: with K0 open the contactor's coil is off, GPIO 15 sees it, and telemetry's `FLAG_BUS_OFF` stays set; the drill runner's connect and the phone page's Clear stop now say "press and let go of RESET on the feeder". **Model:** switch logic stepped at 1 ms from power-up. Results at the defaults: no power at switch-on; none while RESET is held; power 0.5 s after it is let go (1.8 s for a reset pressed at 1.0 s and let go at 1.3 s); none after an e-stop or fob release (pressed 4.0 s, released 5.0 s, run to 30 s), none with RESET taped down across the release, a RESET pressed while the e-stop is still down sets nothing, power again 0.5 s after a RESET following the release, and RESET pressed while running drops the bus at once. **Contacts:** K0's hold contact and RESET's NC carry the coils behind them, 0.32 A with assumed coils (precharge relay 0.04 A, timer 0.03 A, contactor 0.25 A) against 10 A and an assumed 1 A DC-13 rating for the button. **Checks:** `check_estop_reset` fails the build with the latch off (`estop_reset_latch=False`, the old wiring), on any scenario where a release re-powers the bus, and on either contact over its rating; tests make each fire. **The fault that defeats it:** RESET's NO contact welded with the button let go: K0 then sets itself on release and the machine behaves as before 2026-11-18 (`no_welded`, tested). A safety relay module with a monitored manual reset closes that at more cost. **Not modelled or measured:** real coil currents and the button's DC rating (read the sheets), relay drop-out on a sagging pack, and contact bounce on RESET (it only delays the precharge). Press the e-stop, release it and check the wheels stay dark until RESET on every build.

  **Brakes and bounces in a row (2026-11-14, `Feeder.regen_guard`, `contactor_bounce_train`, `contactor_bounces`; firmware `controller.BrakeGuard`, `config.REGEN_BRAKE_HYST_MV`; manifest "regen" and "inrush" lines):** the two items above each solved one event: one slow-down's braking, one contact bounce. **Brakes in a row.** The board decides brake or coast every 10 ms tick from the pack reading on GPIO 36, which sits between the main fuse and the contactor, so it carries the last tick's current across the pack-side 48 mOhm (pack, feed out and back, main fuse). Braking both wheels pushes 5.4 A of charge, which lifts the reading 0.26 V; coasting, the Pi and idle loads pull it 0.04 V low; and the ESP32's ADC adds noise (assumed 0.15 V peak at the battery, +-14 mV at the pin through the 11:1 divider). A guard with one threshold therefore brakes, sees its own rise, coasts, sees the rise gone, and brakes again. `regen_guard` steps the guard tick by tick over the 20% slow-down's 127 ticks for resting pack voltages around 27.6 V, with the noise alternating high and low (the worst case for flipping): the single threshold flipped on every tick, 126 times, for any pack resting between 27.19 and 27.78 V, which is where a LiFePO4 pack sits for a while after coming off the charger. On the simulated board running the real firmware (`tests/feeder_sim.py`, with the reading lifted 260 mV whenever an ESC is driven slower than its wheel) a 5,000 to 4,000 rpm slow-down from a pack 100 mV under the threshold flipped 21 times. **Change:** `controller.BrakeGuard` stops braking when the reading passes `REGEN_BRAKE_MAX_MV` (unchanged, so the pack is never braked into above it) and allows it again only under that less `REGEN_BRAKE_HYST_MV` = 700 mV. The band must exceed rise + coasting sag + twice the noise, 0.60 V with these figures; 10 mV under that the sweep still flips, 10 mV over it flips once at most. On the simulated board the same slow-down now changes mode once and the wheel still arrives at 4,000 rpm (it coasts the rest of the way). Two other figures from the same model: a 20% slow-down of both wheels returns 215 J, so it takes 86 of them to add 1% to a 512 Wh pack and brakes in a row do not creep it toward the cut; and a slow-down that starts while the wheels are holding speed reads the pack 0.18 V low (3.3 A of holding, Pi and idle draw), so braking can start from a pack resting that much above the threshold and the highest cell can reach 3.60 V rather than the 3.58 V above, still under the 3.63 V the check allows (a test fails `regen_brake_max_v` = 28.0 on it). **Bounces in a row.** `contactor_bounce_train` splits the 10 ms bounce window into n closes and opens (n 1 to 10; 20, 50, 80 or 100% of the window open) and integrates the loop by RK4 at a 60th of its fastest time constant: closed, the R-L-C of `contactor_inrush` with the precharge relay still in parallel; open, the contact current stops (its 1/2 L i^2 goes into the arc) and the bus relaxes toward the pack less the load x 4.76 ohm. Tests check it against the closed-form precharge closing and against `contactor_bounce`'s gap for one long bounce. With the idle loads the number of bounces does not matter: no make in any of the 24 trains exceeds 19.3 A, the precharge closing itself, because a touch too short to charge the bus leaves at most the precharge's own 1.43 V gap and the idle load holds the bus there. With the stepper driver enabled (as it would be if the firmware enabled it on the coil's first edge) the worst make is 32.1 A, not the 24.9 A of the one-bounce model: that model assumed the first touch had fully charged the bus, while a touch too short to do so lets the driver's load drag the bus a further 1 V during the open. Still under the assumed 60 A make rating, and the firmware's 150 ms settle time keeps the driver off through all of it. Every break opens at most 6.4 A and puts at most 26 uJ of loop energy into the arcs per train. **Checks:** `check_regen` fails a band narrower than the sweep needs (it names `REGEN_BRAKE_HYST_MV`) and a loaded-reading top cell within 0.02 V of the cut; `check_inrush` fails a bounce train whose worst make passes the contactor's make rating (with no precharge every make is a full inrush, and it fires). A firmware test fails if `REGEN_BRAKE_HYST_MV` and `regen_brake_hyst_v` disagree. **Not modelled or measured:** the real ADC noise with the motors running (log the battery reading on a slow-down; widen the band if it is noisier), the real pack-side resistance, a real contactor's bounce pattern, and the arc's own voltage and duration (only the loop's stored energy is counted).
