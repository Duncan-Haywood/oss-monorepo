# tests/take_survey_jitter.py --tach-capture --sub 8, 2026-11-18 (firmware/esp32/README.md "Periods finer than a microsecond")

The same 72 survey drills as eval/take-survey-capture-2026-11-18.txt (the MCPWM capture on, handler jitter 0 to 3 us),
with the capture's periods handed to PeriodTach in 1/8 us (controller.CaptureClock(sub=8), PeriodTach(sub=8),
config.TACH_CAPTURE_SUB) instead of floored to whole microseconds. The handler's jitter never reaches a capture's
periods, so the six jitter columns are near-repeats of one plant (they differ only through ticks_us() in the tach's
`since` and LaunchCheck's tick log): read the per-jitter counts as about one draw per set, not six.

Compared against (whole-microsecond capture, the same builds): design 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 5 named and advised of 9 (25 of
54), mean share 0.56-0.60; held out 7, 7, 6, 7, 8, 8 (43 of 54), mean share 0.43-0.56.

## Pre-registration (written and committed before the held-out run)

Held out: the same 72 builds at wheel lag 0.45 s, seed 11, capture on, sub 8. PREDICTION: (a) named and advised, summed
over the six jitters, more than the whole-microsecond capture's 43 of 54 on the same builds; (b) no instant build
advised at any jitter; (c) the mean share on spread takes lower than the whole-microsecond capture's at 4 or more of
the six jitters (0.50, 0.48, 0.56, 0.54, 0.45, 0.43). DECISION RULE: if (b) fails, config.TACH_CAPTURE_SUB goes back to
1 (a finer clock must not invent takes). If (a) and (b) hold, TACH_CAPTURE_SUB = 8 stays the capture's default and
`tennisbots tach-jitter`'s clean reference names the finer floor; TACH_CAPTURE itself stays off (never run on a board).
If (a) fails with (b) holding, the README reports no measured gain and TACH_CAPTURE_SUB stays 8 only for the finer
jitter reading.

With exact periods the 6 ms takes fit every candidate from 0 to 8 ms to within 5-9 ppm: the fit's free terms absorb the
take's shape, and nothing is left to tell candidates apart. With whole microseconds the same wheels' rows fall steeply
to the right answer. The floor's error is 0.29 us per edge, about 30-50 ppm of a 6-9 ms period, the size of the
separation it shows, so the floored periods are not just noisier: something in the fit's path reads them differently.
Window traced, and not the cause (tests/take_survey_sub_anchor.py, the held-out half-sine 6 ms build on launcher 0,
22 take-survey wheels each way): controller._anchor picks the same slowest revolution at sub 1 and sub 8 on 21 of 22
wheels, so the fit's window does not move. What differs is each wheel's row (sqrt of the board's squared rms, 1e-6 of
the speed, candidates 0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 ms):
  wheel 1  sub 1 [4849, 4849, 4856, 4849, 4849, 4849, 4870]   sub 8 [1357, 3306, 2602, 1357, 1357, 1357, 3398]
  wheel 3  sub 1 [3548, 3548, 3548, 4275, 3548, 4040, 4584]   sub 8 [1949, 1949, 3138, 1949, 2615, 1949, 1949]
  wheel 6  sub 1 [4935, 4935, 5374, 4935, 4935, 4935, 5521]   sub 8 [2297, 2867, 1943, 3378, 1943, 2777, 2476]
At both units several candidates reach the identical residual to four digits (a 0 ms and a 6 ms take fitting the same
wheel equally), and the others jump above it by amounts that do not follow the candidate: the take appears not to enter
those fits at all, and the jumps look like the tau search's other basins (tests/take_misfit_trace.py's "cliffs"). At
whole microseconds the floor lifts every candidate to a common level and the jumps are small beside it; at 1/8 us the
level falls 2-4 times and the jumps dominate. So the survey's verdict at sub 1 is the trimmed sum of small, mostly
take-blind rows, and this run cannot say how much of its 43 of 54 the take itself earned. Next: why the residual is
identical across candidates (whether drop_from_commands_steps' t0 lands where take_offsets' parts all fall in one
revolution, or the take columns drop out of the solve), traced on these 22 wheels.
