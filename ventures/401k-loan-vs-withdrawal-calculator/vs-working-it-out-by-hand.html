Common questions

Can I compare a 401(k) loan and a withdrawal in a spreadsheet?

Yes. The arithmetic is not hard: a loan returns principal and interest to the account from after-tax cash, a withdrawal is grossed up for income tax and penalty and the gross amount leaves for good. The calculator does the same sums for you and puts both next to an outside loan, so you do not have to build and check the formulas.

What does the calculator include that a quick estimate usually leaves out?

Lost growth on the borrowed or withdrawn money, the plan loan limit (the greater of $10,000 or 50% of the vested balance, capped at $50,000), the set-up fee, and a job-loss check showing the balance still owed after a number of months and the tax and penalty if it became a distribution.

What does it not do?

It does not model hardship-specific rules, penalty exceptions beyond the penalty rate you type, state tax detail, plan fees other than the set-up fee, or an employer match paused during a loan. Its default rates are placeholders, so type your own.

Is anything uploaded?

No. The calculator runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
