Make a Nutrition Facts label from your recipe

List your ingredients by weight, enter the finished batch weight and a serving size. Get an FDA-style Nutrition Facts panel with the rounding rules and percent Daily Values applied, the ingredient list in weight order, and a Contains line for the major allergens. Nutrition data stays on this device.

The built-in values are approximate USDA FoodData Central figures. Branded products differ: copy the numbers from the package label (they are per serving there, so convert to per 100 g) or search FoodData Central.

Nothing is uploaded. The arithmetic runs on this device.

    What it does, and what it leaves to you

    • Does: adds up each nutrient across ingredients, scales to your serving using the finished batch weight, rounds to FDA increments (calories, fats, cholesterol, sodium, carbohydrate, protein, vitamin D, calcium, iron, potassium), and computes percent Daily Value against the 2020 Daily Values. Lists ingredients by weight and detects the nine major allergens in the names you give.
    • Warns: missing values, a missing finished weight, calories that disagree with fat, protein and carbohydrate, and impossible combinations such as more added sugar than total sugar.
    • Not decided: whether your product needs a label or qualifies for an exemption, the correct serving size for your product category (reference amounts), nutrient content or health claims, allergen cross-contact, state cottage food rules, or the accuracy of the numbers you typed. This is a calculation aid, not a regulatory determination. Have the label reviewed before you print and sell under it.

    Common questions

    How do I make a Nutrition Facts label for a homemade food product?

    List every ingredient with its weight in grams and its nutrition per 100 g (from the package or from USDA FoodData Central), weigh the finished batch, pick a serving size, and let the tool add everything up per serving, apply the FDA rounding rules and work out the percent Daily Values. This tool does that arithmetic in your browser and draws the panel.

    Do I need a lab test?

    Not always. FDA allows nutrient values from a database or calculation for many foods, but the label stays your responsibility and accuracy tolerances apply. Many sellers send a first product to a lab to check their calculation. Small businesses can qualify for an exemption (see the guide) and many cottage food sales need no panel at all; state rules differ.

    Why does it ask for the finished weight?

    Baking and cooking drive off water, so a batch that went in at 640 g may weigh 560 g when done. The same calories are then packed into fewer grams, so each serving has more of them. Leaving the finished weight out understates every nutrient.

    How does it find allergens?

    It reads your ingredient names for the nine major allergens (milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame) and their common derivatives, and prints a Contains line. It cannot see inside a branded ingredient, so names like natural flavor or chocolate are flagged for you to check with the supplier.

    Is my recipe uploaded anywhere?

    No. All the arithmetic runs in your browser and nothing is sent to a server. There is also a command-line version that runs on your own machine.
