The model, and what it leaves out

  • Every report row lands in one bucket: sales (item price plus shipping paid), refunds, final value fees, other per-order fees (international, performance), Promoted Listings, insertion fees, store subscription, shipping labels, disputes and holds, tax and adjustments, other. Payouts move money already counted, so they are excluded. The buckets add up to “eBay paid you before cost of goods”.
  • Per item, each order row is that item's; label, refund and ad rows are tied back through the order number (or item number) and split by revenue when an order has several items. Store-level rows stay out of the item table.
  • Profit is that amount minus unit cost × units, except that a fully refunded order returns its units to stock if the box is ticked.
  • Tax and adjustments is whatever is left between an order's net amount and its sale minus fees, usually sales tax eBay collected or a rounding adjustment, so nothing is dropped.
  • Not modelled: your time, packaging, income tax, currency conversion differences, off-eBay sales, and refund fee credits beyond what the refund row shows.

Common questions

Can I see profit per item from my eBay Transaction report without paying for a tool?

Yes. In Seller Hub go to Payments → Reports, choose Transaction report, pick a date range and download the CSV. Drop it in here, type what each item cost you, and the tool splits every order's sale, final value fees, promoted listing charges, shipping labels and refunds across the items in it.

Why is my eBay profit lower than the sale price minus the fee?

Because several costs live in separate rows: the shipping label you bought, Promoted Listings charges, refunds and the fixed per-order fee. The Transaction report lists them all; this tool joins them by order number so each item carries its own costs.

Where do the unit costs come from?

You. The eBay file does not know what you paid for the stock. Type a cost per item next to each row or paste lines like Vintage Nikon FE2 Camera Body,78. Items without a cost are left out of profit rather than counted as free.

What does the shipping label finding mean?

It compares the shipping and handling buyers paid with the labels you bought. Free-shipping listings show a gap by design, and the gap is real margin. Seeing it in dollars shows whether raising the price or fixing package weights matters.

What does the tool not see?

Your time, packaging, what the stock cost unless you enter it, sales tax handled by eBay, income tax, currency conversion differences and anything sold off eBay. Rows it does not recognise go to an “other” line so they stay visible.
