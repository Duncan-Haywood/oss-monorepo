The model, and what it leaves out

  • Every row lands in one bucket: sales (item price and shipping the buyer paid), refunds, final value fees, international, performance fees, Promoted Listings, listing and subscription fees, disputes, shipping labels, credits, other. Payouts and transfers are money already counted, so they are excluded. Anything the fee columns do not explain (seller-collected tax, rounding) is shown as “adjustments” so the total always ties to your Net amount column.
  • Per item, each order's net (sale, fees, label, ads, refund) is split across its items by what the buyer paid for each, joined on the order number.
  • Profit is that amount minus unit cost × units, except that a fully refunded order returns its units to stock if the box is ticked.
  • The take rate excludes shipping labels and disputes, because the buyer's shipping payment is what pays for labels.
  • Not modelled: your time, costs you have not entered, packaging, income tax, labels bought outside eBay, currency conversion differences and sales on other channels.

Common questions

Can I see profit per item from my eBay Transaction report without paying for a tool?

Yes. In Seller Hub go to Reports → Downloads, create a Transaction report for the period you want, download the CSV and drop it in here. Type what each item cost you and the tool splits every order's sale, fees, label, refund and Promoted Listings charge across the items in it. Nothing is uploaded.

Why is my eBay fee rate higher than the category percentage?

eBay charges its final value fee on the item price plus shipping (and sales tax in states where eBay collects it), then adds a fixed per-order fee of $0.30 or $0.40. Promoted Listings, international fees and any performance fees stack on top. The tool adds them up from your own rows and shows each as a share of what buyers paid.

Where does the tool get the cost of what I sold?

From you. The Transaction report has no purchase cost. Type a unit cost next to each item, or paste lines like the item title, a custom label or an item number followed by a comma and the cost. Items without a cost are left out of profit rather than counted as free.

How does it treat shipping labels?

Buyers pay shipping and you spend it on labels, so labels are kept out of the fee rate. The report compares shipping charged to label cost and flags a gap, because free or under-charged shipping is one of the quiet ways a listing turns unprofitable.

What does the tool not see?

Your time, sourcing costs you have not entered, packaging, income tax, labels bought outside eBay, currency conversion differences and sales elsewhere. Rows it does not recognise go to an “other” line so they stay visible.
