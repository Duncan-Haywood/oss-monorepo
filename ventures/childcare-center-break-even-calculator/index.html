Common questions

How many children does a childcare center need to break even?

It depends on your ratios, wages and rent, which is why a rule of thumb fails. Most centers land between 65% and 85% of licensed capacity. Because teachers are hired in whole rooms, the break-even point is a staircase, not a line: enter your own numbers and this tool finds the first fill level where monthly profit is zero or better.

Why does the next child sometimes cost so much?

Licensing sets a maximum number of children per teacher. When a room is exactly at that ratio, child number one over it needs another teacher on the floor. The tuition for one child rarely covers a teacher, so profit drops until enough new children fill the new block.

What staff-to-child ratio should I enter?

The ratio your state licensing agency requires for that age group, or your own tighter ratio if you staff to one. Ratios differ by state and age. This tool does not look them up; check your licensing rules.

What share of tuition should go to staff?

Many operators aim for roughly 50-60% of tuition on classroom staff and 25-30% or less on rent and fixed costs, leaving a thin margin. Those are rules of thumb, not standards. The result panel shows your own staff and fixed shares.

Is this financial advice?

No. It does arithmetic on the numbers you enter. It does not know your licensing rules, subsidy contracts or payroll law, and it does not replace an accountant.
