What it does, and what it leaves to you

    
    
      
  • Hours: lanes × (weekday hours × 5 + weekend hours × 2) = open lane-hours a week, split into peak and off-peak by your peak share; booked hours = open hours × the share booked in each period. A month is 52 ÷ 12 weeks.
    • 
      
  • Revenue: booked peak hours × peak price plus booked off-peak hours × off-peak price, less the league discount on the league share, plus parties × party price, plus food and drink and arcade and shoe rental sales per booked hour, less card and booking-app fees as a share of all revenue.
    • 
      
  • Variable cost: food and drink cost of goods, arcade and shoe rental cost, other cost per booked hour, and the party cost (host, food, cake) as a share of party fees. Fixed: paid staff hours × wage × (1 + payroll load), rent, utilities, insurance, marketing, manager and other costs.
    • 
      
  • Break-even: parties and bookings move together, so profit is a straight line in bookings until the busier period is full; break-even is fixed cost divided by what planned bookings contribute, and it is only reported if the lanes can hold that many hours. Startup: lanes, fit-out, barkitchen, deposit, launch and contingency; the loan is a share of that on level payments. Ramp-up runs sixty months with bookings climbing in a straight line from the month-1 figure; payback is the first month cumulative cash covers your equity.
    • 
      
  • Not decided: whether your catchment can fill the hours; seasonality, weather and school-term swings; planning and zoning, building and fire code, noise, ceiling-height and lane-dimension requirements, floodlighting, accessibility, food and alcohol licensing and coach qualifications; insurance terms; income tax and depreciation; and what a lender will offer.

Common questions

    
How much profit does a bowling center make?

    
It depends on how many lane-hours are booked, what peak and off-peak hours sell for, and what staff, rent and insurance cost. Enter your own figures; the page works out revenue after fees, food and drink, party cost share and fixed costs, then monthly profit and margin at planned bookings.

    
What utilization does a bowling center need to break even?

    
Two figures are shown: the share of open lane-hours that must be booked to cover operating costs, and the higher share that also covers the loan. Below the point where the busier period is full, profit is a straight line in bookings, so both are exact for your inputs. If a break-even would need more hours than the lanes hold, the page says it cannot be reached.

    
How much does it cost to build a bowling center?

    
The page adds a cost per lane, building fit-out, bar and kitchen equipment, a lease deposit, launch cost and a contingency. Every one is your own figure; the page does not assume a benchmark cost.

    
How much do parties and food and drink add?

    
Parties are entered with their cost as a share of the price, so only the remainder counts as profit; their lane time belongs in your booked hours. Food and drink are driven by booked hours times bowlers per hour times spend per bowler, less cost of goods. Both scale with bookings in this model.

    
Does this tell me whether a bowling center will succeed?

    
No. It works only from your figures. It does not know how many bowlers live near the site, what competing centers charge, or whether your site can be permitted. Check zoning, building and fire codes, noise limits and insurance first.

    
Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    
No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
