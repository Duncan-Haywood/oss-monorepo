Miriam at the Sea and the Women with Tambourines

A girl stands among the reeds of the Nile to see what will happen to her baby brother in a basket, and eighty years later the same woman, Miriam the prophetess, stands on the far shore of the sea, takes a tambourine in her hand and leads all the women in a song. Told from the Book of Exodus, chapters two, fourteen and fifteen.

What's true in this story

Who
Miriam the prophetess, sister of Moses and Aaron; their mother Jochebed; the daughter of Pharaoh and her servant; Moses and Aaron; the people of Israel at the sea; with Ziva, an invented girl of fifteen with a tambourine
Tradition
Catholic and Orthodox; both read the Book of Exodus as Scripture and honour Moses, Aaron and Miriam among the holy forefathers, as the Jewish tradition from which the story comes honours them too
Scripture and history
the story is Scripture, the Book of Exodus, chapter two, verses one to ten, and chapter fourteen, verse one, to chapter fifteen, verse twenty-one, told straight in a plain rendering; the book does not name the sister at the river, and she is taken to be Miriam, as Numbers twenty-six, verse fifty-nine, gives Moses only one sister; Miriam's age, where the sea was crossed, and which way the people went are given as open questions
Legend
none told as fact; the rabbis' traditions that Miriam foretold her brother's birth, that the women brought their tambourines out of Egypt on purpose, and that Pharaoh's daughter was called Bithiah, and Josephus's name for her, Thermuthis, are told as later traditions; the Christian reading of the crossing as a picture of baptism is told as Saint Paul's reading
What we invented
the girl Ziva, her mother, her little brother and her tambourine are our own invention, carried over from episode one hundred and sixty-four, and so are their names; so are the look of the house in Goshen, the weaving of the basket, the camp by the sea, and every word between Miriam and Ziva; nothing is added to what Miriam, Moses, Pharaoh's daughter or the people say or do in the book
Feast day
Moses the prophet is remembered on the fourth of September in the Orthodox Church and in the Roman Martyrology; the Orthodox Church remembers the righteous of the Old Testament, Miriam among them, on the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers in the weeks before Christmas, and we give no calendar date of Miriam's own that we cannot vouch for
