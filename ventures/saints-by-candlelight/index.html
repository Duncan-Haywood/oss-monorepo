Saints by Candlelight: bedtime stories of saints, angels and patriarchs

A free podcast of lighthearted, faithful stories from the Catholic and Orthodox traditions, told the way you'd tell an old legend by the fire. Thirty minutes to an hour each, slowing down as they go, so you can drift off before the end and miss nothing important.

150. Mephibosheth at the King's Table

Years after the battle on Mount Gilboa, King David asks a strange question in his palace at Jerusalem, whether anyone is left of the house of Saul, so that he can show him kindness for Jonathan's sake; the answer is a grown man who cannot walk, living quietly across the Jordan in a place whose name sounds like nothing, and who is carried before the king expecting the worst and is given a seat at the table for the rest of his life. Told from the Second Book of Samuel, chapter nine, with the promise Jonathan asked for in First Samuel twenty and the day by the Jordan in Second Samuel nineteen.

149. The Gibeonites and the Mouldy Bread

A small hill town that has heard what the God of Israel has done sends envoys down to Joshua's camp in worn-out sandals, with cracked wineskins and dry, mouldy bread, pretending to come from very far away; the trick works, the leaders of Israel swear peace in the name of the Lord, and when the truth comes out three days later they keep their word anyway, all the way to an all-night march up the mountain to save the town that fooled them. Told from the Book of Joshua, chapters nine and ten.

148. David at Ziklag and the Egyptian in the Field

David and his men come home to find their town burned and their families carried off, and the trail runs cold until they find a sick young Egyptian lying in a field, left behind by his master to die. They give him bread and water and figs, and he leads them to the raiders, and on the way home David makes a rule about the men who stayed behind with the baggage. 145. Balaam and the Donkey That Saw the Angel

The king of Moab hires a famous seer from far beyond the desert to curse the people of Israel camped on his border, but on the road an angel blocks the way, a donkey sees what her master cannot, and every curse Balaam opens his mouth to speak comes out a blessing, ending with a star rising out of Jacob. Is this a Catholic or an Orthodox podcast?

Both. It tells stories that Catholic and Orthodox Christians share: Scripture, the saints of the first thousand years, and the angels. When a saint belongs to only one tradition, the episode says so, and the stories stay off the questions the two Churches disagree about.

Are the stories true?

Each episode says plainly what comes from Scripture, what is history, what is legend handed down about the saint, and what we invented, such as small conversations or a dog with opinions. Nothing invented changes what happens in Scripture or adds a miracle to a real saint as if it were history.

Is it suitable for children?

Yes. It is gentle and lighthearted, with no gore and no frightening scenes, and it is written for grown-ups and older children to enjoy together at bedtime. Younger children may prefer the shorter stories in other saints books.

Who writes and reads it?

The scripts are written by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, following a series guide that sets out the doctrine and honesty rules. They are read by Kokoro, an open-source synthetic voice. Every episode says so at the start.

How do I listen in a podcast app?

Copy the feed address https://example.invalid/saints-by-candlelight/feed.xml into any podcast app that lets you add a show by RSS link, such as Apple Podcasts, Pocket Casts, Overcast or AntennaPod. You can also play every episode on this page.
