Jacob and the Night at the Jabbok

A man who cheated his brother twenty years ago is walking home with a large family and four hundred men coming up the road. He sends gifts ahead, sends his family across a river, and is left alone in the dark with a stranger who will not let go. Genesis 32 and 33, told straight from Scripture, ending with the best hug in the Old Testament.

Now. Notes for the curious.

Everything that happens in tonight's story follows the book of Genesis, chapter thirty-two and chapter thirty-three, verses one to seventeen. The messengers sent to Esau and what they report, Jacob's fear, the division into two companies, his prayer, the gift of five hundred and eighty animals in separate droves, with the servants and their instructions, the crossing of the ford of the Jabbok, the man who wrestled with him until daybreak, the touching of the thigh, the demand for a blessing, the question about names, the new name Israel, the naming of Peniel, the limp, the custom of not eating the sinew, the order of the family, the seven bows, Esau's running and embracing, the meeting of the women and children, the exchange about the gift, the offer to travel together, the slow pace, and the journey to Succoth, are all in the text.
