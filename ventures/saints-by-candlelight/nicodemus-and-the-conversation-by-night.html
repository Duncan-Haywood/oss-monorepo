Nicodemus and the Conversation by Night

A teacher of Israel and a member of the council comes to Jesus by night in Jerusalem at Passover, hears about being born again of water and the Spirit and the wind that blows where it wills, speaks up for him once in the council at the Feast of Tabernacles, and on the last Friday brings a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes to the garden tomb. Told from the Gospel of John, chapters three, seven and nineteen.

What's true in this story

Tradition
Catholic and Orthodox; both read the Gospel of John as Scripture and honour Nicodemus as a saint, with Joseph of Arimathea, and both read his conversation with Jesus as speaking of Baptism

Tonight's story is from the Gospel of John. The night conversation is in chapter three, verses one to twenty-one. The scene in the council at the Feast of Tabernacles is in chapter seven, verses forty-five to fifty-two, and Jesus crying out about living water is a few verses before, in the same chapter. The burial is in chapter nineteen, verses thirty-eight to forty-two, and the linen cloths in the empty tomb are in chapter twenty. What Saint Mark, Saint Matthew and Saint Luke say about Joseph of Arimathea and the stone and the women watching comes from their own accounts of the burial. All of that is told straight, in a plain rendering.

The Gospel does not say where the night conversation happened, or why Nicodemus came by night. Saint Augustine's reading, that he came in the dark because he was still in the dark himself, is from his sermons on this chapter, and Saint John Chrysostom preached on it too. The Feast of Tabernacles ceremonies, the water from Siloam and the great lamps, are described in the Mishnah.
