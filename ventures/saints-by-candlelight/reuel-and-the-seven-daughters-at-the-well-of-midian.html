Tonight's story comes from the Book of Exodus, chapter two, told straight in our own plain rendering, not in any one translation. The basket in the reeds is from the first part of that chapter, and the brickfields, the Egyptian, the two quarrelling men, Moses' flight, the well, the seven daughters, the supper and the naming of Gershom are from verses eleven to twenty-two. The king's death and God hearing, remembering, seeing and knowing are verses twenty-three to twenty-five. Moses keeping Reuel's flock is from chapter three, the road back to Egypt from chapter four, and Zipporah's return with her father from chapter eighteen.

Saint Stephen's retelling, with Moses instructed in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, forty years old when he went out to his people, and forty years in Midian, is from the Acts of the Apostles, chapter seven. Exodus itself does not give those ages, and we have told them as Stephen gives them. The well scenes with Rebekah and Rachel are from Genesis, chapters twenty-four and twenty-nine.

The chapter calls Moses' father-in-law Reuel; later chapters call him Jethro, and Numbers calls Hobab the son of Reuel. We have not tried to settle how the names fit together. Where exactly Midian was is not certain; the north-west of Arabia is the most common view.

The meaning of the name Gershom is the book's own explanation, and the meaning of Zipporah's name is the usual one.

Moses' journey, the stone he sat on, the six unnamed sisters and everything they said, the shepherds' daily habit, beyond what the father's question implies, the bucket lessons, the supper, the lentils, and the last conversation by the tent are our own invention.

That's the story of Reuel and the seven daughters at the well of Midian for tonight. Reuel and his daughters have no feast day of their own, but Moses, the prophet and lawgiver, is remembered on the fourth of September, by the Orthodox Church and in the Roman Martyrology.
