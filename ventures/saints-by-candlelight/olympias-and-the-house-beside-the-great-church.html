Olympias and the House Beside the Great Church

The richest young widow in Constantinople refuses the husband the emperor chooses for her, gives away a fortune that would make a modern billionaire blink, runs a house of two hundred and fifty women beside the cathedral, feeds a bishop with a ruined stomach, and when he is sent into exile is put on trial for burning down the church, and answers. Saint Olympias the deaconess, friend of John Chrysostom.

What is later legend. The coffin carried by the sea to Brochthoi is from the seventh century account of the abbess Sergia, and is told as that account.

What we invented. Zoe, her whole life, the steps, the kitchen, the dish carried along the passage, the big pot, the reading aloud, and the news brought by the monk. The words of Olympias and of John are given in our own plain paraphrase, close to the sense the old writers report. The old Life also says some things about her marriage that we have left out, as they are hard to judge and are not needed for her story.

That's the story of Saint Olympias for tonight. Her feast is on the twenty-fifth of July in the Orthodox Church, and she is remembered on the seventeenth of December in the Roman Martyrology.
