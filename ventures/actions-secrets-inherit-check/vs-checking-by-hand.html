GitHub Actions secrets: inherit Check vs. reviewing workflows by eye

Finding the line secrets: inherit in a workflow file takes no special skill. Deciding what it actually exposes -- whether the call it sits on reaches outside the repository, and whether the job is one a stranger's fork can trigger -- is the part that is easy to get wrong under a quick read. This page names no product or company; "reviewing by eye" is the generic habit every maintainer already has, not a competing tool.

Where a careful read holds up, and where it slips

secrets: inherit does not hide -- it is one line, and a reviewer who knows to look for it will find every instance in a file. What is harder to hold in your head while scanning a real workflow is what each instance actually means. A reusable-workflow call is external only if its uses: value names another repository (owner/repo/.github/workflows/file.yml@ref) rather than a local path (./.github/workflows/file.yml) -- easy to conflate when the line wraps or sits several jobs away from the secrets: inherit it governs. And the fork-reachability question depends on a trigger declared once at the top of the file, under on:, which has to be carried in mind while reading down through every job below it -- easy to lose track of in a workflow with several unrelated jobs, especially when the risky job's name gives no hint that it is the one at risk.

Common questions

What does reviewing a workflow for secrets: inherit by eye actually catch?

secrets: inherit is one distinctive line, and a reviewer who knows to look for it will find every occurrence in a file without much effort. That recognition is real, and this tool starts from the same line -- it does not find a secrets: inherit call a careful reader would have missed.

Why is telling an external reusable-workflow call from a local one easy to get wrong at a glance?

The difference between uses: ./.github/workflows/build.yml and uses: some-org/shared-workflows/.github/workflows/build.yml@v2 is a leading ./ and an owner/repo prefix -- easy to miss when the uses: line is long, wrapped, or several jobs removed from the secrets: inherit line it governs. A reviewer scanning quickly for the string secrets: inherit can confirm the line exists without re-checking which uses: value the surrounding job actually calls, which is exactly the distinction that decides whether the finding is secrets-inherit-external-workflow at all. This tool re-reads the uses: value for every secrets: inherit line it finds, every time.

Why is the fork-trigger connection easy to miss even when the trigger is declared right there in the file?

pull_request_target and workflow_run are usually declared once, at the top of the file, under on:. The job carrying secrets: inherit can be dozens of lines below, in a workflow with several unrelated jobs. Holding "this file's trigger is untrusted" in mind while scanning down through every job's steps, for every workflow file in a repository, is the kind of bookkeeping that is easy to let slip -- especially on a workflow whose name or first job gives no hint that a later job is the one at risk. This tool checks the whole file's trigger against every secrets: inherit line it finds, regardless of how far apart they sit.

Does a clean result from this tool mean my repository's secret handling is safe?

No. It checks two specific, well-documented secrets: inherit patterns in one workflow file at a time. It does not evaluate whether a called reusable workflow is itself trustworthy, and it does not follow a local reusable workflow to see what it in turn calls -- both real ways a repository's secret handling can still be exposed after a clean scan.
