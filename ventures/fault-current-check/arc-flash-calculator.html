How it is calculated

    
The IEEE 1584-2018 model: arcing currents at 600 V, 2.7 kV and 14.3 kV from the bolted current and gap (Eq. 1, Table 1), carried to your voltage by interpolation (Eqs. 16-18) or, at 600 V and below, by Eq. 25; the reduced arcing current from the variation factor (Eq. 2, Table 2); the enclosure size correction factor from the equivalent enclosure size (Eqs. 11-15, Tables 6 and 7); the incident energy at the working distance (Eqs. 3-6, Tables 3-5); and the arc flash boundary, the distance where that energy falls to 1.2 cal/cm². The higher of the two cases is reported. Ported from the MIT-licensed arcflash Python library by Li-aung Yip; its unit tests here reproduce every intermediate and final figure of the standard's worked examples, Annex D.1 (4.16 kV) and D.2 (480 V).

    
Range: three-phase AC, 208 V to 15 kV; bolted fault 0.5-106 kA at 600 V and below, 0.2-65 kA above; gap 6.35-76.2 mm (LV) or 19.05-254 mm; working distance at least 305 mm; enclosure at least four gaps wide. Outside that the page refuses rather than extrapolates. Not modelled: DC, single-phase and above 15 kV systems, time steps where the current changes during the arc (motor decay, a second device), and the device's curve itself. Unverified against commercial study software.

  


  

    
Common questions

    
What inputs does an IEEE 1584-2018 arc flash calculation need?

    
The system voltage (208 V to 15 kV, three-phase), the bolted fault current, the electrode configuration (VCB, VCBB, HCB, VOA or HOA), the gap between conductors, the enclosure height, width and depth, the working distance, and the protective device's clearing time at the arcing current. Typical gaps, enclosure sizes and working distances for each equipment type are tabulated in the standard and preloaded here.

    
Why are there two clearing times?

    
IEEE 1584-2018 asks for a second calculation at a reduced arcing current, because arcing current varies and a breaker or fuse can be much slower at a lower current. In the standard's own 480 V example the device clears the full arcing current in 61.3 ms but the reduced one in 319 ms, and the incident energy rises from 2.77 to 12.70 cal/cm². Using one time for both would understate it by 78 percent.

    
What is the arc flash boundary?

    
The distance from the arc at which the incident energy falls to 1.2 cal/cm², the level associated with the onset of a second-degree burn on bare skin. Inside it, arc-rated protection is needed.

    
Which electrode configuration should I choose?

    
VCB (vertical conductors in a box) is the most common for switchgear, panelboards and MCCs. VCBB applies where vertical conductors end in an insulating barrier, HCB where conductors run horizontally toward the opening, and VOA or HOA in open air. If you are not sure, choose the worst of the three enclosed configurations.

    
Is this the same as the old 2002 method?

    
No. IEEE 1584-2018 replaced the 2002 equations with a model that adds electrode configuration, enclosure size and a voltage interpolation, and its results can differ substantially from 2002 figures for the same equipment. This page uses only the 2018 model.
