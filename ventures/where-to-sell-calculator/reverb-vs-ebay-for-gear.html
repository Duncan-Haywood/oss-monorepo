Reverb vs eBay for selling gear

Reverb charges roughly a third of what eBay does on the same guitar, and the cheaper channel is still not automatically the right one. Here is one instrument worked through both, with every number visible and nothing hidden in a headline percentage.

The item

An $800 guitar with $75 shipping charged to the buyer. It cost $450 to acquire, $65 to ship, and $20 to pack properly — a case, foam, and a double box, because gear that arrives with a cracked headstock is a claim, not a sale. Same instrument, same price, same costs. Only the channel changes.

The structural difference is worth understanding, because it is why the gap is not simply “5% against 13.25%”. eBay’s final value fee normally has payment processing folded into it, so the one percentage is most of the bill. Reverb bills the selling fee and the processor separately, so the 5% headline is not the whole cost — the real comparison here is 8.19% plus $0.49 against 13.25%. Still a large gap on this item: about $44 more in your pocket on Reverb.

Then returns, which move it slightly

Gear buyers inspect on arrival and return postage on an instrument is not $6. Counting an illustrative 6% return rate on Reverb against 8% on eBay, and the heavier return shipping that gear carries, the expected keep across a run of sales is $242.90 on Reverb against $194.98 on eBay — a $47.92 gap, slightly wider than the single-sale one. Returns do not flip this comparison. They can flip others: see how.

And then the thing that does flip it

Reverb pays more per sale. eBay pays more per month of shelf. The cheaper channel loses on the measure that matters when your money is the constraint, because a slower sale ties up $535 of cash for another two weeks and that capital could have been another instrument. Which column you should read depends on which one you are short of:

  • Short of cash — you flip gear, and the money in this guitar is the money for the next one. Read cash per day. The faster channel can be worth more even while charging more in fees.
  • Short of gear — this is a personal instrument, or stock you are not replacing this month. Nothing is waiting on the money, so read profit per sale, and the fee gap is the whole story.

There is a third answer the arithmetic cannot give you: gear buyers on Reverb arrive already looking for gear. Whether that audience will carry a higher price is a judgement about your particular instrument. What the calculator can tell you is the size of the premium you would need — eBay would have to sell this guitar for about $860, roughly 7.5% more, to leave what Reverb leaves at $800.

Run it on your own instrument

Your acquisition cost, your shipping, and your own listing history are what make the comparison mean anything.

Common questions

Is Reverb or eBay cheaper for selling a guitar?

On the worked example of an $800 guitar with $75 shipping charged, Reverb's fees come to $72.15 against eBay's $116.24, so Reverb leaves about $44 more on the sale. Reverb bills a 5% selling fee and payment processing separately; eBay charges around 13.25% with processing already inside it. The gap narrows on cheaper items where the fixed fee matters more, and both rates are inputs you should replace with your own.

Does Reverb being cheaper mean I should list there?

Not on its own. In the same worked example eBay returns $10.66 per day against Reverb's $7.65, because the illustrative 21 days to sell on eBay beats 35 on Reverb. Cheaper fees on a slower sale can be worth less per month of shelf than higher fees on a faster one. If your cash is the constraint, the faster channel can win even while paying more in fees; if the gear is not going anywhere, the cheaper channel wins.

How much more would eBay have to sell for to match Reverb?

About $860 on this example, against the $800 asked on Reverb — roughly 7.5% more, because eBay's percentage fee applies to the higher price too. Whether a listing can carry that premium is a judgement about the buyers on each side, not arithmetic, which is why the calculator prints the price to match rather than picking for you.
