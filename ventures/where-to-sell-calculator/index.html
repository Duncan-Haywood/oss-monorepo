Break-even price is how low you can list before the sale costs you money — your floor when you discount, and it differs by channel because the fee shapes do. It is shown in red where your current price is already under it. Return on cash is profit against what you actually laid out to source, pack and ship; fees come out of the sale rather than your pocket, so they are not counted as invested. What this does, and what it does not

Three things flat fee comparisons get wrong

A per-listing fee is charged whether or not the item sells, so it hurts most on cheap items. A monthly plan is only a real per-order cost once you spread it over the orders you actually expect — at ten orders a month a $39 plan is $3.90 an order, at two hundred it is 20¢. And the fee percentage is not the decision: what matters is the price each channel needs to leave you the same money, which is the last column above. eBay vs Etsy fees on the same item works one example through in full.

What it deliberately leaves out

Fees are not the only difference between channels. A marketplace fee buys you demand you would otherwise have to create, and this page has no way to know how many buyers each channel puts in front of you, how fast an item sells there, or what returns cost. Read the fee gap as one input to the decision, not the decision. It does not recommend a price, a channel, or a business model, and it is not tax, financial or business advice.
