This page compares the free calculator against the most common way tip-pooling eligibility actually gets decided at a small restaurant or bar: out loud, in a shift or staff meeting, with nobody writing the rule down.

Is a staff meeting enough to set tip-pool eligibility?

A staff meeting can settle who wants to be in the pool. It has no way to check that against a federal rule that most owners have never seen written down as two separate tests: managers and supervisors are never eligible, full stop, and back-of-house staff are eligible only when no tip credit is taken. Those two rules get confused for one rule constantly — "the manager helped bus tables, so it's fine" is exactly the sentence a staff meeting produces and the FLSA does not accept.

The staff meeting is not wrong to exist — someone still has to decide who is on shift and who gets a cut. What it cannot do on its own is the rule check, because the rule has two independent branches and a room full of people focused on schedules is not where that gets caught.
