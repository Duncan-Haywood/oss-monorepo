      Common questions

      Can I ever put a manager or shift lead in the tip pool?

      No, never, regardless of whether you take a tip credit. Congress amended the FLSA in 2018 (Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2018, amending 29 U.S.C. 203(m)) to say an employer may not keep tips received by its employees for any purpose, including allowing managers or supervisors to keep any portion of them. That rule does not bend for a manager who also waits tables during a rush — if their job is classified as a manager or supervisor, tip-pool money reaching them is a violation on its own, separate from the back-of-house rule below.

      Can dishwashers or cooks be in the tip pool?

      Only if you take no tip credit at all — pay the full minimum wage in cash to your tipped staff. The Department of Labor's 2021 rule (29 CFR 531.54) calls this a nontraditional tip pool, and it is the one case federal law allows back-of-house staff to share in tips. The moment you take a tip credit, the older, narrower rule applies: the pool is limited to employees who customarily and regularly receive tips — front-of-house only.

      What happens if my tip pool does not comply?

      If you take a tip credit and the pool is later found invalid, DOL and the courts that have ruled on this treat the tip credit as forfeited for every front-of-house employee it was taken against, for the whole period the pool ran — not just for the employee wrongly included. On the worked example below, 230 hours a week of front-of-house time at a $5.12 tip credit is $1,177.60 a week, and $4,710.40 across the four weeks entered. That is back wages owed, and liquidated damages can double it.

      What counts as a tip credit?

      The gap between the full minimum wage and the lower cash wage you actually pay a tipped employee, which federal law lets you close with the tips they keep — up to $5.12 an hour under the federal floor. Several states (California, Nevada, Washington, Oregon, Montana, Alaska, Minnesota, and others) do not allow a tip credit at all, so if your state is one of those, the tip credit taken is $0 and back-of-house staff can be pooled regardless of what this calculator's federal-only exposure figure shows.
