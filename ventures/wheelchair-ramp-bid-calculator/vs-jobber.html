This calculator vs. Jobber

Two different shapes: Jobber is an account-based platform for running a carpentry or accessibility-modification company's whole operation, including scheduling the crew and invoicing the customer once a job is priced. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this wheelchair ramp cost, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

Jobber is a field service management platform used by home service businesses — general contracting, handyman work, carpentry, and remodeling among them — to run client management and online quoting, scheduling and crew dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client-facing hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. Public research puts its founding in 2011, headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada, and reports it is used by more than 200,000 service professionals. Its own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact plan names or dollar figures, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — home service field service management software, sold as a subscription account — are stated, and no dollar amount for Jobber appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of field service software describe scheduling, invoicing, and collecting payment for a job once its scope and price already exist; none describe working out what a wheelchair ramp should cost from its rise, landings, and rails before you quote it — which is the one thing this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what Jobber does. It does not manage a client list, dispatch a crew across a season, or collect a payment. It takes a residential wheelchair ramp — rise-to-run geometry, intermediate or switchback landings, both-side handrails, a wood, composite, aluminum, or concrete surface kit, demo of an existing structure, and a permit allowance — plus loaded wage, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into the walk-away price below which the job loses money, where the bid's cost is actually coming from, a copyable customer-facing bid summary, and a printable customer proposal with scope lines, a deposit split, and a signature block. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.
