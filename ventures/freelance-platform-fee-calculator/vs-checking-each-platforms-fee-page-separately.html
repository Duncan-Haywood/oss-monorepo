This calculator vs. checking each platform's fee page separately

Fiverr's fee page states one number. Upwork's does not state a number at all any more — and reaching for the wrong one instead of your own quoted rate understates what a job actually pays.

What checking each platform's fee page by hand looks like

Fiverr's help pages are straightforward: a flat 20% seller commission, no tiers, applied to every order. Upwork's fee page is not the same kind of page any more. Since May 1, 2025 it does not publish a number — it explains that the freelancer service fee is set per contract, somewhere between 0% and 15%, by Upwork's own pricing system, and shown to you only at proposal time.

Checking both by hand means two tabs, the same job price typed in twice, and a subtraction done separately for each — and one of those two lookups was never going to hand you a rate to subtract with. The gap gets filled somehow, and the somehow is often the tiered 20%/10%/5% formula Upwork retired, because it is still repeated across older blog posts, forum answers, and calculators nobody has updated. Common questions

Why not just check Upwork's fee page and Fiverr's fee page separately?

Because they answer different kinds of question. Fiverr's fee page states one number: a flat 20% seller commission, unchanged in years. Upwork's fee page does not state a number at all -- since May 1, 2025 it only explains that the freelancer service fee is set per contract, somewhere between 0% and 15%, by Upwork's own pricing system. Reading both pages still leaves you to open a second tab, retype the same job price, and do the subtraction yourself for each platform, and one of those two lookups was never going to hand you a number to subtract with in the first place.

What mistake does checking Upwork's fee page by hand risk?

Reaching for the tiered 20%/10%/5% formula Upwork retired on May 1, 2025. That formula is still repeated on older blog posts, forum answers, and other calculators that have not caught up, and unlike Fiverr's flat rate it looks exactly as authoritative as the number it replaced. A freelancer who checks Upwork's fee page, does not find a single rate, and pattern-matches to the old tiers they saw somewhere else ends up computing a fee Upwork no longer charges.

How big is that mistake in practice?

On a $3,000 job, assuming it is the first work billed to this client -- the same assumption the retired tiers' own boundaries used -- an established-relationship freelancer actually quoted 5% keeps $2,850, a 95% effective take-home. Applying the retired 20%-on-the-first-$500, 10%-up-to-$10,000, 5%-above formula instead computes a $350 fee and a $2,650 take-home, 88.3% effective -- $200 less than the freelancer will actually receive on this one job, from a formula that no longer applies to any Upwork contract.

Does this calculator know my exact Upwork fee?

No, and it does not guess with a formula either -- there is no public formula left to guess with. It takes the percentage Upwork already quoted you for this specific contract, shown to you at proposal time, and runs it through the same arithmetic as Fiverr's flat 20% for the same job price.
