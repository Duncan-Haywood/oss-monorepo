How much should I charge for a voice-over project?

It depends on length, how you price it, and -- more than most narrators price for -- how the buyer plans to use the recording. The rest of this page works through both pricing shapes on real numbers, not rounded examples.

Script-based work: base fee, then a usage multiplier

Commercial, e-learning, IVR, explainer, and animation scripts are priced per word or per finished minute, then scaled by usage/licensing term. Take a 150-word script at $0.30 a word:

FigureArithmeticResult
Base fee150 words × $0.30$45.00
Finished minutes (at 150 wpm)150 ÷ 1501.00 min

The same $45.00 base fee, every usage term

Usage termMultiplierTotal
Internal / training use only1.0×$45.00
Web / unrestricted digital use1.3×$58.50
Regional broadcast1.8×$81.00
National broadcast2.75×$123.75
Full buyout (all media, in perpetuity)4.0×$180.00

A $135.00 spread on identical recorded audio, from usage term alone -- the recording does not change; what the buyer is licensing does.

Audiobook narration: per finished hour, not per word

A 75,000-word manuscript, narrated at a rate of $250 per finished hour, with no separate editing/proofing/ mastering fee:

FigureArithmeticResult
Estimated finished hours75,000 ÷ 9,3008.06 hours
Narration fee8.06 × $250$2,016.13
Effective rate per word$2,016.13 ÷ 75,000$0.03

9,300 words per finished hour is a commonly cited rule of thumb, not a fixed conversion -- a slower, more heavily produced narration style yields fewer finished hours per manuscript word, and a faster one yields more.

Common questions

How much should I charge for a voice-over project?

It depends on the script's length, how you price it (per word or per finished minute), and -- critically -- how the buyer plans to use the recording. A 150-word script at $0.30 a word has a base fee of $45.00, but the same script can reasonably run anywhere from $45.00 (internal-only use) to $180.00 (a full buyout) once the usage-term multiplier is applied -- a 4x spread on identical work.

Why does the usage term change the price so much?

A script recorded for one company's internal training video is worth far less to the buyer than the same script running as a national television commercial for a year -- the recording is identical, but what the buyer is licensing is completely different. Rate-guide convention prices usage in tiers for exactly this reason: internal use at the base rate, web use at roughly 1.3x, regional broadcast at roughly 1.8x, national broadcast at roughly 2.75x, and a full buyout at roughly 4x.

How is an audiobook priced differently from a commercial script?

Per finished hour (PFH), the audiobook industry's own convention, most visibly used by ACX -- not per word and not per session hour. A finished hour is estimated from manuscript word count using a commonly cited conversion of roughly 9,300 words per finished hour, then priced against that estimate. A 75,000-word manuscript at $250 per finished hour comes out to roughly 8.06 finished hours and a $2,016.13 narration fee.
