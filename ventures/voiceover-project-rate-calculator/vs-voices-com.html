Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Voices.com?

They solve different problems. Voices.com is a marketplace: you build a profile, upload demos, and audition for jobs that clients post; if you book one, Voices.com handles discovery, escrow, and payment in exchange for a commission. This page is a free calculator: no profile, no auditions, no marketplace fee. You enter your script's word count or an audiobook manuscript's length, pick a pricing method and usage term, and it gives you a project rate to quote a client you already found yourself -- a returning client, a referral, or your own outreach.

What does Voices.com actually charge?

By publicly available pricing, voice talent can join free as a Guest member with a limited profile and no ability to actively pursue postings, or pay for a paid membership (reported around $499/year for the Premium tier) for full search visibility and the ability to submit auditions to client jobs. On top of membership, Voices.com reportedly takes a 20% commission on the value of a booked project, plus a separate 4-6% payment-processing fee depending on payout method -- so on a $1,000 project you would net well under $800 after both. This calculator charges nothing at all and takes no percentage of anything you book.

Does Voices.com price a project the same way this calculator does?

Not the same mechanism. Voices.com clients typically post a job with a budget and voice talent submit competing auditions and quotes, so the price a project settles at is whatever the marketplace's auction dynamic produces -- Voices.com also publishes its own general rate guide as a reference. This calculator has no auction and no marketplace data; it applies commonly cited voice-over rate-guide conventions (a per-word or per-finished-minute base fee scaled by usage/licensing term for script work, or a per-finished-hour rate for audiobooks) to your own numbers, so the starting point is grounded in your own script and rate rather than what another talent happened to bid on a different job.
