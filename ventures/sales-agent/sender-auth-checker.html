Free SPF, DKIM and DMARC checker

Before you send cold email from a domain, check that it authenticates. Look up the three TXT records (for example with dig TXT example.com) and paste them. You get a list of what to fix. Nothing is looked up for you, uploaded or sent.

What it checks

  • SPF: missing or duplicate records, +all and ?all, the ptr mechanism, the 10-lookup limit (it counts this record only, not what each include adds), misspelt terms.
  • DMARC: a record that does not start with v=DMARC1, a missing or invalid policy, p=none, no report address, partial pct, sp=none.
  • DKIM: a missing, revoked or non-base64 key, a key that looks shorter than 2048 bits, and the testing flag.

The warm-up side: the warm-up planner for sending volume and the cold email checker for the message itself.

Questions

Why does SPF fail with more than 10 lookups?

SPF allows at most 10 DNS-querying terms (include, a, mx, ptr, exists, redirect) across the whole chain. Past that, receivers return a permanent error and treat SPF as failed. Remove includes for services you no longer use.

Is DMARC p=none enough?

It is the right starting point because it only monitors, but it blocks nothing. Read the reports, then move to quarantine and reject once every real sender passes.

Does this tool look up my DNS or send anything?

No. You paste records you looked up yourself; the check runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded.
