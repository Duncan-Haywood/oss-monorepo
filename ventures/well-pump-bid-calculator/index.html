      Common questions

      Why does a 120-foot well only bill 20 feet of extra footage?

      Because the base pump price already carries the first 100 feet of setting depth. The default well is 120 ft, so only the 20 ft past the allowance is charged as extra footage. The “included footage” field is there so you can match it to how your own supplier or price book bundles depth — set it to zero and every foot bills separately.

      Why is cable measured separately from well depth?

      Because the cable run is longer than the well is deep. It has to reach from the pump up past the pitless adapter and across to the pressure tank or control box, so the default is 130 ft of cable for a 120 ft well. With 100 ft included, that is 30 extra feet at $1.85, or $55.50. Under-measuring the cable is a common way a well job goes over: the hole depth is known, the horizontal run to the tank often is not until you are on site.

      Is my data uploaded anywhere?

      Why is the effective hourly rate $408 when I set my wage at $42?

      Because most of this job is not labor. The default takes 5.17 hours and prices at $2,109.70, so dividing one by the other gives $408.07 an hour — but $790.50 of that price is material (the pump, tank, cable, and hoist), which is 57.6% of direct cost against labor’s 20.9%. The effective hourly figure is useful for comparing how well your day is being bought, not as a rate to quote.

      What is the $75 hoist line for?

      Pulling and setting a submersible pump on a 120-foot string is not a hand operation. The hoist line covers the rig or tripod time attributable to this job rather than the crew hours, which are counted separately. If you own the hoist outright and would rather carry it in your overhead rate, set the field to zero — it is a plain pass-through, not a percentage.
