How it is built

Run costs: driver pay (driver hours × pay), fuel (loaded plus deadhead miles ÷ mpg × price), maintenance per mile driven, meals per day, hotel per night, tolls and permits, card fees on the whole price, the vehicle's cost ÷ expected runs, and a fixed share: yearly insurance, certifications and equipment ÷ runs per year. True profit is the price (or the minimum, if it is higher) minus those costs, before tax.

Lowest quote: the costs that do not depend on price, divided by (1 − card fees − target margin), as a total, or divided by the loaded miles for a per-mile quote. The test suite checks that quoting exactly that amount earns the target margin for hundreds of random runs. If your minimum charge is already above it, the tool says so.

Common questions

How do I price a pilot car escort run so it makes money?

Start from what the truck is really doing, not the miles on the permit: the loaded miles with the load plus the deadhead to the start point and home from the end point, the driver hours including waiting, tolls, meals and hotel nights on a multi-day move. Add fuel and wear for every mile driven, card fees, the vehicle's cost per run and a share of your yearly insurance, certifications and equipment. Divide the costs that do not scale with price by one minus card fees and your target margin to get the lowest total price, or per-mile rate.

Why does a short escort run lose money?

Because the miles and hours around the load are not billed. A 140-mile run with 60 empty miles to get there and home and five hours of driver time quotes $252 at $1.80 a loaded mile, so most operators set a minimum or day rate. The minimum has to cover the deadhead, the hours and a share of the yearly overhead, not just the fuel.

What should my minimum charge or day rate be?

Enter your shortest typical run: loaded miles, deadhead and hours. The calculator shows the total the run needs for your target margin. Set the minimum at or above that number and the short runs stop being the ones that lose money.

How many escort runs a year do I need to break even?

Take what one run earns above its direct costs, which is everything except the yearly insurance, certification and equipment share, and divide your yearly overhead by that. The calculator shows the runs a year at your price and warns if it is more than the runs you expect.

Should I take the long run or the short run?

Compare profit per run and profit per driver hour. If your days are the limit, the run with the higher profit per driver hour is the better use of a day, even if another run makes more money by itself.
