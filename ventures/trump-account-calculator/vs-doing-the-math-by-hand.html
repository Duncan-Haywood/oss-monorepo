This calculator vs. working out a Trump Account by hand

A Trump Account looks like simple arithmetic — a seed deposit, a contribution cap, a growth rate. The problem is that its withdrawal treatment does not resemble the other child-savings vehicle most people already know, a 529 plan, and a quick by-hand estimate tends to borrow that plan's rules by habit.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Assuming a Trump Account works like a 529 plan, where the whole balance comes out tax-free for a qualified use. It does not -- only money an individual contributed is basis and comes out tax-free; the $1,000 seed deposit, any employer contribution, and every dollar of investment growth are taxed as ordinary income on withdrawal, no matter what the money is used for.

What's the second most common mistake?

Assuming you can choose to withdraw your own tax-free contributions first and leave the taxable money sitting in the account. The IRA pro-rata rule does not allow that -- every withdrawal is part tax-free and part taxable, in the same ratio as your basis is to the total account value, every time, until the account is empty.

What contribution-cap mistake gets made most often?

Treating the $2,500 employer contribution limit as separate from the $5,000 total. It is not additional room -- an employer's $2,500 counts INSIDE the shared $5,000 annual cap, so it leaves only $2,500 for everyone else that year, not $5,000.

What withdrawal-timing mistake gets made most often?

Assuming the account works like a custodial brokerage account that a parent can tap early if needed. No distribution of any kind -- not even of money the family contributed -- is permitted before the account owner turns 18, and a hand estimate often assumes at least the family's own contributions could be pulled out sooner.

Does this calculator know my complete tax situation?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page knows your full return -- other deductions, credits, or state tax treatment, which most states have not yet ruled on for a Trump Account. Both apply the same published federal seed-deposit rule, contribution caps, and pro-rata withdrawal formula to the numbers you enter; verify against the IRS's own guidance or a qualified tax professional before relying on either.
