This calculator vs. Housecall Pro

Two different shapes: Housecall Pro is an account-based platform for running a home service company's whole operation, including scheduling the crew and invoicing the customer once a job is priced. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this one boiler replacement cost, given its scope, its plant, and its extras — before you quote it.

What each tool is for

Housecall Pro is a field service management platform used by home service businesses — HVAC, plumbing, hydronic and steam shops, and other trades that dispatch technicians among them — to run client management and online quoting, scheduling and crew dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client-facing hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. Housecall Pro's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific feature list, plan names, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of field service software describe scheduling, invoicing and collecting payment for a job once its scope and price already exist; none describe resolving scope, plant, fuel, and input MBH into a correct price before you quote it — which is the one thing this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what Housecall Pro does. It does not manage a client list, dispatch a crew across a season, or collect a payment. It takes one job's scope (like-for-like swap, condensing upgrade, or steam-to-hot-water conversion), system, fuel, input MBH, heating zones, and flue height, plus whichever of an indirect water heater, a chimney liner, an oil-to-gas conversion, and primary/secondary piping the job needs, and your crew wage, payroll burden, travel, overhead, and target margin. It turns that into the price, the labor hours behind it, the price per input MBH, the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money — plus, if you already have a price on the table, a check of whether it clears that floor. It also runs three comparisons the plant bid alone does not show: the same plant priced under all three scopes, a same-visit patch against a full replacement and the winters of callbacks it usually costs, and the standard bid against a customer-supplied boiler or a Saturday no-heat call. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.
