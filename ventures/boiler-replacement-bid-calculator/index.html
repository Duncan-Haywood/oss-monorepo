    Common questions

    What does this boiler bid actually add up?

    The price adds up plant-hang labor and the boiler invoice, venting and condensate work, a steam-to-hot-water conversion when that is the scope, heating-zone labor and hardware sized to input MBH, an indirect water heater, a chimney liner sized to flue height, an oil-to-gas conversion, primary/secondary piping, and fuel-specific hardware — plus travel and equipment, overhead, and your target margin, all from the MBH, system, scope, and fuel you enter, not a flat “$4,500 boiler” price.

    Why does a steam-to-hot-water conversion cost so much more than a like-for-like swap on the same MBH?

    Like-for-like, condensing-upgrade, and steam-to-hot-water scopes each start from their own plant, vent, condensate, and conversion hours — steam-to-hot-water adds piping-conversion hours that a like-for-like swap has none of, on top of higher plant hours for the new hydronic circulators and zoning a straight steam replacement never touches. The “Same plant, three scopes” comparison prices your same MBH and zone count under each scope’s starting kit so a like-for-like rate does not get quoted on a conversion by mistake.

    What does the patch-vs-replace comparison show?

    It prices the $280-style “just fix it for now” no-heat call as its own visit — trip, labor, and parts — then stacks the extra callbacks that visit typically triggers this winter and in the winters after, and lines that season total up against the full replacement bid on the same plant. That is the comparison that tells you whether tonight’s patch price still covers the visit, and how many winters of patches it takes before replacing would have been cheaper.

    Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    No account and no network requests — the calculator runs entirely in your browser. Your bid is saved in this browser’s local storage between visits, and a bid link you copy carries those same numbers in the page’s URL hash rather than sending anything to a server. Your business details for the printed proposal are saved separately on this device; the customer’s details are never saved.

    What’s the difference between the bid price and the walk-away price?

    The bid price already includes your target margin on top of labor, materials, and overhead. The walk-away price is the total cost underneath it — plant, venting, zones, and every extra you checked — the point below which the changeout stops being profitable at all.

    Does this check fuel-gas code, combustion, or chimney safety?

    No. Hours and material rates are ballpark shop figures you can edit, not a heat-loss calculation, a fuel-gas-code determination, or a chimney design. It also does not price a furnace changeout or a standalone water-heater swap — those are separate jobs. Whether a flue needs a liner, what size the liner should be, and whether the combustion venting meets code for the fuel and appliance are still a judgment call for the licensed tech on site — this tool prices the plant you choose, it does not certify it.
