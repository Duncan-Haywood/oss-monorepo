FOR ROOFERS AND WINDOW CREWS

Bid the units, the type, and the extras.

Price a skylight the way the trade actually loses money: one tubular sun tunnel in an existing chase versus solar-venting glass with a new cut-in, tile flashing, and a drywall shaft — not the published $800 / unit special. You get the job price, what it works out to per unit, and the number below which the install loses money.

The openings

Pick a common job to start, then replace every rate with a unit invoice and a crew you have timed. Changing type, scope, or roof fills starting hours and material only when you have not already edited that field.

Hours and material rates

These start from the type, scope, and roof you picked. They are ballpark crew figures, not a unit catalog or a guarantee.

Your costs

Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off. Underestimating it is how an $800 / unit tube special that looked profitable was not.
