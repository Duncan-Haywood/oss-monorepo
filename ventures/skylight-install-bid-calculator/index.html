    Common questions

    Why is the shaft $0 on the default job?

    Because the defaults replace an existing tubular unit, and a replacement re-uses the opening and the light shaft that are already there. Shaft hours and material only appear when you enter shafts — which a new installation through a finished ceiling always needs, along with framing, drywall, and paint. That single difference is most of the gap between a $1,379.84 replacement and a new cut-in.

    Why is flashing billed separately from the skylight?

    Because it is matched to the roof, not to the unit: the default adds $85 of flashing against a $380 unit. Flashing kits differ by roof covering, and fitting one to tile or metal takes materially longer than to asphalt. Nearly every skylight leak traced back to installation is a flashing failure rather than a failed unit, which is why it is a visible line here rather than folded into the unit price.

    Why is travel $22.40 on top of the travel-and-equipment charge?

    Because they cover different things. The per-mile line is the vehicle running cost for the eight default miles; the separate travel-and-equipment charge is the fixed cost of turning out at all. The trip also appears a third time, as 1.68 hours of paid crew time. Short-duration roof work is dominated by the cost of showing up — the whole job is only 7.28 hours.

    Does an existing roof warranty survive this?

    Possibly not, and the tool cannot tell you. Cutting a new penetration in a roof still under a manufacturer or workmanship warranty frequently voids it unless the original roofer does the work. Check before you quote a cut-in on a recent roof: it is a common reason a homeowner cancels after accepting a price, and it is cheaper to find out first.
