How it is calculated

The equations are those in Chapter 1 of the ASHRAE Handbook, Fundamentals. Saturation vapour pressure comes from the Hyland and Wexler formulas, over liquid water at and above 0 °C and over ice below it (so below freezing the dew point is the frost point). Humidity ratio is 0.621945 times the vapour pressure over the dry-air pressure; enthalpy is 1.006 t + W (2501 + 1.86 t) kJ/kg of dry air, shown on the IP chart's own datum (0 °F dry air) in IP units; specific volume uses the ideal-gas form with the 1.607858 factor for water vapour. The wet bulb is the thermodynamic wet bulb from the adiabatic-saturation balance, solved by bisection. Pressure comes from the standard atmosphere at your altitude unless you type a barometric pressure.

When neither property is the dry bulb, the tool searches dry bulbs from −76 to 212 °F (−60 to 100 °C) for the one where both match. Airflows are converted to mass of dry air at each stream's own specific volume, not at "standard air", so they stay right at altitude and in hot air. Mixing conserves dry air, water and enthalpy. Coil load is the mass flow times the enthalpy drop; the latent part is the water removed times 2501 kJ/kg, and the rest is sensible. The apparatus dew point is where the coil line, extended, meets saturation; the bypass factor is (leaving − ADP) / (entering − ADP) in dry bulb. The ADP mode mixes the saturated ADP air with bypassed entering air by mass. The evaporative cooler holds the wet bulb constant.

Not modelled: fan and duct heat gain, the enthalpy of the condensate leaving a coil (under 1% of the load), coil performance at other airflows (enter the leaving air you measured or the maker's rating), wet-bulb psychrometer errors from poor airflow over the wick, and supersaturated fog states beyond flagging them. Unverified: checked against published saturation pressures, the standard atmosphere and hand calculations in its unit tests, not against a commercial psychrometric program.

Common questions

How do I find relative humidity from dry bulb and wet bulb?

Choose dry bulb and wet bulb as the two properties and enter both. The tool finds the humidity ratio from the adiabatic-saturation balance in ASHRAE Fundamentals, then the vapour pressure, and divides by the saturation pressure at the dry bulb. Set the altitude too: the same two readings give a different humidity at 5,000 ft than at sea level.

Why does altitude change the answer?

Humidity ratio depends on the vapour pressure as a share of the total pressure. At altitude the total pressure is lower, so the same dew point holds more water per pound of dry air, air is less dense, and a fan moving the same cubic feet per minute moves fewer pounds of air and less heat. A sea-level chart used in Denver under-reads humidity ratio by roughly a fifth.

What is the apparatus dew point of a coil?

The effective coil surface temperature. Draw the line from the air entering a cooling coil through the air leaving it and carry it on until it meets the saturation curve: that temperature is the apparatus dew point. Air that touched the fins would leave there; the leaving air is a mix of that and air that slipped past.

What is a coil bypass factor?

The share of the air that acts as if it never touched the coil. It is the leaving-to-ADP temperature difference divided by the entering-to-ADP difference. Deeper coils and slower face velocities give a lower bypass factor; a typical comfort coil is somewhere around 0.05 to 0.2.

What is the sensible heat ratio?

The share of a coil's total cooling that lowers the temperature rather than removing moisture. A sensible heat ratio of 0.70 means 70% sensible and 30% latent. Humid climates and crowded rooms need a lower ratio, which usually means a colder coil or slower airflow.

How cold can an evaporative cooler get the air?

No colder than the entering wet bulb. A direct evaporative cooler with 85% effectiveness takes the air 85% of the way from the dry bulb down to the wet bulb, adding moisture as it goes, so it works well in dry air and poorly in humid air.
