How much to charge for ADAS calibration on a windshield replacement

The number a customer expects is the glass price they saw on a coupon site or heard from a friend. That number does not include the camera. Most vehicles built since roughly 2018 carry a forward-facing camera, a radar unit, or both mounted to or sighted through the windshield — lane-keep assist, automatic emergency braking, adaptive cruise — and replacing the glass changes what that camera sees even when nothing about the bracket looks disturbed. A bid that quotes the glass and skips the calibration line has priced the wrong job.

Dynamic, static, or both — and they are not interchangeable

A dynamic calibration is a supervised road test at a specified speed on roads with clear lane markings; the system calibrates itself as it drives. A static calibration is done stationary, in a shop bay, with target boards set at exact distances and heights in front of the vehicle. A growing number of vehicles require both — static first to get the camera roughly aimed, then a dynamic drive to fine-tune it. Quoting a vehicle that needs both as if it needs one is the same shape of mistake as quoting a new pit as an existing-pit swap on a sump-pump job: the trip looks the same, the requirement is not.

What each kind actually adds to the bid

On the sample rates in this calculator, an otherwise identical economy-glass, in-shop, cash windshield swap prices about $630.55 (1.35 shop hours, $428.77 walk-away cost) with no calibration required. Add a dynamic calibration — 0.6 hours and an $85 sublet or equipment-recovery cost — and the same job prices about $807.41. A mobile OEM windshield that needs both static and dynamic calibration, at 1.7 calibration hours and a $210 sublet fee on top of a $650 glass cost and a mobile trip, prices about $1,862.80. The calibration line is not a rounding error on any of these — on the dual-calibration job it is roughly a sixth of total hours on its own.

Catch the one that got forgotten before it becomes a comeback

The costliest version of this mistake is not undercharging — it is quoting a calibration requirement and then never actually pricing (or performing) it, because the estimate sheet had a line for "windshield" and nothing for "camera." This calculator flags exactly that: choose a calibration requirement and leave both the hours and the cost at zero, and the bid is marked calibration required but not priced before it goes out, rather than after a customer's lane-keep warning light comes on three weeks later.

Sublet or in-house changes the cost, not whether it is required

Many independent glass shops sublet calibration to a dealer or a specialized calibration center and pass the fee through; others carry their own target-board equipment and a software subscription and price the amortized cost of that gear into the calibration line instead. Either is a legitimate way to run the business. What does not change is that the vehicle either needs the procedure or it does not — the sourcing decision is about your cost, not about whether the line belongs on the bid at all.

What this is not

This is not a determination that any ADAS or safety system is functioning correctly after the work, a substitute for the vehicle manufacturer's own calibration procedure and pre/post-scan requirements, or a recommended market price. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type. Whether a specific vehicle requires calibration, and which kind, is yours to confirm against the manufacturer's service data.

Common questions

How much to charge for ADAS calibration on a windshield replacement?

Price it as its own line, not a markup on the glass. A dynamic calibration (a supervised road test) typically adds around $85 of sublet fee or equipment recovery plus 0.6 hours of shop labor; a static calibration (target boards, in-shop) typically adds around $150 plus 1.2 hours; a vehicle that requires both adds around $210 plus 1.7 hours. On the sample rates in this calculator, adding a dynamic calibration to an otherwise identical economy windshield swap moves the price from about $630.55 to about $807.41.

How do I know if a windshield needs ADAS calibration?

If a forward-facing camera, radar unit, or rain/light sensor is mounted to or sighted through the glass being replaced, the manufacturer specifies a calibration procedure — most vehicles built since roughly 2018 carry at least one of these. The safest habit is to assume calibration is required until the vehicle's own service data says otherwise, not the other way around.

What happens if a shop skips a required calibration?

The camera or sensor can be aimed off-target by only a few millimeters and still power on with no warning light, so the vehicle drives away with lane-keep assist, automatic emergency braking, or adaptive cruise reading the road incorrectly. That is a safety and liability exposure for the shop, not just an unpriced line item — which is why this calculator flags a job as "calibration required but not priced" rather than letting it quote silently.
