Questions people ask

What is a good margin on hearing aids?

Hearing aids carry a high gross markup, but the gross number hides the cost of the service wrapped around the device: the fitting, the follow-up visits included in the price, trial-period returns and commission. This tool subtracts the device and accessories, a return allowance, card and plan fees, commission and the clinician's fitting and follow-up time, then shows what each kind of sale really earns so you can see where the margin is made.

How do trial-period returns affect profit?

Most states require a trial window after a hearing aid sale. A return refunds the patient, but the clinic still lost the fitting time and some device value to restocking or refurbishing. Enter the share of fittings that come back, the share of device cost you lose on each, and the minutes wasted. The tool spreads that expected cost over every kept sale, and the scenario table shows what five more points of returns would cost in a month.

Does bundled pricing earn more than unbundled pricing?

Bundled pricing includes follow-up care in one price, so every extra visit comes out of the same margin. Enter the number of included visits and the minutes each takes to see how much clinician time a bundle really consumes, and compare profit per clinician-hour across your tiers. Unbundled models can be tested by lowering the included visits and pricing them separately.

How is over-the-counter competition modelled?

Over-the-counter hearing aids put pressure on the entry price of private-pay sales. The scenario table cuts every private-pay price by 15 percent while leaving insurer and benefit payments alone, and shows the monthly profit change on your own mix. It is a stress test on your numbers, not a forecast.
