    Common questions

    Why does “set only” on an existing pad cost so much less than a new gravel or concrete pad?

    A new pad adds excavation, base material or forms and a pour, and cure or compaction time before the shed ever arrives — work an existing level pad skips entirely. Use “Same shed, three pads” to see the same shed priced across existing, gravel, and slab so a customer comparing your number to a $2,499 special understands what pad that special assumes.

    Why is a crane set priced so much higher than forklift access?

    Crane access is chosen for tight lots where a forklift or trailer can't reach the pad, and cranes are billed by the hour with their own mobilization — a real cost jump, not padding. Only select it if the site genuinely can't take a forklift; check it against the delivery-mile input too, since a longer haul compounds the access cost.

    Does the electrical option mean this includes a licensed electrician's permit and inspection?

    No. “Lights and outlets” and “Workshop circuit” price the labor and material this calculator assumes for that scope; they are not a permit, an inspection, or a determination that a licensed electrician is or isn't required in your jurisdiction. Confirm local electrical code requirements before wiring a shed.

    Are the pad and delivery figures specific to my region?

    No, they're common ballpark figures for backyard shed installs generally, not a regional materials price or a delivery tariff. Gravel, concrete, and fuel costs vary a lot by area — replace the defaults in calc.mjs with your own supplier and delivery rates.
