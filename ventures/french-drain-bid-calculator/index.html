      Common questions

      Why can a competitor advertise “$25 a foot” for a French drain?

      Because a per-foot price is written around the easiest possible trench, and that trench is the first preset here: 40 feet, shallow, 4-inch perforated, daylighted to grade, in dirt, with the lawn put back. Everything that makes a wet-basement job hard — depth, clay or rock, a drywell or a storm tie-in, catch basins, downspout ties, going under a driveway — is not in that number. Price the actual yard beside the special and the difference becomes line items you can point at.

      How much does digging deeper really cost?

      It moves labor and gravel together, which is why depth is the biggest lever on this page. A shallow 18-inch trench starts at 0.12 hours and $4.50 of gravel per foot. Standard 24–30 inch goes to 0.16 hours and $6.40. A deep 36–48 inch trench is 0.22 hours and $9.20 — nearly double the shallow number on both sides at once. Depth is decided by where the water is, not by the bid, so it is worth settling before you quote a rate.

      Why does soil type multiply the price instead of adding to it?

      Because it scales with every foot you dig. The soil factor multiplies the trench hours only — not the outlet, not the extras — at 1× in dirt or loam, 1.45× in clay, and 2.1× in rock or hardpan. It moves the spoils dump too: $85, $185, and $320 respectively, because rock and clay come out heavier and cost more to take away. A 60-foot run in rock is not a 60-foot run with a surcharge; it is more than twice the digging.

      What’s the difference between daylighting, a drywell, and a storm tie-in?

      Where the water is allowed to go, and it is the second-largest swing here. Daylighting to grade is 1.5 hours and no kit — free, if the grade cooperates. A drywell or pop-up emitter is 3.8 hours and a $380 kit because it is a second excavation. A storm or catch-basin tie-in is 4.2 hours and a $265 kit, and is usually the one that needs a permit and an inspection your jurisdiction sets, so the permit fee is its own field and starts at zero rather than being guessed for you.

      Why are catch basins, downspout ties, and driveway crossings priced one at a time?

      Because a drain that solves the problem is rarely just the trench. Each catch basin is 1.2 hours plus a $165 kit; each downspout tie is 0.6 hours plus a $45 kit; going under a driveway is 0.35 hours per foot, the most expensive foot on the job. Solid (non-perforated) pipe carrying water past where you do not want it collecting runs 0.08 hours and $2.40 a foot. Tick only what the yard actually needs and the price follows the scope instead of a package.

      What’s the difference between the bid price and the walk-away price?

      The bid price already carries your target margin on top of labor, materials, fees, and overhead. The walk-away price is the total cost underneath it — crew hours, pipe, gravel, fabric, the outlet kit, basins and ties, spoils disposal, lawn restore, permit, travel and equipment — the point below which the dig stops being profitable at all. Typing a number into “Price you’re considering” shows you the margin, or the loss, before you send it.
