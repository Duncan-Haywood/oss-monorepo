This calculator vs. tracking it in a spreadsheet

A remodeler pricing rec-room versus framed-finish versus legal-living scope usually ends up with a spreadsheet: a rate block per scope, a ceiling line, a floor line with waste, and a new section whenever the job needs a bath, egress windows, or a stair rebuild it did not have last time. Every formula is ordinary arithmetic. Keeping it wired together as the scope changes under you is the part that gets skipped when a homeowner needs a number today.

What each approach actually does

A spreadsheet can reproduce every line this calculator prices — shell labor and material by scope, a ceiling plane, a floor with waste, a bathroom kit, egress windows, bedroom extras, a stair rebuild, a wet bar, an HVAC extend, permit coordination, travel, overhead, and target margin. Building it means setting up separate rate blocks for rec-room, framed-finish, and legal-living, then remembering which cells to update every time the job's scope, ceiling, or floor choice changes — and adding a new block the next time a job needs a line item the sheet did not have last time.

This calculator keeps shell, ceiling, floor, bath, egress, bedrooms, stairs, wet bar, HVAC, and permit on one screen, already wired to the scope you pick. Changing scope, ceiling, or floor refills the starting hours and material for that choice automatically — a field you have already hand-edited is left alone, but nothing else silently keeps yesterday's number. The “same basement, three scopes” table re-prices your finished square footage under rec-room, framed-finish, and legal-living at once, and a saved bid or a printable customer proposal with a deposit split and a price-held date are both one click, not a second document to build.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet price a basement finish instead?

Yes. Every line this calculator prices — shell labor and material by scope, a ceiling plane, a floor with waste, a bathroom kit, egress windows, bedroom extras, a stair rebuild, a wet bar, an HVAC extend, permit coordination, travel, overhead, and target margin — is ordinary arithmetic you can put in a spreadsheet. Building it means setting up separate rate blocks for rec-room, framed-finish, and legal-living scope, then remembering to update every formula that touches finished square feet whenever the job changes shape.

What does a spreadsheet not do that this calculator does?

Re-price the same basement under all three scopes at once, so you can see where a rec-room rate would lose money on a legal-living suite before you quote it. Switching scope, ceiling, or floor in this calculator refills the starting hours and material for that choice automatically — a spreadsheet's cells stay whatever you last typed until you notice they are wrong. It also turns the same numbers into a printable customer proposal with a deposit split and a price-held date, and a shareable bid link that reopens the exact scenario for you or your crew, neither of which a formula cell produces on its own.

Is a spreadsheet ever the better choice?

Yes, when the job has a wrinkle this calculator does not model — a walkout entrance, a second stair, a kitchenette, or a rate structure this tool's fields cannot represent. A spreadsheet you build yourself can represent any line item you can describe. This calculator trades that flexibility for a form that already has the right fields for one basement finish, wired together so changing scope does not mean rebuilding the sheet.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No account and no network requests — the calculator runs entirely in your browser. Your bid is saved in this browser's local storage between visits, and a bid link you copy carries those same numbers in the page's URL hash rather than sending anything to a server.
