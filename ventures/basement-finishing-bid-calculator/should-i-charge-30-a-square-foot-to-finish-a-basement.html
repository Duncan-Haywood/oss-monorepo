Should I charge $30 a square foot to finish a basement?

An owner brings you a Facebook post: “$30 a square foot, finished basement.” It reads like one number for any basement. It was priced for one scope — a rec-room: paint, a drop ceiling, and carpet on walls that already stand. Ask that same $30/SF to cover a legal-living bedroom suite with a full bath, egress windows, and HVAC, and it stops covering the job long before you get to the punch list.

$30/SF was never wrong. It was priced for the 800 SF rec-room job and it covers that job with room to spare. Nobody rewrites the flyer when the same owner asks for a bedroom suite instead.

Why the gap widens instead of scaling with the square footage

The flat rate moves in a straight line with square feet: 800 SF to 1,000 SF is 25% more dollars. The walk-away price does not move in a straight line, because a bath, two egress windows, two bedrooms, an HVAC extend, and a permit are not square-footage lines — they are their own tickets that a per-SF dump never carried in the first place. That is why a 25% bigger basement can need a 314% bigger check to actually cover it.

This is a scope question, not a size question

The trap is not "the basement got bigger." It is "the basement got a bath, egress, and code compliance, and the number I quoted from never had those in it." A remodeler who matches the flyer number on scope alone — without re-checking which of rec-room, framed-finish, or legal-living the owner is actually asking for — is the one who finds out the wet work and the egress ate the whole margin.

Common questions

Should I charge $30 a square foot to finish a basement?

Only on the cheap-special scope it was priced for. An 800 SF rec-room finish (paint, drop ceiling, carpet, no bath) walks away at $20,600.07, so $24,000 at $30/SF covers it. A 1,000 SF legal-living suite (full bath, two bedrooms, two egress windows, HVAC, permit) walks away at $85,334.53. $30,000 at the same rate loses $55,334.53.

Why does the same $30/SF number cover one basement and not another?

The per-SF number was set by the shell alone — paint, a drop ceiling, and carpet on walls that already stand. It carries no bath, no egress window, no bedroom, no HVAC extend, and no permit. Framed-finish and legal-living add studs, insulation, more drywall, and the habitable-space extras, which is why the walk-away price rises far faster than the square footage does.

How much worse does it get on a larger or heavier legal-living job?

Worse, because the gap is scope-driven, not just size-driven. A 900 SF framed-finish job (studs, insulation, drywall, no bath) already loses $25,866.45 against its own $30/SF number. A 1,000 SF legal-living suite loses $55,334.53. The dollar-per-SF price a homeowner anchors on does not move with the scope; the walk-away price does.
