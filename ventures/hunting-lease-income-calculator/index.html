The arithmetic, and what it leaves out

  • Club lease = acres × rate per acre. Per-hunter = seats × share filled × fee. Day hunts = hunter-days × day rate.
  • Net = gross + tax savings − insurance − upkeep − admin − lost use − platform fee.
  • Break-even rate = (costs − tax savings) ÷ (1 − platform fee) ÷ acres.
  • Not modelled: liability exposure and its legal limits in your state, the lease contract, game-law and licensing rules, income tax on lease income, damage deposits, wildlife damage to crops, and the effect on neighbours or on resale value.

Common questions

How much can a landowner charge for a hunting lease?

It depends heavily on region, game, habitat and access, so this tool holds no market rates. Enter the per-acre or per-hunter price you are actually being offered or that neighbours report, and it shows the net after your insurance, upkeep, admin and lost-use costs.

Is a flat club lease or per-hunter leasing better?

Per-hunter leasing usually grosses more when seats fill but carries vacancy risk and more admin; one club lease is steadier and simpler. The tool compares both, plus paid day hunts, on your numbers and shows how net changes if only half the seats fill.

What does a hunting lease cost me as the landowner?

Typically liability insurance or an umbrella extension, signage and boundary upkeep, roads, food plots or stands you provide, your time, and any use you give up such as grazing or your own hunting. Enter each; the tool subtracts them.

Does leasing land for hunting create liability?

It can. Recreational-use statutes in some states limit a landowner's liability but often not when you charge a fee, so the rules for paid leases differ. Ask your insurer and an attorney, and use a written lease with a hold-harmless clause reviewed locally. This tool does not assess any of that.
