Questions doulas ask

How much should a birth doula charge?

It depends on your market, but the number to anchor on is your pay per worked hour, not the package price. A $1,200 package looks fine until you count 14 hours at the birth, six hours of visits, driving, a month on call and the fee you pay a backup. This calculator works the package price back from the hourly rate you want. Local rates, your experience and what clients in your area pay vary, so treat the result as your floor, not a market quote.

How do I count on-call time?

Most doulas do not bill for it, but it has a cost: weeks of not travelling, drinking or planning. The tool lets you give each on-call day a dollar value you choose, including zero, and shows the hourly rate both ways.

Should I charge more for a long birth?

Many doulas price a package for an average birth and add a per-hour charge past a set number of hours, or a flat fee for births over 24 hours. The sensitivity table shows how fast the hourly rate drops as labor gets longer, which is the case for either.

What does a backup doula really cost?

On the share of births a backup attends, you usually pay the backup part of the fee. Averaged over all your clients that is a steady cost per package, so it belongs in the price. Set the share of births and the share you pay.
