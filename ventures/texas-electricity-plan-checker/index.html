Texas electricity plan checker

The 1,000 kWh price on a plan list is not your price. Paste each plan's Electricity Facts Label and a year of your own usage, and see what every plan would have cost you, month by month: bill credits, tiers, minimum-usage fees, free nights and TDU delivery charges. Each label is checked against its own 500, 1,000 and 2,000 kWh prices, so a misread term shows up instead of a wrong ranking. How it works and when to use something else.

1. Your usage

A Smart Meter Texas CSV (15-minute or daily), or one month per line ("Jan 2025, 1243"), or twelve numbers. A full year compares plans fairly.

2. Plans

Copy all the text of an EFL (open the PDF, select all, copy) and paste it here, then read it into a plan. Check the fields against the label, or type a plan in by hand.

Energy: cents, or tiers like 8.2 to 1000, 12.9. Credits: 125 at 1000-2000; 15 at 2000+. Fees: 9.95 under 1000. Time of use: 0 all 21-6 (cents, all/weekdays/weekends, hours). Label: the three average prices.

    Questions

    Why is the 1,000 kWh price on a plan list not what I pay?

    The average price on an Electricity Facts Label is computed at exactly 500, 1,000 and 2,000 kWh. A plan with a bill credit that pays only between 1,000 and 2,000 kWh can show a very low 1,000 kWh price and cost much more in months you use 900 or 2,100 kWh. Billing your own months on the plan's terms gives your price.

    How does the tool know my label was read correctly?

    It recomputes the label's own three average prices from the terms it read. The label rounds to a tenth of a cent, so correct terms land within about 0.05 cents of each. A mismatch means a term was missed or misread, and the tool says so instead of ranking the plan silently.

    Where do I get my usage?

    Smart Meter Texas lets you download your 15-minute interval or daily usage as a CSV after you register with your ESI ID and meter number. Monthly kWh from your bills also works, but free-nights and free-weekends plans need interval data or an assumed share of use in those hours.

    Is my usage uploaded?

    No. Every calculation runs in your browser. An optional anonymous counter in this browser's local storage records only that a comparison ran and the day.
