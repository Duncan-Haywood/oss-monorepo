This calculator vs. a flat pump special

“$X, any tank, pumped” is not a price for a septic stop — it is a price for an exposed lid and one tank at the included gallons, quietly applied to a buried, two-tank system with baffles and risers too.

What a flat pump special is

A flat pump special is the fastest way to quote a septic stop over the phone: name a number and go. Homeowners like it because it is easy to compare against a competitor's coupon, and it is the number a customer holding one will ask you to match.

The number behind it was usually set for the simplest version of the job — an exposed lid, the truck parked at the tank, one tank at or under the included gallons — and then reused for every call afterward, whether or not the next one has a buried lid, a second tank or pump chamber, baffles, risers, or an inspection report the homeowner asks for once the truck is already there.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter tank gallons, any extra tanks or a pump chamber, hose footage, whether the lid is exposed or buried, baffle replacement, risers, a written inspection report, after-hours dispatch, and your own wage, payroll burden, travel, overhead, and target margin. It prices gallons and hose past the included amount, the buried-lid dig, and every extra as their own lines on top of the base stop, and returns the price, the crew-hour split, the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price — for this job, not a flat number for every tank.

What a flat pump special doesWhat this calculator does instead
Charges the same price whether the lid is sitting exposed at grade or has to be located and dug out of the lawn. Prices a buried lid as its own locate-and-dig hours, separate from the base pump.
Bills the same for a 1,000-gallon single tank and a 2,000-gallon two-tank system, even though the extra gallons and the second tank add hours and dump-fee gallons the special never counted. Prices gallons past the included amount per 100 gallons, and extra tanks or a pump chamber as their own line, on top of the primary tank.
Has no line for baffle replacement, risers, or a written inspection report, which are commonly sold once the truck is already on site. Prices baffles, each riser, and an inspection report as their own hours and material cost.
Has no premium for an after-hours or emergency call, even though that is often the reason the call came in at all. Applies an after-hours dispatch line to hours and fee when the stop is scheduled outside normal hours.
Gives the estimator a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the price, the profit, and the effective hourly rate, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before you commit to it.

Common questions

Why does a flat pump special get a buried, multi-tank job wrong?

Because the special was priced for an exposed lid and one tank at the included gallons and hose run, and the cost drivers for a buried, multi-tank job are not in that price at all. Using this calculator's own shipped defaults, a 1,000-gallon exposed-lid tank with a 40 ft hose costs $351.22 to run, and a two-tank, 2,000-gallon buried system with baffles, two risers, an inspection report, and a 150 ft hose costs $1,419.85 to run -- 4.04 times as much, on the same base stop.

What does a flat pump special usually leave out entirely?

The dig and the extras. A pump special has no line for locating and digging a buried lid, no line for gallons or hose footage past the included amount, and no line for a second tank, a pump chamber, baffle replacement, risers, or a written inspection report. It also has no line for after-hours or emergency dispatch, which is commonly the reason the call came in at all.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own shipped defaults: the exposed-lid, single-tank stop prices at $487.81, on a job that costs $351.22 to run, holding a 28% target margin. The two-tank, buried, baffles-and-risers job costs $1,419.85 to run, and this calculator prices it at $1,972.02, still the same 28% margin. Quoting that job at the pump special's $487.81 price leaves it $932.04 below its own break-even cost -- before counting the $1,484.21 shortfall against what this calculator prices the same job at.

Is a flat pump special ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of jobs -- an exposed lid, a single tank at or under the included gallons, hose within the included run, and no extras -- a flat pump special can land close, because that is probably the job the price was set on. The risk is quoting a buried lid, a second tank, baffles, risers, an inspection, or an after-hours call sight unseen, because the special does not know what is under the lawn until someone digs, and by then the flat number has often already been quoted over the phone.

Does this calculator know my local labor rates or disposal fees?

No. It has no data on your local labor market, your dump facility's fees, or what other pumpers in your area charge. It takes tank gallons, extra tanks, hose footage, access type, baffles, risers, an inspection report, after-hours dispatch, and your own wage, burden, travel, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a price for this job -- the arithmetic a flat pump special skips, not a market survey.
