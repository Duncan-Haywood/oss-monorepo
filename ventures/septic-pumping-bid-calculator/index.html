      Common questions

      Why is disposal the biggest single cost on the job?

      Because the treatment plant charges by volume and does not care how easy the pump-out was. On the defaults, disposal of 1,000 gallons is $160 against $87.78 of loaded labor for 1.75 hours. That is the line most likely to change without warning when a receiving facility reprices, and the one worth re-checking before you honour an old quote.

      Can I reuse the $0.49 per gallon figure as a rate?

      No, and it is shown as a check rather than a rate for that reason. It is simply $487.81 divided by 1,000 gallons, and it falls as volume rises because the trip, the site time, and the travel charge are fixed. A 1,500-gallon tank does not cost 50% more. Quote from the tank size and the access conditions, then use the per-gallon number only to compare against what a competitor advertised.

      Why is hose footage free at 40 feet?

      Because the truck carries 50 ft in the base price. Only footage past that allowance bills, and the defaults sit 10 ft inside it. Long hose pulls are worth entering accurately: they are hard physical work, they slow the job, and they are the usual reason a tank that looked routine from the driveway is not.

      What is not included on the defaults?

      Uncovering a buried lid, baffle replacement, risers, a written inspection report, and the $125 after-hours premium are all off. The $45 permit is entered but not applied unless your jurisdiction requires one for a routine pump-out. These are the extras that turn a $488 service call into a several-hour job, so switch on the ones the site actually needs before quoting.
