Common questions

What counts as “billable wait” in the margin, and why is grace time excluded?

Wait time above your included grace, converted to hours and paid at your loaded labor rate. On the defaults — 45 minutes of wait with a 15-minute grace — that is 30 billable minutes, added to the 2.5-hour route time for 3.0 total paid hours. Grace time is excluded because most contracts (and most couriers) treat a short wait as normal turnaround, not chargeable delay; only the minutes past that threshold load onto the route's real labor cost.

How is direct cost built, and what is left out of it?

Loaded labor (paid hours × labor rate) plus vehicle cost (miles × per-mile rate) plus consumables (stops × per-stop rate) plus overhead (a percent of revenue). On the defaults that is $84 labor + $25.20 vehicle + $9 consumables + $30 overhead = $148.20. Taxes and owner draw are deliberately left out — the result is a contribution margin, the amount left to cover everything above direct route cost, not a net-profit or take-home number.

What does margin per vehicle-hour actually measure, and why compare routes on it instead of total margin?

Contribution margin divided by total paid hours (route time plus billable wait) — on the defaults, $151.80 ÷ 3.0 hours = $50.60/hour. Two routes can show the same total margin while tying up very different amounts of a driver's day; per-hour margin is what actually competes against the next route offer or the next stop on the schedule, which total dollars alone hides.

Is a positive contribution margin enough to accept the route?

No. It only means revenue exceeds this route's direct cost of labor, vehicle, consumables, and overhead. It says nothing about opportunity cost against a better-paying route at the same time slot, about whether the loaded labor rate and overhead percentage you entered are accurate, or about taxes and owner draw, which come out of contribution margin, not before it.

Can I use this for STAT or dedicated routes, not just scheduled ones?

Yes — the route-type selector is a label for your own records; the math is identical for scheduled, STAT, and dedicated routes. STAT routes typically justify a higher loaded labor rate for the urgency and a shorter grace window; dedicated routes usually carry a lower per-stop consumable cost and a higher mileage rate if the vehicle sits idle between runs. Enter each route type's own numbers rather than reusing one set across all three.

Can I enter protected health information or patient details?

No. This calculator is route economics only — revenue, time, miles, and cost inputs — and none of the fields are designed to hold patient, specimen, or protected health information. Nothing you enter is uploaded regardless, but keep PHI out of every field as a matter of policy, not just because the tool is local.
