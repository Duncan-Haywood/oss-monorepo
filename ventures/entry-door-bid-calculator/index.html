      Common questions

      Why can a competitor advertise “$1,200 installed”?

      Usually because the special is written around one specific job, and that job is a preset here: a 36-inch steel prehung unit in a square, rot-free, one-story opening. It really does price low. What the flat number cannot see is sidelights, a transom, rot feet, an out-of-square opening, brickmold, a storm door, and second-story access. Price your actual opening beside the special and the gap becomes a list of line items instead of an argument about who is honest.

      Why do sidelights cost more than just a wider door?

      Because each sidelight is its own sash and glazing unit that has to be set plumb and sealed alongside the main door, not just a bigger single unit. A configuration with two sidelights and a transom is three separate framed openings working as one, and the hang time reflects that — it does not scale the way a single wider slab would.

      Why does rot at the sill change the price so much?

      Because rotted framing has to be cut out and rebuilt before the new unit can be set plumb and square, and that carpentry is billed by the linear foot of rot rather than folded into the door price. An opening with 8 feet of sill and jamb rot is a framing repair with a door hung afterward, not a door hang with a little extra caulk.

      Is this the same as a garage door or a window replacement?

      No. This prices a pedestrian entry unit — the door, sidelights, the opening, and the extras. It is distinct from garage-door replacement (springs, torsion hardware, an opener) and from window replacement (united inches, flashing, interior stops, no full-height framing repair).

      What’s the difference between the bid price and the walk-away price?

      The bid price already includes your target margin on top of labor, materials, and overhead. The walk-away price is the total cost underneath it — the unit, sidelights, rot repair, hardware, and finish — the point below which the job stops being profitable at all. Typing a price you are considering shows you the margin or the loss before you send it.
