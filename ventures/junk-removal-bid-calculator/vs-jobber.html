This calculator vs. Jobber

Two different shapes: Jobber is an account-based field service management platform for running a junk removal company's whole operation. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this one cleanout cost, given its material weight and the landfill ticket, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

Jobber is a field service management platform used by home service businesses — junk removal, lawn care, cleaning, HVAC and plumbing among them — to run client management and online quoting, scheduling and crew dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client-facing hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. Jobber's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific feature list, plan names, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of field service software describe scheduling, invoicing and collecting payment for a job once its scope and price already exist; none describe working out what a cleanout should cost from material weight and a landfill dump ticket before you quote it — which is the one thing this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what Jobber does. It does not schedule crews, dispatch trucks, or invoice a customer, and it stores no customer or job record on a server. It takes each material pile's volume and a starting density (mixed junk, furniture, construction debris, drywall, yard waste, dirt, concrete, shingles), plus per-item appliance, mattress, and tire fees, and turns that into estimated tons, the landfill dump ticket (per-ton rate against a minimum, plus surcharge), loaded labor with stairs and a long carry, drive time, and one dump run per truck-full, then overhead and target margin, producing the bid price, the price per cubic yard, the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money at the scale — plus, if you already have a price on the table, a check of whether it clears that walk-away floor, and a single printable customer proposal for that one job. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Jobber?

They do different jobs, and a hauling company of any size will likely end up touching both eventually. Jobber is a field service management platform used across home service trades to run client management and quoting, scheduling and dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. This calculator does one narrower thing: starting from each material pile's volume and density, plus appliance, mattress, and tire special fees, it estimates the tonnage the landfill will bill, then adds loaded labor for stairs and long carries, drive time, dump runs, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into the bid price, the price per cubic yard, the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money at the scale — before any of it touches a business-management system. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A hauling company that already schedules crews, dispatches trucks, and invoices across every job is better served by Jobber or a comparable field service platform; a hauler who wants an instant, private answer to what one cleanout should cost from its material weight before opening a business system is in the right place here.
