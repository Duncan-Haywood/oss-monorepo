This calculator vs. Housecall Pro

Two different shapes: Housecall Pro runs a home-service business — scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, payments. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this one cleanout cost, given its material weight and the landfill ticket, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

Housecall Pro is field service management software for home service businesses, used as a central hub for the work around a job rather than the pricing of one. It covers a drag-and-drop scheduling calendar, real-time dispatch, automated on-my-way texts with GPS tracking, online booking, recurring service plans, a mobile app that lets a crew pull up the work order and record notes in the field, estimates and invoicing with automated payment reminders, card payments, and a QuickBooks integration. It is sold as an ongoing account for the business, and it publishes that it is used by over 200,000 field service professionals across HVAC, plumbing, electrical, cleaning, junk removal, and other trades. Housecall Pro's current plan names and dollar figures are not verified from this page, so none are repeated here: only its well-documented identity and category — field service management software, sold as an ongoing account — are stated, and no price for Housecall Pro appears anywhere on this page.

This calculator does none of what Housecall Pro does. It does not schedule a job, dispatch a truck, or invoice a customer, and it stores no customer or job record on a server. It takes each material pile's volume and a starting density (mixed junk, furniture, construction debris, drywall, yard waste, dirt, concrete, shingles), plus per-item appliance, mattress, and tire fees, and turns that into estimated tons, the landfill dump ticket (per-ton rate against a minimum, plus surcharge), loaded labor with stairs and a long carry, drive time, and one dump run per truck-full, then overhead and target margin, producing the bid price, the price per cubic yard, the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money at the scale — plus, if you already have a price on the table, a check of whether it clears that walk-away floor, and a single printable customer proposal for that one job. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Housecall Pro?

They do different jobs, and a hauling company of any size will likely end up touching both eventually. A hauling company dispatching crews, booking jobs and chasing invoices across every customer is better served by Housecall Pro or a comparable platform; a hauler who wants an instant, private answer to what one cleanout should cost from its material weight before opening a business system is in the right place here.

Does Housecall Pro price a cleanout for me?

Field service platforms are built to move a job through scheduling, dispatch, invoicing and payment, and they hold whatever price book or flat-rate pricing you put into them. What they do not do is work out that number from a specific pile's material mix, volume, and the landfill's own per-ton dump ticket. This calculator is the step before the price book: it turns those inputs into a single bid price, so the number you load into a platform — or say on the curb before the truck is loaded — has arithmetic under it.
