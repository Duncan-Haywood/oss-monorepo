This checklist vs. a sticky note

A sticky note has one way to mark progress: crossed off or not. It cannot tell you that the one item still standing is waiting on somebody else, with the clock running.

What a sticky note or mental checklist is

A handwritten list, a mental tally, or a few crossed-off lines in a notebook is the fastest way to track a bid's requirements: write down what the solicitation asks for, cross each one off as it gets done. It needs no setup and it is how most independent contractors have always tracked a short list.

Its one signal is how many lines are crossed off. It has no way to mark an item as stuck on someone else's paperwork instead of simply not started, and no way to flag that the deadline is close enough that a stuck item needs escalating today rather than glanced at tomorrow.

What this checklist is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You list each requirement the solicitation asks for, assign it an owner, and mark it Ready, Attention, or Blocked. Only Ready items count toward being done. Blocked is tracked separately from Attention, and the submission deadline is reported in whole calendar days, tipping into urgent at two days or fewer and past-due the moment it goes negative. You get a status board built to surface the one requirement most likely to sink the bid, not a percentage that treats every remaining line the same.

The same bid, read two ways

Five requirements, two days from the deadline: a bid bond, a certificate of insurance, references, and a signed proposal form are all ready. The fifth — a subcontractor's MBE certification — is blocked, waiting on the subcontractor to renew it.

A sticky note's tally reads 4 of 5, or 80% — a number most people read as nearly finished. This checklist reports the bid as not ready, names the one blocked item and that it depends on the subcontractor, and marks the whole submission urgent with 2 days remaining. Both readings come from the same five lines. Only one of them tells you that the remaining item is not a five-minute task — it needs a phone call today, not a glance tomorrow.

Common questions

What does a sticky note or mental checklist miss that this one does not?

A distinction between an item nobody has started and an item that is stuck waiting on someone else. A sticky note has one way to mark progress -- crossed off or not -- so a requirement blocked on a subcontractor's renewal looks identical to one you simply have not gotten to yet. This checklist tracks Blocked as its own status, separate from Attention, so the item that depends on another person is visibly different from the one that is only waiting on you.

Why is "80% done" the most dangerous reading of a bid checklist?

Because a percentage-crossed-off tally treats every remaining item as the same size problem. On this checklist's own worked example -- five requirements, four marked ready and one blocked on a subcontractor's certification, two days from the deadline -- a sticky note reads as 80% done, which sounds nearly finished. This checklist reports ready: false, one blocked item, and urgency: urgent, because the one requirement left is not a five-minute task: it depends on a third party and, with two days remaining, needs escalating today, not glanced at tomorrow.

Doesn't crossing an item off a list also tell you it's ready?

Only if every item that is not crossed off gets the same follow-up, which is exactly what a flat tally cannot guarantee. This checklist separates Ready, Attention, and Blocked, and only Ready counts toward being done -- so a requirement stuck on someone else's paperwork stays visibly distinct from one you have simply not opened yet, instead of collapsing into the same uncrossed line.

How big is the risk in practice?

On this checklist's own worked example: five requirements, four ready, one -- a subcontractor's MBE certification -- blocked, two days before the deadline. A sticky note's tally reads 4 of 5, or 80%, which most people read as nearly finished. This checklist reports the bid as not ready, names the one blocked item and who it is waiting on, and marks the whole bid urgent, because a blocked item with two days left is the one requirement that can still sink the submission -- and it is the one a percentage tally hides best.

Does this checklist require me to stop using my own tracking method?

No. It is a free pass at one solicitation's requirements, not a replacement for however you already run your business. Some contractors will keep a sticky note for quick personal reminders and reach for this checklist specifically when a bid has several requirements, more than one owner, or a tight deadline -- the situations where a flat tally is most likely to hide a blocked item.
