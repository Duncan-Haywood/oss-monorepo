    Common questions

    Why does the effective hourly rate read $639?

    Because it is a check figure, not a labor rate. The default room prices at $15,193.99 over 23.788 hours, but $8,269 of that is the kit, glazing, and finishes — against $1,175.13 of labor. Dividing the whole price by crew hours flatters the number on any material-heavy job. It is useful for spotting a job that has quietly become all-material-no-margin, not for setting a shop rate.

    Does $126.62 per square foot scale to a bigger room?

    Not reliably. It is $15,193.99 over the default 120 sq ft, and several of the biggest inputs — the mobilization, the tie-in to the existing structure, the permit, the site day — do not grow with floor area. A 240 sq ft room costs more than a 120 sq ft one but well short of double. Per-square-foot figures are for comparing two quotes on the same size room.

    What is not in this number?

    The things that most often turn a sunroom quote into a construction project: foundation or slab work, structural modification where the room ties into the house, HVAC extension, electrical circuits, and any engineering or drawings your jurisdiction requires for a permanent addition. A three-season room on an existing deck and a conditioned year-round addition price very differently, and only the first is close to the defaults here.

    Why is the margin a division rather than a markup?

    Because a 25% margin means profit is 25% of the price. The default room costs $11,395.50, so the price is $11,395.50 ÷ 0.75 = $15,193.99. Adding 25% to cost would give $14,244.37 and leave you at exactly a 20% margin — a $950 difference on one room, which is most of a week’s profit on a job this size.
