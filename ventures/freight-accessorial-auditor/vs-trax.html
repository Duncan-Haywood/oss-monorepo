This calculator vs. Trax Technologies

Two different shapes: Trax Technologies audits and pays freight invoices at scale across a shipper's whole carrier network, under an ongoing enterprise account. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — does this one carrier's invoice, checked line by line against your own contract rates, actually owe what it billed, before you pay it.

What each tool is for

Trax Technologies is enterprise transportation spend management (TSM) software, sold under an ongoing account to shippers with freight to audit and pay across many carriers and lanes. Its core freight audit and payment (FAP) service ingests carrier invoices — commonly through EDI and integration with a shipper's TMS or ERP — audits each line against the shipper's contracted rates at scale, and can disburse payment to the carrier on the shipper's behalf, alongside spend visibility and analytics reporting across the whole freight network. Setting it up is an enterprise engagement: onboarding, carrier and rate-card integration, and a contract, not a self-serve signup. Trax's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific plan names, integrations, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page.

This calculator does none of that. It does not integrate with a TMS or ERP, ingest invoices by EDI, or pay a carrier. It takes one carrier's invoice — imported as a CSV export or pasted rows, or typed by hand line by line — maps its columns, and checks each accessorial line against a contract rate card you type once: flat amounts, or per-unit rules for hour, minute, day, cwt, pallet, piece, mile, or stop, each with its own free allowance and cap. It flags duplicate lines, amounts above the rate card, missing codes, and missing evidence, checks a detention charge against the dock clock and a fuel surcharge against the contracted percent of linehaul, and compares eating a line, billing it to the customer, or paying first and filing for a credit. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and the file you import is read locally and never uploaded.
