This calculator vs. Cass Information Systems

Two different shapes: Cass Information Systems audits and pays freight invoices at scale across a shipper's whole carrier network, under an ongoing contracted relationship built for larger freight volumes. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — does this one carrier's invoice, checked line by line against your own contract rates, actually owe what it billed, before you pay it.

What each tool is for

Cass Information Systems (Nasdaq: CASS) is a publicly traded financial services company whose freight business, marketed as Cass Freight, provides freight audit and payment (FAP) processing to shippers: it receives carrier invoices — commonly by EDI or a direct feed from the shipper's TMS or ERP — audits each line against the shipper's contracted rates at scale, and disburses payment to the carrier on the shipper's behalf, alongside consolidated transportation spend data and reporting across the whole freight network. It is set up as an ongoing contracted relationship built for shippers with meaningful freight volume across many carriers and lanes: onboarding and integration, not a self-serve signup. Cass is also known for publishing the monthly Cass Freight Index, a widely cited industry benchmark of North American freight volumes and expenditures — a separate product from the audit-and-payment service itself. Cass's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific plan names, integrations, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page.

This calculator does none of that. It does not integrate with a TMS or ERP, ingest invoices by EDI, or pay a carrier. It takes one carrier's invoice — imported as a CSV export or pasted rows, or typed by hand line by line — maps its columns, and checks each accessorial line against a contract rate card you type once: flat amounts, or per-unit rules for hour, minute, day, cwt, pallet, piece, mile, or stop, each with its own free allowance and cap. It flags duplicate lines, amounts above the rate card, missing codes, and missing evidence, checks a detention charge against the dock clock and a fuel surcharge against the contracted percent of linehaul, and compares eating a line, billing it to the customer, or paying first and filing for a credit. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and the file you import is read locally and never uploaded.
