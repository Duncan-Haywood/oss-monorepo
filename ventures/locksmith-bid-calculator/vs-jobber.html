This calculator vs. Jobber

Two different shapes: Jobber is an account-based platform for running a service company's whole operation, including scheduling the dispatch and invoicing the customer once a call is priced. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this service call cost, given labor and travel time, the after-hours premium, parts, and a minimum charge floor, before you quote it on the phone or at the door.

What each tool is for

Jobber is a field service management platform used by home and trade service businesses — locksmiths, HVAC, plumbing, electrical and general contracting among them — to run client management and online quoting, scheduling and dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client-facing hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. Jobber's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific feature list, plan names, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of field service software describe scheduling a job, dispatching a tech, and invoicing and collecting payment once a price already exists; none describe working out whether an after-hours premium is applied to billed time only rather than to parts, or whether a five-minute lockout still clears the trip cost once a minimum service charge floor is checked — which is what this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what Jobber does. It does not manage a customer list, dispatch a technician, or collect a payment. It takes labor minutes on-site, round-trip travel minutes, an hourly rate, an after-hours or emergency premium applied to billed time only, a trip fee, parts cost and markup, a callback reserve, overhead, target margin, and a minimum service charge floor, and turns that into the price for one call — plus a same-page check of whether today's calls, at that average profit, hit a target daily income, and a printable customer proposal with the scope, deposit, price-held date, and a signature line. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Jobber?

They do different jobs, and a locksmith of any size will likely end up touching both eventually. Jobber is a field service management platform used across home and trade service businesses — including mobile service trades like locksmithing — to run client management and online quoting, scheduling and dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. This calculator does one narrower thing: it takes labor and travel minutes, an after-hours or emergency premium, parts cost and markup, a callback reserve, overhead and target margin, and a minimum service charge floor, and turns that into the price for one call, then checks whether a day's worth of calls hits an income goal. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A locksmith already scheduling dispatches, managing a customer list, and invoicing and collecting payment across an ongoing business is better served by Jobber or a comparable field service platform; a locksmith who wants an instant, private answer to what one call should cost — with the after-hours premium and the minimum charge built in — before quoting it on the phone or at the door, is in the right place here.
