This calculator vs. Jobber

Two different shapes: Jobber is account-based field-service software for running a locksmith business across clients, scheduling, and technicians. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this service call cost, after-hours premium and minimum charge included, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

Jobber is field-service management software founded in 2011 and headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada, used by home-service businesses across HVAC, plumbing, electrical, lawn care, and cleaning trades. It combines client management, quoting, scheduling, invoicing, and payment collection into one subscription system meant to run a whole business across every job and every technician, and it says it serves over 200,000 users. It is sold as an ongoing subscription account. Jobber's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact plan names or dollar figures, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — home-service field-service management software, sold as a subscription account — are stated, and no dollar amount for Jobber appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what Jobber does. It does not manage clients, schedule technicians, send invoices, or collect payments, and it stores no customer or job record on a server. It takes labor minutes on-site, round-trip travel minutes, an hourly rate, whether the call is after-hours or emergency (and the premium that applies to billed time only, never to parts), a trip fee, parts cost and markup, a callback reserve for warranty returns, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into the price for the call and where the minimum service charge floor would take over — plus a printable customer proposal with scope lines and a signature block. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.
