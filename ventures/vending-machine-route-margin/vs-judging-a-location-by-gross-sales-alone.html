This calculator vs. judging a location by gross sales alone

A location report that reads "$105.00 in sales this visit" looks like the better stop next to one that reads "$66.50" -- until commission, card fees, shrink, drive time, and the machine's own monthly cost come out. Gross sales is revenue before every one of those costs, and none of them is the same percentage of sales at every location: a location farther from home base, or on a steeper commission split, or with more shrink, can look bigger on the sales line and still be the worse stop once its own costs are counted against it.

That makes gross sales the right number for knowing what a machine took in. It cannot answer the question that decides whether to keep, renegotiate, or drop a location: what did this visit actually net, after everything it cost to run?

Two locations, one gross-sales ranking that flips

Run through this calculator's own arithmetic, same restock frequency for both: Location A sells 60 units at $1.75 with a 25% commission, a 5% shrink rate, and a 35-minute round-trip drive, for $105.00 in gross sales. Location B sells 38 units at the same price with a 10% commission, 2% shrink, and an 8-minute round trip, for $66.50 -- 37% less than A on the sales line alone.

Once commission, card fees, shrink, mileage, restock labor at $22/hr, and the machine's own monthly cost are taken out, A's costs come to $96.67 against B's $42.06. A nets $8.33 for the visit (about $33.31 projected for the month at 4 visits); B nets $24.44 for the visit (about $97.77 projected for the month) -- B, the location with 37% less gross sales, nets nearly three times A's monthly profit. A gross-sales ranking would have kept A and questioned B; the actual costs say the opposite.

Not a claim about your own locations -- both are illustrative inputs to this calculator's own published arithmetic, chosen to show how commission, shrink, and drive time can outweigh a sales-line difference. Your own numbers may rank the other way, or not flip at all; that is exactly what typing them into the fields above tells you and a sales report alone cannot.

Common questions

Why is gross sales a bad way to rank vending locations?

Because gross sales is revenue before commission, card fees, shrink, mileage, your own restock labor, and the machine's own cost are taken out -- and those five costs are not the same percentage of sales at every location. A location with a 25% commission split and a 35-minute round trip can post higher gross sales than a location with a 10% split five minutes away, and still net far less profit, because the costs that gross sales ignores are larger there.

How big is the gap in practice?

Using this calculator's own arithmetic on two illustrative locations at the same restock frequency: Location A sells 60 units at $1.75 with a 25% commission, 5% shrink, and a 35-minute round trip, for $105.00 in gross sales -- 58% more than Location B's $66.50. But A's costs (commission, card fee, shrink, mileage, labor, machine share) come to $96.67 against B's $42.06, so A nets $8.33 a visit ($33.31 projected for the month) while B nets $24.44 a visit ($97.77 projected for the month) -- B, the location with the lower gross sales, makes nearly three times the monthly profit.

Does a higher commission or longer drive always mean lower profit?

Not always -- a location can have a high commission or a long drive and still be the better stop if its sales volume is high enough to absorb both. The point is not that gross sales is worthless, only that it cannot tell the two cases apart on its own: two locations with different sales, commission splits, shrink, and drive time can rank in either order once actual costs are counted, and gross sales alone gives no way to know which.

What does this calculator use instead of gross sales to judge a location?

Net profit per visit and projected monthly profit, after location commission, card processing fees, shrink/spoilage, drive time and mileage at your own target hourly rate, restock labor, and the machine's amortized monthly cost -- the same figures a DEX report or a cashless dashboard alone will not compute for you.
