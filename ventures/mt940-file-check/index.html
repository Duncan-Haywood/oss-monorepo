    The rules, and what they leave out

    • Tags: :20: reference (16 characters, no leading or trailing /), :25: account, :28C: statement number, :60F: opening balance, :61: transaction lines, :86: details, :62F: closing balance.
    • Arithmetic: opening balance plus every :61: (C adds, D subtracts, RC and RD reverse) must equal the closing balance; the next statement must open where the last one closed.
    • Amounts: a comma as decimal mark, no thousands separator, a trailing comma on whole amounts.
    • Dates: YYMMDD value dates and MMDD entry dates must be real calendar dates.
    • Fixed automatically: dot amounts to commas, the closing - between statements, and, only if you ask, characters outside the SWIFT set.
    • Never guessed: a balance, an amount's value, which line is missing, or whether two similar rows are one payment.
    • Not checked: that the balances match your bank, that the file came from your bank, :86: structured subfields (German ?20 codes), camt.053 XML, or what any one importer will accept. Read from memory of common MT940 practice; bank dialects differ, so confirm against your bank's specification.

    Common questions

    Why does my bank's MT940 file fail to import into DATEV, Xero or QuickBooks?

    The usual causes are an amount written with a dot instead of a comma, a missing :60F: opening or :62F: closing balance, a closing balance that does not equal the opening balance plus the :61: lines, a :20: reference over 16 characters, and umlauts or accents the importer cannot read. The checker names each one with its line number.

    What does 'Balance does not add up' mean?

    The opening balance plus every :61: transaction must equal the closing balance. When it does not, a line is missing, repeated, mis-signed (C and D swapped) or a reversal (RC/RD) was counted the wrong way. The report shows the exact difference so you can spot the amount.

    Why does MT940 use a comma for decimals?

    The SWIFT field definition writes amounts with a comma as the decimal mark and no thousands separator, so 1234.56 is written 1234,56 and a whole amount is written 100,. A dot makes strict importers reject the line.

    Does it change my file without telling me?

    No. Every change is listed with its line. It only rewrites dots to commas, adds the closing - between statements and, if you ask, swaps characters outside the SWIFT set (ä to ae, é to e). It never edits a balance or an amount's value.

    Is my file uploaded anywhere?

    No. It is read in your browser tab and a test in the code asserts there are no network calls. Statements hold account numbers, so do not paste one into a tool that uploads it.
