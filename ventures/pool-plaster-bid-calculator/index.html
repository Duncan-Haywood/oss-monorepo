      Common questions

      Why does a 16×32 pool come out as 1,064 square feet of plaster?

      Because you plaster the walls, not just the water surface. On the defaults the floor is 512 sq ft (16 × 32) and the walls add another 552 — the perimeter, 96 ft, times the average depth of 5.75 ft, which is the mean of the 3.5 ft shallow end and the 8 ft deep end. That is why a “512 square foot pool” buys a bit over twice that much finish material, and why quoting off water-surface area alone underbuys the job by roughly half.

      Why are there two different per-square-foot prices?

      They divide the same price by two different areas. On the defaults the job prices at $7,825.99, which is $7.36 per plaster square foot (over all 1,064 sq ft of shell) but $15.29 per water square foot (over the 512 sq ft footprint a homeowner would measure). Neither is wrong; they answer different questions. Use the plaster figure to sanity-check your material and labor rates, and the water-surface figure only when you are comparing against a competitor who quoted that way.

      What is the drain-and-refill line actually costing me?

      Two things, and the water is the larger one. The default pool holds about 22,023 gallons (512 sq ft × 5.75 ft average depth × 7.48 gallons per cubic foot), which at $0.012 per gallon is $264.27 of water, plus 3.5 hours of attended drain and refill time. Turning the option off removes both. If your district meters filling differently, or you are on a haul-in rate, that per-gallon field is the one to change.

      Why doesn’t a 26% target margin mean multiplying cost by 1.26?

      Because margin is measured against the price, not against the cost. The default job costs $5,791.23 all in, and holding 26% of the price as profit means dividing by 0.74, not multiplying by 1.26: $5,791.23 ÷ 0.74 = $7,825.99. Marking cost up by 26% would give $7,296.95 and leave you at a 20.6% margin — the single most common way a bid that looked profitable on paper is not.

      Does the price include chip-out?

      Not on the defaults. The chip-out selector starts at “none” — a bond-coat resurface over sound plaster — so the chip-out square footage and its hours are both zero. That is the brochure special most homeowners are comparing against. Switch to partial or full chip-out and both the demolition hours and the dumpster line become real, which is usually the difference between the advertised number and the one you can actually deliver.
