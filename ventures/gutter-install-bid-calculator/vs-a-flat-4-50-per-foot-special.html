This calculator vs. a flat $4.50-a-foot special

"$4.50 a foot installed" is not a price for a run of gutter — it is a price for one specific run, quietly applied to every run that calls for a quote.

What a flat per-foot special is

A flat per-foot special is the fastest number a shop can put on a truck wrap or a search ad: one rate, no takeoff, easy for a homeowner to multiply by a tape-measure guess and compare between bids. It works for the shop as a lead magnet and works for the homeowner as a round number to shop around with. Nothing about the rate says which run it was built for.

The rate was set for one job — commonly a smaller, one-story run in standard 5-inch aluminum, mostly outside corners, with the first two downspouts and their footage already included, no fascia repair, and no tear-off — and then quoted to every caller afterward, whether or not the next run looks anything like the one the special was priced on.

What this calculator does instead

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the gutter footage, profile, inside and outside miters, downspouts and downspout footage past the included allowance, story count, fascia footage, and flags for tear-off, steep access, and hidden hangers, plus your crew's wage, payroll burden, and target margin. It turns that into the crew hours the job actually consumes, the price per gutter foot, and a walk-away price below which the job loses money — for this run, not the run the special was built on.

Common questions

Why does a flat $4.50-a-foot special get gutter installs wrong?

Because the labor and material a gutter run needs come from its profile, inside and outside miters, downspouts and downspout footage past the included allowance, story count, fascia condition, and tear-off — not from being "gutter." A 140-foot one-story ranch with two included downspouts and no fascia work, and a 220-foot two-story colonial with two inside miters, four downspouts, 40 feet of fascia repair, and tear-off of the old system, are both "gutter," and a flat per-foot special charges them the same rate.

What job is the $4.50-a-foot number actually built around?

One specific job, usually the easiest one a company is willing to advertise: a smaller, one-story run in standard 5-inch aluminum, mostly outside corners, with the first two downspouts and their footage already included, no fascia repair, and no tear-off. It is a marketing rate meant to get the phone to ring, not a number derived from any particular run's corners, stories, or condition.

What happens when the real job is bigger than the special's job?

On this calculator's own 2-story colonial preset — 220 feet of 6-inch aluminum, two inside miters, four downspouts, 40 feet of fascia repair, and tear-off, priced at this tool's own shop-rate defaults — the walk-away cost of labor, material, and overhead alone comes to about $4,611.88. Matching a $4.50-a-foot special on that run prices it at $990.00, about $3,621.88 under the walk-away cost, before any profit is counted — the job loses money the day it is scheduled, not just when it runs long.

What happens on the job the special was written for?

On this calculator's own 1-story ranch preset — 140 feet of 5-inch aluminum, four outside miters, no inside miters, the first two downspouts included, no fascia, no tear-off — the label this preset ships under is literally "the $4.50/ft special." Its own walk-away cost at this tool's shop-rate defaults is about $1,189.34. Charging $4.50 a foot on 140 feet is $630.00, about $559.34 under the walk-away cost, on the exact job the rate was written to cover — before a single inside miter, extra downspout, or story is added.

Is a flat per-foot special ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of runs close to the one the special was built around — mostly outside corners, downspouts within the included allowance, one story, no fascia, no tear-off — a flat rate can land close, if the shop's own costs happen to sit below it. The risk is applying it outside that band without noticing, because the postcard rate does not know which band a given run is in and neither does the crew until the corners, drops, and stories are actually counted.

Does this calculator know what to charge in my market?

No. It has no data on local rates, material prices, or what competitors advertise. It takes the wage, material cost, and overhead you enter and turns them into a walk-away price for the footage, corners, drops, stories, and fascia you describe — the arithmetic a flat per-foot special skips, not a market survey.

Nothing here says or implies that quoting a flat per-foot special is a bad-faith practice — it is a common, honest way to advertise that works until the run stops matching the one the special was priced on. This page describes where that shortcut breaks down, not a fault in anyone who uses it. The $630.00, $1,189.34, $1,749.03, $990.00, $4,611.88, $6,782.18, and $3,621.88 figures above are this calculator's own arithmetic on the two worked jobs built into its own preset list (open the calculator and pick the "1-story ranch" or "2-story colonial" entry from Job preset to reproduce them), not a claim about any specific contractor's real costs or any specific company's advertised rate.
