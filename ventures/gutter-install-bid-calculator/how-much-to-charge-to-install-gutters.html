How much to charge to install gutters

The number a homeowner wants is a published “$4.50 a foot installed” gutter special, or a price they saw for a ranch hang on a coupon site. That number is not a takeoff. A 140-foot one-story 5-inch aluminum hang with two included downspouts is not the same job as a 220-foot two-story 6-inch hang with extra drops, inside miters, forty feet of fascia, and tear-off of the old system. The trip is the same. The hours are not.

Count the corners and the drops, not just the feet

Gutter-cleaning calculators price stories and downspouts for a wash. Gutter-guard calculators price mesh on an existing trough. A new hang is a different job: the trough, the miters, the drops past the included first two, the stories, and the fascia that is not in the coupon. Quoting all of that from a published per-foot special is how the first inside miter eats the field margin.

A job with the same trip can cost much more

Each extra drop past the included first two is time that never appears on the coupon. A second story is billed as its own hours. Inside miters, fascia, tear-off, and hidden hangers are adders that get forgotten on a brochure special. On the sample rates a 220-foot 6-inch aluminum colonial with two inside miters, four downspouts, 40 feet of fascia, tear-off, and hidden hangers prices about $6,782.18 ($30.83 per foot of gutter, 32.3 crew-hours). The same shop’s 140-foot 5-inch ranch prices about $1,749.03 ($12.49 per foot, 8.1 hours, walk-away $1,189.34). Matching the brochure job’s $4.50 special on the two-story house is how that job becomes a loss — the walk-away on the heavy job is already $4,611.88, and the $4.50 special ($630 on the ranch) loses $559.34 even on the brochure hang.

Copper and tear-off are not automatically a markup on the ranch

A 160-foot two-story copper hang with steep access and tear-off is not the 140-foot ranch plus 15%. On the sample rates that copper job prices about $7,396.97 ($46.23 per foot, 21.6 crew-hours) — more than the brochure hang, not because copper is slower to hang but because story hours, steep access, and an $18-per-foot trough do not live in a published ranch special. An 180-foot one-story 5-inch hang with tear-off and a third downspout prices about $2,851.79 (14.0 hours). Treating either as a blended add-on is how the special that looked fine turns into a loss after the first inside miter.

Load the wage, then keep a walk-away number

A $38/h installer loaded at 32% payroll burden is $50.16. Margin is a share of the price you send, not a markup on cost. The walk-away price is total cost — below that the job makes nothing before you take a dollar home. Replace every rate with your invoices before you send either number.

What this is not

This is not a gutter design, a water-management plan, a building-code determination, a permit, an engineering stamp, or a recommended market price. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type. Footage, profile, stories, miters, downspouts, fascia, and tear-off are yours to confirm. It does not price a cleaning route or a gutter-guard install.

Common questions

How much to charge to install gutters?

Charge from the trip, the hang, inside and outside miters, extra downspouts past the included first two, extra downspout footage past ten feet per drop, second-story hours, fascia, tear-off, loaded labor, overhead, and a margin that is a share of price. A default 140-foot one-story 5-inch aluminum ranch with four outside miters and two included downspouts, at the sample rates in this calculator, prices around $1,749 — about $12.49 per foot of gutter.

Why is a $4.50-a-foot gutter special dangerous?

Homeowners count linear feet. Corners, extra drops, extra downspout footage, a second story, fascia rot, and tear-off do not scale with that number. Matching a cheaper special written for a one-story ranch is how a two-story colonial with fascia becomes a two-day loss. On the sample rates the $4.50 special is $630 on the ranch and already $559 under the walk-away cost.

How much to charge to install gutters on a two-story house with fascia?

A two-story six-inch hang with extra drops and fascia is not a ranch-only special. On the sample rates a 220-foot 6-inch aluminum colonial with two inside miters, four downspouts, 40 feet of fascia, tear-off, and hidden hangers prices around $6,782 ($30.83 per foot, 32.3 crew-hours), while a 140-foot 5-inch ranch prices around $1,749 ($12.49 per foot, 8.1 hours).
