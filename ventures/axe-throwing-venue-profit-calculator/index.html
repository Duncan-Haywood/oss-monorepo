How it is built

Slots: lane slots a month = lanes × hours open a day × bookings per lane-hour × open days × 52 ÷ 12. League nights take their lane time out first (players ÷ throwers per lane, rounded up, times the league night length), and the rest are walk-in slots. Walk-in throwers = walk-in slots × average fill × throwers per lane. Revenue: walk-in throwers × price, plus league players × season fee × seasons a year ÷ 12, plus events × package price, plus every thrower × drinks and snacks spend. Costs: drinks and snacks cost, event cost, an axe and target replacement reserve per thrower, card fees, coaches on the floor for every open hour, rent, utilities, insurance and other fixed costs, and any loan payment.

Payback: build cost ÷ monthly profit before the loan payment. Lowest price: the price per thrower at which margin equals your target, and the price at which payback equals your target; typing either back reproduces the target. Sensitivity: the same venue with walk-in visits cut by 10% and 20% (leagues and events held). Break-even fill: profit is exactly linear in walk-in visits, so it is solved exactly.

Common questions

How much does it cost to open an axe-throwing venue?

Plan on roughly $60,000 to $250,000 for lanes, targets, axes and safety netting, plus a fit-out for flooring, a bar or lounge, a front desk and restrooms, on top of the lease deposit. Enter your own quotes for both and the calculator adds them into one build cost and divides it by monthly operating profit to give a payback.

What fill does an axe-throwing venue need to break even?

It depends on rent, coaches and price per thrower, so the calculator solves it: it scales walk-in bookings up or down until monthly profit is exactly zero and reports that as a percentage of walk-in lane slots. League seasons and corporate events pay for part of the fixed costs before a single walk-in books a lane, so the break-even fill is lower than most owners guess.

How much of an axe-throwing venue's income comes from leagues and corporate events?

Often a third or more of revenue, and the most predictable part because leagues are paid per season and events are booked ahead. The calculator counts a league as players times a season fee, repeated each season, and takes the lane time a league night uses out of walk-in capacity. It warns when leagues or events grow past a third of revenue.

What price per thrower do I need to reach my target margin?

The calculator solves for the price that makes margin equal your target and shows it, next to the price that reaches your payback target. The test suite checks that charging exactly those prices earns the target for hundreds of random venues.
