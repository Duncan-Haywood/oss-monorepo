How much does an axe-throwing venue make?

A venue with 10 lanes open 8 hours, 6 days a week can sell 2080 lane bookings a month. With 18% of walk-in slots filled, three league nights and a dozen corporate events, it keeps about one dollar in eight, and the build takes over five years to come back.

The worked example

A venue with 10 lanes, 8 hours a day, 6 days a week and one booking per lane-hour has 2080 lane slots a month. Three league nights a week of 40 players each, two hours long, take 10 lanes for two hours each night, which leaves 1820 walk-in slots. At an 18% fill with 4 throwers a booking that is 1310 walk-in throwers. Tickets are $22, so walk-ins bring in $28828.80.

The leagues enrol 120 players paying $100 a season, four seasons a year, which is $4000.00 a month. Twelve corporate events at $600 bring in $7200.00. With league and event guests there are 1870 throwers in all, and $6 of drinks and snacks each adds $11222.40.

Four coaches on the floor cost $15841.28 a month at $17 an hour plus 12% payroll cost. With rent, utilities, insurance and other costs, fixed costs are $37041.28.

The price a 20% margin needs

The venue earns 12.5%, short of 20%. With everything else unchanged, the lowest price per thrower that reaches 20% is $25.83, $3.83 more than the $22 entered.

Hitting a 36-month payback

At 62.7 months the venue is past a 36-month payback target. The lowest price that reaches 36 months is $25.72, $3.72 more than now. Payback is measured before any loan payment, so borrowing does not flatter it; a loan still comes out of monthly profit.

If walk-in visits come in under plan

Rent and coaches do not shrink when the lanes are quiet. With 10% fewer walk-in visits, monthly profit falls from $6,383 to $3,139 and payback stretches to 127.4 months. With 20% fewer, profit is -$106 a month, a loss.

A higher price with fewer bookings

Charging $26 and filling 12% of walk-in slots makes $3287.42 a month against $6383.42, and pays back in 121.7 months against 62.7. Raising price does not rescue the venue if it costs more bookings than the extra dollars bring in; the second scenario in the calculator tests exactly that.

Common questions

What fill does an axe-throwing venue need to break even?

It depends on rent, coaches and price per thrower, so the calculator solves it: it scales walk-in bookings up or down until monthly profit is exactly zero and reports that as a percentage of walk-in lane slots. League seasons and corporate events pay for part of the fixed costs before a single walk-in books a lane, so the break-even fill is lower than most owners guess.

How much of an axe-throwing venue's income comes from leagues and corporate events?

Often a third or more of revenue, and the most predictable part because leagues are paid per season and events are booked ahead. The calculator counts a league as players times a season fee, repeated each season, and takes the lane time a league night uses out of walk-in capacity. It warns when leagues or events grow past a third of revenue.

What price per thrower do I need to reach my target margin?

The calculator solves for the price that makes margin equal your target and shows it, next to the price that reaches your payback target. The test suite checks that charging exactly those prices earns the target for hundreds of random venues.
