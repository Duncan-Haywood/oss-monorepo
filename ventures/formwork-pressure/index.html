Questions

How is the lateral pressure of concrete on formwork calculated?

Fresh concrete pushes on a form like a liquid until it starts to stiffen. ACI 347R gives equations for the design pressure from the rate of placement R (feet of rise per hour) and the concrete temperature T: for columns, and for walls placed under 7 ft/h no more than 14 ft high, p = Cw·Cc·(150 + 9000R/T) psf; for taller walls and walls placed at 7 to 15 ft/h, p = Cw·Cc·(150 + 43,400/T + 2800R/T). The result is never less than 600·Cw psf and never more than the full liquid head w·h.

When must forms be designed for full liquid head?

When the equations' conditions are not met: concrete with a slump over 7 inches, self-consolidating concrete, concrete pumped in from the base of the form, forms with external vibrators, internal vibration deeper than 4 ft below the surface, and walls placed faster than 15 ft per hour. Then the pressure is the unit weight times the depth, w·h.

What are Cw and Cc?

Cw adjusts for unit weight: 1.0 for 140 to 150 lb/ft³ concrete, w/145 above that, and 0.5(1 + w/145) but at least 0.80 below 140. Cc adjusts for cement chemistry: 1.0 for Type I, II or III cement without a retarder, 1.2 with a retarder or for other cements and moderate slag or fly ash blends, and 1.4 for those blends with a retarder or for mixes with 70% or more slag or 40% or more fly ash.

How do I get tie spacing from the pressure?

Each tie holds the pressure acting on its share of the form. Divide the tie's safe working load by the design pressure to get the area each tie may carry, then divide that by the horizontal spacing to get the largest vertical spacing. Walers, studs and sheathing need their own checks.
