Common questions

    
How much interest can I charge on a late business invoice in the UK?

    
Under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 you can claim statutory interest at 8% a year over the Bank of England base rate. The base rate used is the one on 31 December for debts that fell due January to June, or on 30 June for debts due July to December. Interest is simple and runs from the day after the due date.


What fixed compensation can I claim?

    
A fixed sum per late invoice: £40 for debts under £1,000, £70 for £1,000 to £9,999.99, and £100 for £10,000 or more. You can also claim reasonable debt recovery costs if the fixed sum does not cover them.


When does a business invoice become late if no date was agreed?

    
Under the Act the default is 30 days after the later of the invoice being received and the goods or services being delivered. If you agreed a payment date or terms, that date applies.


Does this apply to consumers?

    
No. The Act covers contracts where both sides are acting in the course of a business, or a public body is the buyer. It does not cover debts owed by private individuals.


Can I write a different late fee into my terms?

    
Contract terms can set their own interest only if they provide a substantial remedy for late payment; otherwise the statutory rate applies. Check this with a solicitor if it matters.
