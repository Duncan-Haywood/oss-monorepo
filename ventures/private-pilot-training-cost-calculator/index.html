What it does, and what it leaves to you

    
    
      
  • Counts: aircraft = total flight hours x aircraft rate; instructor hours = hours with an instructor x (1 + briefing hours) + extra ground hours; instructor = instructor hours x instructor rate; total = aircraft + instructor + course, medical, written test, examiner and gear; extra hour = aircraft rate + (1 + briefing) x instructor rate; weeks = hours / (flights per week x hours per lesson); club saving = hours x (rental rate - club rate) - dues x months of training.
    • 
      
  • Minimums used: 14 CFR 61.109 (Part 61 private pilot): 40 flight hours, 20 with an instructor, 10 solo. Part 141 schools can have different minimums, and other certificates differ.
    • 
      
  • Not decided: how many hours you will actually need, your school's real rates and any fuel surcharges or minimum block purchases, rates changing during training, the cost of a failed checkride and retest, instrument or other ratings, the medical certificate you qualify for, financing and tax treatment.

Common questions

    
How much does a private pilot license cost?

    
Aircraft rental and instructor time make up almost all of it. Multiply your total flight hours by the aircraft rate, add the instructor's time for lessons, briefings and ground, then add the medical, written test, examiner and gear. The FAA minimum is 40 flight hours, but many schools say students need more, so the page shows the total at several hour counts.

    
What is the FAA minimum number of hours?

    
14 CFR 61.109 requires at least 40 hours of flight time for a private pilot certificate under Part 61, including at least 20 hours with an instructor and 10 hours solo. Schools quote higher averages because most students need more practice than the minimum. Ask your school what its own students need.

    
Is it cheaper to join a flying club?

    
Clubs usually charge monthly dues and a lower hourly rate, so they win when you fly often. The page works out the break-even in flight hours a month from your own figures, and what a club would save or cost over your training.

    
Does flying more often reduce the total cost?

    
Many instructors say frequent lessons mean less repeating of material. This page does not predict that for you. Enter the hours you expect to need, and the table shows what each extra ten hours costs so you can weigh a faster schedule against it.

    
Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    
No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
