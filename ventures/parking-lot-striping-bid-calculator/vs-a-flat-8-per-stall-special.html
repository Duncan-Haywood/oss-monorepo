This calculator vs. a flat $8-a-stall special

“$8 a stall” or “$400 for a 50-space lot” is not a price for parking-lot striping — it is a price for one specific layout, ADA count, and coat count, quietly applied to every lot that calls in.

What a flat price per stall is

A flat rate per stall is the fastest way to quote a striping job: multiply the stall count by a number and read off a price. It is the number a property manager holding last year's invoice or a competitor's flyer will call and ask you to match, whatever their lot actually needs.

The number behind it was set for one job — a faded restripe following existing paint, a couple of ADA stalls, a handful of arrows, one coat, no fire lane — and then reused for every lot afterward, whether or not the next job looks anything like the one the rate was built on.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter standard and ADA stall counts, arrows, stop bars, crosswalks, handicap symbols, fire-lane and curb footage, restripe versus new layout, coats, and night work, plus your own labor hours and material costs. It prices the layout, the ADA aisles, the stencils, and the coats separately by kind, then returns the walk-away price and a bid price above your target margin — for this lot, not a generic one.

What a flat price per stall doesWhat this calculator does instead
Charges the same $/stall whether the job follows existing paint or needs a full string-line new layout, even though new-layout hours sit on every stall, not just the extras. Prices restripe versus new layout separately, so a new-layout job costs more than a restripe of the same stall count.
Has no line for ADA hash aisles and signs, regardless of whether the lot carries two ADA stalls or six. Prices ADA hash line, symbols, and signs separately by count, so an ADA-heavy lot costs more than one with few accessible stalls.
Has no line for arrows, stop bars, crosswalks, fire-lane footage, curb paint, or a second coat, regardless of how much the job needs. Prices each extra separately — stencils, fire lane, curb, and second-coat labor — so a fully-marked lot costs more than a bare restripe.
Gives the estimator a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the walk-away price and the dollar gap against a flat per-stall rate, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before you commit to it.

Applied to the new-layout job, it counts only the stalls and never sees the string-line hours, the extra ADA aisles, the fire lane, the second coat, or the night premium at all. A flat per-stall rate is not wrong because it is simple — it is wrong when it is applied outside the one lot it was built for, and nothing about a flat number tells you when that has happened. This calculator does not know your local market; it replaces the arithmetic a flat rate skips.

Common questions

Why does a flat price per stall get a parking-lot striping bid wrong?

Because the cost driver is restripe versus new layout, ADA hash aisles and signs, stencils and fire-lane footage, and coats -- not a blended stall count. A 120-stall new layout with ADA hash, arrows, a fire lane, and two coats is doing several jobs a 50-stall restripe never touches, none of which scale with stall count the way a per-stall rate assumes.

What does a flat price per stall usually leave out entirely?

New-layout measuring and string-line hours, ADA hash aisle line and signs, arrows and stop-bar stencils, crosswalks, fire-lane and curb footage, and a second coat. A faded restripe with a couple of ADA stalls is doing far less than a new layout with a fire lane and two coats -- and a per-stall number has no line for any of it.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own shipped presets, at its own shipped shop rates: a 50-stall retail restripe prices at $1,126.98, or $22.54 a stall. Applying that same $22.54 rate to a 120-stall new layout with ADA hash, arrows, a fire lane, and two coats quotes $2,704.76, which is $1,877.99 below that job's own $4,582.75 break-even cost, before a dollar of profit, and $3,405.58 below the calculator's own $6,110.34 bid price.

Is a flat price per stall ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of near-identical jobs -- a restripe, one coat, few ADA stalls, no fire lane -- a per-stall rate can land close, and on this calculator's own restripe preset it is exactly the rate that job supports, because it was set from that job. The risk is applying the same rate outside that band without noticing, because the rate does not know which lot it was built on and neither does the estimator until layout, ADA, stencils, and coats are actually spelled out.

Does this calculator know what materials or labor cost in my market?

No. It has no data on local material or labor rates. It takes stall counts, ADA aisles, stencils, fire-lane and curb footage, layout, and coats, plus the labor hours, paint cost, and wage you enter, and turns that into a price for this job -- the arithmetic a flat per-stall rate skips, not a market survey.
