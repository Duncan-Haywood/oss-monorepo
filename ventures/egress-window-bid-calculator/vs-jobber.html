This calculator vs. Jobber

Two different shapes: Jobber is account-based field-service software for running a home-service business's clients, quoting, scheduling, invoicing, and payments. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this egress-window job cost, given its opening type and cut, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

Jobber is field-service management software founded in 2011 and headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada, used by home-service businesses across HVAC, plumbing, electrical, lawn care, and cleaning trades. It combines client management, quoting, scheduling, invoicing, and payment collection into one subscription system meant to run a whole business across every job and every crew, and it says it serves over 200,000 users. It is sold as an ongoing subscription account. Jobber's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact plan names or dollar figures, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — home-service field-service management software, sold as a subscription account — are stated, and no dollar amount for Jobber appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what Jobber does. It does not manage clients, schedule crews, send invoices, or collect payments, and it stores no customer record on a server. It takes one egress-window job — window count, opening type (hopper, slider, casement, fiberglass), whether the opening is a retrofit into an existing hole or a wood, masonry, or poured-concrete cut, well material, extras, who supplies the sash and well, and whether the crew works a weekday, an occupied-basement day, or a Saturday or after-hours call — and turns that into the extra-work share and the walk-away price below which the job loses money, before any of it touches a client or scheduling system. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.
