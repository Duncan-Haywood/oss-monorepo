How much to charge to screen a porch

A published “$55 a square foot” special prices the floor. A residential screened porch is not a floor. It is wall screen that does not scale with square feet, a knee wall that may be nothing or 36 inches of framed work, an existing roof or a new one, and often a ceiling and a second door. Matching the coupon on a 12×16 enclosed porch is how a sold job becomes a two-day loss after the first wall of panels.

The number a square-foot special cannot see

Take a 10×12 attached screen-in under an existing roof, no knee wall, fiberglass mesh, one door. That is 32 lineal feet of wall and 256 square feet of screen — on a 120 square-foot floor. The coupon says $6,600. Raise the same shop to a 12×16 enclosed porch. The floor is 192 square feet, so the coupon says $10,560. The screen did not scale that way: 40 lineal feet, a 36-inch knee wall, a new shed roof, and often beadboard on 192 square feet the 10×12 never had.

Put pet mesh or hurricane mesh on that 12×16. Screen now costs more per square foot the fiberglass 10×12 never priced. 10-foot walls are not “the same job plus two feet.” A second door is not in the square-foot number at all.

How to price it

  1. Measure width along the house and depth out from the wall.
  2. Count screenable wall: three sides if attached, four if it wraps. Wall-screen square feet is that perimeter times the screen height after the knee wall.
  3. Add panels at your widths, knee-wall lineal feet when the job is not full-height screen, and roof or ceiling on the whole floor when you are actually building them.
  4. Price mesh (fiberglass / pet / hurricane), doors, a ceiling fan, existing-screen demo, and the permit allowance.
  5. Load the wage, add materials and mobilization, mark overhead on direct cost, then divide by one minus the target margin. The walk-away price is total cost.

Common questions

How much to charge to screen a porch?

Start from the floor, count the wall-screen square feet a square-foot special cannot see, then add knee wall, roof, ceiling, mesh, extras, and the permit. A published $55 per square foot special only sees width times depth.

Why do panels and knee wall change the price?

A 10-by-12 screen-in under an existing roof is 32 lineal feet of wall and 256 square feet of screen on a 120 square-foot floor. A 12-by-16 enclosed porch adds a knee wall, a new roof, and often a ceiling. Matching the 10-by-12 coupon is how the enclosed job loses money.
