Common questions

Why does a flat per-serve fee not tell me whether a job is worth taking?

Because the flat fee is priced for the ordinary case -- a good address, one or two attempts, a short drive. A defendant who is hard to find adds attempts and time the base fee never covered, a bad address can mean skip tracing before you can even attempt service, and a long drive burns real fuel and vehicle wear whether or not the client's mileage add-on covers all of it. The same headline fee can be a great job or a losing one depending on those four things.

Does this tool tell me whether service was properly effected or how to serve someone who is avoiding service?

No. This calculator only prices your own job economics from numbers you supply. It does not determine whether service was validly effected under any state's rules of civil procedure, whether you are licensed, bonded, or registered to serve process in a given jurisdiction, or whether an affidavit of service will hold up in court -- verify all of that independently.

Why does vehicle cost count for every mile driven, but mileage revenue only for miles beyond what is included?

Because that is what actually happens on the job: fuel and wear cost you money for every mile you drive, but a client's mileage add-on typically only pays once you are past the miles their base fee already covers. Counting only the billed miles as a cost is the most common way a job's real profit gets overstated.

Does anything about my job or client fee schedules get uploaded?

No. Every field on this page runs in your browser only. Nothing is sent to a server, there is no account, and the client fee-schedule rows you type into the comparison table are never saved anywhere -- they exist only in the page while it is open. The job inputs above are different: those are saved locally on this device, so you don't retype them, and a shareable link can carry them to another device. Usage counting only tallies anonymous feature events, and you can turn it off with the button above.
