This calculator vs. ServeManager

Two different shapes: ServeManager is an account-based platform for running a process serving company's whole caseload, including tracking attempts, generating the affidavit, and invoicing the client once a job is done. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what does this specific serve actually net, once extra attempts, rush or skip-trace add-ons, mileage, and your own vehicle cost and time come out of the base fee — and which client's fee schedule really pays the best hourly rate.

What each tool is for

ServeManager is case management software used by process serving companies and agencies to run a caseload: tracking jobs and individual service attempts, generating the affidavit of service once a job is complete, and invoicing clients, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. ServeManager's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific feature list, plan names, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of case management software describe logging an attempt, producing a court-ready affidavit, and billing the client once a job is closed; none describe working out whether a flat per-serve fee still clears the server's own vehicle cost and time once a third attempt and a bad address needing skip tracing are netted out — which is what this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what ServeManager does. It does not track a caseload, log individual service attempts, generate an affidavit, or invoice a client. It takes a base fee, the attempts included versus the attempts it actually took, a rush or skip-trace add-on, mileage revenue against the server's own vehicle cost per mile, drive and paperwork time, and a per-job share of fixed costs, and turns that into net profit and an effective hourly rate for one job — plus a side-by-side ranking of several clients' fee schedules by what they actually pay per hour for the same job, not just their headline base fee. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Should I use this calculator or ServeManager?

They do different jobs, and a process server of any size will likely end up touching both eventually.
