The UK's e-invoicing mandate: April 2029, Peppol, no government portal

The UK has announced that B2B and B2G VAT invoices must be electronic from April 2029, sent between your own service providers over Peppol rather than through a central HMRC portal. That is years out, but UK suppliers to EU customers already meet Peppol and EN 16931 today. Below: the dates as sources give them, what is still open, and how to test a file now.

Which file counts, and how it travels

Sources describe EN 16931 as the base, Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 as the starting profile and a UK-specific variant as still in development. You would send through an accredited Peppol service provider (your accounting software, usually), which reaches your customer's provider directly. A PDF emailed to a customer would not meet the duty once it applies.

Common questions

When does the UK require e-invoices?

Secondary sources report that, after HMRC's 2025 consultation, the government confirmed that from April 2029 VAT invoices in business-to-business and business-to-government transactions must be electronic; one gives 1 April 2029. B2C is out of scope. The sources are vendor and advisory pages, and the detailed roadmap, including whether the rollout is phased, was still reported as pending, so confirm against gov.uk and HMRC. This is a summary, not advice.

Which format will the UK use?

Sources report Peppol as the core network (confirmed on 23 June 2026 by their account), a decentralised four-corner model with no central portal, and a standard built on EN 16931 and Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 with a UK-specific variant still in development. Until that variant is published, a Peppol BIS 3.0 invoice is the closest thing to test against. This checker reads JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X PDFs and has a Peppol BIS 3.0 option.

What does this checker not cover for the UK?

The UK-specific Peppol variant (not yet published as far as these sources say), a service provider's acceptance, HMRC rules on VAT invoice content, and Making Tax Digital submissions. A clean result means the invoice passes EN 16931 and the Peppol rules it knows, not that HMRC or your customer's provider will take it.

How do I check a UK invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker and tick the Peppol option. It tests the Peppol identifiers, electronic addresses, totals and tax breakdown arithmetic, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page. It is an offline observation, not a provider's acceptance and not legal advice.
