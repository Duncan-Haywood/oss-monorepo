Portugal's e-invoicing rules: what applies and what is only expected

Portugal has no B2B e-invoicing mandate with a confirmed start date. What does apply: structured e-invoices for public-sector customers, phased in; certified invoicing software; monthly invoice reporting to the tax authority; and, from 1 January 2027 per most sources, a qualified signature on PDF invoices. Below: the table, where sources disagree, and what a free local checker covers.

Which file would count

For public bodies the route is Peppol-style structured invoices based on EN 16931 (UBL or CII). For B2B there is no format rule yet, so an EN 16931 UBL or CII file is the safest bet. A Portuguese invoice also carries an ATCUD code and a QR code from certified software; this checker does not read them.

Common questions

Is B2B e-invoicing mandatory in Portugal?

Not by a firm date in the sources checked. Structured B2B e-invoicing is voluntary, and the buyer must agree to receive it. Portugal is expected to extend e-invoicing under the EU's ViDA package, but no B2B start date was confirmed. Separately, certified invoicing software and monthly invoice reporting to the tax authority (AT) already apply. Confirm with the AT or your accountant; this page is a summary, not advice.

Does Portugal require e-invoices for public-sector customers?

Yes, in phases. Large suppliers have had to send structured e-invoices to public bodies since 2021. Sources disagree on the date for smaller suppliers, giving 1 January 2026 or 1 January 2027, so check with the entity you invoice.

Do PDF invoices need a signature in Portugal?

Most sources say a PDF invoice needs a qualified electronic signature from 1 January 2027, and is valid without one until 31 December 2026. One source gives 1 January 2026. The 2027 date is better supported, but confirm it with the AT.

Can this checker validate a Portuguese invoice?

Partly. It reads UBL, CII, Factur-X and JSON and applies the EN 16931 arithmetic, tax breakdown, mandatory-field and identifier checks, plus VAT, IBAN and BIC typo catches, each with a rule ID and a fix. For UBL files that name CIUS-PT in BT-24 it also runs the Portuguese national profile's published Schematron rules (AMA, version 2.1.1: VAT identifiers, Deliver to address, number formats). It does not check certified-software requirements, ATCUD codes, qualified signatures or SAF-T files, so a clean result is an offline observation, not approval.
