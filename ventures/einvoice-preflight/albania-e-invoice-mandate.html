Albania's e-invoice rules: cleared by the tax authority since 2021

Albania has had a clearance system since 2021: invoices go through the tax authority, which checks each one and returns a unique number before it counts. Government buyers came first, then cashless business-to-business sales on 1 July 2021. Below: what vendor guides report, where they disagree, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (as vendor guides report it)

DateWhat changes
2019Law 87/2019 sets up the fiscalisation framework run by the General Directorate of Taxes (DPT).
1 Jan 2021E-invoicing for sales to government buyers (B2G).
1 Jul 2021Cashless business-to-business sales are cleared in real time. Penalties were waived at first: the general waiver is reported to have run to 1 January 2022, and one source says a limited set of taxpayers had until 30 June 2022.
1 Sep 2021Cash sales to consumers (B2C) join the system.
TodayIn force. No later deadline was found in the sources read.

Where sources disagree: how long invoices must be kept (5 years in some guides, 10 in others), and one aggregator lists the effective date and format as blank. Which file counts, and how it travels

Guides say Albania accepts UBL 2.1 or UN/CEFACT Cross Industry Invoice XML, signed with a digital certificate from AKSHI, the national agency for information society. The DPT is a clearance intermediary: it checks the invoice and issues a unique invoice number (NIVF) with a security code (NSLF), shown as a QR code on the PDF copy. This is not Peppol. A plain PDF without that number is not a cleared invoice.

What this checker does and does not cover

It applies EN 16931 arithmetic and tax-breakdown rules to UBL, CII and Factur-X files. It does not apply Albania's own schema or fiscal rules, cannot sign with an AKSHI certificate and does not contact the DPT, so it cannot issue an NIVF. Treat a clean result as “the numbers and structure are sound”, not “cleared in Albania”.

Common questions

Does Albania have an e-invoicing mandate?

Yes. Vendor and compliance guides say Albania has required e-invoicing under Law 87/2019 in phases: government buyers from 1 January 2021, cashless business-to-business sales from 1 July 2021 and cash sales to consumers from 1 September 2021. The General Directorate of Taxes (DPT) runs the system and clears each invoice. These are not the law or the DPT itself, so confirm there. This is a summary, not advice.

What format and numbers does Albania use?

Guides say UBL 2.1 or UN/CEFACT Cross Industry Invoice XML, signed with a certificate from the national agency AKSHI. The tax system checks each invoice and returns a unique invoice number (NIVF) and a security code (NSLF), which appear on the printed or PDF copy as a QR code. This is not Peppol and not the EU's EN 16931 model.

What does this checker cover for Albania?

EN 16931 arithmetic and tax-breakdown rules for UBL, CII and Factur-X files. It does not apply Albania's own schema or fiscal rules, cannot sign with an AKSHI certificate and does not talk to the DPT, so it cannot issue an NIVF. A clean result is not clearance.

How do I check an invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker. It tests totals, tax breakdown arithmetic, mandatory fields and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded. It is an offline observation, not the tax authority's acceptance and not legal advice.
