Mauritius's e-invoice rules: every invoice registered with the MRA, widening by turnover

Mauritius runs an invoice fiscalisation system: a business above the turnover threshold issues invoices from an approved e-invoicing system (EBS) that sends each one to the Mauritius Revenue Authority (MRA) platform, which records it and returns an invoice registration number and a QR code. The threshold has been lowered in waves; guides report MUR 40 million from 1 September 2026. Below: what guides report, where they disagree, and how to test a file first.

The timeline (as vendor guides report it)

15 May 2024First wave: businesses with turnover above MUR 100 million (MRA rollout table as cited by one aggregator).
2025Later waves reported, but sources disagree on the dates: one lists 1 August 2025 for a further group above MUR 100 million, another an expansion to MUR 80 million on 1 January 2025. A Thomson Reuters page reports the threshold falling from MUR 100 million to MUR 80 million.

Where sources disagree: the 2025 and 2026 wave dates and thresholds (see the table). One source reports that the Finance Act 2026 raised the maximum fine for not using the system to MUR 500,000 plus up to 24 months\' imprisonment; this is a single aggregator, so confirm it. Guides say non-VAT-registered businesses, makers of only exempt or zero-rated supplies and non-resident businesses not registered for VAT are out of scope. These are vendor and advisory pages, not the MRA or the Finance Acts, which could not be read from this build. Confirm with the MRA. A summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts, and how it travels

Guides describe a registration model: the business's approved e-invoicing system (EBS) sends every invoice, debit note and credit note to the MRA Invoice Fiscalisation Platform (IFP) before it is issued, and the IFP returns an Invoice Registration Number (IRN) and a QR code that go on the invoice. The invoice is not a plain PDF, and this is not Peppol or the EU EN 16931 model. The exact schema was not read from a primary source.

What this checker does and does not cover

It applies EN 16931 arithmetic and tax-breakdown rules to UBL, CII and Factur-X files. It does not apply Mauritius's own schema or fiscal rules and does not contact the MRA, so it cannot obtain an IRN. Treat a clean result as “the numbers and structure are sound”, not “registered in Mauritius”.

Common questions

Does Mauritius have an e-invoicing mandate?

Yes. Compliance guides say Mauritius has required VAT-registered businesses above a turnover threshold to use the MRA e-invoicing system since 15 May 2024, with the threshold lowered in waves and reported at MUR 40 million from 1 September 2026. Sources disagree on some earlier dates. These are not the MRA or the law, so confirm there. This is a summary, not advice.

What format and numbers does Mauritius use?

Guides say the approved e-invoicing system sends each invoice, debit note and credit note to the MRA platform, which returns an Invoice Registration Number (IRN) and a QR code. It is not Peppol and not the EU's EN 16931 model. The schema was not read from a primary source.

What does this checker cover for Mauritius?

EN 16931 arithmetic and tax-breakdown rules for UBL, CII and Factur-X files. It does not apply Mauritius's own schema or fiscal rules and does not talk to the MRA, so it cannot obtain an IRN. A clean result is not registration.

How do I check an invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker. It tests totals, tax breakdown arithmetic, mandatory fields and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded. It is an offline observation, not the tax authority's acceptance and not legal advice.
