Cyprus: public bodies must receive e-invoices, businesses are not yet required to send them

Cypriot public buyers have accepted structured e-invoices since 2019, but no domestic B2B mandate has been reported. The next firm date is the EU-wide cross-border step in 2030. Below: what guides report, what is not known, and what this checker can and cannot tell you.

The rules (as vendor guides report them)

WhoWhat is required
Central government bodiesMust be able to receive structured e-invoices since 18 April 2019 (national law of 26 June 2019 transposing Directive 2014/55/EU).
Sub-central public bodiesSame duty to receive since 18 April 2020.
SuppliersMay send e-invoices through the national platform or Peppol access points; reportedly not required to.
Domestic B2B and B2CNo mandate or announced timeline found.
Intra-EU B2BStructured e-invoicing and digital reporting reported to become mandatory EU-wide from 1 July 2030 (ViDA).

Which file counts

Guides point to the European standard EN 16931, delivered through the national platform or over Peppol. A plain PDF is not a structured e-invoice for a public buyer. Check the buyer's instructions for the syntax it takes.

Common questions

Is e-invoicing mandatory in Cyprus?

Per vendor and advisory guides: not for businesses. Central government bodies have had to be able to receive structured e-invoices since 18 April 2019 and sub-central bodies since 18 April 2020, under the national law transposing Directive 2014/55/EU. Suppliers may send them but are reportedly not required to, and no domestic B2B or B2C mandate was found. These are not the Tax Department (TAD) or the law, so confirm there. This is a summary, not advice.

What changes in 2030?

Under the EU's ViDA package, structured e-invoicing and near-real-time digital reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies are reported to become mandatory in every member state, Cyprus included, from 1 July 2030. Guides found no earlier Cyprus domestic date, and no announcement from the Tax Department. Dates in EU files can still move.

Which formats does Cyprus accept?

Guides describe the EN 16931 standard through the national platform or Peppol access points. The exact syntax list differs between sources, so confirm the one your public buyer takes.

What is the checker useful for in a Cyprus context?

Testing a UBL or CII file before you send it to a public body or an EU customer. It checks totals, tax breakdown arithmetic, identifiers, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos with a rule ID and a fix. It has no Cypriot national rules and cannot confirm acceptance by the national platform. It is an offline observation, not legal advice.
