Poland's KSeF e-invoice mandate: what applies now

Since 2026, Polish VAT-registered businesses issue domestic B2B invoices through KSeF, the national e-invoice system. Below: the dates, the format that counts, and an honest note on what a free local checker can and cannot test.

The timeline (domestic B2B)

DateWhat changes
1 Feb 2026KSeF is mandatory for taxpayers whose 2025 sales including VAT exceeded PLN 200 million.
1 Apr 2026KSeF is mandatory for all other VAT-registered businesses.
1 Jan 2027KSeF-specific financial penalties are scheduled to start; 2026 is a ramp-up year without them.

Special rules apply to the smallest sellers, to some transactions, and to offline and failure modes. This is a summary, not tax advice; confirm with your accountant or the Ministry of Finance.

Which file counts, and how it travels

The invoice is a structured XML document in the FA(3) schema, submitted to KSeF, which validates it and gives it a KSeF number. FA(3) is Poland's own schema, not UBL or CII, so it is a different job from the EN 16931 and Peppol checks this tool runs. A PDF emailed to the customer is no longer the invoice for domestic B2B sales.

Common questions

When did KSeF become mandatory in Poland?

Poland's National e-Invoice System (KSeF) became mandatory on 1 February 2026 for taxpayers whose 2025 sales including VAT exceeded PLN 200 million, and on 1 April 2026 for all other VAT-registered businesses. Special rules apply to the smallest sellers and to some transactions. Confirm your own situation with your accountant or the Polish Ministry of Finance; this page is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice format does KSeF require?

A structured XML invoice in the Polish FA(3) schema, submitted to KSeF, which assigns a KSeF number. FA(3) is Poland's own schema rather than UBL or CII, so this checker's EN 16931 rules do not validate it. A PDF or paper invoice is no longer the legal invoice for domestic B2B sales.

Are there penalties for not using KSeF?

The 2026 rollout runs without the new KSeF-specific financial penalties; they are scheduled to start on 1 January 2027. Confirm the current rules and amounts with the Ministry of Finance before relying on them.

Can I use this checker for Polish invoices?

Only partly. It cannot validate an FA(3) file. It is useful for cross-border sales, where a Polish company issues or receives an EN 16931 invoice (UBL, CII or Factur-X) outside KSeF, and for the VAT, IBAN and BIC typo checks on the data you key into your invoicing software. It is an offline observation, not KSeF's acceptance and not legal advice.
