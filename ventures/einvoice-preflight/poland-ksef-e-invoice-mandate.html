Poland's KSeF e-invoice mandate: what applies now

Poland's national e-invoicing system, KSeF, became mandatory in 2026 in two waves, with the smallest businesses due in 2027. Below: the dates, the penalty position, the format that counts, and which checks on an invoice still help before you submit.

The timeline (domestic B2B)

DateWhat changes
1 Feb 2026Taxpayers with turnover above PLN 200 million must issue B2B invoices through KSeF. All VAT taxpayers can receive them there.
1 Apr 2026Most other VAT-registered businesses must issue through KSeF. The smallest businesses are not yet included.
1 Jan 2027The smallest businesses (monthly invoiced sales up to PLN 10,000, single invoices up to PLN 450) join. Dedicated KSeF penalties were originally due to start on this date.
1 Jan 2028 (proposed)The Ministry of Finance announced in September 2026 that penalties for KSeF errors would be deferred to this date. Advisers report the Council of Ministers adopted the bill on 20 October 2026 (print 3245) and the Sejm sent it on to further work after a first reading on 21 October (search excerpts, 8 Nov 2026; the Sejm text unread); it still needs the Sejm, the Senate and the President's signature, so it is not yet law; the duty to use KSeF is not deferred.

Reported penalties reach 100% of the VAT on the invoice, or 18.7% of the amount due when there is no VAT. Sources for these dates are advisory firms and vendor notes, not the Ministry's own texts, and the rules have moved more than once: confirm with the Ministry of Finance or your accountant. This is a summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts

KSeF accepts structured XML in Poland's own FA(3) schema, submitted to the central system, which returns a KSeF reference number. A PDF, or an EN 16931 UBL or CII file, is not the invoice there unless it is converted to FA(3) and submitted. Your accounting software usually does that conversion, which is why a defect in the content (a wrong total, a missing tax reason, a mistyped IBAN) shows up late, after submission.

What this checker does: drop an FA(3) XML file in and it runs the published FA(3) XSD (element order, patterns, code lists; PL-012 to PL-017), then the checks the Ministry of Finance lists for files KSeF receives (PL-018 to PL-022): UTF-8 without a byte-order mark and no other encoding in the prolog, no processing instructions, none of the characters KSeF refuses (a hidden control character from double-encoded Polish text is the usual one, and the fix says so), at most 1,000,000 bytes, or 3,000,000 with an attachment (which only a batch session takes), an issue date not after today in Poland, check digits on every NIP including third parties (Podmiot3) and the authorised party, the check digit of a Podmiot3 internal id, and two files in one batch with the same seller, invoice kind and number (KSeF's error 440). Then NIP check digits, the tax-rate codes, line amounts, the P_13/P_14 totals per rate, P_15 and the PLN tax on foreign-currency invoices, on every invoice kind: a correction (KOR) is checked as after-rows minus before-rows (StanPrzed), and an advance (ZAL), settlement (ROZ) or their corrections as the payment split across the order's rates by each rate's share of the gross, tax = share × rate / (100 + rate) (PL-023), with the order's value and lines (PL-024, PL-025). On the Ministry's 26 published FA(3) examples it raises no fault and catches 164 of 166 planted total errors. What it does not do: talk to KSeF, check your permissions or session, or know which numbers KSeF already holds. JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X documents are checked against EN 16931 and Peppol BIS 3.0 rules, the right test for invoices you send to buyers in other EU countries.

Common questions

When did Poland's KSeF e-invoicing mandate start?

On 1 February 2026 large taxpayers (turnover above PLN 200 million) had to issue B2B invoices through KSeF; most other VAT-registered businesses followed on 1 April 2026. The smallest businesses (monthly invoiced sales up to PLN 10,000 and single invoices up to PLN 450) are due from 1 January 2027. Receiving through KSeF applied to everyone from February 2026. Exceptions exist; confirm your own date with the Ministry of Finance or your accountant. This page is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice format does KSeF use?

KSeF takes structured XML in Poland's own FA(3) schema, not UBL or CII directly. A PDF or an EN 16931 UBL file is not an invoice in KSeF unless it is converted to FA(3) and submitted. This checker reads FA(3) files against the published FA(3) XSD, the checks KSeF runs on receipt (UTF-8 without BOM, no processing instructions or refused characters, the 1 MB and 3 MB size caps, an issue date not in the future, every party's NIP and internal-id check digits, a repeated invoice number in one batch) and the arithmetic, with its own PL-nnn rule ids; it checks JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X documents against EN 16931 and Peppol BIS 3.0 rules.

What are the penalties for KSeF errors in Poland?

Reported figures are up to 100% of the VAT shown on the invoice, or up to 18.7% of the amount due where there is no VAT. The Ministry of Finance announced in September 2026 that the start of these penalties would move from 1 January 2027 to 1 January 2028, but advisers report that a bill doing so was adopted by the Council of Ministers on 20 October 2026 (print 3245), had its first Sejm reading on 21 October 2026 and was sent on to further work, and has not yet passed the Sejm and Senate or been signed, so it is not yet law. The duty to use KSeF itself is not deferred. Confirm the current status before relying on it.

What can a free EN 16931 checker do for a Polish business?

It cannot submit to KSeF, but it reads an FA(3) file against the FA(3) schema and the checks KSeF runs on receipt (encoding, refused characters, size, dates, NIP check digits, duplicate numbers in a batch), plus rate codes and arithmetic. It can also check the invoice content that both formats share before you key it into your KSeF software or send it across a border: totals and tax breakdown arithmetic, a missing tax reason on a 0% line, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix.
