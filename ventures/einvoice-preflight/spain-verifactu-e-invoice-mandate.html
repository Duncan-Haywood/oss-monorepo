Spain's Verifactu deadline: what applies and when

Spain regulates invoicing software with Verifactu: tamper-evident invoice records, and for many businesses reporting to the Tax Agency. The start was delayed to 2027. Below: the dates as reported, what it is not, and what a free local checker covers.

The timeline (as reported)

DateWhat changes
1 Jan 2027Corporate taxpayers outside the SII immediate-supply reporting regime must use compliant invoicing software.
1 Jul 2027Other businesses and self-employed people who use invoicing software follow.

Exemptions exist (for example taxpayers under TicketBAI in the Basque Country and Navarra). The ministerial order with the technical rules must be issued first, so the dates are working targets. This is a summary from secondary sources, not tax advice; confirm with your adviser or the AEAT.

What it covers, and what it does not

Verifactu is about the software: invoice records that cannot be altered unnoticed, a QR code on the invoice and, in one mode, submission of each record to the AEAT. It is not a Peppol mandate and not the separate Spanish B2B e-invoicing duty, whose timeline depends on regulations this page does not state.

Common questions

What is Spain's Verifactu deadline?

Verifactu is Spain's anti-fraud rule for invoicing software: programs must produce tamper-evident invoice records, and many send them to the Tax Agency (AEAT). After a delay approved in December 2025 (Royal Decree-Law 15/2025), the reported dates are 1 January 2027 for corporate taxpayers outside the SII regime and 1 July 2027 for other businesses and self-employed people who use invoicing software. The technical order that fixes the rules must be issued first, so confirm the dates with your adviser or the AEAT.

Is Verifactu the same as e-invoicing?

No. Verifactu governs how invoicing software records and, optionally, reports invoices to the tax authority. Spain's separate B2B e-invoicing duty (the Crea y Crece law) depends on regulations and has its own timeline, which this page does not state. Neither is a Peppol mandate like Belgium's.

Can this checker validate Verifactu records?

No. It does not read Verifactu records or QR codes. It reads JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X invoices and applies the EN 16931 and Peppol BIS 3.0 rules, which helps with the invoice content itself: totals, tax breakdown, VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix.
