Thailand: e-Tax Invoice

Thailand's electronic tax invoice is optional: the Revenue Department's own notification gives every VAT-registered business the choice of electronic or paper. Below: what that notification says (read 14 November 2026), what is only reported, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

What the Revenue Department's texts say

TopicWhat the texts say
StatusRevenue Department Notification on making, sending or keeping tax invoices or receipts signed with an electronic certificate, dated 7 June 2023 (Royal Gazette vol. 140, special part 167 Ng, 12 July 2023), under Ministerial Regulation No. 384 (B.E. 2565) after Revenue Code Amendment Act No. 53 (B.E. 2564). Clause 10: a VAT-registered business or receipt issuer has the right to choose to make the tax invoice or receipt as electronic data or on paper.
Signing upClause 2: the business applies on form BO.01 through the Revenue Department's system and proves its identity; once the Director-General lists it, it may issue electronically. Director-General Notification No. 15 of 2019, the older basis, lapsed when Amendment Act No. 53 took effect.
Full routeClauses 4 and 11: the invoice holds the particulars of Revenue Code sections 86/4, 86/6, 86/9, 86/10 and 105 bis in the format published on the Revenue Department website, signed with an approved electronic certificate; the data goes to the Revenue Department by the 15th of the following month, directly or through an appointed service provider.
Time Stamp routeFormerly e-Tax Invoice by Email: reported for businesses with annual revenue up to THB 30 million, a PDF timestamped through ETDA. Its notice was not read here.
Reliefs tied to itDirector-General Notification on income tax No. 470 (Royal Gazette, 28 October 2026, read): an individual's income tax exemption of up to THB 200,000 for rooftop solar equipment and installation (contracts from 3 March 2026 to 31 December 2028, Royal Decree No. 805) counts only on an e-Tax Invoice naming the buyer's tax ID. Guides also report a 200 percent deduction on e-Tax Invoice investment and a 1 percent e-withholding rate, reportedly extended to the end of 2027 (not read).
FormatThe data format is the one published on the Revenue Department website (clause 4), not UBL or CII; the standard itself was not read here.

Read on 14 November 2026 from rd.go.th: the Revenue Department Notification of 7 June 2023 and Director-General Notification on income tax No. 470 (sources: eval/mandate-primary-sources-pk-ng-jo-th-2026-11-14.md in the repository). The Time Stamp route's terms and the incentive extension are from secondary guides, not from Revenue Department notices read here.

Common questions

Is e-invoicing mandatory in Thailand?

No. Clause 10 of the Revenue Department Notification of 7 June 2023 (Royal Gazette, 12 July 2023) gives a VAT-registered business or receipt issuer the right to choose electronic or paper tax invoices and receipts. A business that chooses electronic must first be listed by the Director-General after applying on form BO.01.

What are the two e-Tax Invoice routes?

A full route, where the invoice is signed with an electronic certificate and the data reaches the Revenue Department by the 15th of the following month (Notification of 7 June 2023, clauses 4 and 11), and a time-stamp route (formerly e-Tax Invoice by Email) reported for businesses with annual revenue up to THB 30 million, where a PDF is timestamped. The time-stamp notice was not read here.

Why would a business use it if it is voluntary?

Some tax relief is tied to it. Director-General Notification No. 470 (Royal Gazette, 28 October 2026) allows an individual's income tax exemption of up to THB 200,000 for rooftop solar only on an e-Tax Invoice naming the buyer's tax ID. Guides also report a 200 percent deduction on e-Tax Invoice investment, reportedly extended to the end of 2027; check the current terms.

Can this checker validate a Thai e-Tax Invoice?

No. It has no Thai rules, does not sign or timestamp and does not submit anything. For a UBL or CII file it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not a Revenue Department acceptance.
