Thailand: e-Tax Invoice

Thailand's e-invoicing is, in every source read, still voluntary. The Revenue Department offers two routes to a legally valid electronic tax invoice. Below: what secondary guides say about each route, where they differ, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
StatusSources read agree there is no general B2B e-invoicing mandate for 2026 or 2027; one notes talk of a future mandate with no date. Paper tax invoices remain lawful alongside electronic ones.
Full routeThe seller signs the invoice with a digital certificate and submits XML data to the Revenue Department. One source describes monthly submission by the 15th of the following month; another gives monthly submission without that date.
By Email routeAimed at small businesses with annual revenue up to THB 30 million. The invoice can be Word, Excel or PDF and is timestamped through email. A Revenue Department regional page from March 2026 is reported as saying the emailed invoice must carry an ETDA time stamp to count as complete.
IncentivesReported: a 200 percent deduction on eligible e-Tax Invoice investment and a reduced 1 percent e-withholding rate. Cabinet is reported to have approved extending these to the end of 2027 in June 2026, with formal rules pending.
FormatRevenue Department data standards for the full route, not UBL or CII. Not confirmed from a primary source here.

Written from web search summaries and adviser guides (vendor and consultancy write-ups), not from Revenue Department notices or the e-Tax Invoice portal, none of which were read for this page. Incentive dates and thresholds may have changed.

Common questions

Is e-invoicing mandatory in Thailand?

Not according to the sources read: they describe the e-Tax Invoice system as voluntary, with no legislated B2B mandate for 2026 or 2027, and mention a possible future mandate without a date. Paper invoices stay valid. Confirm with the Revenue Department or an adviser, because this page was not written from their notices.

What are the two e-Tax Invoice routes?

A full route, where the invoice is digitally signed and XML data goes to the Revenue Department, and an e-Tax Invoice by Email route for small businesses (annual revenue up to THB 30 million, as reported) where the invoice can be a PDF, Word or Excel file timestamped through email.

Why would a business use it if it is voluntary?

Secondary guides report tax incentives: a 200 percent deduction on eligible investment and a reduced 1 percent withholding rate, reportedly extended by Cabinet to the end of 2027 with formal rules still pending. Check the current terms before relying on them.

Can this checker validate a Thai e-Tax Invoice?

No. It has no Thai rules, does not sign or timestamp and does not submit anything. For a UBL or CII file it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not a Revenue Department acceptance.
