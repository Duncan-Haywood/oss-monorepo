New Zealand's e-invoice rules: voluntary for B2B, Peppol for government

New Zealand has no business-to-business e-invoicing mandate that the sources found put a date on. Central government agencies have had to be able to receive Peppol e-invoices since 2022, and from 1 January 2027 agencies are reported to be required to make large suppliers send them on applicable contracts. Below: what is reported, where sources disagree, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (as vendor guides and compliance sites report it)

DateWhat changes
2019Voluntary Peppol e-invoicing opens to New Zealand businesses; guides give different start days.
2022Central public agencies must be able to receive Peppol e-invoices.
Nov 2024 to May 2025PINT A-NZ becomes the specification to send; one guide gives 15 November 2024, another 15 May 2025, and the older A-NZ Peppol BIS 3.0 extensions are reported to be phased out afterwards. The sources do not agree on which date applies to which side.
TodayB2B e-invoicing is voluntary. No general B2B mandate was found.
1 Jan 2027Government agencies reported to be required to ensure large suppliers send e-invoices on applicable government contracts. One source says MBIE advises this is not a hard cut-off, because agencies apply it through new, renewed or retendered contracts; one source puts the large-supplier line at revenue above NZD 33 million, which others do not repeat.

Where sources disagree: the PINT A-NZ send date, the size of a “large” supplier, and how firm the 2027 date is. These are vendor and advisory pages, not the New Zealand law, MBIE’s eInvoicing pages or the Government Procurement Rules, which were not readable from this build. No penalty is stated; one source says extended non-compliance affects future contracts. Confirm against MBIE. A summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts, and how it travels

New Zealand uses the Peppol network with PINT A-NZ, the Australia and New Zealand variant of Peppol’s international billing specification, built on EN 16931 concepts. MBIE is reported to be the New Zealand Peppol Authority. Invoices go between access points and are not copied to Inland Revenue; GST is still reported through returns. A plain PDF is not a structured e-invoice.

Common questions

Does New Zealand have a B2B e-invoicing mandate?

No general B2B mandate was found in 2025 to 2026 vendor and advisory guides: business-to-business e-invoicing is voluntary. The dated duty is on government buying: central agencies have been required to receive Peppol e-invoices since 2022, and from 1 January 2027 agencies are reported to be required to make large suppliers send e-invoices on applicable contracts. These are not the law or the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), so confirm there. This is a summary, not advice.

What format does New Zealand use?

Vendor guides say Peppol, in the Australia and New Zealand variant of the billing specification, PINT A-NZ, which is built on the European EN 16931 model. MBIE is reported to act as the New Zealand Peppol Authority. Invoices travel between access points and are not sent to Inland Revenue, which still receives GST through returns.

What does this checker cover for New Zealand?

EN 16931 and the Peppol BIS 3.0 rules it knows, for JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X PDFs. It does not apply the PINT A-NZ rule set, has no New Zealand national rules, and no operator or Peppol network lookup. A clean result means the invoice passes EN 16931 and the Peppol rules it knows, not that a New Zealand buyer will accept it.

How do I check an invoice before sending it to a New Zealand agency?

Drop the file into the checker and tick the Peppol option. It tests the Peppol identifiers, electronic addresses, totals and tax breakdown arithmetic, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page.
