Uganda: EFRIS

Uganda issues e-invoices and e-receipts through the Uganda Revenue Authority's EFRIS, using URA-accredited software. Guides report a 2026 extension to twelve more sectors. Below: what they say and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelE-invoices and e-receipts are issued through EFRIS using URA-accredited software or the URA's own tools. Not EN 16931.
WhoAll VAT-registered taxpayers, under Gazette Notice 595 of 2020 (published 23 June 2020, reported).
Start dateSources disagree: from 1 July 2020 to 1 January 2021.
2026 extensionA public notice dated August 2026 reportedly extends EFRIS to twelve sectors (manufacturers, miners, real estate, hotels, fuel stations, construction and others), described as in force from 1 July. One source calls it backdated to July 2025. Unverified.
PenaltiesSources say non-compliance is penalised but give little detail; none is quoted here.

What this checker does and does not cover

The main checker reads UBL, CII and Factur-X and applies EN 16931 and Peppol rules. It does not apply local rules, sign an invoice or call EFRIS. It is still useful on the numbers: wrong totals, a tax breakdown that does not add up and mistyped identifiers are faults any clearance system rejects.

Common questions

What is EFRIS?

The Uganda Revenue Authority's Electronic Fiscal Receipting and Invoicing Solution, where Ugandan e-invoices and e-receipts are issued.

Who has to use it?

VAT-registered taxpayers, under Gazette Notice 595 of 2020 as reported, and from 2026 reportedly twelve further sectors. Confirm scope with the URA.

When did it start?

Guides disagree, from 1 July 2020 to 1 January 2021. This page does not pick one.

Can this checker validate an EFRIS invoice?

No. It has no Ugandan rules and does not call EFRIS. For UBL or CII it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not EFRIS acceptance.
