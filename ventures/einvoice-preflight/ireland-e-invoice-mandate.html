Ireland's e-invoice mandate: what is planned

Ireland plans mandatory structured e-invoicing from November 2028. Below: the three phases, the format and network reported, what is still unconfirmed, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (as vendor guides describe it)

DateWhat changes
Nov 2028Large corporates must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B sales. Every Irish VAT-registered business must be able to receive them.
Nov 2029Issuing extends to all VAT-registered businesses trading within the EU.
Jul 2030Alignment with the EU's ViDA digital reporting requirements for cross-border B2B.

Unconfirmed: these dates come from vendor guides, not from Revenue's own pages, which this page did not read. The final syntax, transmission method and data specification were reported as still to be published, and no penalty amounts are stated here because none were confirmed. This is a summary, not tax advice.

Which file will count, and how it travels

Structured invoices must comply with EN 16931, so a PDF alone will not qualify, and Peppol is reported as the network. Until Revenue publishes the Irish specification, the safest test is an EN 16931 UBL or CII file that also passes Peppol BIS Billing 3.0.

Common questions

When does Ireland's B2B e-invoicing mandate start?

Vendor guides describe three phases: November 2028 (large corporates must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B sales, and every Irish VAT-registered business must be able to receive them), November 2029 (all VAT-registered businesses trading within the EU must issue) and July 2030 (full alignment with the EU's ViDA digital reporting rules). These dates come from vendor guides, not from Revenue's own pages, and technical details are still being finalised. Confirm with Revenue or your accountant.

Which format and network will Ireland use?

Structured invoices must follow EN 16931, so a PDF alone does not qualify, and Peppol is reported as the transmission network. The final syntax and technical specification had not been published in the sources read. This checker tests EN 16931 and Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 files, which is the likely starting point, not a confirmed Irish profile.

What are the penalties for not e-invoicing in Ireland?

None are confirmed in the sources read, so this page states no amount. Check Revenue's guidance once the legislation is published.

Pick a Peppol access point, make sure your accounting software can issue EN 16931 UBL or CII, and test sample invoices.
