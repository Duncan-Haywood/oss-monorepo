Mauritania: STT invoice declaration for public suppliers

Mauritania's tax administration (DGI) runs the STT teledeclaration platform, and a Ministry of Finance circular reported for 1 November 2026 extends it to public entities and their suppliers. Below: what guides report, where they differ, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelSuppliers declare invoices on the DGI's STT platform and the public-entity client validates them there. This is a declaration and payment-control step for state buyers, not a general private-sector clearance system, and not EN 16931.
Source and dateGuides report a Ministry of Finance circular signed 29 September 2026 (one gives the number 071-26) with effect from 1 November 2026. The circular itself was not read.
Effect on suppliersOne account says a public entity may not put an invoice forward for payment unless the supplier has declared it and the public-entity client has validated it on STT. Invoices between private businesses are reported as outside the procedure.
Open pointSources differ on whether public entities must also declare on STT the invoices they issue to their own customers; one reads the circular as covering public entities as buyers only.

Written from web search summaries (LookupTax, e-invoice.app, VAT Calc and news items), not from the circular or the DGI portal, neither of which was read. The circular number, date and scope may be wrong or superseded.

What this checker does and does not cover

Common questions

What is STT in Mauritania?

The tax administration's teledeclaration and telepayment platform, which a reported circular extends to invoices between public entities and their suppliers.

When does the supplier declaration start?

Guides report 1 November 2026, from a circular dated 29 September 2026. The circular was not read, so confirm the date and scope with the DGI.

Does it apply to invoices between private companies?

Reports say no: the procedure covers invoices submitted to public entities for payment.

Can this checker validate a Mauritanian invoice?

No. It has no Mauritanian rules and does not connect to STT. For UBL or CII it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not DGI acceptance.
