Costa Rica: Hacienda electronic vouchers

Costa Rica's tax authority already requires electronic vouchers as signed XML. The change that matters now is version 4.4. Below: what vendor guides report, where they differ, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelElectronic vouchers (comprobantes electrónicos) as digitally signed XML, validated by Hacienda, as reported.
Version 4.4Guides agree that version 4.4 became mandatory on 1 September 2025 after an extension (Resolution MH-DGT-RES-0001-2025 cited), implemented under MH-DGT-RES-0027-2024. One source says over 450,000 taxpayers had to migrate.
Disputed dateOne guide gives 6 October 2025 as mandatory for 4.4 vouchers to use the CIIU 4 activity classification. The sources do not say whether this is a separate date or an error.
ValidationOne vendor says validation is stricter, with errors that passed before now rejected immediately.
FormatHacienda XML schemas and annexes (v4.4), not EN 16931.

What this checker does and does not cover

The main checker reads UBL, CII and Factur-X and applies EN 16931 and Peppol rules. It does not apply local rules, sign an invoice or call JoFotara. Common questions

What changed with version 4.4 in Costa Rica?

Guides report new annexes and codes, stricter validation and the CIIU 4 activity classification on vouchers.

When did version 4.4 become mandatory?

Guides report 1 September 2025. One gives 6 October 2025 for the CIIU 4 requirement; the sources do not reconcile the two.

Is the Costa Rican voucher EN 16931 UBL?

No. It is Hacienda-specified signed XML.

Can this checker validate a Costa Rican voucher?

No. It has no Costa Rican rules and does not call Hacienda. For UBL or CII it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not Hacienda acceptance.
