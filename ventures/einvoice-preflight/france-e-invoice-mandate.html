France's e-invoice mandate: what applies when

France's reform started on 1 September 2026: every business must be able to receive e-invoices, and the largest must issue them. Smaller businesses issue from 1 September 2027. Below: the dates, the formats that count, the mentions a French invoice must carry, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (domestic B2B)

DateWhat changes
1 Sep 2026Every business must be able to receive e-invoices through a certified platform. Large companies and mid-size companies (ETI) must issue them and report other transactions (e-reporting).
1 Sep 2027Small and medium businesses and micro-businesses must issue e-invoices and report their transactions.

Company size is set by French decree, not by this page, and exceptions exist. This is a summary, not tax advice; confirm with your accountant or the DGFiP.

Which files count, and how they travel

Factur-X (a PDF that carries XML inside it), UBL and CII are the accepted formats, on top of the European standard EN 16931. Invoices go through a certified platform (plateforme agréée) that each business chooses; there is no direct send. A rejection comes back after you have submitted.

French invoices also carry mentions that are not in EN 16931: the seller's SIREN or SIRET, the buyer's SIREN for routing, late-payment penalties, the 40 euro recovery indemnity and the early-payment discount. The reform adds more (such as the nature of the operation and the delivery address when it differs); check the current list with your platform.

Common questions

When does the French e-invoicing reform start?

On 1 September 2026 every business established in France must be able to receive e-invoices through a certified platform (plateforme agréée). On the same date large companies and mid-size companies (ETI) must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B sales and report their other transactions (e-reporting). Small and medium businesses and micro-businesses must issue from 1 September 2027. Confirm your own category and dates with your accountant or the tax administration (DGFiP); this page is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice formats does France accept?

Three: Factur-X (a PDF/A-3 with embedded CII XML, the same format Germany calls ZUGFeRD), UBL and UN/CEFACT CII. Each must carry the data the French reform requires on top of the European standard EN 16931. A plain PDF with no embedded XML is not an e-invoice. This checker reads JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X PDFs.

Can I send e-invoices straight to my customer in France?

No. Invoices travel through a certified platform (plateforme agréée, formerly called PDP) chosen by each business; the public portal is reduced to a directory and a data concentrator. So a file that a platform rejects never reaches the customer, which is why testing the file before submitting it saves a round trip.

How do I check a French invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker and tick the French option. It tests the seller's SIREN or SIRET check digit, whether the SIREN inside the French VAT number matches, that the French buyer has a SIREN or SIRET for routing, and the three mentions the Code de commerce requires on a B2B invoice: late-payment penalties, the fixed 40 euro recovery indemnity and the early-payment discount.
