Singapore's GST InvoiceNow requirement: what applies now

Singapore is moving GST-registered businesses onto InvoiceNow, the national Peppol-based e-invoicing network, which also passes invoice data to the tax authority (IRAS). Below: the phase dates as reported, what we could not confirm, and how to test an invoice file before it goes out.

The timeline

DateWho is covered
1 Nov 2025Newly incorporated companies that register for GST voluntarily (incorporated within six months of applying).
1 Apr 2026All new voluntary GST registrants, whatever their incorporation date or structure.
1 Apr 2028Existing GST-registered businesses with annual supplies up to S$200,000 (reported; also compulsory registrants from this date).
1 Apr 2029Annual supplies up to S$1,000,000 (reported).
1 Apr 2030Annual supplies up to S$4,000,000 (reported).
1 Apr 2031Annual supplies above S$4,000,000 (reported).

The 2025 and 2026 dates come from IRAS announcements as summarised by law firms; the 2028 to 2031 steps come from secondary guides and were not read on the IRAS page itself, so treat them as reported, not confirmed. Reverse-charge and overseas-vendor-registration businesses are reported as excluded. A penalty for non-compliance was not found. Confirm with IRAS or your accountant.

How it travels

The invoice is sent through a Peppol access point on the InvoiceNow network, and the access point also transmits the invoice data to IRAS for GST administration. Singapore uses its own Peppol International (PINT) profile on top of the European standard EN 16931 model. Newly incorporated businesses were offered a year of free InvoiceNow services through the InvoiceNow Accelerate Programme; check the current terms.

Common questions

When does Singapore's GST InvoiceNow requirement start?

It started on 1 November 2025 for newly incorporated companies that register for GST voluntarily, and from 1 April 2026 it covers all new voluntary GST registrants. Existing GST-registered businesses are reported to be phased in by annual supplies from 1 April 2028 to 1 April 2031. Confirm your own date on the IRAS GST InvoiceNow page; this page is a summary, not advice.

Which format does InvoiceNow use?

InvoiceNow is Singapore's national e-invoicing network, built on the Peppol network. Invoice data travels through a Peppol access point, which also transmits the invoice data to IRAS. Singapore uses its own Peppol International (PINT) profile; when an invoice's specification identifier (BT-24) names the Singapore profile, this checker runs OpenPeppol's Singapore PINT rules, including the GST rules, in your browser.

What are the penalties for not using InvoiceNow?

We could not confirm a specific penalty for non-compliance with the GST InvoiceNow requirement; one commentator reports none has been published yet. Late GST registration has its own penalties, which are a different matter. Check the IRAS page before relying on any figure.

What can this checker do for a Singapore invoice?

Drop the file into the checker: it tests the totals and tax breakdown arithmetic against the EN 16931 core rules, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. For a UBL invoice that names the Singapore profile it also runs the PINT-SG rules and GST category codes; it is still not an access point's acceptance. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page.
