Estonia's e-invoice rules: public buyers now, buyer's choice since 2025, a full mandate discussed for 2027

Estonia has no single portal and, so far, no general B2B mandate. Public buyers have taken e-invoices since 2019, and since 1 July 2025 a business registered as an e-invoice recipient can require its suppliers to send them. Below: the dates as sources give them, where they disagree, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (as guides and the EU country sheet report it)

DateWhat changes
1 Jul 2019B2G: e-invoicing for public-sector buyers, who receive invoices through private service providers.
Sep 2024The Accounting Act amendments (Bill 428 SE) pass; they were published as RT I, 10.10.2024, 1 and took effect on 1 July 2025, creating the buyer's right below.
1 Jul 2025Buyer's choice: a business registered as an e-invoice recipient (the commercial register, äriregister: Accounting Act § 7¹ (3), read in Riigi Teataja on 7 Nov 2026) can demand structured e-invoices from its suppliers. No registration, no obligation.
2027 (discussed)A universal B2B mandate is described by guides as planned or tentative. The Accounting Act's current text (Riigi Teataja, read 7 Nov 2026) has no such date, so it is a policy target, not law. Not confirmed here.

Where sources disagree: whether a full B2B mandate is coming and when, and whether the 2019 duty fell on suppliers or was replaced by the 2025 right (public buyers are all registered recipients, so in practice they are unchanged). Read in the Accounting Act (7 Nov 2026): § 7¹ (7) lets a registered recipient demand an e-invoice from the seller, treats an invoice as properly made if it meets EN 16931-1 and lets the parties agree another standard; § 7¹ (8) lifts the rule for security agencies, state secrets and professional-secrecy data; the section sets no penalty for ignoring a request. The 2019 and 2027 points above still come from vendor, advisory and European Commission pages. Confirm against the Accounting Act and the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (emta.ee). A summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts, and how it travels

Guides describe EN 16931 as the reference, Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 as implemented, and the national XML e-invoice standard as still usable by agreement. Estonia follows a decentralised model: you send through a service provider, and providers reach each other by roaming agreements or Peppol. A PDF emailed to a registered recipient who has asked for e-invoices would not meet the request.

Common questions

When does Estonia require e-invoices?

Public-sector buyers have been served by e-invoices since 1 July 2019. Since 1 July 2025, under the Accounting Act amendments passed in September 2024, any business registered as an e-invoice recipient (the commercial register, äriregister: Accounting Act § 7¹ (3), read in Riigi Teataja on 7 Nov 2026) can require its suppliers to send structured e-invoices; with no registration there is no obligation. The Act's current text names no 2027 date for a universal B2B mandate (the only 2027 date in it concerns large companies' reporting periods), so a 2027 mandate is a discussion, not a statutory deadline. The Act was read in Estonian; dates elsewhere on this page still come from vendor and advisory pages and the European Commission country sheet, so confirm against the Accounting Act and the Tax and Customs Board. This is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice format does Estonia use?

Guides describe EN 16931 as the reference, with Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 implemented and a national XML e-invoice standard still allowed by agreement. Estonia has no central portal: invoices travel through private service providers that interoperate by roaming agreements and Peppol. This checker reads JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X PDFs and has a Peppol BIS 3.0 option. It does not apply the Estonian national standard.

What does this checker not cover for Estonia?

The Estonian national e-invoice XML standard, a service provider's acceptance, the recipient register lookup, and the monthly VAT return and transaction reporting. A clean result means the invoice passes EN 16931 and the Peppol rules it knows, not that your customer's provider will take it.

Drop the file into the checker and tick the Peppol option.
