Luxembourg's e-invoice mandate: what a draft says

Luxembourg's government has approved a draft bill that would require structured e-invoices between domestic businesses from 2028. It is not law yet. Below: what the sources agree on, what is open, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (as secondary sources describe the draft)

DateWhat changes
17 Jul 2026The Government Council approves the draft bill. It still has to complete the legislative process.
30 Jul 2026Draft law No. 8815 is submitted to the Chamber of Deputies (amending the 2019 e-invoicing law and the VAT law). It may change before it is enacted.
14 Nov 2026Still in the Finance Committee: the Chamber's dossier (read 2026-11-14, last updated 23 October 2026) shows a rapporteur named on 18 September, the bill returned to committee on 8 October, and two opinions filed (the Bar Council and the Chamber of Commerce). No vote yet, and no Council of State opinion is listed.
1 Jan 2028All businesses must be able to receive e-invoices. Alternative technical solutions are reported to be allowed until 30 Jun 2028 (31 Dec 2028 for small businesses).
1 Jul 2028Businesses over at least two of the draft's thresholds (balance sheet, turnover, average full-time staff, on their 2026 figures) must issue e-invoices.
1 Jan 2029All remaining in-scope businesses, including any that cannot supply the figures to place them in the first phase, must issue e-invoices.

Read from the bill on 2026-11-02: the draft text (filed 30 Jul 2026) matches the dates above. The duty to receive and process e-invoices starts on entry into force, 1 Jan 2028; issuing starts 1 Jul 2028 for businesses over two of three 2026 thresholds (balance sheet 7.5 million euros, net turnover 15 million euros, 50 average full-time staff) and 1 Jan 2029 for the rest or where the figures cannot be supplied. Only the e-invoice counts as the invoice (attachments are not invoices), a credit note or correction must use the same format as the original, and an e-invoice needs no recipient acceptance. Issuers must also be able to receive return messages over the common delivery network. The Luxembourg Bar Association's opinion of 30 Sep 2026 asks for an exemption where an e-invoice would expose professional-secrecy information; no amendment was seen. The bill's preamble also looks ahead to EU transaction reporting from 1 Jul 2030, which is a separate measure. Status before that reading: a search on 2026-10-30 found that the Chamber's Finance Committee heard the bill on 18 September 2026 and appointed a rapporteur, with the dates unchanged, and a tracker still listing it as deposited on 30 September with no Council of State opinion filed (secondary reports, nothing later found; the Chamber's site was unreachable then).

Unconfirmed: these dates come from advisory and vendor sources (VATcalc, Sovos, Banqup, Comarch, Marosa, Paperjam) and, for the bill number, the transitional dates and the thresholds, law-firm summaries (KPMG Luxembourg, Arendt, Deloitte Luxembourg) read as search excerpts on 2026-10-26; the bill text was read directly on 2026-11-02 (see above), and it is subject to parliamentary approval and to Grand-Ducal Regulations not yet seen (a 2026-11-07 search found none tied to this bill; the only one named is the 2021 B2G regulation). A search excerpt of a vendor summary (EY, 31 Jul 2026) says the Conseil d'État found the bill incomplete on several essential points; its opinion itself was not read, and legilux.public.lu and efacturation.public.lu are unreachable from the cloud runner. No penalty provision was found in it. This is a summary, not tax advice.

Which file will count, and how it travels

The draft is reported to name Peppol in a four-corner model, with invoices in a format that complies with EN 16931, so a PDF alone will not qualify. The scope reported is domestic B2B, where both parties are established in Luxembourg; cross-border B2B and B2C are excluded. Until a Luxembourg specification is published, the safest test is an EN 16931 UBL or CII file that also passes Peppol BIS Billing 3.0.

Common questions

When does the Luxembourg e-invoicing mandate start?

A draft bill approved by the Luxembourg Government Council on 17 July 2026 would phase domestic B2B e-invoicing in: every business able to receive e-invoices from 1 January 2028, large and medium-sized businesses issuing them from 1 July 2028, and all remaining businesses issuing them from 1 January 2029. It is a draft that still has to pass Parliament, and the dates come from advisory and vendor sources, not the bill text, so confirm them before relying on them.

Which format and network will Luxembourg use?

The draft is reported to name Peppol, in a four-corner model through certified access points, with invoices in a format compliant with the European standard EN 16931. A draft Grand-Ducal Regulation is reported to make Peppol the designated channel with MyGuichet.lu as fallback (several firm summaries agree; the regulation text was not read). No Luxembourg-specific syntax rule set was found, so the safest test is an EN 16931 UBL or CII file that also passes Peppol BIS Billing 3.0.

Does the Luxembourg mandate cover cross-border or consumer sales?

As reported, no: it applies to domestic B2B transactions where supplier and customer are both established in Luxembourg. Cross-border B2B and B2C sales are excluded from the national mandate. The separate EU-level digital reporting requirement for cross-border B2B, expected from 2030, is a different measure.

What are the penalties for not e-invoicing in Luxembourg?

The bill text (draft law No. 8815, read in French on the Chamber of Deputies site on 2026-11-02) contains no fine or penalty for failing to e-invoice. It does set a usage fee for businesses that go past a regulatory cap on invoices sent through the state's alternative solutions: 2 euros per invoice for the first 20 over the cap, 3 for the next 30, 4 for the next 50 and 5 for each after that, excluding VAT, with the cap and collection left to a Grand-Ducal Regulation. An issuer may not charge extra for sending an e-invoice instead of paper. It is still a draft.
