Tunisia: El Fatoora e-invoicing

Tunisia's El Fatoora system runs on the Tunisie TradeNet (TTN) platform. Below: what guides report about who must use it and since when, where they differ, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelCentralised clearance on the government-run TTN platform. Invoices are TEIF XML (the Tunisian Electronic Invoice Format) with the issuer's qualified electronic signature; onboarding needs a qualified certificate from ANCE and an El Fatoora subscription, per EDICOM and the Idaraty portal. This is not EN 16931 or Peppol.
Phase-inGuides describe B2G and some B2B first (the system is dated to 2016 in one guide), large enterprises named by the tax directorate (DGI) later, and a 2025 Finance Law penalty regime reported from 1 July 2025 (Article 71 in one source).
ServicesThe 2026 Finance Law (Article 53 in one source) is reported to extend the duty to VAT-liable service transactions from 1 January 2026. Sources give different enactment dates (12 December or late December 2025), and one reports the Finance Ministry relaxing immediate enforcement for smaller companies in January 2026.
ConsumersOne guide says the transmission duty does not apply in the same terms to invoices to private individuals; minimum content rules remain. Unconfirmed.

Common questions

What is El Fatoora in Tunisia?

The national e-invoicing system run on the Tunisie TradeNet (TTN) platform, where invoices are issued as TEIF XML with the issuer's qualified electronic signature, according to EDICOM and the Idaraty onboarding page.

When did it become mandatory, and for whom?

Guides describe a phase-in: B2G and some B2B first, large enterprises later, and VAT-liable services from 1 January 2026 under Article 53 of the 2026 Finance Law. A 2025 Finance Law penalty regime is reported from 1 July 2025. Sources differ on details; check the Finance Laws and the DGI.

Does it cover invoices to consumers?

One guide says the transmission duty does not apply in the same terms to invoices to private individuals, though minimum content still applies. Unconfirmed.

Can this checker validate a Tunisian invoice?

No. It has no TEIF rules, cannot verify a qualified signature and does not connect to TTN. For UBL or CII it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not TTN acceptance.
