Jordan: JoFotara

Jordan issues invoices through the National Electronic Invoicing System (JoFotara), run by the Income and Sales Tax Department. Below: what the Department's own consolidated regulation and instructions say (read 14 November 2026), what is only reported about the 2025 phase, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

What the Department's texts say

TopicWhat the texts say
Legal basisRegulation No. 34 of 2019 on Organising Invoicing Affairs and Control, issued under Article 23(f) of Income Tax Law No. 34 of 2014 (Official Gazette 5572, 1 May 2019; in force 60 days after publication), as amended by Regulation No. 13 of 2023 (Official Gazette 5851, 16 April 2023). The Department's site hosts this 2023 consolidation.
The e-invoiceArticle 4: for the regulation, an electronic invoice counts when it is issued from the national electronic invoicing program or from software linked to it; the Department rolls this out through the program or direct linking according to the timetable it prepares. The regulation sets no date itself.
Who invoicesArticle 5: every seller of goods or services worth at least JOD 1 issues an invoice in at least two copies with a serial number, the seller's full name and address, the seller's sales tax number (or national number if not registered), the date, and the item, quantity, value and total. The buyer's name is required on deferred, instalment or part-payment sales. Above JOD 10,000 the seller must prove the buyer received it.
ExemptionsArticle 11 and Instructions No. 1 of 2019, Article 4: licensed groceries, mini-markets, supermarkets and small shops, bookshops, greengrocers, household goods shops, bakeries, popular restaurants, home businesses, dairy and sewing-supplies shops with annual sales under JOD 75,000; licensed crafts under JOD 30,000; bread-only bakeries under JOD 150,000. An exempt seller may ask in writing to invoice anyway, and the Director may oblige one whose sales pass the limit. Lawyers with receipts up to JOD 50,000 a year may use receipts and must switch to invoices within 45 days of passing it.
RecordsArticle 8: invoices are kept four years; the national program's data stands in for the paper copies.
PenaltiesArticle 17: a seller who does not invoice under the regulation faces the penalties in the Income Tax Law; the instructions name Article 64 of Law No. 34 of 2014. The amounts were not read here.
Reported, not readAmended Regulation No. 2 of 2025 and mandatory issuance through JoFotara or integrated systems from April 2025; universal registration due 31 May 2024; UBL 2.1 XML as the format, with a QR code and signed document returned.

Read on 14 November 2026 from istd.gov.jo: the consolidated Regulation No. 34 of 2019 (as amended by Regulation No. 13 of 2023) and Instructions No. 1 of 2019 (sources: eval/mandate-primary-sources-pk-ng-jo-th-2026-11-14.md in the repository). Regulation No. 2 of 2025, the April 2025 phase and the format are from vendor guides, not from Income and Sales Tax Department texts read here; the Department's site still hosts the 2023 consolidation.

Common questions

What is JoFotara?

Jordan's National Electronic Invoicing System, run by the Income and Sales Tax Department. Under Regulation No. 34 of 2019 as amended in 2023 (Article 4), an electronic invoice counts when it is issued from the national program or from software linked to it. Secondary guides describe clearance, with a QR code and signed document returned.

When did e-invoicing become mandatory in Jordan?

The regulation itself sets no date: it leaves the rollout to the Department's timetable (Article 4(b)). Vendor guides report mandatory issuance through JoFotara or linked systems from April 2025 under an amended Regulation No. 2 of 2025, which was not found on the Department's site.

Who is exempt?

Licensed groceries, mini-markets, supermarkets, small shops, bookshops, greengrocers, household goods shops, bakeries, popular restaurants, home businesses, dairy and sewing-supplies shops with annual sales under JOD 75,000, licensed crafts under JOD 30,000 and bread-only bakeries under JOD 150,000 (Regulation Article 11; Instructions No. 1 of 2019, Article 4). An exempt seller may still ask the Department in writing to invoice.

Does the invoice have to be UBL?

Neither the regulation nor the instructions name a format. Guides describe UBL 2.1 XML with Jordanian rules on top; confirm the current specification with the Department or your integration provider.

Can this checker validate a JoFotara invoice?

No. It has no Jordanian rules and does not call JoFotara. For a UBL file it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not a JoFotara clearance.
