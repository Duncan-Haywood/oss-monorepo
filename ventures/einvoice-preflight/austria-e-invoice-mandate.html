Austria's e-invoice mandate: government only, business voluntary

Austria requires EN 16931 e-invoices from anyone who bills the federal government, but business-to-business e-invoicing is still voluntary and no domestic date is adopted. Below: what is law, what is only forecast, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

What applies (as guides and vendor summaries describe it)

DateWhat changes
1 Jan 2014Suppliers to federal bodies must invoice electronically (reported as section 5 of the IKT-Konsolidierungsgesetz).
18 Apr 2020Federal bodies must receive EN 16931 invoices; suppliers, including foreign ones, must send them in a compliant format.
NowB2B e-invoicing is voluntary. No domestic B2B mandate date is adopted in law.
1 Jul 2030Reported start of the EU-wide digital reporting for cross-border B2B supplies. It does not create a domestic Austrian invoicing duty.

Unconfirmed: these dates come from vendor and guide pages (Sovos, Marosa VAT, invoicenavigator, e-invoicing.org, VATupdate, dddinvoices) read as search excerpts on 2026-10-26; the legal text and the BMF pages were not read. Forecasts of a B2B mandate between 2027 and 2035 are advisers' guesses, not adopted Austrian legislation. No penalty amount was found and none is stated here. This is a summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts, and how it travels

For federal customers the reported channels are the e-Rechnung.gv.at portal and Peppol, with ebInterface XML (the Austrian standard) or Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 as the format; a PDF alone does not qualify. Private customers set their own terms. If you invoice both, an EN 16931 UBL file that also passes Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 is the safest single format to test.

Open the checker, drop your XML, and tick Also apply the Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 rules. Common questions

Is B2B e-invoicing mandatory in Austria?

Not yet. Sources read in October 2026 agree that business-to-business e-invoicing in Austria is voluntary and that no domestic B2B mandate date has been adopted in law; commentary that guesses at 2027 to 2028 or later is speculation. Confirm with the Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF) or your accountant; this page is a summary, not advice.

Who must send an e-invoice in Austria today?

Suppliers to federal government. Electronic invoicing to federal bodies has been required since 1 January 2014, and since 18 April 2020 that includes foreign suppliers, with invoices that comply with EN 16931. The reported channels are the federal e-Rechnung.gv.at portal and Peppol; the formats are ebInterface XML (the Austrian standard) and Peppol BIS Billing 3.0. This comes from vendor and guide summaries, not the legal text.

Which format should an Austrian business use?

For federal customers, ebInterface or Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 (UBL). For private customers there is no required format; an EN 16931 UBL or CII file is the safest bet if you want to be ready for the EU's cross-border digital reporting, which is reported to start on 1 July 2030 and does not itself create a domestic Austrian obligation.

Can this checker validate an ebInterface invoice?

No. It reads UBL, CII, Factur-X and JSON and applies the EN 16931 arithmetic, tax breakdown, mandatory-field and identifier checks, plus the Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 rules when you tick that option, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typo catches, each with a rule ID and a fix. It does not read ebInterface XML or run the e-Rechnung.gv.at validation, so a clean result is an offline observation, not an acceptance by the portal.

What are the penalties for not e-invoicing in Austria?

None were found in the sources read, so this page states no amount. For federal suppliers the practical consequence reported is that a non-compliant invoice is not processed; check the BMF guidance.
