Rwanda: EBM and EIS

Rwanda's businesses issue invoices through Rwanda Revenue Authority electronic billing machines (EBM), now described as the Electronic Invoicing System. Below: what guides report and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelAn EBM combines a certified invoicing system (CIS) and a sales data controller (SDC); invoices are issued and reported to the RRA. EBM 2.1 is described as the current version. Not EN 16931.
WhoThe RRA is quoted as saying VAT-registered operators and businesses with turnover of RWF 20 million or more must issue EBM receipts. Unverified.
Start dateSources disagree: EBMs date from 2013 for large firms; Edicom gives 1 January 2021 for the Electronic Invoicing System.
2026 orderA summary reports Ministerial Order No 006/26/10/TC of 24 September 2026 on the Electronic Invoicing System, published in the Official Gazette of 25 September 2026 and repealing the 2013 order. Not confirmed against the Gazette.
EnforcementPress reported an RRA crackdown on businesses not using the system in October 2024.

Common questions

What is an EBM?

An electronic billing machine: a certified invoicing system plus a sales data controller that issues receipts and reports them to the Rwanda Revenue Authority.

What is new in 2026?

A summary reports Ministerial Order No 006/26/10/TC of 24 September 2026 on the Electronic Invoicing System. It is unconfirmed; check the Official Gazette.

Who must issue EBM invoices?

VAT-registered businesses and, as the RRA is quoted, those with turnover of RWF 20 million or more. Confirm with the RRA.

Can this checker validate an EBM invoice?

No. It has no Rwandan rules and does not connect to an EBM. For UBL or CII it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not RRA acceptance.
