Nigeria: the Merchant Buyer Solution

Nigeria clears invoices through the Nigeria Revenue Service's Merchant Buyer Solution (MBS), by taxpayer size. Below: what the NRS e-invoicing portal itself says (read 14 November 2026), the rollout table from the NRS public notice of February 2026 as advisers reproduce it, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works

TopicWhat the sources say
IssueThe seller's system sends the invoice data to the MBS, through an NRS-accredited provider or, since 13 December 2025, directly with the taxpayer's own cryptographic keys (NRS portal update, read). Secondary guides say the MBS returns an invoice reference number (IRN), a QR code and a digital signature, and that an invoice needs them to be valid for payment.
RolloutNRS public notice of February 2026, as KPMG Nigeria reproduces its table: large taxpayers (annual turnover above 5 billion naira) went live from November 2025, with compliance enforcement April to June 2026; medium taxpayers (1 to 5 billion naira) pilot April to June 2026, go live 1 July 2026, enforcement January to March 2027; emerging taxpayers (below 1 billion naira) pilot April to June 2027, go live 1 July 2027, enforcement January to March 2028. A July 2026 notice reported in the press gave large taxpayers until 31 July 2026. The notice was not read: nrs.gov.ng answered this build host with a CAPTCHA.
StandardsThe portal's Standards Adopted page (read) names UBL for invoices, credit notes and other business documents, ISO 20022 for financial messaging, ISO 27001, PKI, elliptic-curve cryptography and NTP timestamps. It does not name Peppol or a Peppol PINT profile.
SchemaPortal updates of 13 December 2025 (read): a new invoice_kind field (B2B, B2G or B2C); state and local government fields for the parties; for a B2B buyer with no TIN, the incorporation number in the TIN field as RN- followed by the number; foreign buyers may keep their home-country identifiers.
BuyerOne guide reports a 72-hour window for the buyer to reject an invoice (not confirmed here).
Retail (B2C)Guides report no pre-clearance for retail invoices, which are reported to NRS within 24 hours of issue (not confirmed here).
PenaltyOne summary lists a 200,000 naira penalty without conditions; NRS Information Circular 2026/05 is cited as restating the duties. Its text was not read.

Common questions

What is Nigeria's Merchant Buyer Solution?

Nigeria's national e-invoicing system, run by the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS, successor to the Federal Inland Revenue Service) at einvoice.nrs.gov.ng. The seller's system sends invoice data to the MBS through an NRS-accredited provider or, since 13 December 2025, directly with the taxpayer's own keys. Secondary guides say the MBS returns an invoice reference number (IRN) and a QR code.

Who has to use it, and from when?

By turnover, as the NRS public notice of February 2026 sets out (table reproduced by KPMG Nigeria): large taxpayers above 5 billion naira live since November 2025, with enforcement from April 2026 and a reported final deadline of 31 July 2026; medium taxpayers (1 to 5 billion naira) from 1 July 2026; emerging taxpayers below 1 billion naira from 1 July 2027, with enforcement in early 2028. The notice itself was not read here.

Is the invoice format UBL or Peppol?

The NRS portal lists UBL as an adopted standard for invoices and credit notes, with ISO 20022 for financial messages. It does not name Peppol. The MBS schema adds its own fields, such as invoice_kind (B2B, B2G, B2C) and state and local government codes, so an EN 16931 UBL file is a starting point, not a finished MBS submission.

Can this checker validate a Nigerian invoice?

No. It has no Nigerian rules, does not call the MBS and does not produce an IRN. For a UBL or CII file it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks, which catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults that are likely to be rejected in any clearance system, but a clean result is not an NRS approval.
