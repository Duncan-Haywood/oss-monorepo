Malaysia e-invoicing: MyInvois phases and what to check first

Malaysia's MyInvois system, run by the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN/IRBM), was phased in by annual turnover from 1 August 2024, with a relaxation period reported to run to 31 December 2027. Below: the phases, the RM10,000 rule, the reported penalties, and what a free local checker covers.

Reports also say that from 1 Jan 2026 a transaction above RM10,000 needs an individual e-invoice, not a consolidated one, even during relaxation. Which file counts, and how it travels

Documents are submitted to the MyInvois system as structured XML or JSON in a UBL 2.1-based layout, through the MyInvois portal or API or an intermediary, and validated by LHDN, which returns a unique identifier and a QR code for the validated document. Validation by MyInvois is the one that decides. Consolidated e-invoices for small B2C sales are allowed within limits.

Common questions

When did Malaysian e-invoicing become mandatory?

MyInvois, run by the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN/IRBM), is phased by annual turnover: from 1 August 2024 above RM100 million, 1 January 2025 for RM25 to 100 million, 1 July 2025 for RM5 to 25 million, and 1 January 2026 for RM1 to 5 million. Sources differ on the smallest businesses: the June 2025 announcement as reported put those up to RM1 million on 1 July 2026 with a RM500,000 exemption, while some adviser sites now say businesses below RM1 million are exempt. Check LHDN for your own turnover band; this page is a summary, not advice.

Is there a relaxation period?

Yes, reported as a concession and not a deferment: businesses must still register on MyInvois, but penalties are not enforced during it. One adviser newsletter reports the Phase 4 period was extended to 31 December 2027, with enforcement from 1 January 2028; an older table shows 30 June 2026. Confirm the current date with LHDN.

What are the penalties?

Reported under section 120 of the Income Tax Act 1967: a fine of RM200 to RM20,000 per offence, and each non-compliant transaction can count as a separate offence. Penalties are said not to be enforced during the relaxation period. Amounts can change, so check LHDN.

Can this checker validate a MyInvois document?

Partly. It reads UBL, CII, Factur-X and JSON and applies the EN 16931 arithmetic, tax breakdown, mandatory-field and identifier checks, plus IBAN and BIC typo catches, each with a rule ID and a fix. It does not apply the MyInvois document rules, TIN checks, or the Malaysian tax codes, so a clean result is an offline observation, not approval. The MyInvois validation remains the authority.
