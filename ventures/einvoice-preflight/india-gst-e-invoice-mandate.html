India's GST e-invoicing: who needs it, and what an IRN is

India does not use the European format. Taxpayers above a turnover limit register each invoice with the government's invoice registration portal (IRP), which returns an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and a signed QR code. Below: the thresholds as guides report them, and two free local checks, a GSTIN check and an IRN calculator.

The rules (as secondary guides report them)

ItemWhat guides say
WhoAggregate turnover above ₹5 crore in any financial year since 2017-18, counted across all GSTINs under one PAN; mandatory since 1 Aug 2023. Reported exemptions include banks, insurers, goods transport agencies and some other classes.
Which documentsBusiness-to-business invoices, debit and credit notes, and exports. Business-to-consumer sales are generally not registered, though a dynamic QR code applies above a higher limit.
30-day windowFrom 1 Apr 2025 the portal is reported to refuse documents more than 30 days old for taxpayers with turnover of ₹10 crore or more.
FormatThe government's INV-01 JSON schema, sent to the IRP directly or through a GST suvidha provider; the portal returns the IRN, acknowledgement number and signed QR code.

Common questions

When is e-invoicing mandatory under GST in India?

Per secondary guides, a business whose aggregate turnover in any financial year since 2017-18 exceeded ₹5 crore must issue e-invoices for business-to-business supplies, exports and similar documents, a limit in place since 1 August 2023. From 1 April 2025 the invoice registration portal is reported to refuse documents older than 30 days for taxpayers with aggregate turnover of ₹10 crore or more. Some classes of taxpayer are exempt. Sources here are commentary, not the CBIC notifications, so confirm against them. This is a summary, not advice.

What is an IRN?

The Invoice Reference Number is a 64-character hexadecimal SHA-256 hash that the invoice registration portal (IRP) returns for each registered document. Guides describe it as the hash of the supplier GSTIN, the financial year, the document type (INV, CRN or DBN) and the document number joined in that order. The calculator on this page reproduces one worked sample from a public guide; the portal's own answer is the one that counts.

Does this checker validate India's e-invoice JSON?

No. India uses its own INV-01 JSON schema submitted to the IRP, not EN 16931. This page only checks the shape and check character of a GSTIN and the document-number rule, and calculates the IRN hash locally. A clean result does not mean the IRP will accept the invoice.

Does the GSTIN check tell me a supplier is registered?

No. It tests the fifteen-character shape and the check character, which catches most typing mistakes. Whether the number is registered, active or belongs to your supplier can only be checked on the GST portal.
