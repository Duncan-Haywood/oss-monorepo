Taiwan: the eGUI platform

Taiwan issues electronic Government Uniform Invoices (eGUI) through a Ministry of Finance platform. Read from the law itself (2026-11-14): electronic issuing is one lawful way to issue an invoice, not a general duty, and a business that does issue electronically must send each invoice to the platform within 7 days (to a business) or 2 days (to a consumer). Below: what the texts say, what is still only reported, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works

TopicWhat the sources say
Mandatory?Not as a general rule. VAT Act Article 32(4): a uniform invoice is government-printed, self-printed with approval, or issued, sent or received electronically. Uniform Invoice Regulations Article 7: five kinds of invoice, the e-invoice one of them. Article 7-1(2): a foreign seller of electronic services to individuals with no fixed place in Taiwan (VAT Act Article 6(4)) must issue cloud invoices. Several vendor guides say eGUI has been compulsory for all tax-registered businesses since 1 January 2021; neither text says so. Read on law.moj.gov.tw, the Act as amended 28 May 2025 and the Regulations as amended 12 December 2024.
How it flowsInvoices go to the Ministry of Finance platform through its official Turnkey software or a certified value-added centre (VAC).
FormatThe Message Implementation Guideline (MIG). MIG 4.1 is current. The Ministry of Finance's own Turnkey manual (v3.9, February 2025, as summarised by a search result; the PDF itself was not opened here) says new operators had to use MIG 4.0 from 1 July 2024 because MIG 3.2.1 and 3.1.2 were being retired, and that MIG 4.1 is fully used from 1 January 2026. Whether MIG 4.0 files are still accepted after that date is not stated in what was read.
Transmission deadlineVAT Act Article 32-1 (amended 7 August 2024, in force 1 January 2025) makes a business that issues e-invoices send them and the related data to the platform within the time the Ministry announces. The Ministry's table (Executive Yuan Gazette vol. 30 no. 233, 12 December 2024): 7 days when the buyer is a business, 2 days when not, from the day after issue, correction, voidance or credit note; a deadline falling on a weekend or holiday is not extended; after force majeure, a business may apply within 3 days for time to make up. Unused invoice number ranges: within 10 days of the next two-month period. Vendor guides' "48 hours" for B2C is not in the table.
FineVAT Act Article 48-2: TWD 1,500 to 15,000 for a late or untrue transmission, after a notice to correct, and again for each further failure. News reports describe a six-month guidance period without fines in the first half of 2025; that is not in the Act.
PeppolOne source describes Taiwan as having adopted Peppol; not confirmed here.

The mandate, deadline and fine rows are from the VAT Act, the Uniform Invoice Regulations and their amendment history on the Ministry of Justice's law database (law.moj.gov.tw), and the Ministry of Finance's time-limit table as printed in the Executive Yuan Gazette, all read on 2026-11-14 (eval/mandate-primary-sources-taiwan-oman-2026-11-14.md). The flow, format and Peppol rows are from search summaries and vendor guides (ecosio, Edicom, Vertex, Voxel, VATupdate), not from Ministry of Finance technical texts: the e-invoice platform's site answers 403 from where this page was written.

Common questions

Is e-invoicing mandatory in Taiwan?

No, not for most businesses, on the text of the law. The Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act (Article 32(4)) lets a uniform invoice be printed by the government, self-printed with approval, or issued electronically, and the Regulations Governing the Use of Uniform Invoices (Article 7) list the e-invoice as one of five lawful kinds beside triplicate, duplicate, special and cash-register invoices. The exception written in the Regulations (Article 7-1) is a foreign seller of electronic services to individuals in Taiwan with no fixed place there, which must issue cloud invoices. Vendor guides that say eGUI has been compulsory for every business since 2021 are not borne out by these texts; a ruling for your own business can still differ, so ask your tax office.

What is MIG 4.1?

The current version of the Ministry of Finance Message Implementation Guideline, the structured format for eGUI messages, according to the sources read. The law only says the transmitted data must follow the Ministry's published data-exchange guideline. The Ministry's Turnkey manual (v3.9, as summarised, not opened here) puts full use of MIG 4.1 at 1 January 2026 and retires MIG 3.2.1 and 3.1.2; it does not say, in what was read, whether MIG 4.0 stops being accepted.

How do invoices reach the Ministry of Finance?

A business that issues e-invoices must send each one, and any correction, voidance or credit note, to the Ministry of Finance's e-invoice platform within 7 days when the buyer is a business and within 2 days when the buyer is not, counted from the day after issue (the Ministry's time-limit table, Executive Yuan Gazette, 12 December 2024). The 48 hours some guides give is not in the table. A late or untrue transmission can be fined TWD 1,500 to 15,000 under Article 48-2 of the VAT Act, in force since 1 January 2025. The platform is reached through the Ministry's Turnkey software or a value-added centre, as reported.
