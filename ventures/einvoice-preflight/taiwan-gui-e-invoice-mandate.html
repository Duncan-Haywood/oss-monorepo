Taiwan: the eGUI platform

Taiwan issues electronic Government Uniform Invoices (eGUI) through a Ministry of Finance platform. Sources disagree on whether electronic issuing is compulsory for B2B. Below: what each says, what is common ground, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
Mandatory?Several vendor guides say all business tax-registered entities, domestic and foreign, must issue eGUI in structured form since 1 January 2021, B2B and B2C. A newer guide says the electronic form is one lawful way to issue alongside paper, with no general B2B date published in the Ministry of Finance material it reviewed, and another cites an EY June 2026 tracker as saying domestic issuance is allowed but not mandatory. Unresolved here.
How it flowsInvoices go to the Ministry of Finance platform through its official Turnkey software or a certified value-added centre (VAC).
FormatThe Message Implementation Guideline (MIG). Sources agree MIG 4.1 is current; they differ on whether MIG 4.0 and earlier were discontinued on 1 January 2026 or are still accepted.
TimingVendor guides mention reporting within 7 days for B2B and 48 hours for B2C. Not confirmed against the Ministry text.
PeppolOne source describes Taiwan as having adopted Peppol; not confirmed here.

Common questions

Is e-invoicing mandatory in Taiwan?

The sources disagree. Several vendor guides say eGUI has been compulsory for all tax-registered businesses, B2B and B2C, since 1 January 2021. A newer guide and a tracker it cites say electronic issuing is a lawful option, not a general B2B requirement. Ask the Ministry of Finance or the Fiscal Information Agency before relying on either.

What is MIG 4.1?

The current version of the Ministry of Finance Message Implementation Guideline, the structured format for eGUI messages, according to the sources read. They differ on whether older versions such as MIG 4.0 stopped being accepted on 1 January 2026.

How do invoices reach the Ministry of Finance?

Through its official Turnkey software or through a certified value-added centre, as reported. Vendor guides also give reporting windows of 7 days for B2B and 48 hours for B2C, which were not confirmed against the Ministry text.

Can this checker validate a Taiwanese eGUI?

No. It has no MIG rules and does not use Turnkey. For a UBL or CII file it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not a Ministry of Finance acceptance.
