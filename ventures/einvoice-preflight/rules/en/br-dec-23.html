E-invoice rule explained · EN 16931

BR-DEC-23: Line net amount has more than two decimals

DeutschFrançaisAll rules

Official message

The allowed maximum number of decimals for the Invoice line net amount (BT-131) is 2.
Severity
Error (the invoice is rejected)
Checked in
UBL, CII
Business terms
BT-131

What does BR-DEC-23 mean?

The invoice line net amount (BT-131) may have at most two digits after the decimal point. The check simply counts the characters after the point, so 10.000 fails just like 9.999 does, even though it is the same as 10.00.

Usual causes

  • The line amount was written as quantity × price without rounding, for example 3 × 3.3333 = 9.9999.
  • The software writes amounts with a fixed number of decimals (three or four) for every number.
  • Trailing zeros were kept from a calculation (10.000).

How do I fix BR-DEC-23?

  1. Round each line net amount to two decimals before writing it; the unit price (BT-146) may keep more decimals.
  2. Build the document totals and the VAT breakdown from the rounded line amounts, so the other sum checks still pass.
  3. Set your export to write all monetary amounts with exactly two decimals.

Corrected XML (minimal example we wrote)

UBL

<cac:InvoiceLine>  <cbc:ID>1</cbc:ID>  <cbc:InvoicedQuantity unitCode="H87">3</cbc:InvoicedQuantity>  <cbc:LineExtensionAmount currencyID="EUR">10.00</cbc:LineExtensionAmount>  <cac:Item>    <cbc:Name>Widget</cbc:Name>  </cac:Item>  <cac:Price>    <cbc:PriceAmount currencyID="EUR">3.3333</cbc:PriceAmount>  </cac:Price></cac:InvoiceLine>

Example values only. Put the elements in place of, or beside, what your file has; element order matters in both syntaxes.
