E-invoice rule explained · EN 16931

BR-E-10: Exempt VAT breakdown has no exemption reason

DeutschFrançaisAll rules

Official message

A VAT breakdown (BG-23) with VAT Category code (BT-118) "Exempt from VAT" shall have a VAT exemption reason code (BT-121) or a VAT exemption reason text (BT-120).
A VAT Breakdown (BG-23) with VAT Category code (BT-118) "Exempt from VAT" shall have a VAT exemption reason code (BT-121) or a VAT exemption reason text (BT-120).
Severity
Error (the invoice is rejected)
Checked in
UBL, CII
Business terms
BG-23, BT-118, BT-121, BT-120

What does BR-E-10 mean?

When the VAT breakdown uses category E (exempt from VAT), that entry must carry an exemption reason code (BT-121) or an exemption reason text (BT-120). The check only tests that one of them is there; it does not judge whether the reason is the right one.

Usual causes

  • The software sets category E but leaves the exemption reason empty.
  • The legal basis for the exemption is printed in a note on the invoice instead of in the VAT breakdown.
  • The reason was put on the invoice line, not on the VAT breakdown entry.

How do I fix BR-E-10?

  1. This rule only applies once E has been chosen; which exemption covers your sale is a tax question, so ask your tax adviser if unsure.
  2. In the E breakdown entry, add the exemption text, usually the legal provision your invoice already cites, and/or the matching VATEX code.
  3. Repeat this for every E breakdown entry if there is more than one.

Corrected XML (minimal example we wrote)

UBL

<cac:TaxSubtotal>  <cbc:TaxableAmount currencyID="EUR">500.00</cbc:TaxableAmount>  <cbc:TaxAmount currencyID="EUR">0.00</cbc:TaxAmount>  <cac:TaxCategory>    <cbc:ID>E</cbc:ID>    <cbc:Percent>0</cbc:Percent>    <cbc:TaxExemptionReason>Exempt from VAT under [legal provision your invoice cites]</cbc:TaxExemptionReason>    <cac:TaxScheme>      <cbc:ID>VAT</cbc:ID>    </cac:TaxScheme>  </cac:TaxCategory></cac:TaxSubtotal>

Example values only. Put the elements in place of, or beside, what your file has; element order matters in both syntaxes.
