Hungary's e-invoice rules: reporting is mandatory, exchange is not (yet)

Hungary was an early mover: every domestic B2B invoice has been reported to the tax authority, NAV, through Online Számla since 2020. That is reporting, not a structured e-invoice exchange mandate. Below: what sources agree on, where they disagree, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (as vendor guides report it)

DateWhat changes
Jul 2018NAV Online Számla real-time invoice data reporting starts for invoices above a value threshold.
Jul 2020Reporting covers all domestic B2B invoices regardless of value.
2025 to 2026Sector rules for B2B electricity, gas and water supplies to non-consumers. Sources give 1 Jul 2025 for the first two and differ on water (one says January 2026).
About 2028 to 2029A ViDA-aligned B2B model is reported as being planned (consultation closed early 2026; pilot about 2028, mandate about 2029). Unconfirmed.

Where sources disagree: the sector-rule dates and the archiving period (one guide says 5 years, another 8). These are compliance-vendor guides, not the decrees. Confirm with NAV (nav.gov.hu). A summary, not tax advice.

What gets sent, and how

Invoicing software sends NAV an XML report of each invoice through the Online Számla interface, in NAV's own schema rather than UBL or CII. The invoice itself still goes to the customer in whatever form you agree (PDF, UBL or CII). So the practical risk is a report that disagrees with the invoice: totals that do not add up, or a wrong tax number.

What this checker does and does not cover

It applies EN 16931 (and the Peppol BIS 3.0 rules, if you tick the option) to JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X files. Since November 2026 it also reads the NAV Online Számla 3.0 report (an InvoiceData file, or a manageInvoice request with the Base64 invoice data inside). On those it checks what NAV's specification (3.0, February 2026 edition) lists as blocking errors that can be seen in the file: VAT rates and VAT contents by delivery date, exemption and out-of-scope codes against the customer's VAT status, the HUF exchange rate and NAV's band for EUR, USD and other currencies, line numbering, modification references, private-person customer data, the tax-number check digit and, for a report that is itself the e-invoice, the SHA3-512 electronicInvoiceHash. It also does the arithmetic behind NAV's warnings (line net, VAT and gross, HUF conversions, the totals by VAT rate) with NAV's own tolerance of 1% and at least one unit, and marks those as warnings, as NAV does. On invoices that carry the product fee (termékdíj), it checks the CSK or KT codes, the quantities, units and amounts on the lines against the product fee summary, quantity times rate, and the total charge, following the 13 product fee warnings in Annex I of NAV's specification. It also gives the header and line warnings Annex I lists that a file can show: a customer or fiscal representative given the seller's own tax number, a wrong VAT or county code, a seller EU VAT number that does not match its tax number, an issue date in the future or a correction reported 15 days or more after it was issued, aggregate invoice dates that disagree with their lines, and an exemption code (KBAET, EAM and others) that does not fit the kind of tax number given for the customer.

Corrections and cancellations, checked against their invoice. Drop the original invoice together with the documents that modify or cancel it, and the checker does what NAV does against its own store: a cancellation (STORNO) must bring the net, VAT and forint VAT of the invoice and its earlier corrections back to zero (one unit tolerated), modification numbers must count 1, 2, 3 without a repeat, a created line must not reuse a line number the chain already has, a modified line must exist, the invoice category and the customer's tax number must stay the same, and a correction must not be dated before the invoice or move its delivery date to the day the correction was issued (a common invoicing-program error, NAV says). A bare InvoiceData file does not say whether it is sent as MODIFY or STORNO, so the cancellation sums run on manageInvoice requests, which do.

What it cannot see: anything NAV checks against its own records that is not among the files you drop, such as whether the invoice number is unique, whether the base invoice of a modification was reported, or whether a tax number is registered. On NAV's 30 published sample reports it reports nothing on 27; on the other 3 it found five faults that are in NAV's samples themselves (two summary typos, two placeholder tax numbers, and a line operation the current rules refuse). Treat a clean result as "the report is consistent and passes the checks NAV publishes", not "NAV will accept the report".

Common questions

Is e-invoicing mandatory in Hungary?

Hungary has had mandatory real-time reporting of invoice data to the tax authority (NAV Online Számla) since July 2018, covering all domestic B2B invoices regardless of value since July 2020. That is reporting, not a requirement to exchange structured e-invoices. Compliance-vendor guides say there is no general structured B2B e-invoice mandate in 2026, with narrow sector rules (for example electricity, gas and water supplies to non-consumers) whose dates the sources give differently. Confirm with NAV. This is a summary, not advice.

What format does NAV Online Számla use?

NAV takes invoice data as XML through its Online Számla interface, using NAV's own schema (InvoiceData 3.0) rather than UBL or CII. Most invoicing software sends the report for you. This checker also reads the NAV report itself: drop an InvoiceData file, or the manageInvoice request that carries it, and it checks the file against NAV's schema first (the SCHEMA_VIOLATION gate, element order, code lists and namespace prefixes), then applies NAV's published blocking checks and the arithmetic behind NAV's warnings. It does not send anything to NAV.

Is a Hungarian structured B2B mandate coming?

Vendor guides report a public consultation on an EU ViDA-aligned model that closed in early 2026, with a pilot around 2028 and a full mandate around 2029. These are secondary sources and plans can change, so treat the dates as unconfirmed and check NAV.

How do I check an invoice before sending it to a Hungarian customer?

Drop the file into the checker. It tests EN 16931 arithmetic, the tax breakdown, mandatory fields, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page. It is an offline observation, not NAV's acceptance and not legal advice.
