Benin: the facture normalisée (e-MECeF)

Benin has required a DGI-certified invoice, the facture normalisée, since the early 2020s. Below: what guides report and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelReal-time fiscalisation. The seller issues the invoice through a certified MECeF device, the online e-MECeF platform, or its own billing software (SFE) approved by the DGI. The invoice becomes a facture normalisée only after it is finalised and receives DGI security elements, including the MECeF/DGI code. A PDF made outside that workflow is not one. Not EN 16931.
Legal basisThe General Tax Code as amended by successive finance laws, and a 2018 arrêté (No. 711) on MECeF use. Article 481-3 is cited for the buyer's duty to ask for a facture normalisée.
Who and whenReportedly taxpayers under corporate income tax, business profits tax, TPS or VAT, apart from legal exemptions. Trackers give 1 January 2020 for B2B and B2G. Guides describe the rollout as complete.
Buyers and penaltiesThe DGI reminded buyers in July 2025 to request a facture normalisée. A site citing the government portal reports a minimum fine of 1 million FCFA per infraction. Not verified against the Code.
Open questionsExact exemptions, the turnover threshold some guides cite, and whether any API or ERP connection details have changed since 2020. One 2026 guide mentions a two-factor authentication change on the platform that no official source read confirms.

Common questions

What is a facture normalisée in Benin?

An invoice issued through a DGI-certified system: a physical MECeF device, the online e-MECeF platform, or your own billing software (an SFE) approved by the DGI. It counts as normalised only once it is finalised and carries the DGI security elements, including the MECeF/DGI code.

Since when is it mandatory?

Secondary trackers give 1 January 2020 for B2B and B2G, with the framework dating from 2018 (arrêté 711 on MECeF use). Guides describe the rollout as complete, with no further phases by turnover. Confirm the dates with the Direction Générale des Impôts.

Who must issue one?

Reportedly taxpayers subject to corporate income tax, business profits tax, TPS or VAT, apart from legal exemptions. One guide cites VAT-registered firms and individual consultants above 50 million FCFA turnover. The exemptions are not verified here.

Can this checker validate a Beninese invoice?

No. It has no Benin rules and does not connect to e-MECeF. A PDF made outside the approved workflow is not a facture normalisée, and a clean result here is not DGI acceptance. For UBL or CII it still applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults.
