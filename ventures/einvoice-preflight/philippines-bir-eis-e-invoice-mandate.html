Philippines: the BIR Electronic Invoicing System

The Philippines is introducing mandatory electronic invoicing through the Bureau of Internal Revenue’s Electronic Invoicing System (EIS). Below: how it works as secondary guides describe it, where they differ, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

What this checker does and does not cover

The main checker reads UBL, CII and Factur-X and applies EN 16931 and Peppol rules. It does not apply Philippine rules or call the BIR system. It is still useful before you hand a file to an accredited provider: wrong totals, a tax breakdown that does not add up, a missing tax reason and mistyped identifiers are the faults any invoicing system rejects.

Common questions

What is the Philippines' Electronic Invoicing System (EIS)?

The Bureau of Internal Revenue's national electronic invoicing system. Secondary guides say it was set up by Revenue Regulations No. 11-2025 (issued 27 February 2025, in force 14 March 2025) under the CREATE MORE Act, Republic Act No. 12066. Taxpayers issue invoices electronically and the BIR receives them. BIR issuances were not read for this page.

When does it start?

Guides say the deadline for the first group of taxpayers was extended to 31 December 2026, and some call that a single date for all taxpayers in scope. Regulation numbers and groups differ between sources. Check the BIR revenue issuances for your own group.

Is electronic sales reporting the same thing?

Guides say no: reporting sales data electronically under section 237-A of the Tax Code is a separate duty that applies once the BIR issues implementing rules. Treat that as a lead to confirm.

Can this checker validate a Philippine invoice?

No. It has no Philippine rules, does not call the BIR system and does not produce any BIR acknowledgement. For a UBL or CII file it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks, which catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults that any invoicing system is likely to reject, but a clean result is not BIR approval.
