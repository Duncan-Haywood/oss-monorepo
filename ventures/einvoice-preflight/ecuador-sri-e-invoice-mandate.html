Ecuador: documents go to the SRI as they are issued

In Ecuador, per the sources read, the tax authority SRI authorises electronic invoices, withholding certificates and complementary documents, and press reports that since 1 January 2026 they must be sent the moment they are issued. Below: how it works as vendor guides and press report it, where the sources are thin, and local checks of a RUC and of the 49-digit access key.

How it works (as vendor guides, press and open-source code report)

StepWhat happens
IdentifyThe issuer has a RUC of 13 digits (a person’s cédula plus an establishment number, or a company’s own number). Electronic issuing needs a signing certificate (a .p12 file, per an open-source ERP integration).
Build the keyEach document gets a 49-digit access key (clave de acceso): issue date, document type, the issuer’s RUC, environment, establishment, issuing point, sequence, an 8-digit code, an emission-type digit and a modulus-11 check digit (field lengths 8, 2, 13, 1, 3, 3, 9, 8, 1, 1 per an open-source library’s guide).
Sign and sendThe XML is signed (XAdES-BES, per the same integration) and sent to the SRI’s SOAP reception and authorisation services, which return an authorisation number and date. The technical sheet is unread.
DeliverThe buyer receives the authorised XML and usually a printable PDF (RIDE), per vendor guides.

As reported by press (Primicias, El Mercurio and others, search excerpts): immediate transmission from 1 January 2026 under resolution NAC-DGERCGC25-00000017 and its reforms, replacing a four-working-day window; and, from a resolution published on 28 July 2026 (NAC-DGERCGC26-00000027), a register of billing-software providers, who must hold an establishment and activity of their own in the RUC within 30 working days, while issuers using a third party’s system must show that provider’s RUC in the additional information within 60 working days. The resolutions themselves were not read, the excerpts did not settle who is in scope, and no penalty is stated because none was read. A summary, not tax advice.

Check a RUC or cédula

Tests the length (10 digits for a cédula, 13 for a RUC), the province code, the kind of taxpayer (third digit), the check digit and that the establishment number is not zero. It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the RUC is registered, active, or belongs to the name on the invoice.

The rules (a person: a modulus-10 check over the first nine digits with weights 2,1,2,1,… where a product over 9 loses 9; a public body: modulus 11, weights 3,2,7,6,5,4,3,2, digit 9; a company: modulus 11, weights 4,3,2,7,6,5,4,3,2, digit 10) are the ones open-source implementations and vendor validators describe. They were not read from the SRI’s specification, and the sums for the three samples above were worked by hand. The establishment digits are not covered by the check digit: changing one is not caught beyond the zero test.

Check an access key

Tests 49 digits, the modulus-11 check digit, a plausible issue date and that the issuer’s RUC (digits 11 to 23) passes the test above. It does not ask the SRI whether the document was authorised.

Weights 2 to 7 repeating from the right over the first 48 digits; the digit is 11 minus the sum mod 11, with 11 read as 0 and 10 as 1, as open-source implementations describe it (not from the SRI’s technical sheet). The test key in this repository’s tests is built by the code itself and is not an authorised document.

Common questions

What is the SRI's electronic invoicing scheme?

Ecuador’s tax authority, the SRI (Servicio de Rentas Internas), runs a comprobantes electrónicos scheme: invoices, withholding certificates and complementary documents are issued as signed XML and sent to the SRI’s reception and authorisation web services, which return an authorisation. A 49-digit access key (clave de acceso) identifies each document. This is a summary from press and vendor guides, not advice; confirm with the SRI or your accountant.

When must documents be sent to the SRI?

Press reports say that from 1 January 2026 electronic invoices, withholding certificates and complementary documents must be transmitted to the SRI at the moment they are issued (resolution NAC-DGERCGC25-00000017 and its reforms), where the period before was four working days. Which taxpayers are in scope, and exceptions, were not read in the resolution itself, so check sri.gob.ec.

What changed in July 2026 for billing-software providers?

Press reports a resolution (NAC-DGERCGC26-00000027, published in the Registro Oficial on 28 July 2026) requiring providers that issue, sign, transmit or keep electronic documents for others to register in the RUC with an establishment and activity of their own, within 30 working days, and requiring issuers that use a third-party system to show the provider’s RUC in the additional-information section within 60 working days. These are press excerpts, not the resolution.

What is a valid RUC or access key?

A RUC is 13 digits: a province code, a digit for the kind of taxpayer, a check digit by a modulus-10 or modulus-11 rule and an establishment number. An access key is 49 digits ending in a modulus-11 check digit. A matching digit does not mean a RUC is registered or a document authorised; the SRI answers that. The tests on this page catch typos only.

Can this checker validate an Ecuadorian invoice?

No. It does not read the SRI’s XML schema, check the XAdES signature or obtain an authorisation. It does test a RUC and an access key’s shape and check digits on this page, and a clean result is not an authorisation by the SRI.
