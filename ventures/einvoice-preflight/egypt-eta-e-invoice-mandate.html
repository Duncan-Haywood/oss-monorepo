Egypt: ETA e-invoices and e-receipts

In Egypt, per the guides read, B2B and B2G sales are invoiced through the Egyptian Tax Authority’s electronic system, signed with the seller’s own seal and validated by the ETA, while B2C sales use e-receipts. Below: how it works as secondary guides describe it, where this page’s knowledge is thin, and a local check of a tax registration number’s shape.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

StepWhat happens
IssueThe seller’s billing system builds the invoice as a JSON or XML document, signs it (B2B invoices) with the taxpayer’s electronic seal and submits it to the ETA in real time.
ValidateThe ETA validates the document and returns a UUID; guides say the invoice is not valid until then, and credit and debit notes refer back to that UUID.
WhoVAT-registered businesses, rolled out in waves by taxpayer group from large taxpayers outward. Guides disagree on dates and one reports a lower registration threshold from 2026; none of this was read from the ETA.
IdentifySeller and buyer are identified by the nine-digit tax registration number. For B2C a placeholder is reported for buyers with no number.
B2CRetail sales to consumers go through a separate e-receipt flow, extended to more taxpayers in stages (2022, then 2025 per guides).

Written from web search summaries and vendor guides (EDICOM, Fonoa, ClearTax, GS1 Egypt, lookuptax.com), not from the Ministry of Finance resolutions or the ETA’s SDK, none of which were read for this page. Dates, scope, thresholds and penalties may have changed; treat every date and figure as a lead to check.

Check a tax registration number

Tests the shape (nine digits; spaces and hyphens are ignored). It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the number is registered, active, or belongs to the name on the invoice.

The shape is as secondary guides (lookuptax.com and GS1 Egypt among them) describe it. No check-digit rule was found, and nothing was read from the ETA’s own pages.

Common questions

What is Egypt's ETA e-invoicing system?

The Egyptian Tax Authority's electronic invoicing and e-receipt system. B2B and B2G invoices are issued as structured documents (JSON or XML, per guides), signed with the taxpayer's own electronic seal, and sent to the ETA, which validates them and returns a UUID before the invoice counts. B2C sales use a separate e-receipt flow. This is how secondary guides describe it; the ETA's SDK and the Ministry of Finance resolutions were not read for this page.

When did it start and who is covered?

Guides report a phased B2B rollout that began with large taxpayers in late 2020 and 2021 and widened in waves, and an e-receipt rollout for B2C that began in 2022 and was extended to more taxpayers in 2025. Dates differ between sources, so check the ETA's current notices for your group before relying on any date.

What does an Egyptian tax registration number look like?

Guides report nine digits, often written XXX-XXX-XXX. No public check-digit rule was found, so this page tests shape only; a match does not mean the number is registered or belongs to the name on the invoice. Guides also say an unregistered B2C buyer is entered with a placeholder value, which this page does not check.

Can this checker validate an Egyptian e-invoice?

No. It does not read the ETA's JSON or XML document format, does not sign or submit anything, and has no Egyptian rules. It tests a registration number's shape on this page, and a clean result is not an approval by the Egyptian Tax Authority.
