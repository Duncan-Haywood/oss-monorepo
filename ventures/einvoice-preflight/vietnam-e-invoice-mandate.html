Vietnam: e-invoices since 2022, amended in June 2025

In Vietnam, per the sources read, electronic invoices have been compulsory for most taxpayers since 1 July 2022 under Decree 123/2020 and Circular 78/2021, and Decree 70/2025 amended the rules from 1 June 2025. Below: how it works as secondary summaries report it, where the sources are thin, and a local check of a tax code’s check digit.

How it works (as secondary summaries report)

StepWhat happens
IdentifyThe seller has a tax code (mã số thuế, MST): 10 digits for a business, 13 for a branch, and for individuals the 12-digit citizen ID from 1 July 2025 (vendor guides).
IssueThe invoice is structured electronic data. With a code (reference starts with C), the tax authority authenticates it before it goes to the buyer. Without a code (reference starts with K), the data must be sent to the authority by the day it is sent to the buyer, with exceptions for sectors such as telecoms, insurance, banking, aviation and securities.
Cash registerFrom 1 June 2025, household and individual businesses with annual revenue of VND 1 billion or more in sectors such as retail, catering, hotels and passenger transport must issue invoices from cash registers connected to the tax authority; the buyer’s details are needed only if the buyer asks.
VerifyThe tax authority has a public lookup for tax codes and invoices; this page does not use it.

Reported by fiscal-requirements.com, vatupdate.com, Comarch and Vietnamese legal-news excerpts, read through web search only; the decree and circular texts were not read, and the authority’s own site was unreachable. Where it is unsettled: the revenue threshold wording and scope of the cash-register rule differ between sources; one source describes Circular 32/2025 as replacing Circular 78/2021 while its own citation is inconsistent; invoice fine amounts (Decree 125/2020) were not found in any excerpt, so none is stated here; and the date individuals’ tax codes became their citizen ID number comes from vendor guides. A summary, not tax advice.

Check a tax code

Tests the shape (10 digits, 10 plus a three-digit branch suffix, or 12 digits; spaces and dots are ignored) and, for 10 and 13 digits, the check digit. It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the number is registered, active, or belongs to the name on the invoice.

The rule (the first nine digits weighted 31, 29, 23, 19, 17, 13, 7, 5, 3; the check digit is 10 minus the sum mod 11, and no digit fits when the remainder is 0) is the one the python-stdnum library documents; it reproduces that library’s example 0100233488 by hand. It was not read from the tax authority’s specification, and a 12-digit citizen ID is tested for length only.

Common questions

What is a Vietnamese e-invoice?

Under Decree 123/2020 and Circular 78/2021, businesses issue invoices as structured electronic data. Two kinds exist: one carrying a code from the tax authority, issued after the authority authenticates it, and one without a code, whose data must reach the authority by the day it is sent to the buyer, with sector exceptions. The first character of the invoice reference marks the type: C with a code, K without (secondary summaries; confirm with the tax authority or your accountant).

What changed on 1 June 2025?

Decree 70/2025/ND-CP amended Decree 123/2020 from 1 June 2025, and Circular 32/2025/TT-BTC guides it. Reported changes: cash-register e-invoices for household and individual businesses with annual revenue of VND 1 billion or more in sectors such as retail, catering, hotels and passenger transport; third-party issuance on behalf of households; voluntary VAT e-invoices for foreign e-commerce and platform suppliers; and a longer list of prohibited practices. Sources differ on threshold wording and scope, and the decree text was not read.

What is a valid Vietnamese tax code?

A business code has 10 digits: two for the province, seven serial digits and a check digit that follows from the first nine by a mod 11 rule. A branch adds a hyphen and three digits. Vendor guides say individuals use their 12-digit citizen ID as their tax code from 1 July 2025. A matching digit does not mean the number is registered or active; the tax authority's public lookup answers that. The test on this page catches typos only.

Can this checker validate a Vietnamese invoice?

No. It does not read the authority's XML schema, cannot obtain or verify a tax authority code, and has no Vietnamese rules. It does test a tax code's shape and check digit on this page, and a clean result is not an approval by the tax authority.
