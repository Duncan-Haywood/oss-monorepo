Israel: allocation numbers, now from NIS 5,000

In Israel, per the sources read, a tax invoice above a threshold needs an allocation number from the Tax Authority before the buyer can deduct input VAT, and the threshold fell to NIS 5,000 before VAT on 1 June 2026. Below: how it works as secondary summaries report it, where the sources are thin, and a local check of an ID or company number’s check digit.

How it works (as secondary summaries report)

StepWhat happens
IdentifyThe seller and buyer are identified by an authorised dealer, company or personal ID number of up to 9 digits.
RequestFor a tax invoice above the threshold, the seller’s system asks the Tax Authority’s SHAAM service for an allocation number before the invoice is issued.
IssueThe number is printed or embedded on the invoice. Reported consequence: without it the buyer’s input VAT deduction is at risk (VAT Law section 38(a1)).
ThresholdReported steps, before VAT: NIS 25,000 (2024), NIS 20,000 (2025), NIS 10,000 (1 January 2026), NIS 5,000 (1 June 2026), earlier than the 2028 date first planned.
Dealers’ unionsOne summary reports that from 1 July 2026 transactions between members of a dealers’ union also need numbers, with a consolidated monthly invoice option until 1 July 2027.

Reported by KPMG TaxNewsFlash, Sovos, Banqup, vatupdate.com and Israeli law-firm excerpts, read through web search only; VAT Execution Directive 01/2025 and the Tax Authority’s own pages were not read. Where it is unsettled: the exact scope of exempt transactions, and the dealers’ union dates, rest on single secondary sources.

Check an ID or company number

Tests the shape (5 to 9 digits; spaces and hyphens are ignored) and the check digit. It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the number is registered, active, or belongs to the name on the invoice.

The rule (digits weighted 1 and 2 alternately, the digits of each product added, total a multiple of 10) is the one the python-stdnum library documents for Israeli IDs; it reproduces 000000018 by hand. It was not read from the Tax Authority’s specification.

What this checker does and does not cover

The main checker reads UBL, CII and Factur-X and applies EN 16931 and Peppol rules. It does not call the Israeli Tax Authority’s service, cannot obtain or verify an allocation number, and has no Israeli schema or codes. Treat the ID test here as a typo catch only.

Common questions

What is an Israeli allocation number?

Under the invoice reform, a seller asks the Israel Tax Authority (SHAAM) for an allocation number for a tax invoice above a threshold before issuing it, and the number is shown on the invoice. Summaries say the buyer's input VAT deduction depends on the invoice carrying one (VAT Law section 38(a1)).

What is the threshold?

Summaries from law firms and vendors report that the threshold, before VAT, was NIS 25,000 from 2024, NIS 20,000 in 2025, NIS 10,000 from 1 January 2026 and NIS 5,000 from 1 June 2026, brought forward by VAT Execution Directive 01/2025. The directive itself was not read; check the Tax Authority before relying on a date.

What is a valid Israeli ID or company number?

A personal ID, company number or dealer number has up to 9 digits, padded with leading zeros. Multiply the digits by 1 and 2 alternately from the left, add the digits of each product, and the total must be a multiple of 10. A matching result does not mean the number is registered or belongs to the name on the invoice.

Can this checker validate an Israeli invoice?

No. It does not call SHAAM, cannot request or verify an allocation number, and has no Israeli rules. It tests an ID's shape and check digit on this page, and a clean result is not an approval by the Tax Authority.
