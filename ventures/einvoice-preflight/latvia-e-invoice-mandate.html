Latvia's e-invoice mandate: B2B moved to 2028

Latvia has required e-invoices for sales to state budget bodies since 1 January 2025, but the domestic business-to-business mandate was postponed from 1 January 2026 to 1 January 2028. Below: the dates as sources give them, where they disagree, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (from the Law on Accounting and Cabinet Regulation No. 749)

DateWhat changes
1 Jan 2025Article 11(14) of the Law on Accounting applies to invoices to budget institutions (B2G): the invoice must be a structured e-invoice. For contracts signed by 31 December 2024 the law allows until 1 January 2026 (transitional provision 9).
1 Jan 2026Businesses that invoice budget institutions must send the structured e-invoice data to the State Revenue Service (transitional provision 10). Other businesses may send theirs voluntarily (Regulation No. 749, point 18). The B2B date first set for this day was postponed to 2028.
30 Mar 2026Businesses can voluntarily exchange B2B e-invoices through the eAddress system on latvija.lv, per one vendor guide. Not in the law or the regulation; not confirmed here that it is live.
1 Jan 2028An invoice to any other business registered in Latvia must be a structured e-invoice (transitional provision 8, as amended on 5 June 2025), and its data must reach the State Revenue Service (transitional provision 10).

Read from the primary texts on likumi.lv (the official publisher Latvijas Vēstnesis's consolidated versions) on 2026-11-14: the Law on Accounting (Grāmatvedības likums, version in force from 27 March 2026), its 5 June 2025 amending law, and Cabinet Regulation No. 749 of 9 December 2025 (in force 13 December 2025). Exceptions (Article 11(16)): sales evidenced by a cash-register receipt, invoices generated in the National Health Service's or the State Employment Agency's systems, and some security and police bodies. Delivery (Regulation No. 749, point 4): the official eAddress, a service provider's channel, or another agreed channel, email included; the parties choose. Penalty: Article 43 sets a warning or a fine of up to 86 fine units for breaking the rules on issuing supporting documents, and one fine unit is five euros (Law on Administrative Liability, Article 16), so up to 430 euros; the law does not name e-invoicing in that article, so whether it is the article applied is our reading. One vendor guide still lists 1 January 2027 for B2B; the law says 2028. A summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts, and how it travels

Regulation No. 749 says the XML follows UBL 2.1 and Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 (on EN 16931). It travels through the national eAddress, a service provider (such as a Peppol access point) or another channel the parties agree, and each e-invoice goes to the State Revenue Service once, within five working days of being sent: automatically when eAddress is used, otherwise through the provider's link to the State Revenue Service's API or its own upload and API. A PDF emailed to a customer is no longer the invoice for domestic B2B sales from 2028.

Common questions

When does Latvia's B2B e-invoicing mandate start?

1 January 2028, by the Law on Accounting itself (transitional provision 8, read in Latvian on likumi.lv on 2026-11-14). The Saeima adopted the amending law on 5 June 2025; it was published in Latvijas Vēstnesis No. 111 on 11 June and took effect on 12 June 2025, moving the date from 1 January 2026. The data duty moved with it: transitional provision 10 makes businesses that invoice state budget bodies send structured e-invoice data to the State Revenue Service from 1 January 2026, and every other business from 1 January 2028. Under Cabinet Regulation No. 749 each e-invoice goes to the State Revenue Service once, within five working days of sending it. E-invoicing for sales to budget bodies has applied since 1 January 2025. This is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice format does Latvia use?

Cabinet Regulation No. 749 of 9 December 2025 (point 7) says the XML the State Revenue Service receives follows UBL 2.1 and the Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 specification, which is built on EN 16931. This checker reads JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X PDFs and has a Peppol BIS 3.0 option. It does not check the delivery to the State Revenue Service.

What does this checker not cover for Latvia?

Latvian national rules beyond EN 16931 and Peppol, the structured data submission to the State Revenue Service, and delivery through eAddress. A clean result means the invoice passes EN 16931 and the Peppol rules it knows, not that it will be accepted in Latvia.

How do I check a Latvian invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker and tick the Peppol option. It tests the Peppol identifiers, electronic addresses, totals and tax breakdown arithmetic, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page. It is an offline observation, not an access point's acceptance and not legal advice.
