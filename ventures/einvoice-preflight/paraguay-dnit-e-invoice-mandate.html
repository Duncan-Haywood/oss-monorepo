Paraguay: the SIFEN is phasing in electronic invoices

In Paraguay, per the sources read, the tax authority DNIT (formerly the SET) runs the SIFEN, the national electronic invoicing system, and brings taxpayers in by group on dates set in resolutions. Below: how it works as vendor guides and press report it, where the sources are thin, and a local check of a RUC’s check digit.

How it works (as vendor guides, press and the authority report)

StepWhat happens
IdentifyThe issuer has a RUC: a base number (a cédula for people, 8 digits starting 80 for companies) and a check digit after a hyphen.
Issue a documentThe issuer produces an electronic document (DE, such as an invoice) as signed XML in DNIT’s format and sends it to the SIFEN for approval (vendor guides; the technical manual is unread). One guide describes a 44-digit control code (CDC) on each document.
DeliverThe buyer receives the document, usually as a printable representation (KuDE) with a QR code, per vendor guides.
VerifyDNIT has a public RUC lookup at dnit.gov.py; this page does not use it.

Dates as reported by press (ABC Color, May 2026) and a vendor calendar: groups start on 1 June 2026, 1 September 2026, 1 December 2026, 2 March 2027, 1 June 2027 and 1 September 2027; suppliers to the State from 2 January 2026. Where it is unsettled: the resolution itself (RG 52) was not read, the number of the State-supplier rule differs between sources (RG 41/2025 or RG 21/2024), the legal base is said to include Decree 872/2023, and which group a given company is in must be checked with DNIT. No penalty is stated because none was read. A summary, not tax advice.

Check a RUC

Tests the shape (5 to 9 digits and a check digit; spaces, dots and hyphens are ignored) and the check digit. It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the RUC is registered, active, or belongs to the name on the invoice.

The rule (digits of the base weighted 2,3,4,… from the right, back to 2 after 11; the check digit is 11 minus the sum mod 11, or 0 when the remainder is 0 or 1) is the one open-source implementations and vendor validators describe. It was not read from DNIT’s specification, and no registered RUC with a confirmed digit was reachable, so the sample (80016875-5) was computed by hand and may not be a registered RUC.

Common questions

What is the SIFEN?

The Sistema Integrado de Facturación Electrónica Nacional, run by the tax authority, DNIT (formerly the SET). Issuers send electronic documents (DE) in DNIT’s XML format for approval, and one vendor guide describes a 44-digit control code (CDC) on each. This is a summary from vendor guides and press, not advice; confirm with DNIT or your accountant.

Who has to issue electronic invoices?

Press reports say a DNIT resolution (RG 52, read only through news excerpts) named new issuers in six groups with start dates from 1 June 2026 to 1 September 2027, and that suppliers to the State must issue electronically from 2 January 2026. Sources disagree on which resolution number sets the State rule (RG 41/2025 or RG 21/2024), and the dates were not read in the resolution itself, so check dnit.gov.py.

What is a valid RUC?

A base number and a check digit that follows from it by a mod 11 rule. A matching digit does not mean the RUC is registered or active; DNIT’s public register answers that. The test on this page catches typos only.

Can this checker validate a Paraguayan invoice?

No. It does not read DNIT’s XML schema and cannot obtain or verify an approval. It does test a RUC’s shape and check digit on this page, and a clean result is not an approval by DNIT.
