Saudi Arabia e-invoicing: ZATCA waves and what to check first

Saudi e-invoicing (Fatoora) has applied to every VAT-registered business since 4 December 2021. Its integration phase reaches businesses in waves by revenue, and wave 25 starts on 1 February 2027. Below: the phases, how clearance differs from reporting, and what a free local checker covers.

Phases and waves

DateWhat happens
4 Dec 2021Phase 1 (generation): structured electronic invoices with a QR code for VAT-registered businesses.
1 Jan 2023Phase 2 (integration) begins with wave 1, businesses with 2021 revenue above SAR 3 billion; later waves step the revenue threshold down.
1 Jan to 31 Mar 2026Wave 23, as reported: businesses above SAR 750,000 in the test year.
By 30 Jun 2026Wave 24, as reported: businesses roughly between SAR 375,000 and 750,000.
1 Feb 2027Wave 25, per a ZATCA Governor decision published in the Official Gazette in July 2026; the first to use calendar-year 2025 revenue.

Dates come from EY tax alerts and adviser sites, not from ZATCA's own notices, and they disagree on the revenue test for wave 24 (the years counted and the bracket). Each business is told its wave by ZATCA, usually about six months ahead, and must integrate by the end of the window. Penalty amounts are set in the e-invoicing regulations and are not stated here; check ZATCA. This is a summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts, and how it travels

Invoices are structured XML on UBL 2.1 with ZATCA's own fields and rules, carrying a QR code and a cryptographic stamp. Standard invoices (to businesses and government) are cleared: ZATCA validates and stamps them before you give them to the buyer. Simplified invoices (to consumers) are reported within 24 hours. A PDF alone is not the tax invoice. ZATCA's validation is the one that decides.

Common questions

When does Saudi e-invoicing apply to me?

Phase 1 (generation) has applied since 4 December 2021: VAT-registered businesses issue structured electronic invoices with a QR code, not handwritten or word-processor ones. Phase 2 (integration with ZATCA's Fatoora platform) is rolled out in waves chosen by revenue, announced about six months ahead. As reported by EY and adviser sites: wave 23 was 1 January to 31 March 2026, wave 24 was due by 30 June 2026 and wave 25 starts on 1 February 2027 and is the first to use calendar-year 2025 revenue. Find your wave in your ZATCA notification and confirm with ZATCA or your accountant; this page is a summary, not advice.

What is the difference between clearance and reporting?

Under phase 2, standard (business-to-business and business-to-government) tax invoices are cleared: they are sent to ZATCA for validation and stamping before they go to the buyer. Simplified invoices (sales to consumers) are reported to ZATCA, within 24 hours of issue. Both carry a QR code and a cryptographic stamp.

What format does ZATCA use?

Structured XML based on UBL 2.1, with ZATCA's own data elements and validation rules, signed and submitted through a Fatoora-integrated system. It is not plain EN 16931 and not Peppol, so a clean result in a generic EN 16931 checker does not mean ZATCA will accept the file.

Can this checker validate a Saudi invoice?

Partly. It reads UBL and JSON and applies EN 16931 style arithmetic, tax breakdown, mandatory-field and identifier checks, plus IBAN and BIC typo catches, each with a rule ID and a fix. It does not apply ZATCA's rules, QR code, signature or clearance checks, so a clean result is an offline observation, not approval. ZATCA's own validation remains the authority.
