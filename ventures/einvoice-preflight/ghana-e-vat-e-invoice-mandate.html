Ghana: the GRA E-VAT system

Ghana's VAT-registered businesses are being moved onto the Ghana Revenue Authority's E-VAT system in phases. Below: what guides report, where they disagree, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelInvoices are issued through the GRA's E-VAT system, which validates and records them with the authority. A Ghanaian clearance model, not EN 16931.
PhasesGuides report a launch in October 2022 and mandatory use from 1 July 2023 for about 650 selected large taxpayers, then onboarding of about 2,000 more from September 2024. One aggregator lists the whole B2B mandate as in force from 1 October 2022, which conflicts with the phased accounts.
2026Reports say the GRA intends to extend E-VAT to medium (tier 2) taxpayers from 1 November 2026. The date and scope are not confirmed by a GRA announcement in anything read.
Check firstThe GRA's E-VAT page and notices. Whether you are already in a wave depends on your taxpayer segment.

What this checker does and does not cover

The main checker reads UBL, CII and Factur-X and applies EN 16931 and Peppol rules. It does not apply local rules, sign an invoice or call the GRA system. It is still useful on the numbers: wrong totals, a tax breakdown that does not add up and mistyped identifiers are faults any clearance system rejects.

Common questions

What is E-VAT in Ghana?

The Ghana Revenue Authority's electronic VAT invoicing system. Invoices are issued and reported through it rather than as free-form files.

Is it mandatory for every business?

Not on the evidence read. It was rolled out in phases, large taxpayers first. A wider extension from 1 November 2026 is reported but unconfirmed.

Can this checker validate an E-VAT invoice?

No. It has no Ghanaian rules and does not connect to the GRA. For UBL or CII it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not GRA acceptance.
