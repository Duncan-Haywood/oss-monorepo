Argentina: ARCA gives each invoice a CAE before you hand it over

In Argentina, per the guides read, an issuer sends each invoice’s data to the tax authority, ARCA (formerly AFIP), through its WSFEv1 web service and gets back a CAE (código de autorización electrónico) that must appear on the invoice. Below: how it works as vendor guides report it, where sources disagree, and a local check of a CUIT’s check digit.

How it works (as vendor guides report it)

StepWhat happens
Pick the typeInvoices come in letters: A (a registered taxpayer to another registered taxpayer), B (a registered taxpayer to a final consumer or non-registered buyer) and C (issued by small taxpayers such as monotributistas), among others.
Request authorisationThe issuer’s software authenticates to ARCA and sends the invoice data to WSFEv1, ARCA’s electronic-invoice web service. A homologation (test) environment exists apart from production.
Receive the CAEWhen validation passes, ARCA returns the CAE and its expiry date. Both go on the invoice, which also carries a QR code.
DeliverThe invoice with the CAE goes to the buyer as PDF or other agreed form. The issuer keeps the record.

Where it is unsettled: how long a CAE lasts (one vendor guide says typically 10 days from issue, another 15; neither was checked against an ARCA page), what an expired CAE does to the invoice (one source says the invoice stays valid and the expiry matters for the QR check, and that buyers’ systems may still reject it), and which taxpayers are obliged and from when. These are vendor guides and developer docs read through search excerpts, not ARCA resolutions. No penalty is stated because none was read. A summary, not tax advice.

Check a CUIT

Tests the shape (eleven digits, hyphens and spaces ignored) and the check digit. It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the CUIT is registered or belongs to the name on the invoice. The usual prefixes are 20, 23, 24 and 27 for people and 30, 33 and 34 for companies; the check does not enforce them. When the sum would give 10 there is no valid check digit, so no CUIT is issued like that and the test reports it.

The sample is ARCA’s own CUIT. The rule (weights 5 4 3 2 7 6 5 4 3 2 over the first ten digits, 11 minus the remainder mod 11, with 11 written as 0) reproduces it; it was not read from ARCA’s specification, and no CUIT is looked up. A single wrong digit is never missed (0 of 163,170 in a fixed sample), because the weights are never a multiple of 11.

Common questions

What is a CAE in Argentina?

The código de autorización electrónico: a code ARCA (formerly AFIP) returns after validating an invoice’s data through its web service, with an expiry date. It goes on the invoice. This is a summary from vendor guides, not advice; confirm with ARCA or your accountant.

How long is a CAE valid?

Sources disagree: one vendor guide says typically 10 days from issue, another 15. One says the invoice stays valid after the CAE date and the date matters for the QR check, though third-party systems may still reject it. Check ARCA.

What is the difference between Factura A, B and C?

As vendor guides describe them: A goes from a registered taxpayer (responsable inscripto) to another registered taxpayer, B from a registered taxpayer to a final consumer or non-registered buyer, and C is issued by small taxpayers such as monotributistas. The WSFEv1 specification lists the full set of codes.

Can this checker validate an Argentine invoice?

No. It does not call WSFEv1 or hold a CAE. It does test a CUIT’s shape and check digit on this page, and a clean result is not an authorisation by ARCA.
