Ethiopia: Directive 1142/2026

Ethiopia's Ministry of Revenues has put a legal framework for electronic invoicing in place, but sources read do not show a compliance schedule yet. Below: what guides report and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

How it works (as secondary guides report)

TopicWhat the sources say
ModelInvoices are registered through an Electronic Invoicing System and carry an invoice registration number and a QR code. A compliant sales register system within that framework is described as mandatory for taxpayers who must keep books. Not EN 16931.
Legal basisElectronic Invoicing Directive No. 1142/2026 of the Ministry of Revenues, reported at 31 articles and replacing the limited 2018 provisions. Sources disagree on the date: one says issued 9 June 2026, another published on the Ministry website on 1 July 2026 and effective on publication.
Who and whenOn a schedule the authority issues (an Accreditation Board duty in one guide). Guides say it has not been published. Edicom describes December 2026 as a rollout target, not a general deadline.
Before the directiveEthiopian taxpayers have used fiscal machines (electronic cash registers) under earlier rules; the new framework is a different, registration-based model. Details not verified.

Written on 9 November 2026 from web search summaries and vendor guides (Edicom, VATupdate, VATCalc, LookupTax), not from the Ministry of Revenues directive or the Negarit Gazette, none of which were read. Dates may have changed; the implementation schedule is the document to watch.

Common questions

What is Directive 1142/2026?

A standalone Ministry of Revenues framework for electronic invoicing, reported at 31 articles, that replaces the limited 2018 provisions. Invoices are to be registered through the Electronic Invoicing System with a registration number and QR code.

Is there a deadline yet?

Not that the sources agree on. Guides say the implementation schedule, which designates who must comply and when, is still to be issued. One vendor mentions December 2026 as a rollout target, not a general deadline. Check the Ministry of Revenues.

Who must comply?

Reportedly taxpayers obliged to keep books of accounts, on a schedule the authority issues. The first designated groups are not yet published in the sources read.

Can this checker validate an Ethiopian e-invoice?

No. It has no Ethiopian rules and does not connect to the Electronic Invoicing System. For UBL or CII it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults. A clean result is not Ministry acceptance.
