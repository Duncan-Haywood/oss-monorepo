Peru: every invoice comes back from SUNAT with a CDR receipt

In Peru, per the guides read, a factura or boleta is a comprobante de pago electrónico (CPE): signed XML sent to the tax authority, SUNAT, or to an operator it licenses (an OSE), which answers with a constancia de recepción (CDR) saying accepted, accepted with observations or rejected. Below: how it works as SUNAT pages and vendor guides report it, where sources disagree, and a local check of a RUC’s check digit.

How it works (as vendor guides report it)

StepWhat happens
Issue and signThe issuer builds the voucher as XML (UBL; sources differ on the version, see below) and signs it with a digital certificate. Facturas and boletas are separate voucher types, each with its own series.
SendThe XML goes to SUNAT, or to an OSE (operador de servicios electrónicos) the issuer chose. Web-service methods named in SUNAT material are SendBill (one voucher, answered at once) and SendSummary (a batch, answered later).
Receive the CDRSUNAT or the OSE returns a CDR, a signed receipt that marks the voucher accepted, accepted with observations or rejected. SUNAT says a rejected voucher has no tax validity and the issuer must issue a new one with the faults corrected.
Boletas in a daily summarySales receipts (boletas) and their notes are reported to SUNAT in a Resumen Diario for the day they were issued, rather than one by one.

Where it is unsettled: the UBL version (a 2016 SUNAT homologation manual rests on UBL 2.0; a third-party library lists 2.1 for facturas and boletas), the deadline to send (one SUNAT source says three calendar days counted from the day after issue but does not say for which vouchers; the Resumen Diario deadline was not found), and which taxpayers are obliged and from when. These are SUNAT PDFs and vendor pages read through search excerpts, not the resolutions. No penalty is stated because none was read. A summary, not tax advice.

Check a RUC

Tests the shape (eleven digits) and the check digit. It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the RUC is registered, active or belongs to the name on the invoice. Spaces and hyphens are ignored. The first two digits say what kind of taxpayer it is (10 for a natural person and 20 for a company are the common ones); the check does not enforce them.

The sample is SUNAT’s own RUC. The rule (weights 5 4 3 2 7 6 5 4 3 2 over the first ten digits, 11 minus the remainder mod 11, with 10 written as 0 and 11 as 1) reproduces it; it was not read from SUNAT’s specification. Because 0 and 1 are each reached from two remainders, a RUC ending in 1 can hide a typo; in a fixed sample, 3,346 of 180,000 single-digit errors (1.9%) went unseen.

Common questions

What is a CPE in Peru?

A comprobante de pago electrónico: the electronic form of a factura, boleta or related note, issued as signed XML and reported to the tax authority (SUNAT) directly or through an operator (OSE). This is a summary from SUNAT pages and vendor guides, not advice; confirm with SUNAT or your accountant.

What is a CDR?

The constancia de recepción: the receipt SUNAT, or the OSE, returns after checking a voucher. It marks the voucher accepted, accepted with observations or rejected. SUNAT says a rejected voucher has no tax validity and must be reissued corrected.

What is an OSE?

An operador de servicios electrónicos: a provider licensed by SUNAT that receives vouchers from issuers, validates them and returns the CDR, so the issuer does not send to SUNAT itself. SUNAT material says the CDR’s signature must match the chosen OSE’s RUC.

Can this checker validate a Peruvian invoice?

No. It does not read SUNAT’s UBL profile, and cannot verify a signature or a CDR. It does test a RUC’s shape and check digit on this page, and a clean result is not an acceptance by SUNAT.
