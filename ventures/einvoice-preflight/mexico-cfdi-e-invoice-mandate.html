Mexico's CFDI: every invoice is stamped XML, and 4.0 checks the customer too

In Mexico, per the guides read, a tax invoice is a CFDI: an XML file that a SAT-authorised provider (a PAC) validates, stamps with a unique folio and reports to the tax authority before it counts. Version 4.0 requires the customer's RFC, exact legal name, postal code and tax regime. Below: how it works as vendor guides report it, where sources disagree, a local check of a CFDI 4.0 file against SAT's schema and catalogues, and an RFC check-digit test.

How it works (as vendor guides report it)

StepWhat happens
IssueThe issuer builds the CFDI XML (version 4.0, with complements such as payment or transport where they apply) and signs it with its tax certificate.
StampA PAC validates it against SAT's rules and catalogues, adds the digital stamp and the UUID folio, and reports it to SAT. Without the stamp it is not a valid invoice.
DeliverThe issuer sends the stamped XML (and usually a PDF) to the customer, who needs the XML to deduct the expense.
CancelCancelled with a reason code; for some cases the customer must accept. Replacing a CFDI points to the new one's folio.

Where it is unsettled: the dates of the 3.3 to 4.0 transition (search results disagreed on the start and on the grace period), the cancellation thresholds and time limits, and which complements are required for which sale. These are vendor blogs, not SAT or the Resolución Miscelánea Fiscal. No penalty is stated because none was read. A summary, not tax advice.

Check a CFDI 4.0 file

Choose a CFDI XML file (stamped or not). It is read in this page and never uploaded. Checks: the schema (order, required attributes, patterns, SAT catalogue codes), line amount = quantity x unit value within rounding, each tax = base x rate, the tax summary against the lines, subtotal, discount and total, currency decimals and exchange rate, PPD with payment form 99, and the general-public and foreign-receiver rules. A payment CFDI (type P) also gets its Pagos 2.0 complement read: SAT's Pagos20.xsd, each document's previous balance minus the amount paid against the balance left, EquivalenciaDR, Monto covering what the documents say was paid, the payment taxes (ImpuestosP) against the documents' taxes, and the peso totals (Totales).

Measured on planted defects: 91 of 92 caught by the rule named for them across four CFDI 4.0 files (the miss is the rounding slack an amount written without decimals gets). Payments (Pagos 2.0): 202 of 202 planted defects caught over ten type P files, no false finding on the nine public sample files from CfdiUtils' calculator tests, and 1,446 of 1,446 schema verdicts the same as xmllint on SAT's Pagos20.xsd. Not run on a PAC's rejection yet.

Check an RFC

Tests the length (12 for a company, 13 for a person), the letters, the YYMMDD date part and the check digit. It does not look anything up, so it cannot say whether the RFC is registered or matches a name. The two generic RFCs for sales to the public and to foreigners are accepted by shape. The check digit misses about 16% of single-character mistakes (60 of 385 on a sample company RFC), so a pass is not a confirmation.

The sample is SAT's own RFC. The check-digit rule (mod 11, weights 13 down to 2) reproduces four RFCs I know; it was not read from SAT's specification.

What this checker does and does not cover

The file check above reads CFDI 4.0 against SAT's published XSD and catalogues (via the public-domain phpcfdi mirror of sat.gob.mx), recomputes the sums, and checks each tax regime and CFDI use against the kind of RFC and the use against the receiver's regime, from SAT's catalogue spreadsheet. It does not verify the seal or stamp, check the RFC, name, postal code or regime against SAT's records, or read the foreign-trade complement. Payroll receipts (type N, Nómina 1.2) are checked against nomina12.xsd and the rules in SAT's payroll filling guide: the receipt's single concept, the totals by kind of perception and deduction, other payments, pay periods, the employment data and the CURP and CLABE check digits; not whether the ISR withheld or the subsidy owed is right. For payments it does not know whether the invoices paid exist, are PPD, or carry the balance claimed: that needs SAT's records. Rule ids MX-001 to MX-057 are this tool's own, not SAT's CFDI40xxx error codes. The main checker reads UBL, CII and Factur-X for European and Peppol invoices.

Common questions

What is a CFDI?

A CFDI (Comprobante Fiscal Digital por Internet) is Mexico's electronic tax invoice: an XML file the issuer sends to a PAC (a provider authorised by the tax authority, SAT), which validates it, adds a digital stamp with a unique folio (UUID) and reports it to SAT. A PDF is only a printed representation of the XML. This is a summary from vendor guides, not advice; confirm with SAT or your accountant.

What does CFDI 4.0 require about the customer?

Vendor guides report that version 4.0 makes the receptor block required: the customer's RFC, legal name as it appears on their tax-status certificate, postal code of their tax address and tax regime, plus the CFDI use code. A name or postal code that does not match SAT's record is reported as a reason the PAC rejects the stamp. Sources differ on the dates of the transition from 3.3, so confirm the dates with SAT.

How does cancelling a CFDI work?

As vendor guides report it, a CFDI is cancelled with one of four reason codes, and for some cancellations the receptor must accept the request, with a window of a few business days after which it is treated as accepted. Substitution points to the folio of the replacing CFDI. The exact rules, amount thresholds and time limits are in SAT's rules and were not read here.

Can this checker validate a Mexican CFDI 4.0 file?

Partly, offline. This page reads a CFDI 4.0 XML file in your browser and checks it against SAT's published schema (element order, required attributes, patterns and the catalogue codes in catCFDI.xsd, such as payment form, CFDI use, unit and product keys and postal codes), recomputes the line amounts, taxes, tax summary and total, applies a few well-known 4.0 rules for the general public and foreign receivers, and checks that the CFDI use is one SAT allows with the receiver's tax regime and for a person or a company. For a payment CFDI it checks the Pagos 2.0 complement: balances, conversions between currencies, the payment taxes and the totals in pesos. It does not verify the seal or the PAC stamp, does not look up the RFC, name or postal code in SAT's records and does not know each PAC's own rules, so a clean result is not an acceptance by a PAC or SAT.
