Japan's qualified invoice system: a T-number on every invoice, and a credit that halves in October 2026

Japan, per the guides read, has no clearance mandate. Since 1 October 2023 a buyer claims the consumption-tax input credit only on a qualified invoice from a registered seller, marked with a registration number: the letter T and 13 digits. Buyers' transitional credit for unregistered sellers falls from 80% to 50% on 1 October 2026. Below: the dates as vendor guides report them, where it is unsettled, and a local check for a T-number.

The timeline (as vendor and advisory guides report it)

DateWhat changes
1 Oct 2023Qualified invoice system starts. Input credit needs a qualified invoice from a registered seller; the seller's T-number must appear.
1 Oct 2023 to 30 Sep 2026Transitional: a buyer may claim 80% of input tax on purchases from unregistered sellers.
1 Oct 2026 to 30 Sep 2029Transitional credit falls to 50%. A 2026 tax reform is reported to revise the percentages and to cap relief for large purchases from one unregistered seller, for periods starting on or after 1 Oct 2026.
From 1 Oct 2029No credit without a qualified invoice.

Where it is unsettled: the 2026 revision (one adviser article; its thresholds were ambiguous in the excerpt read) and how the small-business 20% special rule for newly taxable sellers is treated after it. These are secondary sources, not the National Tax Agency (国税庁) or the law. No penalty is stated because none was found; the reported cost is the buyer's lost credit. Confirm against the agency. Check a registration number

Common questions

What is a Japanese qualified invoice?

Since 1 October 2023 a buyer can claim the consumption-tax input credit only with a qualified invoice (適格請求書), issued by a seller registered with the National Tax Agency. It carries the seller's registration number, the letter T followed by 13 digits, plus the tax rate and tax amount per rate. It may be paper or electronic. Japan has no clearance model: you issue and keep it. This is a summary from vendor and advisory guides, not advice.

What happens to the credit on 1 October 2026?

Vendor guides report a transitional measure: a buyer may still claim 80% of the input tax on purchases from sellers with no registration number until 30 September 2026, then 50% until 30 September 2029, then nothing. A 2026 tax reform is reported to revise these percentages and add a cap for large purchases from one unregistered seller. That revision was not read from the tax agency, so confirm the current rule there.

How is a registration number built?

For a corporation it is T plus its 13-digit corporate number, whose first digit is a check digit. An individual gets T plus 13 digits that are not a corporate number. The checker on this page tests the shape and the corporate check digit. A mismatch can be a typo or an individual's number, so it asks you to look the number up on the tax agency's registrant site. It cannot tell if a number is registered.

Can this checker validate a Japanese e-invoice?

Not fully. It tests the T-number shape and check digit on this page, and EN 16931 and Peppol rules in the main checker. It has no Peppol JP PINT profile or Japanese national rules. A clean result is not an acceptance by a buyer or by the tax agency, and not tax advice.
