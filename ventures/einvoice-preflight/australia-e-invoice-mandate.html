Australian e-invoicing: what applies now

Australia sends and receives e-invoices over Peppol, using the PINT A-NZ specification it shares with New Zealand. Unlike the EU countries and Singapore, there is no general business-to-business mandate; the requirement falls on federal government agencies. Below: the dates as reported, what we could not confirm, and how to test an invoice file before it goes out.

The timeline

DateWhat happened
1 Jan 2020Commonwealth pays eligible supplier invoices within five calendar days (reported: invoices up to A$1 million).
1 Jul 2022Non-corporate Commonwealth entities must be able to receive Peppol e-invoices; the five-day policy is reported as extended with the A$1 million cap removed.
15 May 2025PINT A-NZ reported as the only supported specification on the AU/NZ Peppol network (BIS Billing 3.0 retired).
1 Jul 2026Reported target: at least 30% of invoices received by those agencies arrive through Peppol.
Dec 2026Reported deadline: agencies enable automated processing and sending of Peppol e-invoices.

These dates come from commentators' summaries (ecosio, Invopop, Cygnet and others) and were not read on the ATO, Treasury or Department of Finance pages, so treat them as reported, not confirmed. One guide claims every business must send e-invoices on request under the Business eInvoicing Right; other sources, including one dated May 2026, say it was not enacted, and we follow those. No penalty applies to suppliers who do not use Peppol that we could find. Confirm with the ATO or your accountant.

How it travels

The invoice is sent through a Peppol access point, addressed to the buyer's Australian Business Number (ABN) or New Zealand Business Number. The ATO is the Peppol authority for the Australian network. A-NZ invoices follow the European standard EN 16931 model with Australian and New Zealand additions such as ABN and GST rules. Many accounting packages (Xero, MYOB and others) can send over Peppol without extra cost to the supplier; check your own provider's terms.

Test a file before you send it

Open the checker and drop your UBL XML. If its specification identifier (BT-24) names the A-NZ profile, the file is checked by OpenPeppol's PINT A-NZ rules, including the ABN rules, in your browser; any other file gets the EN 16931 core checks: totals and tax breakdown arithmetic, a missing tax reason on a zero-rated or exempt line, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix (the rule reference). A pass here is not an access point's acceptance. No account, nothing uploaded.

Common questions

Is e-invoicing mandatory in Australia?

Not for business-to-business invoices as far as we could confirm: sources agree sending a Peppol e-invoice is voluntary for suppliers, and the proposed Business eInvoicing Right was reported as not enacted. It is a requirement on federal government agencies, which have had to be able to receive Peppol e-invoices since 1 July 2022. Confirm with the ATO or your accountant; this page is a summary, not advice.

What format does Australian e-invoicing use?

Peppol, using the PINT A-NZ specification shared with New Zealand (Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 is reported as retired from the AU/NZ network, with PINT A-NZ the only supported specification from 15 May 2025). When an invoice's specification identifier (BT-24) names the A-NZ profile, this checker runs OpenPeppol's A-NZ rules, including the ABN rules, in your browser.

Do government agencies pay e-invoices faster?

Yes, reportedly: the Commonwealth pays eligible Peppol e-invoices within five calendar days, a policy that began for invoices up to A$1 million in 2020 and was reported as extended without the cap from 1 July 2022. Check the Treasury or Department of Finance guidance for the terms that apply to your contract.

What can this checker do for an Australian invoice?

Drop the file into the checker: it tests the totals and tax breakdown arithmetic against the EN 16931 core rules, each failure with a rule ID and a fix. For a UBL invoice that names the A-NZ profile it runs the PINT A-NZ rules instead, including ABN and GST checks; it is still not an access point's acceptance.
