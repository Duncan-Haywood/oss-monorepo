Slovakia's e-invoice mandate: what to expect from 2027

Slovakia is replacing its IS EFA portal with a Peppol-style network and near real-time reporting. Domestic B2B and B2G e-invoicing is reported to become mandatory on 1 January 2027. Below: the dates as sources give them, where they disagree, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (as vendor guides report it)

DateWhat changes
2026The new law (Law No. 385/2025) is in force and e-invoicing through the new network is voluntary. Sources give 1 January 2026 for the law and early or mid 2026 for the voluntary start.
1 Jan 2027Domestic B2B and B2G e-invoicing becomes mandatory, invoices travel between accredited Peppol access points, and the current IS EFA portal is retired.
1 Jul 2030Cross-border intra-EU transactions, in line with the EU ViDA package.

Where sources disagree: the European Commission's country page still describes B2B as voluntary and IS EFA as replaced in 2027, which reads as older text; one vendor mentions a 1 July 2027 limit to authorised access points that this page could not confirm. These are vendor and secondary sources, not the statute. Confirm against Law 385/2025 and the Financial Administration of the Slovak Republic. A summary, not tax advice.

Which file counts, and how it travels

Guides describe Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 (UBL 2.1, on EN 16931) with a Slovak CIUS, sent through an accredited access point, with transaction data reported to the tax authority. A digital signature is said not to be required. A PDF emailed to the customer would no longer be the invoice for domestic B2B sales.

Common questions

When does Slovakia's B2B e-invoicing mandate start?

Per vendor guides, domestic B2B and B2G e-invoicing becomes mandatory on 1 January 2027, with a voluntary phase first (sources give early or mid 2026) and the current IS EFA portal retired. Cross-border intra-EU obligations are reported to follow on 1 July 2030, in line with the EU ViDA package. The sources are vendor blogs and the European Commission's page still shows older text, so confirm against Law 385/2025 and the Financial Administration. This is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice format does Slovakia use?

Vendor guides describe Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 (UBL 2.1, on EN 16931) with a Slovak CIUS, exchanged between accredited Peppol access points ('digital postmen'), with transaction data reported to the Financial Administration. This checker reads JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X PDFs and has a Peppol BIS 3.0 option. It does not apply the Slovak CIUS or check the reporting document.

What does this checker not cover for Slovakia?

The Slovak CIUS rules, the near real-time tax data report to the Financial Administration, and accreditation of your access point. A clean result means the invoice passes EN 16931 and the Peppol rules it knows, not that it will be accepted in Slovakia.

How do I check a Slovak invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker and tick the Peppol option. It tests the Peppol identifiers, electronic addresses, totals and tax breakdown arithmetic, and VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, each with a rule ID and a fix. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page. It is an offline observation, not an access point's acceptance and not legal advice.
