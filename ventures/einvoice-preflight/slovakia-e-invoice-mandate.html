Slovakia's e-invoice mandate: what Act 385/2025 says

From 1 January 2027 a Slovak VAT payer must issue a structured e-invoice for domestic sales to businesses and legal persons, and everyone it invoices that way must be able to receive one through a certified delivery service. From 1 July 2030 the e-invoice becomes the default for every invoice, with reporting to the Financial Directorate. Read from the Act on slov-lex.sk and the Financial Administration's guidance on 16 November 2026.

Who issues, who receives

Issue (from 1 January 2027): VAT payers registered under § 4, § 4b or § 4c (VAT groups included), for domestic supplies to a taxable person with a seat, place of business, establishment or residence in Slovakia, or to a non-taxable legal person established there. Consumers are not covered. Supplies exempt under §§ 28 to 43 and 47 are left out, and so are the simplified invoices of § 74(3)(a) and (b). Supplies to the Slovak Information Service or Military Intelligence, or involving classified information, must not be e-invoiced.

Receive: every person to whom such an e-invoice must be issued has to be able to receive it through a delivery service (§ 71(5)). The Financial Administration's step-by-step guide spells it out: businesses that are not VAT payers, municipalities, associations and other non-taxable legal persons must be ready to receive; they do not have to issue.

Consent: issuing an e-invoice needs no consent. Sending it any other way than through a delivery service, by email for example, needs the recipient's consent, and the file must still be the structured invoice.

Penalties: up to EUR 10,000 for data not reported, late or wrong, and up to EUR 100,000 if repeated; none for an obvious error that is corrected, or for a proven outage at the delivery service when the data is sent as soon as it is fixed (§ 85o(12) to (14)). E-invoices are kept for 10 years from the end of the year they relate to.

Which file counts, and how it travels

An e-invoice is a document with the § 74(1) particulars, made, sent and received in a format that allows automated processing and in a data structure under the European standard and its list of syntaxes (Commission Implementing Decision 2017/1870). In practice that is EN 16931 in UBL 2.1 or CII D16B, the two the Financial Administration names. A PDF is not one. Slovak invoices are usually sent as Peppol BIS Billing 3.0.

It travels through a certified delivery service ("digitálny poštár") on the Financial Directorate's register. The Act asks for a European delivery standard run on a not-for-profit basis and used in at least half the member states; the Financial Administration says that standard is OpenPeppol's. Its list of 12 November 2026 has 100 certified providers, Slovak and foreign. The Financial Administration's field table (v1.13, 29 October 2026) makes both electronic addresses (BT-34, BT-49) required and names scheme 0245 = DIČ for Slovak parties.

Not confirmed here: the "1 July 2027" access-point limit one vendor mentioned is not in the Act. Vendor guides say the old IS EFA system is retired; the Act repeals §§ 3 to 6 of Act 215/2019 from 1 January 2027, a text this page did not read in its earlier form.

Common questions

When does Slovakia's B2B e-invoicing mandate start?

On 1 January 2027. Act No. 385/2025 Z. z. inserts § 85o into the VAT Act: from that day a Slovak VAT payer that supplies goods or services with a place of supply in Slovakia to a taxable person or a non-taxable legal person established in Slovakia must issue an e-invoice. Exempt supplies and simplified invoices are left out. The general rule that every invoice is an e-invoice, with reporting that replaces the control statement, follows on 1 July 2030. This is a reading of the Act, not advice.

Which invoice format does Slovakia use?

The Act asks for a structured invoice under the European standard EN 16931 and one of its listed syntaxes (Commission Implementing Decision 2017/1870); the Financial Administration names UBL 2.1 or CII D16B. It is sent through a certified delivery service, and the Financial Administration says the European delivery standard that qualifies is OpenPeppol's. Its field table names electronic address scheme 0245 (the DIČ) for Slovak parties.

Do I have to be able to receive e-invoices if I am not a VAT payer?

Yes, from 1 January 2027. Every person to whom a supplier must issue an e-invoice has to be able to receive it through a delivery service (§ 71(5) of the VAT Act as amended). The Financial Administration's guide says this covers businesses that are not VAT payers and non-taxable legal persons such as municipalities and associations. Consumers are not covered.

What does this checker cover for Slovakia?
