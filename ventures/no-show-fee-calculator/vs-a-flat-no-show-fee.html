No-Show Fee Calculator vs. guessing a flat dollar fee

Guessing a flat fee is the default method, because it needs no calculator: pick a round number that sounds reasonable — $20, $25, $50 — and charge it for every no-show, whatever the missed appointment was worth. It works for exactly the services priced near that number. It stops working the moment your price list spans more than one tier, because a flat number carries no information about what any specific session costs — a $200 session and a $35 session lose it at the same rate.

Common questions

What's wrong with just picking a round number like $25?

Nothing, at the one price where $25 happens to be the fee you would have calculated anyway. Below that price it charges more than your own policy would justify; above it, it charges too little to matter. A flat number doesn't know what your service costs, so it can only be right by coincidence.

Can you show it with real numbers?

A $200 massage session and a $35 haircut both run a 50%-of-price cancellation policy. The calculator prices that at $100 for the massage and $17.50 for the haircut — each half the session's own value. A flat $25 guess charges 12.5% of the massage price (too little to offset a $200 loss) and 71% of the haircut price (an aggressive fee for a $35 service). Same guessed number, opposite problem at each end.

Does the gap actually add up to real money?

At 25 bookings a week, an 8% no-show rate, and 70% enforcement, the massage studio's own 50%-of-price policy collects an estimated $7,280 a year against $20,800 of exposure. The same no-shows billed at a flat $25 collect an estimated $1,820 — $5,460 a year left uncollected, not because enforcement failed but because the fee itself was priced for a cheaper service.

Is a price-proportional fee always the right call?

Not automatically — a percentage that feels fair for one service can feel steep for another even at the same price, and this tool does not know your local market or client relationships. What it does is show you the actual dollar consequence of the percentage you pick, at your own price, instead of leaving you to guess a flat number that was never priced against your service at all.
