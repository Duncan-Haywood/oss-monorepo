This calculator vs. Acuity Scheduling

Different jobs: one holds the appointment, the other decides what the missed appointment is worth.

Acuity Scheduling is a well-known appointment-scheduling platform, described on acuityscheduling.com — a booking page clients use themselves, calendar sync, automated reminders, intake forms, and, through a connected payment processor, collecting payment when someone books. It is the kind of tool a trainer, salon, tutor or therapist runs their whole diary through, and the place a cancellation policy is shown to clients when they book.

That is a different job from the one this page does. This calculator has no booking page, no calendar, no client records and no account. It exists for the step that comes before you fill in any of those settings: turning your session price, weekly bookings, no-show rate, notice window and honest waitlist refill rate into a specific fee, an estimate of what the empty slots cost you over a year, and a policy paragraph you can paste into whichever booking system you already use.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Acuity Scheduling?

Both, for different jobs. Acuity Scheduling is an appointment-scheduling platform: a booking page clients use themselves, calendar sync, automated reminders, intake forms, and, through a connected payment processor, collecting payment when someone books. This page is none of that. It has no booking page, no calendar, no client list and no account. It answers the one question a scheduling platform asks you rather than answers: what number should the fee be. Put your session price, weekly bookings, no-show rate, notice window and honest waitlist refill rate in, and it returns a specific fee, what the empty slots cost you over a year, and a policy paragraph you can paste into whichever booking system you already use.

Where does the number in a cancellation policy come from?

From you. A cancellation setting in any booking system is a field: a percentage or amount, and how much notice a client has to give. The software then carries that policy and applies it the same way every time, which is genuinely the hard part of collecting anything. What it cannot do is know your refill rate, so the number itself is still yours to decide. That is the gap this calculator fills, and it is why the two sit next to each other rather than competing.
