Canonical Tag Checker vs. Screaming Frog SEO Spider

These solve different problems and are not in competition with each other. This checker answers one narrow question fast, for free, with no install: does this one URL's canonical tag actually check out? Screaming Frog SEO Spider answers a much bigger question: what does every URL on this site look like, across dozens of SEO signals at once?

Common questions

Is this a replacement for Screaming Frog SEO Spider?

No. Screaming Frog SEO Spider crawls an entire site and reports on dozens of SEO signals at once -- titles, meta descriptions, status codes, redirect chains, hreflang, structured data, and canonicals among them. This checker does one thing for one URL at a time: fetch it, read its canonical tag(s), and follow the canonical to check it actually resolves. It is a narrow, free, scriptable gate, not a crawler.

When should I use this instead of a full crawl?

When you want a fast, no-install answer for one URL -- checking a single page before or after a deploy, or wiring a canonical-tag regression gate into your own CI against a staging URL -- without installing desktop software or running a crawl of the whole site.

When should I use Screaming Frog instead?

When you need the whole picture: every URL on a site, cross-referenced against sitemaps, internal links, and dozens of other SEO signals at once, with a desktop UI for exploring the results. That is a fundamentally different job from checking one URL's canonical evidence, and Screaming Frog is a well-established, purpose-built tool for it.

Do the two tools check the same things?

Canonical tags are one of many checks a full Screaming Frog crawl covers. This tool covers only that one signal, in more focused depth for a single URL: it also follows the canonical to its own destination and reports whether that destination actually resolves, redirects, or is itself marked noindex -- evidence a same-page-only canonical audit would not surface.
