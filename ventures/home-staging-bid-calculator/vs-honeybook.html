This calculator vs. HoneyBook

Two different shapes: HoneyBook is an account-based platform for running a creative or home-services business's client relationships end to end, including sending the contract and collecting the deposit once a job is priced. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this staging job cost, and does the extension fee actually cover what an extra month of carrying the furniture package really costs, before you send the number to the seller.

What each tool is for

HoneyBook is a client relationship and business management platform used by creative and home-services businesses — photographers, event planners, interior designers, and home stagers among them — for branded proposals, contracts with e-signatures, online invoicing and payments, scheduling, and a client-facing hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. HoneyBook's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific feature list, plan names, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of CRM describe sending a proposal, collecting an e-signature and deposit, and invoicing the balance once a price already exists; none describe working out whether a vacant-listing staging price set for a two-month included term actually covers the furniture package's own monthly carrying cost, or whether an extension fee is set high enough to still turn a profit on a listing that runs long — which is what this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what HoneyBook does. It does not send a contract, collect an e-signature or deposit, or invoice a client. It takes rooms staged, the included term, furniture carrying cost, crew labor and mileage, equipment cost, a design consultation fee, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into the price for one vacant-listing job — plus a same-page check of whether the extension fee charged for every month beyond the included term actually clears the furniture package's own carrying cost, and a reconciliation once the listing's real time on market is known. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or HoneyBook?

They do different jobs, and a staging business of any size will likely end up touching both. HoneyBook is a client relationship and business management platform used by creative and home-services businesses -- including home stagers -- for proposals, contracts, online invoicing and payments, scheduling, and a client-facing hub, tied to one account used across every job the company runs. This calculator does one narrower thing: it takes rooms staged, the included term, furniture carrying cost, crew labor and mileage, the equipment cost, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a price for one vacant-listing job, then flags whether the extension fee charged for months beyond the included term actually covers what that extra month costs to keep the furniture package off the market. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A stager already sending contracts, collecting deposits, and invoicing an ongoing client list is better served by HoneyBook or a comparable CRM; a stager who wants an instant, private answer to what one staging job should cost, and whether the extension rate actually protects margin on a slow listing, is in the right place here.
