This calculator vs. a flat per-room rate

“$X a room, however long it takes” is not a price for a staging job — it is a price for install day, quietly stretched over however many months the furniture ends up sitting in the house.

What a flat per-room rate is

A flat per-room number is the fastest way to quote a vacant-listing job over the phone: count the rooms, multiply, and send the number. Sellers and agents like it because it is easy to compare across stagers, and it is the number a shopper holding a competitor's quote will ask you to match.

The number behind it is usually set for install day — the crew hours, the mileage, the furniture going in — and then charged the same whether the listing sells in three weeks or sits on the market for a season, because a flat per-room rate has no line that changes with time.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter rooms staged, an included term, your furniture package's carrying cost per month, crew labor and mileage, equipment, overhead, and target margin. It prices the included term on its own cost, then checks whether the extension fee you charge past that term actually covers what the furniture keeps costing you to hold — storage, insurance, and wear — every month the listing does not sell. You get a price built on how long the furniture is actually deployed, not a flat number that assumes it away.

The same job, priced two ways

Using this calculator's own shipped defaults — 4 rooms staged, a 2-month included term, an expected 3-month time on market, $220/month furniture carrying cost, a $250/month extension fee, 6 crew hours at $30/hour, a 25-mile round trip, $40 in equipment, a $150 consult fee, 10% overhead, and a 30% target margin:

A flat rate of $200 a room for this 4-room job comes to $800. This calculator prices the same job's included term at $1,150.61, holding the target 30% margin on a $700.42 cost — the flat $800 quote still covers that cost, but only at a 12.45% margin, less than half the target. If the listing runs the expected extra month past the included term, this calculator's total price becomes $1,400.61 against a $920.42 total cost, still 34.28% margin — while the flat $800 quote, never adjusted for the extra month, now sits $120.42 below that cost. The flat rate does not just shrink the margin as the listing sits — past the included term, it goes into the red.

Common questions

Why does a flat per-room rate get a staging job wrong?

Because the number is set for install day -- crew hours, mileage, and the rooms furnished -- and has no line for how long the furniture then sits in the house. Using this calculator's own shipped defaults (4 rooms, a 2-month included term, $220/month furniture carrying cost, a $250/month extension fee), a $200-per-room flat quote comes to $800. That covers the calculator's own $700.42 included-term cost, but only at a 12.45% margin against the 30% this calculator targets -- and it does not move at all if the listing runs long.

What does a flat per-room rate usually leave out entirely?

A separate line for time on the market. The furniture package keeps costing storage, insurance, and wear for every month a listing does not sell, and a flat per-room number has no mechanism to bill for that once the included term runs out -- it was set once, on install day, and does not change if the home is still vacant a season later.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own shipped defaults: a $200-per-room flat quote for 4 rooms is $800. This calculator prices the same job's included term at $1,150.61, holding a 30% margin on a $700.42 cost. If the listing runs the expected extra month past the included term, this calculator's total price becomes $1,400.61 against a $920.42 total cost -- still 34.28% margin -- while the flat $800 quote, never adjusted for the extra month, now sits $120.42 below that cost: a real loss, not just a thinner margin, once the furniture has been off the market long enough.

Is a flat per-room rate ever the right way to quote?

For a listing you are confident will sell inside the included term, a flat per-room number can land close, because that is the job it was probably set on. The risk is the listing that does not sell on schedule, because the rate does not know how long a home will sit, and by the time it is clear the term has run out the flat number has usually already been quoted.

Does this calculator know my local furniture rental rates or how long homes sit in my market?

No. It has no data on your market's typical days on market, your supplier's furniture rental costs, or what other stagers in your area charge. It takes rooms staged, an included term, your furniture package's carrying cost per month, crew labor, mileage, equipment, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a price for this job and an extension fee check -- the arithmetic a flat rate skips, not a market survey.
