Trust fund recovery penalty explained

Every payroll deposit has two kinds of money in it. The trust-fund part is income tax withheld from employees plus the employee's half of Social Security and Medicare; the business is holding it for the government. The employer share is the matching half of Social Security and Medicare, which is the business's own cost.

Why the split matters

Late deposits cost the business a failure-to-deposit penalty (2%, 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on how late) plus interest. If trust-fund taxes are not paid over at all, the IRS can assess a separate penalty equal to 100% of the unpaid trust-fund amount against each "responsible person" personally (IRC section 6672), which can reach owners, officers and others with authority over which bills get paid. A corporation or LLC does not by itself protect them from this one.

Worked example

Deposit$7,200 due Oct 9, unpaid on Oct 12
Trust-fund share (assumed 75%)$5,400
Penalty so far (3 days late, 2%)$144
If still unpaid on day 6penalty tier rises to 5% = $360
If still unpaid on day 16tier rises to 10% = $720

What to do

  • Pay the trust-fund portion first when you cannot pay everything; that is the part that follows you personally.
  • Ask a tax professional about penalty abatement (reasonable cause, first-time abatement) and about a payment plan.
  • Do not wait for the IRS notice: the 15% rate applies when an amount stays unpaid more than 10 days after one.
