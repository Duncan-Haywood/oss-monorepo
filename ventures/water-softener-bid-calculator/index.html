      Common questions

      Why are the pipe and drain lines $0 when I entered lengths?

      Because both defaults sit inside their allowances. The base install carries 10 ft of supply pipe and 15 ft of drain line; the defaults enter 8 ft and 6 ft, so neither has chargeable extra footage and the $1.20 and $1.40 per-foot rates never engage. Enter a 25-foot drain run to a distant standpipe and the difference starts billing.

      Then why is there still a $35 drain material line?

      Because the drain kit is not the same thing as drain footage. The air gap, standpipe fitting, and clamps ship as a fixed kit whether the run is two feet or twenty — that is the $35, alongside a $65 bypass valve. Footage bills on top of it, not instead of it.

      Why doesn’t a 35% target margin mean marking cost up 35%?

      Because a margin is a share of the price, not an addition to the cost. The default install costs $856.42, and keeping 35% of the price means $856.42 ÷ 0.65 = $1,317.56. Marking the cost up by 35% would give $1,156.16, which is only a 25.9% margin — a $161 shortfall on a job this size, and proportionally the same on every job you price that way.

      What is deliberately not in this number?

      A water test, a permit, and haul-away of the old unit are all off or zero on the defaults, as is any electrical work and reverse-osmosis or iron-filter equipment. The estimate also assumes an existing loop to tie into. Sizing the unit to actual grains-per-gallon hardness is a judgment call this page does not make for you — the capacity selector is your input, not a recommendation.
