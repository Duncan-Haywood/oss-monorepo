How much to charge to install a sprinkler system

The number a homeowner wants is a published “$400 a zone installed” special, or a “$2,400 for 6 zones” coupon. That number is not a takeoff. A 6-zone front-yard spray system with a 3/4-inch PVB in dirt is not the same job as a 10-zone backyard with extra heads, a 1.5-inch RPZ, rock trench, a drip conversion, a rain sensor, and a master valve. The trip is the same. The hours are not.

Count the zones and the extras, not just “a sprinkler system”

Irrigation-winterization calculators price a blowout, a backflow drain, and a seasonal service call. Lawn-care calculators price mowing. A new sprinkler-system install is a different job: the zones, the heads, the trench, the backflow, and the extras that are not in the coupon. Quoting all of that from a published per-zone special is how the first extra valve and the first extra hundred feet of trench eat the field margin.

A job with the same trip can cost much more

Each extra head past six per zone is time that never appears on the coupon. Rock, a 1.5-inch RPZ, drip conversion, a rain sensor, and a master valve are billed as their own hours. On the sample rates a 10-zone Wi-Fi system with 10 heads per zone, a 1.5-inch RPZ, 420 feet of rock trench, drip, rain sensor, and a master valve prices about $11,568.81 (71.1 crew-hours, $1,156.88 per zone). The same shop’s 6-zone spray system prices about $3,855.96 (22.6 hours, walk-away $2,699.17, $642.66 per zone). Matching the brochure job’s $400/zone special ($2,400 on 6 zones, $4,000 on 10 zones) on the rock drip yard is how that job becomes a loss — the walk-away on the heavy job is already $8,098.17, and the $4,000 special loses about $4,098 there. The $2,400 special loses $299.17 even on the brochure system.

An 8-zone RPZ job and a drip conversion are not automatically a markup on the 6-zone special

An 8-zone spray system with 8 heads per zone, a 12-station controller, a 1-inch RPZ, a rain sensor, and 280 feet of trench is not the 6-zone brochure system plus 15%. On the sample rates that mid job prices about $6,218.16 (34.2 crew-hours). A 6-zone drip conversion in clay with a 1-inch RPZ, rain sensor, and 240 feet of trench prices about $5,888.00 (34.4 hours). A 3/4-inch PVB drops RPZ hours; trench and soil do not disappear with the smaller backflow. Treating either as a blended add-on is how the special that looked fine turns into a loss after the first extra hundred feet of trench.

Load the wage, then keep a walk-away number

A $38/h tech loaded at 32% payroll burden is $50.16. Margin is a share of the price you send, not a markup on cost. The walk-away price is total cost — below that the job makes nothing before you take a dollar home. Replace every rate with your invoices before you send either number.

What this is not

This is not an irrigation design, a plumbing-code determination, a permit, an engineering stamp, or a recommended market price. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type. The zones, heads, trench, soil, backflow, and controller are yours to confirm. It does not price a winterization / blowout or a lawn-care mow.

Common questions

How much to charge to install a sprinkler system?

Charge from the trip, the zones (valve plus included heads), extra heads past six per zone, trench footage times soil, the controller, the backflow (3/4-inch PVB versus 1-inch versus 1.5-inch RPZ), drip conversion, rain sensor, master valve, loaded labor, overhead, and a margin that is a share of price. A default 6-zone spray system with a 3/4-inch PVB in dirt and 180 feet of trench, at the sample rates in this calculator, prices around $3,856 — about 22.6 crew-hours, $642.66 per zone.

Why is a $400-a-zone sprinkler special dangerous?

Homeowners count zones. Extra heads, trench footage, backflow size, and soil do not scale with that number. Matching a cheaper special written for a 6-zone spray system is how a 10-zone rock drip backyard becomes a two-day loss. On the sample rates the $400/zone special already loses about $299 on the brochure system (walk-away $2,699) and loses about $4,098 on the rock drip yard whose walk-away is already $8,098.

How much to charge for a 10-zone irrigation system with drip and a large RPZ?

A 10-zone system with 10 heads per zone, a Wi-Fi controller, a 1.5-inch RPZ, rock trench, drip conversion, a rain sensor, a master valve, and 420 feet of trench is not a 6-zone spray special. On the sample rates that job prices around $11,569 (71.1 crew-hours, $1,156.88 per zone), while a 6-zone spray system prices around $3,856 (22.6 hours).
