This calculator vs. a flat $400-a-zone special

"$400 a zone installed" is not a price for an irrigation system — it is a price for six heads a zone, a 3/4-inch PVB, and dirt, quietly reused for every yard the truck rolls out to, whatever it actually needs.

What a flat $400-a-zone special is

A flat per-zone rate is the fastest way to advertise: one number, easy to post online and easy for a homeowner to multiply by the zone count they heard from a neighbor. It is the number a homeowner holding a competitor's ad will call and ask a shop to match, whatever the yard actually needs.

The rate behind it was set for one job — a brochure 6-zone spray system, six heads a zone, a 3/4-inch PVB, and dirt to trench through — and then reused for every job afterward, whether or not the next yard looks anything like the one it was priced on.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the zone count, heads a zone, trench footage, controller and backflow size, soil, and whether the job adds a drip conversion, a rain sensor, or a master valve. It prices the zones, the extra heads, the trench, and every extra separately, and returns a walk-away price below which the job loses money — for this job, not a generic one.

What a flat $400-a-zone special doesWhat this calculator does instead
Charges the same rate whether the job is a brochure 6-zone spray system with six heads a zone, or a 10-zone system with ten heads a zone. Prices heads past the six included per zone from what was entered, so the two jobs above come out at different numbers instead of one.
Has no line for trench footage or soil, so 180 feet in dirt gets quoted the same as 420 feet through rock. Applies a soil-hour factor to the trench, so a rock trench costs more than the same footage in dirt.
Assumes a 3/4-inch PVB, so a 1.5-inch RPZ with a test-cock assembly and a larger tap gets quoted the same as the smallest backflow made. Prices backflow hours and kit cost by size, so a 1.5-inch RPZ costs more than a 3/4-inch PVB.
Has no line for a drip conversion, a rain sensor, or a master valve, regardless of how much the job needs. Prices each of those separately from its own inputs, so a fully loaded job costs more than a bare spray system.
Gives the shop an ad rate to quote, not a number to check a quote against when a homeowner asks to match a cheaper brochure special on a rock-and-RPZ job. Reports the walk-away price and the dollar gap against the flat rate, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before the crew commits to it.

Common questions

Why does a flat $400-a-zone special get a bid wrong?

Because the cost driver is heads, trench footage, backflow size, and soil, not "a zone" alone. A 6-zone brochure spray system with six heads a zone in dirt is a different job from a 10-zone system with ten heads a zone, a 1.5-inch RPZ, rock trench, and a drip conversion -- and a flat per-zone rate cannot see which one it is looking at.

What does a flat per-zone rate usually leave out entirely?

Heads past the six included in the brochure rate, trench footage and the soil it runs through, backflow size past a 3/4-inch PVB, and the drip conversion, rain sensor, or master valve. A published per-zone rate is priced on a 6-head brochure zone in dirt -- it has nowhere to put the labor for four extra heads a zone, a rock trench, a 1.5-inch RPZ, or a drip-and-rain package.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own shop-rate defaults ($38/hr crew wage, 32% burden, 15% overhead, a 30% target margin): a 6-zone brochure spray system with six heads a zone, a 3/4-inch PVB, dirt, and a 180-foot trench -- this calculator's own default job, and the job the $400/zone special is set on -- has a $2,699.17 break-even cost, already $299.17 above the flat $2,400.00 rate before a dollar of profit. An 8-zone system with a rain sensor and a 1-inch RPZ breaks even at $4,352.71 against a $3,200.00 flat rate. A 10-zone rock job with a 1.5-inch RPZ and the full drip/rain/master package breaks even at $8,098.17 against a $4,000.00 flat rate.

Is a flat per-zone rate ever the right way to quote an irrigation install?

Not on this calculator's own numbers, even for the cheapest job in the comparison: a 6-zone brochure spray system with six heads a zone, a 3/4-inch PVB, and no extras already breaks even above the flat $400-a-zone rate, before a single extra head, foot of rock trench, or upsized backflow enters the bid. The rate is not close on the hard case -- it is already short on the easy one.

Does this calculator know what to charge in my market?

No. It has no data on local labor rates, irrigation pricing, or what any jurisdiction requires for a backflow permit. It takes the zones, heads, trench footage, soil, backflow size, controller, and any drip, rain, or master-valve extras, and turns them into a walk-away price for the job you describe -- the arithmetic a flat per-zone rate skips, not a market survey.
