      Common questions

      Why can a flyer advertise “$400 a zone installed”?

      Because that number is written around one specific yard, and it is the first preset here: a 6-zone front-yard spray system, 6 heads a zone (the included count, so no extra-head labor), a 3/4-inch PVB in dirt, and 180 feet of trench. Priced honestly it really is close to $400 a zone. A 1.5-inch RPZ, rock trench, extra heads, or a drip conversion each add their own labor and material — none of which a flat per-zone number was built to carry.

      Why does backflow-device size change the price so much?

      Because a bigger device is a bigger tap and a test-cock assembly, not just a pricier part. A 3/4-inch PVB bills 1.4 hours and a $185 kit. A 1-inch RPZ bills 2.4 hours and $420. A 1.5-inch RPZ bills 3.6 hours and $780 — more than four times the PVB’s material cost and over twice the labor, on a line most homeowners never notice until the county requires it.

      Why does soil type matter if the trench footage is the same?

      Because the calculator applies a soil multiplier to trench hours, not to price directly. Dirt or loam is the 1× baseline. Clay multiplies trench hours by 1.4×. Rock or hardpan multiplies them by 2× — the same 280 feet of trench takes twice as long in rock as in dirt, which is exactly the kind of site condition a per-zone flyer price cannot see in advance.

      What counts as an “extra head”?

      Anything past 6 heads per zone, the number built into the base per-zone labor rate. A zone with 6 heads or fewer bills no extra-head hours at all. An 8-heads-per-zone yard bills 2 extra heads per zone, each at its own labor rate, on top of every zone in the job — so a dense zone layout costs more than a sparse one even at the same zone count.

      What do drip conversion, a rain sensor, and a master valve add?

      Each is its own toggle with its own hours and kit cost, billed only when checked. None of them ride along with the base zone price, so the difference between a bare 10-zone spray system and the same system with drip, rain sensing, and a master valve is a real, itemized add — not a rounding error on the per-zone rate.
