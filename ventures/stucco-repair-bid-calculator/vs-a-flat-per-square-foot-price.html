This calculator vs. a flat per-square-foot price

“$12 a square foot, done” is not a price for a stucco repair — it is a price for one system and one story, quietly applied to every wall that gets called in.

What a flat per-square-foot price is

A flat per-square-foot price is the fastest way to quote a stucco or EIFS repair over the phone: multiply the repair square footage by a number and read it back. Homeowners shop it because it is easy to compare between bids, and it is the number a customer holding a competitor's quote will ask you to match.

The number behind it was set for one kind of job — usually a first-story hard-coat patch with no tear-off, no flashings, and no color match — and then reused for every wall afterward, whether or not the next one is a second-story EIFS tear-off with flashings, a moisture investigation, and a color match.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the crack feet, hollow and tear-off square footage, the wall system and story, the window and door flashings, control-joint caulk, color match, and moisture investigation, and your own wage, payroll burden, materials, overhead, target margin, and travel. It prices the mix and mesh by system, adds scaffold access by story, prices flashings, color match, and moisture work as their own lines, and returns the job price, the walk-away price, and the price per repair square foot — for this wall and this site, not a flat number for every job.

What a flat per-square-foot price doesWhat this calculator does instead
Charges the same rate whether the system is a hard-coat patch or a synthetic EIFS repair, which uses a heavier mix and mesh rate per square foot. Applies its own mix and mesh cost per square foot by system (hard-coat, one-coat, or EIFS), so a heavier system costs more per square foot than a lighter patch.
Has no line for story access at all — a second- or third-story repair gets the same rate as a first-story one. Adds scaffold or lift hours and rental by story, plus extra hours for a third-story or gable, before any repair square footage is priced.
Quotes the same number for a job with no tear-off and one needing 180 square feet torn back to the substrate, with no separate line for the labor or the dumpster. Prices tear-off by the square foot in crew hours, adds a dumpster line once any tear-off is scoped, and prices hollow area and tear-off separately from surface crack repair.
Has no line for window or door flashings, color match, or a moisture investigation — a job with four flashings and a color match gets the same number as one with none. Prices flashings by the unit, color match as its own material and labor line, and a moisture investigation as its own line, rather than folding any of it into the square-footage number.
Gives the estimator a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the walk-away price and the price per repair square foot, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before you commit to it.

Common questions

Why does a flat per-square-foot price get a stucco repair wrong?

Because the cost driver is the system, the story, the tear-off, and the extras, not the repair square footage alone. An 80 sq ft first-story one-coat delamination repair with one window flashing and a color match needs no scaffold and a lighter mix. A 220 sq ft second-story EIFS repair -- 40 sq ft hollow plus a 180 sq ft tear-off to the substrate, with four window flashings, a moisture investigation, and a color match -- needs a scaffold day, a heavier EIFS mix and mesh rate, and far more crew hours per square foot. A flat per-square-foot price charges both the same because it was priced on the area, not the system, the access, or the scope.

What does a flat per-square-foot price usually leave out entirely?

System, story access, tear-off, flashings, color match, and moisture investigation. A flat price has no line for EIFS mix and mesh costing more per square foot than a hard-coat patch, no line for the scaffold day a second story adds before any repair work starts, no line for window and door flashings by the unit, no line for a color-match tint, and no line for a moisture investigation before repairing over what might be a leak. It also has no separate line for the extra crew hours all of that adds, since the flat number was set on repair square footage alone.

Is a flat per-square-foot price ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of similar jobs -- the same system, the same story, no tear-off, few flashings, no color match or moisture work -- a flat per-square-foot number can land close, because that is probably the job the price was set on. The risk is quoting an EIFS tear-off, an upper-story job, or a job with several flashings and a color match at the same rate sight unseen, because the price does not know the system, the access, or the scope until someone walks the wall, and by then the flat number has often already been quoted.

Does this calculator know my local labor rates or material costs?

No. It has no data on your local labor market, your stucco or EIFS supplier's pricing, or your material costs. It takes the crack feet, hollow and tear-off square footage, system, story, flashings, caulk, color match, and moisture investigation, and your own wage, burden, travel, materials, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a price for this job -- the arithmetic a flat per-square-foot price skips, not a market survey.
