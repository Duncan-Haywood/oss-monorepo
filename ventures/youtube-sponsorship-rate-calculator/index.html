What this does and doesn't do

This applies commonly cited CPM-per-1000-views benchmark ranges — wide ranges, by design — and conventional percentage uplifts for usage and exclusivity to the numbers you enter. It does not know your audience's buying power, your conversion history, or what a specific sponsor will pay this quarter. Treat the output as a starting point for a negotiation, not a quote.

Common questions

How much should I charge for a YouTube sponsorship?

It depends on your median views, niche, and the format you are selling. This calculator applies commonly cited CPM-per-1000-views ranges to your own median views, then adds percentage uplifts for paid-usage rights and category exclusivity, to give a starting price range.

Why median views instead of subscribers?

A sponsor pays for views the sponsored video will actually get. Subscriber counts include people who stopped watching, and a single viral video inflates an average. The median of your last 10 long-form videos, measured about 30 days after upload, is the number sponsors can defend to their own boss.

What are whitelisting and usage rights worth?

When a sponsor runs your video as an ad from your handle or their account, they get paid distribution of your face and voice. Creators commonly charge an extra 20% to 100% of the base fee depending on length; perpetual rights are usually worth more than the top of that range, so treat the figure as a floor.

Is this price a guarantee of what a sponsor will pay?

No. It is a starting point for negotiation based on general market ranges, not a quote or a valuation. Actual rates vary with audience quality, conversion history and how much a specific sponsor wants your audience this quarter.
