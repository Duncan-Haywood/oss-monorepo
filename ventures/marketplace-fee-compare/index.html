        How to read this

        Every percentage here is charged on the whole order — the item price plus any shipping the buyer paid. Folding postage into the price to advertise free shipping does not avoid the fee.

        Marketplace fees are taken out of your proceeds, not paid up front, so they are not counted as cash you invested. That keeps margin comparable across channels that charge very differently.

        Subscriptions are spread over the orders-per-month figure you entered rather than ignored. That is what stops a $39.99 Amazon plan from looking free at two orders a month.

        A marketplace fee buys buyers. Your own store charges no referral fee and will almost always top this table on arithmetic — but it sends you nobody. Read that row as "what I keep on a sale I brought myself", and put the cost of bringing it against the difference.

        Common questions

        Which marketplace has the lowest fees?

        It depends on the category and the price. Etsy's combined transaction and payment fee is about 9.5%, eBay's final value fee about 13.6%, and TikTok Shop's US referral about 6% — but Amazon and Walmart charge by category, from 8% on electronics to 20% on jewelry. On a $40 jewelry item Amazon takes about $8 and Etsy about $3.80; on a $40 electronics item Amazon takes about $3.20 and Etsy still takes $3.80. That is why this page asks for the category before ranking anything.

        Do marketplaces charge fees on the shipping the buyer pays?

        Yes, on all of the marketplaces compared here. The percentage is charged on the whole order — item price plus whatever shipping the buyer was charged. Folding postage into the item price and calling it free shipping does not avoid the fee, because the fee follows the total the buyer pays either way.

        Is selling on my own store really more profitable?

        On fees alone, almost always — an own-store checkout pays payment processing of roughly 2.9% plus 30 cents and no referral fee at all. But a marketplace fee buys buyers. Your own store sends you none, so that higher net profit only exists for a sale you brought yourself, and the advertising or audience-building that brings it is a real cost this comparison does not include.

        Why is Faire not in the comparison?

        Faire is wholesale. The buyer is a shop paying roughly half your retail price, and the economics turn on order size and reorder rate rather than on one retail order. Ranking it beside ten retail channels would produce a number that looks comparable and is not.

        Why is Whatnot not in the comparison?

        Whatnot is a livestream auction. Commission sits on the hammer and payment processing sits on the checkout total — the item plus buyer-paid shipping and tax — with published category caps a flat retail referral rate cannot express. Ranking it beside ten fixed-fee retail channels would produce a number that looks comparable and is not. The Whatnot seller payout calculator prices that split on its own terms.

        Why is Reverb not in the comparison?

        Reverb only accepts musical instruments and gear, where the ten channels above sell almost anything, so seating it in a general-purpose ranking would read as a recommendation to list any item there — which is true for none of the other rows' inventory. Its fee shape (a marketplace percentage plus payment processing) is genuinely close to eBay's, and the Reverb fee calculator compares the two directly on the same guitar or pedal, along with offer-versus-list, shipping, and return handling.

        How does the comparison handle Poshmark's $15 cliff?

        Poshmark keeps a flat $2.95 on any sale under $15.00 and 20% of the sale price from $15.00 up, so its fee is a cliff rather than one percentage: a $14.99 item pays $2.95 and a $15.00 item pays $3.00, while a $5 item pays 59%. This comparison prices that schedule directly, takes the commission on the sale price alone, and leaves postage out of both sides because the Poshmark buyer buys the prepaid label. The Poshmark fee calculator goes further with offer and bundle arithmetic around the $15 line.
