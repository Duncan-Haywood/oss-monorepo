A spreadsheet confirms that quantity times rate adds up. Where a spreadsheet stops being enough

Most brands that watch their 3PL bill already have a sheet: the invoice lines in one tab, the contract rates in another, a column that multiplies one by the other. That catches a rate that is plainly wrong. The leaks that cost real money sit in the comparison with something outside the sheet, and building each comparison once for one month is easy; rebuilding it correctly every month is where it drifts.

Common questions

Can't I just check my 3PL invoice in a spreadsheet?

Yes, and a careful spreadsheet catches the arithmetic: quantity times rate, and the total. What a spreadsheet built for one month rarely holds is the logic around it: a duplicate test across every row, a storage count compared with the average of daily WMS snapshots, an account minimum judged against the corrected charges, a surcharge tested as a percent of the right base, and a rule for charges that are not in the rate card. Each is a few formulas, and each has to be rebuilt or re-checked every month.

What does a spreadsheet usually miss on a 3PL invoice?

The checks that depend on a second document. A line can multiply correctly and still be wrong because the quantity is higher than your order export or your inventory snapshots support, or because the rate is last year's. A minimum billed on top of charges that already reach it adds up fine on its own. A duplicate line looks ordinary read alone. These only show when each line is compared with the contract and with your records.

How does the checker decide what is recoverable?

For each line it multiplies the contract rate by the quantity your own records support, then adds up the rate difference, the quantity difference and any arithmetic difference. A repeated line and a charge with no matching fee in the rate card are recoverable in full. A minimum is judged against the corrected total of the other lines, and surcharges against the corrected base. Where you enter no count, the billed quantity is taken as given and the page says so.

Does the checker replace reading the contract?

No. It finds lines worth questioning and names the record that would settle each one. A written amendment, a tiered rate, a free-storage window or a different counting rule can explain a line, so the dispute note asks the 3PL to review and send supporting detail rather than asserting an error. You remain responsible for checking each finding against your agreement before you send anything.

Is my invoice uploaded when I use the checker?

No. Everything runs in your browser and the rate card, counts and invoice lines are never sent anywhere. A spreadsheet kept in a shared drive may be visible to others; the checker's share link, by contrast, holds your inputs in the link itself, so send it only to people who may see them.
