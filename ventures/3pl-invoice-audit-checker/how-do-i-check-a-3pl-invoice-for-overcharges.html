How do I check a 3PL invoice for overcharges?

Check the rate, then the quantity, then the repeats, then the minimum and surcharges, and dispute what is left in writing. The quantity step is the one most reviews skip, because it needs your own records rather than the invoice alone.

Five steps

  1. Match every line to a rate-card fee. Lay the invoice beside your signed rate card and amendments. Each line should map to one fee: receiving, storage, pick, pack, returns, kitting, a minimum or a surcharge. Compare the billed rate with the contract rate. A line that maps to no fee at all is a question for the 3PL, not a rounding difference.
  2. Compare each quantity with your own records. A line can multiply correctly and still be wrong because the count is too high. Check pallets and cartons received against your receiving log, orders and units against your order export, storage against the average of your daily inventory snapshots, and returns and kits against their logs.
  3. Look for repeats. The same description, quantity, rate and amount twice is worth a question. Read alone, the second copy looks ordinary, so it only shows when you scan every row.
  4. Test the minimum and the surcharges. An account minimum normally covers only the shortfall between the minimum and the month's other charges, so a minimum line on top of charges that already reach it deserves a question. Fuel and handling surcharges are a percent of a base your contract defines; recompute the base from the corrected lines, then the percent.
  5. Total it, then dispute in writing. Add the lines you would question, keep under-charges separate, and send a line-by-line note that cites the contract section and your record for each. Ask for review and supporting detail; a tiered rate, a free-storage window or an amendment can explain a line you had not seen.

Common questions

What are the most common overcharges on a 3PL invoice?

The ones this checker tests for: a rate above the contract rate card, a quantity higher than your own records support (storage on more pallets than the daily inventory snapshots average, picks on more orders than your order export), the same line billed twice, a charge with no matching fee in the rate card, an account minimum billed on top of charges that already reach it, and a fuel or handling surcharge calculated on the wrong base. Any of them can also have an innocent explanation, so each is a question to ask, not a verdict.

Which records do I need to check a 3PL invoice?

Your signed rate card and any written amendments, plus your own counts for the same period: the order export for orders and units shipped, the receiving log or advance shipping notices for pallets and cartons received, daily WMS inventory snapshots for storage, the returns log, and kit work orders if you are billed for kitting. Where you have no record for a category, the checker takes the billed quantity as given and says so.

How do I dispute a 3PL invoice?

Put it in writing, line by line. For each line name the invoice number, the contract section, the rate or quantity you expect and the record that supports it, and ask the 3PL to review and send supporting detail. Check your agreement for a dispute window and for how credits are applied before you pay or short-pay. The checker writes a draft note you can edit; it asks for review rather than asserting an error.

How is a recoverable amount worked out?

For each line, the contract rate is multiplied by the quantity your records support. The overcharge is the rate difference, plus the quantity difference, plus any arithmetic difference, capped at the line amount. A repeated line and a charge with no rate-card fee behind it count in full. Under-charges are never netted against over-charges. The result is an estimate from the numbers you typed in.
