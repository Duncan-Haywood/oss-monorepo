This calculator vs. Aspire

Two different shapes: Aspire is an account-based business-management platform for running a landscape or snow company's whole operation. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this winter's route cost, given its stops, minutes, salt, and storm count — before you send the bid.

What each tool is for

Aspire is a cloud business-management platform, founded in 2013 by Kevin Kehoe and Mark Tipton, headquartered in Chesterfield, Missouri. It is built for landscape maintenance and construction, snow and ice management, and commercial cleaning contractors, and has been part of ServiceTitan since ServiceTitan's 2021 acquisition of it. It bundles CRM and lead tracking, estimating, scheduling, purchasing, job costing, mobile crew time tracking, invoicing, and accounting and payroll integration into one ongoing subscription account, with a mobile app for crews in the field. Its own plans are tiered by a contractor's annual revenue rather than sold at one flat price, and no dollar figure for any plan is published on its site — pricing is reached by requesting a demo. Aspire's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact tier names or any dollar figure, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — a business-management platform serving landscape, snow and ice, and cleaning contractors, sold as a revenue-tiered subscription account — are stated, and no dollar amount for Aspire appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what Aspire does. It does not track a lead, schedule a crew, or invoice a customer, and it stores no customer or job record on a server. It takes one winter's route — each stop's plow minutes, sidewalk minutes, and salt bags, with ice events priced as their own line at their own minutes and salt rather than folded into a plow visit — plus loaded operator-and-truck hourly, payroll burden, salt cost, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into the per-push price, the seasonal price, the per ice-event price, the walk-away price below which the winter loses money, and how many snow events a seasonal quote actually survives once ice is already paid — plus, if you already have a number on the table, a check of whether it clears that walk-away floor, and a single printable customer proposal for that one winter. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Should I use this calculator or Aspire?

They do different jobs, and a snow or landscape company of any size will likely end up touching both eventually.
