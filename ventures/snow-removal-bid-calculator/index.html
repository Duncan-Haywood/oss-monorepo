FOR SNOW-REMOVAL CONTRACTORS

Price the winter, not the driveway.

A $350 seasonal that looked fine at eight storms is often underwater at fourteen — especially if ice is “included.” This calculator prices a route from minutes on site, deadhead, a loaded truck, salt, and the event count you actually expect. You get a per-push price, a seasonal, the ice drain, and the storm count where a cheap quote dies.

Your costs

A long storm on a 4-hour route is often two passes. Set passes to 1.3 or 2 if you typically re-hit the route before it ends. Payroll burden is employer taxes, workers’ compensation, and paid time off — the part that never shows up on the wage.

The winter you are bidding

Use your trigger depth and your last few winters, not a national average. A 1-inch trigger fires more often than a 2-inch trigger. This is not a weather forecast.

Questions contractors actually ask

How do I price a snow removal contract?

Time each stop, price those minutes at a loaded operator-and-truck hourly, add salt, then multiply by the storms and ice events you expect. Do not start from a neighbor’s driveway price.

Per push or seasonal?

Offer both. Seasonal only works if the quote survives a long winter plus ice. Paste a number you are considering and the tool will say how many storms it can absorb.

What about unlimited ice?

Ice is usually salt and sidewalks, not a full plow — and there are often more ice events than snow events. Price them as their own line or the “included” clause eats the season.
