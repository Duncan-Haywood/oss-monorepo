How much should I charge for snow removal?

A $350 seasonal driveway contract that looks comfortable at eight storms a winter is often underwater at fourteen — especially once "ice included" turns out to mean unlimited ice events at no extra charge. Snow removal is priced on the route and the winter you expect, not on one driveway on one good day.

What a flat seasonal price cannot see

Storm count. Eight storms and fourteen storms are different winters. A seasonal price that only pencils out at the low end is a bet you're taking on the weather.

Ice events. Ice knockdown is its own labor and material cost. "Ice included" without a limit means every ice event that winter comes out of the same flat price.

Trigger depth. A 1-inch trigger fires far more often than a 2-inch trigger in many Northeast and Midwest metros. Route minutes at a low trigger depth over a real winter, not a national average, are what the price should be built from.

A bid you can defend

  1. Time each stop — plow, sidewalk, and drive time — and price it at a loaded operator-and-truck hourly.
  2. Add salt as its own cost per application.
  3. Multiply by the storm count and ice-event count you actually expect for the season, using your own trigger depth and recent winters.
  4. Price the seasonal so margin is a share of the whole winter, not a markup on one driveway.

Worked example

A $350 seasonal driveway contract priced against an assumed eight storms and no ice events can look like healthy margin. Run the same route minutes and loaded hourly against fourteen storms and a handful of ice events — a realistic winter in many Northeast and Midwest metros with a 1-inch trigger — and the same $350 price can be underwater before the season ends. The calculator reports the break-even storm count so that number is known before the contract is signed, not discovered in February.

Common questions

How do I price a snow removal contract?

Time each stop (plow + sidewalk + drive time), price those minutes at a loaded operator-and-truck hourly, add salt, then multiply by the storms and ice events you actually expect. Margin is a share of the seasonal price, not a markup on one driveway.

Is a seasonal snow removal contract better than per push?

Seasonal is only better for the contractor if the quote covers a long winter plus ice events. A $350 driveway seasonal that looked fine at eight storms often loses money at fourteen, especially with unlimited ice.

How many snow events should I estimate?

Use your own trigger depth and last few winters, not a national average. In many Northeast and Midwest metros a 1-inch trigger fires more often than a 2-inch trigger. This tool does not forecast weather — it prices the event count you enter.
