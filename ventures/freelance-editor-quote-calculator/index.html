Common questions

    
How do I price an editing job?

    
Estimate the hours, then multiply by the hourly rate you need. Hours are the word count divided by how many words per hour you edit at that level, adjusted for how rough the text is, plus admin time and any extras such as a style sheet. Quote the result as a flat fee and state the per-word equivalent, because clients compare per-word.

    
What are typical editing speeds?

    
They vary a lot with the person and the text. The defaults here are rule-of-thumb figures: about 2,000 words an hour for proofreading, 1,000 for copyediting, 600 for line editing and 350 for developmental editing. They are inputs, so replace them with your own timed pace.

    
Why charge a flat fee instead of hourly?

    
Clients usually want a number up front. A flat fee is safe only if your pace estimate is right, so the page shows what the fee pays per hour if you work slower than planned and suggests a cap on scope or an overage clause.

    
When should I add a rush fee?

    
When the deadline is shorter than your normal turnaround and displaces other work. Enter the working days the client gives and the window below which you charge extra; the surcharge percentage is yours to set.

    
Does it know market rates?

    
No. It works backwards from the hourly income you want. Compare the per-word result with published rate surveys from editors' associations before you send a quote.

    
Is my manuscript or my rate uploaded anywhere?

    
No. Only a word count and your own numbers are entered, everything is worked out in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
