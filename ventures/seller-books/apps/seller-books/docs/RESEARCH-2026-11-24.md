# Research notes, 2026-11-24 (unverified: to confirm before launch)

These notes come from web searches limited to each marketplace's or agency's own domain. The build environment
could not open the pages themselves (blocked by the network policy), so every figure below is **from a search
engine's summary of the page, not from page text that was read**. Some indexed pages may be out of date.

**Nothing in this file is used by the app's calculations.** Fees come from report columns only
(`src/shared/reference/fee-sources.ts` records no rates). The 1099-K notes the app shows are in
`src/shared/reference/form-1099k.ts`, each labelled with how it was checked.

To promote a fee figure into the app:
1. Open the official URL.
2. Copy the sentence that states the rate.
3. Add a `FeeRule` with that quote, the URL and the date read.

`test/unit/reference.test.ts` enforces that shape.

## Form 1099-K

- **Federal.** IRS FAQ IR-2025-107 / Fact Sheet 2025-08. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act reinstated the
  pre-ARPA threshold retroactively: over $20,000 AND over 200 transactions. A threshold does not change
  taxability.
- **States.** See `form-1099k.ts`. Notable findings:
  - Vermont's own page now says $2,000; many third-party tables still say $600.
  - Virginia and Maryland key their rules to IRC §6041(a), which rises to $2,000 for payments after 2025. Neither
    state had published a new figure.
  - North Carolina has no dollar threshold: it receives a copy of every federal 1099-K.
  - Rhode Island has a $100 general 1099 rule for tax years 2025–2026 and follows the federal thresholds from 2027.
  - Montana ($600 in secondary lists) is left out: no official source was found.
