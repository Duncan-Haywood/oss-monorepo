This calculator vs. a flat per-breed price card

Names no specific product or company. This compares a free calculator against the generic method every groomer already has: a price card by size — small, medium, large, giant — quoted the same regardless of coat, matting, or add-ons.

First — the price card is right, and you should have one

A website, a phone quote, or a sheet on the wall needs one number per size before the dog is in front of you. "Medium dogs $75" is a fine headline price, and no calculator replaces having one. What follows is not an argument that the card is wrong. It is what the same flat number stops showing you the moment the dog is on the table and you can see the coat.

The two places the price card goes quiet

1. Coat and matting are invisible to a size chart

A price card answers "what size is the dog," not "how long will this coat actually take." Both groom the same "medium dog" either way. The calculator prices the coat multiplier and the matting time and surcharge separately, so a curly/doodle coat with moderate matting is visibly a 171-minute job instead of silently the same 69 minutes the card assumes.

2. Add-ons and matting can add up to more than the card charges

This is the one that costs real money. The card charges $75 whether the groom needs three add-ons and a matting surcharge or none at all, but this job's own direct costs — $93 — are already above the $75 the card quotes, before the card fee is even taken out. A groomer working off the card alone can finish a "profitable" day of $75 medium dogs and have actually lost money on the ones that came in matted with add-ons, with nothing on the card to say so in advance.

When the price card is still the better tool

For quoting on the phone, it plainly is. A caller wants one number, not a form, and you usually cannot see the coat before the dog arrives. The sensible arrangement is both: quote the card price to book the appointment, then run this calculator once the dog is on the table to see whether that day's version of "the $75 medium groom" still clears your floor — and, if the coat and matting are bad enough, whether the day-of price needs to move before you start.

Every figure on this page is this calculator's own worked example, not a claim about any real groomer or an industry benchmark. Your rates, coats, and add-on pricing will produce different numbers — the point is which numbers to produce, not what they should come out to.

Common questions

Is a flat per-size price card wrong?

No, and it should not go away. A website, a phone quote, or a price list on the wall needs one number per size — "medium dogs $75" is a fine headline price, and no calculator replaces having one. The point of this page is not that the card is wrong. It is that the card is the same number for every medium dog, and a medium poodle with matted fur is not the same job as a medium lab with a smooth coat.

How much do coat type and matting actually add to a groom?

On this calculator's own defaults, a medium-size, medium-coat dog with no matting and no add-ons is a 69-minute groom. Change only the coat to curly/doodle and add moderate matting plus three common add-ons (nail trim, ear cleaning, de-shedding) and the same "medium dog" is a 171-minute groom — nearly three times as long — with $30 of matting surcharge and $52 of add-on charges added on top.

Can the same card price actually lose money?

Yes, and this calculator's own worked example shows exactly that. The simple medium groom nets $61.75 on a $75 card price — an 82.3% margin. The matted, curly-coat, three-add-on medium groom at the identical $75 price has $93 of direct costs before the card fee, so it nets -$20.25 — a real loss, not just a thinner one, because the break-even price for that job ($95.88) is already above the $75 the card charges.

Does the calculator replace the price card?

No, and it is not trying to. The price card is what you quote on the phone before you have seen the coat. This calculator is what you run once the dog is on the table (or from a clear photo and a few questions) to decide whether that day's version of "the $75 medium groom" still clears your floor — and, if the coat and matting are bad enough, whether the card price needs a same-day adjustment before you start.
