How to compare financial aid offers

An award letter mixes four different things under one heading: money you never repay, money you repay with interest, a job, and the bill itself. To compare two letters, sort every line into one of those, then price the loans. This guide does it on three made-up offers, the same ones the comparator loads as its example.

1. Sort the lines

  • Grants and scholarships reduce the price. Check whether each one renews, and on what condition (usually a minimum grade point average).
  • Loans (subsidized, unsubsidized, Parent PLUS, private) do not reduce the price. They move it into the future and add interest.
  • Work-study is the most you can earn, if you find and work the hours.
  • Cost of attendance is the bill plus an estimate of books and living costs. Compare it across colleges on the same lines.

Assumptions: costs rise 3% a year, loans and work-study repeat each year, 2025-26 federal rates, 10-year repayment. All figures are made up.

Regional University and Private College A show the same $27,500 net price in year 1. That is the number most families compare. But Private College A's letter includes $9,000 of first-year-only aid, so from year 2 the net price climbs to about $40,448 a year, and it fills the gap with $54,000 of loans against $22,000. Over four years the family pays about $52,659 more than at Regional University once loan interest is counted.

3. Use the gap in an appeal

If the cheapest offer is not the college you want, the useful question is how far apart the offers really are. In the example Private College A would need about $18,950 more in grants each year to match State University. A financial aid appeal that shows a competing offer and an exact gap is easier to act on than a request for more aid. Whether an office agrees is up to it.

Questions

What is the difference between net price and total cost?

Net price is the cost of attendance minus grants and scholarships: what the college bills you after gift aid. Total cost also counts what the loans will cost you to repay, so a college that fills the gap with loans looks the same as one that fills it with grants on net price alone, and is much dearer in total.

Which of the three example colleges is cheapest?

With the made-up numbers on this page, State University costs $99,099 in total, Regional University $127,211 and Private College A $179,870. Private College A and Regional University both show a $27,500 net price in year 1, but Private College A's net price rises to about $40,448 a year from year 2 because $9,000 of its aid is first-year only, and it comes with $54,000 of loans.

How much more grant would Private College A need to match State University?

About $18,950 more in grants each year in the example. That figure is what to bring to a financial aid appeal: a competing offer and the exact gap, rather than a request for 'more aid'.

Do I have to accept every loan on the letter?

No. Loans on an award letter are offers. You can accept part of a loan or decline it. Borrowing more than the bill, or more than the net price after work-study, is borrowing money you do not need, and the tool flags it.
