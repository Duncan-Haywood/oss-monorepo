      Common questions

      What does this waterproofing bid actually add up?

      The price adds up drain or excavation labor and material by system, a wall-membrane and floor-vapor-barrier area, concrete pour-back and spoil disposal, window wells and covers, floor-crack and wall-crack injection, a new sump pit when checked, a primary pump, a backup pump, discharge pipe and a freeze-proof kit when checked, and a dehumidifier when checked — plus travel and equipment, overhead, and your target margin, all from the perimeter, wall height, and system you enter, not a flat price-per-lineal-foot list.

      Why does an 80 lf basement with an existing sump price so differently from a 120 lf seepage house?

      Interior drain, exterior excavation, and crawl-space encapsulation each start from their own hours and material rates per lineal foot or per 100 sq ft of wall. On top of that, a new sump pit, a backup pump, extra window wells, crack injection, and freeze-proof discharge are all separate line items that do not scale with perimeter footage — a quote written for a brochure job with an existing pit and no wells prices a seepage house with four wells and a new pit the same way, which is exactly how that job loses money after the first well.

      Why do I set hours per 100 square feet of wall instead of per lineal foot for the membrane?

      Wall membrane and floor vapor barrier are area-based work — the wall area is perimeter times wall height, so a tall basement wall costs more to membrane than a short crawl-space wall of the same perimeter, even though both are priced from the same lineal footage of drain or excavation.

      What’s the difference between the bid price and the walk-away price?

      The bid price already includes your target margin on top of labor, materials, and overhead. The walk-away price is the total cost underneath it — drain or excavation, membrane, wells, cracks, pit, pumps, and discharge — the point below which the job stops being profitable at all.

      Does this check hydrostatic pressure, drainage design, or structural conditions?

      No. Hours and material rates are ballpark shop figures you can edit, not an engineering specification, a dry-basement warranty, or a guarantee of hydrostatic performance. Whether a specific foundation needs interior drain versus exterior excavation, how deep a new sump pit should sit, or whether a wall crack is a moisture issue or a structural one is still a judgment call for the site walk — this tool prices the system you choose, it does not choose it for you.

      Why not just quote a flat rate per lineal foot like a lot of the trade does?

      A flat rate does not see window wells, whether the sump pit is new or existing, floor and wall crack injection, a backup pump, or freeze-proof discharge. A shorter perimeter with more of that scope can cost more than a longer, simpler one. See this calculator vs. a flat rate per lineal foot for a worked example.
