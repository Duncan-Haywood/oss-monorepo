Should I hang a customer-supplied door?

Owners call with a slab in the hallway: “I already bought the door. Can you just hang it for $150?” That sounds like free margin because the millwork invoice left the bid. It did not leave the trip, the hang hours, or the chance that the leaf is a 30-inch in a 32-inch jamb.

Zeroing the leaf is not a free hang

A carpenter-supplied hollow slab still carries the millwork invoice. An owner-supplied leaf that actually hangs zeros that line and keeps the same set hours. On a typical 80-inch hang-only — $42 wage, 30% burden, 15% overhead, 20% target margin, six miles — the carpenter-supplied bid is in the mid-$300s. Zeroing a $48 slab drops it, but the owner-supplied walk-away stays above $150. Taking the Facebook special to “just hang what they bought” still loses money.

Wrong size is a second trip

Store shelves default to 80-inch by 24/28/30/32. Plenty of jambs are 96-inch, a pair, or a hair tight. Hanging the wrong slab means a plane pass or a drive back to exchange it. Common questions

Should I hang an interior door the customer bought at a store?

Only after you price the hang without the millwork invoice. Pulling their slab is still a trip, a set, and crew hours. A $150 hang-only special often sits under that walk-away number even when the leaf is free to you.

Does a customer-supplied door make the $150 installed special profitable?

Usually not. Zeroing a $48 hollow-slab invoice drops the bid, but loaded labor, travel, and overhead stay. On a typical 80-inch hang-only the owner-supplied walk-away is still above $150. A slab that binds or is the wrong height adds a second trip and a plane pass.

What if the store-bought slab is the wrong height or binds in the jamb?

That is a second dispatch plus a plane and shim, or a drive back to exchange it. Price that callback on the same opening before you agree to hang whatever they rolled into the driveway. The calculator adds a starting callback trip and a plane kit on top of your current rates.
