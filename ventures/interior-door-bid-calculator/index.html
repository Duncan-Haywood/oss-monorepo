    Common questions

    Why can a flyer advertise “$150 a door installed”?

    Because that number describes one specific job, and it is the first preset here: a hollow-core slab hung on an existing jamb, 80-inch single opening, no paint, no haul-away, no hardware upgrade. Hang-only bills 0.75 hours of hang labor and a $48 door invoice — genuinely close to $150 installed. A prehung swap, a new framed opening, or a pocket conversion is a different job with its own framing and casing hours, and the flyer’s number was never written to cover them.

    Why does a pocket conversion cost so much more than a prehung swap?

    Because it is framing a wall cavity, not swapping a unit. A prehung swap bills 1.4 hours to hang, 0.3 to frame, and a $0 frame kit. A pocket conversion bills 1.8 hours to hang, 3.4 hours to frame, and a $240 frame kit on top of $68 of casing — more than ten times the framing labor of a swap, before the door leaf itself is counted.

    What does the door type change beyond the invoice?

    Both labor and material move together. A hollow-core slab adds zero extra hours and a $48 invoice. A hollow-core prehung unit adds 0.15 hours and $175. A solid-core prehung adds 0.5 hours — it is heavier to hang and shim — and $340. A French pair or barn door adds 1.15 hours and $720, since it is effectively two leaves or a track system instead of one swinging slab.

    Why does an 8-foot or double door cost more than the standard 80-inch single?

    Height and leaf count each carry their own hours and hardware cost, on top of whatever the door type already bills. A 96-inch single adds 0.55 hours and a $95 extra-leaf charge for the taller jamb and casing run. A double opening adds 0.9 hours and $175, since it is two leaves and two sets of hardware in one frame.

    What do paint, haul-away, and hardware upgrades add per door?

    Each is billed per door, not as a flat job fee, so they scale with door count the way a whole- house order actually does. Skipping them on a single-door swap barely moves the price; skipping them on an eight-door whole-house job is the difference between a painter’s punch list and a finished room.
