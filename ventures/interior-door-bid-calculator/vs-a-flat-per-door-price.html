This calculator vs. a flat per-door price

"$150 a door installed" is not a price for an opening — it is a price for whatever job the special was built on, quietly applied to a pocket conversion with a French pair.

What a flat per-door price is

A flat per-door number is the fastest way to quote a door job over the phone: ask how many doors, multiply by the special, skip the walkthrough. Homeowners shop it because it is easy to compare between carpenters, and it is the number a customer holding a competitor's quote will ask you to match.

The number behind it was set for one kind of job — usually a hollow-core slab hung on an existing jamb — and then reused for every door afterward, whether or not the next opening needs framing, a heavier leaf, a taller or double size, or paint and a haul-away.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the door count, the scope (hang-only, prehung-swap, new-opening, or pocket-conversion), the leaf (hollow slab, hollow prehung, solid-core, or French-or-barn), the size (80-inch, 96-inch, or double), and whether paint, haul-away, and new hardware are included, plus your own wage, payroll burden, overhead, and target margin. It prices the hang, frame, and casing hours by scope, adds the leaf and extras invoices, and returns the walk-away price and the price per door — for this opening, not a generic count.

What a flat per-door price doesWhat this calculator does instead
Charges the same rate for a hollow-slab hang and a French-pair pocket conversion — even though one is a slab on an existing jamb and the other is a framed wall opening. Prices frame, casing, and hang hours separately by scope, so a new opening or a pocket costs more than a hang-only swap.
Has no line for the leaf itself, regardless of whether it is a $48 hollow slab or a $720 French-or-barn unit. Prices the leaf invoice by door type, plus an extra-leaf charge for a 96-inch or double opening.
Has no line for paint, haul-away, or a new lockset, regardless of how many of them the job needs. Prices paint, haul-away, and hardware hours and invoices per door, only when each is included.
Estimates travel by eye, with no separate check against trip time or mileage. Prices trip hours plus mileage as loaded labor, on top of the per-door work.
Gives the estimator a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the walk-away price and the dollar gap against what a flat per-door price would have quoted, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before you commit to it.

Common questions

Why does a flat per-door price get an interior-door quote wrong?

Because the cost driver is the scope and the leaf, not the count of doors. A hollow-core slab hung on an existing jamb and a French pair framed into a new pocket wall are both "one door," but one is 0.75 hours of hanging and a $48 leaf while the other is a framed pocket, a heavier leaf, paint, a haul-away, and new hardware. A flat per-door price charges both the same.

What does a flat per-door price usually leave out entirely?

The scope. A per-door number has no line for framing a new opening or a wall pocket, no line for the jump from a hollow slab to a solid-core or French-or-barn leaf, no line for an 8-foot or double opening needing more casing, and no line for paint, haul-away, or a new lockset -- each its own hours and its own invoice.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own worked examples: a hang-only hollow-slab door at 80 inches costs $271.31 to run and prices at $339.14 with a 20% target margin -- already above a flat $150 quote before scope changes at all. Framing the same single opening as a pocket conversion for a French-or-barn pair at 96 inches, with paint, haul-away, and new hardware, costs $2,258.93 to run and prices at $2,823.66. The same $150 quote that already falls short on the easy hang is $2,108.93 below the pocket-conversion job's own break-even cost -- before a dollar of profit.

Is a flat per-door price ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of similar jobs -- hang-only swaps on existing jambs, the same leaf and size every time -- a per-door number can land close, because that is probably the job it was built on. The risk is applying it to a job with a new opening, a pocket wall, a heavier leaf, or extras mixed in without pricing the scope separately, because the flat number does not know what the opening needs and neither does the estimator until the wall is already open.

Does this calculator know what door leaf costs or labor rates are in my market?

No. It has no data on your local millwork supplier's prices or your local labor market. It takes the door count, scope, leaf, size, and extras you choose, plus your own wage, payroll burden, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a price for this opening -- the arithmetic a flat per-door price skips, not a market survey.
