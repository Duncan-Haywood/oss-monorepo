Common questions

How do I work out a flat-rate price for a plumbing job?

Take your true cost per billable hour, multiply by the hours the job takes including travel, divide by one minus your target margin, then add the parts at cost plus a tiered markup and round up. On the default numbers a 2.75-hour job with $120.00 of parts at a true cost of $100.00 an hour prices at $595.00, which leaves $200.00 (33.6%). Enter your own figures; the defaults are invented.

What is my true cost per hour as a plumber?

Your loaded wage spread over the hours you can actually bill, plus truck, insurance, tools and overhead over the same hours. On the defaults that is $70.00 for wages and burden plus $30.00 of truck, insurance, tools and overhead, $100.00 for every billable hour, because only 1,300 of 2,080 paid hours are sold.

What should my minimum callout price be?

The travel time plus your shortest visit, at true cost and your margin, rounded up. On the defaults that is 1.25 hours and $180.00 before any parts.

Is a flat rate better than time and materials?

It depends on how reliably you estimate hours. On the defaults the flat price of $595.00 leaves $200.00 if the job goes to plan, $62.50 if it runs 50% long and loses $75.00 at double the time; time and materials at $135.00 an hour keeps earning as the clock runs but the customer bears the overrun. Neither is advice; it is arithmetic on the numbers you type.
