    Common questions

    How much should I charge to replace a water heater?

    There is no flat installed price. A same-day 50-gallon electric tank in a garage is a different job from a condensing tankless on a 3/4-inch gas upsize with a sidewall vent and an attic carry. This calculator prices the heater type, the location, the vent, and the gas or electrical path from your own wage and material inputs.

    Is this a plumbing-code ruling or a first-hour-rating calculation?

    No. Outputs are arithmetic on the numbers you enter. Capacity hints are starting points, not a first-hour rating, fuel-gas table, or manufacturer clearance you can stamp or submit.

    How much does upsizing the gas line add to a tankless bid?

    On a 10-foot run at this calculator's default wage, burden, overhead, and margin, moving a gas tankless from "existing line looks adequate" to a 3/4-inch upsize adds 4 labor hours and $270 in materials — about $804 more on the bid ($1,525.94 to $2,329.59). Most 1/2-inch lines that fed the old atmospheric tank cannot flow enough gas for a condensing tankless, so pricing the existing line as reusable is how a competitive-looking bid loses money on the first callback.

    What does panel work add to an electric tankless bid?

    At this calculator's defaults, a 7 GPM electric tankless priced on "existing circuit looks adequate" comes to $1,433.20. The same job priced on the panel work an electric tankless usually needs — two or three dedicated 40 A 240 V circuits, not the single 30 A breaker the old tank used — comes to $2,700.97: 3.2 more labor hours and $620 more in electrical materials, about $1,267.77 higher. Pricing the old circuit as reusable on an electric tankless is the electrical-side version of the gas-line mistake above.
