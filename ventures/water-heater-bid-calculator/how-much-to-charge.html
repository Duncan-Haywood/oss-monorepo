What a flat installed price cannot see

Tank vs. tankless vs. heat pump. Each has its own equipment cost, install hours, and venting requirement. A straight tank swap and a tankless conversion are different jobs, not different price tiers of the same job.

Access. An attic or crawl-space carry adds real labor a closet or garage swap never sees.

Venting. B-vent and concentric sidewall venting are different materials and different labor, and a tankless conversion frequently needs a vent change the old tank never had.

Gas and electrical upsizes. A tankless unit's higher BTU input can require a 3/4-inch gas line upsize; some units need dedicated 240 V circuits and panel work. Neither shows up in a "swap the tank" price.

A bid you can defend

  1. Price the heater type — tank, tankless, or heat pump — and its own equipment and labor.
  2. Add access hours for attic or crawl-space work.
  3. Price the actual vent path, not the old one.
  4. Add a gas-line upsize or electrical/panel work where the new unit needs it.
  5. Load the wage, then add overhead, and price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin) for a walk-away number.

Worked example

A same-day 50-gallon electric tank swap in a garage, no venting to change and no access penalty, is a straightforward, comparatively quick job. A condensing tankless conversion in the same house, on a 3/4-inch gas-line upsize, a new sidewall vent, and an attic carry to reach the old unit, is several times the labor and material — before either quote has priced dollars per gallon of capacity delivered.
