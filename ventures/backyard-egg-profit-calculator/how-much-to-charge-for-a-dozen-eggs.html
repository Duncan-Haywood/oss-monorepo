How much to charge for a dozen eggs: feed, hours and winter lay rate

Short answer: add up a year of feed, bedding, replacement hens and cartons, add your hours at the wage you want, and divide by the dozens you actually sell. The calculator does it for your own numbers.

A worked flock

20 hens laying 70 percent of the days, 8 percent of eggs cracked or kept at home, a quarter pound of feed per hen per day at 45 cents a pound, $30 a month of bedding and other costs, $8 pullets that lay for 2 years, 35 cents of carton per dozen, 4 hours a week and a $15 an hour wage, selling at $5 a dozen.

  • The flock lays 5110 eggs a year and you sell 392 dozen, about 7.5 a week.
  • Cash cost is $1,398.37 a year: $821.25 feed, $360.00 other, $80.00 replacement hens, $137.12 cartons. Per dozen that is $3.57, and 0.53 hours.
  • Revenue is $1,958.83, so the margin is $560.46 a year, $2.69 an hour for your time.
  • Pay yourself $15 an hour and the year is a loss of $2,559.54. The price that earns your wage is $11.53 a dozen; cash breakeven is $3.57.

Feed and winter decide it

Feed at $0.56 a pound cuts profit per hour to $1.71; at $0.90 it is -$1.25 an hour and the price you need is $13.63. Hens laying at three quarters of the rate (52.5%) cost $4.64 a dozen; at half the rate (35%) they cost $6.79 and the price you need is $22.72. A dark winter with a $4 price, 60-cent feed, 45 percent lay and 15 percent lost has a cash cost of $6.95 a dozen and loses $3,805.69 a year after wage. Try the dark winter button in the calculator.

At $10 a dozen the same flock earns $12.11 an hour: pasture-raised, delivered or subscription eggs sell for more because they are a different product, not because the hens are cheaper.

How to measure your own numbers

  • Count eggs every day for a month, then divide by hens and days to get your lay rate.
  • Weigh a week of feed per hen, and include scratch, treats and oyster shell.
  • Time one ordinary week of chores, washing, packing and selling.
  • Check your state's rules on selling eggs, cartons and labelling before you scale up.

Common questions

How much should I charge for a dozen eggs?

Enough that the price covers your cash cost and pays your time. On the default numbers that is $11.53 a dozen for a $15 an hour wage, against a cash breakeven of $3.57.

What costs the most?

Feed, at $821.25 of the $1,398.37 cash cost a year, 58.7%. After that your own hours matter: 208 hours a year at $15 is $3,120.00, more than all the cash costs together.
