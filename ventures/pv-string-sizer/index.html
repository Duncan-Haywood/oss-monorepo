How it is calculated

Module voltage at a cell temperature T is the STC voltage times (1 + coefficient x (T - 25)), or plus the coefficient in mV per degree times (T - 25). The cold case uses the coldest air temperature as the cell temperature (cells at dawn sit at air temperature, and the open-circuit voltage is highest then). The hot case uses the hot-day air temperature plus the cell's rise above the air: (NOCT - 20) x 1000/800 at full sun, or the rise you enter. This is the temperature-coefficient method that NEC 690.7 allows when the module maker gives a coefficient.

Longest string = the lowest of the inverter maximum, the module maximum system voltage and the limit you enter, divided by the cold Voc, rounded down. Shortest string = the MPPT minimum divided by the hot Vmp, rounded up. Strings per MPPT = the input's short-circuit limit divided by 1.25 x Isc, rounded down (NEC 690.8(A)(1)); conductor ampacity is 1.25 x 1.25 x Isc (690.8(B)). Layouts split whole strings of one length as evenly as possible over the MPPT inputs.

Not modelled: uneven string lengths on different MPPTs, module-level power electronics (optimisers and microinverters change the rules), bifacial gain, inverter start-up voltage, ageing of Vmp, voltage drop, conductor temperature and conduit-fill corrections, and rapid-shutdown requirements. Unverified: checked by hand calculation and unit tests only, not against a manufacturer's string-sizing tool. Use your own datasheets, site temperatures and code edition.

Common questions

How many solar panels can I put in one string?

As many as keep the string's open-circuit voltage at the coldest expected temperature below the lowest of the inverter's maximum DC input, the module's maximum system voltage and the voltage limit that applies to your installation. Voltage rises as cells get colder, so the count comes from the record low, not from the datasheet's 25 C value.

What is the minimum number of panels per string?

Enough that the string's maximum-power voltage on the hottest afternoon stays above the inverter's MPPT minimum. Hot cells lose voltage, so a string that looks fine at 25 C can drop out of the MPPT window in summer.

Which temperatures should I use?

For the cold case, the extreme annual minimum dry-bulb temperature for the site (installers commonly use the ASHRAE extreme minimum). For the hot case, a high design air temperature such as the 2 percent dry-bulb value; the tool adds the cell's rise above the air from the module's NOCT, or a rise you enter.

Why is the short-circuit current multiplied by 1.25?

NEC 690.8(A)(1) sets the maximum PV circuit current at 125 percent of the module short-circuit current, to cover irradiance above the test condition. Conductors are then sized at 125 percent of that again (690.8(B)), 156 percent of Isc, before temperature and conduit-fill corrections.

What happens if the string Vmp goes above the MPPT maximum in the cold?

It is not a safety limit: the inverter cannot hold the maximum power point and loses some energy on cold, bright mornings. The tool flags the lengths where that happens and keeps them in the list when they are under the hard voltage limit.

My datasheet gives the temperature coefficient in mV per degree. Can I use it?

Yes. Choose mV per C next to the coefficient. Enter it as a per-module value with its minus sign, for example -133 mV per C.
