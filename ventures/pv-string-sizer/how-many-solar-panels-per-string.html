FOR SOLAR INSTALLERS, DESIGNERS AND DIY BUILDERS

How many solar panels per string?

The quick answer people reach for is the inverter's maximum DC voltage divided by the module's Voc. With a 600 V limit and a 49.5 V module that gives 12. On a -20 C morning those 12 modules make about 666 V, and the right answer is 10.

Size your strings with the free calculator

Who this is for

Anyone laying out string inverters: installers checking a design before the permit set, designers comparing inverters, and people building their own system who want to know why the inverter maker's tool rejected a string. If your system uses microinverters or DC optimisers, the module-level electronics set the string rules instead, and this check is not the one you need.

Two limits, two temperatures

Module voltage goes up as cells get colder and down as they get hotter. A string has to satisfy both ends:

  • The longest string is set by the open-circuit voltage on the coldest morning, when the sun first hits cold cells and nothing is drawing current yet. It must stay under the lowest of the inverter's maximum DC input, the module's maximum system voltage and the limit that applies to the installation. This one is a hard limit: exceeding it can damage the inverter.
  • The shortest string is set by the maximum-power voltage on the hottest afternoon, when cells run 25 to 35 C above the air. It must stay above the inverter's MPPT minimum, or the inverter loses the maximum power point and the array underproduces.

Worked example

An invented module, inverter and site (example numbers, not a real product): Voc 49.5 V, Vmp 41.4 V, Isc 10.3 A, Voc coefficient -0.27 %/C, Vmp coefficient -0.35 %/C, NOCT 45 C; inverter 600 V maximum, MPPT 100 to 550 V, two MPPT inputs that each accept 26 A short-circuit; site extreme minimum -20 C, hot-day air 35 C.

With 24 modules on the job, two layouts use every module: three strings of 8 (two on one input, one on the other) or four strings of 6 (two per input). Two strings of 12 would be over the cold-voltage limit. The calculator lists every option with its DC/AC ratio and flags lengths that leave the MPPT window when cold.

Where the inputs come from

  • Coldest temperature: installers commonly use the ASHRAE extreme annual mean minimum dry-bulb for the nearest station. Your AHJ may name a source.
  • Hot-day temperature: a high design dry-bulb (for example the 2 percent value). The tool adds the cell's rise above the air from NOCT; roof-flush mounts with little air gap run hotter than NOCT suggests, so enter a larger rise if you know it.
  • Coefficients: on the module datasheet, in % per degree or mV per degree. If only a Pmax coefficient is given, it is a reasonable stand-in for Vmp.

When to use something else

The inverter maker's own string-sizing tool knows its equipment's limits better than a datasheet does; use it for the final design. Microinverter and optimiser systems follow the module-level device's rules. And the code edition your jurisdiction adopted, not this page, decides the voltage limit and the conductor rules.
