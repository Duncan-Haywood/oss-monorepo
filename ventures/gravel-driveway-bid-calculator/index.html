Bid the trucks, the depth, and the dirt work.

Price a gravel driveway the way the trade actually loses money: compacted depth to tons, then whole truckloads, soil prep, fabric, and a street apron — not the published $4-a-foot dump. You get the job price, what it works out to per square foot and per ton, and the number below which the drive loses money.

The drive

Pick a common job to start, then replace every rate with your quarry ticket and a crew you have timed. Changing stone, prep, or delivery fills starting hours and material only when you have not already edited that field.

Hours and quarry rates

These start from the stone, prep, and delivery you picked. They are ballpark crew figures, not a quarry catalog or a guarantee.

Your costs

Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off. Underestimating it is how a per-foot dump that looked profitable was not.
