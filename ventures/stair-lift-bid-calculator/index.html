    Common questions

    Why do the rates change when I pick a different rail or chair?

    Because each rail, chair, and attachment carries its own suggested hours and invoice cost, and the page fills those fields for you when you switch. Anything you have typed yourself is left alone — once you edit a field it stops being overwritten, so your own numbers survive a change of selection. That is what lets the presets be a starting point rather than a cage.

    Why is equipment two-thirds of the job?

    Because a stair lift is a purchased machine with a half-day of installation. On the defaults — one straight single-flight rail, perch chair — equipment invoices at $2,655.60 against $626.81 of labor for 11.48 hours, out of a $4,855.02 price. Your dealer cost is the number that decides whether this job is profitable; crew speed barely moves it.

    Why is a curved rail so much more than a straight one?

    Because it is a different product, not a longer version of the same one. A curved rail is measured, custom-bent, and built for one staircase, which adds both invoice cost and lead time, and it cannot be recovered and resold the way a straight rail can. Selecting it changes the rail kit’s cost and hours together, which is the honest way to price it — a percentage uplift on the straight rate understates it.

    Does this price a rental or a buy-back?

    No. It prices a supply-and-install at your target margin, and many stair lifts are sold on rental or with an agreed removal and buy-back after a short term — a materially different economic model, because you keep an asset with residual value. If you offer that, price the job here first to establish your true cost, then apply your own rental terms to it.
