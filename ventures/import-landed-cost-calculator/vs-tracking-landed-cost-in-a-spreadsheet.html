Common questions

Why not just track landed cost in a spreadsheet?

Plenty of importers do, and a spreadsheet is a fine tool for tracking a history of shipments over time. The failure mode is not the spreadsheet itself — it is the Merchandise Processing Fee's floor and ceiling and the Harbor Maintenance Fee's ocean-only condition, both of which are easy to type once and forget to update when a shipment's value crosses the floor, the ceiling, or the ocean-vs-air line. A calculator that recomputes those conditions on every input avoids that particular drift; a spreadsheet formula copied down a column does not check itself.

Does this calculator replace my spreadsheet?

Not necessarily. This tool prices one shipment at a time and does not save a history across sessions the way a spreadsheet's rows do. Many importers use both: this calculator to get one shipment's arithmetic right, including the MPF/HMF edge cases, and their own spreadsheet to log the result alongside supplier, tracking number, and margin for every shipment over a season.
