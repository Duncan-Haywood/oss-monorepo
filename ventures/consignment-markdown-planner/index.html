CONSIGNMENT MARKDOWN PLANNER · RUNS IN YOUR BROWSER

When should a consignment shop mark things down, and what should it decline?

Every week an item sits, it uses a rack slot you pay for. A fixed rule (25% off at 30 days, 50% at 60) treats a $12 tee and a $210 handbag the same. Paste your items — price, how fast it sells, your share of the sale and what a slot costs per week — and get the exact markdown schedule with the highest expected profit for each, which items are not worth taking in, and what a fixed rule leaves on the table.

For consignment and resale boutiques, thrift, vintage and furniture shops. Nothing is uploaded: the text box is analysed in this browser and never leaves your device.

    Common questions

    Does it upload my item list?

    No. The browser page works on the text you paste, in your own browser, and never sends it anywhere. The CLI reads a local file or stdin and makes no network call.

    How is the best schedule found?

    Each week an item can sell with a chance that rises with the discount, and every week on the floor costs shelf space. The tool works backwards week by week over the markdown levels you allow, never raising a price, and keeps the schedule with the highest expected net to the shop. It is checked against trying every possible schedule in the tests.

    Why would it tell me to decline an item?

    If the shop's cut of the likely sale does not cover shelf space and handling, taking the item loses money even at the best schedule. The tool says what price or shop share would make the item worth taking.

    Where do the sell rates and the lift come from?

    From your own history: how many weeks items like this usually sit, and how much faster they sell after a markdown. They are your estimates, so treat the plan as a decision aid, not a forecast.

    Who is it for?

    Consignment and resale boutiques, thrift and vintage shops and furniture consignors deciding when to mark things down and what to accept.
