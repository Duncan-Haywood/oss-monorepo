How should a consignment shop mark down? 30/60/90-day schedule math

Short answer: mark an item down when the extra chance of selling it this week is worth more than the cut you give up and the shelf week you save. A fixed schedule gets close; working it per item gets closer, and also tells you which items were never worth the slot.

A worked example

A designer silk blazer is tagged at $120. It sells about 8% of weeks at full price, you keep 40% of the sale, a rack slot costs $0.60 a week, handling is $2, and the item stays 12 weeks. Each 10% off lifts the weekly sell chance by 35% of its base rate.

  • Never marking down: the blazer sells 63% of the time and nets the shop $23.61.
  • A fixed 25% off at week 5 and 50% off at week 9: $25.45.
  • The best schedule, 10% off from week 1, 25% off in weeks 8-11 and 40% off in week 12: $26.76, selling 81% of the time, with the consignor paid about $49.23 on average.

The gain per item is small; across a floor of hundreds of items it is the difference between a rack that turns and one that fills with last season.

Which items to decline

A $12 graphic tee that sells 10% of weeks, with a 40% shop share and the same $0.60 slot, nets -$1.63 even on its best schedule (25% off from week 1, 40% off from week 6). It would only pay at a price of about $17.93 or a shop share near 59.8%. Tell consignors that items under that price are not accepted, or take them at a higher split.

What to feed it

  1. Weekly sell rate from your own history: items of that kind sold per week they were on the floor.
  2. Shelf cost per week: rent and floor staff time for the area, divided by item slots.
  3. Lift: compare sell-through in the weeks before and after your last markdowns.

Check the consignment agreement first: some promise consignors a markdown schedule or price floor, and those terms come before any optimum.

Questions

How long should a consignment shop wait before marking an item down?

There is no fixed answer; it depends on how fast items like it sell and what a week of shelf space costs you. Items that sell quickly at full price can hold their tag longer. Items on a costly slot or with a slow sell rate pay to start marking down early. The planner finds the best start week for each item from your own numbers.

Is a 30/60/90-day markdown schedule good enough?

It is a sensible default and rarely a disaster, but it treats every item the same. The planner compares it with the best schedule for each item and prints the dollars the fixed rule leaves behind.

Which items should a consignment shop refuse?

Items whose expected shop cut, over the weeks they are likely to sit, does not cover shelf space and handling. Low-ticket items on a costly shelf are the usual losers. The planner names the price or shop share at which each declined item would pay.
