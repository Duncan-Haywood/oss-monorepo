How much to charge for window tinting

A posted "$180 sedan / $220 SUV" price list gives you one number for the whole vehicle class. It cannot tell you that film tier alone can move the same seven-window job by nearly $200, or that a light two-window job should be priced at a minimum instead of the pane math.

Film tier moves the price more than pane count alone suggests

Take one job -- a full seven-pane kit (two front doors, two rear doors, two rear quarters, one rear windshield), no old tint to strip -- and change only the film:

Same glass, same labor -- $189.14 to $370.27, on film tier alone. A price list that quotes one number per vehicle size cannot see which film the customer picked, and ceramic's cost-per-square-foot is over three times dyed's.

Stripping old tint first adds real time, not a flat fee

Removal is counted in minutes per pane, not as a percentage add-on -- and it is not evenly spread across the vehicle. The rear windshield's defroster grid means the old film has to come off with a heat gun and patience instead of a fast peel, so it alone adds 30 minutes where a door window adds 8:

The minimum job fee exists for exactly this case: a job small enough that the pane math alone would undercharge for the time it takes to schedule, set up, and run the bay. Without a floor, the lightest jobs on the board would be the least profitable ones.

Common questions

How much should I charge to tint a car window?

It depends on pane count, film tier, and whether old tint has to come off first -- there is no single per-vehicle number. At this calculator's own shop-rate defaults ($28/hr installer rate, a 15% waste factor, $12 booth overhead, a 35% target margin), a full seven-pane kit in ceramic film with no removal prices at $370.27, the same kit in dyed film prices at $189.14, and a front-two-only dyed job with no removal floors at the $120.00 minimum job fee.

How much does film tier change the price?

On the same seven-pane kit with no removal, dyed film prices at $189.14, carbon at $261.59, and ceramic at $370.27 -- ceramic runs nearly twice a dyed quote on identical glass, because ceramic film costs $5.50 per square foot against dyed film's $1.75.

Does stripping old tint change the price much?

Yes. Adding removal to the same ceramic seven-pane kit adds 82 minutes of strip time, most of it the rear windshield's defroster grid, on top of 120 install minutes -- raising labor cost from $56.00 to $94.27 and the final price from $370.27 to $429.14.

Why does a small job floor at a minimum instead of pricing lower?

Because the pane and film math alone can price below what it costs to open the bay for the day. A front-two-only dyed job with no removal prices at $60.46 on materials and labor alone, well under a $120.00 minimum job fee, so the minimum applies and the customer is quoted $120.00 instead.

Is a flat per-vehicle price ever close to right?

Only for a narrow band of near-identical jobs. A $200 flat per-vehicle rate overcharges the front-two-only dyed job by $80.00 and undercharges the default full ceramic kit by $170.27 -- and a flat number cannot tell you which side of that line a given vehicle falls on.
