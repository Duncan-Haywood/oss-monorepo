Common questions

Why not just quote CNC jobs in a spreadsheet?

Plenty of shops do, and a spreadsheet is a fine place to keep a running log of every quote you've sent. The failure mode is not the spreadsheet itself — it is that a per-part cost cell built for one quantity keeps getting reused on the next RFQ without being rebuilt for its lot size, so the flat setup-and-programming charge silently understates cost the moment the quantity changes.

Does this calculator replace my quoting spreadsheet?

Not necessarily. This tool costs one job at a time and does not keep a running log across quotes the way a spreadsheet's rows do. Many shops use both: this calculator to get one job's yield, scrap and per-lot arithmetic right whenever the part or the quantity changes, and their own spreadsheet to track every quote sent and won over time.
