MLS Listing Photo Preflight vs. BoxBrownie

Different jobs: BoxBrownie is a paid service that edits, stages, and enhances listing photos. This is a free, offline check for whether a delivery folder will get mechanically rejected before any of that editing happens.

What each one is for

BoxBrownie is a well-known online real estate photo editing service, described on its own site as offering photo enhancement, virtual staging, floor plans, and related property-marketing image services: a photographer or agent uploads source images, an editor works on them, and finished images come back, ordered and paid for per photo or per package. It is the kind of service a listing team reaches for when the photos are correctly captured but need retouching, sky replacement, decluttering, or virtual furniture added before they go live. BoxBrownie's own current pricing and turnaround times are not restated here, since this page cannot confirm today's numbers — only its well-documented category and service shape are described.

This checker does none of that. It has no editor, no upload step, and no per-photo charge. It reads each file's header and filename — never the decoded image — to answer a narrower, earlier question: will this delivery folder clear the mechanical floor a listing system actually enforces? Resolution, byte size, format, crop shape, a brokerage-branded filename, and a staged photo missing its unaltered original are all things it flags before you pay anyone to touch the images or upload them anywhere.

Questions

Should I use this checker or a service like BoxBrownie?

Most agents end up using both, for different reasons. BoxBrownie is a paid online service, described on its own site, that edits real estate photos, builds virtual staging and floor plans, and returns finished images through a human-in-the-loop editing pipeline you upload to and pay per photo. It makes a set of photos look better. This checker does not touch how a photo looks: it reads each file's header and filename in your browser to catch the mechanical reasons a delivery gets kicked back — too small, too large, wrong format, an odd crop, a branded filename, or a staged photo with no paired original — before you pay anyone to edit it or upload it anywhere. Running this first means you send a clean, correctly-shaped folder to an editing service, and you catch a branding or pairing problem for free instead of paying for a re-edit.
