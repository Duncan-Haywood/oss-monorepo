FOR INDEPENDENT INTERIOR PAINTERS

Bid the surfaces, not the floor plan.

Price an interior painting job the way the trade actually estimates one: each surface divided by the production rate you really hit, extra coats at a fraction of first-coat time, then paint, payroll burden, overhead, and the margin you intend to keep. You get the job price, what it works out to per floor square foot, and the number below which the job loses money.

The house

One row per surface. Production rate is how much of that surface one painter covers per hour on the first coat — pick a type to start from a common figure, then replace it with a rate timed from your own jobs.

Your costs

Extra-coat factor is second-coat time as a fraction of first-coat hours — not a second full first coat. Ceiling height only slows wall rows; trim and doors stay on their own rates. The common $/sq ft field is the rule of thumb homeowners quote; it is scored against your cost, not recommended as a price.
