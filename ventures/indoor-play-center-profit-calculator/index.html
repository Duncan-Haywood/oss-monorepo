What it does, and what it leaves to you

    
    
      
  • Visits: weekdays open × weekday visits plus weekend days open × weekend visits = child visits a week; a month is 52 ÷ 12 weeks.
    • 
      
  • Revenue: visits × the blend of walk-in admission and the lower revenue per member or pass visit, plus parties × party price, plus cafe spend per visit, less card and booking fees as a share of all revenue.
    • 
      
  • Variable cost: cafe cost of goods, other cost per visit, and per party the food and supplies plus the extra staff minutes at the loaded wage. Fixed: paid staff hours × wage × (1 + payroll load), rent, utilities, insurance, marketing, manager and other costs.
    • 
      
  • Break-even: parties move with visits, so profit is a straight line in attendance and break-even attendance is fixed cost divided by what planned demand contributes. Startup: fit-out per square foot, play equipment, cafe equipment, deposit, launch and contingency; the loan is a share of that on level payments. Ramp-up runs sixty months with demand climbing in a straight line from the month-1 figure; payback is the first month cumulative cash covers your equity.
    • 
      
  • Not decided: whether your catchment can supply the visits; seasonality and school-term swings; child-safety, play-equipment inspection, building and fire code, occupancy, food-licensing, waiver and staff background-check rules; insurance terms; income tax and depreciation; and what a lender will offer.

Common questions

    
How much profit does an indoor play centre make?

    
It depends on how many children come, what a visit and a party earn, and what staff, rent and insurance cost. Enter your own figures; the page works out revenue after fees, cafe and party costs, fixed costs, monthly profit and margin at planned attendance.

    
How many visits a week does an indoor playground need to break even?

    
Two figures are shown: visits a week that cover operating costs, and the higher number that also covers the loan. Parties scale with visits in this model, and profit is a straight line in attendance, so both are exact for your inputs.

    
How much does it cost to open an indoor play centre?

    
The page adds fit-out per square foot, play equipment, cafe equipment, lease deposit, launch cost and a contingency. Every one is your own figure; the page does not assume a benchmark cost.

    
How much do birthday parties add?

    
Party revenue is parties a week times your price, less party food and the extra staff minutes each party takes. They are entered separately because party margin is usually much higher than a walk-in visit, and the page shows what they add to profit.

    
Does this tell me whether a play centre will succeed?

    
No. It works only from your figures. It does not know how many families live near the site, what competitors charge, or how your reviews will go. Check your catchment, child-safety and play-equipment rules, building and fire codes, insurance and staffing rules first.

    
Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    
No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
