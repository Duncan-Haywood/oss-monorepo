Why did the 1099-K threshold keep changing?

Four different numbers have governed Form 1099-K since 2022, and the platforms that follow them did not all move in lockstep. Here is the full timeline, in order, and why a form that already arrived for 2024 can look inconsistent with what the rule says today.

The 2024 wrinkle

The IRS's October 2025 Fact Sheet 2025-08 confirmed that OBBBA's restoration of $20,000/200 applies retroactively back to the 2022 tax year — meaning the law now treats $20,000/200 as having technically applied even in 2024. But that retroactive correction did not undo what platforms had already done: a platform that issued a 2024 Form 1099-K under the real $5,000 threshold in effect at the time did not have to withdraw or correct it, and one that stayed silent under the old $600 assumption faced no penalty either way.

So if you received a 1099-K for 2024 on payments between $5,000 and $20,000, that form is not a mistake — it reflects the rule that was actually in force when it was issued, even though the standing rule today is higher. What stayed the same throughout

The threshold applies per platform, never combined across the different apps and marketplaces you use. And meeting or missing a threshold has never changed whether the underlying income is taxable — a 1099-K is paperwork about payments already received, not the event that makes them reportable.

Common questions

What was the 1099-K threshold before 2022?

$20,000 in gross payments and more than 200 transactions, both required, from 2011 through 2021. That is also the current threshold — the number spent three years lower and then came back.

What changed in 2022?

The American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA), passed in 2021, legislated a much lower $600 threshold with no transaction-count minimum, intended to take effect for the 2022 tax year. The IRS delayed enforcement of that lower figure by notice, first for 2022 and then again for 2023, so platforms kept using $20,000/200 in practice for both years even though the statute on the books said $600.

What actually happened in 2024?

The IRS announced a real transition threshold of $5,000, with no transaction-count minimum, as a step toward the eventual $600 figure (Notice 2023-74). This one was not delayed — platforms actually issued Form 1099-K under the $5,000 rule for payments made in 2024, filed in early 2025.

What happened for 2025 and after?

The next planned step was $2,500 for 2025, on the way down to $600 for 2026. Before that lower 2025 figure ever took effect, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed 2025-07-04, repealed ARPA's threshold entirely and restored $20,000/200 transactions. The IRS's Fact Sheet 2025-08 (October 2025) confirmed this applies retroactively back to the 2022 tax year — so the $600 and $2,500 figures never actually took legal effect for any year, and $20,000/200 is the standing rule for 2025, 2026, and beyond.

If the law now says $20,000 applied to 2024 too, does that undo the 1099-K I already got for 2024?

No. The IRS confirmed that a platform that already issued a 2024 Form 1099-K under the real $5,000 rule that was in effect at the time did not have to withdraw or correct it, and a platform that did not issue one under that rule faces no penalty either way. The retroactive correction changes what the standing legal threshold is considered to have been; it does not reach back and cancel a form that already arrived in your mailbox.
