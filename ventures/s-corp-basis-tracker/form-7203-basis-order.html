The order S corporation basis is adjusted in, with a three-year example

Form 7203 asks for your stock basis and debt basis in a fixed sequence. The sequence matters because basis cannot go below zero and each step uses what the one before it left.

  1. Add capital contributions and stock purchases, and the year's income items, including tax-exempt income. A new loan you make directly to the company adds debt basis.
  2. If debt basis was reduced by earlier losses, the year's net increase (income less distributions and nondeductible expenses) restores debt basis first, up to what you are still owed, and only then stock basis.
  3. Subtract distributions from stock basis, but not below zero. What is left over is the excess distribution.
  4. Subtract nondeductible expenses, again not below zero. Any excess carries forward.
  5. Subtract losses and deductions, including losses suspended from earlier years, first from stock basis and then from debt basis. What neither can absorb is suspended.
  6. Loan repayments come last. Repaying debt whose basis was reduced gives gain on the reduced share.

Questions

Why does debt basis come back before stock basis?

When income follows a year of losses that ate into debt basis, the rules put the loan back to its face amount first, because that basis was used last. Only the remainder rebuilds stock basis.

Do losses or distributions come first?

Distributions. They reduce stock basis before nondeductible expenses and losses, so a loss in the same year cannot make a distribution tax free.

Can I just keep a spreadsheet?

Yes, if you follow the same order. The tracker applies it for you and checks that basis is conserved from step to step.
