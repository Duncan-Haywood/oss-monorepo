This calculator vs. the flat 10% rule

"Take 10% off the item price" is the mental math every Mercari seller already runs. It is exactly right on a plain, current-schedule listing with no buyer-paid shipping — and it is wrong the moment shipping enters the sale or the listing predates January 6, 2025.

What the flat 10% rule is

Mercari's own headline is "sellers pay a 10% selling fee," so most sellers do the fastest possible math: take the item price, knock off a tenth, and call the rest the payout. Buyer-paid shipping, when there is any, is assumed to pass straight through untouched, because it looks like Mercari's cut and the item price's cut are two separate things.

That assumption holds in exactly one case, and breaks in two different ways depending on what the rule never asked about: whether the buyer paid for shipping, and which fee schedule the listing is actually on.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the item price, any buyer-paid shipping, your own shipping-label cost, what you paid for the item, and a target profit, and tell it which fee schedule the listing is on. It runs Mercari's exact published arithmetic for that schedule — or both schedules side by side — instead of a flat percentage that was never built to see either variable.

Takes 10% off the item price only, and assumes buyer-paid shipping passes through with no fee at all. Charges the current schedule's 10% fee on the item price plus buyer-paid shipping combined, exactly as Mercari does.
Has no line for a listing's fee schedule — it gives the same answer whether the listing is current or legacy. Asks which schedule the listing is on and runs that schedule's exact fees, or compares both side by side.
Has no line for the legacy payment processing fee (2.9% of item plus shipping, plus a flat $0.50) that a pre-2025, unedited listing still owes. Adds the legacy processing fee automatically whenever the legacy-schedule box is checked.
Stops at the fee, so it cannot say what to charge for a target profit after your own shipping-label cost and what you paid for the item. Solves for the item price that hits a target profit, on either schedule, after those costs.
Gives one number, with no way to check whether editing an old listing (and moving it to the current schedule) actually helps. Compares both schedules on the same sale, so that decision is a number instead of a guess.

The flat 10% rule is not wrong because it is careless — on a plain current-schedule sale with no buyer-paid shipping, it is exactly right, to the cent. It stops being right the moment shipping is charged separately or the listing is old enough to still carry the legacy processing fee, and it has no way to tell you that either condition has been met. This calculator does not replace judgment about your prices; it replaces the arithmetic the flat rule never ran.

Common questions

Why does the flat 10% rule get a Mercari payout wrong?

Because it only ever takes 10% off the item price, and assumes buyer-paid shipping passes straight through untouched. On a $45 item with $15 buyer-paid shipping, that guess says a $55.50 payout. The real payout is $54.00 under the current schedule, because the 10% fee actually applies to the combined $60, not the $45 item price alone — a $1.50 gap the guess never sees.

What does the flat 10% rule miss on an older, legacy-schedule listing?

The payment processing fee. A listing made before January 6, 2025 that has not since been edited still runs the old schedule — 10% of the item price only, plus 2.9% of the combined item-plus-shipping total, plus a flat $0.50. On the same $45-item, $15-shipping sale, that processing fee is $2.24, and the flat 10% guess has no line for it at all: it says $55.50 while the real legacy payout is $53.26, a $2.24 gap almost fifty percent wider than the current-schedule gap on the identical sale.

How much does the flat 10% rule overstate payout, in dollars?

On this page's worked example — a $45 item plus $15 buyer-paid shipping — the flat 10% guess says $55.50. The real payout is $54.00 on the current schedule (a $1.50 overstatement) and $53.26 on a legacy listing (a $2.24 overstatement). Across 20 such sales in a month, that is $30.00 missing on the current schedule and $44.80 missing on a legacy one — money the guess never accounted for, not money Mercari is taking unfairly.

Is the flat 10% rule ever exactly right?

Yes — on a current-schedule listing with no buyer-paid shipping. A $50 item with free shipping (the cost folded into the item price) pays exactly 10% of $50, or $5.00, for a $45.00 payout under both the guess and the real schedule, because there is no shipping for the guess to miss and no legacy processing fee to omit. The gap only opens once shipping is charged separately or the listing predates January 6, 2025.

Does this calculator know which schedule my listing is on?

No, and it does not try to guess. You tell it with the legacy-schedule checkbox — checked for a listing made before January 6, 2025 that has not since been edited, unchecked for anything created or edited on or after that date — and it runs the exact arithmetic for whichever schedule you select, plus a side-by-side comparison of both, rather than assuming a flat rate that ignores the question.
