This calculator vs. doing Mercari fee math yourself

10% of the item price is the easy part, and most sellers can do that in their head. What mental math cannot see is which of Mercari's two fee schedules a listing actually runs, or that the current schedule's 10% reaches buyer-paid shipping too — so the same "just take 10%" instinct produces a confidently wrong number the moment shipping or an older listing is involved.

What each approach actually does

Doing it yourself gets the right answer for the one case where the math really is just 10% of the item price: a current-schedule sale (listing created or updated on or after January 6, 2025) with no buyer-paid shipping. Add buyer-paid shipping, or an older listing that might still be on the legacy schedule, and mental math has to guess at two things it cannot see from the headline rate alone — what the fee base includes, and which schedule applies at all.

This calculator already applies the right formula for whichever schedule you tell it: the current schedule's 10% on item price plus buyer-paid shipping with no separate processing fee, or the legacy schedule's 10% on item price only plus a 2.9% + $0.50 processing fee — and it can compare both side by side when you are not sure which one an old, unedited listing is still running.

Common questions

Isn't Mercari's fee just 10% -- why would I get the math wrong doing it myself?

Because 10% of what is the part that trips people up, not the rate itself. Under the current schedule (listings created or updated on or after January 6, 2025), the 10% applies to the item price plus any shipping the buyer paid -- so a $20 item with $6 buyer-paid shipping pays 10% of $26 ($2.60), not 10% of the $20 item price alone ($2.00). Doing the multiplication in your head against the wrong base gives you a confidently wrong number every time, not an occasional slip.

When is doing it yourself actually fine?

A current-schedule sale with no buyer-paid shipping: take 10% of the item price and you have the fee, correctly, in your head. The moment shipping enters the picture, or the listing is old enough that you are not sure which schedule it runs, mental math starts guessing at inputs it cannot see -- which schedule, and what the fee base actually includes -- rather than just doing arithmetic wrong.

What does manual math most often get wrong here?

Three things, in rough order of how often they bite: forgetting the current schedule's 10% reaches buyer-paid shipping, not just the item price; forgetting that a legacy (pre-January-2025, unedited) listing adds a separate 2.9% + $0.50 processing fee on top of its 10% selling fee, so the two schedules are not both simply '10%'; and folding the buyer's 3.6% Buyer Protection Fee into the seller-side deduction, when it is charged to the buyer on top of what they pay and never reduces the seller's payout at all.

Does this replace checking my actual payout statement?

No, and it is not built to. Both a mental calculation and this calculator are estimates from the fee schedule as published; Mercari can change that schedule, and your own payout statement is the only record of what a specific sale actually settled at. Verify against that statement before you rely on either estimate for pricing decisions.
