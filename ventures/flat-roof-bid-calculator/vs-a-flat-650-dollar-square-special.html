This calculator vs. a flat $650/square special

“$650 a square” is not a price for a flat roof — it is a price for one specific membrane, scope, and extras package, quietly applied to every roof that calls in.

What a flat $650/square special is

A flat rate per square is the fastest way to quote a low-slope roof: multiply the roof's squares by a number and read off a price. Property owners shop it because it is easy to compare between bids, and it is the number a prospect holding a competitor's postcard will ask you to match.

The number behind it was set for one roof — a 45-mil TPO recover over an existing one-story garage, no tear-off, no wet insulation, no parapet, no drains beyond a couple already there — and then reused for every roof afterward, whether or not the next one looks anything like the one the rate was built on.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the membrane (TPO 45/60/80-mil, EPDM, PVC, or modified bitumen), scope (recover, tear-off, or tear-off plus wet insulation), slope, stories, plan dimensions, parapet, drains, HVAC curbs, walk pads, cover board, and permit, plus your own labor hours and material costs. It prices the membrane, tear-off, insulation, and every extra separately by kit, then returns the walk-away price and a bid price above your target margin — for this roof, not a generic one.

What a flat $650/square special doesWhat this calculator does instead
Charges the same $/square whether the membrane is 45-mil TPO or 80-mil welded TPO — even though the 80-mil kit alone costs 79% more material per square and adds welder hours a 45-mil recover never touches. Prices membrane hours and material by mil and type, so an 80-mil welded roof is never quoted off a 45-mil recover rate.
Has no line for tear-off or wet insulation, regardless of whether the job is a recover over an existing roof or a full tear-off to deck with two layers of polyiso. Prices tear-off hours, dumpster cost, and insulation hours and material separately by scope, so a full tear-off with wet insulation is never priced the same as a recover.
Has no line for parapet flashing, drains, or HVAC curbs, regardless of how many the roof needs. Prices parapet lineal feet, drain count, and curb count separately, so a roof ringed in parapet with six drains and four curbs costs more than a plain garage deck.
Has no line for cover board, a permit allowance, or extra hours for a two- or three-story deck. Prices cover board, permit fee, and story-height hours separately when scoped, so a multi-story commercial tear-off is never priced like a one-story recover.
Gives the estimator a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the walk-away price and the dollar gap against what a flat rate would have quoted the same roof, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before you commit to it.

A flat rate is not wrong because it is simple — it is wrong when it is applied outside the one roof it was built for, and nothing about a per-square number tells you when that has happened. This calculator does not know your local market; it replaces the arithmetic a flat rate skips.

Common questions

Why does a flat $650/square special get a flat-roof quote wrong?

Because the cost driver is membrane type, recover versus tear-off, insulation, parapet, drains, and curbs, not roof squares alone. An 80-mil TPO tear-off with two layers of polyiso, a welder, parapet flashing, drains, and HVAC curbs needs far more labor and material per square than a 45-mil recover over an existing roof -- none of which scale with square count the way a flat rate assumes. A $650/square special charges both the same.

What does a flat $650/square special usually leave out entirely?

Tear-off, insulation, parapet flashing, drains, HVAC curbs, cover board, and welding. A recover job with an existing roof left in place is doing far less than a full tear-off to deck with two layers of wet insulation, 80 lineal feet of parapet, six drains, four curbs, and a welded 80-mil membrane -- and a per-square number has no line for any of it.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own shipped presets, at its own shipped shop rates ($36/hr crew wage, 32% burden, $160 travel and equipment, 15% overhead, a 25% target margin, $350 mobilization, $425 permit): a 12-square TPO-45 recover on a 40x30 one-story garage costs $4,895.79 to build. The flat $650/square special charges $7,800.00 for that roof -- $2,904.21 over cost, and $1,272.28 over the calculator's own bid price. Applied to a 40.04-square 80x50 commercial TPO-80 tear-off with two layers of polyiso, 80 lf of parapet, six drains, four HVAC curbs, and cover board, the same $650/square special charges only $26,026.00 -- $21,908.71 below that roof's own $47,934.71 walk-away cost, before a dollar of profit, and $37,886.94 below the calculator's own bid price.

Is a flat $650/square special ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of near-identical roofs -- same membrane, same scope, no parapet or curbs either time -- a flat rate can land close, and on this calculator's own cheap-end preset it lands generous. The risk is applying it outside that band without noticing, because the rate does not know which roof it was built on and neither does the estimator until membrane, scope, insulation, and extras are actually spelled out.

Does this calculator determine building code, wind-uplift rating, or engineering requirements for the roof?

No. The membrane rates, waste, and insulation figures behind this calculator are common starting points for residential and light-commercial low-slope bids. They are not a building-code opinion, a permit, a wind-uplift rating, or an engineering stamp, and neither this page nor the calculator it describes determines any of those things. Confirm the jurisdiction and a qualified professional before sending a price, especially on multi-story or high-wind-zone work.
