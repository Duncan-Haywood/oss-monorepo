This calendar vs. the flat 25-30% set-aside rule

Setting aside a flat 25-30% of every payment is the savings habit most freelancers and gig workers hear first, usually from another freelancer or a "how much to save for taxes" search result. It is a decent way to stop yourself from spending money you will owe later. It is not a payment plan: it does not say how much of what you have saved is actually due, on which date, or whether 25-30% is anywhere near your real number.

That makes it useful for a different moment than this page: building the savings habit before you have a prior-year return to anchor a real number to. This page does not ask you to guess a percentage of revenue. It takes numbers you already have — last year's total tax, and optionally this year's own estimate — applies the IRS's own published safe-harbor formula (the smaller of 90% of this year's estimated tax or 100%/110% of last year's tax), and turns the result into four real due dates, adjusted for weekends and federal holidays, plus a downloadable calendar file with a reminder on each one.

The two are not competing for the same job. A flat percentage set-aside is a habit worth keeping all year, especially early on when you have no prior-year return to anchor to. This calendar is what turns whatever you have already saved into an exact required payment and a due date you can trust, once you have last year's number to work from.

This calendar needs no account and no app install: it is a static page, every number is computed in your browser, and the downloaded .ics file keeps working in your calendar app long after you close this tab.

Common questions

Should I just set aside a flat 25-30% of every payment for taxes?

As a savings habit, it is a reasonable starting point — it keeps money out of your checking account before you spend it. But it is not a payment plan: it does not say how much of what you have saved is actually due on a given date, or whether 25-30% is even close to your real safe-harbor requirement, which depends on last year's actual tax and (for higher earners) last year's adjusted gross income, not a round percentage of this year's revenue.

What does a flat percentage rule get wrong?

It is anchored to the wrong number. The IRS safe harbor is the smaller of 90% of this year's estimated tax or 100% of last year's total tax (110% above the income threshold) — a figure driven by your actual tax liability, deductions, and filing status, not a flat share of revenue. A filer with heavy deductions can over-save under a flat rule; a filer whose income jumped can under-save and still owe a penalty even while feeling like they set aside "enough."

Does a flat percentage rule tell me when to actually pay?

No. It is silent on due dates entirely — it answers "how much to set aside," not "how much is due on which date." The nominal quarterly dates shift for weekends and federal holidays, sometimes by more than a day, so a filer relying only on a savings habit still has to separately track four dates a year or risk a late payment even with the money already set aside.

Is anything I enter uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculator this page links to runs entirely in your browser; nothing you enter is uploaded.
