What this does and doesn't do

This computes the IRS safe harbor — the smaller of 90% of this year's estimated tax or 100% (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000, or $75,000 married filing separately) of last year's tax — and splits it evenly across four quarters, the same default method IRS Form 2210 uses. It does not file anything, does not know your actual final tax bill, and does not model the Annualized Income Installment Method for income that arrived unevenly across the year. Treat every number here as a planning target to verify against the current year's official IRS instructions or a tax professional before paying, never as a filed amount or a determination made on your behalf.

Common questions

How much estimated tax do I actually have to pay each quarter?

The IRS safe harbor is the smaller of 90% of this year's estimated tax or 100% of last year's total tax (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately). Paying that amount avoids an underpayment penalty even if your actual final tax bill is higher. This calculator applies that rule to your own numbers and splits the result evenly across the four quarters.

Are the quarterly due dates always April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15?

Those are the nominal dates, but each one shifts to the next business day when it falls on a weekend or a federal holiday, and the shift can chain through more than one day. Recent real examples: the Q1 2022 deadline moved to April 18 (Emancipation Day observed the 15th, then a weekend), and the Q4 2023 deadline moved to January 16, 2024 because January 15 was Martin Luther King Jr. Day that year. This tool computes the adjusted date for the tax year you enter rather than assuming the 15th.

What if my income is uneven across the year?

This tool splits the annual requirement into four equal quarters, which is the IRS's own default (regular) method under Form 2210. If your income arrives unevenly — a seasonal business, one large late-year payment — the Annualized Income Installment Method (Form 2210 Schedule AI) can lower or eliminate a penalty on the earlier, lower-income quarters. This tool does not compute that method; a tax professional or the IRS instructions for Form 2210 can.

What if I already missed or underpaid an earlier quarter?

This calculator tracks that too, once you enter what you've actually paid per quarter. It compares the total required by each due date that has already passed against the total you've actually paid across every quarter, not just one row at a time. If you're behind, the schedule shows a catch-up amount for the next payment — larger than the flat quarterly share, sized to get back to the safe-harbor pace in one payment. If you paid ahead of schedule instead, the next payment can be smaller, down to zero, and is never shown as negative.
