Questions

What are FF and FL?

FF (flatness) measures how bumpy a floor is over short distances, from the change in slope between readings 12 inches apart. FL (levelness) measures how far the floor wanders from level over 10 feet. Higher is better for both, and the scale is linear: a floor with FF 50 is about twice as flat as one with FF 25. ASTM E1155 is the US test method that defines them.

What readings do I need?

Elevations every 12 inches along straight sample lines, from a dipstick-type profiler, a laser level and rod, a total station or a robotic profiler, or a point cloud from a laser scanner. For FL a line needs at least 11 consecutive readings (10 feet). The tool pools every line in a section, as the method does.

How is the overall F-number for several sections worked out?

Each section's FF and FL come from all its readings pooled. The overall value for the floor is the area-weighted mean of the section values, the reading of ASTM E1155 section 9.15 that secondary copies of the standard give. Check it against your own copy of the standard before a contract result rests on it.

Is the grind estimate a grind plan?

No. It lowers the readings that stand highest above their neighbours until the section's FF reaches your target, and reports how many readings that took and how deep. It sees the floor only every 12 inches, cannot add material, and ignores what grinding does to wear surfaces, hardeners or embedded items. Use it to judge whether remediation is a small or a large job, then plan it with the flooring contractor.

Is my data uploaded?

No. The file is read and every calculation runs in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. An optional anonymous counter in this browser's local storage records only which features were used and the day.
