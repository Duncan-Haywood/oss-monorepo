This calculator vs. HomeLight's Net Proceeds Calculator

HomeLight's Net Proceeds Calculator is a free estimator, published as part of an agent-matching business, that starts from an estimated home value to give a rough sense of proceeds before you have an offer. This page starts from an offer you actually have and compares two offers side by side — with nothing sent to a server and no expert-matching flow.

What each one is for

HomeLight's Net Proceeds Calculator is a free estimator on HomeLight's own site. Per HomeLight's own description, it takes an estimated home value, a remaining mortgage balance, real estate agent commissions, and seller preparation and closing costs, and produces what HomeLight itself calls a rough estimate based on a traditional, agent-assisted home sale. HomeLight separately publishes a Cash Offer Comparison Tool that, per its own description, compares a cash offer side by side with an agent-led sale estimate — a comparison between two different sale paths, not between two real offers of the same kind.

This page starts one step later: after a listing agreement exists and an offer is on the table. It takes the actual sale price on that offer (or two competing offers) and the actual rates from your listing agreement and closing estimate — not an estimated home value — and turns them into cash to the seller.

HomeLight is a real estate agent-matching company, and its own net-proceeds content directs a seller toward "Talk to an Expert" for a full cost and proceeds breakdown — a reasonable next step for a business built on referring sellers to agents, and worth knowing plainly rather than discovering partway through. This page has no expert-matching flow, no lead form, and no account: it is arithmetic that runs on your own device and stops there.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or HomeLight's Net Proceeds Calculator?

Different starting points. HomeLight's Net Proceeds Calculator, per HomeLight's own description of it, takes an estimated home value, a remaining mortgage balance, real estate agent commissions, and seller preparation and closing costs, and produces what HomeLight itself calls a rough estimate based on a traditional, agent-assisted home sale — useful before you have listed or received an offer. This page starts from a real number: the price on an offer you actually received (or two, side by side), and turns it into cash at closing using the rates from your own listing agreement and closing estimate rather than an estimated value. Use HomeLight's tool for an early, rough estimate; use this one once offers exist and you are deciding between them.

Does HomeLight's calculator compare two competing offers side by side?

Not the same comparison. HomeLight publishes a separate Cash Offer Comparison Tool that, per HomeLight's own description, compares a cash offer side by side with an agent-led sale estimate — one sale path against another, not two real offers of the same kind that a seller has actually received. This page's offer comparison holds the loan payoff and closing-cost assumptions constant and puts two full offers next to each other, so the number that changes is the one that actually differs between them.

Where does HomeLight's calculator send me next?

HomeLight is a real estate agent-matching company, and its own net-proceeds content directs sellers to "Talk to an Expert" for a full cost and proceeds breakdown — consistent with a business built on referring sellers to agents. That is a reasonable thing for HomeLight to offer and not a criticism of it. This page has no expert-matching flow of any kind: it is a static calculator with no lead capture.

Does anything I type here leave my browser?

No. This calculator runs entirely on your own device: there is no account, no address lookup, and nothing you enter is sent anywhere. HomeLight's calculator runs on HomeLight's own site as part of a real estate agent-matching business, which is reasonable for what it is and worth knowing if you would rather not have a proceeds estimate tied to an agent-referral flow.
