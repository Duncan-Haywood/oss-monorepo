Declared policy field reference

Every field is optional — a field you leave out is reported not policed, never assumed. Booleans: required_pull_request_reviews is entered as a plain required_approving_review_count number; the rest (require_code_owner_reviews, require_last_push_approval, enforce_admins, allow_force_pushes, allow_deletions, required_conversation_resolution, required_linear_history, required_signatures, strict_status_checks) are true/false. required_status_check_contexts is a comma-separated list (or a JSON array) of check names that must all be present — extra actual checks beyond the list are fine.

Common questions

How does branch protection drift from what was intended?

Someone disables a rule for one urgent hotfix — turns off required reviews, or enables force pushes to fix a bad merge — and the settings page gives no reminder to turn it back on. Nothing breaks visibly until the next force push overwrites history, or the next merge lands with no review, on a rule everyone still believed was enforced.

Does this tool read my branch protection settings for me?

No. It makes no network calls at all. Fetch your own settings with gh api repos/:owner/:repo/branches/:branch/protection, paste the JSON in, write your declared policy in the second box, and the comparison happens entirely in this page.

What if my policy doesn't mention a rule?

It is reported as "not policed", never silently treated as a pass or a fail. A policy that only cares about force pushes says nothing about signed commits, and this tool will not invent an opinion your policy never stated.

What if the actual settings are stricter than the policy requires?

That is reported as "stronger than policy", not drift. Drift specifically means weaker than declared — more required reviewers than the policy demands, or an extra required status check, is never flagged as a problem.

Does a clean result mean my branch protection is fully correct?

No. It checks only the rules your own policy declares, against the specific fields GitHub's branch-protection API exposes. It is not a security audit, a compliance attestation, or a review of whether the policy itself is any good.
