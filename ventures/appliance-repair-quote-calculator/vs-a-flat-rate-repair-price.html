This calculator vs. a flat rate repair price

“Refrigerator not cooling, $635, done” is not a price for a repair — it is a price for one guess at what is actually wrong, quietly applied to every call that starts with the same complaint.

What a flat rate repair price is

A flat rate repair price comes from a price book: a fixed dollar figure per job category, looked up by the customer's complaint and quoted before the unit is opened up. It is fast to quote over the phone, and it is the number a customer holding a competitor's flat rate will ask you to match.

The number behind each category was set once, usually from the most common version of that complaint — a simple part with a short repair — and then reused for every call in that category, whether the actual fault turns out to be the common one or a much bigger job hiding behind the same symptom.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter your diagnostic, repair, and travel hours, your loaded labor rate, trip cost, parts cost and your own markup on it, a callback or warranty reserve, your overhead rate, and your target margin. It prices the labor, the marked-up parts, and the reserve as their own lines and returns the walk-away price, the target-margin quote, and your effective hourly rate — for this call, not a book price for every call under the same complaint.

Prices parts at your own cost with your own markup applied, so a call that turns up an expensive part costs more than one that does not — on this call, not the category average.
Has one labor figure baked into the category price, with no separate line for a repair that runs longer than the common case. Prices diagnostic, repair, and travel hours separately at your loaded rate, so a longer repair is priced for the hours it actually takes.
Has no line for a callback or warranty reserve — if the fix does not hold, that cost comes out of the same flat number. Adds a callback/warranty reserve as its own percentage of direct cost, so a repeat visit is already funded rather than eating the job's margin.

Gives the technician a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the walk-away price and the effective hourly rate, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before you say a number at the door.

Common questions

Why does a flat rate repair price get an appliance repair wrong?

Because a flat rate price book prices the complaint, not the repair. "Refrigerator not cooling" is one line in the book with one price attached, whether the actual fault is a $10 evaporator fan that swaps in under an hour or a sealed-system compressor that needs recovery, brazing, a new drier, recharge, and half a day. Two calls that sound identical on the phone can carry completely different parts cost and repair hours once the tech is actually inside the unit, and a price set from the symptom alone cannot tell them apart.

What does a flat rate repair price usually leave out entirely?

Your own labor rate, your parts markup, a callback or warranty reserve, and overhead. A price-book rate is usually set once, off an averaged or vendor-supplied job, and then quoted for every call in that category regardless of what your diagnostic actually finds, what you pay for the part that day, or what your shop's real burden and overhead run. It has no line that moves when the repair turns out bigger than the category suggested.

Is a flat rate repair price ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow, genuinely common repair -- the same part, the same access, the same hours, most times you see that complaint -- a flat rate number can land close, because that is probably the job the price was averaged from. The risk is the call that turns out to be the harder version of the same symptom, because the price does not know which one it is until the panel is off, and by then the flat number has often already been quoted at the door.

Does this calculator know my local labor rates or parts costs?

No. It has no price book and no data on your local labor market or parts supplier pricing. It takes your diagnostic, repair, and travel hours, your loaded labor rate, trip cost, parts cost and your own markup on it, a callback/warranty reserve, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a price for this call -- the arithmetic a flat rate price skips, not a market survey.
