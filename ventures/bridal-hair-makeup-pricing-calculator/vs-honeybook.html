This calculator vs. HoneyBook

Two different shapes: HoneyBook runs a bridal hair and makeup business's client relationship end to end -- proposal, contract, invoice and payment -- as an ongoing account. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question -- what should this wedding's booking cost, given the bride, bridal party, hair-only, makeup-only and kids' services on it, the early-start fee, mileage, and whether the artists already booked can finish the whole group before the must-be-ready deadline.

What each one is for

HoneyBook is a client management platform widely used by creative and service businesses that book project-based work, and it is well documented as one of the industry-standard tools among bridal hair and makeup artists specifically -- HoneyBook publishes its own "Bridal Hair & Makeup Proposal" and "Bridal Hair & Makeup Pricing Guide" templates for exactly this trade. Its signature feature is a combined proposal-contract-invoice document (its own term is a "smart file") that lets a bride review package options, sign the contract, and pay a deposit in one flow, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every client the business books. HoneyBook's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific plan names or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of platform describe building a proposal, collecting a signature and a deposit, and invoicing the remaining balance once a price already exists; none describe pricing the bride, bridal party, hair-only, makeup-only and kids' tiers against an early-start fee and mileage, or checking whether the artists already on the schedule can physically finish the group in time -- which is what this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what HoneyBook does. It does not manage a client pipeline, send a contract, collect a signature, or take a payment. It takes one wedding-day booking's services, prices each tier, applies the early-start fee and mileage, tops a small booking up to a service minimum, credits a trial fee against the wedding-day balance so the discount is visible rather than hidden in the timing, and checks the booked artists' capacity against the must-be-ready deadline -- then reports how many additional artists the schedule needs, if any.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or HoneyBook?

They do different jobs, and a mobile bridal hair and makeup artist may well end up using both. HoneyBook is a client management platform widely used across the wedding industry -- it publishes its own bridal hair and makeup proposal and pricing-guide templates -- built around an account-based "smart file" that combines a proposal, contract and invoice into one document a bride reviews, signs and pays a deposit against. It manages the client relationship on an ongoing basis across every booking a business takes. This calculator does one narrower thing: given the bride, bridal party, hair-only, makeup-only and kids' services on one wedding-day booking, it prices each tier, applies an early-start fee and mileage, tops a small booking up to a service minimum, credits a trial fee against the balance, and checks whether the artists already booked can finish the whole group before the must-be-ready deadline -- flagging exactly how many more artists the schedule needs. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. An artist already managing an ongoing client pipeline, sending contracts, and collecting payments is better served by HoneyBook or a comparable platform; an artist who wants an instant, private total and staffing check for one wedding's booking is in the right place here.
