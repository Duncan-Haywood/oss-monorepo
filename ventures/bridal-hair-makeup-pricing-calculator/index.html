Common questions

Does crediting the trial fee toward the wedding day cost me money?

Yes, and that is easy to miss. "Your trial fee comes off your wedding day total if you book" reads as goodwill, but it lowers the combined amount you collect across both bookings by up to the trial fee itself — the same booking is worth less to you than the identical booking without that policy. This calculator shows the total contract value both ways so the discount is a number you chose rather than one that fell out of a rate card nobody added up.

How do I know if I need a second artist?

Add up every person's service minutes plus a buffer for setup and product changes, then compare that to the minutes available from arrival to the moment the bride has to be dressed, multiplied by the artists already booked. If the group needs more minutes than that window has, one artist cannot finish everyone on time no matter how early they start within the window — the fix is an additional artist, not a faster pace. The calculator runs this check and reports exactly how many more artists the schedule needs.

Why does the bride get extra minutes on top of the bridal-party rate?

A bride's look is typically more detailed and often includes a final check right before photos, so it takes longer than the identical service on a bridal-party member. Pricing the bride separately from the party, and scheduling extra minutes for her specifically, keeps both the price and the timeline honest about that difference instead of averaging it away.

What is the point of a dollar service minimum if I also have a headcount minimum?

They answer different questions. A headcount minimum is about whether a small booking is worth the morning's commitment at all, regardless of price. A dollar minimum is the enforceable floor once you have decided to take it — it tops up a small or heavily-discounted booking to a total that still covers your time and travel, rather than declining it outright.
