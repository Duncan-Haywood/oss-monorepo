    Common questions

    Why is the tub itself half the price?

    Because that is what the job mostly is. On the defaults the unit and materials invoice at $1,875.60 against $643.19 of labor for 11.78 hours, in a $3,757.32 price. Walk-in tubs vary enormously in dealer cost — a soaker and a hydrotherapy unit with a heated seat are not the same purchase — so your invoice cost, not your hourly rate, is what decides whether this bid works.

    Is 11.78 hours realistic when installers quote two days?

    Those are usually two different scopes. This is the tub swap: demolition of the existing unit, setting and levelling, supply and drain connection, and the surround. The second day in a typical retail quote is normally tile or wall-panel finishing, an electrical circuit for the pump and heater, or a door widening — real work, but it is not in this number unless you add it.

    What is easy to miss when quoting one of these?

    Three things, in rough order of how often they blow up a job: the drain, because a walk-in tub needs a fast-drain path and an existing shallow trap arm may not give one; the electrical, since most powered units want a dedicated GFCI circuit; and getting the unit into the room at all — they are large, rigid, and frequently will not pass an existing bathroom door. Walk those three before you commit to a price.

    Should I be making medical or accessibility claims in the proposal?

    No, and this tool deliberately produces none. A walk-in tub is often bought for a mobility or safety reason, but grab-bar placement, entry height, and whether a specific unit suits a specific person are clinical and code questions, not pricing ones. Quote the installation; leave the suitability judgment to an occupational therapist or the applicable accessibility standard.
