Should I install a customer-supplied walk-in tub?

Owners call with a crate in the garage: “I already bought the walk-in tub. Can you just set it for $1,499?” That sounds like free margin because the $4,200 whirlpool invoice left the bid. It did not leave the trip, the set hours, the grab bars, or the chance that the drain is on the other end of the alcove.

Zeroing the tub invoice is not a free set

A contractor-supplied whirlpool still carries the unit invoice. An owner-supplied tub that actually fits zeros that line and keeps the same set hours. On a typical aging-in-place alcove swap — whirlpool, outward door, grab bars, handheld, permit, $42 wage, 30% burden, 15% overhead, 20% target margin, 14 miles — the you-supply bid is $8,640.43. Zeroing the $4,200 tub drops it to $2,602.93, but the owner-supplied walk-away stays at $2,082.34. Taking the Facebook special to “just set what they bought” still loses $583.34.

Wrong unit is a second trip

Showroom and big-box walk-in tubs default to a 60-inch alcove and a left- or right-hand drain. Plenty of baths are a 54-inch opening, a shower conversion, or the opposite rough-in. Setting the wrong unit means a fit pass or a drive back to exchange it. Price that callback on the same bath before you agree. The calculator adds a starting second trip, two fit hours, and a $95 restock fee on top of the rates you already typed.

This is not a walk-in shower, a toilet, or a stair lift

A walk-in shower is tile, slope, and glass. A toilet is a commode swap. A stair lift is a chair on a rail. A hot tub is an outdoor crane and a pad. This prices a walk-in tub with a door: who bought the unit, whether it fits the alcove, and the $1,499 just-set special.

How to use the comparison

Open the walk-in tub bid calculator, enter the bath you are standing in, and put their install-only number in “price you’re considering.” The who-supplies table scores that number against you-supply, owner-supplies-it-fits, and owner-supplies-wrong-unit. Nothing is uploaded.
