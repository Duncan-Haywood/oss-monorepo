The model, and what it leaves out

  • Etsy fees = revenue × (transaction % + processing %) + orders × per-order fee. These rates are approximations you can edit, not a quote.
  • Profit before ads = revenue × your margin − Etsy fees. Profit after ads & fees = that minus ad spend, crediting the ads with every order.
  • Break-even ROAS = revenue ÷ profit before ads. Below it the listing loses money even if the ad caused every order.
  • Actions. Pause: spend with no order, or a loss crediting every order to the ad. Lower: profitable then, but a loss after removing your sold-anyway share. Keep: profitable at that share.
  • One margin for every listing. Real margins differ by item, so a result near a threshold is a prompt to check that item's own cost, not a finding.
  • Not modelled: Offsite Ads fees, listing fees, refunds after the report period, currency conversion, sales tax and VAT, regional fee differences, and any effect ads have on a listing's organic ranking.

Common questions

Where do I get the Etsy Ads data?

In Shop Manager open Stats, then Marketing, then Etsy Ads, and download the listing-level table for a date range as CSV. Drop the file here or paste its text. Column names vary, so the tool matches common spellings of listing, views, clicks, orders, revenue and spend, and tells you if it cannot find Revenue or Spend.

Why does it ask for my margin and Etsy fees?

Etsy's dashboard shows ad revenue and spend but not what the item cost you, and ROAS ignores Etsy's own fees. Margin is profit as a share of revenue after item cost, materials and shipping you pay but before Etsy fees and ads. The fee boxes start at roughly 6.5% transaction, 3% processing and $0.25 per order, which are approximations: change them to match your own statement.

What is break-even ROAS?

The ROAS at which ad spend equals the profit you would have made on the credited orders before ads. Below it the listing loses money even if the ad caused every order. It is higher than you might expect because fees and costs come out of revenue first.

What does 'would have sold anyway' do?

Etsy credits an ad with orders that follow an ad click, and some of those buyers would have bought anyway. The share you enter is removed from the profit before ads when judging each listing. It is your guess, not a measurement; try 0, 30 and 50.

How are pause, lower and keep decided?

Pause: spend with no order, or a loss even if the ad caused every order. Lower: profitable if the ad caused every order but a loss at your sold-anyway share. Keep: still profitable at that share. These are prompts to look, not instructions, and small samples are noisy.
