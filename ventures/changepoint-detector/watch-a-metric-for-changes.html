Get alerted when a metric really shifts, not every time it wobbles

Daily sign-ups, orders, a sensor reading, a conversion rate. You do not want a ping for every odd day. You want to hear once, within a few days, when the level has moved for good. This watch refits your series after each new row with Bayesian online changepoint detection (Adams and MacKay, 2007; PELT, Killick et al. 2012, as an option) and posts to Slack, Discord, Teams or Mattermost once per change, after five points of evidence.

What a message looks like

orders changed from 2026-05-01: level up from 49.9 to 54.9 (5.62 noise sd), Bayesian probability 98%; 7 points since.

From the tool's own test: 120 quiet days at about 50, then a step to 55. The run on day 124 says "watching: a possible change from 2026-05-01"; the run on day 127 posts the message above; later runs stay silent about it.

So the default is quiet and misses small steps: it reports a 2-sd step about three times in four and a 1-sd step rarely. If missing a change costs you more than a false alarm, run with --detector pelt (all 52 marked changes reached, about three times the alerts) or --detector bocpd-window, which sums the Bayesian probability over five neighbouring points (10 of 10 two-sd steps on fresh seeds; 46 of 52 marked changes; 48 alerts per 1,000 checks; it was tested as the default and failed our false-alarm bar).

The rule for picking the defaults was written down before the first run, and the labelled real series were scored once afterwards without changing anything. Script and full output: eval/watch-replay.mjs, eval/watch-replay.out.txt.

Set it up in your own GitHub account

  1. Make the folder (no clone of our repository needed; Node 18+ and curl): 
    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Duncan-Haywood/oss-monorepo/main/ventures/changepoint-detector/deploy/make-watch-repo.sh | sh -s -- metric-watch
  2. Put your series in metric-watch/data/metric.csv (a date column and a value column, oldest first), or plan to use a CSV link instead.
  3. Push the folder to a new GitHub repository. In its Settings > Secrets and variables > Actions, add the variable METRIC_CSV (data/metric.csv or the link) and, for chat messages, the secret WATCH_WEBHOOK_URL. With no webhook, a new change fails the run and GitHub emails you.
  4. Run the watch-metric workflow once by hand. The first run records the changes already in your history as the baseline and does not alert on them; after that it runs daily.

Or anywhere with Node: node watch.mjs data.csv --state watch-state.json, from cron, a CI job or a notebook. Options for counts (--model poisson), volatility (--model meanvar), a weekly cycle (--period 7) and growth (--log) are in node watch.mjs --help.

What you would use otherwise

  • A fixed threshold. Simple, but you have to know the level in advance, and a slow series crosses it on noise.
  • Anomaly detection in a monitoring or BI product. Good at single odd points and usually part of a paid plan. A lasting step in level is a different question, and it is the one this answers.
  • R's changepoint or Python's ruptures in a scheduled script. More complete methods, but you write the scheduling, the "already told you" memory and the message yourself. This is that glue, done and tested.

Limits

  • A series whose level wanders all the time (a bank balance, an exchange rate, a price) will keep alerting: on TCPD's bank series the default sent 42 alerts in 465 checks, none at a marked change. Watch a quantity that holds a level between changes, or its daily change instead of its level.
  • Five points of evidence means a change is reported no sooner than five rows after it. Use --confirm 3 to hear sooner at the cost of more false alarms.
  • It reports when the data changed, not why. Whether your release, a holiday or an outage caused it needs what you know about that date.

Questions

How is this different from a threshold or anomaly alert?

A threshold or anomaly alert fires on single odd points. This watch asks whether the level of the series has changed for good: it refits the series each day with Bayesian online changepoint detection (Adams and MacKay 2007) and reports a change once five new points sit on its far side, then never again for that change.

Where does it run, and who sees my data?

In a GitHub repository you own, on GitHub Actions, or on any machine with Node 18 or later. Your data and your webhook URL stay in your account; nothing is sent to the people who made the tool.

How many false alarms should I expect?

Measured by replaying series one point at a time: on 8,000 daily checks of independent noise with no change, none; on sticky (autocorrelated) noise, about 7 per 1,000 checks. On 31 real labelled series from the Turing Change Point Dataset it raised 30 alerts per 1,000 checks, a third of them at a change a human annotator marked; most of the rest were on wandering series such as a bank balance and exchange rates, where the level never holds still.
