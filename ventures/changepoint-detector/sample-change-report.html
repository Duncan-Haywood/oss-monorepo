What changed

Agreed changes: Every one of 6 settings agrees on 1 change: 1899 (down). On 7 labelled real series held out from tuning (Turing Change Point Dataset), keeping only these agreed changes scored F1 0.71, against 0.30 for the single level model whose results follow and 0.64 for reporting no change at all.

Level model alone: PELT splits the 100 points of volume into 2 segments (1 change in level). From 1899 (after 1898) the mean moves down from 1098 to 850, 2.15 noise standard deviations; the Bayesian method puts 79% probability on a change within 3 points of it.

Analyst's notes

  • Annual flow at Aswan, 1871-1970 (public domain).
  • The 1898 change matches the literature (Cobb 1978).

Assumptions and limits. Changes are found with PELT (Killick, Fearnhead and Eckley 2012, exact, penalised) and probabilities with Bayesian online changepoint detection (Adams and MacKay 2007). Both treat points inside a segment as independent around a constant level; strong seasonality or autocorrelation can show up as extra changes. A detected change shows that the data before and after are better described as two segments than one. It does not show what caused the change. 100 points analysed.
