Analyst's notes

  • Total fertility rate (births per 1,000 women), Canada 1931-1968; carData::Hartnagel, column tfr, via Rdatasets.
  • The plain level model cuts this series into 8 flat segments (7 changes: 1933, 1942, 1946, 1953, 1963, 1965, 1967); letting the level wander between jumps leaves 3.
  • 1946 is the first full post-war year, the start of the baby boom; 1965 and 1967 are the steepest steps of its end.

Assumptions and limits. Between jumps the level is modelled as a random walk (it may wander a little every step) with independent Gaussian noise around it, following Gaetano Romano, Guillem Rigaill, Vincent Runge and Paul Fearnhead (Journal of the American Statistical Association (2022), doi 10.1080/01621459.2021.1909598); each stretch is scored by a local-level Kalman filter and the best set of jumps is found exactly. Slow wander is not counted as a change, so a gradual shift spread over many steps will not be listed. Seasonality, a trend other than a steady growth, autocorrelated noise and heavy tails are not modelled; heavy-tailed or bursty series (prices, counts) can still show extra jumps. A listed jump shows that the data are better described with a sudden step there than by wander alone. It does not show what caused the change. 38 points analysed.
