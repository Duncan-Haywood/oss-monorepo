How much of your state and local tax is actually deductible now?

The federal cap on the SALT (state and local tax) itemized deduction jumped from a flat $10,000 to $40,000 for tax year 2025 (confirmed at $40,400 for 2026 by the IRS's own release) — but it phases down by 30% of the amount your income exceeds $500,000, never below a $10,000 floor, and reverts to the old flat $10,000 cap in 2030. This applies the exact cap, the phase-down, and shows whether the extra deduction even beats the standard deduction once you add it up.

1. Filing status, tax year & income

MAGI here is modified adjusted gross income — for most filers with no foreign income or certain exclusions, this is close to the AGI on your return. 2027-2029 figures are projected from the statute's own 1%/year formula — the IRS has only published official figures through 2026.

2. State and local tax paid, and your other itemized deductions

State & local tax paid is the total of property tax plus state/local income OR sales tax (whichever you itemize), before any cap is applied — your county tax bill and W-2 state withholding (or Schedule A worksheet) show this. Other itemized deductions is everything else on Schedule A — mortgage interest, charitable giving, medical expenses above the AGI floor. Standard deduction is your own reference figure for your filing status and tax year (see the reference table below) — enter it yourself so this tool never asserts a number it can't confirm applies to your return.

What this does not cover

  • Not a full tax return. This estimates one deduction and one comparison only — not your total tax liability, other deductions, credits, or Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT), which the SALT deduction can interact with separately.
  • State conformity. This is a FEDERAL deduction. Whether your state's own return allows a SALT deduction, and how much, is a separate question this tool does not answer.
  • Pass-through entity tax (PTET) workarounds — many states let business owners pay state tax at the entity level as a fully deductible business expense, sidestepping this cap entirely on the business side. This calculator only models the individual itemized deduction, not a PTET election.
  • 2027-2029 figures are projections, not official IRS numbers — see the FAQ.

Common questions

How much state and local tax can I deduct now?

Up to $40,000 for tax year 2025 ($20,000 if married filing separately), up from the prior flat $10,000 cap. The IRS has confirmed $40,400 ($20,200 MFS) for tax year 2026. The cap and its income threshold both rise 1% per year through 2029, then the whole increase reverts to a flat $10,000 cap starting tax year 2030 under current law.

What is the SALT cap phase-down?

For 2025, the $40,000 cap is reduced by 30% of the amount your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) exceeds $500,000 ($250,000 married filing separately) -- but it never drops below a $10,000 floor ($5,000 MFS). At a MAGI of roughly $600,000 ($300,000 MFS) or more, the cap is fully phased down to that floor and the increase provides no benefit at all.

Does the SALT deduction actually save me money if I would take the standard deduction anyway?

Only the portion that pushes your TOTAL itemized deductions past your standard deduction is genuinely additive. If your other itemized deductions (mortgage interest, charitable giving, etc.) already exceed the standard deduction on their own, every dollar of your SALT deduction saves you tax. If they don't, the first part of your SALT deduction just duplicates a benefit you would get anyway by not itemizing at all -- this calculator shows that split explicitly.

Are the 2027-2029 figures official?

No. The IRS has published official figures only through tax year 2026. Figures for 2027-2029 shown here are projected by applying the statute's own 1%-per-year indexing formula to the confirmed 2026 base, and are clearly labeled as projections, not official IRS figures -- verify against the IRS's own Revenue Procedure once it is published for that year.
