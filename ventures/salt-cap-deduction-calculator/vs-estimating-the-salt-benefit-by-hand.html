This calculator vs. estimating the SALT benefit by hand

The new SALT cap looks like a simple lookup — one number, $40,000 (or $40,400 for 2026). The problem is that the number that actually applies to you depends on a phase-down most headlines skip, and even the right cap can produce $0 of real benefit once compared against the standard deduction.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake estimating the SALT benefit by hand?

Assuming the whole $40,000 (or $40,400 for 2026) cap is deductible just because you paid that much in state and local tax, without checking whether your income triggers the 30% phase-down first. At $560,000 MAGI in 2026, for example, the real cap is only about $23,900 -- more than $16,000 below the headline figure.

What's the second most common mistake?

Treating the entire SALT deduction as pure savings without comparing total itemized deductions to the standard deduction first. If your other itemized deductions don't already clear the standard deduction on their own, part of your SALT deduction just duplicates a benefit you'd get anyway by not itemizing -- only the excess over the standard deduction is genuinely additive.

What about the phase-down math itself?

A by-hand estimate often gets the phase-down rate or the floor wrong -- it's 30% of the excess MAGI over the threshold (not 6%, the rate the separate senior deduction in this portfolio uses), and the cap never drops below a $10,000 floor ($5,000 MFS) no matter how high income goes.
