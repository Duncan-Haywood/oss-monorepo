This calculator vs. picking a rate off the going local price

Matching what other teachers post nearby tells you the number is competitive. It says nothing about whether it covers your own cancellations, prep and travel time, studio costs, or the income you actually need — the arithmetic this calculator does instead.

What picking the going local rate is

Checking what nearby teachers or studios charge and matching it — or pricing a little under it — is the fastest way to set a rate. It needs no arithmetic, and it keeps a new teacher's price from looking out of line with the market they are entering.

What it cannot do is see the week behind that number. The going rate is an average (or a guess at one) across teachers with different cancellation rates, different amounts of drive time, different prep habits, and different income needs. Copying it assumes your week looks like theirs.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter your lesson price, scheduled lessons per week, teaching weeks per year, cancellation rate, prep and travel minutes per lesson, monthly costs, and target annual pay. It returns the break-even price, the price that reaches your target, your current annual revenue, and the effective hourly rate once prep, travel, and cancelled slots are counted — for your own week, not the going rate for lessons in general.

One going rate, two very different weeks

Using this calculator's own figures: a teacher who checks nearby listings and settles on the going local rate of $50 a lesson, teaching 16 lessons a week for 44 weeks a year with a 15% cancellation rate, 10 minutes of prep and 25 minutes of travel per lesson (a house-call teacher), $120 a month in costs, and a $45,000 annual pay target, nets $28,480 a year across 1,114.7 real hours of scheduled, unpaid, and travel time — an effective hourly of just $25.55, and $16,520 short of the stated target. The price that actually reaches $45,000 under this same week is $77.61, not $50 — a $27.61-per-lesson gap the going rate never surfaces, because it was set by teachers whose weeks may carry none of this teacher's travel time or cancellation rate.

Common questions

What does picking a rate off the going local price actually tell you?

It tells you what nearby teachers or studios post, which is a starting point for staying competitive -- not a statement about whether that number covers your own cancellations, prep and travel time, studio costs, or income target. Two teachers charging the same $50 can be in very different positions: one drives nowhere and rarely gets cancelled on, the other spends 25 minutes traveling to every lesson and loses 15% of bookings to no-shows. The going rate cannot see either difference.

How big is the gap in practice?

Using this calculator's own figures: a teacher charging the going local rate of $50 a lesson, teaching 16 lessons a week for 44 weeks with 15% cancellations, 10 minutes of prep and 25 minutes of travel per lesson, and $120 a month in costs, nets $28,480 a year for 1,114.7 real hours of work -- an effective hourly of $25.55, and $16,520 short of a $45,000 target. The price that actually reaches that target is $77.61, not $50 -- a $27.61 gap the going rate never surfaces because it was never asked to.

Is charging the going local rate ever fine?

For a teacher whose cancellations, prep, travel, and costs happen to sit near what the going rate assumes -- often a studio teacher with no drive time and a stable roster -- the going rate can land close to a sustainable number, because it was probably set by teachers in a similar position. The risk is copying that number into a different week: adding travel, a higher no-show rate, or a specific income target changes the math the going rate never accounted for, and by the time a teacher notices the shortfall a year of lessons has already been taught at it.

Does this calculator know what other teachers in my area charge?

No. It has no data on your local market, competing studios, or what a going rate is in your city. It takes your own lesson price, schedule, cancellation rate, prep and travel time, monthly costs, and target pay, and returns the price, break-even, and effective hourly your own week produces -- the arithmetic a rate copied from someone else's listing skips, not a market survey.
