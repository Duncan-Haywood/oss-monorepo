Media Kit Generator vs. assembling one by hand in a document editor

A word processor or slide deck can hold the same bio, platform stats, audience notes, past brand deals, and rate card this generator does — most media kits ever sent were built exactly that way. What it does not do on its own: flag a missing partnerships contact or rate card, keep a plain-text copy in sync with the formatted one every time you edit it, or lay out the page. This tool does all three the moment the form is filled in.

What each approach actually does

A document editor — a word processor, a slide deck, a blank page in any general-purpose tool — will hold anything you type: bio, platform reach, audience breakdown, past collaborations, testimonials, a rate card. Nothing about the blank page tells you what a brand expects to find, so a kit missing a way to actually contact you, or with no stated rate, ships exactly as easily as a complete one. Producing a plain-text version for an intake form that rejects attachments means building a second copy and manually keeping it matching every time the formatted one changes. Layout — type sizes, spacing, where the stats table sits relative to the bio — is a design decision you make yourself, each time you revise it.

This generator takes the same content through one form and builds both files at once: a self-contained, print-friendly HTML one-sheet and a plain-text edition, generated from the identical data so the two can never drift apart. Before it builds anything it requires a creator or channel name and a bio; separately, without blocking generation, it warns when there is no partnerships contact email, no platforms listed, no rate card, or no photo link — the specific gaps a brand notices first when deciding whether to pay you, not a spelling or grammar check. The form autosaves to your own browser as you type, so closing the tab mid-edit does not lose the draft.

Common questions

Can I just build a sponsorship media kit in a word processor or slide deck myself?

Yes — a document editor holds the same bio, platform stats, audience notes, past brand deals, and rate card this generator does, and plenty of media kits in circulation were built exactly that way. What it does not do on its own is tell you the kit is missing something a brand expects, produce a plain-text copy alongside the formatted one, or lay out the page — all three are on you, every time you update it.

What does the generator check that a blank document does not?

A blank document accepts whatever you type and stays silent about what you left out. This generator requires a creator name and a bio before it will build anything, and separately warns — without blocking — when there is no partnerships contact email, no platforms listed, no rate card, or no photo link, because those are the specific gaps a brand notices first when deciding whether to pay you.

Why does a plain-text version matter?

Plenty of brand intake forms and partnership emails reject an attached file or strip its formatting, and a document built in a word processor or slide tool has no plain-text edition unless you build one separately and then keep it matching every time you update the original. This generator produces both from the same form in the same pass, so the two never drift apart.

Is a document editor ever the better choice?

Yes, for a layout this generator does not offer — a designed multi-page deck with custom typography and photo placement, or a kit built around a visual identity a document or design tool can express and this one, deliberately kept to one plain template, cannot. This generator trades that design flexibility for a page that appears the moment the form is filled in, with no layout decisions to make.
