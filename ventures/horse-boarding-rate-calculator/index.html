What it does, and what it leaves to you

    
    
      
  • Counts: hay per horse per month = pounds a day x 30.4 days x price per ton / 2,000; feed and bedding per horse = hay + grain + bedding; fixed per month = chore hours x 30.4 x wage + land + insurance / 12 + utilities + repairs / 12; rate = ((fixed + yearly profit / 12) / (stalls x occupancy) + feed and bedding) / (1 - card fee).

  • Not decided: what the local market pays, pasture or full-care tiers and lesson or training income, liability waivers and insurance terms, sales tax and zoning rules, boarder default and late-fee policy, and seasonal changes in hay price.

Common questions

    
How do I set a horse boarding rate?

    
Add up what each boarded horse costs you every month (hay, grain, bedding), then the barn's fixed costs (land payment, insurance, utilities, repairs and the labour of doing chores), plus the profit you want. Divide the fixed part and profit by the horses actually in the barn, add the feed and bedding, and gross up for card fees.

    
Why does occupancy matter so much?

    
Fixed costs do not shrink when a stall is empty, so the horses who are there carry them. The page shows the rate needed at 60 to 100 percent full, and the occupancy at which your current rate just covers costs.

    
Should I count my own chore labour?

    
Yes, at what you would pay someone else. Otherwise the rate only looks profitable because your unpaid hours subsidise it, and the barn stops working the day you cannot do the chores.

    
Does it know what other barns charge?

    
No. It works backwards from your costs and the profit you want. Compare the result with boarding rates nearby before you publish it.

    
Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    
No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
