Common questions

    
What is the EU 30-day lowest price rule?

    
When a shop announces a price reduction, the previous price it shows must be the lowest price it applied in the 30 days before the reduction. This is Article 6a of the Price Indication Directive, added by the Omnibus Directive and applied since 28 May 2022.

    
Does the percentage off have to use the same prior price?

    
Yes. If a shop advertises a percentage reduction, it has to be calculated against that prior price, not against a list price or a price that was only briefly higher.

    
What if the product is new or the price drops in steps?

    
Member States may set different rules for goods on the market for under 30 days, for perishable goods and for progressive reductions, where the prior price is usually the price before the first reduction. Check your country.

    
Does raising the price just before a sale help?

    
No. The prior price is the lowest price in the 30 days, so a short rise does not move it.

    
What are the penalties?

    
They are set by each Member State, and for widespread infringements the Directive requires fines that can reach at least 4 percent of annual turnover in the countries concerned.

    
Is my price history uploaded anywhere?

    
No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
