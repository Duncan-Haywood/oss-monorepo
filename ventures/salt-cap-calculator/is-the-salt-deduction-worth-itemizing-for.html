Is the SALT deduction worth itemizing for?

Sometimes none of it -- even after OBBBA raised the cap to $40,000.

The calculator on this page's own index tells you your SALT deduction after the cap and phase-out. That figure is real, but it answers a narrower question than "does this help my refund." It only helps once your total itemized deductions -- SALT plus mortgage interest, charitable gifts, and anything else you itemize -- clear your standard deduction. And even then, only the slice that pushes you past the standard deduction is SALT's own contribution; the rest just matches ground the standard deduction covers for free.

Common questions

Does a bigger SALT cap always mean a bigger tax benefit?

No. The SALT deduction only helps at all if your total itemized deductions -- SALT plus mortgage interest, charitable gifts, and anything else you itemize -- exceed your standard deduction. If they don't, you take the standard deduction instead and the SALT cap, however generous, contributes nothing to your refund.

What does "partly additive" mean?

It means only part of your SALT deduction is genuine extra savings. If your other itemized deductions alone fall short of the standard deduction, the first slice of your SALT deduction just closes that gap -- matching ground the standard deduction already covered for free. Only the remainder, the part that pushes your total itemized deductions past the standard deduction, is SALT's own contribution.

What does "wholly absorbed" mean?

It means your SALT deduction, even after the OBBBA cap and phase-out are applied, adds nothing: your other itemized deductions plus SALT still don't exceed the standard deduction, so you take the standard deduction either way and the marginal benefit of the SALT deduction is zero.

Why doesn't the calculator's own "deduction" figure answer this?

The calculator's deductible amount is the SALT-specific figure after the cap and phase-out -- a real number, but only half the picture. It says nothing about your other itemized deductions or the standard deduction, so a large capped SALT deduction can still add zero to your actual refund once the standard-deduction comparison is applied, exactly as the wholly-absorbed example on this page shows.
