This calculator vs. working out the SALT cap phase-out by hand

The arithmetic looks simple — 30 cents per dollar over a threshold. The part that goes wrong by hand is the shape of the rule: which filing statuses share a cap, where the reduction stops, and what it is a percentage of.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Assuming a married-filing-jointly return gets double the single cap — it does not. Single, head-of-household, and married-filing-jointly filers all share the exact same $40,000 (2025) / $40,400 (2026) cap and $500,000/$505,000 threshold. Only married filing separately gets a different, halved figure.

What's the second most common mistake?

Assuming the phase-out eventually takes the deduction to zero. It does not — the cap can never drop below its permanent-law floor of $10,000 ($5,000 married filing separately), no matter how high MAGI goes. A hand estimate that keeps subtracting 30% of excess MAGI past that point understates the deduction.

What arithmetic mistake gets made most often?

Applying the 30% reduction to the WRONG base — to total income rather than to the excess above the threshold, or to the taxes-paid figure rather than to the cap itself. The reduction is 30 cents per dollar of MAGI above the threshold, subtracted from the CAP, not from the amount of tax paid.

What planning mistake gets made most often?

Not checking whether a pass-through entity tax (PTET) election is available. Several states let a partnership or S corporation pay state tax at the entity level, letting the owner deduct that share above this individual cap entirely — a hand estimate that only looks at the personal SALT cap can miss a substantially larger deduction already available through the business.

Does this calculator know my complete tax situation?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page computes your MAGI, knows whether itemizing beats your standard deduction, or models a state's PTET election. Both apply the same published federal cap and phase-out rules to the numbers you enter; verify against IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional before relying on either.
