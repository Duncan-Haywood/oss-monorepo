This calculator vs. Aspire

Two different shapes: Aspire is an account-based business-management platform for running a land-clearing or landscape company's whole operation. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this forestry mulching job cost, given its acreage, brush density, and machine — before you send the bid.

What each tool is for

Aspire is a cloud business-management platform, founded in 2013 by Kevin Kehoe and Mark Tipton, headquartered in Chesterfield, Missouri. It is built for landscape maintenance and construction contractors, irrigation, snow and ice management, and commercial cleaning companies, and has been part of ServiceTitan since ServiceTitan's 2021 acquisition of it. It bundles CRM and lead tracking, estimating, scheduling, purchasing, job costing, mobile crew time tracking, invoicing, and accounting and payroll integration into one ongoing subscription account, with a mobile app for crews in the field. Its own plans are tiered by a contractor's annual revenue rather than sold at one flat price, and no dollar figure for any plan is published on its site — pricing is reached by requesting a demo. Aspire's estimating module lets a contractor build a database of production rates and predefined service assemblies, per its own public material, which could in principle be configured for forestry mulching among other services. Aspire's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact tier names or any dollar figure, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category are stated, and no dollar amount for Aspire appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what Aspire does, and it needs no setup or configuration for forestry mulching specifically — that math is already built in. It does not track a lead, schedule a crew, or invoice a customer, and it stores no customer or job record on a server. It takes head width, forward advance speed while cutting, the number of passes needed to finish the mat, a terrain factor, and the share of the hour actually spent cutting, and derives a production rate in acres per machine-hour from them. Cost per machine hour is built from operator time, fuel at load, carbide tooth wear spread over the life of a set, a service reserve, and the machine payment and insurance spread over billable hours a month, with mobilization kept as its own line because a trailer trip does not scale with acreage. It reports machine hours, days on site, total cost, the price at a target margin, price per acre and break-even per acre, price per machine hour and per day, and a side-by-side of the same acreage at four brush density classes against one flat quoted price per acre — plus a printable customer proposal for that one job. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Should I use this calculator or Aspire?

They do different jobs, and a land-clearing or landscape company of any size will likely end up touching both eventually. Does Aspire price a forestry mulching job the way this calculator does?

Aspire's own public material describes building a database of production rates and predefined assemblies for common landscape services inside its estimating module, which a contractor can configure for whatever services they sell, forestry mulching included. This calculator is not a configurable estimating module -- it is a single-purpose worksheet built specifically around forestry mulching's own physics: head width, forward advance speed, number of passes to finish the mat, terrain factor, and a separate carbide-tooth-wear line most flat per-acre quotes never budget for at all. A contractor who wants one system holding estimates for every service line they sell, tied to CRM and job costing, is better served by Aspire; an operator who wants a free, instant, already-built forestry mulching production-rate model with no setup is in the right place here.
