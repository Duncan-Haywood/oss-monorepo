# Charging dock

Design v0.1, generated by `python -m orm_cad dock` into
[`manufacturing/dock/`](../manufacturing/dock/).

## What it is

- A **3 mm aluminium base plate**, 640 x 460 mm, pegged to the lawn with six
  steel tent pegs. The mower drives onto it forwards.
- Two **printed wheel guides** that the rear wheels run between, which
  centre the mower on the contacts.
- A **printed contact tower** at the far end, hollow and open at the back
  for the charger lead, with two **spring stainless strips** 50 mm apart
  at 65 mm above the plate. They press on the two spring contacts in the
  mower's front contact block.
- A **29.4 V 2 A Li-ion charger** (7S). Its mains side stays indoors or in an
  IP-rated enclosure; only the low-voltage lead runs to the dock.

## Dead until docked

Bare contacts on a lawn get touched by children, pets and wet grass. The
strips must carry no voltage until a mower is actually on them:

1. The charger output goes through a MOSFET (or relay) in the tower that is
   off by default.
2. The mower's contact block puts a known resistance (for example 10 kΩ)
   across its contacts through a diode when it is not charging.
3. A small sense circuit in the tower feeds a few milliamps through the
   strips, sees that resistance, and only then switches the charger on.
   It switches off again when the current stops.

The mower's ESP32 sees the charging voltage on GPIO 4 (through an
optocoupler) and refuses to run the blade while it is on the dock.

This circuit is described, not yet designed to a schematic: it is the
first thing to finish before a dock goes outside.

## Docking

The planner's path starts and ends at the `dock` point of the lawn file.
To dock, the Pi drives to a point 1 m in front of the dock on its axis,
turns to face it, and creeps in at 0.1 m/s until the ESP32 reports
`FLAG_CHARGING` or the bump sensors trip (then it backs out and retries).
RTK accuracy is enough to hit the guides; the guides do the last
centimetres.

## Base station

If you run your own RTK base, the dock is a convenient place for its
receiver and link, but the base antenna needs a clear sky view and a mount
that never moves: a roof or a post set in concrete is better than the dock.
See [navigation.md](navigation.md).
