Questions people ask

Why does my bonus check look so small?

Most employers withhold federal income tax on a bonus at a flat 22% (the supplemental rate), then take Social Security, Medicare and your state's share on top. With a state rate of 5% that is about 35 cents of every dollar before anything else. The tool lists each piece.

Is a bonus really taxed at a higher rate?

No. The extra withholding is not a higher tax. Withholding is a prepayment: the bonus is added to your income for the year and taxed at your real bracket when you file. If 22% was more than your bracket, the difference comes back as a refund; if your bracket is higher, you owe the rest. The tool shows which.

How much do I need to gross up to pay a bonus net?

Divide the net you want by what is left after every tax that applies, or let the tool solve it, including the Social Security cap and the Additional Medicare Tax once the wages pass their lines. This is what employers do when they promise a bonus after tax, for example a relocation or sign-on payment.

Does putting a bonus in a 401(k) help?

A deferral takes income tax off that part now, but not Social Security or Medicare, which are still taken on the full bonus. The table shows take-home and the after-tax value of the bonus at several deferral shares. Contribution limits and your plan's rules apply and are not checked here.

Is this tax or payroll advice?

No. It is arithmetic on rates you type in. It does not run a payroll, know your W-4 or your state's rules, or file anything. The federal rates in the boxes are U.S. figures that change; confirm them before relying on a number.
