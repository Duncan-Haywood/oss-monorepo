Questions ghostwriters ask

How much does a ghostwriter charge for a book?

Quotes for a full-length nonfiction book commonly run from low five figures to well over $100,000 depending on experience, and the range is wide because scope differs. The honest way to price is from hours: drafting, interviews, research, revision rounds and project management, times the hourly rate you need, plus premiums. This tool does that arithmetic and shows the per-word and per-page rate that falls out. The figures are not market data.

Should a ghostwriter charge per word, per hour or a flat fee?

Clients usually want a flat fee and writers are safest when that flat fee was derived from hours. Per-word pricing hides interview and revision time; hourly pricing worries clients who cannot cap the cost. Build the flat fee from hours, then check the implied per-word rate against what the client expects.

Why charge more for uncredited work?

Credited work gives you a portfolio line and referrals; uncredited work gives you neither and often an NDA. Many ghostwriters add a confidentiality premium for that. The tool lets you set the percentage, and shows how much of the fee it accounts for.

How do I handle clients who want unlimited revisions?

Price a fixed number of revision rounds into the fee and a separate price for each extra round. The tool shows what one extra round costs at your rate so you can write it into the agreement.

What if the client's budget is below my number?

Type their budget. The tool shows the hourly rate you would earn at that fee, and how many words of book that budget buys at your target rate with the same interviews and research, so you can offer a shorter book or a smaller scope instead of a discount.
