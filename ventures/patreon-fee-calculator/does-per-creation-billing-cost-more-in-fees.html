Does per-creation billing cost more in fees?

A per-creation pledge charges a patron once for every post you publish instead of once a month, so a $5-a-month supporter under per-creation billing might see four separate $1.25 charges in a busy month instead of one $5 charge. Same total pledge — but not the same fee bill, because payment processing charges a fixed fee on every charge, not on every dollar. On Patreon's Standard plan, the one every new page is on since August 2025, there is no small-charge discount to soften that at all.

The worked comparison: Standard plan

Take 100 patrons, each pledging $5 a month, on the Standard plan (10% platform fee, one flat processing rate regardless of charge size). The gross revenue is $500 either way. Here is what changes:

FigureMonthly billing (1 charge)Per-creation billing (4 posts, 4 charges)
Charge amount$5.00$1.25
Processing rate for that charge~2.9% + $0.30~2.9% + $0.30 (same — Standard has no small-charge rate)
Processing fee, this patron$0.445$1.345 (4 × $0.33625)
Total processing fees, 100 patrons$44.50$134.50
Platform fee (10% of $500 either way)$50.00$50.00
Total fees$94.50$184.50
Net payout$405.50$315.50

$90.00 less, from identical $500 gross revenue, purely from how the charge was split into four instead of one. The percentage portion of the fee is the same either way (2.9% of $500 is $14.50 total, whichever way it's charged); the entire gap is the $0.30 fixed fee, paid four times instead of once.

Does a legacy plan change this?

Partly. A legacy plan — a page created on or before August 4, 2025 — keeps its Lite/Pro/Premium platform fee and also gets a lower "micropayment" processing rate (about 5% + $0.10) on any charge under $3. On a Legacy Pro plan (8%), the identical 100-patron, $5-pledge, 4-post example pays $84.50 in total fees under monthly billing and $105.00 under per-creation billing — a $20.50 gap, much smaller than the Standard plan's $90.00, because the legacy rate's lower fixed fee ($0.10 instead of $0.30) partly offsets paying it four times. It is a real mitigant, not a myth — but it does not close the gap to zero.

When the gap closes

The gap shrinks as the per-post charge gets larger, on either plan, because the fixed fee stops dominating a bigger charge. A creator who posts once a month sees no split at all, since there is only one charge either way. The trap is specifically a low per-post amount multiplied by several posts in the same month — and it bites hardest on the Standard plan, which is what a page created today is on.

Common questions

Does per-creation billing cost more in fees than monthly billing?

Often, yes, for the same total pledge. Payment processing charges a fixed fee every time a patron is charged, and per-creation billing charges more often for the same money. Splitting a $5 monthly pledge across 4 posts means four $1.25 charges instead of one $5 charge, and each of those four charges pays its own fixed fee -- on Patreon's Standard plan, with no small-charge discount, that fixed $0.30 is paid four times instead of once.

How much difference does that actually make on the Standard plan?

On 100 patrons at a $5 monthly pledge with the Standard plan's 10% platform fee, monthly billing pays $94.50 in total fees against $500 gross revenue, for a $405.50 net payout. The same $500 billed per-creation across 4 posts pays $184.50 in total fees, for a $315.50 net payout -- $90.00 less, from identical revenue, purely because of how the charge was split.

Does a legacy plan's micropayment rate change this?

It softens the gap but does not close it. A legacy plan (a page created on or before August 4, 2025) gets a lower processing rate -- about 5% plus a $0.10 fixed fee instead of the standard 2.9% plus $0.30 -- on any charge under $3. On a Legacy Pro plan (8% platform fee), the same 100-patron example pays $84.50 in total fees under monthly billing and $105.00 under per-creation billing -- a $20.50 gap, well below the Standard plan's $90.00 gap, because the legacy micropayment rate's lower fixed fee partly offsets charging four times instead of once.

Is per-creation billing always worse?

No. The gap shrinks as the per-post charge gets larger, because the fixed part of the fee stops dominating a bigger charge. A creator publishing once a month sees no split at all, since there is then only one charge either way. The trap is specifically low per-post amounts multiplied by several posts a month, and it is worse on the Standard plan than on a legacy plan because the Standard plan has no small-charge discount to soften it.

Why would a creator use per-creation billing at all, then?

It ties what a patron pays to how much the creator actually publishes that month, which some audiences find fairer than a flat monthly charge regardless of output. That is a real trade-off worth making deliberately -- this page is not an argument against per-creation billing, only against choosing it without seeing what it costs in fees for a given post cadence, pledge size, and plan.

Could Patreon's rates change and make this stale?

Yes. Patreon retired the three-tier plan for new creators in August 2025 in favor of the single Standard plan this page uses as its main example, and it publishes no live feed a static page could read.
