    What's recognised

    InputHandling

    Hooping is assumed to happen while the machine is stopped, with one operator for the whole job. Digitizing is treated as a cost you recover inside the quote. Bobbin changes, hoop-burn rejects and re-stitches are not modelled; raise the garment cost or lower the efficiency to allow for them.

    Common questions

    Does it upload my job?

    No. The page works on the numbers you type, in your own browser, and never sends them anywhere. The CLI reads a local file or stdin and makes no network call.

    How is the quote worked out?

    Clock time is job setup plus color setup, plus one run per hoop (stitches divided by stitches per minute at your real efficiency, plus thread changes) for each batch of pieces across your heads, plus hooping minutes per piece. Cost adds garments, thread and backing, labor and overhead for that time, and digitizing. The quote is cost divided by one minus your target margin, at least your minimum charge, plus any rush surcharge, rounded up to the cent per piece.

    Why does the per-piece price drop as quantity grows?

    Digitizing, job setup and color setup are paid once however many pieces you stitch, so their share of each piece shrinks. The break table shows the drop at each quantity and flags where the minimum charge or setup dominates.

    Is a flat price per 1000 stitches enough?

    It ignores setup, digitizing, color changes and hooping, so on a small run it can sell below your cost. The page compares it with the quote that hits your margin at every quantity and shows the quantity from which it covers your cost.

    What does the take-the-job verdict mean?

    Effective hourly rate is what is left of the price per clock hour after garments, thread, overhead and digitizing are paid, before you pay yourself. If it is below your floor the page tells you the price that reaches it.
