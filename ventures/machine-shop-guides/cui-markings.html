CUI markings: how to read them on a drawing or RFQ

A drawing arrives with CUI//SP-CTI//FEDCON across the top, or an RFQ says "FOUO", or a PDF carries Distribution Statement C and nothing else. Each of these tells a shop something different about where the file may live and who may see it.     Questions for the buyer

      The parts of a CUI marking

      Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) is government information that is not classified but still has to be protected. The rules for marking it are in 32 CFR Part 2002, and the list of categories and markings is the CUI Registry kept by the National Archives (NARA). A shop mostly meets five pieces.

      1. The banner

      The line at the top (and usually the bottom) of every page. It starts with the control marking: The CUI control marking may consist of either the word “CONTROLLED” or the acronym “CUI,” at the designator's discretion. After it, separated by double slashes, come the category markings and then any dissemination controls: CUI//SP-CTI//NOFORN. The banner covers the whole document and must be the same on each page of the document that includes CUI. Two pages with different banners is a marking error worth asking about.

      2. The category: CTI, EXPT, PROPIN

      The category says which law or rule makes the information CUI. The three a machine shop sees most:

      • CTI, Controlled Technical Information. The Registry: Controlled Technical Information means technical information with military or space application that is subject to controls on the access, use, reproduction, modification, performance, display, release, disclosure, or dissemination. Its examples include engineering drawings, and associated lists, specifications, standards, process sheets, manuals, technical reports, technical orders. That is most of a defense RFQ package.
      • EXPT, Export Controlled. Unclassified information concerning certain items, commodities, technology, software, or other information whose export could reasonably be expected to adversely affect the United States national security and nonproliferation objectives. ITAR and EAR data fall here, and those rules on foreign persons apply on top of the CUI rules.
      • PROPIN, General Proprietary Business Information. A company's financial information, trade secrets and product designs, in the Registry's description.

      An SP- in front means CUI Specified: CUI Specified is the subset of CUI in which the authorizing law, regulation, or Government-wide policy contains specific handling controls that it requires or permits agencies to use that differ from those for CUI Basic. The Registry lists CTI's banner as CUI//SP-CTI. Any other category code is in the Registry's category list.

      3. Dissemination controls: NOFORN, FEDCON, NOCON and the rest

      After the second double slash. Only the designating agency may apply limited dissemination controls to CUI. The ones that change what a shop may do:

      • NOFORN (portion mark NF): Information may not be disseminated in any form to foreign governments, foreign nationals, foreign or international organizations, or non-US citizens. Check every person in the shop and at your platers and heat-treaters who would see the file.
      • FEDCON: federal employees and contractors, so long as dissemination is in furtherance of that contractual purpose. You may hold it for the job, and pass it to a supplier for work on that job.
      • NOCON: No dissemination authorized to individuals or employers who enter into a contract with the United States (any department or agency) to perform a specific job, supply labor and materials, or for the sale of products and services. A contractor holding a NOCON file should ask why. FED ONLY leaves contractors out the same way.
      • DL ONLY: Dissemination authorized only to those individuals, organizations, or entities included on an accompanying dissemination list. Ask for the list if it did not come with the file.
      • REL TO and DISPLAY ONLY, followed by country codes: foreign release to the countries named, with DISPLAY ONLY allowing sight but no copy.

      4. "Controlled by" and the other lines on page one

      All documents containing CUI must carry an indicator of who designated the CUI within it. It may take any form that identifies the designating agency, including letterhead or other standard agency indicators, or adding a “Controlled by” line, and may appear only on the first page or cover. Some documents add the category, a point of contact and a decontrol date in the same block. A decontrol date matters: Authorized holders may consider specific items of CUI as decontrolled as of the date indicated, requiring no further review by, or communication with, the designator.

      5. Portion markings

      Paragraphs or notes marked (CUI) or (U) for uncontrolled. Agencies are permitted and encouraged to portion mark all CUI, so you will see them on some documents and not others. Portion marks with no banner are a marking error: ask for a properly marked copy.

      When the marking is missing or old

      FOUO, SBU and other legacy markings

      Markings from before the CUI Program, such as "For Official Use Only" (FOUO) or "Sensitive But Unclassified" (SBU), are legacy markings. The rule: If legacy markings remain on information, the legacy markings are void and no longer indicate that the information is protected or that it is or qualifies as CUI. That does not make an FOUO drawing safe to post on the shop's shared drive. The same section says The lack of a CUI marking on information that qualifies as CUI does not exempt the authorized holder from abiding by applicable handling requirements. So ask the buyer whether it is CUI, and for a marked copy. Agencies are told to do the same when they receive one: An agency receiving an incorrectly marked document should notify either the disseminating entity or the designating agency, and request a properly marked document.

      A distribution statement and no banner

      Many older drawings carry only a distribution statement. DFARS 252.204-7012 ties the two together: Controlled technical information would meet the criteria, if disseminated, for distribution statements B through F. The Registry's CTI page says the same from the other side: controlled technical information is to be marked with one of statements B through F. So the page treats a drawing marked B, C, D, E or F as CUI until the buyer says otherwise. Statement A is approved for public release.

      No marking at all

      Covered defense information under DFARS 252.204-7012 is information Marked or otherwise identified in the contract, task order, or delivery order and provided to the contractor by or on behalf of DoD in support of the performance of the contract, or created by the contractor for it. "Otherwise identified in the contract" means a purchase order can make a file CUI without a word on the file. Read the PO's clauses: if 252.204-7012 is there, ask which deliverables and drawings it covers.

      What CUI on your systems means

      For a contractor system that holds covered defense information, DFARS 252.204-7012 says the covered contractor information system shall be subject to the security requirements in National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Special Publication (SP) 800-171: the 110 requirements that CMMC Level 2 assesses. One CUI drawing in the general inbox puts the inbox, the laptop that opened it, the CAM workstation, the shared drive and the cloud backup in scope. That is why many shops keep CUI in one place: a separate email account and folder, or an enclave, and nothing else.       Common questions

      What does CUI//SP-CTI mean on a drawing?

      CUI is the control marking, so the drawing is Controlled Unclassified Information. SP-CTI is the category: Controlled Technical Information, and SP- means CUI Specified, where the law behind it sets its own handling rules. Under DFARS 252.204-7012 the systems that hold it are in scope for NIST SP 800-171, which is CMMC Level 2.

      What do NOFORN and FEDCON mean in a CUI banner?

      They are limited dissemination controls, after the second double slash. NOFORN means no foreign nationals or non-US citizens may receive it. FEDCON means federal employees and contractors only, for work on the contract. NOCON and FED ONLY leave contractors out, so a shop holding one should ask the sender.

      Is a drawing marked FOUO CUI?

      Not by that marking alone: FOUO is a legacy marking from before the CUI Program, and 32 CFR 2002.20 says legacy markings are void. But unmarked CUI is still CUI, so ask the buyer whether the file is CUI and for a properly marked copy.
