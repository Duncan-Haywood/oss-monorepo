CMMC scoping worksheet: which assets are in scope

Before a CMMC assessment, the rule asks you to say which of your assets are in scope and in which category. In a machine shop that means the CAM workstations, the DNC server, the CNC controllers, the CMM, the printers, the phones and the email service, not only the office PCs. List them here, answer a few questions for each, and the page shows which category of 32 CFR 170.19 your answers point to, what each one has to have documented, and what is still open.     The five categories, from the rule

    For Level 2, table 3 to 32 CFR 170.19(c)(1) sorts every asset into one of five categories. The descriptions below are quoted from it.

    • CUI Assets: Assets that process, store, or transmit CUI. Documented in the asset inventory, the SSP and the network diagram, and assessed against all Level 2 requirements. In a shop: the mailbox the drawings arrive in, the file share, the CAM workstation, the DNC server.
    • Security Protection Assets: Assets that provide security functions or capabilities to the OSA's CMMC Assessment Scope. Assessed against the requirements relevant to what they do. The firewall, MFA, endpoint protection, the log collector, the backup of in-scope systems.
    • Contractor Risk Managed Assets (CRMA): Assets that can, but are not intended to, process, store, or transmit CUI because of security policy, procedures, and practices in place. Assets are not required to be physically or logically separated from CUI assets. The key word is policy: without a written rule that keeps CUI off it, an asset that can reach CUI is not a CRMA. If the SSP raises questions, the assessor can conduct a limited check to identify deficiencies.
    • Specialized Assets: Assets that can process, store, or transmit CUI but are unable to be fully secured, including: Internet of Things (IoT) devices, Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) devices, Operational Technology (OT), Government Furnished Equipment (GFE), Restricted Information Systems, and Test Equipment. Documented, and you must Show these assets are managed using the contractor's risk-based security policies, procedures, and practices. Not assessed against the other requirements. This is where most CNC controllers, machine monitoring boxes and CMMs land: they run programs made from CUI drawings and often run old operating systems you cannot patch.
    • Out-of-Scope Assets: Assets that cannot process, store, or transmit CUI; and do not provide security protections for CUI Assets, physically or logically separated from CUI assets. You have to Prepare to justify the inability of an Out-of-Scope Asset to process, store, or transmit CUI, and Assets that fall into any in-scope asset category cannot be considered an Out-of-Scope Asset. One special case: An endpoint hosting a VDI client configured to not allow any processing, storage, or transmission of CUI beyond the Keyboard/Video/Mouse sent to the VDI client is considered an Out-of-Scope Asset.

    Outside services: email, cloud storage, your MSP

    Table 4 to 170.19(c)(2)(i) scopes an External Service Provider by what it holds. A cloud provider that holds CUI: The CSP shall meet the FedRAMP requirements in 48 CFR 252.204-7012, which the clause words as security requirements equivalent to the FedRAMP Moderate baseline. A non-cloud provider that holds CUI is assessed as part of your assessment. A provider that holds only Security Protection Data (logs, configuration), such as an MSP's monitoring tool, is assessed as a Security Protection Asset. And A service provider that does not process CUI or SPD does not meet the CMMC definition of an ESP. Either way, the SSP describes the service and the provider's customer responsibility matrix says who does what.

    Level 1 is simpler

    For Level 1, paragraph (b): OSA information systems which process, store, or transmit FCI are in scope for CMMC Level 1 and must be self-assessed against applicable CMMC security requirements. Specialized Assets are not part of the Level 1 CMMC Assessment Scope and are not assessed against CMMC security requirements. And There are no documentation requirements for out-of-scope assets. Switch the level above to see the Level 1 sort; then work through the 15 requirements on the Level 1 self-assessment.

    Why scope first

    Every asset you put in scope as a CUI Asset has to meet all 110 requirements, and every one becomes a line in the SPRS score and the SSP. A small enclave (one mailbox, one share, two workstations, the shop-floor machines as Specialized Assets) is often far less work than securing the whole office. Whether an enclave fits your contracts is your decision and your assessor's; the worksheet only makes the answers visible. For which level applies to you at all, see CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2.

    Common questions

    What are the CMMC asset categories?

    For Level 2, 32 CFR 170.19 sorts every asset into five: CUI Assets, Security Protection Assets, Contractor Risk Managed Assets, Specialized Assets and Out-of-Scope Assets. Each has its own documentation and assessment requirements.

    Are CNC controllers in CMMC scope?

    Usually as Specialized Assets: equipment that can process CUI but cannot be fully secured, such as operational technology and test equipment. They are documented and managed under your risk-based policies, but not assessed against the other requirements. At Level 1, Specialized Assets are not part of the assessment scope.

    Why scope before writing the SSP?

    Every asset you put in scope as a CUI Asset has to meet all 110 requirements and becomes a line in the SSP and the SPRS score. A small enclave, such as one mailbox, one share, two workstations and the shop-floor machines as Specialized Assets, is often far less work than securing the whole office.
