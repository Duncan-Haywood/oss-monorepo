Machine shop quoting software: the four kinds, and how to choose

Search for quoting software for a CNC or fabrication shop and you get a list of brand names with nothing to tell them apart. They fall into four kinds, and the kind matters more than the brand. Each needs something different from your shop before it gives a good number, and each breaks in a different place. This page sorts them, gives the prices that public listings show (with where each figure came from), and lists the questions worth asking in a demo.

About the prices below. No vendor's own pricing page could be read when this page was written. Every figure comes from a third-party listing, linked, as that listing showed it on 24 November 2026. Listings disagree with each other and with vendors, and prices change. Treat each figure as a starting point for the conversation and get the current price from the vendor. We are building a product in this category (see the end of the page), so read our comparison with that in mind.

Questions to ask in a demo

  1. Whose rates does it price with? A price should come from your shop rate, your material costs and your times. If the software brings its own "typical" rates, ask where they come from and how you replace them.
  2. Can you see every line behind the price? When a quote is wrong you need to see whether it was the material, the setup or the cycle time. A single number you cannot take apart cannot be corrected.
  3. Does it read what your customers actually send? Bring three real RFQs to the demo: an email with a PDF drawing, a STEP model, and a scan. Watch what it gets right and what you would have to retype.
  4. Quantity breaks. Setup and programming are paid once per lot, so the price per part should fall with quantity on its own. Check that the software recalculates each quantity rather than applying a discount table.
  5. Does it learn from your results? Ask how quoted hours are compared with actual hours, and how the difference flows back into the next quote.
  6. Controlled drawings. If a contract sends you drawings marked CUI, a cloud service that stores them for you must meet security requirements equivalent to the FedRAMP Moderate baseline under DFARS 252.204-7012(b)(2)(ii)(D). Ask the vendor in writing whether that applies to them, and keep CUI and export-controlled drawings out of any tool that says no. See CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2.
  7. Getting your data out. Ask how you export your quotes, rates and customer history if you leave.
  8. The whole price. Per user or per shop, setup and training fees, the contract term, and what happens at renewal.

Before you buy anything: the free route

Most of what quoting software does can be tried free, in your browser, with nothing uploaded. It is also a good way to find out what you would want from paid software:

  1. RFQ review checklist: paste the RFQ. It pulls out part numbers, revisions, quantities, material, finish, tolerances, dates and contract clauses, and writes the questions for the buyer.
  2. Drawing review: drop the drawing PDF. It reads the title block, revision table, general tolerances, notes and callouts.
  3. Stock size from a STEP file: drop the model to get the bounding box, the stock to cut and, from an STL, a first cycle time from your own feeds.
  4. Job-shop quote calculator: price it from your own rates at each quantity, and write the customer quote.
  5. Quoted vs actual hours: paste your job history to see how far your quotes run from the hours you actually spend, and correct the next quote by that amount.

Common questions

What kinds of machine shop quoting software are there?

Four: a spreadsheet or calculator, the quoting module inside a job-shop ERP, a standalone quoting platform, and the newer AI RFQ readers that draft the parts and operations from the email, drawing and model for an estimator to check. Each needs your own rates before it gives a good number.

How much does machine shop quoting software cost?

Public listings checked on 24 November 2026 showed QuotedPart at $199 a month per shop (Toolradar), JobBOSS² at about $3,000 a year (SoftwareConnect) and ProShop at $650 to $715 a month (G2). We could confirm no public price for Paperless Parts or the AI quoting entrants. Listings disagree, so get the current price from the vendor.

Can I upload CUI drawings to a quoting tool?

Only to a service that may hold them. Under DFARS 252.204-7012(b)(2)(ii)(D), a cloud service that stores covered defense information for you must meet security requirements equivalent to the FedRAMP Moderate baseline. Ask the vendor in writing, and keep CUI and export-controlled drawings out of any tool that says no.
