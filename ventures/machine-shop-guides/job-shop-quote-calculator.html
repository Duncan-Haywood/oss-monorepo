What this calculator does and does not do

It adds up the lines you give it and spreads the once-per-lot costs (setup, programming, any flat lot charge) over the quantity. That spreading is why a 5-off costs more per part than a 100-off: the quantity breaks are arithmetic, not a discount schedule we invented. It does not estimate a cycle time from a drawing, look up a material price or tell you what the market will pay; those numbers are yours.

Markup here is applied to cost (price = cost × (1 + markup)). If you think in margin instead, a 20% margin on price is a 25% markup on cost. For a deeper model with bar and plate yield, tool life and outside-process minimums, there is also a separate CNC machining quote calculator.
