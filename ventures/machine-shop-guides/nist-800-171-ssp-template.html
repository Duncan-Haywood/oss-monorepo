What this SSP template covers

Requirement 3.12.4 of NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 asks you to develop, document and periodically update system security plans that describe the system boundaries, the environments of operation, how each security requirement is implemented, and the relationships with or connections to other systems. NIST does not prescribe a format, so this template is laid out the way a small shop's plan usually reads: contacts, the system (purpose, CUI, boundary, environment, inventory, data flow, connections, users), a summary, one entry per requirement, and the POA&M. See what goes in an SSP and a POA&M for each part in plain language.

Without an SSP the DoD Assessment Methodology (v1.2.1) says an assessment cannot be completed, which is why 3.12.4 has no point value but blocks the score. The review above flags the parts of 3.12.4 the plan does not yet cover as "to fix", and every requirement row that would not survive a read: met with no implementation statement, met with nowhere to see the evidence, not applicable with no reason, a gap with no planned action or no date, and dates that have passed.

The score and the date you post in SPRS

The score is the same arithmetic as the SPRS score calculator: 110 minus 5, 3 or 1 point for each requirement not met, with partial credit for 3.5.3 (MFA) and 3.13.11 (FIPS-validated cryptography). A Basic assessment in SPRS also asks for the date you expect to reach 110; the summary takes it from the latest milestone date on your gaps, and only when every gap has one. If you already went through the calculator in this browser, "Start from my SPRS calculator answers" copies those statuses in without touching anything you have written.

Downloads

  • SSP (HTML): one self-contained file that prints cleanly and opens in Word or Google Docs for your own formatting.
  • SSP (Markdown): for a wiki or a document repository.
  • POA&M (CSV): one row per gap, heaviest first: requirement, points, status, the weakness as written, planned action, responsible, milestone, provider.
  • Plan file (JSON): everything you entered; open it here next time to keep editing.

Writing the plan is the shop's job: no template knows how your shop actually works. The statements here are what you type; the page adds none. Keep the finished SSP with your other CUI documentation and review it at least yearly and whenever the system changes.
