    How to review an RFQ before you quote

    Many quoting mistakes are not arithmetic. They are a requirement nobody read: a first article report on lot one, a domestic-melt material cert, an approved-processor list for the anodize, a CUI marking on the drawing. ISO 9001 and AS9100 both ask for this review before you commit to supply (clause 8.2.3, review of the requirements for products and services); a small shop without either still saves money doing it.

    1. The part

    Part number and drawing revision for every line. Quote to a revision, and write it on the quote, so a later drawing change is a new quote and not your loss. Check the drawing's notes block too: general tolerances, the finish spec, marking, and any "no substitution" note often live there and not in the email.

    2. Quantities

    The lot sizes to price, and the annual usage if the buyer will say. A single quantity with no annual figure is worth one question: setup is spread over the lot, so the price per part at 10 and at 400 a year are very different numbers.

    3. Material and finish

    Alloy and temper (6061-T6, 17-4 PH H1150), any material spec, and whether certs must travel with the parts. For finishes, the spec, type, class and color, plus masking. If the buyer names approved or NADCAP processors, get a price from one of them before you price the finish.

    4. Tolerances

    The tightest tolerance decides the machine, the inspection and sometimes whether you can make it at all. If a tight callout looks like a title-block default rather than a functional need, ask; buyers often loosen it.

    5. Clauses and quality requirements

    On defense work, the flowed-down clauses set obligations before the first chip: protecting CUI to NIST SP 800-171 (DFARS 252.204-7012), a current SPRS score (252.204-7019 and -7020), a CMMC level (252.204-7021), specialty metals melted in the US or a qualifying country (252.225-7009), export control (252.225-7048), a named quality standard (FAR 52.246-11) and DPAS-rated orders (52.211-15). Each one this page finds is listed with its title and what it means for the shop. Read the clause itself before you sign. For CUI and SPRS: the CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2 guide and the SPRS score calculator.

    6. Dates

    When the quote is due and when the parts are needed. Lead time on material and outside processing often sets the delivery date more than your own machine time does.

    7. Ask once, in one email

    Buyers answer one clear list faster than five separate emails. The questions above are written to be sent as they are, or edited first.
