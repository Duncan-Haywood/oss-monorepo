What goes in an SSP and a POA&M

Two documents carry most of a small shop's NIST SP 800-171 effort: the system security plan (SSP), which says what your system is and how each requirement is met, and the plan of action and milestones (POA&M), which says how and when you will close what is not. Without an SSP there is no score at all; with a weak POA&M the score you post has nothing behind it.

The SSP: requirement 3.12.4

NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 requirement 3.12.4 asks you to develop, document and periodically update system security plans that describe system boundaries, system environments of operation, how security requirements are implemented, and the relationships with or connections to other systems. The DoD Assessment Methodology (v1.2.1, 24 June 2020) gives 3.12.4 no point value because without an SSP an assessment cannot be completed. In practice, for a small machine shop, that means:

  1. Scope and boundary. Which people, computers, accounts, cloud services and machines store, process or transmit CUI. Name them: the estimator's PC, the CAM workstation, the file share, the email service, the shop-floor DNC or controllers that receive programs, the printers that produce travelers.
  2. Network diagram and data flow. How a CUI drawing gets from the customer to the spindle and to the inspection report, and where it is stored at each step.
  3. Asset inventory. Hardware, software and services in scope, with owners. Requirement 3.4.1 asks for baseline configurations and inventories anyway.
  4. One implementation statement per requirement. For each of the 110: met, not met or partially met (for 3.5.3 and 3.13.11), how it is done in your shop, who is responsible, and where the evidence is (a setting, a log, a signed form).
  5. Connections to other systems. Your managed service provider, your ERP or quoting software, your customer's portal, any vendor with remote access to a machine.
  6. Roles and review date. Who owns the plan and when it is next reviewed.

The free SSP template walks through each of these parts and all 110 requirements in your browser, flags what is missing, and downloads the plan and the POA&M.

Write the implementation statements in plain language about what actually happens. An assessor (or your own senior official, who affirms the score) should be able to read a statement and know where to look to see it working.

The POA&M: requirement 3.12.2

Requirement 3.12.2 asks you to develop and implement plans of action designed to correct deficiencies and reduce or eliminate vulnerabilities. NIST does not prescribe a format. A workable POA&M row has: the requirement, the weakness as you found it, the planned action, who is responsible, the resources needed, milestone dates, and the current status. The SPRS score calculator downloads your gaps in exactly those columns as a starter.

When you post a Basic assessment in SPRS, you also give the date you expect to reach a score of 110. Your POA&M milestones are where that date comes from.

POA&M limits under CMMC

32 CFR 170.21 limits what a CMMC Level 2 assessment can leave open:

  • Conditional status needs a score of at least 80% of the maximum (88 of 110).
  • Open items are generally limited to 1-point requirements. The stated exception is 3.13.11 when you encrypt CUI but the cryptography is not FIPS-validated (a 3-point partial deduction).
  • Six requirements can never be on a POA&M, whatever their points (170.21(a)(2)(iii)): 3.1.20 external connections, 3.1.22 control of public information, 3.12.4 the system security plan, 3.10.3 escorting visitors, 3.10.4 physical access logs and 3.10.5 managing physical access devices. Each is worth 1 point or none, so they are easy to leave open by mistake. The SPRS calculator and the SSP template flag them.
  • Open items must be closed and confirmed by a closeout assessment within 180 days, or the conditional status expires.
  • Level 1 allows no POA&M at all (32 CFR 170.15).

So for planning, sort your gaps by weight. Every 5- and 3-point gap has to be closed before a Level 2 assessment, not deferred to a POA&M. The calculator flags those for you.

Keep them alive

Both documents go stale the day a new machine, a new laptop or a new customer portal arrives. Under 32 CFR 170.16 a Level 2 (Self) shop affirms compliance every year and keeps assessment artifacts for six years; that affirmation means the SSP still describes the shop as it is.
