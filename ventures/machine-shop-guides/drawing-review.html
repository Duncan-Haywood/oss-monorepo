          How to read a drawing before you quote

          The RFQ email says what the buyer wants. The drawing says what you are on the hook for. A review takes ten minutes on a simple part and saves the quote that forgot the anodize masking or the material cert. In order:

          1. Title block and revision

          Part number, revision, title, material and finish usually sit in the bottom-right block. Quote to the revision and write it on your quote. Then check the revision table (often top right): its last row should match the title block. When it doesn't, someone updated one and not the other, and you need to know which drawing the buyer will inspect against.

          2. The general tolerance block

          "Unless otherwise specified" sets the tolerance for every dimension that has none of its own, usually by decimal places (.XX, .XXX), plus angles and sometimes fractions. A drawing toleranced by an ISO 2768 class instead uses the tables in that standard, by size range. This block often decides the machine and the inspection more than the few toleranced callouts do, because it covers most of the part. If there is no block and no tolerance on a dimension, ask.

          3. The notes

          Notes are where cost hides: finish specs and masking, heat treat and hardness, part marking (MIL-STD-130 for IUID), edge breaks, cleanliness, packaging, certs and traceability, and "critical" or key characteristics that need 100% inspection. Read every note; this page tags each one with what it is likely to cost, and a gap in the numbering usually means a note is missing from your copy.

          4. The tightest callouts

          The tightest tolerance and the finest surface finish decide the process. If a tight callout looks like a default rather than a functional need, ask: buyers loosen tolerances more often than you'd think.

          5. Markings

          On defense drawings, a distribution statement sets who may receive the drawing. Statement A means approved for public release; B to F limit distribution, so check before you forward it to a plating or heat-treat house. A CUI marking means the systems that hold the file must meet NIST SP 800-171 (see the CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2 guide), and an export-control (ITAR or EAR) warning limits who in the shop may see it.

          6. Then price it

          With the drawing read, the RFQ review covers the buyer's terms, stock size from the STEP file gives the material and a first cycle time, and the quote calculator prices it from your own rates.
