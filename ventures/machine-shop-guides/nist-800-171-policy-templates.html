NIST 800-171 policy templates: the 14 security policies, filled in

CMMC Level 2 assessors ask for written policies. NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 has 14 requirement families, from Access Control to System and Information Integrity, and a small shop usually writes one policy for each. This page writes all 14 as drafts: each restates its family's requirements in NIST's own words, carries your company, system, policy owner and review interval, and copies the implementation statements from your SSP into its procedures. Edit them, approve them, then follow them.

Nothing you type leaves your browser, and this page keeps none of it: download the files before you close it. A template is a starting point, not a policy: it becomes one when your organization edits it to match what it really does and approves it.

Policies, procedures and the SSP


The system security plan says how each requirement is implemented in your system. A policy says what the organization commits to and who answers for it; a procedure says how the work is done, step by step. Assessors read them together: the NIST SP 800-171A assessment methods for most requirements list policy and procedure documents among the things they examine, next to the SSP and the system itself. Keeping the procedures in the policy, filled from the SSP's statements, means the three say the same thing.


Write the SSP first (or at least the implementation statements), then come back here and bring them in: the procedures fill themselves, and the "procedures written" column shows which families still have gaps. When the shop changes how something is done, change the SSP and the procedure together.


One policy or fourteen?


Neither NIST nor the CMMC rule says how many documents to have. One per family is the usual layout because it lines up with how assessments are organized and lets a different owner keep each one; a small shop can just as well keep the full set as one manual, which is what "Download all chosen" gives you. What matters is that each requirement is covered by something written, approved, and followed.


Requirement text: NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 (update 1), from NIST's own requirements spreadsheet, the same text CMMC Workspace uses. Read the full publication and its assessment objectives (NIST SP 800-171A) before deciding a requirement is met.








  
Common questions

  
What policies does NIST 800-171 require?

  
NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 has 14 requirement families, from Access Control to System and Information Integrity. Small shops usually keep one written policy per family; neither NIST nor the CMMC rule sets the number of documents, only that each requirement is covered by something written, approved and followed.

  
What goes in a NIST 800-171 policy?

  
This template writes purpose, scope, roles, the family's requirements in NIST's own words, a procedure for each requirement (copied from your SSP's implementation statements where you have them), exceptions, enforcement and review. Everything but the requirement text is boilerplate to edit.

  
How often should security policies be reviewed?

  
NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 sets no interval for policies. The template uses the interval you pick (6, 12, 24 or 36 months) and also asks for a review after a significant change to the system, its scope or its service providers, or after an incident.

  
Are these policies ready to use as they are?

  
No. They are drafts: the procedures need your own steps, the boilerplate needs to match how your organization works, and a policy counts only once it is approved and followed. Nothing on this page is an assessment.
