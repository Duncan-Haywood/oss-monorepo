    What a first article inspection report is

    A first article inspection (FAI) is the full inspection of one part made by the production process, done to show that the process can make the part to the drawing before the shop runs the lot. In aerospace and defense, customers usually ask for it in the format of AS9102, an SAE International standard (current revision C). The standard itself is sold by SAE; this page does not reproduce it. The report it describes has three forms:

    • Form 1, part number accountability: the part, its drawing and revision, the organisation that made it, any sub-assemblies, and why this FAI was done (a full or partial FAI, and the reason).
    • Form 2, product accountability: raw material, special processes (heat treat, plating, anodize, NDT) and functional tests, each with its specification, the supplier and the certificate of conformance.
    • Form 3, characteristic accountability: every design characteristic, numbered to a balloon on the drawing, with its requirement, the measured result, the tooling used and a nonconformance number where it failed. This checker covers Form 3.

    When one is due

    A first article is normally due on a new part and again after a change that could affect the part: the design, the manufacturing process or source, the inspection method, the tooling or the location. A partial (delta) FAI covers only the characteristics a change touched. A lapse in production is a trigger too, but customers differ on it: Lockheed Martin's Q2A quality clause, for one, asks for a full FAI after a lapse of more than two years (Q2A rev 12). Your customer's PO clauses and quality manual decide; read them, and the RFQ review checklist · Drawing review flags an FAI requirement when it is in the RFQ, so you can price the inspection time into the quote.

    Ballooning the drawing

    Number every characteristic once, in a sequence with no gaps: dimensions, geometric tolerances, notes, finishes, thread callouts, markings. A feature called out "4X" can carry one balloon with four results, or four balloons (4.1 to 4.4), depending on what your customer asks for; either way every place needs a result. Basic dimensions are not inspected on their own: their tolerance lives in the feature control frame that refers to them. Reference dimensions in parentheses are not inspected.

    Why first article reports get sent back

    Customers' supplier guides list the same handful of errors (for one, GKN Aerospace's common FAIR mistakes):

    • A characteristic on the drawing with no balloon, or a balloon with no result. The checker catches skipped and repeated numbers and blank results; it cannot see a feature you never ballooned.
    • "Accept" written where a value can be measured. Many customers want the actual value. The checker flags a pass mark on a dimension with numeric limits.
    • A result outside its limits with no nonconformance number, or a report submitted as complete with a reject on it.
    • Fewer results than places on a multi-place feature.
    • Material and special-process certificates missing from Form 2, or the drawing revision on Form 1 not matching the revision the parts were made to.

    How the limits are read

    .500 ±.005 and .500 +/- .005 are bilateral; .500 +.002/-.000 unequal; 1.005/1.000 and 12.00-12.05 are limit dimensions; R.03 MAX and MIN .120 one-sided; 32 Ra a maximum. A geometric tolerance such as ⌖ Ø.010 Ⓜ A B C or Flatness .002 is read as zero to the tolerance, with the result being the measured deviation; bonus tolerance from a material-condition modifier is not added, so a result this page rejects might still be acceptable once the bonus is worked out, never the other way round. A bare number with no tolerance takes the general tolerance you enter for its decimal places. Threads and notes are attribute checks: record ACC, GO or PASS, or REJ, NO-GO or FAIL.

    "Tolerance used" is how far the worst reading sits from the middle of the band, as a share of half the band (100% is on the limit), or as a share of the maximum for one-sided limits. A first article near the limit is legal, but it is worth a look at gauge capability before you run the lot.
