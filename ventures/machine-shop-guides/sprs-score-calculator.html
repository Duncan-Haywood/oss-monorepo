How the score works

The NIST SP 800-171 DoD Assessment Methodology, Version 1.2.1 (24 June 2020) starts every assessment at 110, one point per requirement, and subtracts a weighted value for each requirement that is not implemented. Annex A of that document gives each requirement a value of 5, 3 or 1: the heavy ones are those whose absence most exposes the network or the CUI on it. If you implemented nothing, the score bottoms out at −203, the range the DoD CIO uses in its CMMC briefing on NIST alignment. This calculator's table sums to exactly that floor, which is the check that every weight is in place.

Two requirements can earn partial credit, both in the methodology and in 32 CFR 170.24, the CMMC scoring rule:

  • 3.5.3 multifactor authentication: 5 points off if MFA is not implemented for any users, 3 if it is implemented only for remote and privileged users.
  • 3.13.11 FIPS-validated cryptography: 5 points off if you do not encrypt CUI, 3 if you encrypt but the module is not FIPS-validated.

3.12.4, the system security plan, has no point value. The methodology says that without an SSP an assessment cannot be completed at all, so this calculator does not show a final score when you mark it not met.

What the Conditional Level 2 check means

Under 32 CFR 170.21 a CMMC Level 2 assessment can end in Conditional status, with a plan of action and milestones (POA&M) to close within 180 days, only if the score is at least 80% of 110 (88) and every open item is one the rule allows on a POA&M. In general that means 1-point requirements only; the one stated exception is 3.13.11 at its 3-point partial value. Section 170.21(a)(2)(iii) also names six requirements that may never be left on a POA&M, whatever their points, and the calculator checks them: 3.1.20 (external connections), 3.1.22 (public information), 3.12.4 (the system security plan), and 3.10.3, 3.10.4 and 3.10.5 (escorting visitors, physical access logs, managing physical access devices). Each is worth 1 point or none, so a 1-point-only reading would wrongly let them through. The downloaded POA&M starter marks each gap allowed or not.

Where this score goes

DFARS 252.204-7019 and 252.204-7020 require a current (no more than three years old) NIST SP 800-171 DoD Assessment in SPRS for contracts that carry DFARS 252.204-7012. A Basic assessment is the contractor's own self-assessment; the score, the assessment date and the date you expect to reach 110 go into SPRS. CMMC Level 2 (Self) adds a triennial self-assessment and an annual affirmation under 32 CFR 170.16. See CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2 for a small shop and what goes in an SSP and a POA&M.
