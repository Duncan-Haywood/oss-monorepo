      What an internal audit has to show

      ISO 9001 asks for internal audits in clause 9.2: a programme that covers the quality system at planned intervals, auditors chosen so the audit is objective and impartial (in practice, nobody audits their own work), results reported to management, and the corrections and corrective actions that follow (clause 10.2). A registrar's auditor reads the audit records to see that the programme ran and that what it found was acted on. In plain terms, each audit record should carry:

      • Scope and criteria: the clauses or processes covered, and the documents audited against (the standard, your quality manual, the customer's requirements).
      • Who and when: the auditor, the people interviewed, the date.
      • Objective evidence: for every result, what was looked at: the record, the job, the gauge, the person. "Conforms" with nothing written is hard to defend; a nonconformity with nothing written is not a finding.
      • Graded findings: major and minor nonconformities, observations and opportunities for improvement. Every nonconformity gets a corrective action.

      Major, minor, observation

      The standard does not grade findings; registrars and customers do, and most use the same lines. A major nonconformity is a requirement not met at all, or a failure that could let bad product reach the customer (no final inspection before shipping). A minor one is a single lapse in a system that otherwise works (one gauge past its calibration date). An observation is something that could become a nonconformity; an opportunity for improvement is a suggestion. CMMC Workspace's Audits tab uses the same four grades.

      Who can audit in a small shop

      The rule is independence, not a job title: the auditor should not audit their own work. In a shop of ten, that usually means the office manager audits production, the lead machinist audits purchasing and the office, and an outside auditor (or a neighbouring shop, in a swap) covers what the quality lead owns. Type who owns what is audited, and the page flags the auditor if their name is on that list.

      How often

      The standard sets no interval; the programme decides, weighted by how important a process is and how it has gone. One workable pattern for a small shop is four audits a year that between them touch every applicable clause, with production and inspection audited more often. "Plan the year" splits the clauses evenly in the standard's order as a starting point; move clauses between audits to suit how your shop works.

      Common questions

      What does an ISO 9001 internal audit cover?

      Over the programme, every clause of the quality system that applies to the shop, 4 to 10, at intervals the shop plans by how important each process is and how it has gone. One audit can take a few clauses or a process; the programme as a whole has to reach them all.

      How often should internal audits be done?

      ISO 9001 sets no interval; the shop plans it. One workable plan for a small shop is four audits a year that together touch every applicable clause, with production and inspection more often. CMMC Workspace flags any applicable clause not audited in 12 months and not planned.

      Can someone in the shop audit their own department?

      No. Clause 9.2 asks for auditors chosen so the audit is objective and impartial, which in practice means nobody audits their own work. In a small shop people audit each other's areas, or an outside auditor covers what the quality lead owns.

      What is the difference between a major and a minor nonconformity?

      The standard does not grade findings; registrars do. A major is a requirement not met at all, or a lapse that could let bad product reach the customer. A minor is a single lapse in a system that otherwise works. Both need a corrective action; an observation or improvement need not.
