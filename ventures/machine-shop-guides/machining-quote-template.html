Machining quote template (free Excel)

A quote spreadsheet for CNC and job shops. You type your own rates and the part, and it prices the lot at six quantity breaks. A second sheet holds the quote you send the buyer: price each and extended price, part number and revision, lead time and validity, and none of your cost lines. It ships with no rates, prices or markup of ours. Every number in it is yours.

What is in it

Sheet 1: Quote worksheet (yours, not the customer's)

BlockWhat you type
Your ratesSetup rate, programming rate and machine rate per hour, markup on cost, and a scrap allowance on material. Work them out from your books: how to price a machined part shows how.
The partPart number and revision, material cost per part, setup hours per lot, programming hours, how many lots you expect the program to serve, and cycle minutes per part.
Calibration factorsOptional. If your setups usually run 15% over the estimate, type 1.15. Leave it blank for 1. The quoted vs actual page works the factor out from your finished jobs.
Other operationsUp to six rows for deburr, inspection, heat treat, anodize or plating. Each has minutes per part at a rate, a flat charge per lot (an outside vendor's lot minimum, say), or both.
Quantity breaksSix quantities, pre-filled 1, 5, 10, 25, 50 and 100. Change them to what the RFQ asks for, and clear a column you don't need.

For each quantity it works out material, setup, programming, machining, other operations, the cost of the lot, the price of the lot, cost and price each, and how much of each part's price is setup and programming. Then it gives the price each as it goes on the quote, rounded up to the cent, and the extended price.

Sheet 2: Customer quote

Your shop and the customer, the buyer's RFQ reference, quote number and date, validity in days (with the date it runs out worked out for you), part number and revision (taken from sheet 1), description, material, finish, lead time, payment and shipping terms. Then quantity, price each and extended price, and your notes and exclusions. It never shows material, setup, machining or markup.

The Before you send it row lists what a buyer would send back: no revision, no validity, no lead time, no material, no quote number. It also catches a larger lot that costs less in total than the smaller one before it, because a buyer will just order the larger lot.

The formulas, written out

These are the formulas the browser calculator uses. The template's test fills both with the same numbers, recalculates the workbook in LibreOffice and checks every line at every break against the calculator's code, down to the cent on the quote.

LinePer lot of quantity q
Materialq × material per part × (1 + scrap %)
Setupsetup hours × setup factor × setup rate, once per lot
Programmingprogramming hours × programming rate ÷ lots expected over the program's life
Machiningq × cycle minutes × cycle factor ÷ 60 × machine rate
Other operationsq × Σ(minutes × rate) ÷ 60 + Σ flat lot charges
Pricecost × (1 + markup %). Markup is on cost: a 25% markup is a 20% margin.
Price each on the quoteprice ÷ q, rounded up to the cent; extended = that × q, so the quote adds up line by line and never comes in under the calculated price

The quantity breaks are this same sum run again at each quantity. A 100-off is cheaper per part than a 5-off because the setup, the programming and the flat lot charges are spread over more parts. It is arithmetic, not a discount table.

When a spreadsheet stops being enough

A template is the right tool for a handful of quotes a week. It gets slow when you have to retype the same part at a new revision, look up what you quoted last time, see which quotes won, or turn an emailed drawing into the inputs. Common questions

Is there a free machining quote template for Excel?

Yes: a two-sheet .xlsx with no macros. The quote worksheet prices material with scrap, setup, programming spread over the lots expected, cycle time, up to six other operations and markup at six quantity breaks, from your own rates. The customer quote sheet shows price each and extended price only.

Why is the price each rounded up to the cent?

So the quote never comes in under the calculated price, and so the extended price is the quoted price each times the quantity and the quote adds up line by line.

Should the cost lines go on the quote I send?

No. Material, setup, machining and markup are your internal numbers. The template keeps them on the quote worksheet; the customer quote sheet carries part number and revision, quantities, price each, extended price, lead time, validity and terms.
