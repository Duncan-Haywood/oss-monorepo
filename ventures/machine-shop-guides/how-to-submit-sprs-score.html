How to submit your SPRS score

The Supplier Performance Risk System (SPRS) is where a defense contractor posts its NIST SP 800-171 score and its CMMC self-assessments. You reach it through PIEE, you need one specific role to type anything, and a senior person in the company affirms CMMC results. This page puts the steps from SPRS's own public guides in one place, in the order a small shop meets them.

What this is based on

SPRS's public pages and guides, read on 28 November 2026: the Access Instructions, SPRS Vendor Cyber Reports Access (Release V4.0, December 2024), the NIST SP 800-171 Quick Entry Guide (Version 4.0, May 2024), the CMMC Level 1 Self-Assessment Quick Entry Guide (Version 4.0, December 2024), the CMMC Level 2 Self-Assessment Quick Entry Guide (Version 4.0, February 2025) and the Affirming Official for CMMC tutorial transcript. Button names are as those guides print them; screens change, so if yours differs, the guide on the SPRS NIST page or SPRS CMMC page wins.

SPRS only stores results. It does not run the assessment: in its own words, "The NIST SP 800-171 Basic Assessment cannot be performed in SPRS, SPRS only stores the results of NIST SP 800-171 Assessments." Work out the score first (the SPRS score calculator does the arithmetic) and write the system security plan before you log in.

Step 1. Have SAM, a CAGE code and a CAM in place

PIEE, the single sign-on that SPRS sits behind, works from your company's registration in the System for Award Management (SAM.gov). SPRS's access instructions list what has to exist first:

  • the company registered in SAM, with an Electronic Business point of contact (EBPOC);
  • a CAGE code added to the PIEE vendor group structure;
  • at least one Contractor Account Administrator (CAM) per CAGE. The CAM approves the company's PIEE registrations and role requests, and is "typically" the EBPOC or someone they designate.

A shop that sells to a prime but has never registered in SAM has none of these yet. A score can only be posted against a CAGE code, so this comes first. The PIEE vendor getting-started help walks the company setup.

Step 2. Get the "SPRS Cyber Vendor User" role in PIEE

SPRS has two vendor roles, and only one of them can type:

  • SPRS Cyber Vendor User: view, enter, edit or delete NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC information, for every CAGE in the company hierarchy. You need this one.
  • Contractor/Vendor (Support Role): view only, to monitor the company's data and scoring.

Already have a PIEE account (for Wide Area WorkFlow invoicing, say): log in to PIEE, choose My Account, then Add Additional Roles; confirm your profile and supervisor details; pick SPRS – Supplier Performance Risk System from the application list, tick the role, Add Roles, enter your CAGE as the location code, then finish the justification, summary and agreement steps.

New to PIEE: on the PIEE home page choose New User, read the New Vendor Organization – Getting Started Help, then Register; agree to the privacy statement, choose the user type Vendor, pick an authentication method (SPRS says a PKI certificate is not required for vendors), fill in the user profile and supervisor details, then add the SPRS role and CAGE as above.

Either way the request goes to your company's CAM. Until the CAM activates it, the SPRS tile in PIEE can be "visible, but not active". Check where a request stands under My Account, then Manage Roles. Two snags the guides name:

  • The only CAM is the one asking. Nobody else can approve it, so the guide says to ask PIEE to activate it: disa.global.servicedesk.mbx.eb-ticket-requests@mail.mil.
  • The view-only role, requested by a new user, is also reviewed by the SPRS program office, and SPRS warns it "can take multiple business days". Ask for the role weeks before a bid, not the night before.

Step 3. Open Cyber Reports and pick your CAGE

Log in to PIEE, choose the SPRS tile, then Cyber Reports (newer guides call it Cyber Reports (CMMC & NIST)). Pick the hierarchy from the drop-down and choose Run Cyber Reports. The first CAGE shown is the one on your PIEE profile; the one in brackets is the Highest Level Owner (HLO) of your hierarchy. An asterisk next to the CAGE means you have the Cyber Vendor User role for it. No asterisk, no editing: go back to step 2.

The hierarchy comes from SAM. If a CAGE is missing or sits under the wrong parent, SPRS cannot fix it: your EBPOC corrects it in SAM, and the NIST guide says updates "typically flow to SPRS within 48 hours". You cannot add a CAGE that is not in your hierarchy.

Step 4a. NIST SP 800-171 Basic Assessment (DFARS 252.204-7019 and -7020)

This is the score DFARS 252.204-7019 and -7020 ask for: -7019 makes a current one (not more than 3 years old unless the solicitation says less) a condition of award, and -7020(g) bars a prime from awarding a covered subcontract unless the sub has completed at least a Basic assessment within the last 3 years. That is why a prime asks a small shop for it. In Cyber Reports, open the NIST SP 800-171 Assessments tab and choose Add New Assessment. SPRS keeps these fields for each assessment, so have them ready:

FieldWhere it comes from
Assessment dateThe date you finished scoring.
Score110 minus 5, 3 or 1 for each requirement not met, per the DoD Assessment Methodology (calculator).
ScopeWhich systems the score covers (an enclave, or the whole company).
Plan of action completion dateThe date your open items will be done and you will reach 110.
Included CAGE code(s)Every CAGE the assessed systems serve.
SSP name, version and dateFrom the cover of your system security plan.

Save, and SPRS gives the assessment a ten-character DoD unique identifier. Its first two letters show the confidence level: SB for Basic (yours), SM and SH for the Medium and High assessments DoD performs. The guide notes the methodology and SSP "should be completed prior to entering assessment summary results". You can edit your Basic assessment later with the pencil icon; only Basic is yours to add, edit or delete. A result turns red in SPRS when the assessment date is more than three years old.

Step 4b. CMMC Level 1 self-assessment

Open the CMMC Assessments tab and choose Add New Level 1 CMMC Self-Assessment. Enter the details, then Continue to Affirmation. The guide's note: compliance with every security requirement of FAR 52.204-21 is required to reach a "Final Level 1 Self-Assessment"; Level 1 has no partial result and no plan of action. Check the 15 first with the Level 1 self-assessment checklist.

  • A Final Level 1 Self-Assessment "will automatically become 'No CMMC Status (Expired Assessment)' after 1 year". Put the date in a calendar (affirmation due dates writes the file).
  • Final Level 1 Self-Assessment "is the only CMMC Status Type that will be visible to Government Personnel". A draft or a result waiting on affirmation does not count toward a bid.

Step 4c. CMMC Level 2 self-assessment

Level 2 is entered requirement by requirement, not as one number. Open CMMC Assessments, then the CMMC Level 2 (Self) tab, and choose Add New Level 2 CMMC Self-Assessment. For each requirement you can open its assessment objectives with Requirement Objectives, pick the compliance status, and Save and Continue through each family. Every requirement must be answered before you can go on. Then add the assessment scope, the employee count and the included CAGEs (Open CAGE Hierarchy, or type them separated by commas).

SPRS then scores it, and only two results can be affirmed:

ScoreStatus SPRS givesHow long it lasts
110CMMC L2 Final Level 2 Self-AssessmentThree years, with annual affirmations
88 to 109CMMC L2 Conditional Level 2 Self-Assessment180 days, to close the POA&M
Below 88Cannot be affirmedNone

One catch the guide spells out: "If a requirement is not able to be subject to a Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M), then the Status Type will be No CMMC Status regardless of score." A 105 with one of the 5-point requirements open is not Conditional. The SPRS score calculator runs the same Conditional check (the 88 points, and the requirements 32 CFR 170.21 never allows on a POA&M) before you log in, and SSP and POA&M covers what the plan has to say.

Step 5. The affirming official

A CMMC result counts only once it is affirmed by the Affirming Official (AO): in 32 CFR 170's words, quoted in SPRS's tutorial, the "senior level representative" of the company "who is responsible for ensuring the OSA's compliance with the CMMC Program requirements and has the authority to affirm" it. In a small shop that is usually the owner or president, not the person who did the typing.

  • Hand it over. If the person entering the assessment is not the AO, enter the AO's email and choose Transfer to AO. The record waits with the status Pending Affirmation.
  • The AO needs their own PIEE account with the Cyber Vendor User role (step 2). Their name and details come from their PIEE registration and can only be changed in PIEE.
  • Affirming: the AO opens the record (the pencil icon at Level 1; the pencil or the Affirm button at Level 2), reviews it, chooses Continue to Affirmation, ticks the box certifying the affirmation statement and chooses Affirm. SPRS then shows the final status and assigns a CMMC unique identifier (UID).
  • Every year after: at Level 2 the annual affirmation is made from the Affirm button in the Affirmation Expiration Date column. For C3PAO assessments the tutorial says that button appears 60 days before each annual expiration.

The affirmation is a statement of the company's compliance, signed by a named senior official. Affirm what is implemented, with the evidence to show it, not what the templates say.

Before you log in: a short list

  • SAM registration active; EBPOC and CAM named; your CAGE code(s).
  • PIEE account with SPRS Cyber Vendor User, requested well ahead; the AO's own account too.
  • The assessment done, with the date, the scope and the score, and for Level 2 the status of all 110 requirements.
  • The SSP's name, version and date; for a score under 110, the POA&M and the date you will reach 110.
  • A calendar entry for the expiry: a year for Level 1, 180 days for a Conditional Level 2, annual affirmations and three years for a Final Level 2, three years for a NIST Basic score.

Questions on the SPRS application go to the SPRS help desk linked from the SPRS home page. SPRS points questions on how to conduct a NIST SP 800-171 assessment to your program office, your contracts representative or DCMA, and questions on interpreting CMMC documents to the DoD CIO's CMMC mailbox listed on SPRS's CMMC page.

Common questions

How do I submit my SPRS score?

Log in to PIEE with the SPRS Cyber Vendor User role, open SPRS, then Cyber Reports, pick your CAGE hierarchy and run it. For a NIST SP 800-171 Basic Assessment, choose Add New Assessment on the NIST SP 800-171 Assessments tab and enter the date, score, scope, plan of action completion date, CAGE codes and the SSP's name, version and date. SPRS stores the result; it does not perform the assessment.

What PIEE role do I need to enter an SPRS score?

SPRS Cyber Vendor User. It lets you enter, edit or delete NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC information for every CAGE in your company hierarchy. The Contractor/Vendor (Support Role) is view only. Your company's Contractor Account Administrator (CAM) approves the request; until then the SPRS tile can show but not work.

What CMMC Level 2 self-assessment score can be affirmed in SPRS?

Only 110, which gives a Final Level 2 Self-Assessment valid three years with annual affirmations, and 88 to 109, which gives a Conditional status valid 180 days. If an open requirement is one that may not go on a POA&M, SPRS gives No CMMC Status whatever the score.

Who affirms a CMMC self-assessment in SPRS?

The Affirming Official: the senior person responsible for the company's CMMC compliance, with their own PIEE account and the Cyber Vendor User role. Whoever entered the assessment can send it to them with Transfer to AO; the AO reviews it, ticks the affirmation statement and chooses Affirm.
