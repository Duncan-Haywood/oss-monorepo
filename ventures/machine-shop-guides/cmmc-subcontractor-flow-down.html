CMMC flow-down: which level your subcontractors and suppliers need

If your prime put DFARS 252.204-7021 in your purchase order, the CMMC requirement does not stop at your shop. You have to flow it down to the heat treater, plater, anodizer, grinder or inspection lab you send the work to, at the level that fits what you send them, and check their status before you award the job. This page sets out the rule word for word from 32 CFR 170.23 and the clause, read on eCFR on 27 November 2026.

Commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) items are excluded from the flow-down in 252.204-7021(f)(1). A supplier's level is set by the information it handles, not by your own level: a Level 2 shop can flow Level 1 to a supplier that only gets FCI. 32 CFR 170.23(b) adds that "the DoD may provide specific guidance pertaining to flow-down", so read your prime's own flow-down instructions too.

The rule, word for word

32 CFR 170.23, "Application to subcontractors", opens:

Prime contractors shall comply and shall require subcontractors to comply with and to flow down CMMC requirements, such that compliance will be required throughout the supply chain at all tiers with the applicable CMMC level and assessment type for each subcontract

and then sets the level for each case:

(1) If a subcontractor will only process, store, or transmit FCI (and not CUI) in performance of the subcontract, then a CMMC Status of Level 1 (Self) is required for the subcontractor.

(2) If a subcontractor will process, store, or transmit CUI in performance of the subcontract, then a CMMC Status of Level 2 (Self) is the minimum requirement for the subcontractor.

(3) If a subcontractor will process, store, or transmit CUI in performance of the subcontract and the associated prime contract has a requirement for a CMMC Status of Level 2 (C3PAO), then the CMMC Status of Level 2 (C3PAO) is the minimum requirement for the subcontractor.

(4) If a subcontractor will process, store, or transmit CUI in performance of the subcontract and the associated prime contract has a requirement for the CMMC Status of Level 3 (DIBCAC), then the CMMC Status of Level 2 (C3PAO) is the minimum requirement for the subcontractor.

"At all tiers" means the rule repeats down the chain: if your plater sends CUI on to its own stripping house, it flows the requirement down in turn.

What the clause makes you do, and when

DFARS 252.204-7021 (NOV 2025) turns the rule into four duties for you as the higher-tier contractor:

  • Pick the right level for each subcontract. Paragraph (d)(1)(ii): Consult 32 CFR 170.23 related to the flowdown of the CMMC requirements, and flow down the correct CMMC level to subcontracts and other contractual instruments.
  • Put the clause in the subcontract. Paragraph (f)(1): insert the substance of this clause, including this paragraph (f) and excluding paragraph (e)(1) in subcontracts, commercial ones included, except COTS items, when the subcontract will involve FCI or CUI. Paragraph (e)(1), the one left out, is the duty to give the Contracting Officer your CMMC UIDs; your supplier reports to you, not to the government's contracting officer.
  • Check before award. Paragraph (f)(2): Prior to awarding a subcontract or other contractual instrument, ensure that the subcontractor has a current CMMC certificate or current CMMC status at the CMMC level that is appropriate for the information that is being flowed down to the subcontractor based on the requirements at 32 CFR 170.23.
  • Keep the supplier's affirmation current. Paragraph (d)(4): ensure all subcontractors and suppliers complete prior to subcontract award, and maintain on an annual basis, an affirmation by their affirming official, for each of their systems that handle the FCI or CUI.

What "current" means for a supplier's status

The clause defines it in paragraph (a). In plain terms:

  • Final Level 1 (Self): not older than 1 year, with an affirmation not older than 1 year.
  • Final Level 2 (Self or C3PAO): not older than 3 years, with an affirmation of continuous compliance not older than 1 year.
  • Conditional Level 2 (Self or C3PAO): not older than 180 days, with an affirmation. A supplier on a Conditional status has a POA&M to close out inside that window (paragraph (d)(5)).
  • In every case, with No changes in compliance with the requirements at 32 CFR part 170 since the status date.

So "they passed CMMC" is not enough. Ask each supplier for the level and assessment type, the CMMC status date, the date of the latest affirmation and the CMMC UID, and note when each one lapses. The CMMC affirmation due dates page works the dates out from a status date the same way for your own shop.

Where a machine shop meets this

What you send decides the level, so decide what each supplier actually needs before the job goes out:

  • Outside processes (heat treat, plating, anodizing, passivation, painting, NDT). If the processor gets the CUI-marked drawing or model, the subcontract carries CUI and Level 2 applies. If it gets only what is needed to do the process, such as part number, quantity and process specification, ask your prime whether that package is FCI rather than CUI. The marking and your prime's guidance decide that, not a guess.
  • Machining you sub out to another shop on overflow work almost always means sending the drawing and model. Treat it as the same level as your own job.
  • Inspection and calibration labs that receive drawings or inspection data for the part are handling whatever that data is.
  • Material suppliers selling stock from a catalog are often COTS (excluded). A mill or distributor cutting to a print you send is not just selling catalog stock: look at what the print is.

Keeping CUI away from a supplier who does not need it is often cheaper for both of you than asking a small plating shop to stand up 110 NIST SP 800-171 requirements. If the work needs the CUI, it needs a supplier at Level 2.

Where Phase 2 leaves this (status from 24 November 2026)

As our CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2 guide records (checked 24 November 2026), CMMC Phase 2 was suspended on 13 July 2026, and a DFARS class deviation lets requiring activities specify only Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) while it lasts. So new prime contracts should not require Level 2 (C3PAO), and the C3PAO rows in the table above apply only where a contract already says so. The flow-down duty itself is in the clause and did not stop. We could not re-open the DoW CIO pages on 27 November 2026; check the DoW CIO CMMC page before you rely on that status.

A supplier register, in five columns

A spreadsheet is enough to start. One row per supplier per kind of work:

  1. Supplier and the work they do for you.
  2. What they receive: nothing controlled, FCI or CUI.
  3. Level required, from the table above (and the prime contract's level for CUI).
  4. Their status: level, Final or Conditional, status date, CMMC UID, latest affirmation date.
  5. When it lapses: a year from the affirmation, three years (Level 2) or one year (Level 1) from the status date, 180 days for Conditional.

Check the row before each award, as (f)(2) asks, not once a year.

Common questions

What CMMC level does my subcontractor need?

It depends on what the subcontract sends them. Under 32 CFR 170.23, a supplier that will only process, store or transmit FCI needs Level 1 (Self). One that handles CUI needs Level 2 (Self) at least, and Level 2 (C3PAO) when the prime contract requires Level 2 (C3PAO) or Level 3 (DIBCAC).

Do I have to flow CMMC down to my plater or heat treater?

Yes, when the subcontract will involve FCI or CUI. DFARS 252.204-7021(f)(1) has you insert the substance of the clause, except paragraph (e)(1), in subcontracts that will contain a requirement to process, store or transmit FCI or CUI, commercial ones included; commercially available off-the-shelf items are excluded.

What do I check before I award a subcontract?

That the supplier has a current CMMC status at the level the information calls for (252.204-7021(f)(2)) and has completed its affirmation before award (paragraph (d)(4)). Current means a Final Level 1 status within 1 year, a Final Level 2 status within 3 years, or a Conditional status within 180 days, each with an affirmation no older than 1 year.
