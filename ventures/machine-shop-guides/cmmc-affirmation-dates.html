CMMC affirmation and assessment due dates

Passing a CMMC assessment starts a calendar. Your Affirming Official affirms in SPRS after the assessment and every year after it, a Conditional Level 2 status has 180 days to be closed out, and the next assessment comes round in one year (Level 1) or three (Level 2). Enter the dates you already have and the page works out what is due next, lays out the years to your next assessment, and gives you a calendar file that reminds you 60 and 7 days ahead.

    The rules behind the dates

    • Affirmation, 32 CFR 170.22(a). The Affirming Official must affirm the continuing compliance of their respective organizations with the specified security requirement after every assessment, including POA&M closeout, and annually thereafter. Affirmations are entered electronically in SPRS. Three kinds, then: one after the assessment, one after a POA&M closeout, and one every year. This page counts the year from the latest affirmation you enter.
    • Level 1 (Self), 32 CFR 170.15(a)(1). the OSA must conduct a Level 1 self-assessment on an annual basis and submit the results in SPRS. Level 1 has no Conditional status: every one of the 15 requirements is met, or the level is not claimed (the Level 1 self-assessment walks through them).
    • Level 2 (Self), 32 CFR 170.16(a)(1). the OSA must conduct a Level 2 self-assessment every three years. A score of at least 88 with only the allowed gaps gives a Conditional status (the SPRS score calculator checks this), and the POA&M closeout self-assessment has to be posted to SPRS within 180 days of the CMMC Status Date associated with the Conditional Level 2 (Self) status. A closeout that passes gives a Final status; the three years still count from the original status date.
    • Level 2 (C3PAO), 32 CFR 170.17. The same shape with an assessor: a certification assessment by an authorized C3PAO every three years, results posted by the C3PAO in eMASS (which passes them to SPRS), a POA&M closeout assessment by a C3PAO within 180 days of a Conditional status date, and the affirmation after each assessment and annually. If the 180 days pass without a closeout, the Conditional status expires.
    • Missing an affirmation. The final rule's summary says an assessment lapses if it is not affirmed annually. Put the date in a calendar the Affirming Official reads, not only in someone's head.

    Why shops miss these dates

    The assessment is a project with a deadline everyone knows. The affirmation a year later is a five-minute task with no one chasing it, often owned by the company officer rather than whoever ran the assessment, and SPRS does not remind you. A Conditional status adds a 180-day clock that starts on the status date, not when you start fixing things. Writing the dates down the day the status posts, with reminders far enough ahead to fix anything that drifted since, is most of the job. Your system security plan should name the Affirming Official and the date of the next review, and the SSP and POA&M guide covers what a closeout has to show.

    Common questions

    How often does a CMMC affirmation have to be made?

    After every assessment, including a POA&M closeout, and annually thereafter (32 CFR 170.22(a)). The Affirming Official enters each one in SPRS. This page counts the year from the latest affirmation you enter.

    How long do I have to close out a Conditional CMMC Level 2 status?

    180 days from the Conditional CMMC Status Date. For Level 2 (Self) the closeout self-assessment is posted to SPRS within that time; for Level 2 (C3PAO) a C3PAO performs the closeout assessment. If the 180 days pass without a closeout, the Conditional status expires.

    When is the next CMMC assessment due?

    A year after the CMMC Status Date for Level 1 (Self), and three years after it for Level 2, by yourself or by a C3PAO. A POA&M closeout does not restart the three years: they count from the original status date.
