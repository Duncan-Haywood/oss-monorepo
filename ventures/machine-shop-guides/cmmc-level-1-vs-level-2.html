CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2 for a small machine shop

Which level you need depends on one question: does your shop receive Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) under the contract, or only Federal Contract Information (FCI)? This guide walks through the difference, what each level asks of you, and where CMMC stands on 24 November 2026.

Status check, 24 November 2026

  • Phase 1 is in force. It began on 10 November 2025, when the DFARS rule implementing CMMC took effect (DFARS Case 2019-D041 final rule, published 10 September 2025). Phase 1 solicitations generally call for Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) assessments.
  • Phase 2 is suspended. Phase 2, which would have started requiring third-party (C3PAO) Level 2 certification on 10 November 2026, was suspended by the Department of War on 13 July 2026 (Department of War release; DoW CIO implementing memo). A DFARS class deviation (tracking number 2026-O0025, Revision 3) lets requiring activities specify only Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) while the suspension lasts.
  • Self-assessments still apply, and so does DFARS 252.204-7012 (safeguarding covered defense information). A CMMC Reform Task Force took industry comments until 14 August 2026. We found no announced restart date for Phase 2 as of 24 November 2026.

FCI or CUI: the question that decides the level

FCI is information provided by or generated for the government under a contract that is not intended for public release: a purchase order, a delivery schedule, a part number on a government job. CUI is a narrower category the government marks for safeguarding; for a machine shop the usual example is a drawing or model of a defense part that arrives marked CUI (often with export-control markings). If every contract you hold or bid only involves FCI, you are looking at Level 1. If any contract sends you CUI, the systems that touch it are in scope for Level 2.

The contract tells you. Look for these clauses in your purchase orders and flow-downs from your prime:

  • FAR 52.204-21, Basic Safeguarding of Covered Contractor Information Systems: the 15 basic requirements behind Level 1.
  • DFARS 252.204-7012, Safeguarding Covered Defense Information and Cyber Incident Reporting: requires NIST SP 800-171 on systems that handle covered defense information, and 72-hour incident reporting.
  • DFARS 252.204-7019 and 252.204-7020: require a current NIST SP 800-171 DoD Assessment score in SPRS (no more than three years old).
  • DFARS 252.204-7021: names the CMMC level and assessment type the contract requires.

Level 1: 15 requirements, a yearly self-assessment

Under 32 CFR 170.15, Level 1 covers the 15 basic safeguarding requirements of FAR 52.204-21(b)(1): limit system access to authorized users, control what they can do, verify and control external connections, control information posted publicly, identify and authenticate users, sanitize media before disposal, limit physical access, escort visitors, monitor communications at the boundary, separate public-facing systems, fix flaws promptly, run malicious-code protection, keep it updated, and scan. You assess yourself every year, enter the result in SPRS, and a senior official affirms it. Each requirement is simply met or not met; there is no point score and no plan of action to defer a gap, so every requirement must be met. Check yours with the CMMC Level 1 self-assessment checklist.

Level 2: 110 requirements, a score, and possibly an assessor

Level 2 covers the 110 requirements of NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2. Under 32 CFR 170.16, Level 2 (Self) means a self-assessment every three years with an annual affirmation, the score posted in SPRS, and assessment artifacts kept for six years. Level 2 (C3PAO) under 32 CFR 170.17 is the same 110 requirements assessed by an authorized third party every three years; while Phase 2 is suspended, new solicitations should not require it. Scoring follows 32 CFR 170.24: start at 110 and subtract 5, 3 or 1 for each requirement not met. A score of at least 88 with only permitted items open can earn Conditional status with a POA&M to close within 180 days (32 CFR 170.21).

What usually puts a small shop in Level 2 scope

CUI rarely stays in one place. A drawing arrives by email, is saved to a shared folder, opened on a CAM workstation, turned into a program that goes to a machine controller, and printed for the operator and the inspector. Each of those is a system or a piece of media that may be in scope. Shops often cut the work by keeping CUI in one enclave: one email account or file share for CUI jobs, a small set of workstations, and a written procedure for prints. Whether that fits your shop is a scoping decision under 32 CFR 170.19, and worth getting right before you write the SSP.

Two common mistakes

  • Posting 110 because the policies exist. A requirement is met when it is implemented and you have evidence, not when a template says it is. The score you post is affirmed by a senior official, and a false affirmation carries legal risk.
  • Treating the Phase 2 suspension as a pause on everything. DFARS 252.204-7012 and the SPRS score requirements of 7019/7020 did not stop on 13 July 2026.
