This calculator vs. Vendr

Vendr is a well-known SaaS buying platform, described on vendr.com as software purchasing and renewal management backed by a team of buyers and pricing data gathered across its customer base. Bring it a renewal and its buyers negotiate directly with the vendor on your behalf, aiming to land a lower price than you would get alone, and it can also carry the procurement paperwork for the deal. Pricing is by quote, and using it means engaging Vendr's team to run a negotiation, not opening a page and typing numbers.

That makes Vendr the right choice for one job: having someone else negotiate a lower price on a SaaS renewal. This page does not do that job, and does not try to. It negotiates with no one, contacts no vendor, and has no pricing data from other customers — it takes the seat counts and price you already have for one renewal and tells you how much of that price is unused seats, which is a number worth knowing whether or not anyone ever negotiates the price itself.

Of the seats I am about to pay for at whatever price is quoted, how many are actually unused? This calculator — type purchased seats, seats used, price, and months left; get the avoidable spend in seconds, no signup.
Do I want Vendr's negotiators or procurement team handling the purchase paperwork? Vendr or a comparable platform — this page has no negotiators and touches no paperwork.
Is it worth even starting a renegotiation, or would trimming unused seats alone cover the overage? This calculator's numbers first — knowing the unused-seat cost is a reasonable input to that decision, negotiated price or not.

Nothing stops these two from being used together: a true-up here can be the number you bring into a Vendr negotiation, or the reason a negotiation is not needed at all because trimming seats already closes the gap. This calculator is deliberately the opposite shape from a buying platform: nothing to engage, no fee, and nothing you type ever leaves your browser. That makes it useful for sizing the unused-seat cost on a renewal you already have numbers for, and the wrong tool for having someone negotiate the vendor down — that is exactly the job a platform like Vendr is built for.

It needs no account, no negotiator, and no sign-in: it is a static page, the arithmetic runs in your browser, and once it has loaded it keeps working with the connection off.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or a platform like Vendr?

They do different jobs. Vendr is a paid SaaS buying platform, described on Vendr's own site at vendr.com, that negotiates software purchases and renewals on a customer's behalf — its team of buyers uses market pricing data gathered across its customer base to push a vendor toward a lower price, and it can also front the paperwork and procurement workflow for the purchase. That is a negotiation service: someone else's buyers talk to the vendor for you, typically as part of a paid plan or a percentage-of-savings fee. This page does not negotiate anything and does not talk to any vendor. It has no market pricing data and no buyers — it takes the renewal quote already in front of you (purchased seats, seats actually used, price, months left) and turns it into an avoidable-spend figure in your browser, right now, with no signup and nothing sent anywhere.
