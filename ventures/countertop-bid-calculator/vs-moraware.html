This calculator vs. Moraware CounterGo

Two different shapes: CounterGo is an account-based platform for drawing, quoting, and cut-listing a stone shop's jobs across its whole production floor. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this job cost, before you draw it or quote it.

What each tool is for

Moraware CounterGo is drawing, quoting, and cut-layout software built specifically for countertop and stone fabrication shops, from single-shop operators to multi-location fabricators. Shops use it to draw a room's actual counter layout, generate a customer-facing quote from that drawing, and lay finished pieces out on slabs so the shop floor knows what to cut. Moraware sells CounterGo on an ongoing, account-based subscription and also offers a separate product, Systemize, for tracking a job through templating, fabrication, and installation, which shops commonly run alongside it. Moraware's own site does not publish specific plan names or dollar pricing, so no figure for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category are stated, and no dollar amount for CounterGo appears on this page. Public descriptions of that software describe drawing a room and laying cut pieces out on slabs the shop has already bought; none describe estimating, from a rough takeoff of finished square footage, cutouts, edges, and waterfalls alone, what the whole job should sell for before a drawing exists — which is the one thing this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what CounterGo does. It does not draw a room, store a customer record, or lay finished pieces out on a specific slab for the saw. It takes one job's finished square footage, its layout shape (straight, L, U, or island) and the waste percent that shape implies, sink and cooktop cutout hours by mount type, edge profile, backsplash length, and waterfall ends, plus fabricator wage, payroll burden, and target margin, and turns that into the slabs the takeoff actually consumes, the price per finished square foot, the bid price, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money — plus, if you already have a number on the table, a check of whether it clears that walk-away floor, and a printable customer proposal. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Moraware CounterGo?

They do different jobs, and a stone fabrication shop of any size will likely end up touching both. Moraware CounterGo is drawing, quoting, and cut-layout software used by countertop and stone fabrication shops, sold on an ongoing per-user account basis, often alongside Moraware's separate Systemize product for tracking a job through production. It is built to draw a room's actual layout, generate a customer quote from that drawing, and lay the pieces out on slabs for the shop floor. This calculator does one narrower thing: starting from finished square footage, layout waste by shape, sink and cooktop cutouts, edge profile, backsplash, and waterfall ends, it turns that into the price per finished square foot, the whole slabs the takeoff actually consumes, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money, before you have quoted or drawn anything. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A shop that already draws every room and needs one system tracking quotes, templates, and slab layout through production is better served by CounterGo or a comparable stone-shop platform; a fabricator who wants an instant, private gut-check on what a job should cost from a rough takeoff, before opening a drawing tool, is in the right place here.
