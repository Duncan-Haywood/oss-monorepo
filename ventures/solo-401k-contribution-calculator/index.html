How much can you put in your Solo 401(k) in 2026?

  • Sole proprietor / single-member LLC only. This applies the net-self-employment-earnings formula for a Schedule C filer with no W-2 wages from the business. An S-corp or partnership uses a different formula (W-2 wages or a K-1 allocation) and this tool does not compute it.

Common questions

What is the 2026 Solo 401(k) contribution limit?

The employee elective deferral is $24,500 for 2026 ($32,500 with the age-50 catch-up, or $35,750 with the SECURE 2.0 enhanced catch-up for ages 60-63). Added to a 20% employer profit-sharing contribution from net self-employment earnings, the combined total is capped at $72,000, or $80,000 with the standard catch-up, or $83,250 with the enhanced 60-63 catch-up.

How is the employer profit-sharing contribution calculated for a sole proprietor?

Net profit minus half of self-employment tax gives net earnings from self-employment. A plan stating a 25% employer contribution rate converts to an effective 20% rate applied to that net-earnings figure for a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, because the contribution is itself computed on earnings net of the contribution.

Does the elective-deferral limit apply separately to a Solo 401(k) and a W-2 job's 401(k)?

No. The elective-deferral limit applies per person across every 401(k) plan combined, including a separate employer's plan. Deferrals already made at a W-2 job reduce the room left for the Solo 401(k) dollar for dollar.

What is the enhanced catch-up for ages 60-63?

SECURE 2.0 gives ages 60, 61, 62, and 63 an $11,250 catch-up for 2026 instead of the standard $8,000 catch-up — it replaces rather than stacks with the standard amount, and the plan document must specifically allow it.
