How much should I charge for vinyl siding?

Enough to cover the squares you will actually hang, not enough to match the cheapest postcard on the block. A one-story vinyl wrap with four windows and a two-story fiber-cement tear-off with gables are not the same job, and a flat price per square foot cannot see the difference.

Price the net wall, then the waste

Start with length × height on each elevation. Subtract openings once — windows and doors are not siding. Convert the remainder to squares (100 sq ft) and add waste. Vinyl often needs about 10%; fiber cement and engineered wood run higher because of cuts and breakage. That waste is material you buy and hours you spend. Leaving it out is how a “measured” bid still comes up a square short.

Height, tear-off, and openings are not “included”

A ranch is the install you already priced. A second story is not twice the hours, but it is not the same hours either — staging, carrying, and ladder time add a real fraction. Tear-off is its own hours plus a dumpster. Each window and door is wrap and J-channel, not a hole that makes the job cheaper. If those lines are “included” in an $8-a-foot special, they are unpaid.

Linear trim is a second materials list

Starter, outside corners, inside corners, J-channel, and undersill are priced by the foot, not by the square. A small house with many openings can spend as much on trim as on an extra square of siding. Contractors who quote only the field and “figure trim in” are guessing the line that most often blows the takeoff.

Then load the wage and keep a margin

An installer paid $32 often costs more than $42 once employer taxes, ladder-and-tear-off workers’ compensation, liability, and paid time off are counted. Overhead sits on top of that direct cost. Margin is a share of the price the customer sees, not a markup you add last and forget. The walk-away number is total cost: below it the job makes nothing, even before you pay yourself.

Common questions

How much should I charge for vinyl siding?

Charge the hours and material the job will actually take — net wall area plus waste, times stories, plus tear-off, housewrap, openings, linear trim, travel, and a dumpster — then divide cost by one minus your target margin. A flat price per square foot hides the difference between a one-story vinyl wrap and a two-story fiber-cement tear-off.

Why do two houses of the same size bid so differently?

Waste, height, openings, and tear-off. A gable elevation is not a ranch wall, and twelve windows are not four. A neighborhood special that ignores those lines is how a crew works a week for a three-day price.
