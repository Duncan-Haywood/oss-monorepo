Common questions

Which US states currently require a pay range in the job posting itself?

As of this tool's last check (September 2026): Colorado, California, Washington, New York, Hawaii, Illinois, Maryland, Minnesota, New Jersey, Vermont, the District of Columbia, Massachusetts, Virginia, and Maine. Each has its own effective date and employer-size threshold, shown in the table below. Several other states and cities require disclosure at a different point in the process (upon request, before an interview, after an offer) rather than in the ad — this tool does not cover those.

How does this tool decide whether my posting text has a range?

It is a literal text scan: it looks for two dollar figures close together, joined by a dash, en dash, or the word "to" or "through" (like "$60,000 - $80,000" or "$28.50 to $34.00 per hour"). It does not understand whether the number is a good-faith range, a wage versus a bonus figure, or whether your specific posting is otherwise compliant — it only reports what the wording contains.

Is this legal advice, or a guarantee my posting is compliant?

No. This is a text-pattern scan plus a reference lookup against a fixed table of state posting-range laws, current as of the date shown. It does not know your employer's size, whether the role is exempt, or your specific state's current statute text, and it does not cover city or county ordinances layered on top of state law. Verify against your state's labor agency or an employment attorney before relying on this.

Is anything I type uploaded anywhere?

No. The posting text you paste and the states you pick are scanned and looked up entirely in your browser from a fixed table shipped with the page. Nothing is sent anywhere.
