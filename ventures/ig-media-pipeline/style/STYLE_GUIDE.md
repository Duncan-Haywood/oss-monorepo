# Style guide

This is the taste every judging step is held to: scoring frames, directing RAW edits, building
reels, critiquing reels. Edit it to change the look; every stage re-reads it on its next run.

## Who it is for

A personal Instagram account of travel, landscape, night-sky and city photography and
videography, with the people in the photographer's life. Viewers scroll fast on a phone. A post
has about one second to earn a pause and about three to earn a watch-through.

What Instagram rewards: **watch time** (completion and replays), **sends** (people DM it to a friend:
the biggest lever for reaching non-followers) and **saves**. Every reel is made for one of three
outcomes: a replay (an awe loop), a send ("we should go here") or a save (a spot, a time, a tip).
Instagram says it recommends less often what is low-resolution, watermarked, **muted**, **framed with
borders**, **mostly text**, or already posted, and nothing over 3 minutes to new audiences. All of
this footage is the photographer's own: AI is for craft (interpolation, parallax, reframing), never
for generated scenery, objects or people.

## What makes a keeper

Score 0-10 for "belongs in a curated feed". Most frames are 3-6. Reserve 8+ for frames you would
lead a post with.

1. **One clear subject.** You can say what the picture is of in three words. Clutter, mergers at
   the frame edge and dead centres lose points.
2. **Light.** Golden and blue hour, window light, practical lights at night, clean star fields.
   Flat midday light needs a strong subject to survive.
3. **A moment.** Expressions mid-laugh, a gesture, motion caught at its peak, a glance. Of a burst,
   the frame with eyes open and the best expression wins.
4. **Sense of place.** For travel, one establishing frame per set that says where you are.
5. **Phone-sized readability.** The subject must read at 1080 px wide. Tiny subjects in big
   landscapes need strong shape or contrast.
6. **Technical floor.** Missed focus on the subject, motion blur that isn't intentional, blown
   faces, and crooked horizons are disqualifying unless the frame is a rare moment.

Skip: near-duplicates of a better frame, test shots, lens-cap and pocket frames, screens,
paperwork, anything showing private documents or children in a way a parent wouldn't post.

Never name or guess the identity of a person. Describe people by role in the frame ("couple",
"friend at the table").

## Crops

- **Feed photos**: 4:5 portrait unless the config says 3:4. Keep the subject on a third or centred
  with symmetry. Leave headroom; never cut at joints.
- **Reels**: 9:16. Pick the focus so the subject sits in the middle 60% of the height; the top
  and bottom 15% get covered by Instagram's UI.

## The look (RAW edits)

Natural, clean and slightly warm, never "filtered".

- Exposure for the subject, then protect highlights. Skies keep detail; faces never clip.
- White balance: keep the mood of the light (warm sunset stays warm, blue hour stays blue) but
  correct casts on skin. Night city: neutral-to-cool shadows, warm practicals.
- Contrast moderate; lift shadows only until they read on a phone. Blacks are black.
- Vibrance before saturation. Saturation rarely above +10. Skin must look like skin.
- Night sky: cool-neutral, noise reduction on, stars crisp, no crushed foreground.
- Straighten horizons and verticals of buildings.
- When a frame is a judgment call, edit lighter.

## Reels

Three lengths:

- **Short, 7-15 s** (or 20-30 s): for new audiences. Cut on every beat or every other beat:
  0.4-0.8 s a still, 0.8-1.5 s a clip, 8-16 shots, one ~2 s hero hold that still moves. "Photo
  dump on the beat" suits this stills-heavy library. Several per event, each with its own hook.
- **Medium, 30-60 s**: for followers. An energy arc: a fast open (hook plus 3-4 quick cuts), a
  slower core with longer holds on the best moments, a fast close into the loop. Pace by contrast:
  wide against detail, motion against stillness, loud against quiet.
- **Long, 60-180 s**: recaps of a trip or a big event, in three acts (drop in, build through
  contrasting moments, payoff), mostly video and timelapse with stills as accents, re-hooking every
  ~20 s. Never over 180 s.

Hard rules (a reel that breaks one does not ship):

1. **Never silent.** A music bed from the licensed library, cut to its beats, levelled to -14 LUFS,
   with natural sound (waves, wind, laughter, a toast) underneath. The track goes in the caption
   file so a trending sound can be swapped in when posting.
2. **No borders.** No letterbox or pillarbox bars. The blurred-copy "fit" layout at most once per
   reel, never in the first 3 s, never over 20% of the runtime. For wide frames prefer a slow
   full-bleed pan across the frame, then a tighter subject-aware crop, then a different shot.
3. **The first second moves.** A clip, a speed ramp, a whip into the shot or a real pan. A still
   with a slow push-in is not a hook. No fade from black, no title card, no establishing shot unless
   it is spectacular and moving.
4. **Nothing static.** Something changes or moves at least every ~1.5 s; every still moves.
5. **Never crop the subject.** Faces, buildings, a road's curve, a summit stay inside the centre
   80%, clear of Instagram's top ~14% and bottom ~35% and the button column on the right.
6. **Loop.** The last shot cuts cleanly back to the first: matching motion, colour or composition.
7. **Clean pictures.** Night and astro clips are denoised before export (the platform's encoder
   turns noise into blocks); export 1080x1920, H.264 ~16-20 Mb/s, 30 fps (60 only for real motion).

Craft:

- Mostly hard cuts on the beat. Effect transitions only on motion: a whip pan, a match cut or a
  zoom-through between places, a 360 spin, a morph between two similar frames. Never gimmicky.
- Speed ramps are gentle (100% -> 35-50% over a long ramp), often hiding a cut with a whip or a
  match cut. A ramp to 10% reads as a glitch.
- Vary scale: wide, medium, detail. Never two near-identical shots in a row.
- One event or one trip per reel. Mixing cameras is fine when the colour matches.

Formats that suit this library, best first:

1. **The shot vs. the scene**: a 360 or action-cam clip of the photographer setting up, cut on a
   beat to the finished photo taken at the same spot within ~10 minutes.
2. **Before -> after**: the camera JPEG wiped to the finished edit, 7-12 s, optionally with a small
   settings tag. Earns saves.
3. **Day to night**: one place from golden hour through blue hour to the Milky Way, timelapses and
   moving stills. A replay format, medium length.
4. **Road-trip hyperlapse recap**: driving footage sped up with ramps into the stops; long, plus a
   15 s cut-down.
5. **City at night**: practical lights and motion, people as silhouettes, on the beat.
6. **360 signature moves**: a tiny planet as opener or closer, spins and orbits between places.
7. **Single-place awe loop**: 7-10 s, 2-4 shots, a seamless loop.

For the strongest short of an event make two hook variants (different first shot and angle) for
Instagram's trial reels.

## Carousels

- 4:5 (1080x1350). 5-10 slides for an event story; up to 20 only as a photo dump.
- Slide one opens a loop: a striking frame with a reason to swipe, or the "before" of a
  before/after. Close on the best frame or a strong detail.
- Per event: one story carousel and, when edits allow, one before/after carousel.
- No borders except, rarely, a frame that cannot be cropped to 4:5 shown whole on a blurred copy.

## Captions

- First line: specific and keyword-rich (place, what it is, when). Instagram search reads captions.
- Then one or two lines of story or a tip, plain words.
- Then 3-5 relevant hashtags (#landscapephotography, #sonyalpha, #milkywayphotography...): no broad
  or unrelated tags, no emoji strings, never names of people.
- Covers: the subject inside the centre 3:4, which is what the profile grid shows.

## Critique checklist

A reel ships only if every check passes:

- the first second moves and earns a pause; the hook is the best the material allows;
- every shot is sharp and well exposed; nothing is static for more than ~1.5 s;
- no two adjacent shots are redundant; the subject is never cropped or under the interface;
- pacing fits the length (beat-cut short, arc for medium, acts and re-hooks for long), with one
  place to breathe;
- transitions are motivated, speed changes are clean;
- it is never silent, has no borders beyond the one allowed fit, and loops back to the start.

Otherwise revise: swap the weakest shot, trim long holds, find a moving hook, reorder for the loop.
Judge against the best the candidates allow, not an ideal they don't contain, and stay consistent
with earlier rounds.
