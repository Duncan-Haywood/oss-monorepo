Common questions

Which is better, the simplified or the regular home office deduction?

The simplified method is $5 per square foot up to 300 square feet, so at most $1,500, with no depreciation and no records of actual expenses. The regular method deducts the business-use share of actual expenses plus depreciation if you own, and has no size cap. It usually wins for larger offices, higher housing costs, or renters with high rent; the simplified method wins for small offices and low costs. This calculator computes both and compares their tax value.

What is the gross income limit on the home office deduction?

The deduction cannot exceed the gross income from the business less your other business expenses; it cannot create or increase a loss. Under the regular method the business share of mortgage interest and real estate taxes is allowed first, then operating expenses, then depreciation, and anything above the limit carries forward. Under the simplified method the excess is lost.

Does the regular method create a tax bill when I sell my home?

It can. Depreciation taken, or allowable, on the office space reduces your basis, and that part of the gain is taxed as unrecaptured Section 1250 gain at up to 25% when you sell, even if you never claimed the depreciation. The simplified method has no depreciation, so it avoids this.

Can employees deduct a home office?

No. The IRS says an employee cannot deduct expenses for the business use of a home on a personal return. The deduction is for self-employed filers, such as sole proprietors and single-member LLCs filing Schedule C.
