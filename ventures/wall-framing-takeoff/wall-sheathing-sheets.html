How many sheets of OSB for exterior walls: counted panel by panel, not area / 32

The usual way to order wall sheathing is the wall area (perimeter times wall height) divided by 32 square feet for a 4x8 sheet of OSB or plywood, plus 10%, and some people take the windows and doors off first. Sheets are hung by the stud, though: every joint lands on a stud, the openings are cut out after the sheet is up, and the wall's height decides whether one sheet reaches the top. This page counts the sheets that way on four houses at three wall heights and sets the count beside both rules, with wall-sheathing-bench.mjs in the tool's repository, which runs the same engine as the wall sheathing calculator (measured 2026-11-28).

Four generated rectangles, 40 x 24, 48 x 28, 60 x 32 and 70 x 36 ft, framed with 2x4 studs at 16 in on centre, one bottom plate and a double top plate. Each has one 3 x 4 ft window per 12 ft of perimeter (11, 13, 15 and 18 windows) and two 3 x 6 ft 10-1/2 in doors, spread evenly round the walls; the openings are 173 to 257 sq ft, about a sixth of the wall. With precut studs an 8 ft wall stands 97-1/8 in, a 9 ft wall 109-1/8 in and a 10 ft wall 121-1/8 in.

What the counts show

  • Standing up, count the perimeter, not the area. The four houses took 32, 39, 47 and 55 sheets at every wall height: the perimeter divided by 4 ft, rounded up, plus 0 to 2 where a wall's length does not land on a stud every 48 in. A taller wall changes the sheet length (4x9, 4x10, 4x12), not how many.
  • Do not take the openings off. Wall area less openings, plus 10%, was short on all 32 cases: by 2 to 11 sheets standing up and 6 to 19 laid flat. A window cut out of a sheet leaves a piece too small to sheathe anything else on these walls.
  • The gross area rule is close only by luck. With 4x8s standing up on 96 in of wall it bought 3 to 4 sheets too many; with 4x12s on 10 ft walls it was 2 to 5 short, because a 48 sq ft sheet covers only 4 ft of wall however tall it is.
  • Laid flat, the top course decides. Two full courses on 96 in of wall: the rule was 0 to 3 sheets over. A 13-1/8 in top course on 9 ft walls: within 1. A 12 in third course just to cover the last 1-1/8 in of an 8 ft wall: 5 to 7 short. A 25-1/8 in top course on 10 ft walls, too tall to take two from one sheet: 7 to 10 short.

What this does not show

Four generated rectangular houses with evenly spaced openings, not real plans or a builder's order. No gable ends, rim boards, bump-outs, garage door headers or corner overhangs (the calculator has options for the last two). Large glazing could leave a cut-out big enough to reuse; on these walls none was. The count does not add sheets for damage or culls, and does not check sheathing thickness, span rating, nailing, blocking or whether the walls are braced or shear walls, which come from your plans, the sheathing maker and your building department.

Common questions

How many sheets of OSB do I need for my exterior walls?

With vertical sheets one sheet tall, start from the perimeter, not the area: the perimeter in feet divided by 4, rounded up, plus a sheet or two where wall lengths do not land on a stud every 4 ft. On four generated houses that count was 32, 39, 47 and 55 sheets, the same at 8, 9 and 10 ft walls; only the sheet length changes with the wall height. Do not subtract the windows and doors.

Should I subtract windows and doors when ordering wall sheathing?

No. Sheets go on over the openings and the openings are cut out afterwards, and a 3 x 4 ft cut-out is too small and the wrong shape to sheathe anything else on a plain wall. Wall area less openings, divided by the sheet area, plus 10% was 2 to 11 sheets short of the counted layout on all 16 vertical cases and 6 to 19 short on all 16 horizontal ones.

Do 4x8 sheets cover an 8 ft wall?

Not from the bottom of the plate to the top. An 8 ft wall framed with 92-5/8 in precut studs, one bottom plate and a double top plate is 97-1/8 in tall, so a 96 in sheet leaves 1-1/8 in of plate bare. Covering it all takes 4x9 sheets standing up (which can also lap the rim board below) or a third course of 4x8s laid flat; in the flat case that took 5 to 7 sheets more than area / 32 + 10% on these houses.

Is 10% enough waste for wall sheathing?

Only in some layouts. Counted waste, the share of the sheets bought that is not left on the wall, was 16.8 to 18.4% for 4x8s standing up on 96 in of wall and 22.6 to 28.7% for taller sheets standing up, mostly because the window and door cut-outs are about a sixth of the wall. The area rule still came close with full-height sheets because it does not subtract the openings; with 4x12s on a 10 ft wall it was 2 to 5 sheets short.

Should wall sheathing run vertical or horizontal?

That is a question for your shear-wall design and your building department, not the order. For the count: at 96 in of cover, 4x8s laid flat in two courses took 1 to 4 sheets more than the same sheets standing up, and laid flat on 10 ft walls, whose third course is 25-1/8 in tall, too tall to take two from one sheet, they took 7 to 10 sheets more than the area rule.
