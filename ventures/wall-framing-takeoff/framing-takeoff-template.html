Framing takeoff template (Excel): a wall schedule and lumber order

A free spreadsheet for a wall framing takeoff. Type one row per wall from the floor plan; its formulas give the studs, kings, jacks, cripples, sills, backing, header and plate footage, and a lumber order under the walls turns those into boards with your yard's lengths, a cull allowance and your prices. No macros, no sign-up; it opens in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice.

The example is Open House Plans 1, the generated CC0 house worked on how to do a framing takeoff: 14 walls, 220 ft of wall, 8 doors and 10 windows.

What is in the sheet

  • Settings: default stud spacing (16, 19.2 or 24 in; a wall can override it), plate and header board lengths, a cull and mistake allowance, and how many cripples you cut from one stud.
  • Wall schedule (40 rows): wall name, length, exterior or not, doors and windows with their total widths, and the corners and tees backed on that wall.
  • Per wall, by formula: layout studs (one every spacing from the start, plus the end stud, less the ones that fall in openings), two kings and two jacks per opening, cripples above every opening and below every window, rough sills, two backing studs per corner or tee, header footage at two plies, sill, bottom plate (less door widths) and double top plate footage, and a check column that flags openings longer than the wall.
  • Lumber order: precut studs for full-height members, jacks and cripples; plates and sills as boards of your length, with exterior bottom plates on their own treated line; header stock; boards with your allowance; unit price and amount; and the one-stud-per-foot figure beside the sheet's own count.

How close a spreadsheet gets: one house, counted both ways

A spreadsheet works wall by wall with rules of thumb; it cannot see where each stud lands beside an opening, or that an end stud can serve as a king. To show what that costs, the example sheet's formulas were run (in LibreOffice, and in the same arithmetic in framing-takeoff-template.mjs) against the free tool's stud-by-stud layout of the same house at 16 inches on center, its pieces packed into the board lengths a yard sells (measured 2026-11-28).

The template buys 2,966 lineal ft before any allowance; the counted order buys 2,679 lineal ft, so the spreadsheet orders 10.7% more. Plate footage agrees (515 ft of 2x4 and 114 ft of treated plate both ways). The gap is the studs: 13 extra layout studs, 25 too few cripples, and jacks and cripples bought as whole precut studs where the layout cuts them from the offcuts of longer boards. At 24 inches on center the template buys 2,541 lineal ft against 2,356 lineal ft counted, 7.9% more.

One house, generated, not a builder's real order; another plan will land elsewhere, high or low. The point is the direction of the error you cannot see from inside a spreadsheet.

Using it

  1. Go round the plan and give each wall one row. Measure exterior walls to the outside of the framing and decide which walls run through at the corners so no length is counted twice.
  2. Put each wall's doors and windows in its row: the count and the total rough-opening width in feet.
  3. Count corners and tees once each, on the wall that gets the backing.
  4. Set your board lengths and allowance, type your yard's prices, and check the flagged rows.
  5. Size every header from your span table or engineer: the sheet carries footage only.

Questions

Is there a free framing takeoff template in Excel?

Yes. This page's .xlsx has a wall schedule with 40 rows (length, exterior or not, stud spacing, doors, windows and their widths, corners and tees) whose formulas give layout studs, kings, jacks, cripples, rough sills, backing studs and header, sill and plate footage, and a lumber order under it with your board lengths, a cull allowance and unit prices. It opens in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice, with no macros.

How accurate is a spreadsheet framing takeoff?

On one generated 34 by 26 ft house with 220 ft of wall, the template's rules bought 2,966 lineal ft of lumber before any allowance where a stud-by-stud layout packed into board lengths bought 2,679 lineal ft: 10.7% more. Layout studs came out 13 high and cripples 25 low. A spreadsheet works per wall and cannot see where each stud lands next to an opening, corner or tee.

What does a framing takeoff spreadsheet leave out?

Header sizes (it carries header footage, two plies per opening, sized from your span table), floor and roof framing, sheathing, blocking, fireblocking and nails. It also buys jacks and cripples as precut studs and plates as whole boards, rather than cutting short pieces from offcuts.
