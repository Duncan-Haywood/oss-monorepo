Paver patio calculator

Type each rectangle of paving in feet (add a planter or slab as a subtracted rectangle) and your paver size, and you get pavers with waste, excavation, compacted base in cubic yards and tons, bedding sand, geotextile fabric, edge restraint, joint sand bags and a cost if you type prices. Every default (base depth, joint, compaction, waste, bag sizes) is a figure to replace with your own. Nothing leaves your browser.

How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Pavers. Net area (rectangles less subtracted ones) divided by the paver plus joint, plus waste, rounded up.

  2. Base, sand and fabric. The dig is base plus sand plus paver deep and wider than the paving by your overhang; base is bought loose, so its volume is multiplied by your compaction factor and converted to tons at your density.
    3. 
    
  3. Edge and joints. Edge restraint follows the perimeter (or the feet you type); joint sand fills the gap volume at your density, in whole bags.

Questions

  
How many pavers and how much base gravel for a 16 by 12 ft patio with a 4 by 4 ft planter?

  
Paving 192 sq ft less a 16 sq ft planter leaves 176 sq ft. With 8 by 4 in pavers and a 1/8 in joint that is 756.18 pavers, 832 with 10% waste (4.73 per sq ft). Dug 7-3/8 in deep (4 in base, 1 in sand, 2-3/8 in paver) and 6 in wider than the paving on each side, the base is 3.16 cu yd loose after a 1.25 compaction factor, about 4.43 tons at 1.4 tons per cu yd. The sizes, depths and factors are the ones you type; the calculator only adds them.

  
How much edge restraint and joint sand does that patio need?

  
Typing 56 ft of edge restraint, the two sides that are not against the house and the planter, takes 6 pieces of 10 ft and 56 spikes at 12 in. The joint sand is the gap volume, about 158 lb at 100 lb per cu ft, so 4 bags of 50 lb. A 4 in base is a typed default; your soil, frost and traffic may call for more.
