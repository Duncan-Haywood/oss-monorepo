Electrical rough-in calculator

Type your walls (length in inches, doors and windows with offset and width) and the box counts follow the habitable-room wall-space rule: a space of 24 in or more between doors, no point more than 6 ft from a receptacle. You get receptacle boxes, switch boxes (one per door), a cable estimate in rolls, and a cost if you type prices. The same electrical engine as the whole-house takeoff. Kitchens, baths, garages, closets, the panel and local code amendments are not counted. Nothing leaves your browser.

How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Wall spaces. Doors break a wall into spaces; windows do not. A space under the minimum (24 in) gets none.
    2. 
    
  2. Receptacles. ceil(space / 144 in) per space, placed evenly, on each room-facing face: one for an exterior wall, two for an interior wall ("sheathed": false).
    3. 
    
  3. Switches and cable. One switch box per door; cable is boxes times your feet per box, plus waste, in whole rolls.

Questions

  
How many receptacle and switch boxes does a front wall and a hall need?

  
A 20 ft front wall with a door and a window has two wall spaces, 100 in and 104 in, because a door breaks a space and a window does not. Each is under 144 in, so each gets one receptacle: 2. A 12 ft interior hall wall with one door has two spaces, 48 in and 64 in, and an interior wall has two faces, so it gets 4 receptacles. With one switch box per door the total is 6 receptacles and 2 switches: 8 boxes.

  
How much cable and what cost does that come to?

  
Eight boxes at 12 ft of cable per box is 96 ft, or 105.6 ft with 10% waste, which is 1 roll of 250 ft. With the typed prices (2.50 per receptacle, 3.00 per switch, 1.50 per box and 90 per roll) that is 15 + 6 + 12 + 90 = 123.00. The feet per box and the prices are yours to replace with your electrician's.
