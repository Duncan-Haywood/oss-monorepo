How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Courses. Each wall is hung in horizontal 48 in courses; a door or window that crosses a course is cut out of it.
    2. 
    
  2. Packing. Pieces of the same course width are packed into sheets longest first, so a short piece reuses the offcut of a long one. Ripped strips are not reused.
    3. 
    
  3. Finishing. Tape from the measured seams and angles; compound at 0.01 gal per sq ft plus 10% waste; screws at 1 per sq ft plus 10%.

Questions

  
How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 12 x 12 room?

  
For a 12 x 12 ft room with 8 ft walls, one 3 ft door and a ceiling, hung horizontally in 4 x 8 ft sheets, the calculator counts 17 sheets: 508 sq ft of faces, which is 15.9 sheets by area, plus the cuts that cannot share a sheet. Count by area alone and you buy one too few.

  
Should I hang drywall horizontally or vertically?

  
This calculator hangs walls horizontally in 48 in courses, the usual way on 8 ft walls: two courses and a single flat joint instead of a joint at every stud bay. Longer sheets (12 ft) mean fewer end joints. Your hanger may choose differently, and the count changes with it.

  
How much joint compound and tape does a room need?

  
The calculator measures tape from the seams, wall-to-ceiling angles and inside corners, and estimates compound at 0.01 gal per sq ft plus 10% waste, about 450 sq ft per 4.5 gal pail. For the 12 x 12 ft room that is 1 roll of tape and 2 pails of compound.

  
How many screws for a sheet of drywall?

  
A common rule is about 1 screw per sq ft of board, so roughly 32 screws for a 4 x 8 ft sheet. The calculator adds 10% waste and the rate is yours to change; follow your local code and the board maker's spacing.
