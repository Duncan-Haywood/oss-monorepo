Retaining wall block calculator

Type each straight wall as a length and an exposed height in feet, and your block and cap size, and you get blocks with waste (including the buried first course), cap blocks, compacted base gravel and drainage stone in cubic yards and tons, fabric, drain pipe, geogrid when you type its spacing, cap adhesive and a cost if you type prices. Every default (block size, burial, base depth, drainage width, compaction, waste) is a figure to replace with your own. Nothing is uploaded; it runs in your browser. It does not design the wall: surcharge, slopes, water, soil, corners and permits are yours to check.

How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Blocks and caps. Exposed height over block height rounds up to whole courses, plus the courses needed to bury your embedment; each course is the length over the block length rounded up; waste is added and the total rounded up. Caps are a separate unit along the top.
    2. 
    
  2. Gravel and fabric. The base trench is the block depth plus your overhang each side, bought loose, so its volume is multiplied by your compaction factor. Drainage stone fills your width behind the whole built height; fabric wraps it with a 2 ft lap. Tons come from your densities.
    3. 
    
  3. Pipe, grid and adhesive. Drain pipe follows the wall (or the feet you type); geogrid layers go every N courses at your length when you type them; adhesive is tubes at your feet per tube.

Questions

  
How many retaining wall blocks and caps for a 40 ft wall 3 ft high with a 12 ft return 2 ft high?

  
The 40 ft wall shows 6 courses of 6 in blocks and buries 1 more, so 7 courses of 30 blocks (16 in long) is 210, 221 with 5% waste; the 12 ft run is 5 courses of 9, 48 with waste. That is 269 blocks, 1.87 per sq ft of face, and 42 cap blocks. The block sizes, burial and waste are the ones you type; the calculator only adds them.

  
How much gravel, fabric and drain pipe does that wall need?

  
A 12 in block with 6 in of base each side sits on a 24 in wide, 6 in deep trench: 2.41 cu yd loose after a 1.25 compaction factor, about 3.37 tons at 1.4 tons per cu yd. The drainage zone, 12 in wide behind the full built height, is 6.30 cu yd or about 8.5 tons at 1.35. Fabric is 326 sq ft, the drain pipe runs the 52 ft of wall and 3 tubes of cap adhesive cover it. Walls over about 4 ft usually need geogrid and an engineer's design; check your jurisdiction.
