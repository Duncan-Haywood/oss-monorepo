Floor joist and subfloor calculator

Type a floor's width and depth in inches, the joist size and spacing, and whether it has a rim board, a beam and rows of blocking. You get the joist lines and their centres (laid so a 4 ft sheet edge lands on a joist at 16 or 24 in), rim boards, blocking, and the subfloor sheets course by course with end joints on joists and the offcut waste. The same floor engine as the whole-house takeoff. Joist size and spacing are your inputs; they are not checked against a span table or code, and hangers, fasteners and bearing are not decided here.

How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Joists. They run the depth between two rim boards. The first and last sit at the floor's edges and the rest are centred on multiples of the spacing. A beam position splits each joist in two so shorter boards can be used.
    2. 
    
  2. Rim and blocking. Rim boards close the four edges; each blocking row adds a block between every pair of joists.
    3. 
    
  3. Subfloor. Sheets run across the joists in courses, alternate courses start on a different joist, and every end joint lands on a joist centre. Offcut waste is the sheet area bought less the floor area.

Questions

  
How many joists and subfloor sheets does a 24 by 14 ft floor need?

  
A 24 ft by 14 ft floor (288 by 168 in) with 2x10 joists at 16 in on centre, a rim board all round and one row of blocking needs 19 joist lines, 4 rim boards and 18 blocks, plus 14 sheets of 4x8 subfloor for 336 sq ft, a 25.0% offcut because the last course is only 24 in wide and is ripped from 4 sheets.

  
What changes at 24 in on centre?

  
Type spacing 24 and the joist lines drop from 19 to 13 and the blocks from 18 to 12, while the sheets stay at 14 because they depend on the floor area, not the joists. Joist size and spacing are your inputs: this page does not check them against a span table or code.
