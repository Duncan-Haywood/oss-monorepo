How many bundles of shingles? Hip vs. gable waste, measured on 16 roofs

The usual way to order shingles is the roof's area in squares (100 sq ft), times three bundles a square, plus 10% waste for a gable or 15% for a hip. This page lays every field shingle on 16 rectangular roofs (four house sizes at four pitches, each as a gable and as a hip), counts the bundles, and checks the rule against the count. The figures come from shingle-bench.mjs in the tool's repository (measured 2026-11-28).

How the shingles were laid

A laminated shingle 39-3/8 in long and 13-1/4 in tall, laid at a 5-5/8 in exposure, 22 to a bundle: a common size and count, not any one maker's product. Courses run from the eave up to the ridge, and the top course is whole shingles cut to height under the cap. Joints fall every 39-3/8 in from a reference line that steps 6-1/2 in each course and repeats every six courses, so joints in neighbouring courses are at least 6-1/2 in apart. The reference is the left rake on a gable and the centre of each plane on a hip.

On a hip plane each course is shorter at both ends as it climbs, so its end pieces are cut on the diagonal; a piece is sized by its bottom edge, its longest. Two ways of cutting the ends are counted. "Ends from new shingles" cuts every end piece from a whole shingle and bins the offcut. "Offcuts reused" keeps every offcut at least 6 in long and fills a later end piece that fits in it, smallest fit first; a diagonal recut loses another 13-1/4 in of shingle height along the slope. On a gable that is the rake offcut starting the next course, which is what most crews do.

What the 16 roofs show

  • Gable: 5.4 to 11.4% over the surface with every end cut from a new shingle, 8.0% on average; 0.7 to 3.7% with the offcuts reused, 1.7% on average. Squares × 3 + 10% was never short, and bought 3 to 10 bundles more than the reused layout.
  • Hip: 12.0 to 21.4% with every end from a new shingle, 16.5% on average, and the 15% rule ran short on 5 of the 16, by 1 or 2 bundles, all on the two smaller houses. With the offcuts reused, 2.3 to 5.3%, 3.6% on average, and 15% bought 3 to 13 bundles too many.
  • Hip vs. gable: the hip took 0 to 2 more bundles than the gable with offcuts reused on both, and 3 to 6 more with every end from a new shingle.
  • The cutting habit matters more than the roof shape. Reusing offcuts saved 2 to 4 bundles on the gables and 5 to 9 on the hips.

So the extra 5 points a hip allowance adds is about right for a crew that bins its hip offcuts, a little short on small hip roofs, and generous for one that reuses them. The rule's three bundles a square already carry about 1.5% of slack with this bundle, since three bundles cover 101.5 sq ft.

What this does not show

Invented rectangular houses (24 x 36 to 36 x 60 ft) with a 12 in overhang all round, not a builder's real roof. No valleys, dormers, skylights, vents or chimneys, no cross wings, no starter strip, hip and ridge cap, damaged shingles or mistakes, and no prices. One shingle size and one stair-step pattern typed by us, not a shingle maker's instructions; end pieces shorter than a maker allows at a rake were not adjusted. Shingle sizes, exposure and bundle counts differ by product: read the wrapper.

Common questions

How many bundles of shingles do I need per square?

Three is the usual rule: a square is 100 sq ft of roof, and laminated shingle bundles are packed to cover about a third of a square. The shingle measured here, 39-3/8 x 13-1/4 in at a 5-5/8 in exposure with 22 to a bundle, covers 33.8 sq ft a bundle, so three bundles cover 101.5 sq ft before any cutting. Check your own wrapper: bundle counts and exposures differ by product.

What waste factor should I use for shingles on a hip roof?

On 16 rectangular test roofs at 4:12 to 10:12, laying every course and cutting each hip end from a fresh shingle needed 12.0 to 21.4% more shingle than the roof surface (16.5% on average), and the usual 15% ran short on 5 of the 16, by 1 or 2 bundles. Using the hip offcuts for later hip cuts brought it to 2.3 to 5.3% (3.6% on average), and 15% then bought 3 to 13 bundles too many.

Does a hip roof take more shingles than a gable roof?

A little. With the same pitch and overhang all round the two roofs have the same surface, and on the 16 test roofs the hip took 0 to 2 more bundles than the gable when the offcuts were reused on both, and 3 to 6 more when every end was cut from a fresh shingle. The hip and ridge cap is a separate product, and a hip roof has more of it.

Is 10% waste enough for a gable roof?

On these 16 gables it always was. Cutting each rake end from a fresh shingle needed 5.4 to 11.4% (8.0% on average), and squares x 3 x 1.10 bought 0 to 6 bundles more than that layout. With the rake offcuts used to start the next course, as most crews do, the cutting waste fell to 0.7 to 3.7% and 10% bought 3 to 10 bundles more than needed.

Can I order my shingles from these percentages?

Not as a figure to order by. They are invented rectangular houses with a 12 in overhang, one shingle size and a typed stair-step pattern, with no valleys, dormers, skylights, chimneys, starter strip, cap or damaged shingles. Measure your own roof, follow the shingle maker's instructions, and ask your supplier about returns.
