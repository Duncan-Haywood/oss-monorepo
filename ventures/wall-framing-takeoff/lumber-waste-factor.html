Framing lumber waste factor: how much to add, measured on three houses

A waste factor on a framing order covers two different things: the offcuts left when pieces are cut from boards, and the boards you lose to culls (crooked, split or wany), mistakes and theft. Only the first can be worked out from the plan, and it depends almost entirely on how the order is planned. This page measures it on three houses for four ways of buying the same walls, in lineal feet of 2x4 and 2x6 (headers left out), from waste-bench.mjs in the tool's repository (measured 2026-11-28).

What this means for the factor you add

  • Planned and cut from a full length list: cutting waste is under half a percent, so almost all of any flat factor you add is a cull and mistake allowance. A flat 10% on these orders left 9.5 to 9.8% for that.
  • The yard sends only 8 and 16 ft: 1.4 to 2.6% goes to cutting. Plan for it, then add your cull allowance on top.
  • Everything from 16 ft boards: 4.0 to 5.8% goes to cutting, but you handle 36 to 39% fewer boards than the planned full-length order, because two stud-length pieces come out of each 16.
  • No cut plan: 18.9 to 29.8% goes to cutting, so a flat 10% runs short before a single board is culled.

The takeoff tool keeps the two apart: it orders the cutting-stock solution (its cut list says which piece comes from which board) and adds the cull allowance you type, per lumber size, as spare boards on their own line.

What this does not show

Two generated plans and one invented house, with flat board prices per foot and a 1/8 in kerf, not a builder's real order or a permit set. The cull, mistake and theft share is not measured here: it depends on the lumber grade, the yard and the crew. Header sizes are your inputs and are not checked against a span table or code, and headers are left out of the figures.

Common questions

What waste factor should I add to a framing lumber order?

Split it in two. Cutting waste depends on how the order is planned: on three test houses it was 0.2 to 0.4% of the lumber used when every piece was laid out and cut from boards of 8 to 16 ft, 1.4 to 2.6% when the yard sent only 8 and 16 ft boards, 4.0 to 5.8% from 16 ft boards alone, and 18.9 to 29.8% when each piece was bought on its own board. Culls, mistakes and theft are a second allowance that depends on your yard and crew, and these houses cannot measure it.

Why is a planned order's waste so low?

Short pieces (cripples, jacks, sills, blocking, plate ends) come out of the offcuts of long boards, and precut studs need no cutting at all. A cutting-stock order packs pieces into boards so little is left over; most of what remains is saw kerf and the ends too short to use.

Is a flat 10% waste factor too much?

For cutting alone on a planned order, yes: on these houses a flat 10% on top of a planned order left about 9.5 to 9.8% of the order for culls and mistakes. With no planning, 10% did not even cover the cutting waste, which was 19 to 30%. Whether the rest is right for culls depends on your lumber grade and crew.

Can I trust these percentages for my own house?

Not as a figure to order by. They come from two generated plans and one invented house, with flat board prices and a 1/8 in kerf, not a builder's real order. They show how the way you buy changes the waste; your own walls in the takeoff give your own figure.
