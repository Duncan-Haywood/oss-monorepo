Fireblocking calculator

Type your walls (length in inches, stud size, openings) and the cavity limit your code or inspector gives, and the blocking follows: how many rows each tall wall needs, one block per stud bay cut to the bay width, packed into boards, with a cost if you type a board price. The same fireblock engine as the whole-house takeoff. The limit is the figure you type; it is not looked up. Ceiling and floor levels, soffits, stairs, chases and penetrations are not counted.


  
Do the whole house: framing, sheathing, plumbing and more in one takeoff


  
How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Rows. A wall as tall as or under the cavity limit needs none, since the plates close its top and bottom. A taller wall gets ceil(height / limit) - 1 rows, spaced evenly (or the number you type in rows).
    2. 
    
  2. Blocks. One block per row in each bay between neighbouring full-height studs, cut to the clear gap, from the wall's own stud size.
    3. 
    
  3. Boards. Blocks are packed into boards first-fit decreasing with the saw kerf, then waste (5% by default) is added and rounded up.
    4. 
  


  
Questions

  
How many fireblocking blocks and boards do three tall walls need at a 10 ft cavity limit?

  
Each wall here is 144 in studs plus two plates, 147 in tall, which is over the typed 10 ft (120 in) limit, so each gets 1 row of blocks: 13 blocks in the 20 ft great-room wall, 8 in the 10 ft entry wall and 6 in the 8 ft closet wall, 27 blocks in all. Packed into 8 ft boards with a saw kerf, the 2x4 blocks fill 4 boards (buy 5 with 5% waste) and the 2x6 blocks fill 2 (buy 3), 8 boards, 44.00 at 5.50 a board.

  
What changes if my code or inspector wants a tighter limit?

  
Type a smaller cavity limit and the rows follow: at 5 ft the same walls need 2 rows each, 54 blocks, 12 boards (8 of 2x4 and 4 of 2x6), 66.00. The limit is always the figure you type; the page does not look up a code, and ceiling and floor levels, soffits, stairs, chases and penetrations are not counted.
