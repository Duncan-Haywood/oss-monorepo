The method

  
    
    
  1. Area. Exterior wall area net of doors and windows, from the same siding layout the takeoff uses, so the two trades agree.
    2. 
    
  2. Barrier and lath. Area plus laps; two barrier layers by default (confirm with your code), lath in 18 sq ft sheets with waste, fasteners every 6 in along studs 16 in apart.
    3. 
    
  3. Plaster. Area times total coat thickness gives wet volume; rebound is added and the result divided by what one bag yields (0.6 cu ft for 80 lb by default), rounded up.
    4. 
    
  4. Trim. Weep screed along every wall base, casing bead round each opening, corner bead at each outside corner, and control joints so no panel runs past 18 ft or 144 sq ft, packed into stock lengths.

Questions

  
How many bags of stucco do I need for a 40 x 30 ft house?

  
For a 40 x 30 ft rectangle with 8 ft walls, one 3 ft door and two 3 x 4 ft windows, the wall area net of openings is 1,076 sq ft. At a typical 7/8 in over three coats that is 78.5 cu ft of wet plaster; with 15% rebound and 0.6 cu ft per 80 lb bag it is 151 bags. The coat thicknesses, yield and rebound are figures you type from your plaster maker.

  
How much lath and trim does that house need?

  
The same house takes 73 sheets of 18 sq ft lath with laps and 10% waste, 1,953 fasteners (2 boxes of 1,000), 2 rolls of barrier at two layers, 16 pieces of weep screed, 6 of casing bead, 5 of corner bead and 7 of control joint, all in 10 ft stock.

  
What is not counted?

  
Window and door flashing, floor-line expansion joints, color coat, sealer, scaffolding, labor, and double lath where soffits or ceilings need it. Thickness, lath type and barrier layers depend on your code and your plaster maker, so treat the result as an estimate to check against your supplier's own takeoff.
