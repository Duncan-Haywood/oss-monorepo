  3. Units. Ridge vent in 4 ft pieces and soffit vents rounded up, checked against half the eave length.

Questions

  
How much attic ventilation do I need for a 24 x 40 ft attic?

  
A 24 x 40 ft attic is 960 sq ft. At the code's 1 sq ft of net free area per 150 sq ft of attic that is 922 sq in (6.4 sq ft). With a ridge vent taking half, the calculator gives 25.6 ft of ridge vent (7 four-foot pieces at 18 sq in per foot) and 9 soffit vents of 56 sq in each. At 1/300, where the code allows it, the need halves to 461 sq in.

  
What is the 1/150 and 1/300 attic ventilation rule?

  
IRC R806.2 asks for net free ventilating area of at least 1/150 of the attic floor area. It allows 1/300 where a Class I or II vapor retarder is on the warm side of the ceiling, or where 40% to 50% of the vent area is high (a ridge vent) and the rest low in the eaves. Which one applies to your house is for you and your building inspector; the calculator takes the ratio as your input.

  
What does net free area mean on a vent label?

  
Net free area (NFA) is the open airflow area after the louver, screen or baffle takes its share. Ridge vents are labelled in square inches per foot and soffit vents in square inches per unit. Type the figures from the product you will buy: the defaults here (18 sq in per foot, 56 sq in per 16 in unit) are placeholders, not any maker's numbers.

  
Why split the vents between ridge and soffit?

  
Air enters low and leaves high, so the roof moves air by temperature even with no wind. The calculator puts half the area at the ridge by default, and if the ridge is too short to supply that share it takes what fits and moves the rest to the eaves. With no ridge vent, all the area is low, which moves air only when wind pushes it.
