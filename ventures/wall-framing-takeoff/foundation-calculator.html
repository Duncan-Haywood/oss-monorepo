Foundation calculator: footing, stem wall and slab

Type the outside width and depth of a foundation in inches, the footing and stem wall sizes, the slab thickness and the rebar you plan, and whether it is a stem-wall foundation or a monolithic slab. You get concrete by pour in cubic yards, the ready-mix order with trucks and a short-load warning, rebar by bar and lap, form contact area and plywood, gravel under the slab and vapor barrier. The same foundation engine as the whole-house takeoff. Every size and bar schedule is your input; nothing here checks frost depth, soil bearing or reinforcement against a code or an engineer, and it does not design a foundation.

How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Concrete. Each pour is a closed-form volume: footing width times depth around the centreline, stem wall thickness times height, slab area times thickness, and any interior footing runs. Waste (5% by default) is added to the order, which is rounded up to whole trucks, and a last load under about 3 yd is flagged.
    2. 
    
  2. Rebar. Footing and stem bars run continuous around the perimeter, stem verticals at the spacing you type, slab mats both ways. Splices lap 40 bar diameters in 20 ft stock, and the weight is length times the standard pounds per foot.
    3. 
    
  3. Forms and base. Stem wall faces and any formed footing sides give contact area and 4x8 plywood sheets with 10% spare; gravel and a vapor barrier with laps go under the slab.

Questions

  
How much concrete and rebar does a 40 by 28 ft foundation need?

  
A 40 ft by 28 ft footing, stem wall and slab foundation (20 by 10 in footings, an 8 in stem wall 32 in high, a 4 in slab on 4 in of gravel, plus one interior footing run) takes 29.18 cu yd of concrete before waste: 6.65 in the footing, 8.52 in the stem wall, 11.94 in the slab and 2.07 in the interior footing. With 5% waste the order is 30.64 cu yd, or 4 trucks of 10 yd where the last carries only 0.64 yd, with about 1099 lb of rebar.

  
What happens if the footing is 24 by 12 in instead of 20 by 10?

  
The order rises from 30.64 to 33.24 cu yd (still 4 trucks, but the last now carries 3.24 yd) while the rebar stays about the same, 1099 to 1096 lb, because bars are counted by run and not by footing size. Footing size, frost depth and bar schedules are your inputs: this page does not check them against a code or an engineer's design.
