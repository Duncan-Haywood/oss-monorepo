How to do a framing takeoff from plans: one house, worked step by step

A framing takeoff turns a floor plan into a list of every stud, plate, header and cripple, then into the boards you order. This page works one house through the six steps a framer or estimator follows, with every count computed by the tool's layout engine from takeoff-example.mjs in its repository (measured 2026-11-28), so you can check your own method against it.

The house is Open House Plans 1, a generated single-storey plan released CC0: four exterior walls in a 34 by 26 ft rectangle, ten interior walls, 8 doors and 10 windows. Walls are 2x4 with 92-5/8 in precut studs, a treated bottom plate on the exterior walls, 2x10 headers (the plan's default, not checked against a span table) and studs at 16 inches on center.

Run the takeoff on your own plan

1. Make the wall schedule

Go round the floor plan and list each wall once, with its length, stud size, and the doors and windows in it. Measure exterior walls to the outside of the framing and decide which walls run through at the corners, so no length is counted twice.

The same pass counts the joints: 4 exterior corners and 12 places an interior wall tees into another wall.

2. Take off the plates

Each wall has a bottom plate and a double top plate. Bottom plates on a slab or in contact with concrete are usually pressure treated, and are cut out at door openings. The top plates lap at corners and tees and are spliced over studs, so the pieces are a little longer than three times the wall length less the doors.

PlateLineal ft
Treated 2x4 bottom plate (exterior walls)114
2x4 top plates, cap plates and interior bottom plates515

That is 70 plate pieces in all.

3. Lay out the studs

On each wall, put a stud at both ends and one on every 16 inch mark from the start end, so sheathing breaks on a stud. Studs that land inside a door or window come out. This house gets 130 layout and end studs at 16 inches on center (91 at 24). The stud count page shows the rule on a single 12 ft wall.

4. Frame every door and window

Each opening gets a full-height king stud and a jack stud on each side, a header across the jacks, and short cripples on the layout above the header. Windows also get a rough sill with cripples under it. Over the house's 18 openings:

MemberPieces
King studs35
Jack studs36
Header plies (2x10, two per opening)36
Cripples (above headers and under sills)87
Rough sills10

There are 35 kings, not 36, because one door sits so close to a wall end that the end stud serves as its king. Three doors are wider than 4 ft and are framed here with one jack a side; wide openings usually need more, so set jacks per opening from your span table or engineer.

5. Add corners and tees

Where walls meet, add backing so both walls have nailing for drywall and the walls tie together. With two backing studs at each corner wall end and two at each tee, this house adds 8 corner and 24 tee backing studs. Framers differ here (three-stud corners, ladder blocking, California corners), so use what your crew builds.

6. Turn the pieces into a board order

The house now has 436 pieces, 197 of them full-height members (layout studs, kings and backing). Most full-height members come from precut studs (190 here); jacks, cripples, sills, headers and plates are cut from longer boards, short pieces from the offcuts of long ones. Then add your own allowance for culls and mistakes, which a plan cannot measure (how big the cutting share is for each way of buying).

Checks before you send it

  • Against one stud per foot: the rule says 220 studs; the layout has 197 full-height members plus 36 jacks and 87 cripples. If your count is far from the layout, look for missed openings or tees (the rule against a counted order on three houses).
  • Headers and jacks: every header size and jack count is an input. Check each against your span table, plans or engineer.
  • What this leaves out: sheathing, blocking, fireblocking, nails, floor and roof framing are separate takeoffs (the sheathing, fireblocking and nail calculators use the same walls).

Common questions

How do you do a framing takeoff from plans?

Work the plan set in order: list every wall with its length and stud size, take the plates off as lineal feet (one bottom, two top), lay the studs out at the spacing from one end, add a king and jack on each side of every door and window with its header, sill and cripples, add backing studs at every corner and tee, then pack all the pieces into the board lengths your yard sells and add your cull allowance.

How many pieces are in a framing takeoff for a small house?

On Open House Plans 1, a generated 34 by 26 ft single-storey plan with 220 ft of wall, 8 doors and 10 windows, the walls came to 436 pieces at 16 inches on center: 130 layout studs, 35 kings, 36 jacks, 36 header plies, 87 cripples, 10 rough sills, 32 corner and tee backing studs and 70 plate pieces, bought as 276 boards.

Is one stud per foot close enough for a takeoff?

Not on this house. One stud per foot says 220; the layout has 197 full-height members (layout studs, kings and corner and tee backing) plus 36 jacks and 87 cripples cut from other boards. The rule can be over or under on a given house, which is why a takeoff counts by layout.

How much lumber does 24 inch spacing save?

On this house, 24 inches on center cut the layout studs from 130 to 91 and the cripples from 87 to 64, and the 2x4 and header lumber bought from about 2,679 to 2,356 lineal feet, 12% less. Use it only where your design and code allow that spacing.
