Gutter calculator

Type the house and the overhangs. The framing engine lays out the roof, finds the eave runs gutters hang on, packs them into stock lengths and counts downspouts, hangers, end caps, elbows, straps and mitred corners. Nothing leaves your browser.


  
Add gutters to a whole-house framing takeoff


  
The method

  
    
    
  1. Eave runs. Gutters hang on eaves only: the two long sides of a gable roof (rakes shed onto the ground), all four sides of a hip. Each run is the wall length plus the overhang at both ends.
    2. 
    
  2. Sections. Runs are packed into your stock lengths, first fit decreasing, then your waste is added.
    3. 
    
  3. Downspouts. Per run, the larger of one per 40 ft of gutter and one per 600 sq ft of roof plan area drained; each takes two elbows and straps every 6 ft of drop. Change the 40 to match your supplier's guidance.
    4. 
    
  4. Fittings. Hangers every spacing along each run, two end caps per run, and four outside mitres on a hip roof.

Questions

  
How much gutter do I need for a 24 x 40 ft gable roof?

  
For a 24 ft wide, 40 ft long house with 12 in overhangs, a gable roof drains to its two long eaves. Each eave run is 42 ft (40 ft plus the 12 in rake overhang at each end), so 84 ft of gutter, which is 10 sections of 10 ft once 10% waste is added.

  
How many downspouts does a 24 x 40 ft house need?

  
The calculator takes the larger of two rules of thumb for each eave run: one downspout per 40 ft of gutter, and one per 600 sq ft of roof plan area drained. A 24 x 40 ft gable roof has 1092 sq ft of plan area and two 42 ft runs, so it gets 4 downspouts, 2 per run, and with a 10 ft drop that is 4 downspout pieces, 8 elbows and 8 straps. These are sizing habits, not a drainage design.

  
How many gutter hangers do I need?

  
Hangers go every 24 in along each run by default, plus one at the end: 22 on each 42 ft run, 44 in all for this house. Close the spacing to 16 in or less where snow or ice load is heavy.

  
What is not counted?

  
Slope, splash blocks, leaf guards, sealant, screws, and a cross wing of a different pitch. Local rainfall and the manufacturer's tables decide the real downspout count. Check the result against your plans and your supplier.
