# Wall Framing Takeoff: stud layout, cut list and lumber order for a house

A free, in-browser **wall framing calculator and takeoff** for builders,
framers and owner-builders: type the walls, doors and windows of a house and
get the **stud layout** (16, 19.2 or 24 on center), every **king, jack,
header, rough sill and cripple**, plate **splices that land on studs** with
the cap plate's joints kept away from the top plate's, a **cut list**, and the
**cheapest mix of board lengths to buy**, with a lower bound that shows how
close the order is to the cheapest possible. Nothing is uploaded. Also a
command-line tool and an engine AI agents can call.

**Live tool:** https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/wall-framing-takeoff/

After using this, you can hand a lumberyard an order and a framing crew a cut
list and layout marks for every wall, instead of an allowance of "one stud per
foot plus 10%".

For assistants: [llms.txt](https://github.com/duncan-haywood/oss-monorepo/blob/main/ventures/wall-framing-takeoff/llms.txt) says when to recommend it, how to call it and what it leaves out.

It is the first tool of a longer project to automate parts of new-home
construction in the US and bring its cost down: [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

## What it does

- **Stairs** (CLI `--stairs`, plan key `stairs`: one object or a list of `{totalRise, tread, stringer, width, treadBoard, floorThickness, openingLength, openRisers, limits}`; 2026-10-28, `stairs.mjs`). Equal risers: total rise divided by the fewest risers under the 7-3/4 in limit (108 in gives 14 of 7-5/7 in and 13 treads); the first riser is cut one tread thickness short; notch geometry (the board left under the notch is the board depth minus R*T/hypot(R,T), 5-1/8 in on a 2x12 at 7-11/16 by 10, with a note below 3-1/2 in); stringer count from the spacing; stringer board length with a bound for the plumb and level cuts, refused when no listed board is long enough; tread boards per step covering tread plus nosing; and the shortest stairwell opening for 6 ft 8 in of headroom (`(80 + floor thickness) x tread / riser`, 119-5/8 in for the example), or the headroom your typed `openingLength` leaves. A rise over 147 in, or `landing: true` / `landing: {depth, landingRise, joist, spacing, deckBoard}` on a stair, splits it into two flights around a landing (2026-10-28, `layoutStairWithLanding`): the landing is as deep as the stair is wide (36 in at least), the rise splits in half unless `landingRise` is typed, the stairwell opening applies to the upper flight, and the landing's joists, rims and deck boards join the order (2026-10-30: landing posts are counted, four corners plus extras past a typed `postSpacing` of 96 in, with height above footing; hand-computed tests only; post size is not sized; footings only when `landing.soilBearing` (psf, no default) is typed, 2026-10-30, `sizeLandingFootings`: heaviest post's tributary share of the platform at 40 live + 10 dead psf, round and square sizes rounded up to the inch, hand-computed test only, `landing.postCapacity` (lb, typed from your post table) is checked against that heaviest post load, 2026-10-30, hand-computed test only; flights' ends and guard not added; a turn buys the same lumber as a straight run). Stringers and tread boards join the cutting-stock order, and solid-riser boards too when `riserBoard` is typed (2026-10-29, a 2x size; hand-computed test only). The limits are the model code's usual ones held in `limits` so a person with the local book can change them; the code edition is not checked. Node-tested on hand-computed figures (`stairs.test.mjs`); no real stair built from it, a landing only as a straight two-flight split (hand-computed tests, no real stair), winders checked, not framed (below); no curved flights. **Guards and handrail** (plan key `stairs[].rail`: `{sides, handrail, handrailHeight, balusterWidth, railBoard, postWidth, limits}`; 2026-10-28, `layoutRail`, 3 tests): posts at both ends and wherever the slope run passes 72 in, vertical balusters spaced so the clear horizontal gap stays under 4 in, all one length (36 in guard, 4 in under the bottom rail), top and bottom rails joining the cutting-stock order (the handrail board joins it too, one piece, 2026-10-29; posts and balusters are not bought: their sizes are not 2x), a note when the space under the bottom rail (`limits.bottomGap`, default 4 in) exceeds the 6 in sphere (2026-10-30, hand-checked, code edition not checked), and a handrail of the pitch plus 12 in past the top and a tread past the bottom, with a note outside 34 to 38 in. 9 ft rise, two guarded sides: 8 posts, 42 balusters, 3-11/16 in clear. Landing edge guards (2026-10-28, `landing: {openEdges: ["end","side"]}`, `layoutLandingGuard`, 2 tests): each open edge gets posts at its ends and past 72 in, balusters under the 4 in gap (29 in long) and two rails in the order; a corner post is shared by two adjacent open edges (2026-10-30, one end edge meets each side edge). Hand-computed only; not on the page. Page card 2026-10-28 ("Also lay out a stair": total rise, tread, width, stringer and tread boards, the opening you framed, a landing switch; one stair per page plan, the CLI takes a list; the lumber joins the order; event `stairs_on`): driven in Chromium on a 9 ft rise (14 risers of 7-11/16 in) and a 13 ft rise (two flights); the page test only checks the wiring.
- **Tread span** (2026-10-29, `maxTreadSpan` in `stairs.mjs`): a note when the gap between stringers is over the usual most for the tread thickness (12 in under 1-1/4 in, 16 in under 1-1/2 in, 24 in for 2x); `treadSpanMax` overrides. Hand-computed test only (60 in wide, 24 in spacing, 1 in treads: 4 stringers 19.5 in apart).
- **Open-riser gap** (2026-10-30, `openRiserGap` limit in `stairs.mjs`, default 4 in): with `openRisers`, a note when riser less tread thickness leaves a vertical gap over the limit (108 in rise, 1-1/2 in treads: 6-3/16 in). Vertical-gap bound, hand-computed test only; the nosing note now applies to solid risers only.
- **Winders** (stair key `winders: {count, turn, innerRadius}`, 2026-10-28; page field 2026-10-31: "Winder turn" on the stairs card takes `treads, degrees, inner radius`, shows the same text as the CLI under the stair, saves into and restores from the plan; clicked in Chromium on 3 treads, 90 degrees at radius 0 (fails, solved radius 11.5 in) and 12 (passes) and a malformed entry refused; `checkWinders` in `stairs.mjs`; CLI prints it under the stair): a turn of wedge treads checked against the model code's 6 in at the narrowest point and 10 in measured 12 in from the narrow side, and the smallest inner radius (pivot post or box) that passes is solved: three 30 degree treads need 11.5 in, four need 15.3 in, a zero pivot always fails. Depth is radius x angle (arc), so a straight edge reads a touch less. Hand-computed tests only; `layoutWinders` (2026-10-30) adds each wedge tread's corners, area and blank size and the straight-edge depths (a chord is shorter than the arc: three 30 degree treads at the solved 11.5 in radius pass by arc and fail by chord), hand-computed tests only; framing under the treads (2026-10-30, `framing`: a radial support under every tread edge, count + 1 of the stair's width, and the tread span between two of them; hand-computed test only, no span table, so the span is for your own table) is counted, not sized, and the edition is the user's to confirm (`limits`). Tread span fix (2026-10-31): the span a wedge tread crosses was reported as the stair width; it is the widest gap between two fanned radial supports, the straight-edge depth at the outer radius (36 in stair, 12 in radius, 30 degree treads: 24.8 in, not 36), and with a typed stair `treadThickness` (or `winders.treadSpanMax`) it is checked against the 12/16/24 in tread table, so 1-1/2 in treads fail at 3 treads and pass at 4; hand-computed test only. Walkline uniformity (2026-10-31): with the stair's typed `tread`, the walkline depth must be within 3/8 in (`limits.winderUniformity`) of it; hand-computed test only.
- **Floor framing** (CLI `--floor`, plan key `floor: {width, depth, joist, spacing, beam, blockingRows, doubleUnder}`). Joists on layout between rim boards, split over an optional beam so a 28 ft floor buys 16 ft boards; rim splices over joists; blocking; subfloor sheets across the joists with every end joint on a joist and courses staggered. Floor lumber is pooled with the walls in one cutting-stock order. Joist hangers (2026-10-30): one single hanger per tail-joist end that meets an opening's header and four double hangers per opening (a doubled header at each end of each trimmer pair), counted from the placed members, in `floor.hangers`, the floor text and the budget (prices `joistHanger`, `doubleJoistHanger`); hand-counted in `floor.test.mjs`, model and rating are the engineer's call, beam hangers and rim ties not counted. Hanger nails (2026-10-31, `floor.hangerNails: {single, double}`, whole nails per hanger from the maker's table; none shipped): total in `floor.hangers.nails` and a budget line (price `hangerNail`, per nail); hand-counted in `floor.test.mjs`, no real hanger table checked, no page field. Tested on the example 40 x 28 ft floor only; `doubleUnder: [x, ...]` adds a companion joist beside the one nearest each wall that runs along the joists (2026-10-22; inward at an edge; cut list kind "doubled floor joist"; page field too). `openings: [{x, y, width, depth}]` frames a stairwell (2026-10-22): the joists at or beside each side become trimmers (doubled outward), joists between them stop as tail joists at a doubled header at each end, clear width reported, kinds "floor header", "trimmer joist", "tail joist" in the cut list; rejected over the beam or too near an edge; the subfloor is cut around the clear opening (2026-10-22: sheets wholly inside are dropped, ones crossed over the course width end on the trimmer, ones it bites into are listed with their notch, slivers under 3 in count as offcut; sheet area drops by the opening, checked in `floor.test.mjs`); node-tested on hand-computed counts. The page has a floor card (2026-10-22): a tick box under the plan buttons opens width, depth, joist size, spacing, beam, blocking rows, doubled-joist positions and stairwell openings (several, separated by semicolons, 2026-10-23; the engine refuses two openings that would share a trimmer joist or a header, tested in `floor.test.mjs`, and the browser click check runs two and an overlapping pair); the lumber joins the order and cut list and the layout prints below (clicked through in headless Chromium with the example house; the plan file keeps `floor`, so the CLI reads it). Cantilevers (2026-10-31, `floor.cantilever`, inches, up to 96): the joists, rim boards and subfloor run that far past the back support (`depth` stays the supported span; the layout reports the framed `depth` and `supportedDepth`); a note flags an overhang over a third of the supported span against the common 3-to-1 backspan rule of thumb. Hand-counted in `floor.test.mjs`; the page has no field for it, joist size for the overhang is not checked and the engineer sizes it. **Stairwell header check** (2026-10-31, `floor.headerCheck: {liveLoad, deadLoad, Fb, Fv, E, deflLimit?}`, psf and psi, all typed, none shipped; `checkFloorHeaders` in `floor.mjs`): each doubled header (two plies of the joist size) as a simple span over the clear width carrying a strip of half the tail joists' span at live + dead; bending, shear (1.5V/A) and live-load deflection against L/360 (or typed), one row and one note per header with what it breaks. Hand-arithmetic tests in `floor.test.mjs` (53.125 lb/ft on a 25.5 in tail, bending 28.0 psi on 2-2x10 over 46-1/2 in); stair stringer reactions, walls above and trimmer load are not in it, no page field, not checked against a real engineer's header. Reported in `floor.headerChecks`. **Header end reaction** (2026-10-31): each row also carries `reaction` (wL/2, lb at each end, the load a trimmer hanger takes), and an optional typed `headerCheck.hangerCapacity` (lb, from the hanger maker's table; none shipped) fails the header when the reaction exceeds it. Hand-arithmetic test in `floor.test.mjs`; no real hanger table checked. **Header check on the page** (2026-10-31): one field on the floor card takes `live, dead, Fb, Fv, E` and optionally the deflection ratio and hanger capacity (all typed, none shipped), writes `floor.headerCheck`, restores from a saved plan, refuses malformed input; driven in headless Chromium (`eval-browser-click.mjs`) and source-asserted in `page.test.mjs`. **Trimmer check** (2026-10-31, same `headerCheck` figures; `checkTrimmer` in `floor.mjs`, `floor.trimmerChecks`): the doubled trimmer beside a stairwell as a simple span carrying a half-spacing strip at live + dead plus one header's end reaction as a point load at the header's position; bending (larger of under-load and zero-shear point), shear (1.5V/A) and live deflection (200-station closed-form scan) against the typed strengths. Hand statics test in `floor.test.mjs` (reactions sum to the load, moment at the load); not checked against a real engineer's trimmer, other header's reaction and walls above not in it, no page field. **Trimmer bearing** (2026-10-31): an optional typed `headerCheck.bearingCapacity` (lb allowable end reaction, from your engineer; none shipped) fails a trimmer whose larger support reaction exceeds it; hand test in `floor.test.mjs`, no page field, no real bearing table checked. **Stair reaction on the header** (2026-10-31): `stairs[].stringerCheck` now also reports `totalReaction` (stringer count x per-stringer top reaction) and its live share; typing those into `floor.headerCheck.stairReaction`, `stairReactionLive` (defaults to all of it) and `stairSide` ("front"/"back", the header the flight hangs from) adds a mid-span point load to that header (bending, shear, end reaction, deflection), and the trimmer check inherits the larger reaction. Hand-arithmetic tests in `floor.test.mjs` and `stairs.test.mjs`; `headerCheck.stairFrom: N` copies the figure from `plan.stairs[N]` for you (CLI, and the page's front/back header selector from 2026-10-31, `linkStairReaction` in `stairs.mjs`; the page selector is source-tested only, not clicked in Chromium), mid-span is the worst case, not a located load. **Header ties** (2026-10-31): optional typed `headerCheck.tieForce` and `tieCapacity` (lb, from your engineer and the strap maker's table; none shipped, both required together) give `ties` = ceil(force / capacity) per header row and a note; hand test in `floor.test.mjs`; the load path, nailing and strap model are the engineer's, no page field.
- **Joist holes and notches** (2026-10-31, `checkPenetrations()` in `floor.mjs`, plan key `floor.penetrations: [{type: "hole"|"notch", at, size, length, fromEdge, end}]`, limits overridable in `floor.limits`): each item is checked against 1/3-depth holes with 2 in of wood left top and bottom, 1/6-depth notches no longer than 1/3 of the depth and kept out of the middle third of the span, 1/4-depth end notches, and a hole centre at least one joist depth from the support (the last is a rule of thumb). - **Siding** (2026-10-28, CLI `--siding` and a page card, plan key `siding: {exposure, stock, waste, wrapRollSqFt, wrapLap, corners, cornerStock, height, boardPrice, wrapPrice, cornerPrice}`; `siding.mjs`). - **Layout.** An end stud at each end; a stud centred on every multiple of the
  spacing from the start end (the second stud's edge at 15-1/4" for 16 o.c.).
  Openings replace the layout studs they cover with kings, jacks, a built-up
  header and cripples kept on layout above the header and under a window's
  rough sill. Corner and tee backing studs are placed at the nearest spot clear
  of other members. Door openings cut the bottom plate.
- **Plates.** Plates longer than the longest board are spliced over the centre
  of a full-length stud, choosing joints that minimise the cost of the pieces;
  cap plate joints are kept at least the offset you set (24" by default, the
  figure the IRC's section R602.3.2 uses in recent editions; check yours) from
  top plate joints.
- **Self-check.** Every wall is verified after layout: every member inside the
  wall, no two members in the same space, every rough opening clear, a header
  over every opening, every plate joint over a full stud. The page shows the
  result for each wall.
- **Order.** For each lumber size, a cutting-stock problem over the board
  lengths and prices you list (8 to 16 ft plus the precut stud by default):
  Gilmore-Gomory column generation for a lower bound and a near-cheapest order,
  first-fit-decreasing kept as a fallback, and an exact dynamic program for
  groups small enough to finish. Saw kerf is counted on every cut.
- **Sheathing** (opt in: the page checkbox, `--sheathing`, or a `sheathing`
  block in the plan). Vertical panels from each wall's start end, full wall
  height, every joint on the centre of framing that runs the panel's height (a
  stud, king, corner or tee backing stud, or the cripple/header/sill column
  around an opening), so at 19.2 o.c. a panel is ripped short to the last stud
  that fits. Openings are cut out of the panel that covers them. The sheet is
  the cheapest one at least as long as the wall (4x9 for the precut 8 ft wall's
  97-1/8"); the order is a cutting-stock problem over 48" strips, pooled across
  all walls, so one wall's remainder becomes the next wall's panel, with an LP
  bound. Walls marked `"sheathed": false` (interior) are skipped. Where a joint
  has to fall over an opening with no cripple under it, the note says to fit a
  nailer, and `nailerLumber` (also in the CLI's sheathing section) gives the
  nailers as a cut list and a board order from the plan's stock, to add to the
  framing order. With `--sheathing` (and on the page) the nailers are also pooled into the framing order itself, so they can come out of its offcuts.
- **Outputs.** Order with totals, cut list, board-by-board cutting plan with
  offcuts, layout marks per wall, an elevation drawing per wall, CSV export, and
  a plan file you can save and reopen.

## Method and citation

- Gilmore, P. C. & Gomory, R. E. (1961). A linear programming approach to the
  cutting-stock problem. *Operations Research* 9(6), 849-859.
  doi:[10.1287/opre.9.6.849](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.9.6.849). Column
  generation for the LP relaxation, with a bounded knapsack (dynamic programming
  in 1/16") as the pricing problem, over several board lengths each with its own
  price. The LP is solved through its dual (a dense simplex with Bland's rule),
  whose origin is always feasible, so no phase one is needed. Integer orders come
  from sequential rounding: take the whole part of the LP solution, re-solve the
  pieces left, repeat. The engine exports this as `PAPERS`.

## Verification

`node --test` (42 tests across engine, CLI, page and usage). What they check:

- **Hand-counted walls.** A plain 10 ft wall at 16 o.c. has studs at exactly
  0, 15-1/4, 31-1/4, ... 111-1/4 and 118-1/2. A 36 x 48 window gets kings at
  37 and 77-1/2, 81" jacks, two 39" header plies, a 36" sill, two 2-3/8" top
  cripples and four 31-1/2" bottom cripples at the hand-worked positions. A door
  cuts the bottom plate at its rough opening.
- **400 seeded random walls** (lengths 4 to 47 ft, 2x4 and 2x6, 16/19.2/24
  o.c., three wall heights, up to five openings, corners and tees): every valid
  one passes the self-check above.
- **Plate splices** on walls up to 50 ft: every piece fits a 16 ft board, every
  joint is over a full stud, every cap joint at least 24" from every top joint.
- **Optimiser against the exact solver** on 120 seeded cases: every piece is
  cut exactly once, no board is overfilled (kerf counted), the cost is never
  below the LP bound, and the shipped order equals the exact cheapest order on
  every case the exact solver finishes.
- **Sheathing:** a 10 ft wall gets joints at 48 and 96; at 19.2 o.c. the first
  panel is ripped to the 38.4 stud; window cut-outs add up to the opening
  width; on 400 seeded random walls (60 to 456 in, three spacings, up to
  three openings) the panels tile the wall, every joint has framing under it,
  and the cost is never below the bound.
- **The LP solver** reproduces a textbook LP and its dual exactly.

`node bench.mjs` measures the optimiser (seeded, reproducible):

copies  pieces  boards     cost    bound    gap    FFD cost   each piece alone   ms
     1     345     253  2384.16  2384.12  0.002%    2450.00            2915.04   117
     2     690     504  4768.88  4768.23  0.014%    4901.72            5830.08   148
     4    1380    1004  9532.60  9531.45  0.012%    9786.00           11660.16   180

"Flat" prices are proportional to length; "priced" ones rise per foot with
length, as longer boards often do. Rounding the LP finds the exact cheapest
order on 85% of small cases; first-fit-decreasing on 65% with flat prices and
29% with priced ones, where its habit of starting every board on the longest
length costs 9.5% on average. On whole houses the order is within 0.014% of the
LP bound, so there is almost nothing left for a better integer method to find
there; the gap that matters is against planning by hand (the "each piece alone"
column is the extreme case: 22% more board).

## Advanced framing (2026-10-22)

Plan `"advanced": { "twoStudCorners": true, "ladderTees": true, "singleTopPlate": true }` (or the
"Advanced framing" menu on the page):

- `twoStudCorners` puts one backing stud in a `corner: true` wall instead of
  `cornerExtraStuds` (the end stud plus one), for drywall clips.
- `ladderTees` replaces the tee backing studs with flat 2x blocks every 24 in
  between the full-height studs either side of the tee, for the partition to
  nail to. Where those studs are more than one stud spacing apart, or an
  opening is between them, that tee keeps standard backing and a note says so.
  The blocks come out of offcuts in the order.
- `singleTopPlate` (2026-10-22) leaves out the cap plate, so the wall is 1-1/2 in
  shorter. With nothing lapping the top plate's joints, each joint, corner and
  tee takes a steel plate tie; the count is `plateTies` on the wall and a note

  edition), which this tool cannot see. On the example house it takes 345 pieces
  / 253 boards / $2,384.16 down to 329 / 238 / $2,173.88 (-8.8%, 20 ties).

## Panelised vs stick, from piece counts (2026-10-29)

`node panelised-bench.mjs` counts what a crew handles, not what it costs: no labour rate is shipped. Stick: every member is cut and set on site. Panelised: the same members are built flat in a shop (plates trimmed at the panel breaks, so there are more saw cuts), and the site stands panels, nails the seams between them (face nails along the end studs every 16 in, a typed figure), nails the bottom plate to the floor and the cap plate over the joints. Sheathing is left out.

| method | example house (invented) | Open House Plans 1 (CC0, generated) |
|---|---|---|
| stick: members set on site / saw cuts / site nails | 345 / 345 / 1,871 | 436 / 436 / 2,311 |
| panels up to 8 ft | 32 panels, 417 cuts, 509 site nails (27%) | 37 panels, 505 cuts, 532 site nails (23%) |
| panels up to 12 ft | 23 panels, 390 cuts, 446 site nails (24%) | 24 panels, 466 cuts, 441 site nails (19%) |
| panels up to 16 ft | 16 panels, 369 cuts, 397 site nails (21%) | 22 panels, 460 cuts, 427 site nails (18%) |

Reading: panels cut the things lifted into place by a factor of 11 to 22 and move roughly three quarters or more of the framing nails to the shop table, at the price of 5 to 21% more saw cuts. Whether that is cheaper depends on shop labour, crane or lift, and shipping, which this tool does not know. Open House Plans 2 does not lay out (two windows 3 in apart). Two generated or invented houses, no real crew's time study; the nail rules are the defaults of `fasteners.mjs`, not a code table. `panelised-bench.test.mjs` checks a 20 ft wall by hand (two panels, one seam, 41 site nails) and the ordering across limits.

## DXF floor plan import (2026-10-27)

`dxf.mjs`, CLI `node cli.mjs house.dxf [--plan-only] [--units=ft]`, and the page's "Open plan file" button (now accepts `.dxf`; counted as `dxf_opened`). Reads an ASCII DXF (LINE, LWPOLYLINE, POLYLINE) and builds the plan walls instead of typing them: wall centre lines on a layer whose name contains "wall" are merged when collinear (gaps up to 1 in), the longest loop of walls meeting end to end becomes the exterior (`corner: true`, walked so the first meets the last), a wall ending on another wall's side becomes a partition with `tee` and the through wall's `tees`, an unconnected partition end sets `freeEnds`, and lines on "door" or "window" layers lying along a wall become openings (offset and width from the line). Units come from `$INSUNITS` (in, ft, mm, cm, m) or `--units`. **Double-line walls (2026-10-27):** architects draw each wall as two face lines. `pairDoubleLines` pairs parallel runs 2.5 to 14 in apart that overlap by at least half the shorter one, merging a face broken by a butting partition or a door gap first, and takes the average of the two lines' ends as the centre line (a mitred corner's inner and outer faces land on the corner centre). Automatic when at least 3 pairs make up 60% of the wall runs; `doubleLine: true|false` or CLI `--double-line` / `--single-line` override it. A partition drawn between faces keeps its face-to-face length (said in the notes); the snap widens to half the thickness so tees still join. **Rectangle walls (2026-10-27):** some generators draw each wall piece between openings as a closed rectangle. Those are read as centre lines along the long axis (`rectWalls`; a short post or door stub takes the axis that leaves a thickness the long pieces use), corners trimmed to centre to centre, and a gap between collinear pieces up to 20 ft with a door- or window-layer line in it (jamb, glazing, garage-door line) becomes a bridged wall plus an opening of the gap's width (`bridgeGaps`; door if any door-layer line, else window). Checked on four plans generated by [Open House Plans](../open-house-plans/) (2 bed, 3 bed farmhouse with 2-car garage, 4 bed craftsman, 3 bed modern): each finds its 4-wall exterior loop, and on the two kept as fixtures (`fixtures/open-house-plans-*.dxf`, CC0) every opening width matches the generator's own `openings.csv` (18 of 18 and 30 of 30). A third and fourth house match by count (33 of 33, 24 of 24). **Door and window block symbols (2026-10-27):** a file that places openings as block INSERTs (what most CAD drawings do) now reads them. `parseDxf` keeps each block's bounding box (LINE, LWPOLYLINE, ARC, CIRCLE) and each INSERT's point, scale and rotation; `insertOpenings` moves the box into the drawing, finds the wall it touches within 8 in, and takes the box's extent along that wall as the width (a door's swing arc makes the box square; the width is still the leaf). Kind comes from the layer or the block name matching the door or window pattern; other blocks are counted and skipped, and a symbol over an opening a line already marks is not counted twice. Tested on hand-written blocks only (`dxf.test.mjs`: a 36 in door with a swing arc, a unit window scaled to 72 in, a toilet block skipped, a duplicate); nested blocks, dynamic blocks and mirrored scales are not read. **Jamb-and-arc door symbols (2026-10-28):** four real door symbols from LibreCAD's plan library (`plan/doors/d1` to `d4`, GPL, so not kept here; a double door and three single doors, drawn only as jambs along the wall plus a swing arc per leaf) put on a DOOR layer in a 40 x 28 ft house came out as several 4 to 9 in "doors" each. `joinJambs` now treats door-layer pieces within 12 in as one jamb and joins two jambs into one opening when a door-layer ARC has a radius near the gap (one leaf) or half of it (two leaves) and sits between them; wider pieces (already openings) are never joined. The four symbols now read as one door each, 131, 71, 81 and 71-to-81 in wide, **outer edge of jamb to outer edge of jamb**, not the rough opening (the note says so). Still a symbol library, not an architect's plan; test uses made-up numbers with the same structure (`dxf.test.mjs`). **Layer flags (2026-10-29):** `--wall-layer=RE`, `--door-layer=RE` and `--window-layer=RE` (case-insensitive patterns) name the layers when the file does not call them wall, door or window. Found by running the reader on real files from the ezdxf test set (MIT): all five answered "No lines on a layer matching /wall/i", listing layers such as `T-2DPLINE-ALL` and `LEICA_DISTO_3D`. With the flag and `--units=m`, a Leica Disto laser-measurer sketch reads as two interior walls (22.7 and 36.1 ft) and a survey polyline file as a closed loop; neither is a house plan, so this shows the reader copes with real layer names and unit-less files, not that it frames an architect's plan. **Nested blocks (2026-10-29):** a block that INSERTs other blocks (a door built from a leaf block and a frame block) now has the children's boxes folded into its own, turned, scaled and moved by the inner INSERT; a negative scale mirrors; cycles are ignored. Tested on hand-written blocks only (`dxf.test.mjs`). Those are machine-drawn files from a sister tool, **not yet an architect's DXF**, and the takeoff then says what the engine cannot frame: windows closer than 4.5 in (a mullion; make them one wider opening) and a door within 4.5 in of a wall end need hand framing.

**Shared kings (2026-10-27, `framingZones` in `engine.mjs`, test in `engine.test.mjs`):** two openings whose king studs would overlap now share one set when the gap fits both openings' jacks plus the larger king count (4.5 in with one jack and one king each). The shared king sits against the left opening's jacks, labelled "shared king"; a leftover sliver is noted for blocking. Closer than that is still refused, now with the gap and the minimum in the message. Checked by hand-computed positions and `verifyWall`, not against a real framer's practice. Two houses still report one X junction.

**Openings near a wall end (2026-10-26, `framingZones` in `engine.mjs`, test in `engine.test.mjs`):** when the kings no longer fit between an opening's jacks and the end stud, the end stud serves as the king and the kings that do not fit are dropped (down to none), on either end. The layout says so in the wall's notes. The jacks must still clear the end stud (opening at least 3 in from the end with one jack); closer is refused with that figure. Checked against the per-wall self-check on hand-built walls only, not a framer's corner detail (some framers add a second corner stud or a flat nailer; the corner backing option still applies).
Tested otherwise only on a hand-built 4.5 in and 6 in house (`dxf.test.mjs`). Not read, and said in the output notes: dynamic block symbols, arcs, wall thickness, opening heights (defaults 82.5 in door, 48 in window), header sizes (placeholder 2x10 everywhere: set yours), crossing walls (reported), curved walls, binary DXF. Lengths are the drawn centre-line lengths. Tested in `dxf.test.mjs` on hand-built DXF text (a 40 x 28 ft house in feet, inches and millimetres, split lines, open outline, polylines) and through the takeoff engine; **not yet tried on a real architect's DXF**, which will have layer names, blocks and double-line walls this does not handle.

## IFC floor plan import (2026-10-27)

`ifc.mjs`, CLI `node cli.mjs house.ifc [--plan-only] [--units=ft]`. Reads an IFC STEP text file: each `IfcWall` / `IfcWallStandardCase` becomes a centre line from its `Axis` polyline (or, lacking one, the X dimension of an extruded rectangle profile), placement chains (`IfcLocalPlacement`, rotated by the reference direction, plan rotation only) are composed to world coordinates, and `IfcDoor` / `IfcWindow` become openings of `OverallWidth` starting at their placement origin and running along its local X. Length units come from `IfcSIUnit` or an inch/foot `IfcConversionBasedUnit`. The result goes through the DXF reader's wall logic, so exterior loop, tees, free ends and opening offsets behave identically. **Checked on hand-written IFC4 fragments and, since 2026-10-27, on buildingSMART's real Simple-Scene house** (IFC 2x3 and 4.3, CC BY 4.0, `fixtures/`): its walls have no Axis, so the reader now takes a mesh wall (triangulated face set, polygonal face set, faceted B-rep, extruded closed profile) from the plan bounding box of its body in the wall's own frame (long side = length), and reads units from `IfcUnitAssignment` only (the 4.3 file's second, unprefixed `IfcSIUnit` had made every length 1000 times too long). Both files give the four wall lengths 1.8, 3.8, 4.2 and 6.0 m. The file has no doors or windows, and its walls do not form a closed loop, so openings and corners from a real export are still untested. Earlier text: **checked only on hand-written IFC4 fragments** (`ifc.test.mjs`, `cli.test.mjs`); not yet on a real Revit, ArchiCAD or Vectorworks export. **Opening size and sill (2026-10-28)**, checked on buildingSMART's real `wall-with-opening-and-window.ifc` (IFC4, CC BY 4.0, `fixtures/`): a 3 m wall, an `IfcOpeningElement` at x = 1000 mm, z = 500 mm, and a 1000 x 1000 mm window nested in the opening's placement. Offset and width were already right; the reader now also takes `OverallHeight` and the placement elevation (above the lowest wall base) as the rough-opening height and `headerHeight`, so this window frames with a 500 mm sill instead of the 48 in default. Doors and windows without an `OverallHeight` keep the defaults. Still one small real file: doors, multi-storey and non-nested placements remain unchecked. **Storeys (2026-10-29).** A multi-storey export used to be merged into one plan: on the ArchiCAD house from the ThatOpen engine test set (KIT's FZK-Haus, two storeys, 13 walls, 5 doors, 11 windows; not committed, its licence is unclear) the lower and upper exterior walls overlapped into 9 walls and a 1 m window read with a 3.5 m sill. The reader now follows `IfcRelContainedInSpatialStructure`, orders `IfcBuildingStorey` by elevation, and reads one storey (the lowest by default; `--storey=N` or a storey name; `storey` in the API), measuring sills from that storey\'s own wall base. FZK-Haus storey 1 gives 9 walls (four exterior 10 x 12 m, five interior) and 14 openings, 2.0 x 1.2 m windows on a 0.8 m sill; storey 2 gives 4 walls and 2 windows on a 0.8 m sill. Tested on a hand-written two-storey file (`ifc.test.mjs`). Elements the file does not place in a storey are kept in every storey. Roof geometry above the top storey is not read.

Known gaps, which a real export will probably hit: walls with swept or boolean bodies and no Axis, openings whose origin is the centre of the width, IFCZIP/IFCXML, multi-storey files (all storeys are merged into one plan), wall thickness and opening heights (defaults apply).

## Corner plate laps

Set `"plateLaps": true` in the plan (or pick it on the page). A wall with
`corner: true` meets the end of the wall listed before it (the first wall meets
the last). At each corner the earlier wall's top plate runs through by the later
wall's depth while the later wall's top plate stops short by the earlier wall's
depth; the cap plate laps the other way, tying the corner. Piece lengths and the
splice points change, and so can the board order; plate totals do not (the test
checks it). Splices still sit over full studs.

**Tee laps (2026-10-21).** A partition can say where it butts a through wall:
`"tee": {"wall": "Front", "at": "14'", "end": "start"}` (`end` is `start` or
`end`; `at` is the partition's centre along the through wall). With `plateLaps` on,
the partition's cap plate runs over the through wall by its depth and the through
wall's cap is cut for the partition's depth, each run spliced on its own. Unlike
corners, the cap total changes (by the through wall's depth minus the partition's). The page has a "Butts into (tee lap)" field per wall: `Front @ 14'` (add `end` for the wall's far end); checked in headless Chromium (valid entry runs clean, a malformed one is reported, no page errors).

## Limits

- **Headers from your own span table** (2026-10-22, `headers.mjs`, CLI `--headers`). Put `"headerTable": [{"upTo": 36, "header": "2x6", "plies": 2}, ...]` in the plan, rows copied from the span table your code or engineer gives for your load, building width and stud size. An opening with `"header": "auto"` (or none) takes the first row whose `upTo` covers its rough width and the row's plies; a header you picked yourself is kept and listed as UNDER when it is shallower or has fewer plies than the table asks for. A span past the last row, a row with an unknown size and a table that gets lighter for longer spans are errors. No code values ship with the tool, so it checks your table, not the code. Node-tested on a made-up table (`headers.test.mjs`); not on the page yet.
- **Header table on the page** (2026-10-24, `index.html`, `eval-browser-click.mjs`). Section 3 has a "Size headers from your own span table" box: one row per line, `longest span, size, plies` (`3 ft, 2x6`). An opening whose header is set to `auto` takes the first row that covers it, a hand-picked header lighter than the table asks for is listed under the box, an `auto` with no table is refused, and the saved plan file keeps the table and the `auto` choices (the same `headerTable` key the CLI reads). Same `resolveHeaders` as the CLI. Checked with a click through in headless Chromium (3 rows, two openings resolved, empty table refused); the table is yours, no code values are shipped.
- **No structural checks.** Header size, plies, jack count, stud size and
  spacing are inputs. Nothing checks them against a span table, a load or a
  building code.
- **Two courses (2026-10-21):** when no listed sheet is as long as the wall (say 12 ft 4-1/2 in of wall and nothing past a 4x10), `chooseCourses` picks a lower sheet and an upper course of at least `minCourse` (12 in) by lowest price per strip, counting the bands that cut crosswise from one sheet (four 24-1/2 in bands from a 4x9). The horizontal joint gets a piece of the wall's stud size on edge in every bay between studs (`jointBlocks`; bays an opening cuts out at that height are skipped, an existing blocking row counts), the pieces join the nailer cut list and board order, and the wall check fails if the joint has nothing to nail to along its length. Vertical joints of both courses sit on the same studs. Tested on 300 seeded walls of 125 to 154 in studs. Not checked against an engineered shear-wall schedule: blocking and nail spacing at the joint are yours to confirm.
- **Horizontal sheathing (2026-10-21):** `sheathing.orientation: "horizontal"` (CLI `--horizontal`, page tick box "sheets long side along the wall") lays sheets long side along the wall in courses one sheet width high from the floor. End joints land on stud centres (the same bearing test as vertical panels), alternate courses start half a sheet in so joints are staggered, a last course under `minCourse` (12 in) is avoided by ripping the course below it too, and every course boundary gets blocking on edge between studs (`jointBlocking`, the same pieces as the two-course joint). Each listed sheet length is tried for the whole house and the cheapest order wins; full courses pool into one cutting-stock order and a ripped course is cut crosswise into bands. Measured on the example house at 1 per square foot: horizontal 1344 (4x12) against 1296 vertical (4x9), because the ripped courses and the blocking-heavy joints cost more than full-height panels on 9 ft walls; horizontal is for walls the vertical layout cannot cover or where your inspector or shear schedule wants it. Tested on 200 seeded walls (every edge on framing, courses add to the wall height, no sliver course, joints staggered). Not modelled: mixing two sheet lengths in one house, a horizontal layout that lets a joint fall over an opening (it hand-sheathes that wall instead), and nailing schedules.
- **Rim lap (2026-10-21):** `sheathing.rimLap: 9.25` (CLI `--rim-lap=9.25`, page field "rim lap (in)") runs vertical sheathing that many inches below the bottom plate to cover the floor's rim board, so the sheet is picked for wall height plus the lap (a taller sheet or two courses where needed) and a note says to nail it to the rim. Panels start at a negative height; the self-check and order are unchanged. It does not check the floor's rim depth or that anything is there to nail to; horizontal sheets ignore it (a note says so).
- **Corner overhang (2026-10-21):** `sheathing.overhang: true` (CLI `--overhang`, page tick box "run sheathing past corners") runs each wall's vertical sheathing past its end by the next wall's stud depth where that wall has `corner: true` (same earlier-wall-runs-through reading as `plateLaps`), so the sheets cover the corner's end stud. The last panel is longer by that amount, and the wall's self-check covers the extra width. Not applied to horizontal sheets (a note says so), and the later wall's sheathing is not trimmed back for the sheet's thickness.
- **Not modelled yet:** let-in or
  structural bracing, header spacers (listed in the notes), hardware and
  fasteners, walls that change height or have an off-centre peak, multiple storeys,
  window and door rough openings given by unit size (enter rough openings), and
  plates lapping at corners that do not close a loop (`plateLaps` reads the walls as a
  closed loop in the order listed).
- **Openings within 1-1/2" plus their kings and jacks of a wall end** are
  rejected rather than framed: frame those ends by hand.
- **Prices default to 1 per foot of board**, so the default order minimises
  length bought. No lumber price is stated or implied.
- The example house is invented.

## Gable walls

Set `"gable": {"pitch": 6}` on a wall (rise per 12 in of run) to frame a symmetric gable end: a level bottom plate, studs whose lengths follow the roof (the long point of the angled cut; the eave studs are `studLength`), and top and cap plates measured along the slope, spliced over studs with the usual cap offset. Add `"peak": 80` (inches from the wall start) for an off-centre peak: `pitch` is then the start side's rise per 12 in, and the far side gets the pitch that reaches the same peak height, so both eaves stay level (result `gable.farPitch`). Openings are framed as in a level wall (2026-10-21): header, jacks and sill at their heights from the bottom plate, kings and top cripples cut to the long point of the slope, the bottom plate cut across a door; the header must fit under the eave-line top plate, so a window in the triangle itself is not done. Blocking is not done for gable walls. Sheathing is (2026-10-22, `sheatheGables` in `sheathing.mjs`, vertical sheets): panels end on full-height studs within a sheet width, each is cut from a sheet at least as long as its long point (two courses when the peak is taller than every sheet), and the gable has its own sheet order. Offcut triangles are reused (2026-10-24, `nestedHeight`/`nestPairs` in `sheathing.mjs`): the sloped top course of one panel can be cut from the same sheet piece as another's, one turned end for end, as tall as the two heights added at the same distance from the edge; pairs are chosen greedily by area saved and kept only if the full cutting-stock order comes out cheaper than buying every course as its bounding box (`summary.unnestedCost`, `panel.nestedWith`). On a 20 ft, 6:12 gable with the default sheets and a price of 1 per sq ft this takes the order from 292 to 256; a steeper 24 ft, 8:12 gable has no pair that fits one sheet and stays at the bounding-box order. Tested against hand heights (a 48 in course 20..44 in turned on itself is 64 in) and as never dearer; a nested cut has not been tried on real sheets, and a course that straddles the peak is not nested. On the page since 2026-10-24 (walls table: "Gable pitch" and "Gable peak at"; saved in plan files, bad pitch refused; page test and a browser click check, no engine change). Not yet the horizontal layout's gable courses, or nailers over openings; the roof-line area is checked against a closed form in `engine.test.mjs`.  Tested in `engine.test.mjs` on a 20 ft, 6:12 wall: layout self-check clean, plate length equals run x sqrt(1 + slope^2).

## Shop panels

`node cli.mjs plan.json --panel-max=12ft` (or the "Longest shop panel" box on the page, then the drawings button) splits each wall on the drawings into panels no longer than the limit. `panels.mjs` picks the fewest panels, then the most even widths, and cuts only where no stud, king, jack, cripple, header, sill or block crosses the line, so each panel is built from whole members; plates are cut to panel length and the end studs of neighbouring panels are nailed together on site. A wide opening can leave no clear cut line inside the limit; that panel is flagged "over limit", not hidden. Tested on the sample house (`panels.test.mjs`, `drawings.test.mjs`, `cli.test.mjs`) and clicked through in headless Chromium (`eval-browser-click.mjs`). Add `--sheathing-psf=1.5` (lb per sq ft; 1.5 is typical of 7/16 in OSB or 1/2 in plywood, check the data sheet) and each panel also shows its sheathing weight (panel width times wall height, less the rough sizes of the openings inside it; none on gable walls) and a total (`panelParts` `sheathLb`, `totalLb`; node-tested against a hand count; engine, CLI and the "Sheathing weight on shop panels" page box, which feeds the drawings download; clicked through in headless Chromium 2026-10-23: a weight of 0 gives shorter drawings than 1.5). `--panel-order` (with `--panel-max`) prints the lumber order for the shop panels: every panel's pieces, plates trimmed to panel length, pooled and cut from stock (`panelOrder` in `panels.mjs`), beside the whole-wall order. On the sample house at 12 ft: 250 boards at 2385.88 against 253 boards at 2384.16 for whole walls (each plate break costs a kerf and shifts the packing; nailers are pooled in with `--sheathing`). `--pull-lists` adds a pull list per panel: which boards (size, length, number) it is built from and the cuts on each, with a note when a board also feeds another panel (`assignBoards`, greedy: keep a board's cuts together in one panel where the demand allows; every panel piece placed exactly once, node-tested). CLI and, since the "Download shop-panel lumber order" button (needs a panel length), the page: its download holds the order and the pull lists, and without a panel length the status line asks for one (headless Chromium click check, 2026-10-23, `eval-browser-click.mjs`; the example plan only). A layout aid, not engineering: lifting, bracing and shipping are the builder's call.

## Bid leveling (2026-10-28)

`node cli.mjs plan.json --level-bids` (`bidlevel.mjs`) lines each sub's bid up against the budget's lines, so the low number is compared on the same scope. A plan's `bids` is `[{ bidder, lines: [{ item, qty, unitPrice | amount }] }]`, `item` naming a budget line. A bid more than 5% short of the model's quantity is priced up at its own unit price; a line left out is priced at the plan's typed price or the median of the other bids' unit prices; a gap with neither is listed as unleveled, never guessed. Over-quantity is flagged and not removed. Exit code 1 on an unknown item or a line without an amount. Tests (`bidlevel.test.mjs`, 5, bids made up): no real bid has been run through it. Page card "Level subcontractor bids" (2026-10-28): lines of `bidder; item; qty; unit price` (or `bidder; item; amount`) under the budget table, saved into and restored from `plan.bids`, usage event `bids_leveled`; checked in headless Chromium on the example house with made-up bids (page.test.mjs asserts the wiring only).
