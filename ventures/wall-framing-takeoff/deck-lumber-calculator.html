The method

  
    
    
  1. Joists. One on every multiple of the spacing along the width, plus one at the far end, each running the depth between the ledger and the outer rim.
    2. 
    
  2. Beam and posts. The beam sits under the joists a little in from the outer edge; posts stand under it at an even spacing no wider than the maximum.
    3. 
    
  3. Footings. One round footing per post, in cubic yards and bags of mix with a waste allowance.
    4. 
    
  4. Decking. Rows across the joists, each board's end joints on a joist centre and staggered from the row beside it. The lumber is bought as an order of stock lengths.
    5. 
    
  5. Hardware. Hangers, post bases, ties and ledger fasteners counted from the layout.

Questions

  
How much lumber do I need for a 16 x 12 ft deck?

  
For a 16 ft by 12 ft ledger-attached deck 36 in above grade with 2x8 joists at 16 in on center, the calculator counts 13 joists, a double 2x10 beam, 3 6x6 posts, 26 deck boards of 16 ft and 16 bags of concrete for the footings. Change any figure to match your deck.

  
How many joists do I need for a deck?

  
Joists sit on every multiple of the spacing along the deck's width, plus one at the far end. At 16 in on center a 16 ft wide deck takes 13 joists. At 12 in it takes more and at 24 in fewer, but the decking you choose may need the closer spacing.

  
How many deck boards do I need?

  
Boards run across the joists, so the number of rows is the deck's depth divided by a board's width plus its gap, rounded up. A 12 ft deep deck of 5-1/2 in boards with a 3/16 in gap is 26 rows. End joints are placed on a joist centre and staggered, and the last row may need ripping.

  
Does this size my joists, beam or footings?

  
No. Joist, beam and post sizes are inputs you take from your local code's span tables or an engineer, and footing depth and diameter are typed figures because frost depth is local. The page counts lumber and hardware for the sizes you pick. A building department or licensed engineer decides what is allowed.
