Questions

  
How many bags of insulation do I need for a 40 x 30 ft house?

  
For a 40 x 30 ft rectangle with 8 ft walls, one 3 ft door and two 3 x 4 ft windows framed in 2x4 at 16 in on center, the calculator finds 890 sq ft of open cavity. With 5% waste and bags that cover 88 sq ft, that is 11 bags. Change any figure to match your plan.

  
Is a 2x4 wall with R-15 batts really R-15?

  
No. Framing conducts heat around the batt, so the whole wall is lower than the label. With the studs, plates, headers and cripples this calculator places, framing is 17.3% of the opaque wall, and the R-15 cavity gives about R-13.1 (R-16.9 through the cavity alone). A 2x6 wall with R-21 batts comes to about R-17.8 by the same method.

  
How much insulation does framing take out of a wall?

  
Counted from the members placed rather than guessed: in the 40 x 30 ft example, framing takes 16.6% of the wall panel area in 2x4 walls and 17.3% in 2x6 walls, so the open cavity is a little over 80% of the panel. The common 25% framing-factor guess is more pessimistic than a layout with 16 in spacing and one door and two windows needs.

  
What is not counted?

  
Attic and ceiling insulation, rim joists, air sealing, windows and doors (their U-factors are yours to type), and compressed batts. Bag coverage and R-values come from your product's label, and the effective R uses typical handbook layer values you can replace. It is an estimate to check against your supplier, not an energy-code compliance check.
