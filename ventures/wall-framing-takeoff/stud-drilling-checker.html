Questions

  
What size hole can I drill in a 2x4 stud?

  
In a load-bearing 2x4 wall the commonly stated residential limit is 40% of the stud width: 1.4 in on a 3.5 in stud, with the edge of the hole at least 5/8 in from each face. In a non-bearing wall, or where the bored studs are doubled, the limit is 60%: 2.1 in. A 1.5 in hole in a bearing 2x4 is over the limit by 0.1 in; the same hole in a non-bearing 2x4 passes with 1 in left on each side.

  
How big a notch can I cut in a stud?

  
A notch is limited to 25% of the stud width in a bearing or exterior wall (7/8 in on a 2x4, 1.375 in on a 2x6) and 40% in a non-bearing wall (1.4 in on a 2x4). A hole and a notch are never allowed at the same section of a stud. A 1 in notch in a bearing 2x4 is 28.6% and fails; the checker says it needs a 2x6.

  
Can a 3 inch drain go through a 2x6 stud wall?

  
Not through a single bearing 2x6. A 3 in DWV drain is 3.5 in outside; with the 1/8 in clearance the checker adds, the hole is 3-5/8 in, 65.9% of a 5.5 in stud. Even doubled (60%, 3.3 in) it fails, so the options are a 2x8 wall, a chase or furred-out wall, or running the drain where it crosses plates and joists instead of studs. Your plumber and inspector decide which.

  
Where do these drilling limits come from?

  
They are the limits commonly stated for solid-sawn studs in IRC R602.6: bored holes 40% of the width in a bearing wall (60% in a non-bearing wall or in doubled studs, no more than two doubled studs bored in a row), at least 5/8 in of wood left at each edge, notches 25% bearing and 40% non-bearing. The edition your jurisdiction adopted, local amendments and engineered studs (LVL, I-studs, steel) can differ; the percentages are inputs in the whole-house tool, not hard-wired claims.
