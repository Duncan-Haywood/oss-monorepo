Framing nail calculator

Type your walls (length in inches, doors and windows with offset, width, height and header) and the framing nails follow, counted from the studs, kings, jacks, cripples, headers, sills and plates the takeoff actually places, by connection, with waste, boxes and a cost if you type the box size and price. The same fastener engine as the whole-house takeoff. Nail size and type, anchors and sheathing nails are not counted here. Nothing leaves your browser.

How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Members first. The takeoff lays out every stud, king, jack, cripple, header ply, sill and plate for each wall, with openings cut out of the plates.
    2. 
    
  2. Nails per connection. Two end nails at each stud end, face nails along plates at 16 in, jacks to kings at 12 in, header plies in two rows at 16 in, and so on, each one a typed setting with a common-practice default; a run gets one nail at each end plus one per spacing.
    3. 
    
  3. Waste and boxes. The total plus waste (10% by default) is the order; with nailsPerBox it becomes whole boxes, and with price a cost.

Questions

  
How many nails does a 20 ft wall with a door and a window plus a 12 ft hall wall need?

  
Framed at 16 in on centre with 2x4 studs, the two walls come to 326 nails counted: 172 end nails in studs, kings and cripples (2 at each end), 48 face nails joining jacks to kings (12 in apart), 24 at header ends, 26 joining header plies (2 rows at 16 in), 4 at rough sill ends, 25 from the bottom plates to the floor and 27 from the cap plates to the top plates. With 10% waste that is 359 nails to buy, so one 2,500-nail box.

  
What does that cost, and are these the nail counts my inspector wants?

  
One box at 45.00 is 45.00. The counts follow typed schedule defaults (two end nails per stud end, plates at 16 in, jacks at 12 in), which are common framing practice and not a code table: your engineer's nailing schedule or your code edition governs, and you change any of the numbers in the fasteners block. Sheathing nails are left out here; the whole-house takeoff counts them when sheathing is on.
