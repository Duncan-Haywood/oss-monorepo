Framing nail calculator

Type your walls (length in inches, stud size, openings) and the nailing schedule your engineer or code gives, and the nails follow: end nails for every stud, king and cripple, jack and header nails, sill nails, backing, and the plate nails along each wall, with 10% waste and a box count if you type nails per box and a box price. The same fastener engine as the whole-house takeoff. Every nail count is a figure you can change; the defaults are common practice, not a code table. Nail size and type, sheathing nails, hold-downs and anchors are not counted on this page.

How it is counted

  
    
    
  1. Connections. Each member the takeoff placed is walked once: 2 end nails at each end of every stud, king and cripple, jack-to-king face nails at 12 in, header ends and header plies, sill ends, corner and tee backing at 24 in, and bottom and cap plates at 16 in.

  2. Waste. The total is raised by the waste fraction (10% by default) and rounded up.

Questions

  
How many framing nails do three walls need, and how many boxes?

  
Three walls here (a 20 ft front wall with a window and a door, a 12 ft side wall and a 20 ft 2x6 back wall) need 387 nails counted: 220 stud, king and cripple end nails, 32 jack-to-king, 16 header-end, 18 header-ply, 4 sill, 10 corner-backing, 43 bottom-plate and 44 cap-plate nails. With 10% waste that is 426 to buy, 1 box of 5000, 48.00 at 48 a box.

  
What changes if the nailing schedule calls for three nails per stud end?

  
Type studEndNails 3 and the stud-end line goes from 220 to 330, the total from 387 to 497 and the order from 426 to 547, still 1 box, 48.00. Every count is a typed schedule figure with a common-practice default; it is not read from a code table, and nail size, type, sheathing nails and anchors are not counted here.
