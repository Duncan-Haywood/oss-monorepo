This calculator vs. working out the Child Tax Credit by hand

The headline number — $2,200 per child — is easy to remember. The part that goes wrong by hand is everything downstream of it: the smaller credit for dependents who do not qualify, the rounding rule on the phase-out, and what the refundable portion actually promises.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Forgetting the $500 Credit for Other Dependents entirely -- treating a dependent who has only an ITIN, or who turned 17 during the year, as simply not counting for anything. They usually still qualify for the separate, smaller, entirely nonrefundable $500 credit.

What's the second most common mistake?

Getting the phase-out rounding wrong. The reduction is $50 for every $1,000 OR PART OF $1,000 of MAGI over the threshold -- so $1 over the line costs the same full $50 as $999 over it. A hand estimate that uses a continuous percentage instead of this stepped, round-up rule understates the reduction just above each $1,000 boundary.

What arithmetic mistake gets made most often?

Treating the refundable Additional Child Tax Credit ceiling as a guaranteed refund rather than a maximum. The actual refund depends on how much of the credit is needed to offset tax liability first -- a hand estimate that reports the ceiling as "your refund" can overstate what actually comes back.

What eligibility mistake gets made most often?

Missing the OBBBA filer Social Security number requirement -- assuming that because the CHILD has a valid SSN, the credit is automatically available. The taxpayer (and, on a joint return, at least one spouse) must also have a valid SSN, or neither credit can be claimed at all.

Does this calculator know my complete tax situation?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page computes your total tax liability or models any credit or deduction besides these two. Both apply the same published federal credit and phase-out rules to the numbers you enter; verify against IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional before relying on either.
