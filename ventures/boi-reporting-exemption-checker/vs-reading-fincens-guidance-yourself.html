Common questions

Why not just read FinCEN's guidance myself?

FinCEN.gov is the primary source and this tool does not replace it — every answer this checker gives traces back to FinCEN's own August 11, 2026 final rule and the Federal Register notice. The friction is that the rule text describes categories in general while you still have to work out which category your own entity falls into, compute your own deadline from your own registration date, and figure out which owners the reporting requirement actually covers. The specific risk is search-result age: a page written before the August 2026 final rule, or a cached summary of the earlier 2025 interim exemption, can describe a broader filing obligation than the current permanent rule does.

Does this checker replace a lawyer?

No, and it is not trying to. This tool applies a published federal rule to the facts you enter and produces a likely outcome, not a determination of your entity's actual filing status. It is not legal advice and does not replace a qualified attorney. If your entity's formation jurisdiction is unclear, its registration status is contested, or an owner's U.S.-person status is genuinely uncertain, that is a question for counsel, not a web page.

How is this different from reading a law firm's client alert about BOI?

A client alert is written and reviewed by a licensed attorney, which is its real strength — this tool does not replace that either. The same date-drift risk applies: an alert written before FinCEN's August 14, 2026 effective date can still describe the March 2025 interim exemption as though it were provisional, when the August 2026 final rule made it permanent. This checker states which rule and effective date it applies on every result.
