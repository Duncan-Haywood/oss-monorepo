Do I still need to file a BOI report in 2026?

Short answer: if your company was formed in the United States, no — FinCEN's August 11, 2026 final rule (effective August 14, 2026) permanently ended the Corporate Transparency Act's beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting requirement for every U.S.-formed entity and every U.S. person, regardless of size or industry. The only exception is a foreign-formed entity registered to do business in a U.S. state or Tribal jurisdiction — that kind of company must still file. Why did this keep changing -- 2024, 2025, and now 2026?

The Corporate Transparency Act's reporting rule took effect January 1, 2024, requiring most U.S. companies to file. In March 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule exempting U.S.-formed entities and U.S. persons on an interim basis -- the exemption existed, but as agency policy that a future rulemaking or court order could still unwind. FinCEN's August 11, 2026 final rule (effective August 14, 2026) converted that interim exemption into a permanent one through formal rulemaking, which is a materially different legal footing even though the practical result -- no filing owed -- looks the same to most owners.

That is why a client alert or search result written any time before August 2026 may still describe the exemption as provisional rather than permanent, even though it was describing the correct practical outcome at the time.

I already filed a BOI report in 2024. Do I need to do anything now?

No. If your company was formed in the United States, FinCEN's August 2026 final rule permanently exempted it, so there is nothing left to file, update, or withdraw. A report you filed in 2024 does not need to be corrected, deleted, or resubmitted -- the exemption ends the obligation going forward, it does not undo the earlier filing.

My company was formed outside the U.S. Am I exempt too?

Only if it has not registered to do business in any U.S. state or Tribal jurisdiction. A foreign-formed entity that has registered to do business here is a "foreign reporting company" and the August 2026 rule did not exempt it -- it still must file, though only for beneficial owners who are not U.S. persons. That distinction — formed outside the U.S. versus registered to do business here — is the one detail that changes the answer, so run the specifics through the checker above rather than assuming from the general rule alone.

Could this reverse again?

It is legally harder to reverse than the 2025 interim rule was, because a final rule issued through notice-and-comment rulemaking cannot be undone with a single agency memo the way an interim rule can -- reversing it would need another full rulemaking or a court ruling. That is not the same as impossible. This page reflects the rule as of 2026-08-14; recheck FinCEN.gov before relying on an old answer, especially if you are reading this well after that date.
