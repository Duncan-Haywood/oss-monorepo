This calculator vs. doing it by hand

The "no tax on tips" and "no tax on overtime" deductions are arithmetic anyone can do with a calculator or worksheet. The problem is that each has a non-obvious step that a quick by-hand estimate tends to skip — the overtime deduction is only half the overtime rate, and the phase-out rounds up, not down.

Common questions

What's the most common mistake estimating the overtime deduction by hand?

Using the full time-and-a-half overtime rate instead of only the half-time premium. Qualified overtime compensation is just the extra 0.5x portion of the overtime rate over the regular rate -- someone earning $30/hr on overtime from a $20/hr base has $10/hr of qualified premium, not $30/hr. Using the full rate overstates the deduction by 3x.

What's the most common mistake with the income phase-out?

Forgetting it applies at all, or rounding the excess MAGI down to the nearest $1,000 instead of up. The statute reduces the deduction by $100 per $1,000 of MAGI over the threshold, 'or fraction thereof' -- so $150,001 of MAGI already costs the first $100, the same as $150,999 does. Rounding down understates the phase-out and overstates the deduction near a $1,000 boundary.

Can I just add up my full overtime pay and my tips and call it the deduction?

No. Tips are capped at $25,000 and overtime premium is capped separately at $12,500 (single/head of household) or $25,000 (joint) -- and both totals shrink independently once MAGI clears $150,000 single or $300,000 joint. Adding uncapped totals together overstates the deduction for anyone near or above either cap or threshold.

Does a self-employed tip earner get the full amount regardless of profit?

No. For self-employment income, the deduction cannot exceed net profit from the specific tipped trade or business, even if qualified tips received and the phased-out cap would otherwise allow more -- a common omission in a quick by-hand estimate that only looks at gross tips.

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page knows your full return -- other deductions, credits, self-employment tax, or state conformity.
