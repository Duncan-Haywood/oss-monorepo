    Common questions

    Why is a $165 toilet a $645 job?

    Because almost none of the cost is the fixture. On the defaults the toilet is $165 of a $644.71 price; the rest is 2.76 hours of loaded labor, a wax ring, a supply line, a $55 offset flange, the vehicle, overhead, and margin. This is the arithmetic to show a homeowner who has priced the same toilet at a big-box store and wants to know what the rest is for.

    Why is travel 1.11 hours for six miles?

    Because the trip is not just driving. The default allows a fixed turnout time plus a per-mile component, which together cover loading, parking, walking the fixture in, and getting back out — the real elapsed cost of attending a short job. On a 2.76-hour call, 40% of the paid time is arriving and leaving, which is exactly why a flat “one hour labor” quote loses money on small plumbing work.

    What is the $55 offset flange for, and do I always need one?

    No — it is in the defaults because it is the commonest surprise, not because every job needs it. It shifts the toilet slightly off the existing waste pipe centre when the new fixture rough-in does not match the old one, which happens most often on older homes with a 10-inch or 14-inch rough-in. Set it to zero when you have confirmed a matching 12-inch rough-in; leave it in when you are quoting sight-unseen.

    Is haul-away of the old toilet included?

    Not on the defaults, and neither is a bidet seat or a wax-free seal. Disposal is worth pricing rather than absorbing: many transfer stations charge for porcelain, and an old toilet left at the kerb is a complaint. Turn the option on when the customer expects the old unit to leave with you, which most do unless you have said otherwise in writing.
