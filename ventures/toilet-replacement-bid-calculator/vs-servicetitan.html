What each tool is for

ServiceTitan is a field service management platform, founded in 2007 by Ara Mahdessian and Vahe Kuzoyan, headquartered in Glendale, California. It serves HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and other trades with scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, marketing, reporting, and job costing across a whole business, with a mobile app for crews in the field. It is an account-based platform: you sign up and use it as an ongoing service across a whole company, not a one-off calculator. ServiceTitan's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact plan names or dollar figures, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — field service management software serving plumbing among other trades, sold as a subscription account — are stated, and no dollar amount for ServiceTitan appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what ServiceTitan does. It does not schedule techs, dispatch crews, or invoice a customer, and it stores no customer or job record on a server. It takes one toilet job's toilet count, scope (swap-only wax-and-set, new-supply with a new shutoff, flange-repair on a rotten closet, or a full reset), toilet type (two-piece, comfort-height, one-piece, or bidet combo), rough-in (10, 12, or 14 inch), optional haul-away, wax-free seal, and bidet seat, plus your crew wage, payroll burden, overhead, and target margin. It turns that into the price, the price per toilet, the extra-work share of the job — the part a flat installed-price special leaves out — the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money, plus, if you already have a price on the table, a check of whether it clears that floor. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Should I use this calculator or ServiceTitan?

They do different jobs, and a plumbing company of any size will likely end up touching both eventually. Does this calculator determine the code requirement or size the rough-in for me?

No. Hours and material rates are ballpark shop figures you can edit, not a plumbing-code determination or a fixture specification. Whether a flange needs repair, whether a rough-in reads 10, 12, or 14 inch, and what a given closet actually needs are still a judgment call for the licensed plumber on site. Verify every figure against your own takeoff and a licensed professional before you submit a bid.
