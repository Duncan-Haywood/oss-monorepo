This calculator vs. a flat $4/sq ft encapsulation special

“$4 a square foot” is not a price for a crawl space — it is a price for one specific scope and access, quietly applied to every crawl that calls in.

What a flat $4/sq ft encapsulation special is

A flat rate per square foot is the fastest way to quote: multiply the crawl's floor area by a number and read off a price. Homeowners shop it because it is easy to compare between bids, and it is the number a homeowner holding a competitor's flyer will ask you to match.

The number behind it was set for one crawl — a dry, walkable hatch crawl getting 6-mil liner-only, no wall liner, no dehumidifier — and then reused for every crawl afterward, whether or not the next one looks anything like the one the rate was built on.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the floor and stem-wall dimensions, scope (liner-only, sealed encapsulate, or encapsulate plus dehumidifier), access (hatch, tight belly-crawl, or wet), insulation, and extras such as clean-out, support piers, a dedicated circuit, and exterior grading, plus your own labor hours and material costs. It prices the liner, the access premium, and the dehumidifier separately by scope and access type, then returns the walk-away price and a bid price above your target margin — for this crawl, not a generic one.

What a flat $4/sq ft special doesWhat this calculator does instead
Charges the same $/sf whether the scope is 6-mil liner-only or a sealed encapsulation with a wall liner and a rim seal — even though a sealed encapsulation adds wall-liner hours and material that a dirt-only liner never touches. Prices floor liner and wall liner separately by scope, so a sealed encapsulation is never quoted off a liner-only rate.
Has no line for access difficulty, regardless of whether the crawl is a walkable 36-inch hatch or an 18-inch wet belly-crawl needing a pump-out first. Prices access hours and an access premium per square foot by access type — hatch, tight, or wet — so a wet belly-crawl costs more than an easy hatch job of the same footprint.
Has no line for a commercial dehumidifier, its install hours, or the dedicated circuit it needs, regardless of whether the crawl gets one. Prices the dehumidifier invoice, its install hours, and a dedicated-circuit line item separately when scoped, so a fully conditioned crawl costs more than a shell liner.
Has no line for clean-out, support piers, or exterior grading, regardless of how many the crawl needs. Prices clean-out yardage, piers, and grading separately when scoped, so a crawl with real extras is never priced the same as one without.
Gives the estimator a number to quote, not a number to check a quote against. Reports the walk-away price and the dollar gap against what a flat rate would have quoted the same crawl, so a shopped price can be scored against real cost before you commit to it.

Common questions

Why does a flat $4/sq ft encapsulation special get a crawl space quote wrong?

Because the cost driver is scope and access, not floor area alone. Liner-only on a dry, walkable hatch crawl needs a fraction of the labor and material that a sealed encapsulation with a wall liner, a rim seal, and a commercial dehumidifier needs on a wet, 18-inch belly crawl -- none of which scale with square footage the way a flat rate assumes. A $4/sq ft special charges both the same.

What does a flat $4/sq ft encapsulation special usually leave out entirely?

Access difficulty, wall liner, sealing, and a dehumidifier. A dry ranch a crew can stand up in is doing far less than a wet, 18-inch belly crawl that needs a pump-out first, a 20-mil wall liner, rim foam, a dedicated circuit, and a 90-pint dehumidifier running on a drain -- and a per-square-foot number has no line for any of it.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own shipped presets, at its own shipped shop rates ($32/hr crew wage, 30% burden, $95 travel and equipment, 15% overhead, a 20% target margin): a 1,120 sq ft dry ranch with hatch access and 6-mil liner-only costs $1,962.79 to build. The flat $4/sq ft special charges $4,480.00 for that crawl -- $2,517.21 over cost, and $2,026.51 over the calculator's own bid price. Applied to an 864 sq ft wet, tight belly-crawl needing sealed encapsulation, a 20-mil wall liner, rim foam, and a 90-pint dehumidifier, the same $4/sq ft special charges only $3,456.00 -- $5,808.20 below that crawl's own $9,264.20 walk-away cost, before a dollar of profit, and $8,124.24 below the calculator's own bid price.

Is a flat $4/sq ft encapsulation special ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of near-identical crawls -- same access, same scope, no dehumidifier either time -- a flat rate can land close, and on this calculator's own cheap-end preset it lands generous. The risk is applying it outside that band without noticing, because the rate does not know which crawl it was built on and neither does the estimator until access, scope, and extras are actually spelled out.

Does this calculator know what materials or labor cost in my market?

No. It has no data on local material or labor rates. It takes the crawl's floor and stem-wall dimensions, scope (liner-only, sealed encapsulate, or encapsulate plus dehumidifier), access (hatch, tight belly-crawl, or wet), insulation, and extras, plus your own labor hours and material costs, and turns that into a price for this crawl -- the arithmetic a flat rate skips, not a market survey.
