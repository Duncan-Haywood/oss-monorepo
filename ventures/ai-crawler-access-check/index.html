Common questions

Which AI crawlers does this check?

A curated list of 20 named user-agent tokens publicly documented by their operators: GPTBot, ChatGPT-User and OAI-SearchBot (OpenAI); ClaudeBot, Claude-User and Claude-SearchBot (Anthropic); Google-Extended (Google); Applebot-Extended (Apple); CCBot (Common Crawl); PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User (Perplexity); Bytespider (ByteDance); Amazonbot (Amazon); Meta-ExternalAgent, Meta-ExternalFetcher and FacebookBot (Meta); DuckAssistBot (DuckDuckGo); MistralAI-User (Mistral AI); Google-CloudVertexBot (Google); and Diffbot. It's a snapshot, not a live feed — operators add crawlers and change stated purposes over time, so verify anything load-bearing directly with the operator.

Does blocking a crawler here mean it actually obeys robots.txt?

No. robots.txt is a request, not an enforcement mechanism, and this tool only reports what your file says. Reputable operators state that they honor it for the tokens listed here, but nothing stops a request that lies about its own user-agent, and robots.txt cannot verify who is really asking.

What does "partially blocked" mean?

The crawler is allowed on some of the paths checked and blocked on others — most often because it falls through to your wildcard (User-agent: *) group, which usually blocks more than the root path.

Is my robots.txt uploaded anywhere?

No. The browser page parses the text you paste entirely in your own browser and never sends it anywhere. The CLI fetches only the domain you name, or reads a local file.

What is llms.txt?

An emerging, optional convention (see llmstxt.org) for a plain-text file at /llms.txt that gives an AI assistant a short, structured map of a site's key pages. This tool checks the basic shape the convention describes — an H1 title, an optional summary, and Markdown link sections — not full spec conformance.
