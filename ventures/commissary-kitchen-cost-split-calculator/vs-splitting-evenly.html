This calculator vs. splitting the kitchen bill evenly

Dividing a commissary kitchen's pooled monthly costs by the number of tenants is the simplest possible split, and it needs no calculator at all. The problem is that "simple" and "fair" are only the same thing when every tenant uses the kitchen about the same amount — and in a shared commissary, they usually don't.

Common questions

What's wrong with just splitting the pooled cost evenly across every tenant?

Nothing, if every tenant genuinely uses the kitchen about the same amount. It goes wrong the moment usage diverges: a tenant who books 40 hours a week and fills a walk-in shelf pays the same as one who stops in for 5 hours to prep, and the light user is quietly subsidizing the heavy one every month.

Why split by both hours and storage instead of just hours?

Because the two drive different costs. Booked hours track variable costs like utilities and staff oversight; storage units track a more fixed draw on the walk-in and freezer space regardless of how many hours a tenant is actually cooking. A tenant who rents a full walk-in shelf but rarely cooks on-site would pay almost nothing under an hours-only split, which is why the blended method exists.

How does this avoid the rounding errors a spreadsheet split usually has?

Every dollar figure is tracked in integer cents internally, and the proportional shares are allocated with a largest-remainder method: each tenant's ideal share is rounded to the nearest cent, then any leftover or shortfall cent is assigned to the tenants whose rounded share moved furthest from their ideal one. A plain spreadsheet formula that rounds each share independently can leave a few cents of the pool unaccounted for; this calculator's tenant totals always sum exactly to the pool total.

Does this replace a written shared-kitchen or tenant agreement?

No. This calculator only does the arithmetic on the costs, hours, and storage figures you enter. It does not draft, review, or enforce any agreement between the commissary operator and its tenants — that stays a conversation and a contract between the people involved.
