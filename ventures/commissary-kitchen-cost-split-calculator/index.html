Common questions

How should a commissary kitchen split costs among food trucks?

By usage, not headcount. A tenant who books 40 hours a week and fills a walk-in shelf is using far more of the pooled rent, utilities, and cleaning than one who stops in for 5 hours to prep. Splitting by hours booked and storage units used charges each tenant roughly what their footprint actually costs; an even split charges the light user for the heavy user's share every month.

What if one tenant barely uses the kitchen?

Under an hours- or storage-based split, a tenant with very little booked time and no storage owes very little of the proportional pool — their bill is close to just the flat base fee, if one is charged. That is the point of splitting by usage instead of by tenant count: a light user's bill should look light.

Is an equal split ever fair?

When usage is genuinely similar — a handful of trucks each booking roughly the same hours and storage — an equal split is fair and simpler to explain, and the equal-split option here exists for exactly that case. It stops being fair the moment one tenant's usage diverges meaningfully from the rest, which is the case a usage-based split is for.

Why do the default blend weights favor hours over storage?

70% hours / 30% storage is a starting point, not a rule, chosen because booked kitchen time is usually the larger driver of a commissary's variable cost (utilities, cleaning, staff oversight) while storage is a smaller, more fixed draw. Every kitchen's cost mix is different — the two weights are inputs you can set to whatever split reflects your own costs.

Does anything about my tenants or costs get uploaded?

No. Every number on this page runs in your browser only. Nothing is sent to a server, there is no account, and nothing about your costs, tenants, hours, or storage is ever saved or uploaded. Usage counting only tallies anonymous feature events, and you can turn it off with the button below.
