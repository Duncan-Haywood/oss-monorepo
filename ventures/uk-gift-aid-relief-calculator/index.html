Common questions

    
What is Gift Aid?

    
Gift Aid lets a charity claim 25p for every £1 you give, because your gift is treated as made after basic-rate tax. A £80 gift is worth £100 to the charity.

    
Can I get tax relief as a higher-rate taxpayer?

    
Yes. You can claim back the difference between your tax rate and the 20% the charity reclaims: 20% of the gross gift at 40%, and 25% at 45%. You claim it through Self Assessment or a tax code change, not from the charity.

    
Can a gift help with the £100,000 income trap?

    
Yes. Gift Aid gifts reduce adjusted net income, so between £100,000 and £125,140 a gift also restores personal allowance, giving effective relief of up to 40% of the gross gift on top of the 20% the charity claims.

    
Do I have to pay enough tax?

    
Yes. Your income tax and capital gains tax for the year must cover the tax the charity reclaims on all your Gift Aid donations, or you may owe the shortfall.

    
Can I treat a gift as made last year?

    
Yes. You can elect on your Self Assessment return to carry a gift back to the previous tax year, if that year gives more relief.

    
Which tax year and nations does this cover?

    
2026/27 rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scottish income tax bands differ and are not modelled; check GOV.UK.
