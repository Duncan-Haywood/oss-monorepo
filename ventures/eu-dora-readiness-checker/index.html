What it does, and what it leaves to you

    
    
      
  • Scope: DORA lists the financial entities it covers (Article 2). It has applied since 17 January 2025.
    • 
      
  • Duty areas: ICT risk management (Arts 5 to 16), incident handling and reporting (Arts 17 to 23), resilience testing (Arts 24 to 27) and third-party risk (Arts 28 to 44).
    • 
      
  • Contracts: the Article 30 terms every ICT contract must carry, with extra terms for critical or important functions.
    • 
      
  • Incident clocks: initial notification within 4 hours of classification and at most 24 hours from awareness, intermediate within 72 hours, final within one month, as set by the incident-reporting technical standards.
    • 
      
  • Not decided: whether an incident is major under the classification thresholds, your supervisor's reporting channel and weekend rules, national add-ons, whether you are designated critical, and whether your controls are good enough, only whether they exist.

Common questions

    
Does DORA apply to my company?

    
DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) applies since 17 January 2025 to banks, payment and e-money institutions, investment firms, insurers, crypto-asset service providers, fund managers, trading venues and a list of other financial entities. It does not apply directly to small insurance intermediaries. Software and cloud vendors are not regulated directly unless designated critical, but their financial-sector customers must put DORA terms in the contract.

    
How fast must a major ICT incident be reported?

    
The initial notification is due within 4 hours of classifying the incident as major and no later than 24 hours after becoming aware of it. The intermediate report follows within 72 hours of the initial one, and the final report, with root cause, within one month of the last intermediate report.

    
What must a contract with an ICT provider contain?

    
Article 30 lists the terms: service and subcontracting description, data locations, data protection, data return on insolvency or exit, service levels, incident assistance, cooperation with authorities, termination rights and training. For critical or important functions it adds full SLAs, contingency testing, TLPT cooperation, unrestricted audit and access rights and an exit plan with a transition period.

    
I sell software to banks. What do I need to do?

    
Expect your customers to send DORA addenda. Use the provider mode here to see which Article 30 terms your contracts still lack, so you can answer a security questionnaire from your own list instead of from a stranger's.

No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls. Do not type real incident details; the clocks only need times.
