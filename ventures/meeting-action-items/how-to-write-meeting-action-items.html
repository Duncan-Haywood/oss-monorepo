How to write meeting action items that get done

Most meetings end with everyone nodding and nobody knowing who does what. An action item that gets done has one owner, one verb and one date. The rest of this page is how to get there, with an example you can check against the tool.

1. One owner, a person not a team

"Marketing will look at it" is owned by nobody. Name one person. If two people share a job, write two items, each with an owner, so each can be ticked off separately.

2. Start with a verb

"Safari toggle" is a topic. "Fix the Safari toggle" is a task: the reader can tell when it is finished.

3. Write the date, not the weekday

"By Friday" means a different day depending on when someone reads the notes. Resolve it against the meeting date: said on Monday 2026-10-19, "by Friday" is 2026-10-23, and "next Friday" is 2026-10-30.

4. Keep decisions and open questions apart

A decision ("keep the free tier through December") needs no follow-up. An open question ("who owns the refund wording?") is an action item waiting for an owner. Mixing them hides the second kind.

Worked example

Raw notes from a Monday sync (meeting date 2026-10-19):

Ben: I'll fix the Safari toggle by Wednesday.
Ana: We decided to keep the free tier through December.
Cleo will book the venue for the offsite next Friday.
We need to update the onboarding checklist before the webinar.

5. Send it the same day

Group the list by person and email it before anyone forgets the meeting. Paste your notes into the free tool to get that email, a CSV and calendar entries. Nothing is uploaded.
