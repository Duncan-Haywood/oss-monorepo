How much of your health insurance can you deduct in 2026?

For a sole proprietor or single-member LLC: the deduction for the health, dental, and long-term-care premiums you pay is the smaller of your premiums and your earned income from the business — net profit minus half of self-employment tax minus your own retirement contribution — and it is zero for any month you or your spouse could have joined an employer-subsidized plan.

Common questions

What is the self-employed health insurance deduction?

It is an adjustment to income (Form 1040 Schedule 1, line 17, figured on Form 7206) for health, dental, and qualifying long-term-care premiums you pay for yourself, your spouse, and your dependents when you have net profit from your own business. It lowers income tax but not self-employment tax, and you do not need to itemize.

What limits the deduction?

The deduction cannot exceed earned income from the business under which the plan is established: net profit minus the deductible half of self-employment tax minus your own SEP, SIMPLE, or Solo 401(k) contribution. It is also zero for any month you or your spouse were eligible for an employer-subsidized health plan, even if you did not enroll.

How is self-employment tax figured here?

On 92.35% of net profit: 12.4% Social Security up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 (less any W-2 Social Security wages already taxed), plus 2.9% Medicare with no cap. Half of the total is the deductible part. The additional 0.9% Medicare tax is not modelled, and there is no self-employment tax when net earnings are under $400.

What are the 2026 long-term-care premium limits?

Per person, by age before the end of 2026: $500 at 40 or younger, $930 at 41 to 50, $1,860 at 51 to 60, $4,960 at 61 to 70, and $6,200 at 71 or older (Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Only the part of a premium up to the limit counts.

Does the retirement contribution create a circular calculation?

Yes. A SEP or Solo 401(k) contribution depends on plan compensation, and the deduction depends on that contribution. This tool does not solve that loop: it subtracts the retirement contribution you enter, so use the amount your plan computation actually produced.
