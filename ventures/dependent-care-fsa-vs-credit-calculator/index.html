How it is calculated

FSA: each dollar you elect saves your federal marginal rate plus your state rate plus the payroll tax rate you enter. Credit: expenses up to the cap, less your FSA election, times a rate that starts at 50%, drops one point for each $2,000 of AGI over $15,000 to 35%, then one more point per $2,000 over $75,000 ($150,000 joint) to 20%. FSA dollars lower AGI. Both are limited to the lower earner's income. The tool tries every election in $50 steps plus the maximum and shows the best.

Not modelled: state credits, married filing separately, employer plan limits, grace periods, provider and qualifying-person rules, and bracket edges. Unverified: checked by hand calculation and unit tests only. Have a tax professional review your situation.

Common questions

Should I use a dependent care FSA or the child care tax credit?

It depends on your marginal tax rate. The FSA saves your federal, state and payroll tax rates on every dollar you set aside, up to $7,500. The credit saves 20% to 50% of up to $3,000 (one child) or $6,000 (two or more) of expenses. Higher earners usually win with the FSA; lower earners often win with the credit. This tool compares them with your numbers.

Can I use both the FSA and the credit?

Yes, but not on the same dollars. Every dollar you run through the FSA reduces the expenses that count for the credit, dollar for dollar. Because the credit cap is only $3,000 or $6,000 while the FSA limit is $7,500, a large FSA election can use up the credit entirely. The tool tests every split and shows the best one.

What changed for 2026?

Federal law raised the dependent care FSA limit to $7,500 per household starting in 2026 and increased the child and dependent care credit rate for tax years after 2025, up to 50% at low incomes with a 20% floor at high incomes. Check the current year's figures with a tax professional or the IRS before relying on this.

Why is the lower earner's income asked for?

Both the FSA exclusion and the credit are limited to the earned income of the lower-earning spouse. If that income is smaller than your care costs, only that amount counts. Leave it blank if it is higher than your expenses.

Is the credit refundable?

No. It can only reduce your federal income tax to zero. If you know your tax before credits, enter it and the tool limits the credit to it. The FSA saves tax whether or not you owe income tax.

What is not modelled?

State credits, married filing separately, employer plans with lower limits, a grace period or carryover, care paid to relatives who cannot be claimed, the qualifying-person and provider rules, and tax at bracket edges. It applies flat rates you type in.
