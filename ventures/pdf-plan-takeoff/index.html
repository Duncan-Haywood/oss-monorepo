Why the scale check matters

Every takeoff number is a measured line times a scale, so a wrong scale is wrong on every line of the estimate. The two usual ways it goes wrong: a sheet with several views at different scales (a plan at 1/4" and a detail at 1-1/2" on the same page), and a set printed at a size other than the one it was drawn for. An 11x17 print of a 22x34 set still says 1/4" = 1'-0" under every view, but every line is half as long, so lengths come out half and areas a quarter. This tool picks the note for the view each measurement sits in, and it reads the drafter's own dimension strings: a 24' - 0" dimension labels a line, and the line's length on the sheet says what scale the sheet is really at. When most dimensions agree with twice the printed scale, it says so and corrects for it.

How it works

The PDF is read in this tab with no library: the drawing's paths are flattened to line segments in the sheet's own coordinates (points, 1/72 inch), and its text comes out with positions. Clicks snap to segment ends, midpoints and the nearest point on a line. Scale notes are read in architectural (3/16" = 1'-0"), engineering (1" = 20') and metric (1:100) forms. A dimension's span is measured between the witness lines that cross its dimension line, not to the line's ends, since most programs carry the line a little past them. Fill area rasterises the line work around your click and fills the region it closes, ignoring curves (door swings, fixtures) so a door drawn with its leaf closes its opening; the filled region is shown so you can see what was measured.

How it was checked

On 14 public drawing sets (180 pages of Revit sheets from open architecture projects on GitHub) it read 397 scale notes on 142 pages and matched 1,385 dimension strings to their lines. On 47 pages the dimensions settled the scale: 44 agreed with a printed note and 3 pages with no note named one (1/8", 1/8" and 3/16"). Shrunk to half size, 43 of the 44 were flagged as half-size prints and none of the 180 originals was; the one miss is a sheet whose two views are at 1/4" and 1/2", where a half-size 1/2" view looks exactly like a full-size 1/4" one. The fill-area tool did poorly on the same sets: most of their plans are image tiles with dimension lines crossing the rooms, and only 5 of 298 rooms Revit tagged with an area came within 2% of the tag. Questions

How is this different from Bluebeam, PlanSwift or On-Screen Takeoff?

Those are full estimating desktops with a license fee per seat; most read the scale only when you set it per page. This is a free browser tool for the measuring step: it reads the scale notes for you, checks them against the dimensions, and exports a CSV you can paste into the estimate you already keep. It does not price, assemble or keep a project database.

Does it work on scanned drawings?

It shows a rasterised sheet's line work and you can measure on it after calibrating with two clicks on a known dimension, but snapping and the scale check need the vector line work and text that a CAD or BIM export has. JPEG-compressed scans are read with the browser's own image decoder.

Is my drawing uploaded?

No. The PDF is read by code running in this tab; nothing is sent anywhere.

Can I run it on my own machine?

Yes. It is MIT licensed and has no dependencies: node cli.mjs set.pdf prints every sheet's scale notes and the dimension check. Source on GitHub.
