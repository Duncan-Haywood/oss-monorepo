How to track retainage in QuickBooks Online

QuickBooks Online can bill a job in progress, but it has no retainage field on an invoice. The method answers on Intuit's own community forum keep giving is a Retainage Receivable account and a Retainage item: a negative line holds the money back each month, and a positive line on a later invoice bills it when it is released. This page sets that up step by step, works one subcontract through it, and lists the three places it goes wrong.

Before you start

  • Accrual or cash? The method below is written for books kept on the accrual basis. Intuit describes the Retainage detail type as one used in construction when income is recorded on an accrual basis (Intuit community). If you file on the cash basis, read the cash-basis trap first and ask your accountant.
  • Progress invoicing (several partial invoices from one estimate) is on the Plus and Advanced plans, under Settings → Account and settings → Sales → Progress Invoicing (Intuit help). You do not need it for retainage: a plain invoice per pay app works too.
  • Menu names change. We read Intuit's pages on 2026-11-25 through search results, not inside a live QuickBooks company, so check each label against your own screen.

Set it up once

  1. Create the account. Settings (gear) → Chart of accounts → New. Account type Other Current Assets, detail type Retainage, name it Retainage Receivable. Intuit's guide to detail types describes Retainage as the type to use when customers routinely hold back part of the contract amount until the project is finished (Intuit help).
  2. Create the item. Sales → Products and services → New → Service (or Non-inventory). Name it Retainage, tick "I sell this product/service to my customers", and choose Retainage Receivable as its income account. Leave the price blank; it changes every invoice (Intuit community).
  3. Make the item non-taxable if you charge sales tax on invoices, so the holdback does not reduce the tax line. Whether retainage is taxed when billed or when released depends on your state: ask your accountant.

Each pay application

  1. Bill the work completed (and stored materials, if your contract pays for them) with your normal sales items, either one line per schedule-of-values line or one line for the period.
  2. On the next line, choose the Retainage item and enter the amount held back as a negative number. QuickBooks will not work out a percentage for you; the calculator below does (Intuit community).
  3. The invoice total is now the current payment due on your pay application. Check it matches before you send it.

Behind the scenes, on accrual books, the full value of the work goes to income, the payment due goes to accounts receivable, and the holdback goes to Retainage Receivable, where it waits.

When retainage is released

Raise a new invoice to the same customer and project with the Retainage item as a positive line for the amount released. No new income is booked, because it was booked when the work was billed: the amount moves from Retainage Receivable to accounts receivable, and the customer's payment clears it like any other invoice. A partial release (say, at substantial completion) is the same with a smaller amount.

Release usually comes with paperwork: a final conditional lien waiver, close-out documents, sometimes consent of surety. See lien waivers and how release works.

Worked example

A $150,000 electrical subcontract with 10% retainage, billed in three pay apps, then released.

InvoiceWork lineRetainage lineInvoice totalRetainage Receivable after
Pay app 1$40,000.00-$4,000.00$36,000.00$4,000.00
Pay app 2$50,000.00-$5,000.00$45,000.00$9,000.00
Pay app 3$60,000.00-$6,000.00$54,000.00$15,000.00
Retainage release$15,000.00$15,000.00$0.00
Totals$150,000.00$0.00$150,000.00

Income for the job is $150,000 by the end of pay app 3, and the customer pays $150,000 in all. Until the release invoice is sent, $15,000 is real money owed to you that sits outside accounts receivable.

Three places it goes wrong

1. The A/R aging report leaves retainage out

Because retainage sits in an asset account rather than accounts receivable, the standard A/R aging report does not show it, and it is easy to forget to bill. To see what is owed: open Retainage Receivable from the chart of accounts, or run Transaction Detail by Account and filter it to that account (Intuit community). Filter or group by customer and project to see retainage per job. Put each job's release conditions and expected date in a list you review every month; the free retainage tracker ages that list for you, flags completed jobs with no release billed and totals it by GC.

2. The cash-basis trap

On a cash-basis company, users report that a retainage item mapped to a balance-sheet account makes the cash-basis profit and loss show income before the retainage is paid (Intuit community). The answers on Intuit's forums disagree on the fix: some map the item to an income account instead, others put retainage on a separate invoice. Which is right depends on how you file. This is a question for your accountant, not a setting to guess.

3. Retainage you hold on your own subs

If you pay lower-tier subcontractors and hold retainage from them, that is the mirror image: money you owe, which belongs in a liability account (Retainage Payable) and comes off their bills, not your invoices. Do not mix the two in one account.

QuickBooks Desktop

Desktop uses the same idea: a retainage account and an item tied to it, entered as a negative amount on the progress invoice, with any percentage worked out by hand (Intuit community). Progress invoicing is turned on under Edit → Preferences → Jobs & Estimates → Company Preferences (Intuit community, answered for a Premier edition).
