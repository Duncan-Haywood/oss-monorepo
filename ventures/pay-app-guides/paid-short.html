Paid short: when the GC pays less than your pay application asked for

The check came in and it is less than line 8. On a commercial job that gap almost always has one of three causes, and each is handled differently: the GC certified less than you applied for (a cut to the application), the GC deducted a backcharge from the payment, or the GC is holding money for something it has not said. This page separates the three, tells you what to ask, and lists the lien waiver amounts that match the money you actually received.

Lien waivers that match the money

    Email to the GC

    The example: an application for $45,000.00 that the GC certified at $42,000.00, with a $1,800.00 cleanup backcharge and one check for $38,000.00. Of the $7,000.00 short, $3,000.00 is the cut, $1,800.00 the backcharge and $2,200.00 has no reason given. The arithmetic is the whole tool: it does not read your subcontract.

    The three causes, and what each one needs

    • The certified amount is lower. The GC (or the architect above it) reduced your percent complete on some lines, or did not accept stored materials. Nothing is "deducted": the application was approved for less. Ask which lines and why, agree the numbers, and the difference is billed on next month's application as work completed. Your continuation sheet should then match what was certified, so the next "previous applications" column is right (how the columns carry forward).
    • A backcharge was deducted. The GC took money off for its own costs: cleanup, damage to other work, a crew it sent to finish your scope, a delay it says you caused. Ask for each in writing with the backup. Most subcontracts have a backcharge clause, and many require the GC to give notice and a chance to fix the problem before it does the work itself and charges you. Read yours; if the notice was not given, say so in writing.
    • Money is held with no reason. Often a missing document: a lien waiver from you or your supplier, a certificate of insurance, certified payroll, a sworn statement. Ask what is needed; it is usually paid once it goes in. If nothing is missing and the money is simply late, your state's prompt payment law may set a deadline and interest (by state), and the subcontract's payment clause decides whether the owner's payment is a condition (pay-if-paid or pay-when-paid).

    Don't waive what you were not paid

    A GC paying short will often still ask for an unconditional waiver for the whole application. An unconditional waiver usually gives up lien rights for the amount it states whether or not the money arrived (conditional vs unconditional). Give one for each payment you actually received, for that amount, once it has cleared; keep the balance under a conditional waiver or out of the waiver altogether. Some states fix the waiver wording by statute, so check the form for your state.

    In your books: close it, don't let it age

    Once you know a balance will not be paid, record it as what it is. A backcharge you accept is a reduction of the contract income on that job; a balance you will not collect is a write-off; a balance you still dispute is a claim you track until it is settled. Leaving it as "owed" on the aging report hides the real cash position and makes the next follow-up call about money that is not coming. In QuickBooks that is usually a credit memo against the pay application's invoice; ask your accountant which account it should hit.

    Common questions

    Why did the GC pay less than my pay application asked for?

    Usually one of three causes: the application was certified for less than you applied for, the GC deducted a backcharge, or the GC is holding money without saying why, often for a missing document such as a lien waiver, certificate of insurance, certified payroll or sworn statement.

    What lien waiver should I sign after a short payment?

    One for each payment you actually received, for that amount, once it has cleared. Keep the unpaid balance under a conditional waiver or out of the waiver altogether, and check whether your state fixes the waiver wording by statute.

    How do I record a short payment in my books?

    As what it is: a backcharge you accept reduces the job's contract income, a balance you will not collect is a write-off, and a balance you still dispute is a claim you track until it is settled. In QuickBooks that is usually a credit memo against the pay application's invoice; ask your accountant which account it should hit.
