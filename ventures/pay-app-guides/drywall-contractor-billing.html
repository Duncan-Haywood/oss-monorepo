Pay applications for drywall and framing contractors

Drywall, metal stud framing, taping and finishing, and acoustical ceiling subs bill commercial work on a monthly pay application. The work moves floor by floor in clear phases (framing, board, tape, finish), board is stocked on floors ahead of hanging, and extras come up constantly from other trades' changes. This page covers how to bill each part and where drywall subs most often lose money, with the free guide or tool for each.

Where drywall subs lose money on pay apps

  • Extras that never become change orders. Drywall picks up more small extras than most trades. Each unsigned one is easy for the GC to deny later. Same-day tickets fix that.
  • Back charges accepted without paper. Ask for each in writing with its basis before it comes off the check, and keep it on your change order log as a deduct you can dispute.
  • Finishing underfunded. Loading framing and board leaves tape and finish, the slowest phase, with too little value. Check the SOV's balance.
  • Overbilling that catches up. Billing ahead of cost feels good until the last floors. Compare billed to cost-to-cost percent complete each month.

Common questions

How should a drywall contractor set up the schedule of values?

By floor or area and by phase (framing, board, tape and finish, ceilings, punch), with stocked material on its own line. Each month's percent then comes from what is done on each floor.

Can I bill for drywall board delivered to the floor but not hung?

Often yes, as stored materials, if your subcontract allows it; attach delivery tickets and photos. Move it to work completed as it is hung.

How do I dispute a back charge on a pay application?

Ask for it in writing with its basis (who, what, when, cost), compare it with your subcontract's back-charge clause, and answer in writing. Keep it on your log so it is not forgotten when the next check arrives.

How do I get paid for extra work caused by other trades?

Write a T&M ticket or change order request the day it happens and get it signed by the GC's superintendent, then bill it once approved.
