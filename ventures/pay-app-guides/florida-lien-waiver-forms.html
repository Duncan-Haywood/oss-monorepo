Florida lien waiver forms

Florida sets the wording of lien waivers in statute (Fla. Stat. § 713.20). How strict: Forms "may" be used, but no one may require a different form. Below are both statutory forms, word for word, with a blank line wherever the statute leaves one. Print the one you need, fill it in by hand, and keep a copy.

What Florida asks of you

  • "A right to claim a lien may not be waived in advance", and a waiver reaches only work already furnished (§ 713.20(2)).
  • "A person may not require a lienor to furnish a lien waiver or release of lien that is different from the forms" (§ 713.20(6)). If the GC's form is different, you can ask for the statutory one.
  • But a waiver you do sign that is not like the statutory forms "is enforceable in accordance with the terms" (§ 713.20(8)). Read it before you sign it.
  • You may condition a waiver given for a check on the check being paid (§ 713.20(7)).
  • Neither statutory form says "conditional": each "hereby waives and releases" the lien "in consideration of" the payment, and the statute gives no wording for the condition that (7) allows. The progress form "does not cover any retention or labor, services, or materials furnished after the date specified" (§ 713.20(4)); the final form has no such exception (§ 713.20(5)). So if you sign the progress form before the check clears, it is the condition you add, not the form, that protects you; ask your attorney how to word it.

Notes on the forms

  • Florida: a waiver or release given in exchange for, or to induce, a progress or final payment may be in substantially the form of § 713.20(4) or (5). No one may require a lienor to furnish a waiver different from those forms (§ 713.20(6)); a waiver that is not substantially similar is enforceable by its own terms (§ 713.20(8)).
  • A lien right may not be waived in advance and may be waived only to the extent of labor, services or materials furnished (§ 713.20(2)).
  • The progress form does not cover retention or anything furnished after the date given (§ 713.20(4)).
  • A lienor who signs a waiver in exchange for a check may condition it on payment of the check (§ 713.20(7)); this page does not print condition wording.

The undersigned lienor, in consideration of the sum of Sum, hereby waives and releases its lien and right to claim a lien for labor, services, or materials furnished through Furnished Through to Name of Your Customer on the job of Name of the Owner to the following property:

Common questions

Does Florida have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes. Fla. Stat. § 713.20 sets 2 forms: progress and final. How strict: Forms "may" be used, but no one may require a different form. The forms on this page are printed word for word from the statute as read on 2026-11-25.

Which Florida lien waiver form goes with which payment?

Waiver and Release of Lien upon Progress Payment: unconditional progress waivers. Waiver and Release of Lien upon Final Payment: unconditional final waivers. Florida's statute has no separate conditional waiver form. Fla. Stat. § 713.20(4) and (5) give one form for a progress payment and one for the final payment, each a waiver and release given in consideration of the payment, with no condition in its text; this page lists those two as the unconditional waivers. § 713.20(7) lets a lienor who signs one in exchange for a check condition it on payment of the check, but the statute gives no wording for that condition, and this page does not supply any. Before signing a Florida waiver ahead of payment, ask counsel.

When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver in Florida?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared your bank. Before that, give the conditional form if Florida has one, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed amounts out of a progress waiver.
