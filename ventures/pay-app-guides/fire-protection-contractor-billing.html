Pay applications for fire sprinkler and fire protection: design, rough-in, the hydro test and the AHJ's acceptance

Fire sprinkler pay applications bill work that has to pass someone other than the GC: the layout is approved by the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) before pipe goes up, the piping is pressure tested in front of an inspector, and the last money usually waits for the fire marshal's final acceptance. Heads are set months after the mains, once the ceiling grid is in. This page covers each step, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where fire sprinkler subs lose money on pay apps

  • Pipe and heads in the shop, not on the bill. Material bought for a long job is cash spent months ahead. If the subcontract allows stored materials, bill them with the backup it asks for, or ask for that right before you sign.
  • Heads moved for free. Ceiling, light and duct changes move heads late in the job. A head relocated on a verbal OK is the extra most often never paid; a ticket signed the same day is the record.
  • Retests nobody priced. Pipe cut, hit or left open by another trade means another test in front of the inspector. Photograph it and get a ticket signed before the retest.
  • Painted or damaged heads. A head painted over or knocked by another trade has to be replaced, not cleaned. Write it up with photos as a back charge to whoever caused it, rather than swapping it at your cost.
  • Retainage waiting on someone else's inspection. The last money often waits for the fire marshal, the fire alarm tie-in or another trade's punch. Track the retainage per job and ask in writing what is still open.

Common questions

How should a fire sprinkler contractor set up the schedule of values?

By building, floor or area and by phase: design and permit, underground, mains and branch lines (rough-in), heads and drops after the ceiling (trim), the riser, fire pump and standpipes where they are in your scope, then testing, acceptance and close-out on lines of their own. Small lines let the GC check each month's percent from the floor.

Can I bill for design and hydraulic calculations before any pipe is installed?

Usually, if design is in your scope and has its own line: bill it when the drawings and calculations are approved by the AHJ, not when they are sent. Keep the line modest; a large design line is the first thing a reviewer reads as front-loading.

When do I bill the hydrostatic test?

When it is done and passed, ideally on a testing line of its own. Many jurisdictions' forms for the Contractor's Material and Test Certificate (for example Philadelphia's) state the aboveground test as at least 200 psi for two hours, or 50 psi above static pressure where that is higher; a retest caused by another trade's damage is extra work on a signed ticket.

Why is my final sprinkler retainage held?

Usually because acceptance or close-out is not complete: the AHJ's final inspection, the signed test certificates, as-builts, the owner's manuals and spare heads, or a final lien waiver. Ask the GC in writing which items are open and send them together.
