Pay application software for subcontractors, compared

A specialty subcontractor bills most commercial jobs once a month: a schedule of values, the work done this period, stored materials, retainage held, and a lien waiver for each payment. You can do that in a spreadsheet, in a contractor suite, in a billing tool built for subs, or inside the general contractor's own payment portal, and some GCs charge you for the portal whatever else you use. This page lays out the options with their published prices, so you can see what the monthly paperwork is costing you.

The GC's portal: a fee on your subcontract even if you buy nothing

On many commercial jobs you do not choose the billing software. The GC tells you to submit through its portal, and some portals charge the subcontractor. These are the rules the portal vendors publish for their US service, read on 2026-11-25 through search results limited to their own help sites. Each GC sets up its own projects, so ask yours which rule applies before you price the job.

What to ask before you choose

  • How many pay apps a month, across how many GCs? Under five a month, a good spreadsheet is hard to beat. Past that, the time spent retyping previous-period columns and chasing waivers is the real cost.
  • Which portals do your GCs require? If most of your work goes through one portal, its fee and its forms set the floor. A billing tool still helps if it keeps your own record of every application across GCs.
  • Retainage by line, and its release. Check that the tool tracks retainage per line and per job and shows what is still held, since QuickBooks Online does not (why).
  • Change orders into the schedule of values. Approved changes should become billable lines without retyping; pending ones should not be billable at all (change order log).
  • Lien waivers that match your state. Several states require their statutory wording exactly (which states).
  • What you keep if you stop paying. Ask whether you can still open and export every past pay application after cancelling. Billing records outlive the job by years.
