Change order log for subcontractors

Paste your change order log, or start from the example. The page works out the revised contract sum, how much is pending and at risk and how long it has waited, flags billing on unsigned or rejected changes, and writes the approved change orders as schedule-of-values lines for next month's pay application.

Your log stays in this browser. It does the arithmetic and checks; your contract decides what may be billed and when notice is due.

How the numbers are worked out

  
    
    
  • Revised contract sum = original contract + approved additions + approved deductions. Pending, rejected and void change orders do not count.
    • 
    
  • If every pending change order is approved = revised contract + pending. The difference is money you may have performed and not yet secured.
    • 
    
  • Waiting = days from the submitted date to the as-of date, for pending change orders. Days to approve = approved date − submitted date.
    • 
    
  • Checks: anything billed on a pending, rejected or void change order; billing outside zero to the approved amount; pending changes past your flag; pending additions above 10% of the original contract; repeated numbers; approved with no approval date.
    • 
    
  • Not decided here: whether a change is owed, notice and claim deadlines in your subcontract, markup limits, and what your state's lien waiver forms allow.
    • 
  






  
Common questions

  
What goes in a change order log?

  
One row per change: its number, a description, its status (pending, approved, rejected or void), the amount, any days of schedule time it asks for, the date it was submitted, the date it was approved, and what has been billed against it.

  
Can I bill a pending change order on my pay application?

  
Usually not. Most pay applications only carry executed change orders, so pending work is tracked in the log and billed the month it is signed. Some subcontracts allow billing work approved in principle; check yours.

  
How do approved change orders get onto the schedule of values?

  
Each approved change order becomes its own line, CO-1, CO-2 and so on, with its full amount as the scheduled value; a deductive change order is a negative line. This page writes those lines for you in the columns the free pay application generator reads.

  
Worked example: change orders and the schedule of values.

  
How do I follow up on a change order the GC has not signed?

  
In writing, to the person who approves change orders on the job (usually the GC's project manager, not the billing contact), restating the number, the scope, the price and any time it asks for, and asking what is needed to sign it. Note the date of each follow-up next to the date submitted, so the log shows how long it has waited and how often you asked. Your subcontract may set how long the GC has to answer, a notice period for claims, or what happens if nobody answers; check it, because this page does not.
