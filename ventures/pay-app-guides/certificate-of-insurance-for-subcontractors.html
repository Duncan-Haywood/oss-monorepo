Certificates of insurance for subcontractors: what the GC checks, and why one gets sent back

Before you start work, and again every time a policy renews, the GC asks for a certificate of insurance (COI), almost always on the ACORD 25 form your agent fills in. The GC's compliance person compares it with the insurance exhibit in your subcontract. A certificate that falls short gets sent back, and on many jobs a missing or lapsed one also stops your pay application. This guide covers what is on the form, the four things exhibits usually ask for, the paper that proves each one, and how to keep renewals from catching you out.

The certificate is a summary, not the coverage

The ACORD 25 says, in its own top box, that it is issued as a matter of information only, confers no rights on the certificate holder, and does not amend, extend or alter the coverage of the policies it lists. Its notice box adds that if the certificate holder is an additional insured, the policy must provide for that or be endorsed, and that a waiver of subrogation may need an endorsement too (Wellesley College, how to read an ACORD certificate; a sample ACORD 25 filed with the Texas Department of Insurance).

So ticking "ADDL INSD" or typing "GC is additional insured" in the description box proves nothing by itself. A careful GC asks for the endorsement pages as well, and a certificate sent alone is the most common reason one comes back.

The ACORD 25, box by box

BoxWhat the GC reads in it
ProducerYour agent or broker, who issued the certificate. The GC calls them, not you, to check it.
InsuredYour company's legal name. It should match the name on the subcontract; a trade name or a sister company is a common mismatch.
Insurers A to FEach insurance company, by letter. Some exhibits set a minimum insurer rating; the GC looks the companies up.
CoveragesOne row per policy: general liability, auto, umbrella or excess, workers' compensation and employer's liability, and others. Each has a policy number, effective and expiry dates, and limits.
ADDL INSD and SUBR WVDColumns marking which policies carry additional insured status and a waiver of subrogation. A tick is a claim; the endorsement is the proof.
Description of operationsWhere the agent names the project and the parties the exhibit lists (owner, GC, architect) and the endorsements that apply to them.
Certificate holderThe GC, by its exact legal name and address. One certificate per GC, so each GC that holds one needs the renewal.
CancellationHow notice of cancellation is given under the policy's own terms. The certificate does not promise the holder a notice the policy does not give.

The four things an insurance exhibit usually asks for

Exhibits differ, and yours is the one that counts. These are the requests that come up again and again, with the standard ISO endorsement form an agent usually uses. Your insurer may use its own form with the same effect; ask your agent whether it meets the exhibit's wording.

RequestWhat it doesThe paper that proves itWhy it gets sent back
LimitsThe least each policy must pay: each occurrence and aggregate for general liability, a combined single limit for auto, employer's liability for workers' comp, and an umbrella on top.The limits on the certificate.A limit below the exhibit's figure, or an umbrella used to reach it when the exhibit does not say a combination of primary and umbrella is accepted.
Additional insured, ongoing operationsPuts the GC (and others named) under your liability policy for claims arising from your work while it is under way.An additional insured endorsement, often ISO CG 20 10.The certificate is ticked but no endorsement is attached, or the endorsement lists only the GC when the exhibit also names the owner.
Additional insured, completed operationsExtends that cover to claims that arise after your work is finished, for the period the exhibit sets.A completed operations endorsement, often ISO CG 20 37, sent with the ongoing one.Only the ongoing operations form is attached. Editions of CG 20 10 after the 11 85 one cover ongoing operations only, which is why CG 20 37 exists (Insurance Journal).
Waiver of subrogationAfter your insurer pays a claim, it gives up the right to recover that money from the parties named.A waiver endorsement, often ISO CG 24 04 on general liability, plus a waiver on the auto and workers' comp policies when the exhibit asks for those.The waiver is on general liability only when the exhibit asks for it on every policy, or it is scheduled to the wrong party.

These are secondary sources; the endorsement your insurer issues, and your exhibit, are what count.

Keeping certificates from holding up a pay application

  1. Read the exhibit before you sign the subcontract. Send it to your agent and ask, in writing, whether your policies meet it as written and what any missing endorsement costs. An endorsement you cannot get is a contract term to negotiate, not a surprise at the first pay app.
  2. One certificate per GC, with the endorsements attached. Ask the agent for the certificate with that GC as holder, the project and parties in the description box, and the endorsement pages the exhibit names, in one PDF.
  3. Keep a list of who holds which certificate. When a policy renews, every GC holding the old certificate needs the new one. Without a list, the GC you forgot finds out first, usually when it reviews your next pay application.
  4. Send renewals before the expiry date. Agents usually issue renewal certificates once the new policy is bound. Ask for them a few weeks out, and send them to each GC with the policy period and the jobs they cover.
  5. Put the certificate in the pay application backup when the GC asks for it there, so the reviewer does not have to look it up (what goes in the backup). At closeout it usually goes in the binder too, with the completed operations endorsement (getting retainage released).

Collecting certificates from your own lower-tier subs works the same way in reverse: compare each one with the exhibit you flowed down, and ask for the endorsement pages.

Common questions

Does listing the GC as additional insured on the certificate make them one?

No. The ACORD 25 says it is issued for information only and confers no rights on the certificate holder, and that an additional insured needs the policy itself to provide for it or be endorsed. Send the additional insured endorsement page with the certificate.

What is the difference between waiver of subrogation and primary and noncontributory?

Primary and noncontributory decides whose policy pays first: yours pays before the GC's own policy and does not ask it to share. Waiver of subrogation decides what your insurer can do after it pays: it gives up the right to recover that payment from the parties named. A subcontract can ask for both, and each usually needs its own endorsement.

Can a GC hold my pay application because my certificate expired?

Many subcontracts make current certificates a condition of payment, so check yours. Either way, a lapsed certificate on file is a common reason a pay application stalls, so send the renewal to every GC holding the old one before the expiry date.

Who issues the certificate of insurance?

Your insurance agent or broker, the producer named in the top box of the ACORD 25. Ask them for one per GC, with that GC (and anyone else the exhibit names) as certificate holder, and for the endorsement pages the exhibit asks for.
