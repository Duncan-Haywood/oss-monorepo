Pay applications for electrical contractors: the schedule of values, gear, extras and retainage

An electrical subcontract on a commercial job is usually billed once a month on a pay application: a schedule of values (SOV) with each line's percent complete, plus stored materials, change orders and retainage. Electrical work has its own traps. Gear and fixtures are expensive and arrive long before they are installed, rough-in and trim happen months apart, and public jobs add a certified payroll every week. This page walks through each one and links the free guide or tool for it.

Where electrical subs lose money on pay apps

  • Gear billed as work before it is set. If the GC rejects the line, the whole application comes back. Bill delivered gear as stored materials with the invoice attached, under the contract's stored-material rules (on federal work, FAR 52.232-5 sets when off-site material may be paid).
  • Extras done on a verbal OK. Unsigned tickets are the extras most often never paid. A ticket signed the same day, then a change order request, is the habit that gets them paid.
  • A front-loaded SOV. Loading mobilization and rough-in leaves trim and close-out underfunded, and a GC who notices may cut the early lines. Check yours against a balanced SOV.
  • Retainage nobody chases. Ten jobs each holding 5 to 10% is real money; the retainage tracker lists what is owed and since when.

Common questions

Can I bill for switchgear before it is installed?

Usually, as stored materials, if your subcontract allows it and you attach what it asks for (invoices, delivery proof, photos, and for off-site storage often insurance and title papers). Move it from stored to work completed in the month it is installed so it is billed once.

How should an electrical contractor break down the schedule of values?

By area or floor and by phase (underground, rough-in, gear, fixtures, trim, low voltage, testing and close-out), with expensive material on its own lines. Small lines make each month's percent easy to check and keep late work funded.

Do electrical subs need certified payroll?

On federal and federally assisted construction covered by Davis-Bacon, yes: every contractor and subcontractor submits a weekly certified payroll for the weeks it has workers on site. Several states require the same on state public works, some in their own online systems.

How do I get paid for added circuits and devices?

Write a T&M ticket or change order request and get it signed before or on the day of the work, then bill it once the change order is approved. Bills for unsigned extras are the ones most often refused.
