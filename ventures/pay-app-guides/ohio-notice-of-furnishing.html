Ohio notice of furnishing: who gets it, the 21 days, and the form

On a private Ohio job whose owner recorded a notice of commencement, a subcontractor or material supplier keeps its mechanic's lien rights by serving a notice of furnishing on the owner's designee and the original contractor named in it, within 21 days after first furnishing: the statute's words are “within twenty-one days after performing the first labor or work or furnishing the first materials” (Ohio Rev. Code § 1311.05(A)). This page works out your last day, covers who serves it and on whom, what a late notice keeps, how to serve it, the lien affidavit that comes after, and prints the statute's form word for word.

Dates count calendar days as the sections state them. This page names a weekend but does not move a deadline off it or check Ohio's holidays, so plan on the unmoved date. By certified mail, service of a notice of furnishing “is complete on the date of the mailing” (§ 1311.19(B)).

Who serves it, and on whom

  • Subcontractors and material suppliers serve it, at any tier, when someone has recorded a notice of commencement for the job. “No original contractor has to serve a notice of furnishing to preserve lien rights arising from a contract with an owner, part owner, or lessee” (§ 1311.05(A)).
  • It goes to the designee and the original contractor named in the notice of commencement, at the addresses it lists. With no designee named, it goes to the owner it names; with several owners, “service of the notice of furnishing to the first owner, part owner, or lessee named in the notice of commencement is sufficient” (§ 1311.05(A)).
  • Hired by the original contractor? “No subcontractor or material supplier who is in direct privity of contract with the original contractor has to serve a notice of furnishing upon the original contractor” (§ 1311.05(A)). You still serve the designee.
  • The lender named in the notice of commencement may get a copy, but you are not required to send one (§ 1311.05(A)).
  • No notice needed: from a laborer (“No laborer must serve a notice of furnishing”, § 1311.05(G)); on a home construction contract (§ 1311.05(E)); and where the owner “fails to record a notice of commencement” (§ 1311.05(H)).

First, get the notice of commencement

Everything the notice of furnishing needs comes from the notice of commencement: the designee's name and address, the original contractor and its address, and the property. The owner records it with the county recorder and posts a copy on the property during the work (§ 1311.04(G)).

Ask for it in writing. The original contractor that has it must serve you a copy “Within ten days after the date a subcontractor, material supplier, or laborer serves a written request for a copy of the notice of commencement” (§ 1311.04(E)); a subcontractor that hired you and has it must do the same (§ 1311.04(F)).

The 21 days wait for it. If it is recorded late, your time runs “until twenty-one days after the notice of commencement has been recorded”, and you “need not serve a notice of furnishing to preserve lien rights for the period before the notice of commencement is recorded” (§ 1311.04(I)). If the owner's side fails to serve it on your written request, the time runs “until twenty-one days after the notice of commencement actually has been served to the subcontractor or material supplier” (§ 1311.04(J)). So send the request early and keep proof of delivery.

What a late notice still protects

A late notice does not end the lien outright. It “preserves the subcontractor's or material supplier's lien rights for amounts owing for labor and work performed and materials furnished within the twenty-one-day period immediately preceding service of the notice of furnishing and thereafter, but does not revive any prior lien rights” (§ 1311.05(D)(1)).

So serve it the day you start. A notice served 40 days after you start still covers the 21 days before it and everything after, but your first 19 days of work fall outside the lien, and a GC that stops paying usually stops on the oldest money.

How to serve it

Any notice under the chapter is served by the sheriff, by “Certified or registered mail, overnight delivery service, hand delivery, or any other method which includes a written evidence of receipt”, or on a corporation's statutory agent (§ 1311.19(A)). Service is normally complete on receipt, but “if service of a notice of furnishing is made by certified mail, service is complete on the date of the mailing” (§ 1311.19(B)). If the owner or designee refuses or does not claim it at the address in the notice of commencement, service is complete when first attempted.

Print two copies, one per recipient, and keep each certified mail receipt with the job: it is your proof of the date.

After the notice: the lien affidavit

The notice is not the lien. If you go unpaid, you record an affidavit with the county recorder within 60 days of your last work on a one- or two-family dwelling or residential condo unit, and within 75 days on anything else (§ 1311.06(B)(1), (3)). Within 30 days after recording, serve a copy on the owner or designee (§ 1311.07).

Public jobs in Ohio are not liened. There a sub or supplier not in contract with the principal contractor serves a different notice of furnishing on the principal contractor within 21 days after first furnishing on the site (§ 1311.261), and the claim runs against the funds and the bond: see state public job bond claims.

The form, word for word (§ 1311.05(B))

“The notice of furnishing shall be in substantially the following form”. The captions in parentheses sit under each blank line. The statute gives the original contractor's address and the date no line of their own; one is added here to write on.

Notice of Furnishing

(For use in connection with improvements to property other than public improvements)

To: ________________

(Name of owner, part owner, or lessee or designee from the notice of commencement)

________________

(Address from the notice of commencement)

To: ________________

(Name of original contractor from notice of commencement)

________________

(Address of original contractor from notice of commencement)

Please take notice that the undersigned is performing certain labor or work or furnishing certain materials to ________________

(name and address of other contracting party)

________________

in connection with the improvement to the real property located at ________________

The labor, work, or materials were performed or furnished first or will be performed or furnished first on ________________ (date).

WARNING TO OWNER: THIS NOTICE IS REQUIRED BY THE OHIO MECHANICS' LIEN LAW. IF YOU HAVE ANY QUESTIONS ABOUT YOUR RIGHTS AND DUTIES UNDER THESE STATUTES YOU SHOULD SEEK LEGAL ASSISTANCE TO PROTECT YOU FROM THE POSSIBILITY OF PAYING TWICE FOR THE IMPROVEMENTS TO YOUR PROPERTY.

________________

(Name and address of lien claimant)

By ________________

(Name and capacity of party signing for lien claimant)

________________

(Address of party signing)

Date: ________________

The location is enough “if it reasonably identifies the real property” (§ 1311.05(C)). Source: Ohio Rev. Common questions

Who has to send an Ohio notice of furnishing?

A subcontractor or material supplier on a private Ohio job whose owner recorded a notice of commencement. An original contractor (in contract with the owner) does not, and no laborer must (Ohio Rev. Code § 1311.05(A), (G)). None is needed on a home construction contract or where no notice of commencement was recorded (§ 1311.05(E), (H)).

When is the Ohio notice of furnishing due?

Within 21 days after you first perform labor or work or furnish materials (§ 1311.05(A)). If the notice of commencement is recorded after you start, you have 21 days from its recording; if you asked for it in writing and were not served a copy within 10 days, 21 days from when a copy actually reaches you (§ 1311.04(I), (J)).

Who gets the Ohio notice of furnishing?

The owner's designee named in the notice of commencement (or the owner it names, if there is no designee) and the original contractor it names, at the addresses it lists. A sub hired directly by the original contractor need not serve the original contractor (§ 1311.05(A)). A copy to the lender named in it is optional.

What happens if I serve the Ohio notice of furnishing late?

It still protects your work in the 21 days before you served it and everything after, but nothing earlier (§ 1311.05(D)). Work from before that window is outside the lien.

How do I serve an Ohio notice of furnishing?

By certified or registered mail, overnight delivery, hand delivery or another method with written evidence of receipt, or by the sheriff (§ 1311.19(A)). By certified mail, service is complete on the day you mail it (§ 1311.19(B)); keep the receipt.

Is there an official Ohio notice of furnishing form?

Yes. § 1311.05(B) says the notice shall be in substantially the form it prints, with its WARNING TO OWNER; it is on this page word for word. Public jobs use a different notice, served on the principal contractor (§ 1311.261).
