Arizona preliminary 20-day notice: who gets it, when, and the form

On a private Arizona job, the preliminary twenty day notice is “a necessary prerequisite to the validity of any claim of lien” (A.R.S. § 33-992.01(B)): no notice and no proof of mailing, no enforceable lien (§ 33-981(D)). It is due 20 days after you first furnish labor or materials to the jobsite. Dates count calendar days and are not moved off a weekend or holiday here, so mail well before the last day. “Completion” is the earliest of thirty days after the permit office's final inspection and written final acceptance, or sixty consecutive days with no labor (strikes, material shortages and acts of God aside); with no permit or no final inspection, it is the last day anything was furnished (§ 33-993(C), (D)).

Who gets the notice

  • The owner or reputed owner: the person who causes the improvement to be built, including a lessee or a buyer under contract (§ 33-992.01(A)(3), (B)).
  • The original contractor or reputed contractor: the contractor with “a direct contractual relationship with the owner” (§ 33-992.01(A)(2)).
  • The construction lender, if any, or reputed lender (§ 33-992.01(A)(1)). Ask the owner: it must tell you (below).
  • The person you contracted with, when that is someone else, such as the sub that hired you.

Only “a person performing actual labor for wages” is exempt, so the original contractor sends one too, to the owner and the lender. One notice to each covers everything you furnish for the improvement (§ 33-992.01(G)) with two exceptions: send a new notice when your estimated total grows by thirty percent or more over the last notice's figure, and a separate one for work under contracts with more than one subcontractor.

Owner-occupied homes. On the dwelling of a person who owned it and meant to live in it before the work began, no lien is allowed “except by a person having executed in writing a contract directly with the owner-occupant” (§ 33-1002(B)). A sub or supplier on such a home has no lien to protect, and a contract term waiving this is void. A home built by a builder or an investor to sell or rent is not owner-occupied.

What the notice must say

Unlike California, Arizona prints a form: the notice “shall follow substantially the following form” (§ 33-992.01(D)). Its contents (§ 33-992.01(C)):

ItemSection
A general description of what you furnished or will furnish, and an estimate of its total price§ 33-992.01(C)(1)
Your name and address§ 33-992.01(C)(2)
The name of the person who contracted with you§ 33-992.01(C)(3)
A legal description, subdivision plat, street address or other description of the jobsite “sufficient for identification”§ 33-992.01(C)(4)
The “this is not a lien” statement and the Notice to Property Owner, in bold§ 33-992.01(C)(5), (D)
The two ten-day paragraphs (corrections and the payment bond), in type at least as large as the largest on the page§ 33-992.01(D)
The acknowledgment of receipt§ 33-992.02(1)

Missing bold or small type alone does not make an otherwise compliant notice defective (§ 33-992.01(I)), and work you add later outside the description, or less than thirty percent over the estimate, does not either (§ 33-992.01(H)).

How to mail it, and how to prove it

  • Mail. By “first class mail sent with a certificate of mailing, registered or certified mail, postage prepaid in all cases”, to each person's residence or business address. “Service is complete at the time of the deposit of notice in the mail” (§ 33-992.01(F)).
  • Proof, the first way. The acknowledgment of receipt at the foot of the form, signed and returned by the person served (§ 33-992.02(1)).
  • Proof, the second way. If it is not signed and returned “within thirty days from the date of mailing”, your own affidavit of the time, place and manner of mailing, naming each person and address, with the certificate of mailing or the certified or registered mail receipt attached (§ 33-992.02(2)). So keep the receipts the day you mail.
  • The owner's ten days. Ask in writing for the job's details and, within ten days, the owner must give you the legal description, the names and addresses of the owner, original contractor and lender, and a copy of any recorded payment bond (§ 33-992.01(J)). If what comes back shows your notice was wrong, send an amended notice within thirty days (§ 33-992.01(K)).

The lien itself is recorded “within one hundred twenty days after completion” or, if the owner records a notice of completion, “within sixty days after recordation of such notice”, with a copy of the preliminary notice and its proof of mailing attached (§ 33-993(A)(6)). An owner who records a notice of completion must mail it within fifteen days to everyone who sent a preliminary notice; anyone it misses keeps the 120 days (§ 33-993(I)). This is one more reason to send the notice: it puts you on the owner's list.

The statements, word for word (§ 33-992.01(D))

Arizona Preliminary Twenty Day Lien Notice

In accordance with Arizona Revised Statutes section 33-992.01, this is not a lien. This is not a reflection on the integrity of any contractor or subcontractor.

Notice to Property Owner

If bills are not paid in full for the labor, professional services, materials, machinery, fixtures or tools furnished, or to be furnished, a mechanic's lien leading to the loss, through court foreclosure proceedings, of all or part of your property being improved may be placed against the property. You may wish to protect yourself against this consequence by either:

1. Requiring your contractor to furnish a conditional waiver and release pursuant to Arizona Revised Statutes section 33-1008, subsection D, paragraphs 1 and 3 signed by the person or firm giving you this notice before you make payment to your contractor.

2. Requiring your contractor to furnish an unconditional waiver and release pursuant to Arizona Revised Statutes section 33-1008, subsection D, paragraphs 2 and 4 signed by the person or firm giving you this notice after you make payment to your contractor.

3. Using any other method or device that is appropriate under the circumstances.

Within ten days after the receipt of this preliminary twenty day notice the owner or other interested party is required to furnish all information necessary to correct any inaccuracies in the notice pursuant to Arizona Revised Statutes section 33-992.01, subsection J or lose as a defense any inaccuracy of that information.

Within ten days after the receipt of this preliminary twenty day notice if any payment bond has been recorded in compliance with Arizona Revised Statutes section 33-1003, the owner must provide a copy of the payment bond, including the name and address of the surety company and bonding agent providing the payment bond to the person who has given the preliminary twenty day notice. In the event that the owner or other interested party fails to provide the bond information within that ten day period, the claimant shall retain lien rights to the extent precluded or prejudiced from asserting a claim against the bond as a result of not timely receiving the bond information.

Common questions

Who has to send an Arizona preliminary 20-day notice?

Everyone who furnishes labor, professional services, materials, machinery, fixtures or tools to a private Arizona job and may want a lien, except a person performing actual labor for wages (A.R.S. § 33-992.01(B)). It goes to the owner or reputed owner, the original contractor, the construction lender if any, and the person you contracted with. Without it, and proof of mailing, the lien cannot be enforced (§ 33-981(D)).

When is the Arizona preliminary notice due?

Not later than 20 days after you first furnish labor or materials to the jobsite (§ 33-992.01(C)). Mailed by first class mail with a certificate of mailing, registered or certified mail, service is complete when the notice is deposited in the mail (§ 33-992.01(F)).

What if I send the Arizona preliminary notice late?

You can still send it, but the lien then covers only what you furnished within the 20 days before the notice was served and afterwards (§ 33-992.01(E)). Everything earlier is outside the lien.

Is there an official Arizona preliminary notice form?

Yes. § 33-992.01(D) sets out the form the notice must substantially follow, the Arizona Preliminary Twenty Day Lien Notice, with its Notice to Property Owner in bold and two ten-day paragraphs in type at least as large as the largest on the page. The acknowledgment of receipt from § 33-992.02 goes at the end.

Can a subcontractor lien an owner-occupied home in Arizona?

No. On the dwelling of a person who owned it and intended to live in it before the work began, only a person with a written contract directly with the owner-occupant may claim a lien (§ 33-1002(B)). A subcontractor or supplier on that home has no lien right for the notice to protect.
