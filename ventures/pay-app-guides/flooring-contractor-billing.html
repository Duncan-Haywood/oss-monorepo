Pay applications for commercial flooring and tile: floor prep, stored material and attic stock

Commercial flooring and tile pay applications bill finishes laid near the end of a job, on slabs other trades have used for months. The money is often lost before the first box is opened: moisture testing, slab prep that was not in the price, and material delivered weeks before the floor is ready. This page covers each, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where flooring subs lose money on pay apps

  • Mitigation done before anyone agreed to pay. A slab that fails a moisture test is a decision for the GC, not a free fix. Ask in writing first; a change order request with the test results is the record.
  • Prep beyond the scope, on a verbal OK. Grinding and leveling a slab that was not flat take crew days. A ticket signed the day the work is done is the one that gets paid.
  • Boxes in a storage room, not on the bill. Material delivered and paid for weeks before the floor is ready is cash you have spent. If the subcontract allows stored materials, bill them.
  • Attic stock nobody signed for. Spare material handed over without a receipt is a close-out item that stays open, and the last retainage waits with it.

Common questions

How should a flooring contractor set up the schedule of values?

By floor or area and by material (carpet, resilient, tile, base), with moisture testing, floor prep and attic stock on lines of their own. Each month's percent then comes from what is laid on each floor.

Who pays for slab moisture mitigation?

Whoever your subcontract and the specifications say. If it is not in your price, report the test results, ask for a change order before you apply anything and keep the results with it.

Can I bill for flooring delivered but not installed?

Often yes, as stored materials, if your subcontract allows it; attach invoices, delivery tickets and photos. Move it to work completed as it is installed, so it is billed once.

What is attic stock, and when is it billed?

Spare flooring the specifications ask you to leave with the owner for future repairs. Bill it when it is handed over, and get the hand-over signed: it is usually on the close-out list final retainage waits on.
