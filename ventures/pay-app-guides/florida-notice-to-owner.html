Florida Notice to Owner: the 45 days, who gets a copy, and the form

On a private Florida job, a subcontractor, sub-subcontractor or supplier who did not contract with the owner has to serve a Notice to Owner before it can record a claim of lien. It is due 45 days after you first furnish work, and a late one loses the lien outright: the failure “to timely serve it, is a complete defense to enforcement of a lien by any person” (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(2)(a)). Enter your dates for the last day, the certified-mail date and the claim of lien date, then fill in the job for the statutory form, word for word.

Your dates

Dates count calendar days and are not moved off a weekend or holiday: the sections read here say nothing about either, so serve well before the last day. The notice must also reach the owner “before the date of the owner’s disbursement of the final payment after the contractor has furnished the affidavit” (§ 713.06(2)(a)); on a short job that can come before day 45.

Who serves it, and on whom

Section 713.06 is headed “Liens of persons not in privity”. It requires “All lienors under this section, except laborers” to serve the notice on the owner (§ 713.06(2)(a)). A contractor who contracted with the owner does not serve one.

You areServe the notice on
Subcontractor, or supplier to the contractorThe owner
Sub-subcontractor, or supplier to a subcontractorThe owner, and a copy on the contractor
Supplier to a sub-subcontractorThe owner, a copy on the contractor, and the subcontractor if you know its name and address
Anyone aboveAlso a copy on any person the owner's notice of commencement names to receive one (§ 713.06(2)(b)); missing that copy “does not invalidate an otherwise valid lien”

Addresses come from the recorded notice of commencement. Without one, a lienor “may rely on the information contained in the building permit application” (§ 713.06(2)(e)). If the address there is incomplete, § 713.18(3)(b) lets you complete it from the property appraiser or another public record.

How to serve it, and the 40-day mail rule

  • Means (§ 713.18(1)): hand delivery; a common carrier delivery service, or registered, Global Express Guaranteed or certified mail, “with evidence of delivery, which may be in an electronic format”; or posting on the site, only if neither of those can be done.
  • Served on mailing. A notice to owner is served on the day you mail it if it goes by registered, Global Express Guaranteed or certified mail, is “mailed within 40 days after the date the lienor first furnishes labor, services, or materials”, and you keep a mail log (the USPS certified number, who it went to, the USPS date stamp) or USPS tracking records (§ 713.18(2)). That is why the calculator shows a mail-by date five days before the 45th day.
  • After day 40, or by courier, it is served only when it is delivered, and that must still be by day 45.
  • Refused or unclaimed. A document sent to the address in the notice of commencement (or the permit application, or the last known address) and returned “refused”, “moved, not forwardable” or “unclaimed” is still served on the mailing date (§ 713.18(3)(a)).
  • Several owners. Served on one owner, or one partner of a partnership that owns the property, it is “deemed served on all owners and partners” (§ 713.18(4)).

Late, wrong, or missing

California's late preliminary notice still covers the last 20 days of work. Florida's does not: “the failure to serve the notice, or to timely serve it, is a complete defense to enforcement of a lien by any person” (§ 713.06(2)(a)). Errors in the contents are treated more gently: if you “substantially complied”, errors or omissions do not stop enforcement against someone they did not harm, “However, a lienor must strictly comply with the time requirements” (§ 713.06(2)(f)).

The notice is not a lien. “The serving of the notice does not dispense with recording the claim of lien” (§ 713.06(2)(a)), which is due “not later than 90 days after the final furnishing of the labor or services or materials by the lienor” (§ 713.08(5)) and is served on the owner before recording or within 15 days after (§ 713.08(4)(c)). The claim of lien asks for “the date and method of service of the notice to owner” (§ 713.08(1)(h)), so keep the mail log or receipt with the job.

Common questions

Who has to send a Florida Notice to Owner?

Subcontractors, sub-subcontractors and suppliers who did not contract with the owner. Laborers are excepted, and a contractor who contracted with the owner does not serve one (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(2)(a)). Sub-subcontractors and suppliers to a subcontractor also serve a copy on the contractor, and everyone serves a copy on anyone the notice of commencement names to receive one.

What is the deadline for a Florida Notice to Owner?

Before you start, or not later than 45 days after you first furnish labor, services or materials, and in any event before the owner's final payment to the contractor after the contractor's final affidavit (§ 713.06(2)(a)). Sent by certified, registered or Global Express Guaranteed mail within 40 days, with a mail log or USPS tracking, it counts as served on the day it is mailed (§ 713.18(2)).

What happens if I serve the Notice to Owner late?

Florida does not save part of the work the way some states do. Failing to serve the notice, or to serve it on time, is a complete defense to enforcement of the lien by any person, and the time requirements must be strictly complied with (§ 713.06(2)(a), (f)). Small errors in the contents can be forgiven; the date cannot.

Is there an official Florida Notice to Owner form?

Yes. Section 713.06(2)(c) prints the form, and the notice may be in substantially that form but must include its information and its warning. The draft on this page is that form word for word with your details in the blanks.

How long do I have to record a claim of lien in Florida?

Not later than 90 days after your final furnishing of labor, services or materials (§ 713.08(5)), and the claim of lien must be served on the owner before recording or within 15 days after (§ 713.08(4)(c)). Serving the Notice to Owner does not replace recording the claim of lien.
