Statutory lien waiver forms by state

In most states the parties write their own lien waivers. A minority set the wording in statute, and in some of them a waiver that does not follow the statutory form does not release anything. If you bill on jobs in those states, the GC's in-house waiver may be the wrong document, and the right one is free in the statute. This page lists the states whose text we read on the legislature's own site, what each requires, and the traps.

What each state asks of you

Arizona

  • A progress waiver covers work "through" a date you fill in "only and does not cover any retention, pending modifications and changes or items furnished after that date".
  • The unconditional forms carry a notice: "This document is enforceable against you if you sign it, even if you have not been paid. If you have not been paid, use a conditional release form."
  • No statement waiving lien rights binds you unless it is one of these waivers or you "had actually received payment in full for the claim" (§ 33-1008(B)).

Nevada

  • The strictest wording rule in the list: the waiver must be "in the following forms", not substantially like them.
  • No through date. The blanks are Property Name, Property Location, Undersigned's Customer, Invoice/Payment Application Number and Payment Amount, so a progress waiver is tied to one pay application.
  • A joint check counts as payment to you only up to the smallest of the check, the amount meant for you, or your balance on that work (§ 108.2457(3)).

Nevada's lien waiver forms, in full and printable

Texas

  • No one "may require" you to sign an unconditional waiver unless you "received payment in that amount in good and sufficient funds" (§ 53.283).
  • The unconditional forms need a notice at the top "in bold type at least as large as the largest type used in the document, but not smaller than 10-point type".
  • Progress forms except "unpaid retention, pending modifications and changes, or other items furnished".
  • One exception to watch: on a single-family house, townhouse or duplex, a waiver written into the contract before work starts can be enforceable (§ 53.282(a)(3)). It does not apply to someone who supplies only materials.

Texas's lien waiver forms, in full and printable

Utah

  • A waiver binds only once you sign it and receive the payment it names, and for a progress payment "only to the extent of the payment" (§ 38-1a-802(2)).
  • If the check "fails to clear ... for any reason", the waiver is void and your lien, bond and contract rights are untouched (§ 38-1a-802(3)).
  • The progress form excludes "any retention withheld; any items, modifications, or changes pending approval; disputed items and claims; or items furnished or invoiced after the Payment Period".
  • A payer can put the waiver on the check itself, with a restrictive endorsement in the statute's wording, and must circle "progress" or "final".

Utah's lien waiver forms, in full and printable

Florida

  • "A right to claim a lien may not be waived in advance", and a waiver reaches only work already furnished (§ 713.20(2)).
  • "A person may not require a lienor to furnish a lien waiver or release of lien that is different from the forms" (§ 713.20(6)). If the GC's form is different, you can ask for the statutory one.
  • But a waiver you do sign that is not like the statutory forms "is enforceable in accordance with the terms" (§ 713.20(8)). Read it before you sign it.
  • You may condition a waiver given for a check on the check being paid (§ 713.20(7)).
  • Neither statutory form says "conditional": each "hereby waives and releases" the lien "in consideration of" the payment, and the statute gives no wording for the condition that (7) allows. The progress form "does not cover any retention or labor, services, or materials furnished after the date specified" (§ 713.20(4)); the final form has no such exception (§ 713.20(5)). So if you sign the progress form before the check clears, it is the condition you add, not the form, that protects you; ask your attorney how to word it.

Florida's lien waiver forms, in full and printable

Michigan

  • Michigan's words are "partial" and "full" rather than progress and final. Conditional waivers take effect "upon payment of the amount indicated".
  • Once fully paid you "shall provide" a full unconditional waiver; when partly paid, a partial unconditional waiver if the owner asks (§ 570.1115(2)–(3)).
  • Each form ends: "DO NOT SIGN BLANK OR INCOMPLETE FORMS. RETAIN A COPY."
  • On residential work, an owner may not rely on a waiver handed over by someone other than you, once you have sent a notice of furnishing, without first checking it with you.

Michigan's lien waiver forms, in full and printable

Wyoming

  • One form for every payment. It reserves "monies withheld as retainage" and labor or materials not yet paid for, and states an amount you have not been paid.
  • Its last sentences pull two ways: the owner may rely on it "even if the undersigned accepts payment in uncertified funds and such payment is subsequently dishonored", yet the waiver "shall not apply ... if payment tendered by the owner is dishonored". Ask a Wyoming lawyer how that reads for a check from the GC rather than the owner.
  • Your preliminary notice to the owner tells them to ask for lien waivers and attaches this form (W.S. 29-10-101(a)).
  • The form ends in an acknowledgment before a notarial officer, so plan for a notary when you sign it.

Wyoming's lien waiver form, in full and printable

California

  • A waiver given for a payment is "null, void, and unenforceable unless it is in substantially the following form", one form per kind: conditional progress (§ 8132), unconditional progress (§ 8134), conditional final (§ 8136) and unconditional final (§ 8138).
  • Watch the change-order sentence. Every form waives rights for work done "pursuant to a written change order that has been fully executed by the parties prior to the date that this document is signed by the claimant ... unless listed as an Exception below". If a signed change order is still unpaid, list it.
  • The progress forms do not affect "Retentions", "Extras for which the claimant has not received payment" or your contract rights. The conditional progress form also lists earlier conditional waivers you gave "but has not received payment", with their dates and amounts.
  • The final forms have one exception: "Disputed claims for extras in the amount of" a sum you fill in.
  • The unconditional forms carry a "Notice to Claimant" that must be "in at least as large a type as the largest type otherwise in the form": "THIS DOCUMENT IS ENFORCEABLE AGAINST YOU IF YOU SIGN IT, EVEN IF YOU HAVE NOT BEEN PAID."
  • A conditional waiver releases the owner, lender or bond surety only with evidence of payment: your endorsement on a check that has been paid, or your written acknowledgment of payment (§ 8124). No contract term can waive your rights unless you sign one of these waivers (§ 8122), and a statement giving them up is void unless it is one of these waivers or you have been paid in full (§ 8126).

Missouri

  • One form, and only for "residential real property" as § 429.016 defines it: new homes of four units or fewer, condominiums, townhouses and cooperatives built to be sold or occupied. Repair, remodeling or additions to an owner-occupied home of four units or fewer fall under § 429.013 instead, and commercial work has no statutory form.
  • On that property, a waiver given "in exchange for a payment of less than the amount claimed due at that time" waives nothing unless it is the statutory unconditional final waiver (§ 429.016.25). Partial and conditional waivers are allowed, with no set wording.
  • The unconditional final form is "a complete and absolute waiver": it covers work before the date signed and later work under agreements made before that date, and it binds you "notwithstanding claimant's failure to receive any promised payment" (§ 429.016.26, .29). Sign it only once the money has cleared.
  • If you recorded a notice of rights and have been paid in full, you must give it within five calendar days of a written request, or face a presumption of slander of title and a $500 statutory penalty (§ 429.016.30).

Missouri's lien waiver form, in full and printable

Georgia

  • Georgia's Code is published officially only through LexisNexis, which we could not open. We read the two Acts that wrote the current section instead, as published by the State: SB 315 (2020, in force 1 January 2021), which rewrote all of § 44-14-366, and SB 143 (2021), which rewrote the affidavit of nonpayment. Check the current Code before relying on this section.
  • Two forms: a "Waiver and Release of Lien and Payment Bond Rights Upon Interim Payment" ((d)) and one "Upon Final Payment" ((e)). Neither is called conditional or unconditional. Each waives "upon the receipt of the sum of $____". The interim form covers work through a date you fill in and excepts "any retained amounts"; the final form has no exception.
  • The trap is the 90 days. Once you sign, the waiver is conclusively effective at the earliest of the money actually arriving, a separate written acknowledgment of payment in full, or "ninety days after the date of the execution of the waiver and release", unless before then you file an affidavit of nonpayment "in the county in which the property is located" (§ 44-14-366(g)(2)). The form's own notice says so: you are deemed to have waived "EVEN IF YOU HAVE NOT ACTUALLY RECEIVED SUCH PAYMENT, 90 DAYS AFTER THE DATE STATED ABOVE UNLESS YOU FILE AN AFFIDAVIT OF NONPAYMENT". Before 2021 the period was 60 days and a claim of lien also stopped it; the 2020 Act struck both.
  • The affidavit of nonpayment has its own form, sworn before a notary. Within seven days of filing it you send a copy to the owner by registered or certified mail or statutory overnight delivery, and in some cases to the contractor at the address on the notice of commencement.
  • The waiver is limited to lien and labor or material bond rights and "shall not be deemed to affect any other rights or remedies of the claimant" (§ 44-14-366(a)), which the 2020 Act added. It must be "in at least 12 point font" and "need not be in boldface capital letters". It is signed under seal, with lines for a witness and an address.

Mississippi

  • Mississippi's Code is also published officially only through LexisNexis. Both sections were created new by SB 2622 (2014, approved 11 April 2014), which we read as sent to the Governor on the Legislature's site. We found no later amendment in the Legislature's lists of bills for 2015 to 2026, but check the current Code before relying on this section.
  • Two forms, an "Interim Waiver and Release Upon Payment" and a "Waiver and Release Upon Final Payment" (§ 85-7-433(1), (2)). Their wording follows Georgia's pre-2021 forms closely. Each waives "upon the receipt of the sum of $____". The interim form covers work through a date you fill in and excepts "any retained amounts"; the final form has no exception. A blank filled in wrongly does not invalidate the form "if the subject matter of the release reasonably may be determined" (§ 85-7-419(2), (3)).
  • The trap is the 60 days. Once you sign, the amount is "conclusively deemed paid in full" at the earliest of the money actually arriving, a separate written acknowledgment of payment in full, or "sixty (60) days after the date of the execution of the waiver and release", unless before then you file an affidavit of nonpayment in the county where the property is and send the owner a copy (§ 85-7-419(5)(b)). The form's notice also names filing a claim of lien. These are the 60 days and the two ways out that Georgia dropped in 2021.
  • The affidavit of nonpayment has its own form (§ 85-7-433(3)). Within two days of filing it, a copy goes to the owner by registered or certified mail or statutory overnight delivery. Once filed, the waiver is suspended until you are paid in full ((5)(e)). Once you are paid, you must on request sign an affidavit of payment in full, which voids the first affidavit when recorded ((5)(c)).
  • Every form, the affidavit included, is sworn before a notary ("SWORN TO AND SUBSCRIBED BEFORE ME"). The final form prints its signature and notary lines both above and below its notice, so plan for a notary when you sign either one. No lien or bond right can be waived before the work is furnished (§ 85-7-419(1)).

Massachusetts

  • The rule comes first: a covenant or agreement in or connected with a construction contract "purporting to bar the filing of a notice of contract or the taking of any steps to enforce a lien", or to subordinate those rights, "is against public policy and is void and unenforceable" (c. 254, § 32). Four things are excepted, among them lien waivers by a principal on a lien bond and "statements ... of amounts due or paid".
  • The fourth exception is a form: "partial waivers and subordinations of liens given by persons who have filed or recorded notices of contract under section two substantially in the following form with no material deviation therefrom". It is a progress waiver. Massachusetts prints no final or unconditional form.
  • Its header is your pay application: original contract amount, approved change orders, adjusted contract amount, completed to date, less retainage, total payable to date, less previous payments, current amount due, pending change orders and disputed claims (lines 1 to 10). It must match the application it goes with.
  • It takes effect "upon receipt of" the payment, stated in words and figures. It keeps back "retainage, unpaid agreed or pending change orders, and disputed claims as stated above". So fill in lines 5, 9 and 10, or those amounts are not excepted.
  • Part (b) also subordinates your lien for work through "the twenty-fifth day after the end of the above payment period", to the extent the lender actually advances funds by then. It is "Signed under the penalties of perjury".
  • Whether a general-form waiver is enforceable on a Massachusetts job is a question for a Massachusetts lawyer. Section 32 is the place to start.

A monthly routine that works in all of them

  1. Send this month's pay application with a conditional progress waiver for this month's amount, on your state's form where it has one.
  2. Once last month's payment has cleared, send an unconditional progress waiver for that amount, and not before.
  3. Keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed items out of every progress waiver. Most of the statutory forms already say so; check that the GC's form does too.
  4. On the final payment, sign the final conditional waiver with the final pay app, and the final unconditional one only once everything, retainage included, has cleared.

Common questions

Which states have a statutory lien waiver form?

This page covers Arizona, Nevada, Texas, Utah, Florida, Michigan, Wyoming, Georgia, Mississippi, Missouri (unconditional final waivers on residential property only), Massachusetts (a partial waiver and subordination of lien, for those who recorded a notice of contract) and California, each linked to its statute. Statutes change, so open the linked section before relying on a row.

Do I have to use my state's exact lien waiver wording?

It depends on the state. Arizona, Texas, Michigan and Wyoming ask for the form substantially as written; Nevada's statute makes a waiver unenforceable unless it is in the statutory forms, with no "substantially"; in Florida the forms may be used, but no one may require a different form.

When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared. Send a conditional progress waiver with each pay application, an unconditional one for last month's amount once that payment clears, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed items out of every progress waiver.
