Pay applications for site work and excavation contractors: unit prices, rock and haul tickets

Excavation, grading and site utility subcontracts are often written with unit prices: so much per cubic yard of cut, per linear foot of pipe, per ton of stone or asphalt, with separate prices for rock and unsuitable soil. The monthly pay application then bills quantities measured or ticketed, and most disputes are about the quantities. This page covers quantity billing, rock and overruns, stored pipe and structures, and weather days, with the free guide or tool for each.

Where site work subs lose money on pay apps

  • Rock billed as dirt. Rock or unsuitable soil dug out without a measured, signed quantity ends up inside the unclassified line at the cheaper price.
  • Tickets that do not add up. A missing load ticket is a quantity the GC will not pay. Total the tickets before the pay app goes out.
  • Overruns dug before the change order. Under most subcontracts the GC pays the contract quantity until a change is approved. Ask while the work is happening.
  • Front-loaded mobilization. A mobilization line well above its cost is the first thing a reviewer cuts. Front-loading check compares each line's markup with the job's.

Common questions

How do you bill unit-price excavation on a pay application?

Each line carries a unit, a unit price and a contract quantity. Each month you report the quantity installed, measured by survey, count or tickets; the amount is that quantity times the unit price.

How do I get paid for rock excavation?

Bill it on its own line at the rock unit price if your subcontract has one, else as a change order. Measure it, get the quantity signed in the field before or while it is removed, and keep the measurement as backup.

Can I bill for pipe and structures delivered but not installed?

Often yes, as stored materials, if your subcontract allows it; attach invoices and delivery tickets. Move them to work completed as they are installed, so they are billed once.

Are weather days paid on a site work job?

Usually as extra time, not money, unless your subcontract says otherwise. Record each day lost in a daily report and ask for the time in writing, so it is there if a delay claim is needed later.
