What Wyoming asks of you

  • One form for every payment. It reserves "monies withheld as retainage" and labor or materials not yet paid for, and states an amount you have not been paid.
  • Its last sentences pull two ways: the owner may rely on it "even if the undersigned accepts payment in uncertified funds and such payment is subsequently dishonored", yet the waiver "shall not apply ... if payment tendered by the owner is dishonored". Ask a Wyoming lawyer how that reads for a check from the GC rather than the owner.
  • Your preliminary notice to the owner tells them to ask for lien waivers and attaches this form (W.S. 29-10-101(a)).
  • The form ends in an acknowledgment before a notarial officer, so plan for a notary when you sign it.

Notes on the form

  • Wyoming: the form for waiver of a lien "shall be completed in substantially the following form" (Wyo. Stat. § 29-10-101(b)). It is the one form the statute gives, used here for progress and final waivers alike, and it ends in an acknowledgment before a notarial officer, who fills in its blanks.
  • The waiver reserves lien rights for the retainage withheld and the amount not yet paid that you enter, and for labor or materials furnished later. On a final waiver both amounts are usually $0.00.
  • The form says the waiver may be relied on, and stays in effect, if the undersigned accepts uncertified funds that are later dishonored or revoked, and also that it does not apply if payment tendered by the owner is dishonored or revoked. Both sentences are printed as the statute gives them; ask counsel before signing against a check that has not cleared.
  • Wyo. Stat. § 29-2-112 also calls for a preliminary notice of the right to a lien, sent to the record owner within the time periods it specifies (form in § 29-10-101(a)). That notice can be printed too; the deadline is not checked.

Common questions

Does Wyoming have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes. W.S. 29-10-101(b) sets one form: a single lien waiver. How strict: "Shall be completed in substantially the following form". The form on this page is printed word for word from the statute as read on 2026-11-25.

When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver in Wyoming?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared your bank. Before that, give the conditional form if Wyoming has one, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed amounts out of a progress waiver.
