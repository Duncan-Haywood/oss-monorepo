Georgia lien waiver forms

Georgia sets the wording of lien waivers in statute (O.C.G.A. § 44-14-366). How strict: "Shall substantially follow the language of the following form", "in at least 12 point font"; no statement waives lien or bond rights unless it is one of these forms and you have been paid. Below are both statutory forms, word for word, with a blank line wherever the statute leaves one. Print the one you need, fill it in by hand, and keep a copy.

What Georgia asks of you

  • Georgia's Code is published officially only through LexisNexis, which we could not open. We read the two Acts that wrote the current section instead, as published by the State: SB 315 (2020, in force 1 January 2021), which rewrote all of § 44-14-366, and SB 143 (2021), which rewrote the affidavit of nonpayment. Check the current Code before relying on this section.
  • Two forms: a "Waiver and Release of Lien and Payment Bond Rights Upon Interim Payment" ((d)) and one "Upon Final Payment" ((e)). Neither is called conditional or unconditional. Each waives "upon the receipt of the sum of $____". The interim form covers work through a date you fill in and excepts "any retained amounts"; the final form has no exception.
  • The trap is the 90 days. Once you sign, the waiver is conclusively effective at the earliest of the money actually arriving, a separate written acknowledgment of payment in full, or "ninety days after the date of the execution of the waiver and release", unless before then you file an affidavit of nonpayment "in the county in which the property is located" (§ 44-14-366(g)(2)). The form's own notice says so: you are deemed to have waived "EVEN IF YOU HAVE NOT ACTUALLY RECEIVED SUCH PAYMENT, 90 DAYS AFTER THE DATE STATED ABOVE UNLESS YOU FILE AN AFFIDAVIT OF NONPAYMENT". Before 2021 the period was 60 days and a claim of lien also stopped it; the 2020 Act struck both.
  • The affidavit of nonpayment has its own form, sworn before a notary. Within seven days of filing it you send a copy to the owner by registered or certified mail or statutory overnight delivery, and in some cases to the contractor at the address on the notice of commencement.
  • The waiver is limited to lien and labor or material bond rights and "shall not be deemed to affect any other rights or remedies of the claimant" (§ 44-14-366(a)), which the 2020 Act added. It must be "in at least 12 point font" and "need not be in boldface capital letters". It is signed under seal, with lines for a witness and an address.

Notes on the forms

  • Georgia: O.C.G.A. § 44-14-366 gives two forms, one upon interim payment ((d)) and one upon final payment ((e)), which a waiver "shall substantially follow", printed "in at least 12 point font". Neither is conditional or unconditional; the same form is used whether or not the money has arrived.
  • Ninety days (§ 44-14-366(g)(2)): once signed, the waiver is conclusively effective at the earliest of the payment actually arriving, a separate written acknowledgment of payment in full, or 90 days after it was signed, unless before then you file an affidavit of nonpayment in the county where the property is. If you sign before you are paid and the money does not come, the 90 days run anyway. The statute names only the affidavit of nonpayment as stopping them.
  • The interim waiver covers lien and bond rights through the date you enter and excepts retained amounts; the final waiver has no exception. Both are limited to lien and labor or material bond rights and do not affect your other rights or remedies (§ 44-14-366(a)), and no lien or bond right can be waived before the work is furnished ((b)).
  • Sign under seal, date it by hand, and have it witnessed: the form has lines for the seal, a witness and an address.
  • Wording: rebuilt from the two Acts that wrote § 44-14-366, SB 315 (2020, in force 1 Jan 2021) and SB 143 (2021), as passed and published by the State of Georgia. The official Code (published through LexisNexis) was not read; check it, or have counsel check it, before relying on the form.

Common questions

Does Georgia have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes. O.C.G.A. § 44-14-366 sets 2 forms: interim payment and final payment, each waiving "upon the receipt of the sum". How strict: "Shall substantially follow the language of the following form", "in at least 12 point font"; no statement waives lien or bond rights unless it is one of these forms and you have been paid. The forms on this page are printed word for word from the statute as read on 2026-11-26.

Which Georgia lien waiver form goes with which payment?

Waiver and Release of Lien and Payment Bond Rights upon Interim Payment: progress payments, before or after the money clears (the form itself is neither conditional nor unconditional). Waiver and Release of Lien and Payment Bond Rights upon Final Payment: final payments, before or after the money clears (the form itself is neither conditional nor unconditional).

When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver in Georgia?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared your bank. Before that, give the conditional form if Georgia has one, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed amounts out of a progress waiver.
