Pay applications for finish carpentry and millwork: shop-built casework, field measure and install

Millwork and finish carpentry pay applications bill work that is mostly built before it reaches the job: shop drawings, field measurements, casework and paneling made in your own shop, then the install. Most of the money is spent weeks before anything is set, which is why the stored-materials line matters more here than in most trades. This page covers each step, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where millwork subs lose money on pay apps

  • Casework in the shop, not on the bill. Material, labor and shop time spent before delivery are cash you have already paid. If the subcontract allows off-site stored materials, bill them, with the backup it asks for.
  • Building to an unconfirmed dimension. A rebuild because a wall moved is paid when the question was asked in writing first. An RFI with your measured dimension is the record.
  • A shop drawing line billed when sent. Billing drawings before they are approved, or making the line large, is what a reviewer cuts. Front-loading check compares each line's markup with the job's.
  • Repairs done for free. Finished wood scratched by later trades is repaired by someone. A ticket signed before the repair is the one that gets paid.

Common questions

How should a millwork contractor set up the schedule of values?

By floor or area and by type of work (casework, countertops, trim and paneling, doors and hardware), with shop drawings, attic stock and punch on small lines of their own. Each month's percent then comes from what is installed on each floor.

Can I bill for casework built in my shop before it is delivered?

Only if your subcontract allows stored materials off site. Expect to show invoices and photos, and often a bill of sale, insurance on the stored goods and a right for the GC to inspect them. Move them to work completed as they are installed, so they are billed once.

Should shop drawings be their own line on the pay application?

Yes, if the subcontract allows it, as a small line billed when the drawings are approved. A large line billed before approval reads as front-loading.

What happens when field dimensions do not match the drawings?

Send an RFI before you build, with the dimension you measured and a proposed answer. If the answer adds work, price it as a change order before you do it.
