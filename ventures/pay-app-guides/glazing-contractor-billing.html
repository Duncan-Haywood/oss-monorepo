Pay applications for glass, glazing and curtain wall: engineering, fabricated units and water testing

Glazing pay applications bill systems that are engineered and fabricated long before they are set: storefront and curtain wall framing, insulating glass made to order, entrances and hardware. Lead times are long, so a glazing sub can carry most of a job's material cost months before the first unit goes in. This page covers each step, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where glazing subs lose money on pay apps

  • Fabricated units in a yard, not on the bill. Framing and glass made to order are cash spent months ahead. If the subcontract allows off-site stored materials, bill them with the backup it asks for, or ask for that right before you sign.
  • Return trips for openings not ready. A crew sent home because the opening is not ready is a cost someone owes. A ticket or a written notice the same day is the record.
  • Retests nobody priced. A water test that fails because of another trade's flashing is retested at someone's cost. Ask for a change order before the retest.
  • A broken lite replaced for free. Glass broken after it is set is replaced by someone. Photograph it and get a ticket signed before ordering the new lite.

Common questions

How should a glazing contractor set up the schedule of values?

By elevation or area and by system (curtain wall, storefront, windows, entrances, interior glass), with engineering, the mockup and testing on lines of their own, and material split from install where the subcontract allows stored materials.

Can I bill for curtain wall or glass stored at the fabricator?

Only if your subcontract allows stored materials off site. Expect to show invoices and photos, and often a bill of sale, insurance on the stored goods and a right for the GC to inspect them. Move them to work completed as they are set, so they are billed once.

Should the mockup and water testing be their own lines?

Yes, when the specifications ask for them: they are real work billed when done and passed, and separate lines keep them from being argued into the wall price. A retest caused by another trade is a change order.

Why is my final glazing retainage held?

Often because close-out is not complete: the punch list, final cleaning, the warranties or a final lien waiver. Ask the GC in writing which items are open, and send them together.
