Texas lien notice deadlines: one notice for every month you are not paid

On a private Texas job, a subcontractor or supplier keeps its lien rights by sending a notice of claim for unpaid labor or materials to the owner and the general contractor, month by month. Each month of unpaid work has its own last day: the 15th day of the third month after the month you did the work (the second month on a residential project). Enter your months for every date, the retainage notice and the lien affidavit, then fill in the statutory form, word for word. These are the rules for original contracts signed on or after 1 January 2022.

The date to plan on is the statutory one. When it falls on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday, the extended date is shown too: “the period is extended to include the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday” (Tex. Prop. Code § 53.003(e)). Chapter 53 does not say which days are legal holidays; the extension here counts the national and state holidays listed in Gov't Code § 662.003(a) and (b) on the days that section names, so treat it as the latest possible day, not the target.

Who sends it, and to whom

Since the 2021 rewrite (H.B. 2237) every tier follows the same clock. Section 53.056(a) says “a claimant other than an original contractor must give the notice prescribed by Subsections (a-1) and (a-2) for the lien to be valid”, and (a-1) says it goes “to the owner or reputed owner and the original contractor”.

The notice “may include an invoice or billing statement” (§ 53.056(a-3)). A softer step is optional: a claimant “may give to the original contractor a written notice of an unpaid labor or materials invoice that is past due”, but “A notice under this subsection is not required for a lien to be valid” (§ 53.056(a-4)).

Residential construction project means a job under a contract to build or repair an owner's “single-family house, duplex, triplex, or quadruplex” or condominium unit, owned by adults and “used or intended to be used as a dwelling by one of the owners” (§ 53.001(8)-(10)). Everything else, including spec homes and apartment buildings, is on the commercial clock.

How to deliver it

    “If a written notice is received by the person entitled to receive it, the method by which the notice was delivered is immaterial” (§ 53.003(d)). Keep the certified mail receipts: the lien affidavit must state “the date each notice of the claim was sent to the owner and the method by which the notice was sent” (§ 53.054(a)(8)).

    What the notice does: once an owner receives it, “the owner may withhold from payments to the original contractor an amount necessary to pay the claim” (§ 53.081(a)). That is why a GC often pays a sub soon after one arrives.

    Retainage: a separate notice and a shorter clock

    Retainage that is not in a monthly notice needs its own. “To the extent that a claim for unpaid retainage is not included wholly or partly in a notice provided under Section 53.056”, a claimant “whose contract provides for retainage must give notice under this section for a lien for unpaid retainage to be valid” (§ 53.057(a)). It is due “not later than the earlier of” 30 days after your contract is completed, terminated or abandoned, or 30 days after the original contract is terminated or abandoned (§ 53.057(a-1)).

    The owner must hold back 10 percent “During the progress of work under an original contract for which a mechanic's lien may be claimed and for 30 days after the work under the contract is completed” (§ 53.101(a)). A lien on those reserved funds needs an affidavit filed “not later than the 30th day after the earliest of the date” the work is completed, the original contract is terminated or the GC abandons it, “except as allowed by Section 53.057(f)” (§ 53.103). Watch for the owner's affidavit of completion: it is “prima facie evidence” of the completion date (§ 53.106(d)).

    After the notices: the lien affidavit

    A notice is not a lien. A claimant other than the original contractor files an affidavit with the county clerk “not later than the 15th day of the fourth month after the later of” the month it last provided labor or materials or the month it would normally have delivered the last specially fabricated materials (§ 53.052(b)); “the 15th day of the third month” on a residential project (§ 53.052(c)); and for retainage, “not later than the 15th day of the third month after the month in which the original contract under which the claimant performed was completed, terminated, or abandoned” (§ 53.052(d)). The calculator above gives the first two from your last month.

    Within five days of filing, send a copy “to the owner or reputed owner” and “to the original contractor” (§ 53.055). A suit to foreclose “must be brought” by “the first anniversary of the last day a claimant may file the lien affidavit” (§ 53.158(a)), unless the record owner agrees in writing to extend it.

    On a homestead, the lien also needs a written contract signed before work starts, by both spouses if the owner is married, and filed with the county clerk (§ 53.254(a)-(e)), and the notice to the owner “must include or have attached” a statement the section prints (§ 53.254(g)).     Common questions

    When is the Texas notice of claim for unpaid labor or materials due?

    On a commercial job, by the 15th day of the third month after the month the labor or materials were provided; on a residential construction project, the 15th day of the second month (Tex. Prop. Code § 53.056(a-1)). Each month of unpaid work has its own date: March work is due 15 June on a commercial job and 15 May on a residential one.

    Who has to send the Texas notice of claim, and to whom?

    Every claimant other than the original contractor: subcontractors and suppliers at any tier on an original contract entered into on or after 1 January 2022. The notice goes to the owner or reputed owner and to the original contractor (§ 53.056(a), (a-1)).

    What if the Texas notice deadline falls on a weekend or holiday?

    Section 53.003(e) extends a deadline that falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday to the next day that is not one. Chapter 53 does not define legal holiday, so this page plans on the unmoved date and shows the extended date separately, counting the national and state holidays of Gov't Code § 662.003.

    Do I need a separate notice for retainage in Texas?

    Yes, for retainage not already included in a monthly notice, if your contract provides for retainage. The notice of claim for unpaid retainage is due by the earlier of the 30th day after your contract is completed, terminated or abandoned, or the 30th day after the original contract is terminated or abandoned (§ 53.057(a), (a-1)).

    When do I file the Texas lien affidavit?

    A subcontractor or supplier files with the county clerk by the 15th day of the fourth month after the month it last provided labor or materials (third month on a residential project), and by the 15th day of the third month after the original contract ended for a retainage lien (§ 53.052(b)-(d)). Send a copy to the owner and the original contractor within five days of filing (§ 53.055).
