G706, G706A and G707: the final payment affidavits, and a builder for the exceptions

At close-out the last check, retainage included, usually waits on a small stack of sworn papers. On AIA jobs they are the G706 (Contractor's Affidavit of Payment of Debts and Claims), the G706A (Contractor's Affidavit of Release of Liens) and, on bonded work, the G707 (Consent of Surety to Final Payment). GCs often ask their subs for the same three, on AIA's forms or their own. This page says what each one has you swear, and builds the part that goes wrong: the exceptions list, the final waivers still to collect and what to attach, from your own list of subs and suppliers.

The G706 lists the papers that go with it if required by the Owner: your own Contractor's Release or Waiver of Liens, conditional upon receipt of final payment, separate final waivers from your subs and suppliers with a list of them, and the G706A. AIA's G706 summary says it is typically executed as a condition of final payment, and that The contractor may also be required to furnish a lien bond or indemnity bond to protect the owner with respect to each exception.

Where subs get the G706 wrong

  • "None" when something is open. A supplier's last invoice, a sub's retainage you have not paid on, a disputed extra: each is an exception until it is paid or settled. The affidavit is sworn, so a wrong "None" is a false statement, not a paperwork slip.
  • Unconditional waivers given before the check clears. Your own waiver in the package should be the one AIA's list names, conditional upon receipt of final payment. Which waiver when.
  • A generic release in a statutory-form state. Twelve states have their own lien waiver forms; in those, attach the state's forms. Lien waiver forms by state.
  • The wrong county on the notary block. AIA's instructions: Indicate the state and county where the Affidavit is made. This is not necessarily the same location as the Project.
  • Asking the surety last. On a bonded job the consent of surety is its own request to your bond agent, and it can be the slowest paper in the stack.

      Common questions
      
  
      What is an AIA G706?
      
  
      The Contractor's Affidavit of Payment of Debts and Claims: a sworn, notarized statement, sent with the final pay application, that everyone who furnished labor, materials or equipment has been paid and every known claim settled, except the items listed under Exceptions. AIA's own summary says it is typically executed as a condition of final payment.
      
  
      Do subcontractors have to sign a G706?
      
  
      Only if your subcontract asks for it, but many do: a GC that owes the owner a G706 package often asks each sub for the same papers, on the AIA forms or its own versions, before releasing the sub's final payment and retainage. Read your subcontract's close-out or final payment section.
      
  
      What goes in the exceptions on a G706?
      
  
      Every subcontractor, supplier or worker you have not paid in full, with the amount, and every known claim that is not settled, including disputed ones. If there are none, AIA's instructions say to enter None. The owner can ask you to bond each exception.
      
  
      What is the difference between G706 and G706A?
      
  
      G706 swears you have paid your debts and claims, except those listed. G706A swears that the lien waivers attached cover you and everyone below you who may have lien rights, except those listed. Owners who want both get them together, with the waivers attached.
      
  
      What is a G707 consent of surety?
      
  
      The surety's written approval of final payment on a bonded contract, agreeing that final payment does not end its obligations under the bond. The G706 form says that whenever a surety is involved, consent of surety is required. Ask your bond agent early.
