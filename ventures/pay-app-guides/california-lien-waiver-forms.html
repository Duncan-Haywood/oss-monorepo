California lien waiver forms

California sets the wording of lien waivers in statute (Civ. Code §§ 8132–8138). How strict: "Null, void, and unenforceable unless it is in substantially the following form". Below are all four statutory forms, word for word, with a blank line wherever the statute leaves one. Print the one you need, fill it in by hand, and keep a copy.

What California asks of you

  • A waiver given for a payment is "null, void, and unenforceable unless it is in substantially the following form", one form per kind: conditional progress (§ 8132), unconditional progress (§ 8134), conditional final (§ 8136) and unconditional final (§ 8138).
  • Watch the change-order sentence. Every form waives rights for work done "pursuant to a written change order that has been fully executed by the parties prior to the date that this document is signed by the claimant ... unless listed as an Exception below". If a signed change order is still unpaid, list it.
  • The progress forms do not affect "Retentions", "Extras for which the claimant has not received payment" or your contract rights. The conditional progress form also lists earlier conditional waivers you gave "but has not received payment", with their dates and amounts.
  • The final forms have one exception: "Disputed claims for extras in the amount of" a sum you fill in.
  • The unconditional forms carry a "Notice to Claimant" that must be "in at least as large a type as the largest type otherwise in the form": "THIS DOCUMENT IS ENFORCEABLE AGAINST YOU IF YOU SIGN IT, EVEN IF YOU HAVE NOT BEEN PAID."
  • A conditional waiver releases the owner, lender or bond surety only with evidence of payment: your endorsement on a check that has been paid, or your written acknowledgment of payment (§ 8124). No contract term can waive your rights unless you sign one of these waivers (§ 8122), and a statement giving them up is void unless it is one of these waivers or you have been paid in full (§ 8126).

Notes on the forms

  • California: a waiver and release given for a payment is null, void, and unenforceable unless it is in substantially the form of Cal. Civ. Code § 8132, 8134, 8136 or 8138, and it releases the owner, construction lender or payment-bond surety only if it is signed by the claimant (§ 8124(a)).
  • California: a conditional release also needs evidence of payment before it releases them: the claimant's endorsement on a single or joint payee check that has been paid by the financial institution on which it was drawn, or the claimant's written acknowledgment of payment (§ 8124(b)).
  • California's unconditional forms say they are enforceable against the claimant "even if you have not been paid" (§§ 8134, 8138): use them only once the payment has been received.
  • California's final forms list one exception, disputed claims for extras in a stated amount (§§ 8136, 8138); the progress forms except retentions, unpaid extras and contract rights.

Common questions

Does California have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes. Civ. Code §§ 8132–8138 sets 4 forms: conditional and unconditional, progress and final. How strict: "Null, void, and unenforceable unless it is in substantially the following form". The forms on this page are printed word for word from the statute as read on 2026-11-25.

Which California lien waiver form goes with which payment?

Conditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment: conditional progress waivers. Unconditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment: unconditional progress waivers. Conditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment: conditional final waivers. Unconditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment: unconditional final waivers.

When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver in California?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared your bank. Before that, give the conditional form if California has one, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed amounts out of a progress waiver.
