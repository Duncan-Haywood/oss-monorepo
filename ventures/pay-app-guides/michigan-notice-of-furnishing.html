Michigan notice of furnishing: who gets it, the 20 days, and the form

On a Michigan job, a subcontractor or supplier keeps its construction lien by serving a notice of furnishing on the designee and the general contractor named in the job's notice of commencement, within 20 days after first furnishing labor or material (MCL 570.1109(1)). The claim of lien, if you ever record one, must have “a proof of service of a notice of furnishing” attached (MCL 570.1111(4)). This page covers who sends it, to whom, how the 20 days run, what a late notice costs, and the statute's own form word for word.

Who sends it, and who gets it

  • Subcontractors and suppliers send it. Every one of them, at any tier, that wants a lien. “A contractor is not required to provide a notice of furnishing to preserve lien rights arising from his or her contract directly with an owner or lessee” (MCL 570.1109(1)).
  • It goes to the designee and the general contractor, “as named in the notice of commencement at the address shown in the notice of commencement”. The designee is the person the owner or lessee names in the notice of commencement for this (MCL 570.1108(2)(d)).
  • No designee? “If a designee has not been named in the notice of commencement, or if the designee has died, service must be made on the owner or lessee named in the notice of commencement.”
  • Laborers (workers paid wages) send one only after pay goes unpaid: within 30 days after wages were due, or by the fifth day of the second month after unpaid fringe benefits or withholdings were due (MCL 570.1109(2), (3)).

Serve it “either personally or by certified mail”; by certified mail, “service is complete on mailing”. Keep the certified mail receipts: they are the proof of service the claim of lien needs later.

The days are calendar days as the statute states them; this page does not move a date off a weekend or holiday, so serve early.

First, get the notice of commencement

Everything the notice of furnishing needs comes from the notice of commencement: the designee's name and address, the general contractor, and the legal description. On a commercial job the owner records it with the register of deeds before work starts and posts a copy on site (MCL 570.1108(1), (8)). On a home (a residential structure) it is not recorded: the owner prepares one when someone asks for it in writing (MCL 570.1108a(1)).

Ask for it in writing, by certified mail, from the owner, the designee or whoever hired you. Each of them that has it must send you a copy, with a blank notice of furnishing attached, “within 10 days after the date of mailing of a written request by certified mail” (MCL 570.1108(5) to (7)). On a home, include a blank notice of commencement form with the request (MCL 570.1108a(5)).

The 20 days wait for it. If the owner never records one, your time runs “until 20 days after the notice of commencement has been recorded” (MCL 570.1108(10)). If the owner, lessee or designee does not give you a copy when you ask, the time runs “until 20 days after the notice of commencement actually has been furnished” to you (MCL 570.1108(11); 570.1108a(9) for a home). So send the request early and keep its receipt too: it fixes the date your 20 days could start from.

What a late notice still protects

A late notice does not end the lien outright:

  • Work after the notice stays covered: a late notice “does not defeat the lien claimant's right to a construction lien for work performed or materials furnished by the lien claimant after the service of the notice of furnishing” (MCL 570.1109(5)).
  • Work before the notice stays covered “except to the extent that payments were made by or on behalf of the owner or lessee to the contractor pursuant to either a contractor's sworn statement or a waiver of lien in accordance with this act” for your work (MCL 570.1109(6)).

That exception is the risk. An owner who has not heard from you pays the general contractor against a sworn statement that lists your work, and to that extent your lien for the earlier work is gone, even if the GC never pays you. Served on time, the owner knows you are on the job and can insist on your Michigan lien waiver before paying.

After the notice: the claim of lien

The notice is not the lien. The lien “shall cease to exist unless, within 90 days after the lien claimant's last furnishing of labor or material for the improvement, pursuant to the lien claimant's contract, a claim of lien is recorded in the office of the register of deeds for each county” where the property is (MCL 570.1111(1)). A subcontractor's or supplier's claim of lien “shall have attached to it a proof of service of a notice of furnishing” (MCL 570.1111(4)), and within 15 days after recording you serve a copy of the claim and the proof of service on the designee (MCL 570.1111(5)).

Public jobs in Michigan are bonded, not liened: see state public job bond claims.

The form, word for word (MCL 570.1109(4))

A notice of furnishing “if not given on the form attached to the notice of commencement, must be in substantially the following form”. The captions in parentheses sit under each blank line.

NOTICE OF FURNISHING

To: ________________

(name of designee (or owner or lessee) from notice of commencement)

________________

(address from notice of commencement)

Please take notice that the undersigned is furnishing to ________________

(name and address of other contracting party)

certain labor or material for ________________,

(describe type of work)

in connection with the improvements to the real property described in the notice of commencement recorded in liber ________, on page ________, ________________ records,

(name of county)

________________

or (a copy of which is attached to this notice)

WARNING TO OWNER: THIS NOTICE IS REQUIRED BY THE MICHIGAN CONSTRUCTION LIEN ACT. IF YOU HAVE QUESTIONS ABOUT YOUR RIGHTS AND DUTIES UNDER THIS ACT, YOU SHOULD CONTACT AN ATTORNEY TO PROTECT YOU FROM THE POSSIBILITY OF PAYING TWICE FOR THE IMPROVEMENTS TO YOUR PROPERTY.

________________

(name and address of lien claimant)

by ________________

(name and capacity of party signing for lien claimant)

________________

(address of party signing)

Date: ________________

The liber and page are on the register of deeds' recording stamp on the notice of commencement. A home's notice of commencement is not recorded, so attach a copy of it instead. Common questions

Who has to send a Michigan notice of furnishing?

Every subcontractor and supplier that wants to keep a construction lien. A contractor with a contract directly with the owner or lessee does not need one (MCL 570.1109(1)). Laborers send one only when wages or fringe benefits go unpaid (570.1109(2), (3)).

When is the Michigan notice of furnishing due?

Within 20 days after you first furnish labor or material (MCL 570.1109(1)). If the owner never recorded a notice of commencement, or did not give you one when you asked for it in writing by certified mail, the 20 days start when it is recorded or given to you (570.1108(10), (11)).

Who gets the Michigan notice of furnishing?

The designee and the general contractor named in the notice of commencement, at the addresses shown there, delivered in person or sent by certified mail. If no designee is named, or the designee has died, it goes to the owner or lessee named in the notice of commencement (MCL 570.1109(1)).

What happens if I send the Michigan notice of furnishing late?

You keep your lien for work after the notice. For work before it, you keep the lien except to the extent the owner already paid the general contractor for your work on a contractor's sworn statement or a lien waiver (MCL 570.1109(5), (6)).

Is there an official Michigan notice of furnishing form?

Yes. The form attached to the notice of commencement, or one in substantially the form MCL 570.1109(4) prints, with its WARNING TO OWNER. The claim of lien you record later must have a proof of service of the notice attached (570.1111(4)).
