What a construction invoice should show

PartWhat goes in itWhy it matters
HeaderYour company, address and licence number; bill to; job name and address; subcontract or PO no.GCs route invoices by job and PO. An invoice with no PO or job name waits in a pile.
Invoice no., date, work throughYour own number, the date sent, the last day of work it coversThe period stops the same work being billed twice, and the date starts the clock on payment terms.
LinesItem, description, quantity, unit, unit price; a lump sum leaves quantity blankUnit-price lines (SF, LF, CY, EA) let the GC check quantities against the field.
Change ordersEach signed change order on its own line, numbered CO-1, CO-2Unsigned extra work billed as base scope is the most common reason an invoice is cut. Bill it once it is signed.
RetainageHeld as its own negative line at the rate in your contract, and the running total held on the jobYou bill retainage separately at the end, so you need to know how much is out there.
Deposit appliedAny advance the customer already paid, credited backWithout it the customer pays twice for the same work, or holds the invoice to ask.
Amount due, terms, due dateSubtotal less retainage, plus any tax, less the deposit; net days and the date that givesA due date in writing is what a follow-up email points to.

The template has every part above. A line's amount is quantity times unit price, rounded to the cent; a lump-sum line with no quantity counts as one. Retainage is the subtotal times your rate, shown as a negative line, and "retainage held on this job to date" adds it to what you type in as held before, so you know what to bill when the job closes out (getting retainage released).

Invoice or pay application?

Use whichever your contract or customer asks for. Commercial subcontracts with a schedule of values usually call for a monthly pay application; small jobs, service work, residential work and time-and-materials work are usually invoiced.

InvoicePay application
Who asks for itHomeowners, property managers, service customers, many small GCsCommercial GCs and owners, usually written into the subcontract
What it showsThis period's work onlyEvery schedule-of-values line: previous, this period, stored, total to date, percent, balance to finish
RetainageOne line on this invoice; you keep the running totalCalculated on the whole job to date on the summary page

If the GC sends back your invoice and asks for a pay application, start from the free schedule of values template: put each of your invoice lines on the schedule as a line with its full contract value, and what you have billed so far in "from previous application". If you bill a homeowner in stages, a draw schedule is the usual middle ground.

What the template leaves to you

It does arithmetic only. Your contract decides the retainage rate, the payment terms and whether stored materials can be billed; state law may cap retainage (public caps for 26 states) and set how fast a GC must pay you (prompt payment laws). It never guesses sales tax: whether construction labor or materials are taxable depends on your state and the kind of contract, so type the amount your accountant gives you, or leave it blank. If the customer wants a conditional lien waiver with each invoice, make it out for the amount due.

Next invoice: save a copy, bump the invoice number and dates, replace the lines with this period's work, move "retainage held on this job to date" into "held before this invoice", and clear the deposit once it has all been applied.

Common questions

What should a construction invoice include?

Your company name, address and licence number; who it is billed to; the job name and address; the subcontract or PO number; an invoice number and date; the period the work was done; each line of work with a quantity and unit price or a lump sum; any signed change orders on their own lines; retainage held; any deposit applied; the amount due; and the payment terms and due date.

How do I show retainage on a contractor invoice?

Bill the full value of the work, then subtract retainage as its own line, so the GC sees both the gross and the amount due. Keep a running total of retainage held on the job, because you will bill it separately at the end. The template does both: the retainage line is the subtotal times your rate, and a second line adds it to what was held before.

When do I need a pay application instead of an invoice?

When the GC or owner asks for one. Commercial subcontracts with a schedule of values usually require a monthly pay application (a summary page and continuation sheet showing every line's billed to date, retainage and balance to finish) rather than an invoice. Small jobs, service work, T&M and residential work are usually invoiced.

Does the template work in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the .xlsx to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets, or open it in Excel or LibreOffice Calc. It contains only ordinary formulas: no macros, no links to other files and no add-ins.

Should I charge sales tax on a construction invoice?

It depends on your state, the kind of contract and the kind of work, and states treat labor and materials differently. The template leaves sales tax as an amount you type in, so it never guesses a rate. Ask your accountant or your state's revenue department.
