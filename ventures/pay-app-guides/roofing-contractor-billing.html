Pay applications for commercial roofing contractors: roof areas, stored materials and the warranty

Commercial roofing pay applications bill a roof in pieces: by roof area and by system (tear-off, insulation, membrane, flashing, sheet metal), with expensive material often delivered and loaded weeks before it is installed. What turns up at tear-off (rotted deck, wet insulation) is extra work, and the last retainage often waits on the manufacturer's warranty. This page covers each, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where roofing subs lose money on pay apps

  • Material on the roof, not on the bill. Material loaded weeks before install is cash the sub has spent. If the subcontract allows stored materials, bill it with the delivery tickets.
  • Deck covered before it was counted. Rotted deck replaced and covered the same day without a measured, signed quantity is hard to bill later.
  • Retainage waiting on a warranty nobody started. Final retainage that waits on a manufacturer's inspection waits longer when the inspection is booked at the end.
  • Late payment nobody charges for. Many states set when a GC must pay a sub after it is paid, some with interest. Check your state.

Common questions

How should a roofing contractor set up the schedule of values?

By roof area or building and by system (tear-off, insulation, membrane, flashing and sheet metal), with close-out and the warranty on a small line of its own. Each month's percent then comes from what is done on each roof.

Can I bill for roofing materials loaded on the roof but not installed?

Often yes, as stored materials, if your subcontract allows it; attach invoices, delivery tickets and photos. Move them to work completed as they are installed, so they are billed once.

How do I bill deck replacement found during tear-off?

At the unit price your subcontract sets for it, if it has one, else as a change order. Measure and photograph the area before it is covered and get the quantity signed by the GC that day.

Why is my final roofing retainage held?

Often because close-out is not complete: the manufacturer's warranty, the punch list, as-builts or a final lien waiver. Ask the GC in writing which items are open, and send them together.
