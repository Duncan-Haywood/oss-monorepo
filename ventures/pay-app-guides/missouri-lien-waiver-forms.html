Missouri lien waiver form

Missouri sets the wording of lien waivers in statute (RSMo § 429.016.27). How strict: On residential real property an unconditional final waiver "shall only be valid if it is on a form that is substantially as follows"; elsewhere no form. Below is the statutory form, word for word, with a blank line wherever the statute leaves one. Print it, fill it in by hand, and keep a copy.

What Missouri asks of you

  • One form, and only for "residential real property" as § 429.016 defines it: new homes of four units or fewer, condominiums, townhouses and cooperatives built to be sold or occupied. Repair, remodeling or additions to an owner-occupied home of four units or fewer fall under § 429.013 instead, and commercial work has no statutory form.
  • On that property, a waiver given "in exchange for a payment of less than the amount claimed due at that time" waives nothing unless it is the statutory unconditional final waiver (§ 429.016.25). Partial and conditional waivers are allowed, with no set wording.
  • The unconditional final form is "a complete and absolute waiver": it covers work before the date signed and later work under agreements made before that date, and it binds you "notwithstanding claimant's failure to receive any promised payment" (§ 429.016.26, .29). Sign it only once the money has cleared.
  • If you recorded a notice of rights and have been paid in full, you must give it within five calendar days of a written request, or face a presumption of slander of title and a $500 statutory penalty (§ 429.016.30).

Notes on the form

  • Missouri: on residential real property (§ 429.016.1-.2) an unconditional, final mechanic's lien waiver is valid only if it is on a form substantially as in § 429.016.27. It waives all lien rights for work before the date signed and for later work under agreements made before that date (§ 429.016.26), and binds the claimant even if the promised payment never arrives (§ 429.016.29).
  • Claimant's legal name and the name, title or position, address and telephone number of the signer must be typed or printed above or below the signature, and the date signed beside it (§ 429.016.27).
  • A claimant that recorded a notice of rights and has been paid in full must give this waiver within five calendar days of a written request (§ 429.016.30).
  • Only the lien right is waived: a waiver signed for less than full payment does not release any other claim, remedy or cause of action (§ 429.016.28).

Common questions

Does Missouri have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes. RSMo § 429.016.27 sets one form: unconditional final, residential property only. How strict: On residential real property an unconditional final waiver "shall only be valid if it is on a form that is substantially as follows"; elsewhere no form. When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver in Missouri?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared your bank. Before that, give the conditional form if Missouri has one, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed amounts out of a progress waiver.
