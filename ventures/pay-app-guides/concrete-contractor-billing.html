Pay applications for concrete contractors: unit prices, quantities and extras

Concrete subcontracts (foundations, slabs, flatwork, site concrete and paving) are often written by unit price: so much per cubic yard, square foot or linear foot. The monthly pay application then bills the quantity placed, not a percent. That changes how the schedule of values is set up, what the GC checks and where the errors creep in. This page covers unit-price billing, extras and retainage, with the free guide or tool for each.

Where concrete subs lose money on pay apps

  • Rounding. A unit price times a quantity has to come out to the cent the same way every month, or the to-date total drifts from the quantities. Keep the quantity and price, and let the dollars be computed.
  • Overruns placed before the change order. Under most subcontracts the GC pays the contract quantity until a change is approved. Ask early.
  • Pump and standby time on a verbal OK. Tickets signed on the day of the pour are the ones that get paid.
  • Late payment nobody charges for. Many states set when a GC must pay a sub after it is paid, some with interest. Check your state.

Common questions

How do you bill a unit-price contract on a pay application?

Each line carries a unit, a unit price and a contract quantity. Each month you report the quantity placed this period; the amount is that quantity times the unit price, and the to-date quantity shows how much of the contract quantity is used.

What happens when concrete quantities run over the contract quantity?

Under most subcontracts the extra quantity needs an approved change order before it is paid. Track quantity to date against the contract quantity and ask for the change before the overrun.

How should pump time and standby be billed?

As extra work on a T&M ticket signed the same day, showing the pump, crew hours, equipment and any material, unless your subcontract already prices it.

Can lump-sum concrete work be billed by percent complete?

Yes. Split the schedule of values by element and area (footings, walls, slabs, decks, flatwork) and bill each line's percent complete.
