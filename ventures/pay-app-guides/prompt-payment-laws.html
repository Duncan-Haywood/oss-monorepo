Prompt payment laws for subcontractors: how long the GC has to pay you

Most states have a prompt payment law that sets how fast a general contractor must pass the owner's payment down to the subcontractor who did the work, and many add interest when it is late. The clock usually starts when the GC receives the owner's payment, not when you send your pay application, so the first thing to find out each month is when the owner paid.

What to do when a GC pays late

  • Find out when the owner paid. On public jobs this is often a public record; on private jobs, ask the GC in writing. The deadline counts from that date.
  • Put it in writing. Send a short letter or email naming the pay application, the amount, the statute and the interest so far. Several of these laws make suspending work or claiming interest depend on written notice.
  • Watch your lien deadlines at the same time. Prompt payment interest does not stop the clock on mechanics lien or bond claim deadlines. The free mechanics lien deadline calculator works out the dates.
  • Do not sign an unconditional waiver for money you have not received. See conditional vs unconditional lien waivers.
  • Read the subcontract. Pay-when-paid and pay-if-paid clauses, contract interest rates and dispute terms change what these statutes give you, differently in each state. That question is for a construction attorney in your state.
