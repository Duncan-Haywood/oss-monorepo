Why pay applications get sent back, and a checker

A pay application that comes back for corrections usually misses that month's payment cycle, and the GC's reviewer rarely says everything that is wrong the first time. Most of what they mark is not arithmetic inside the new application; it is the new application disagreeing with last month's approved one. A spreadsheet cannot see that. This page lists what a reviewer compares and checks your draft against last month's application line by line.

Before you send it

  • Start from the approved copy. If the GC cut a line last month, its "from previous" this month is the cut figure. Ask for the approved continuation sheet if you did not get it back.
  • Attach what changed. Signed change orders for new CO lines, invoices and storage proof for new stored materials, and the written retainage reduction if the rate moved.
  • Send the right waiver. A conditional progress waiver for this application's amount, with unpaid change orders written in as exceptions (which waiver when; the change order log writes the exceptions).
  • Meet the cut-off. Most GCs collect sub pay apps by a set day so they can bill the owner; late ones roll to next month.

Common questions

Why do pay applications get sent back?

Most often because this month's sheet does not match last month's approved one: a "from previous" column copied from the submitted file instead of the approved one, an edited scheduled value, a new line with no change order behind it, previous certificates typed as last month's payment, or stored materials that vanish or are billed twice.

What goes in previous certificates for payment?

Last month's total earned less retainage, which is the amount certified to date. Typing it as what was paid, or as the last payment alone, makes this month's payment due wrong.

Can I change a scheduled value on a pay application?

Not once the schedule of values is approved. Changes come in as change order lines, and every line stays in the same order and numbering until closeout.
