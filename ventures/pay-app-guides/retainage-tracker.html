Retainage tracker for subcontractors

Retainage is often the profit on a job, and it is the money most likely to be forgotten: it does not show on an ordinary A/R aging report because nobody invoiced it yet. Paste one row per job, or start from the example. The page works out retainage still outstanding, when each job's release falls due from the terms you enter, how long the overdue ones have waited, completed jobs with no release billed, totals by GC, and a chase list in the order worth calling.

Your job list stays in this browser. It does the arithmetic and flags; your subcontract (and, on some public work, a statute) decides when retainage is actually owed.

The default release days apply to any job whose own row leaves them blank. Leave it blank if your jobs differ; a completed job with no days gets no due date and is listed so you can fill it in. Public-work release rules for some states are in retainage explained.

How the numbers are worked out

  
    
    
  • Outstanding = retainage held to date − retainage released to date, per job. Jobs paid in full drop out of the totals.
    • 
    
  • Due = completion date + release days (the job's own, or the default). No completion date: the job is still open and nothing is due. No release days: no due date, and the job is listed.
    • 
    
  • Overdue = days from the due date to the as-of date. Aging buckets: not complete; complete, not yet due; no release days; overdue 1–30, 31–90, 91–180 and over 180 days.
    • 
    
  • Carrying cost = outstanding × your rate × days overdue ÷ 365, simple interest. It is what the wait costs you at your own cost of money, not interest anyone owes you; any statutory or contract interest is set by its own terms (see prompt payment laws).
    • 
    
  • Checks: completed jobs with no release billed; overdue jobs; more released than held; retainage above 10% of the contract; release billed before completion; completion dates after the as-of date; one GC holding over half of your retainage; repeated job names.
    • 
    
  • Not decided here: whether a condition to release (closeout documents, final waiver, punch list, owner acceptance, pay-when-paid) has been met, and what your contract or state law says.
    • 
  






  
Common questions

  
How do I track retainage across jobs?

  
Keep one row per job with the retainage held to date, the retainage released, the completion date, the number of days your subcontract allows for release after completion, and the date you billed the release. Outstanding retainage is held minus released; the release is due on the completion date plus the release days.

  
Why does my A/R aging report not show retainage?

  
Retainage is usually not invoiced until it is released, so in many setups (QuickBooks Online among them) it sits in a separate retainage receivable account and never ages like an open invoice. A retainage tracker ages it from the date the release fell due instead. See retainage in QuickBooks.

  
When is retainage overdue?

  
When the date your subcontract (or, on some public work, a statute) sets for release has passed and it has not been paid. That date is usually a number of days after substantial or final completion, often conditional on closeout documents and a final lien waiver. This page uses the days you enter; it does not decide what your contract or state law says.
