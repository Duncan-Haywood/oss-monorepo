Pay applications for structural steel and misc metals: detailing, fabrication, erection by sequence

Steel pay applications bill a job in three stages that are months apart: detailing, fabrication in the shop, then erection by sequence. Embeds and anchor bolts go to the site before almost anything else, and miscellaneous metals (stairs, rails, lintels) trail to the end. This page covers each, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where steel subs lose money on pay apps

  • Fabricated steel in the yard, not on the bill. Mill steel, shop labor and coatings are cash spent months before erection. If the subcontract allows off-site stored materials, bill them by sequence with the backup it asks for.
  • Rework on a revised drawing nobody priced. A change after pieces are cut is paid when it was raised in writing first. An RFI that names the pieces already fabricated is the record.
  • Embeds billed with the frame. Embeds go to the site months before the steel. Billed on their own line, they are paid that month, not at erection.
  • Back charges with no record. A fit-up problem caused by an anchor bolt set out of tolerance is not your cost. Survey or photograph it before you fix it and write it up the same day.

Common questions

How should a steel contractor set up the schedule of values?

By sequence or area, with detailing, fabrication and erection on separate lines, embeds and anchor bolts on their own line, and miscellaneous metals (stairs, rails, lintels) apart from the frame. Each month's percent then comes from what is approved, fabricated and standing.

Can I bill for steel fabricated in my shop before it is delivered?

Only if your subcontract allows stored materials off site. Expect to show invoices, photos or a fabrication list, and often a bill of sale, insurance on the stored steel and a right for the GC to inspect it. Move it to work completed as it is erected, so it is billed once.

Can a steel pay application be billed by the ton?

Yes, when the subcontract prices it that way: each line carries tons and a unit price, and each month you report the tons fabricated or erected, backed by the shop's fabrication list or the erection sequence.

How do I get paid for rework after a design change?

Send an RFI as soon as the change arrives, naming the pieces already fabricated or erected, and price the rework and extra steel as a change order before you do it.
