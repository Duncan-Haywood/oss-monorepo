Punch list template and tracker for subcontractors

The punch list is the last thing between a subcontractor and its retainage. At the walk-through the GC, architect, owner or an inspector lists what is left to fix; you fix each item, mark it done, and the GC back-checks and accepts it. Items that are fixed but never walked, or that belong to another trade, keep the list open for weeks. Paste your list below to see where each item stands and send one back-check request.

What each punch item should record

A template is only these columns. Keep one row per item, numbered, so the GC and you are talking about the same thing.

ColumnWhy it matters
Item numberUse the GC's numbers when the list came from them. "Item 14 is done" beats "the plate by the lobby".
LocationRoom, gridline or floor, as the drawings name it, so the crew and the back-check find the same spot.
DescriptionWhat is wrong, in the words of whoever listed it. Rewording it invites "that is not what I wrote".
Listed by, listed and due datesGC, architect, owner, inspector or your own walk, and when they need it. Without a due date, nothing is ever overdue and nothing is ever finished.
Fixed (date and who)Your record that the work was done, and the date you asked for the back-check from.
Accepted (date and who)The GC's initials or a name and date. This is what you show when retainage is held "for the punch list".
Not oursThe reason an item is another trade's work or built as drawn. Tell the GC in writing; it stays out of your count here, not off their list.

      Closing a punch list fast
      
  
        
    
      1. Walk your own scope first. A list you write before the GC's walk is shorter than the one they write, and it is yours to schedule.
        2. 
    
      2. Get the list in writing, numbered, with due dates. Ask for it the day of the walk. Push back on items that are not yours that same week, in writing; once a list has been open a month, nobody remembers who caused the damaged tile.
        3. 
    
      3. Fix in batches by area and record the date and who fixed each item as you go.
        4. 
    
      4. Ask for one back-check per batch. One email naming every fixed item (the tracker writes it) gets them walked together, instead of a call per item.
        5. 
    
      5. Get each item initialled. A signed or initialled list is what ends the "still open" conversation when you ask for retainage. Then send the request: getting retainage released lists the close-out documents usually asked for alongside the punch list and writes the request letter.

      Common questions
      
  
      What is a punch list in construction?
      
  
      The list of small items still to fix or finish near the end of a job, written at a walk-through by the GC, architect, owner or an inspector. Each item is fixed, marked done, then back-checked and accepted by whoever listed it.
      
  
      Who is responsible for punch list items?
      
  
      The trade whose work the item is. An item that is another trade's work, or work built as the drawings show, should be raised with the GC in writing straight away, so it is reassigned instead of staying on your list.
      
  
      Does the punch list hold up retainage?
      
  
      Often. Many subcontracts tie retainage release to completion and acceptance of your scope, and an open punch item is the most common reason given for holding the balance. Your subcontract says what release actually depends on.
