Mississippi lien waiver forms

Mississippi sets the wording of lien waivers in statute (Miss. Code §§ 85-7-419, 85-7-433). How strict: "Shall substantially follow" the form; a form without its NOTICE is "unenforceable and invalid". Below are both statutory forms, word for word, with a blank line wherever the statute leaves one. Print the one you need, fill it in by hand, and keep a copy.

What Mississippi asks of you

  • Mississippi's Code is also published officially only through LexisNexis. Both sections were created new by SB 2622 (2014, approved 11 April 2014), which we read as sent to the Governor on the Legislature's site. We found no later amendment in the Legislature's lists of bills for 2015 to 2026, but check the current Code before relying on this section.
  • Two forms, an "Interim Waiver and Release Upon Payment" and a "Waiver and Release Upon Final Payment" (§ 85-7-433(1), (2)). Their wording follows Georgia's pre-2021 forms closely. Each waives "upon the receipt of the sum of $____". The interim form covers work through a date you fill in and excepts "any retained amounts"; the final form has no exception. A blank filled in wrongly does not invalidate the form "if the subject matter of the release reasonably may be determined" (§ 85-7-419(2), (3)).
  • The trap is the 60 days. Once you sign, the amount is "conclusively deemed paid in full" at the earliest of the money actually arriving, a separate written acknowledgment of payment in full, or "sixty (60) days after the date of the execution of the waiver and release", unless before then you file an affidavit of nonpayment in the county where the property is and send the owner a copy (§ 85-7-419(5)(b)). The form's notice also names filing a claim of lien. These are the 60 days and the two ways out that Georgia dropped in 2021.
  • The affidavit of nonpayment has its own form (§ 85-7-433(3)). Within two days of filing it, a copy goes to the owner by registered or certified mail or statutory overnight delivery. Once filed, the waiver is suspended until you are paid in full ((5)(e)). Once you are paid, you must on request sign an affidavit of payment in full, which voids the first affidavit when recorded ((5)(c)).
  • Every form, the affidavit included, is sworn before a notary ("SWORN TO AND SUBSCRIBED BEFORE ME"). The final form prints its signature and notary lines both above and below its notice, so plan for a notary when you sign either one. No lien or bond right can be waived before the work is furnished (§ 85-7-419(1)).

Notes on the forms

  • Mississippi: Miss. Code Ann. § 85-7-433 gives two forms, an interim waiver and release upon payment ((1)) and a waiver and release upon final payment ((2)), which a waiver "shall substantially follow" (§ 85-7-419(2), (3)). Neither is conditional or unconditional; the same form is used whether or not the money has arrived. A blank filled in wrongly does not invalidate the form "if the subject matter of the release reasonably may be determined".
  • Sixty days (§ 85-7-419(5)(b)): once signed, the amount is conclusively deemed paid in full at the earliest of the payment actually arriving, a separate written acknowledgment of payment in full, or 60 days after the waiver was signed, unless before then you file an affidavit of nonpayment in the county where the property is and send the owner a copy. If you sign before you are paid and the money does not come, the 60 days run anyway. The form's NOTICE also names filing a claim of lien.
  • The interim waiver covers lien and bond rights through the date you enter and excepts retained amounts; the final waiver has no exception. No lien or bond right can be waived before the work is furnished (§ 85-7-419(1)).
  • Each form is sworn before a notary: sign it in the notary's presence. The final payment form prints its signature and notary lines twice, above and below the notice, as the statute does.
  • Wording: from SB 2622 (2014), the Act that created §§ 85-7-419 and 85-7-433, as sent to the Governor and published by the Mississippi Legislature; no later amendment was found. The official Code (published through LexisNexis) was not read; check it, or have counsel check it, before relying on the form.

Common questions

Does Mississippi have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes. Miss. Code §§ 85-7-419, 85-7-433 sets 2 forms: interim payment and final payment, each waiving "upon the receipt of the sum", plus an affidavit of nonpayment. How strict: "Shall substantially follow" the form; a form without its NOTICE is "unenforceable and invalid". The forms on this page are printed word for word from the statute as read on 2026-11-26.

Which Mississippi lien waiver form goes with which payment?

Interim Waiver and Release upon Payment: progress payments, before or after the money clears (the form itself is neither conditional nor unconditional). Waiver and Release upon Final Payment: final payments, before or after the money clears (the form itself is neither conditional nor unconditional).

When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver in Mississippi?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared your bank. Before that, give the conditional form if Mississippi has one, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed amounts out of a progress waiver.
