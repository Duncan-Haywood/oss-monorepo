T&M tickets: price them, get them signed, turn them into change orders

A time and material (T&M) ticket is the field record of extra work: who worked, how many hours, what material and equipment went in, signed by the GC's superintendent the same day. It is not money yet. On most jobs the ticket has to become an executed change order before it can go on the pay application, and a ticket that never makes that trip is extra work done for free. Paste your tickets below to price them at your own markups and see which ones are at risk.

What a ticket should show

A ticket the GC can dispute is a ticket you will discount. These are the fields reviewers look for; your subcontract may name more.

FieldWhy it matters
Ticket number and date of the workOne ticket per day per crew. A ticket that covers a week is hard to check against the GC's daily reports.
Where and whyLocation, and what directed the work (an RFI answer, a field directive, an ASI, a named person). That is what ties the work to a change and not to your base scope.
Each worker or trade, with hoursName or classification and hours, regular and overtime apart. Unnamed labor is the first line a GC strikes.
Material, with quantitiesWhat was installed, how much, and the supplier invoice when it comes in.
Equipment, with hours or daysOwned or rented, and for how long; the rental ticket for rented equipment.
GC signature, printed name and dateSigned on the day. A signature confirms the time and material were spent; it usually does not agree the price or that it is extra, so the change order still matters.

T&M ticket log

One row per line: labor, material, equipment, lower-tier sub or other. Rows with the same ticket number are one ticket. Columns: Ticket, Date, Type, Description, Qty, Rate, Signed by, CO (an Amount column works for lump-sum lines). Leave Signed by blank for an unsigned ticket, and fill CO once the ticket is on a change order. It runs in this browser tab; nothing is uploaded.

Markups from your subcontract

No markup is assumed: a blank is 0%. Use the overhead and profit percentages your subcontract allows on changes, by cost type; the example's figures are only an example.

      Change order request

      
  
      
  
      The request lists only figures computed above. It does not give notice of a claim, reserve rights or say what your contract entitles you to; if a change is disputed, read your subcontract's changes and claims clauses, and talk to a construction attorney before a deadline in them passes.
      

      


      
  
      From ticket to pay application
      
  
        
    
      1. Get it signed on the day. A superintendent who signs tickets every afternoon remembers the work. One handed a stack at month end does not, and the stack gets cut.
        2. 
    
      2. Price it at the subcontract's rates. Labor at your labor rates (burdened, if the subcontract allows), material at invoice cost, equipment at the agreed rates, then the markup the subcontract allows on each. If the work was directed as "T&M not to exceed", say on each ticket how much of the cap is used.
        3. 
    
      3. Send a change order request. Group the signed tickets for one change and ask for a change order number. The log above flags any signed ticket that has waited longer than your chase window.
        4. 
    
      4. Bill it when the change order is executed. An executed change order becomes a new line on the schedule of values (how change orders enter the SOV). Billing a ticket before then is a common reason pay apps get sent back (pay application checker).
        5. 
    
      5. Keep the notice clock in view. Subcontracts usually set a deadline for written notice of extra work or a claim. A signed ticket may or may not count as that notice; your subcontract decides, not this page.

      Common questions
      
  
      Can I bill a T&M ticket on my pay application?
      
  
      On most jobs, not until the ticket becomes an executed change order, which then becomes a new line on the schedule of values. Billing a ticket before then is a common reason pay apps get sent back.
      
  
      What should a T&M ticket include?
      
  
      The ticket number and date of the work, where and why it was done, each worker or trade with regular and overtime hours apart, material with quantities, equipment with hours or days, and the GC's signature, printed name and date, signed on the day.
      
  
      Does a signed T&M ticket mean the GC agreed to pay?
      
  
      Usually not. A signature confirms the time and material were spent; it usually does not agree the price or that the work is extra, so the change order still matters. Whether a ticket counts as written notice of extra work is for your subcontract to decide.
