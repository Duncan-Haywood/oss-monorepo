Pay applications for masonry contractors: walls by area, stored units and cold weather

Masonry pay applications bill walls: CMU, brick veneer and stone, by building area or elevation, with pallets of units delivered well before they are laid. The mockup, cold-weather protection and the final cleaning are where money is often left on the table. This page covers each, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where masonry subs lose money on pay apps

  • Pallets on site, not on the bill. Units delivered and paid for weeks before they are laid are cash the sub has spent. If the subcontract allows stored materials, bill them.
  • Cold-weather protection on a verbal OK. Heaters, enclosures and fuel that run every day of a cold spell add up. A ticket signed each day is the one that gets paid.
  • A front-loaded scaffolding line. Scaffolding billed up front is a line a reviewer cuts. Front-loading check compares each line's markup with the job's.
  • Final cleaning that never closes. Retainage that waits on cleaning and punch waits longer without a written list both sides have agreed.

Common questions

How should a masonry contractor set up the schedule of values?

By area or elevation and by material (CMU, brick, stone, cast stone), with the mockup, scaffolding and cleaning on their own lines. Each month's percent then comes from the walls that are up.

Can I bill for brick and block delivered but not laid?

Often yes, as stored materials, if your subcontract allows it; attach invoices, delivery tickets and photos. Move them to work completed as they are laid, so they are billed once.

Who pays for cold-weather masonry protection?

Whoever your subcontract says. If it is not in your price, ask for a change order before the protection goes up and keep a signed ticket for each day it is used.

Should the mockup be its own line on the pay application?

Yes, when the subcontract asks for one: it is real work that can be billed when built and approved, and a separate line keeps it from being argued into the wall price.
