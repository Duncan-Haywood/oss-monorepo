Pay applications for HVAC, plumbing and mechanical contractors

Mechanical subcontractors (HVAC, plumbing, piping and sheet metal) bill most commercial jobs on a monthly pay application. What sets the trade apart: equipment such as rooftop units, boilers and water heaters is a large share of the contract and arrives in a few deliveries, the last part of the job (start-up, testing and balancing, O&M manuals, training) is easy to underfund, and retainage often waits on those close-out items. This page covers how to bill each one, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where mechanical subs lose money on pay apps

  • Equipment billed before the contract allows it. Read the stored-materials clause first: some allow on-site material only, some off-site with conditions. On federal work, FAR 52.232-5 sets the rule for off-site material.
  • Start-up and balancing left with too little value. When the SOV is loaded toward rough-in, the last months bring little cash while retainage is still held. The front-loading check shows the balanced version.
  • Close-out documents gathered at the end. Retainage is often released only once the O&M manuals, warranties and test reports are in. Start the binder at the first equipment delivery.
  • Short payments nobody reconciles. When a check comes in under the certified amount, find which lines were cut before the next application repeats the gap.

Common questions

How do HVAC contractors bill for equipment that is not installed yet?

As stored materials, if the subcontract allows it, with the invoices, delivery proof and photos it asks for; off-site storage usually needs more, such as insurance and title papers. When the unit is set, move its value from stored materials to work completed.

Should start-up and testing and balancing be separate schedule of values lines?

It is common and it helps: each is billed when done, and giving them a fair value keeps cash coming at the end of the job instead of leaving only retainage.

Why is my retainage held after the work is finished?

Most often because close-out items are missing: O&M manuals, warranties, as-builts, test and balance reports or training. Check your subcontract's release conditions and send the full package with your release request.

Do plumbing and HVAC subs need lien waivers from their suppliers?

GCs often ask for waivers from your suppliers and lower-tier subs with each pay application. Collect conditional waivers with the application and unconditional ones once you have paid them.
