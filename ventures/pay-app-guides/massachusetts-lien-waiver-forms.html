Massachusetts lien waiver form

Massachusetts sets the wording of lien waivers in statute (M.G.L. c. 254, § 32(4)). How strict: Agreements barring a lien are "void and unenforceable", except this form given "substantially in the following form with no material deviation therefrom". Below is the statutory form, word for word, with a blank line wherever the statute leaves one. Print it, fill it in by hand, and keep a copy.

What Massachusetts asks of you

  • The rule comes first: a covenant or agreement in or connected with a construction contract "purporting to bar the filing of a notice of contract or the taking of any steps to enforce a lien", or to subordinate those rights, "is against public policy and is void and unenforceable" (c. 254, § 32). Four things are excepted, among them lien waivers by a principal on a lien bond and "statements ... of amounts due or paid".
  • The fourth exception is a form: "partial waivers and subordinations of liens given by persons who have filed or recorded notices of contract under section two substantially in the following form with no material deviation therefrom". It is a progress waiver. Massachusetts prints no final or unconditional form.
  • Its header is your pay application: original contract amount, approved change orders, adjusted contract amount, completed to date, less retainage, total payable to date, less previous payments, current amount due, pending change orders and disputed claims (lines 1 to 10). It must match the application it goes with.
  • It takes effect "upon receipt of" the payment, stated in words and figures. It keeps back "retainage, unpaid agreed or pending change orders, and disputed claims as stated above". So fill in lines 5, 9 and 10, or those amounts are not excepted.
  • Part (b) also subordinates your lien for work through "the twenty-fifth day after the end of the above payment period", to the extent the lender actually advances funds by then. It is "Signed under the penalties of perjury".
  • Whether a general-form waiver is enforceable on a Massachusetts job is a question for a Massachusetts lawyer. Section 32 is the place to start.

Notes on the form

  • Massachusetts: Mass. Gen. Laws c. 254, § 32 makes a covenant or agreement purporting to bar the filing of a notice of contract or the taking of steps to enforce a lien, or to subordinate those rights, void and unenforceable, except as § 32(1)-(4) list. This form is exception (4), for persons who have filed or recorded notices of contract under § 2, and must be substantially in the statute's form with no material deviation.
  • The waiver takes effect upon receipt of the payment it names, and excepts retainage, unpaid agreed or pending change orders, and disputed claims as stated on lines 5, 9 and 10. Part (b) also subordinates lien rights for work through the twenty-fifth day after the payment period, to the extent the lender/mortgagee actually advances funds by then.
  • It is signed under the penalties of perjury: check every amount against the application before signing.
  • Giving it does not affect the lien rights of any other person claiming a lien under chapter 254 (§ 32).

Common questions

Does Massachusetts have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes. M.G.L. c. 254, § 32(4) sets one form: a partial waiver and subordination of lien, for persons who filed or recorded a notice of contract. How strict: Agreements barring a lien are "void and unenforceable", except this form given "substantially in the following form with no material deviation therefrom". The form on this page is printed word for word from the statute as read on 2026-11-26.

Which Massachusetts lien waiver form goes with which payment?

Partial Waiver and Subordination of Lien: A partial (progress) waiver on a Massachusetts job given by a person who has filed or recorded a notice of contract under Mass. Gen. Laws c. 254, § 2. Section 32(4) exempts partial waivers and subordinations of lien given by such persons from § 32's rule that agreements barring lien filing are void, when they are substantially in this form with no material deviation from it..

When should I sign an unconditional lien waiver in Massachusetts?

Only once the payment it covers has cleared your bank. Before that, give the conditional form if Massachusetts has one, and keep retainage, unapproved change orders and disputed amounts out of a progress waiver.
