How to bill stored materials on a pay application

Switchgear, fixtures, a generator or a truckload of pipe can be paid for before it is installed, as stored materials. The money is real, and so are the two ways it goes wrong: billing the same material twice once it is installed, and billing material the contract does not let you bill yet. This page explains the monthly rollforward and gives you a tool that does it line by line.

Where stored materials sit on the continuation sheet

The continuation sheet has one column for work completed from previous applications, one for work completed this period, and a separate one for materials presently stored (not in either work column). The three add up to total completed and stored to date, which is what the line's percentage, balance to finish and retainage are worked from.

The stored column is a snapshot: what is on hand, unused, at the end of this period. It is not a running total. Each month it rolls forward:

stored now = stored on last pay app + delivered this period − installed this period
work this period = installed from stored + other work this period

Installed material moves out of the stored column and into this period's work in the same month. Done that way, a dollar of material is billed exactly once: first as stored, then as work. The schedule of values calculator and the pay application generator both take the result.

Worked example: switchgear

A $60,000 switchgear line. Last month $15,000 of gear was delivered and billed as stored, along with $9,000 of earlier work. This month $9,000 of that gear is set and terminated, with $1,500 of extra labour.

ColumnRightBilled twice
From previous applications$9,000.00$9,000.00
This period ($9,000 installed + $1,500 labour)$10,500.00$10,500.00
Materials presently stored$6,000.00$15,000.00
Total completed and stored$25,500.00$34,500.00

Leaving the stored column at $15,000 bills the installed $9,000 a second time. A careful reviewer catches it, sends the pay app back, and the whole month's payment waits. One who doesn't leaves you over-billed, which comes back later as a deduction.

On site and off site

Material delivered to the job site is the usual case. Material at a supplier, fabricator, your shop or a warehouse is off site, and contracts treat it more strictly because the owner is paying for something it cannot see and may not own.

On federal work the rule is written down. The payment clause for fixed-price construction contracts, FAR 52.232-5 (read 2026-11-25), says the contracting officer "may authorize material delivered on the site and preparatory work done to be taken into consideration", and that "Material delivered to the Contractor at locations other than the site also may be taken into consideration if" the consideration is "specifically authorized by this contract" and the contractor furnishes "satisfactory evidence that it has acquired title to such material" and "that the material will be used to perform this contract."

Private and state contracts set their own terms, usually in the payment article of the prime contract, flowed down to you by the subcontract. Read that article before billing anything off site. The tool below flags off-site material unless you tick that your contract allows it.

The backup reviewers commonly ask for

Your contract lists what it requires. This is the list to have ready before you ask:

  • The supplier's invoice for each item billed as stored, matching the amount on the line. Some contracts pay invoice cost only, not your markup, until it is installed.
  • Delivery tickets or receiving reports showing it arrived, and a dated photo of it on site.
  • For off-site material: evidence of title (a paid invoice or bill of sale), a certificate of insurance covering it where it is kept, the storage address, the material tagged or set apart for the job, and access for the owner to inspect it.
  • Paid-to-date proof, if the contract makes payment for stored material depend on you having paid the supplier. A conditional waiver from the supplier may serve.
  • A rollforward like the one below, so the reviewer can see last month's stored, this month's deliveries, what was installed and what is left.

Retainage usually applies to stored materials too, sometimes at a different rate from work. See retainage explained.

Common questions

  
Where do stored materials go on a pay application?

  
On the continuation sheet they go in their own column, materials presently stored, which holds material bought for the job and not yet installed. It is added to work completed to give total completed and stored to date, and it is not part of the work-completed columns.

  
What happens to stored materials when they are installed?

  
The installed value leaves the stored column and is billed as work completed this period. Stored now equals stored on the last pay app plus deliveries this period minus what was installed. If installed material is added to work completed without being taken out of stored, it is billed twice.

  
Can I bill for materials stored off site?

  
Only if your contract allows it. The federal construction payment clause, FAR 52.232-5, counts off-site material only when the contract specifically authorizes it and the contractor shows evidence of title and that the material will be used on that contract. Private contracts vary; read the payment article of yours.

  
Can I bill my markup on stored materials?

  
It depends on the contract. Some pay stored material at invoice cost and release the rest of the line's value as it is installed. If yours is silent, ask the GC's project accountant before the first pay app rather than after a rejection.
