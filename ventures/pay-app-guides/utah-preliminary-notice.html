Utah preliminary notice: the 20-day registry filing

On a Utah job the preliminary notice is not mailed to anyone: it is filed with the State Construction Registry, “no later than 20 days after the day on which the person commences providing construction work on the real property” (Utah Code § 38-1a-501(1)(a)). On a private job everyone who may want a lien files one, the general contractor too, and “A person who fails to file a preliminary notice as required in this section may not claim a construction lien” (§ 38-1a-501(1)(e)). Enter your dates for the last day, what a late filing still protects, the notice of completion cut-off and the lien recording date, then list what the filing must carry so you have it in hand when you file.

Who files, and how many

  • Private jobs: everyone who may want a lien. The section speaks of “A person who desires to claim a construction lien” and names no exceptions, so the original contractor, every sub at every tier and every supplier files (§ 38-1a-501(1)(a), (e)). Utah differs from Arizona and California here, where the GC in contract with the owner sends nothing.
  • Government jobs: subcontractors. A person with a contract with the owner and a laborer paid wages are excepted; a temporary labor or professional employer company is not (§ 38-1b-202(1)). The notice is attached to the project's notice of commencement, and the claim is on the payment bond, not the property (see state public job bond claims).
  • One per project. “a person required by this section to give preliminary notice is required to give only one notice for each construction project” (§ 38-1a-501(3)(a)), and it covers your work “under a single original contract, including construction work that the person provides to more than one supervisory subcontractor under that original contract” (§ 38-1a-501(1)(b)). A second original contract on the same project needs its own (§ 38-1a-501(3)(b)).
  • Why the GC's filing matters to everyone. Every construction lien on the project “relates back to, and takes effect as of, the time of the first preliminary notice filing” (§ 38-1a-503(1)). Ask the GC for its entry number: a sub whose notice links to the original contractor's notice by that number has substantially complied (§ 38-1a-501(2)(b)).

Filing late, and the notice of completion

  • Late is not too late, at first. You may file after the 20 days, but you “may not claim a construction lien for construction work the person provides to the construction project before the date that is five days after the preliminary notice is filed” (§ 38-1a-501(1)(c)(ii)). File the day you notice the miss: every day of delay is a day of work outside the lien.
  • The hard stop. Once an owner, GC, lender, surety or title company files a notice of completion (§ 38-1a-507), a preliminary notice “has no effect if it is filed more than 10 days after the filing of a notice of completion” (§ 38-1a-501(1)(d)).
  • Big commercial jobs: the balance statement. On a nonresidential job over $500,000 and 120 days with no payment bond, the owner or GC files a notice of intent to obtain final completion at least 45 days ahead, and you then amend your preliminary notice within 20 days with what is still owed and what is in dispute (§ 38-1a-506(1) to (3)).
  • Government jobs. A late notice “becomes effective on the date that is five days after the date on which the preliminary notice is filed” (§ 38-1b-202(4)), and work before then can be claimed only against the person who hired you (§ 38-1b-202(5)).

What the filing must carry

The registry's own form asks the questions; § 38-1a-501(1)(h) says what “A preliminary notice shall include”. Fill in what you know to see what is still missing before you sit down to file. This is a checklist, not a form to send.

One of the four identifiers is enough (§ 38-1a-501(1)(h)(vii)); the GC's entry number is the easiest to get and links your notice to its filing. If the registry tells you a parcel number is new to it, either yours is the first filing on the project or the number is wrong (§ 38-1a-501(1)(g)): check it. Filing by paper or another “alternate means” leaves you responsible for correcting it before the 20 days run (§ 38-1a-501(4)). Not legal advice.

After the notice: the lien and the lawsuit

  • Record the lien with the county recorder no later than 180 days after final completion of the original contract if no notice of completion is filed, or 90 days after a notice of completion is filed, “but not later than 180 days after the date on which final completion of the original contract occurs” (§ 38-1a-502(1)(a)). A sub doing substantial work after the certificate of occupancy or final inspection has 180 days after its own last work (§ 38-1a-502(1)(b)).
  • Copy the owner by delivery or certified mail within 30 days of filing the lien; missing it costs you costs and attorney fees against the owner, not the lien (§ 38-1a-502(4)).
  • Sue to enforce within 180 days after filing the notice of lien, and record a notice of pendency of the action in the same time or the lien is void against most others (§ 38-1a-701(2), (3)).
  • Owner-occupied homes. A lien on an owner-occupied residence must say how the owner can have it removed under § 38-11-107 (§ 38-1a-502(2)(i)); Utah's Residence Lien Restriction and Lien Recovery Fund Act may bar the lien if the owner paid the contractor in full. Read that chapter before liening a home.

Common questions

Who has to file a Utah preliminary notice?

On a private Utah job, everyone who may want a construction lien, the original contractor included: a person who fails to file one “may not claim a construction lien” (Utah Code § 38-1a-501(1)(e)). On a government job only subcontractors file; a person with a contract with the owner and a laborer paid wages do not (§ 38-1b-202(1)(a)).

When is the Utah preliminary notice due?

No later than 20 days after the day you start providing construction work on the property (§ 38-1a-501(1)(a)). It is filed with the State Construction Registry, not mailed to the owner. On a government job where you started before the notice of commencement, it is the later of 20 days after you started and 20 days after that notice was filed (§ 38-1b-202(1)(a)).

What happens if I file the Utah preliminary notice late?

On a private job you may still file, but you cannot claim a lien for work provided before the fifth day after the late filing (§ 38-1a-501(1)(c)). Once a notice of completion has been filed, a preliminary notice filed more than 10 days after it has no effect at all (§ 38-1a-501(1)(d)).

How many preliminary notices do I need on one Utah project?

One per project, covering all your work under a single original contract, even if you work for more than one sub on it (§ 38-1a-501(1)(b), (3)(a)). Work under a second original contract on the same project needs its own notice (§ 38-1a-501(3)(b)).

How long do I have to record a Utah mechanics lien?

180 days after final completion of the original contract if no notice of completion is filed, or 90 days after a notice of completion is filed, but never later than 180 days after final completion (§ 38-1a-502(1)(a)). Then sue to enforce within 180 days of recording (§ 38-1a-701(2)).
