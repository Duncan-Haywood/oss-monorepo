Pay applications for commercial painting contractors: floors, coats and damage by others

Commercial painting comes at the end of the job: the work is billed by floor or area and by coat, and much of the last month is touch-up after other trades and punch. That is where painting subs lose money: repainting damage they did not cause, and retainage that waits on a punch list that keeps growing. This page covers each, with the free guide or tool for it.

Where painting subs lose money on pay apps

  • Repainting other trades' damage for free. Walls damaged after they were finished are extra work. Without a photo and a signed ticket, they are usually repaired at the painter's cost.
  • A punch list that never closes. Items that are other trades' damage should be marked so and raised with the GC in writing, so they leave your list.
  • Back charges found on the check. A check smaller than the application is easier to fight with the back charge in writing. Paid short? Reconcile the check reconciles it.
  • Late payment nobody charges for. Many states set when a GC must pay a sub after it is paid, some with interest. Check your state.

Common questions

How should a painting contractor set up the schedule of values?

By floor or area and by phase (prime, finish coats, wallcovering, special coatings, exterior), with touch-up and punch on a small line of its own. Each month's percent then comes from what is done on each floor.

How do I get paid for repainting damage caused by other trades?

Photograph the damage, write a T&M ticket or change order request naming it and get it signed by the GC's superintendent before the repair, then bill it once approved.

Can a painting contractor bill for wallcovering before it is hung?

Often yes, as stored materials, if your subcontract allows it; attach the invoices. Move it to work completed as it is hung, so it is billed once.

When is a painter's retainage released?

When your subcontract says, which is often after the GC is paid its own retainage and the punch list is accepted. Ask which items are open in writing, and mark any that are other trades' damage.
