Common questions

    
How do I work out my Self Assessment tax bill for 2025/26?

    
Add up your income: employment pay, self-employed profit (income less expenses or the £1,000 trading allowance), property profit, savings interest and dividends. Take off the personal allowance, tax non-savings income first, then savings, then dividends, add Class 4 National Insurance on self-employed profit and any student loan, then subtract the tax already paid through PAYE. The result is due by 31 January 2027.

    
What are payments on account?

    
If your Self Assessment bill (income tax and Class 4 National Insurance, after tax paid at source) is £1,000 or more and less than 80% of your liability was collected at source, HMRC asks for two advance payments towards next year, each half of this year's bill, on 31 January and 31 July. The first falls on the same day as the balance, so the January bill can be about 150% of your tax.

    
Do I pay tax on the first £1,000 of side income?

    
No, if it is trading income. The £1,000 trading allowance relieves your first £1,000 of self-employed income instead of your actual expenses, whichever is better. Property income has its own separate £1,000 allowance.

    
How much is Class 4 National Insurance?

    
6% on self-employed profit between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above that for 2025/26. Class 2 is no longer compulsory.

    
How are savings interest and dividends taxed?

    
After your other income. You can have up to £5,000 of savings interest at 0% if your other taxable income is small, a Personal Savings Allowance of £1,000 (basic rate) or £500 (higher rate), and a £500 Dividend Allowance. Dividends above that are taxed at 8.75%, 33.75% or 39.35%.
