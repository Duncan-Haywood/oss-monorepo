How much does Whatnot take on a $2,400 graded card?

Not 8% of $2,400. On the published US row for comics, TCG, sports singles and toys & hobbies, commission is 8% of the first $1,500 of the hammer and 0% on the rest. Processing is separate and has no cap. Price a big lot with a flat 8% and you overstate the fee by $72.

The formula

commission = 8% × min(hammer, $1,500)

processing = 2.9% × (hammer + shipping charged + tax) + $0.30

payout = hammer − commission − processing

Worked example: a $2,400 graded lot

No shipping, no tax, 2.9% + $0.30 processing.

LineAmount
Hammer$2,400.00
Commission (8% of the first $1,500; $900 above is 0%)$120.00
Processing (2.9% of $2,400 + $0.30)$69.90
Total fees$189.90
Payout$2,210.10

Effective take on the hammer: 7.91%. A flat 8% commission would give $192.00 commission, $261.90 in fees (10.91%) and a $2,138.10 payout.

The cap only helps above $1,500

At $800 the same row is $64.00 commission + $23.50 processing = $87.50 (10.94% of the hammer). At $1,500 it is $120.00 + $43.80 = $163.80 (10.92%). At $5,000 commission is still $120.00, so fees are $265.30 (5.31%). Commission stops growing at $1,500; processing never stops.

Common questions

How much does Whatnot take on a $2,400 graded card?

On the published US row for comics, TCG, sports singles and toys, a $2,400 hammer with no shipping or tax is $120.00 commission (8% of the first $1,500, 0% on the other $900) plus $69.90 processing (2.9% of $2,400 + $0.30) = $189.90 in fees and a $2,210.10 payout. That is 7.91% of the hammer, not the $192.00 commission a flat 8% would suggest.

Is the $1,500 commission cap permanent?

No. Whatnot publishes it as a limited-time US shape and it varies by category and region, so treat it as an input, not a fact. The calculator's category presets are starting points: replace the rate and cap with the line on your own payout statement.

Does the cap lower the processing fee too?

No. Processing is 2.9% + $0.30 of what the buyer paid at checkout (item, shipping and tax) and the commission cap does not touch it. On the $2,400 lot processing is $69.90 either way; the cap saves $72.00 of commission only.
