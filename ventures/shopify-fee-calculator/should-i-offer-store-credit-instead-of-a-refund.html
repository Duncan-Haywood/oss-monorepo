What this checks

Merchants search “should I offer store credit instead of a refund” because a cash refund looks like the whole order disappearing, and Shopify’s refund button is the default. Credit keeps the original payment. The missing piece is redemption: unused credit is breakage; redeemed credit is another unit of goods and postage. An exchange pays that postage now.

On the defaults — $48 item, $6 shipping charged, $14 cost, $7 return label, 85% restockable, 70% of credit redeemed, processor keeps the original fee — store credit keeps $22.64, an exchange keeps $16.64, and a cash refund keeps −$11.36. Common questions

Should I offer store credit instead of a refund on Shopify?

Store credit keeps the original payment in the shop. The cost is the next item they take if they redeem, plus the same return label and restock as a refund. On the calculator defaults ($48 item, $7 return label, 85% restockable, 70% redemption) credit keeps $22.64 after the return and a cash refund keeps -$11.36. That is arithmetic on your inputs, not advice to refuse refunds.

Is an exchange cheaper than store credit?

An exchange pays the return label and a replacement outbound now. Store credit only spends COGS, pack, and postage if they come back. When every credit is redeemed and the returned unit is fully restockable, the two paths leave the same amount. If some credit expires unused, credit keeps more.

Does Shopify Payments refund the processing fee?

It depends on the processor and the refund. Shopify Payments often returns its fee when you refund the order; PayPal often keeps it. The processor-fee-kept input is 100% by default so the table stays conservative. Set it to 0% if your statement shows the fee coming back.
