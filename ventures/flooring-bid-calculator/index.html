Bid the waste and the tear-out, not the room count.

Price a flooring install the way the job actually costs: purchased material after waste, demo hours that are not the same as install hours, and stairs by the tread. You get the job price, what it works out to per installed square foot and per labor hour, and the number below which the job loses money.

Stairs, transitions, and prep

Stairs are not hallway square footage. Thirteen treads can take as long as the kitchen. Transitions, furniture moves, leveling, and shoe moulding are the extras a walk-through finds and a room-count quote forgets.

Your costs

Flooring cost is what you pay the supplier, applied to purchased square footage after waste. Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, and paid time off — the part of a helper’s cost that never shows up on their wage.

Questions installers actually type

How much waste should I add to an LVP bid?

Straight-lay LVP commonly starts around 8%. Diagonal or herringbone often needs 12% or more. Waste is extra material you buy, not extra floor the customer sees. Change the percent on each room; the bid shows the dollars that waste costs.

Why does a price per square foot underbid a kitchen or stairs?

Tear-out is a different job from the install. Ceramic tile demo can take more hours than laying new LVP in the same room. Stairs are priced per tread, not as hallway square footage. A blended $/sq ft on the finished floor cannot see either one.

Is this a quote I can send a customer?

No. It is arithmetic on the numbers you enter — a draft to check against your own invoices, payroll, and a walk-through. Copy the bid summary, then verify every figure before you send a price.
