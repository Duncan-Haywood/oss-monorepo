What goes in each sheet

  • Contact: the people the model's owner history names, with their email, company and phone where the file holds them, and you, if you give your email above.
  • Facility, Floor, Space: the building, its storeys and its rooms (number in Name, room name in Description, floor from where the room sits, areas from the model's stored quantities).
  • Type and Component: maintainable products only (equipment, terminals, controls, furniture, lifts, doors, windows), not walls, slabs, pipe runs or fittings. Manufacturer, model, warranty and life from the type's properties, standard (Pset_ManufacturerTypeInformation) or COBie-named (Revit's COBie.Type.Manufacturer counts as Manufacturer). Two Revit type objects with the same name and class are one Type.
  • Zone and System: IfcZone and IfcSystem groups, one row per member, and the zone names a Revit COBie set-up keeps on each room (VentilationZoneName and the like).
  • Attribute: every other property of a floor, room, type or component, once per name.

A value the model does not hold is left blank, never made up, and the QC lists it. Numbers stay in the model's units, which the Facility row names.

How it was checked

On 25 public IFC exports (Revit, ARCHICAD, Tekla and others), every file wrote a workbook. Rooms from geometry were checked against what the files say themselves: of 869 doors and windows that the file's own space boundaries pair with rooms, 791 got exactly those rooms from geometry alone, 37 one of their two, and 19 a room the file does not name. Of 1,227 components a file puts in a room, with that hidden, 935 were placed in the same room, 89 in another and 203 in none (left blank). On the Duplex model, IfcOpenShell's COBie exporter (ifcfm 0.9.0, its legacy preset; its current preset stops with an error on both files tried) writes the same number of floors, rooms, zones, types and components, and leaves the room of 38 components as n/a; here they are placed in rooms (method and numbers).
