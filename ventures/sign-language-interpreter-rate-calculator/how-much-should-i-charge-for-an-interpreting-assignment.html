How much should a sign language interpreter charge for an assignment?

Start from your own hourly rate, then apply the conventions that actually move the number: a booking minimum, a team-interpreting surcharge on long high-stakes work, and a late-cancellation fee schedule. The rest of this page works through each on real numbers, not rounded examples.

The booking minimum changes a short request

A 1.5-hour request at $65/hour, standard business hours, one interpreter:

FigureArithmeticResult
Raw hourly math (what it looks like at a glance)1.5 hrs × $65$97.50
Billable hours under a 2-hour minimummax(1.5, 2)2 hrs
Actual interpreting fee2 hrs × $65$130.00

A $32.50 gap between the number a quick mental calculation gives and the number the commonly cited freelance booking minimum actually bills -- the interpreter has reserved two hours of unsellable time, not 90 minutes.

A team-interpreting assignment doubles the fee, not the rate

A 3-hour legal deposition at $65/hour -- past the length and in a setting RID's own guidance names for team-interpreting consideration:

StaffingArithmeticTotal
Single interpreter3 hrs × $65$195.00
Two-interpreter team$195.00 × 2$390.00

Both interpreters bill the same three-hour block, so the fee reflects two professionals working the assignment, not a higher hourly rate for one. This calculator flags the recommendation on a booking like this rather than pricing it automatically -- the actual staffing decision depends on the venue, the funder, and the interpreters involved.

A late cancellation is not automatically the full fee

A $200.00 scheduled booking, cancelled with two different amounts of notice, under a commonly cited three-tier schedule (full fee inside 24 hours, half fee 24-48 hours, no fee beyond 48 hours):

A twelve-hour difference in notice given is a $100.00 difference in what is owed -- the schedule rewards earlier notice without waiving the fee outright.

Common questions

How much should a freelance sign language interpreter charge for an assignment?

Start from your own hourly rate, then apply the conventions that actually move the number. A 1.5-hour request at $65/hour is not a $97.50 invoice -- the commonly cited two-hour freelance booking minimum applies, so the assignment bills as 2 hours at $65 for a $130.00 total, a $32.50 difference the raw hourly math never shows.

Why does a two-hour minimum matter if the assignment is shorter?

A freelance interpreter who accepts a 45-minute booking has turned away any other work that could have filled that same block, including the travel time on either side of it. A minimum-booking policy is the industry's commonly cited answer to that: the interpreter is paid for the time reserved, not only the time actually interpreting.

How much does staffing a team of two interpreters actually add?

It doubles the interpreting fee, because both interpreters are billing the same block. A 3-hour legal assignment at $65/hour prices at $195.00 for a single interpreter; the same booking staffed as a recommended two-interpreter team prices at $390.00 -- the fee reflects two professionals working the assignment, not one at a higher rate.
