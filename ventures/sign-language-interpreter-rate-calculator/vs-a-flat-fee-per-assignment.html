This calculator vs. a flat fee per assignment

A flat fee per assignment — one memorized number, quoted for most bookings — is the fastest way to answer "what do you charge?" on the phone. It stops working the moment a booking's travel, timing, or staffing needs differ from whatever booking the number was built around, because none of that is tracked separately in a single figure.

What each one is for

A flat fee per assignment is quick to quote and easy for a client to hear. What it does not do is separate the price into anything an interpreter can check against a specific booking — it does not add travel and mileage for a booking that is further away, it does not apply an after-hours differential to an evening or weekend request, and it cannot flag the one thing RID's own guidance says to check on a long, high-stakes booking: whether it should be staffed with a team.

This calculator bills the greater of hours requested or your booking minimum, adds portal-to-portal travel time and mileage, applies an evening/weekend or holiday differential, and flags — never auto-prices — a two-interpreter team recommendation on assignments RID's Standard Practice Papers name as long or high-stakes enough to need one.

Common questions

What is a flat fee per assignment, and why do interpreters use it?

One number an interpreter has memorized and quotes for most bookings — often built around their usual hourly rate and the commonly cited two-hour minimum. It is fast to say on the phone or in a text and easy for a client to hear, which is why many freelancers default to it for a routine-looking booking.

What does a flat fee miss on a short booking?

Travel. A flat number sized around the two-hour minimum can look right for a nearby booking and still leave real portal-to-portal drive time and mileage uncharged the moment the assignment is further away — a memorized number does not know how far today's booking is.

What does a flat fee miss on an evening or weekend booking?

The after-hours differential. A flat fee built around a standard-hours rate does not distinguish a weekday afternoon from a Saturday evening, so it quietly under-quotes the booking that is actually costing the interpreter their weekend.

Can a flat fee tell me when a booking needs a second interpreter?

No. A flat fee is a price, not a staffing check. Whether a long legal, medical, or mental-health assignment should be staffed with a two-interpreter team depends on the setting and length, per RID's own Standard Practice Paper guidance — a memorized number has no way to flag that, and quoting it as-is prices one interpreter for work the field's own guidance says two should share.
