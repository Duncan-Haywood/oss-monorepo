Where is purgatory in the Bible?

The Scripture behind the Catholic teaching on purgatory, why it is not a second chance, and why prayer for the dead is older than Christianity.

Download the video (64 s, vertical 720x1280, CC BY-SA 4.0) · Download the card

The one-minute script

Where is purgatory in the Bible? The word isn't there. Neither is the word Trinity. The question is whether the idea is.

Start with what purgatory isn't. It isn't a second chance, and it isn't a halfway hell. The Catechism says it's for people who die in God's friendship, already assured of heaven, who still need to be made clean. Nothing unclean enters heaven, says Revelation.

Now Saint Paul. In First Corinthians three, each person's work is tested by fire on the last day. If it burns, "he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved, yet so as by fire." Saved, and still something to suffer. That's the shape of it.

And in Second Maccabees, Judas Maccabeus has a sacrifice offered for fallen soldiers so they might be freed from sin. Praying for the dead only makes sense if it can help them.

That's purgatory. Not God's anger. God finishing what he started.

Fair to the other side

Most Protestant Christians do not accept 2 Maccabees as Scripture, and read 1 Corinthians 3 as the testing of a minister's work on judgment day rather than a purification of the person. Orthodox Christians pray for the dead but generally do not define a purgatory. The Catholic case does not stand on one verse; it rests on these texts together with the Church's practice of praying for the dead from its earliest centuries.
