## Script

Here's a fact that surprises people. The idea that the universe is expanding was first published by a Catholic priest.

His name was Georges Lemaître. He was ordained in nineteen twenty-three, in Belgium, and he was also a physicist. In nineteen twenty-seven he published a paper showing that Einstein's equations allowed a universe that grows, and that distant galaxies should be moving away from us at speeds proportional to their distance. Edwin Hubble's famous observations came two years later.

A few years after that, Lemaître proposed that the universe began from a single "primeval atom". That's the idea we now call the Big Bang.

In twenty eighteen the International Astronomical Union recommended renaming Hubble's law the Hubble-Lemaître law.

He saw no war between his collar and his chalkboard. The Catechism puts it simply: truth cannot contradict truth. The same God made the mind that does the maths and the universe it describes.

## Fair to the other side

Lemaître himself insisted his theory was physics, not proof of creation, and he was uneasy when, in 1951, Pope Pius XII seemed to tie the Big Bang to Genesis. A fair version of this story keeps that: the point is not that science proves the faith, but that a priest could do first-rate science without any conflict.
