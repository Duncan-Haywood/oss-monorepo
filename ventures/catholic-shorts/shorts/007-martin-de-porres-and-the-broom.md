## Script

Today is the feast of Saint Martin de Porres.

He was born in Lima, Peru, in fifteen seventy-nine. His father was a Spanish nobleman. His mother, Ana, was a freed woman of African and Native descent. His father left, and his mother took in laundry to feed her children.

Martin trained as a barber, which in those days also meant a kind of surgeon. As a young man he went to the Dominicans. At first he served as a helper, the lowest place in the house. Later he took vows as a lay brother.

He swept floors. He nursed the sick, any sick, rich or poor, Spanish, African or Native. He fed the hungry at the convent door. People called him the saint of the broom.

There's a legend that he even talked the convent mice into moving out to the garden, and fed them there so they'd stop eating the linens. Legend or not, it tells you who he was.

Saint Martin de Porres, pray for us.

## Fair to the other side

Not every detail of a saint's life is equally certain. His dates, family and canonization are well documented; stories such as the mice, and the many miracles attributed to him, come from devotional tradition, and the script marks the mice story as legend.
