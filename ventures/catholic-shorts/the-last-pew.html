The last pew

A one-minute story, invented, about a man in the back pew and the usher who finally sat next to him.

The one-minute script

Every Sunday for eleven years, Tom sat in the last pew. He came in after the opening hymn and left during the closing one. He never went up for Communion. He never shook anyone's hand at the sign of peace, because there was never anyone near enough.

Frank, the usher, noticed. Frank noticed everything. One Sunday, instead of standing at the back, he sat down next to Tom.

Tom stiffened. "I'm not much of a Catholic," he said.

"Neither was the tax collector," Frank said. "He stood at the back too. Jesus said he was the one who went home right with God."

Tom didn't answer. But at the sign of peace, Frank held out his hand, and Tom took it.

The next week, Tom came in during the opening hymn.

The week after that, he sat three rows from the back. And the week after that, he went to confession.

It started with someone sitting down.

What is true here

This one is fiction: Tom and Frank are invented. The point it makes is about welcome, and it does not claim that sitting with someone is a substitute for the sacraments; it ends with Tom going to confession on his own.

Sources

  • Original fiction, written for this series. Luke 18:13 (the tax collector "standing afar off") is the verse it leans on.
