How to build an RFP compliance matrix

A compliance matrix is a table with one row per requirement in a solicitation. It exists for one reason: a proposal that skips a mandatory requirement can be scored non-responsive before anyone reads how good the rest is. The matrix is how a team proves to itself that every shall has an answer.

The columns

ColumnWhat goes in it
IDA stable number (REQ-001). Reviewers refer to it in comments.
Source sectionWhere the requirement appears in the solicitation (3.2.1, L.4, Attachment B). Evaluators search by this.
RequirementThe sentence as written. Do not paraphrase; paraphrase is where obligations get softened.
OwnerOne named person, not a team.
Proposal sectionThe page or heading in your response that answers it.
StatusNot started, drafted, reviewed, done.
ComplianceFully, partially, or not compliant, with a note. Say "partially" in the matrix before the evaluator says it for you.

Finding the requirements

  • Read three parts separately: the instructions to offerors (how to respond), the statement of work (what to deliver), and the evaluation criteria (how you will be scored). Requirements hide in all three.
  • Mark the trigger words: shall, must, is required to, will (when the contractor is the subject). Treat should and preferred as desirable, and shall not as a prohibition.
  • Split lists. "The Contractor shall: (a) ...; (b) ...; (c) ..." is three requirements, each of which can be missed separately.
  • Ignore the buyer's own duties. "The County will evaluate proposals within 30 days" is not yours to answer.
  • Pull instructions out separately. Page limits, fonts, copies, forms, the due date and the question deadline apply to the whole package. Check them once, at the end, with a fresh pair of eyes.

Mistakes that cost bids

  • Answering the scope and forgetting the forms: signed certifications, addendum acknowledgements, a notarised affidavit.
  • Counting pages after the layout changed, or using a smaller font than the instructions allow.
  • Treating a sentence with two obligations as one row.
  • Reading only the statement of work, when a requirement sits in the instructions or an attachment.
  • Not re-running the matrix after an addendum changes the text.

Common questions

What columns should an RFP compliance matrix have?

At minimum: a requirement ID, the solicitation section it comes from, the requirement text, who owns the response, the proposal section that answers it, and a status. Many teams add a compliance level (fully, partially, not compliant) and a notes column for assumptions.

How do I find every requirement in an RFP?

Read the instructions to offerors, the statement of work and the evaluation criteria separately. Mark every sentence with shall, must, is required to, or a contractor-subject will, split lists into one row per item, and keep prohibitions and desirable (should) items distinct from mandatory ones.

What makes a proposal non-responsive?

Commonly: missing a mandatory requirement, exceeding a page limit, missing a signed form or an addendum acknowledgement, submitting late or through the wrong channel. Rules vary by buyer, so read the instructions to offerors for the consequences of each.
