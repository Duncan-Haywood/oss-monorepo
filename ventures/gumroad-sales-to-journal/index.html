The model, and what it leaves out

  • Rows: each row is one Gumroad sale, refund, chargeback or payout with a price, shipping, tax, Gumroad fee, affiliate fee and Net. Columns are matched by name, amounts like $25.00 and -$1.63 are read to cents, and rows are grouped by calendar month.
  • Categories: product sales and shipping (income), tax (a liability, not revenue), Gumroad fees and affiliate commissions (expenses), refunds and chargebacks (contra). Where the Net includes the tax column it is credited to the tax liability; where it does not, Gumroad collected it and it is not journalled. Anything a row's Net holds that these do not explain goes to an uncategorised account and is listed.
  • Payouts: a payout row is journalled as debit Bank, credit Gumroad clearing. If sales carry a Payout ID, the sales for each ID are added up and compared to the payout row; a difference is reported. Failed payouts and unfinished sales are skipped and named.
  • Month check: type the month's net from your Gumroad earnings summary into the box and the page says whether the rows add up to it, and by how much they are off if not. Do not post a journal that disagrees.
  • Not checked: that the export agrees with your Gumroad account or your books, sales tax or VAT treatment anywhere, fees or adjustments reported outside the sales export, and that the accounts exist in your software.

Common questions

How do I turn a Gumroad sales export into a journal entry?

Download the sales CSV from your Gumroad dashboard (Analytics or Sales), paste it here and press Read, check and build journals. The page checks every row against its own columns, books product sales, shipping, tax, Gumroad fees, affiliate commissions, refunds and chargebacks separately, and writes one journal per month that balances to a Gumroad clearing account.

Why not book the Gumroad payout as income?

A Gumroad payout is net of Gumroad's fees, affiliate commissions, refunds and chargebacks, and the amount arrives weeks after the sales. Booking the payout as income understates revenue, hides the fees and puts the sale in the wrong month. This page books gross sales and each cost to its own account in the month it happened, and the payout clears the Gumroad clearing account into the bank.

How is sales tax or VAT handled?

Where a row's Net includes the tax column, the tax is credited to a liability account, never to revenue. Where the Net excludes it, Gumroad collected the tax itself and it is never paid to you, so it is not journalled and the page tells you how much. If the file has no Net column you choose which applies.

How are refunds and chargebacks booked?

Separate refund and chargeback rows, or a Refunded or Chargedback flag on a sale, are booked as contra entries against sales in the month of the refund, in their own accounts. If Gumroad returns its fee on a refund, the returned fee is credited back to the fees account.

What does a row with money no rule explains mean?

If a row's Net is not accounted for by its price, shipping, tax and fee columns, the difference is journalled to an uncategorised account so the journal still equals the Net amounts, and it is listed for you to review. Nothing is dropped.

Which accounting software can I import to?

Xero (manual journal CSV), QuickBooks Online (journal entry CSV), QuickBooks Desktop (IIF general journal) and a plain CSV for anything else. Rename the accounts for each category before downloading so they match your chart of accounts.

Is it safe to paste a Gumroad export into a web page?

The page runs in your browser, the file is read locally and nothing is uploaded; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls. Remove buyer email and name columns if you would rather not have them on screen: only dates, types, amounts and IDs are used.

What does this not check?

Whether the export matches your Gumroad account or your books, sales tax or VAT treatment in any jurisdiction, fees or adjustments Gumroad reports outside the sales export, and account names or codes that must already exist in your accounting software.
