November 2023, Table 1, read from tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3341.pdf. The EBU's
own 48 kHz WAV set answered 403 from this cloud session, so each signal is
synthesised from the table's wording in 64-bit floats at 48 kHz.

The sample-peak column fails 7 of 9 cases, so these signals do test the
oversampling.

What this does and does not show:

- **Momentary and short-term max are new** (`maxWindowLoudness`, every window
  start one sample apart). A 100 ms grid would miss case 13 by up to 0.58 dB
  (a 400 ms tone straddled by 50 ms); per-sample windows read every file within
  0.006 dB. `master-cli.mjs` now prints both.
- **Cases 20-23 depend on a filter the table does not give.** It says the burst
  is "lowpass (anti-aliasing) filtered and downsampled". Here: a 1023-tap
  Blackman-windowed sinc cut at 20 kHz. That filter alone takes the band-limited
  peak to -0.237 dBTP (16x sinc on the same samples); cut at 18 kHz it is -0.534,
  at 22 kHz -0.102. Against that 16x figure the 4x sinc reads within 0.04 dB at
  every offset and cut; the Annex 2 FIR reads 0.06-0.18 dB under it. The EBU's
  own files would remove this choice.
- **The Annex 2 FIR is the looser meter here**: inside tolerance everywhere, but
  0.2-0.3 dB under on cases 15 and 17 (its phases' taps sum to 0.973-1.002, not
  1). master.mjs keeps the windowed sinc as its true peak; `truePeakAnnex2` is
  there to compare.
- Not run: case 6 (5.0 channels; master.mjs reads mono and stereo), 7 and 8
  (authentic programme, only in the EBU set), 11 and 14 (the "live" halves of
  10 and 13). Loudness range (Tech 3342) is not computed.
