# Recording a men's-choir line against the MIDI guide

ROADMAP 5a. For a singer, or a few, who want to record a chant line over a
Chant Forge backing. Nothing here has been tried with real singers yet; treat
it as a first protocol and fix it after the first session.

## 1. Make the guide files

```
node cli.mjs corpus/veni-creator.gabc -o veni.mid --final 50 --fit 8 --stretch --cues veni.cues
```

- `--final 50` puts the final on D3, comfortable for baritones and basses.
  Use `--final 52` or `53` for tenors. Check the printed range (here C3 to B3).
- `--genre deep-house` (or another name from `--help`) adds the backing.
- Slow the tempo for a first take: `--bpm 90`. Speed it up once the line is known.
- The cue sheet lists bar, beat, seconds, note name, length in beats and syllable.
  Print it. The first lines for Veni Creator at 124 BPM look like this:

```
  1   2.5     0.73    C3    0.5   
  1     3     0.97    D3    0.5   á
  1   3.5     1.21    E3    0.5   tor
  1     4     1.45    D3    0.5   
  1   4.5     1.69    G3    0.5   Spí
  2     1     1.94    A3    0.5   ri
  2   1.5     2.18    G3    0.5   tus,
  2   2.5     2.66    G3    0.5   Men
```

## 2. Learn the line first

1. Sing the Latin text alone, spoken, to the syllables in the cue sheet.
   Church Latin vowels: a e i o u as in Italian, and "c" before e or i is "ch".
2. Play the MIDI guide with a plain piano or flute sound and sing along.
3. Mark breaths on the sheet at the rests (blank syllable rows with a gap).

## 3. Record

- Hear the backing in headphones only, so it does not leak into the mic.
- Record 44.1 or 48 kHz, 24-bit, mono, one take per file. Leave 2 s of room tone before and after.
- One voice per track. For a choir sound, record 4 to 8 takes, each a little
  different in vowel and timing, and pan them. Do not copy one take; identical
  copies sound like one voice made louder.
- Keep peaks under about -6 dBFS so the master check has headroom.

## 4. Check the result

Mix the takes and the backing to a WAV, then:

```
node master-cli.mjs mix.wav
```

It reports integrated loudness, true peak, mono compatibility and clipping
against streaming targets. Fix what it flags before building the release kit
(`release-cli.mjs`).

## 5. What to report back

After a first session, note in ROADMAP.md: the key and BPM used, which cue-sheet
rows were hard to hit, whether `--stretch` or padding sang better, and any
syllable the cue sheet split wrongly. That is the evidence the converter has not had.

## Rights

The chant text and melodies in the corpus are pre-1929 and public domain
(`corpus/README.md`). Your recording is yours. Say so in the release kit's
rights field, and answer the AI-disclosure question truthfully: the MIDI line
is generated by code from the chant; the voices are human.
