Questions people ask

How many beehives do I need to make a profit?

It depends on your winter loss, honey yield and selling price, because fixed costs (insurance, an extractor, registration) are spread over the colonies you keep. The planner shows the break-even number of colonies for your inputs and projects colony count and profit year by year.

Is beekeeping profitable?

Often only at the margin: honey alone rarely pays for a small yard once equipment and your time are counted. Selling nucs, renting colonies for pollination and selling retail rather than bulk usually decide it. The scenario table shows what each line adds to honey alone.

How much does winter loss matter?

More than yield. Each colony lost must be replaced by a split that needs a queen, equipment and a season to produce. The sensitivity table re-runs your plan at 10% to 60% loss.

Is this beekeeping advice?

No. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type in. Colony health, disease and mite management, local regulations and food-safety rules for selling honey are out of scope.

Not decided

Colony health, disease and mite pressure, swarming, drought and forage, local registration and inspection rules, food-safety and labelling rules for selling honey, insurance, and tax. Yields, loss rates and prices vary hugely by region and year; the defaults are placeholders, not forecasts.
