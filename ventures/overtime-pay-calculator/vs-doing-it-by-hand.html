This calculator vs. doing it by hand

Blended-rate overtime is arithmetic anyone can do with a calculator or a spreadsheet. The problem is that the correct method has two non-obvious steps — weighting the regular rate by hours instead of averaging it, and applying only half the blended rate as the overtime premium instead of the full 1.5x — and a hand calculation that misses either one produces a wrong total that still looks reasonable.

Common questions

What's the most common mistake when calculating blended-rate overtime by hand?

Averaging the two hourly rates instead of weighting by hours. A simple average treats 5 hours at one rate the same as 35 hours at it; the correct regular rate is total straight-time pay divided by total hours, which weights each rate by how much of the week was actually worked at it.

Why does applying the full 1.5x rate to overtime hours overpay every time?

Because the straight-time total already counts every hour worked once, including the overtime hours, at their own rate. Multiplying the overtime hours by the full blended rate again and adding 1.5x on top double-counts that first 1x. The correct premium is only the extra half-time (0.5x the blended rate) on top of straight-time pay that is already there.

Can I just use one employee's usual rate as the "regular rate" for the week?

No, if they worked at more than one rate that week. The FLSA regular rate for a week with multiple hourly rates is specific to that week's mix of hours — it changes whenever the split between rates changes, even for the same employee doing the same two jobs. A rate reused from a previous week or from one job title is not the regular rate for a week with a different mix.

Is a spreadsheet formula good enough once it's built once?

Only if it is built correctly and nobody adds a third rate, changes the threshold, or copies a cell reference wrong later. A formula that hardcodes two rate columns silently produces a wrong total the day a third rate row is added and the SUM range isn't updated — a common way a working sheet quietly breaks weeks after it was checked.

Does this calculator know my state's overtime rules?

No — neither this calculator nor a spreadsheet built from the FAQ here knows your state's daily-overtime, double-time, or bonus-inclusion rules. Both apply the federal FLSA weighted-average formula to the numbers you enter; check your state's rules and your own pay policy before relying on the total.
