Common questions

    
What is the 60% tax trap?

    
Between £100,000 and £125,140 of adjusted net income the personal allowance is withdrawn at £1 for every £2 earned. That makes the effective income tax rate 60% on that slice (40% plus 20% from the lost allowance), or 62% for an employee once 2% National Insurance is added.

    
Who does it affect?

    
Anyone whose adjusted net income, after pension contributions and Gift Aid, falls between £100,000 and £125,140, including employees, sole traders and company directors taking a salary.

    
How do I avoid the 60% trap?

    
Pay into a pension so adjusted net income falls back to £100,000. The contribution earns 40% higher-rate relief plus the restored allowance, so each £1 in the pension can cost around 40p of take-home (38p by salary sacrifice for an employee).

    
Is salary sacrifice better than a personal pension here?

    
For an employee it usually costs slightly less because it also saves National Insurance. It needs your employer's agreement and must not take pay below minimum wage.

    
Does this cover Scotland?

    
No. It uses the England, Wales and Northern Ireland bands. Scottish bands have a different higher-rate threshold and rates.
