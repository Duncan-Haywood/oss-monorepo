    Common questions

    Why is my price so much higher than the advertised $18,995 pneumatic special?

    That number is usually the cheapest combination this tool can price: a through-floor cut, an acrylic tube, no pit, no feeder, no permit. Adding a real hoistway, a hydraulic or traction drive, a pit, a 240 V feeder, or an inspection is exactly what the flat number leaves out. Use the “Same house, four drives” and “five hoistways” comparisons to see how much of the gap each upgrade accounts for.

    If the customer already bought their own unit, is the install free?

    No. Zeroing the unit invoice removes only the equipment cost — setting a customer-supplied Stiltz or Savaria still takes the same crew hours, and an opening that does not match the unit is a second trip on your dime. See the “Same house, who bought the unit” comparison and the linked guide before quoting a customer-supplied job at parts cost alone.

    Does this include the state elevator inspection and permit fee?

    Only if you check “Permit and inspection,” which adds the hours and dollar figure you set for it. The calculator does not look up your state or municipality's actual fee, inspector availability, or turnaround time — confirm those locally before you promise a date.

    Does the price change automatically for a rush job, like a hospital discharge?

    Not on its own. The “Same house, when they need it” table shows how a weekday slot, discharge-week, and weekend access compare using a starting expedite-freight and rush-inspection assumption, but your quoted price still comes from the fields you set above it. If the job really is a rush, raise your own hours, travel, and other-cost inputs to match — the comparison table is a reference, not an automatic switch.
