How many lifts does this building need?

Type the floors above the lobby and how many people work on each, and the lifts you have in mind. The page works out the up-peak round trip time, how often a car leaves the lobby and how many people the lifts move in five minutes, searches for the smallest designs that meet your targets, and simulates a morning of random arrivals to show the waits. Free, and it runs in your browser.

How it is worked out, and what the simulation found about the formula

1. Lifts

Rated persons default to rated load / 75 kg, rounded down; type a number to override. The capacity factor is the share of rated persons on an average up-peak trip (80% is the usual convention). Dynamics and door times below are placeholders: use the lift maker's figures.

2. Floors above the lobby

One served floor per line, lowest first: name, rise (m), population. The rise is the height from the floor below (the lobby, or the top of an express zone, for the first line). Or fill a uniform building:

Population is people who arrive at the lobby in the morning and go to that floor: net area per person times usable area, less absentees, is the usual estimate.

3. Targets

Your design targets. Placeholder values; recommended ranges by building type are in CIBSE Guide D and similar references.

Questions

What is the up-peak round trip time?

The average time a lift takes to load at the lobby, run up making its probable stops to its probable highest floor, and come back. Dividing it by the number of lifts gives the interval between departures; 300 seconds times the passengers per trip times the lifts, divided by it, gives the five-minute handling capacity.

Does a short interval mean short waits?

Only if the lifts are not pushed near their handling capacity. In this tool's simulation of random arrivals, the mean wait was about half the interval at 80% of the formula's handling capacity and about equal to the interval at 100%, across 27 test buildings.

Is this a substitute for a lift consultant?

No. It is an estimate for early planning, checking a proposal or learning the method. Up-peak is one traffic pattern; lunch and down-peak, real group controllers and local codes need a qualified vertical transportation consultant.

Is my building uploaded?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser. An optional anonymous counter in this browser's local storage records only that a calculation ran and the day.
