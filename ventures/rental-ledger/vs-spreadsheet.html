Rental Ledger vs. a landlord spreadsheet

Most small landlords start with a spreadsheet, and for one property it works. The trouble starts with the mid-month first year, a clock for every improvement and a loss limit that carries over. This page compares the two on those jobs.

What each one is for

A spreadsheet is flexible and yours. For one property and one year it is often enough, and a preparer can open it anywhere. The cost is that every rule lives in a formula you wrote, and it only stays right as long as every row is copied correctly.

Rental Ledger keeps the same rows and applies the rules for you: depreciation, the loss allowance, the rent collection grid and the 1099 list. It shows the arithmetic and exports back to CSV.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet do rental depreciation and Schedule E?

Yes. A careful spreadsheet can. The hard parts are the mid-month first year, a separate clock for every improvement, and keeping the totals tied to the rows when you add a property or a year.

What does a spreadsheet not do for you?

It does not tell you that a large expense logged as a repair looks like an improvement, list the contractors who crossed the 1099-NEC threshold, carry a suspended passive loss forward, or estimate the tax on a sale. You have to build and maintain each of those yourself.

What does Rental Ledger not do that a spreadsheet can?

It is federal and residential only. A spreadsheet can be bent to cover short-term rentals, commercial 39-year property, state rules or anything else your preparer needs, because you control every cell.

Can I move between the two?

Yes. Rental Ledger imports a CSV of rows and exports Schedule E and all entries as CSV, so a preparer can open the result in any spreadsheet.

Is anything I enter uploaded anywhere?

No. Rental Ledger runs entirely in your browser; nothing you enter is uploaded.
