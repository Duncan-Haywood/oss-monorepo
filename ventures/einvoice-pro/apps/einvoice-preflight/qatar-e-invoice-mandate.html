Qatar: draft e-invoicing law

Qatar has approved a draft law on electronic invoices but has not yet published a start date, a format or penalties. Below: what is confirmed, what is only third-party estimate, and what a free local file check can and cannot tell you.

What the sources say

TopicWhat the sources say
ApprovalThe Council of Ministers approved a draft e-invoicing law and its executive regulations on 6 May 2026, as reported by EY and others. The Ministry of Finance prepared it with the General Tax Authority (GTA).
Scope of the lawThe legal structure for issuing electronic invoices and credit notes, with the stated aims of transparency and regulatory oversight.
Next stepsReported: review by the Shura Council, then enactment by the Amir and publication in the Official Gazette. Technical specifications are expected from the GTA afterwards.
Dates, penalties, scopeNot published. Deadlines and penalties are left to the executive regulations. Some guides say 2027 or a phased rollout by taxpayer size; those are estimates, and sources conflict on how binding the measure is today.
FormatNot specified. A Peppol-based model is guessed by some third-party sites, unconfirmed.
VAT linkQatar has no VAT in force; one source expects the e-invoicing system to be ready for a possible future VAT, which is speculation.

Written from web search summaries, an EY tax alert and adviser guides, not from the Council of Ministers decision, the Official Gazette or the GTA site, none of which could be read for this page. Last checked 5 November 2026.

Common questions

When does Qatar e-invoicing become mandatory?

No date has been published. The Council of Ministers approved a draft e-invoicing law and its executive regulations on 6 May 2026; the executive regulations are expected to set deadlines and penalties. Some third-party guides expect 2027, which is an estimate, not an official statement.

What did Qatar approve in May 2026?

A draft law, prepared by the Ministry of Finance with the General Tax Authority, that sets the legal framework for issuing electronic invoices and credit notes. Reported next steps are review by the Shura Council and enactment by the Amir.

What format will Qatar use?

Not specified in anything read for this page. Some third-party sites guess a Peppol-based model; treat that as unconfirmed until the General Tax Authority publishes technical specifications.

Can this checker validate a Qatari invoice?

No Qatari rules exist to check against yet. For a UBL or CII file it applies EN 16931 and Peppol checks that catch totals, tax breakdown and identifier faults, which any future system is likely to reject too. A clean result is not an acceptance by the General Tax Authority.
