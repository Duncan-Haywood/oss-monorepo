Prop Firm Challenge Calculator vs. a trading spreadsheet

A spreadsheet can tell you how many winning trades a profit target needs. It rarely plays the order of wins and losses against a trailing drawdown, a daily loss limit and a time limit at once — which is what this free, private simulator does with your own win rate and risk.

Where a spreadsheet stops being enough

Most challenge entrants already keep a sheet: win rate, average win, average loss, a column that multiplies them. It answers whether the strategy makes money on average. A challenge is not an average; it is a path with walls on both sides and a deadline, and the same win rate passes or fails depending on the sequence.

Common questions

Can't I just model a prop firm challenge in a spreadsheet?

Yes. A spreadsheet is good at the deterministic part: how many winning trades reach the target and how many losses hit the drawdown. What it handles badly is the order of wins and losses, because the same win rate passes or fails depending on the sequence. A simulation plays thousands of sequences against the daily loss limit, the drawdown floor and the clock together, and reports the share that pass.

What does a trading spreadsheet usually leave out of a challenge?

The interaction between rules. A trailing drawdown that rises with your high, a daily loss limit that resets each day, a minimum number of trading days and a time limit all depend on the path your balance takes. Averages cannot show a path, so a sheet built on average win and average loss tends to say the strategy passes whenever its expectancy is positive.

Does the calculator replace backtesting my strategy?

No. It takes a win rate and a reward-to-risk ratio you supply, from a backtest or a journal, and shows what the challenge rules do to them. If those inputs are optimistic the output is optimistic. It is a simulation of assumed inputs, not a forecast, and trading carries a risk of loss.
