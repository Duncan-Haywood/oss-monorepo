      Common questions

      How do I convert as-purchased price to edible-portion cost?

      Divide the as-purchased (AP) price per unit by the yield percentage (as a decimal): EP cost = AP cost / (yield % / 100). A $3.50/lb item with a 75% trim yield costs $4.67/lb once you account for what is trimmed away and never reaches a plate.

      Can a yield percentage be over 100%?

      Yes. Dried beans, rice, and stocks absorb liquid and gain weight as they cook, so their edible-portion weight can exceed what was purchased dry. The same formula applies -- it just lowers the effective per-unit cost instead of raising it.

      How is plate cost different from food-cost percentage?

      Plate cost is a dollar figure: the total recipe cost divided by the portions a batch yields. Food-cost percentage relates that dollar figure to a menu price -- plate cost divided by menu price, times 100. You need a menu price to get a percentage; you do not need one to get a plate cost.

      How do I find the menu price for a target food-cost percentage?

      Divide the plate cost by the target percentage (as a decimal): price = plate cost / (target % / 100). A $4 plate cost at a 30% target food-cost percentage needs a $13.33 menu price.
