Recipe Costing Calculator vs. estimating recipe cost in a spreadsheet

A kitchen costing more than one recipe usually ends up with a spreadsheet: a column for as-purchased price, a formula dividing by yield percentage, a sum across ingredient rows. Each formula is ordinary arithmetic. Getting the AP-to-EP direction right for an ingredient that gains weight when prepped, and rebuilding the target-price formula for each dish, is the part that gets skipped when a menu needs a price today.

What each approach actually does

A spreadsheet can reproduce every step this calculator runs — the AP-to-EP yield conversion, the per-portion division, and the target-price solve. Building it means one column per ingredient for as-purchased price and yield percentage, a formula dividing one by the other, a sum across rows divided by portions, and your own formula to solve backwards for the menu price at a target food-cost percentage.

This calculator keeps the AP-to-EP formula, the per-batch rollup, and the target-price solve on one screen with no formula to write or debug, and flags a yield percentage over 100% as expected for an ingredient that gains weight when prepped rather than leaving it looking like a typo in a cell.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet do this math instead?

Yes. The AP-to-EP yield conversion, the per-portion division, and the target-price formula are each ordinary arithmetic. Building it means one column per ingredient for as-purchased price and yield percentage, a formula dividing one by the other, and a sum across rows divided by portions -- then a second formula solving backwards for the menu price at your target food-cost percentage.

What does a spreadsheet not do that this calculator does?

Keep the AP-to-EP formula, the per-batch rollup, and the target-price solve on one screen with no formula to write or debug, and flag a yield percentage over 100% as expected for an ingredient that gains weight when prepped rather than leaving it looking like a typo in a cell.

Is a spreadsheet ever the better choice?

Yes, for a full recipe book with dozens of dishes, sub-recipes nested inside other recipes, or a live link to a supplier's price list. A spreadsheet or dedicated recipe-management software can represent all of that. This calculator trades that scope for a form that costs one recipe at a time with no setup.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Ingredient prices, quantities, and menu prices are never uploaded, and the optional usage counter records only which features were used and the day, never your numbers.
