This calculator vs. working out the 2026 estate tax exclusion by hand

The headline number is simple — $15,000,000 per person. The part that goes wrong by hand is portability, the GST trap, and whether last year's "sunset" warning still applies (it does not).

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Assuming a married couple automatically gets $30,000,000 just for being married. The second $15,000,000 only applies if a surviving spouse actually elects portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion (DSUE) by timely filing Form 706 -- it is not automatic.

What's the second most common mistake?

Assuming the generation-skipping transfer (GST) exemption is portable between spouses the same way the estate/gift exclusion is. It is not, and never has been -- a deceased spouse's unused GST exemption cannot be added to a survivor's own.

What arithmetic mistake gets made most often?

Forgetting that lifetime taxable gifts reduce the exclusion available at death. Estate and gift tax share one unified exclusion -- a taxable gift made years earlier uses up part of the same bucket that would otherwise shield the estate.

What sunset mistake gets made most often?

Relying on an older article warning that the exemption would revert to roughly $7,000,000 at the start of 2026. That sunset was real under prior law, but the One Big Beautiful Bill Act permanently cancelled it and set the 2026 exclusion at $15,000,000 instead, with no expiration date.

Does this calculator know my complete estate plan?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page knows about a marital deduction, a charitable bequest, valuation discounts, or state estate tax. Both apply the same published federal exclusion, portability, and rate rules to the numbers you enter; verify against a qualified estate planning attorney or CPA before relying on either.
