Common questions

Can't I just do the copy-paste test myself without this tool?

Yes -- the copy-paste test is a manual technique first, and this checker only automates it. Copy the text out of your resume file, paste it into any plain-text editor, and look at what comes out. That alone catches the most obvious breakage: a paste that is almost entirely blank, or one that is visibly scrambled. What a quick look tends to miss is covered below.

What does an eyeball scan of the pasted text usually miss?

Three things most often. First, icon-font characters (used for decorative bullets or checkmarks in some templates) often render as a tiny invisible box or nothing at all in a plain editor, so their presence is easy to not notice even though an ATS parser sees the same unreadable code point. Second, whether your contact info sits early enough in the extracted text is a position you would have to measure, not something that jumps out visually. Third, four or more spaces in the middle of a line -- the signature of a collapsed multi-column layout -- looks like ordinary spacing to the eye; nothing about it looks wrong until you count the spaces.

Why would a private-use character be hard to spot by eye?

Characters in the Unicode Private Use Area (U+E000-U+F8FF) have no standard glyph. Outside the font that defines them, most editors render them as a small blank box, a question mark, or nothing visible at all -- so a bullet character that displayed as a checkmark icon in your resume software can paste as something that looks like empty space next to your bullet text. Scanning past a line that reads ' Led a team of six' registers as normal; only a tool that checks the character's code point, not its rendered appearance, catches it reliably.

So is doing it by eye pointless?

No -- it is the same underlying technique and it is free. What changes is consistency and what gets checked. A human read is good at noticing the paste is obviously broken or clearly fine; it is not good at counting how many lines have a 4-space mid-line gap, measuring what fraction of the document your email address falls within, or reliably spotting a code point that renders as nothing. This checker runs the same fixed set of checks against the same paste every time, in the browser, with nothing uploaded -- a second pair of eyes that never gets tired of counting spaces.
