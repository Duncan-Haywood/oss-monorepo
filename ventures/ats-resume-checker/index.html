ATS RESUME CHECKER · RUNS IN YOUR BROWSER · NOTHING UPLOADED

Will your resume even reach a human? Run the copy-paste test.

Most job applications go through an applicant tracking system before anyone reads them, and it extracts your resume's text the same mechanical way copying it out by hand does. Select all the text in your resume file, copy it, and paste it below — this page reads the exact same extraction for the specific, well-documented formatting problems that break it: icon-font bullets that paste as garbage, missing section headings, contact info buried where a parser won't look, and layouts that scramble when flattened to plain text.

Nothing is uploaded. The text you paste is read in this browser and never leaves your device — see the privacy note in the footer.

1. Copy the text out of your resume file

Open your resume (PDF or Word), select all the text (Ctrl+A / Cmd+A), and copy it (Ctrl+C / Cmd+C). Do not retype it — the whole point is testing what a mechanical extraction produces, not what you meant to write.

      What this does and doesn't check

      Steps 1–2 read the text you paste for symptoms that are documented to break ATS text extraction: icon-font characters standing in for bullets, corrupted text, missing standard section headings, missing or buried contact information, missing dates, and signs of a multi-column layout collapsing into one line. Step 3 separately compares your wording with one job posting you paste. It does not check achievement phrasing or grammar — and it cannot see your file's actual visual layout (columns, text boxes, images), only what copied out as text, which is exactly what most parsers see too.

      Common questions

      What is the ATS copy-paste test?

      Copy all the text out of your resume file (Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C from the PDF or Word document — never retype it) and look at what comes out. Applicant tracking systems extract resume text the same mechanical way, so a garbled, out-of-order, or incomplete copy is a strong sign the same content will not parse correctly.

      Does this tell me whether my resume will pass a specific company's ATS?

      No. ATS products differ in how they extract and score text, and this tool has no visibility into any specific employer's system. It reports evidence from the text you pasted — formatting symptoms that are documented to cause parsing problems across most systems — and leaves the decision to you.

      Why paste text instead of uploading my resume file?

      Uploading a file means a server somewhere reads your resume's contents. Pasting text you already copied keeps everything in your browser — nothing about your resume ever leaves your device — and it doubles as the copy-paste test itself: if the paste comes out garbled, that is the finding.

      What if my resume is already text and copies out perfectly?

      Then the format check will likely report no findings, which is a good sign for the checks it runs — but format is only half of it. Step 3 compares your wording with a job posting you paste; it still does not judge achievement phrasing or grammar.
