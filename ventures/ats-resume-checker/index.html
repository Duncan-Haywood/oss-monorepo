What this does and doesn't check

This reads the text you paste for symptoms that are documented to break ATS text extraction: icon-font characters standing in for bullets, corrupted text, missing standard section headings, missing or buried contact information, missing dates, and signs of a multi-column layout collapsing into one line. It does not check keyword match against a specific job posting, achievement phrasing, or grammar — and it cannot see your file's actual visual layout (columns, text boxes, images), only what copied out as text, which is exactly what most parsers see too.

Common questions

What is the ATS copy-paste test?

Copy all the text out of your resume file (Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C from the PDF or Word document — never retype it) and look at what comes out. Applicant tracking systems extract resume text the same mechanical way, so a garbled, out-of-order, or incomplete copy is a strong sign the same content will not parse correctly.

Does this tell me whether my resume will pass a specific company's ATS?

No. ATS products differ in how they extract and score text, and this tool has no visibility into any specific employer's system. It reports evidence from the text you pasted — formatting symptoms that are documented to cause parsing problems across most systems — and leaves the decision to you.

Why paste text instead of uploading my resume file?

Uploading a file means a server somewhere reads your resume's contents. Pasting text you already copied keeps everything in your browser — nothing about your resume ever leaves your device — and it doubles as the copy-paste test itself: if the paste comes out garbled, that is the finding.

What if my resume is already text and copies out perfectly?

Then this tool will likely report no findings, which is a good sign for the format checks it runs — but it does not check word choice, relevance to a specific job description, or achievement phrasing, which matter just as much and are outside what a structural check can see.
