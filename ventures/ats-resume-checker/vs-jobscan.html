ATS Resume Checker vs. Jobscan

Two different jobs. Jobscan matches your resume’s wording against one specific job posting, behind an account. This page runs the free, no-account, no-upload copy-paste test for a narrower, earlier question: does the text that comes out of your resume file even extract cleanly, before keywords are worth comparing at all?

What each tool is for

Jobscan is a resume-optimization service built around one core mechanism: you paste in your resume and a target job description, and it scores the overlap between them — hard skills, soft skills, keyword density, and related signals — then tells you which terms from the posting are missing from your resume. That is genuinely useful for tailoring one application to one posting. It runs on an account, has a limited free tier, and a paid plan for scanning more than a handful of applications, based on independent review coverage of the product (Jobscan’s own site was not reachable to confirm current limits or pricing from the session that generated this page, so no dollar figure or scan count is stated here as verified fact).

This checker does a different, earlier job. It needs no job posting and no account. You paste the text copied out of your resume file — the same mechanical extraction an ATS performs — and it flags the specific, documented symptoms of a format that breaks that extraction: icon-font bullet characters that paste as garbage, corrupted text, missing standard section headings, contact info buried where a parser won’t look, missing dates, and signs of a collapsed multi-column layout. It runs free in any browser and nothing you paste is uploaded anywhere.

Common questions

Should I use this checker or Jobscan?

They answer different questions, and most job seekers end up wanting both at different points. Jobscan asks: does this resume's wording match the specific job posting I'm applying to? You give it your resume and a job description, it scores hard skills, soft skills and keyword overlap between the two, and tells you which keywords to add. That needs an account, and doing it for more than a handful of applications needs a paid plan. This checker asks a narrower, earlier question: does the text that comes out of my resume file even extract cleanly, before keywords matter at all? You paste text copied out of your resume file -- no job posting needed -- and it flags icon-font bullets, corrupted characters, missing section headings, buried contact info, missing dates, and signs of a collapsed multi-column layout: the structural problems that can make an ATS misread or drop content regardless of which keywords are in it. A resume that fails the format check will likely score badly on a keyword match too, for the wrong reason -- the parser never read the words correctly to compare them. Run the format check first; if it's clean, a keyword tool like Jobscan is the right next step for tailoring to one specific posting.

Does Jobscan check for the same formatting problems this tool does?

Jobscan's own stated mechanism is a match-rate score between your resume and a job description -- hard skills, soft skills, keyword density, and related signals -- and it does also report on some formatting and section-heading issues as part of that scan, according to independent reviews of the product. What this page will not do is describe Jobscan's exact scoring formula, scan limits or current pricing tiers as fact, because Jobscan's own site is not reachable from the session that generated this page and only third-party review coverage was available to check against, which this page treats as informative rather than authoritative. The one difference stated with confidence is the one both tools' own positioning agrees on: Jobscan's central job is matching a resume against a specific job posting's keywords, behind an account; this tool's only job is the format check, with no posting, no account and nothing uploaded.
