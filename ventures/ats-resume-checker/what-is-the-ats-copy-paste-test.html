What is the ATS copy-paste test, and why does it work?

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is the software that reads your resume before a person does. Most of them work the same mechanical way: they extract the text from your file — PDF or Word — and parse it into fields like contact info, work history, and skills. They do not "see" your resume the way a human reading a printed page does; they read the underlying text stream, in whatever order the file exposes it.

That is exactly what your operating system's copy command does too. So copying all the text out of your resume file and looking at what comes out is a genuine, if imperfect, simulation of what an ATS parser sees — and it needs no special software, no account, and no upload.

How to run it

  1. Open your resume file (PDF or Word — whatever you actually send to employers).
  2. Select all the text (Ctrl+A / Cmd+A) and copy it (Ctrl+C / Cmd+C).
  3. Paste it somewhere you can actually read it — a plain text editor, or the ATS resume checker on this site, which also flags the specific symptoms below automatically.
  4. Read what came out, not what you remember writing. Is everything there? In the right order? Readable?

What to look for

Garbled or missing characters. If bullet points, checkmarks, or icons turn into odd boxes or unreadable characters, your template is using an icon font for decoration — the character that displays as a bullet in your design software has no meaning outside it, and an ATS reading the raw text sees the same garbage character this test does.

Sections that don't show up at all. If your Skills or Education section is completely missing from the pasted text, that content is probably living in a text box, a graphic, or a table cell that didn't copy as plain text — which usually means it won't extract for an ATS either.

Contact info in the wrong place, or missing. If your name, email, or phone number don't appear near the top — or don't appear at all — check whether they're set in a header or footer. Some ATS platforms deliberately ignore header/footer content because it's inconsistent across resume templates.

Content that reads out of order. Two-column layouts and tables are designed to be read visually, top-to-bottom within each column. A mechanical text extraction usually reads left-to-right per line instead, which can interleave your job titles with your skills list into a scrambled mess.

No dates anywhere. If your employment dates don't show up as plain text (say, because they're in a graphic timeline), an ATS can't build a work-history timeline from them.

What this test can't tell you

It's a proxy, not a guarantee. Different ATS products (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Taleo, and others) use different parsing engines, and some are considerably more forgiving than others. A resume that copies out cleanly is more likely to parse well broadly, but no free tool — this one included — can promise a specific result on a specific company's system. It also says nothing about whether your resume's content is compelling, relevant to the role, or well-written; that's a separate, and arguably more important, question.
