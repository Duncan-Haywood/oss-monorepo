Questions people ask

Should I sell my oil and gas mineral rights for the offer I received?

This tool does not tell you to. It shows what the offer is worth against the royalties you would otherwise collect: the present value of those royalties at your own discount rate, the annual return the buyer earns at the offered price, and the same comparison after tax. An offer that implies a buyer return far above what you could earn elsewhere is paying you less than the stream is worth to you.

What is the implied return on a mineral rights offer?

It is the discount rate at which the royalties you would give up are worth exactly the offer, an internal rate of return. Mineral buyers typically price at a high return because production declines and prices move. Compare the number with what the money could earn if you kept it, and with what other buyers would offer.

Why does the tool ask about production decline?

Royalties from a producing well fall fast in the first years and flatten later. A buyer prices that curve in; a rule of thumb like a multiple of last year's check ignores it. The grid shows how the value moves if the decline is steeper or gentler than you assume. Your operator's reports or a petroleum engineer can give a better figure.

What about wells that have not been drilled yet?

Enter the monthly royalty a new well might pay, the year it starts and the chance you give it of being drilled. The tool weights it by that chance and also shows the value without it, so you can see how much of the price rests on a well that does not exist yet.

Can I sell only part of the term?

Yes. Some offers buy the royalties for a fixed number of years. Enter the years the offer buys; the tool values the years you sell and, separately, the years you keep.

How is tax handled?

You type a rate for the lump sum, applied to the offer minus your basis, and a rate for royalty income. It is a rough comparison only. Depletion, capital gain or ordinary income treatment depends on how the sale is structured, so confirm with a tax professional before signing anything.

Is this legal, tax or financial advice?

No. It is arithmetic on numbers you type in, with simplified assumptions: yearly end-of-year payments, a constant decline and a constant price change. Have an oil and gas attorney read any deed or purchase agreement, and check for liens, co-owners and leases in force before you sign.

Not decided

The real production decline of your wells, future commodity prices, whether undrilled locations will be developed, title, liens and co-owners, the deed language (what minerals, depths and royalty share actually transfer), how the sale is taxed, what the right discount rate is for you, and whether to sell at all. Royalties are modelled once a year at year end with a constant decline and a constant price change.
