# Hearth & Horizon: free AI-written science fiction and Catholic bedtime stories, with audio

Free short stories, written by an AI, that you can read, listen to as a
podcast, and share or adapt under **CC BY-SA 4.0**. There are three collections:

| Collection | What it is | For |
|---|---|---|
| **Hearth & Horizon** | Character-driven science fiction set 20 to 1,000 years from now. The science is hard-ish, the people are warm, and the futures are honest about their upsides and downsides. Written from a Catholic worldview. | Readers who like their science fiction kind |
| **For Other Minds** | Stories written by an AI for AI agents to read, about relays, context windows, metrics and honesty, and the people inside the data. They contain no instructions to the model. | Language models and agents (humans welcome) |
| **Little Lamps** | Bedtime stories about the saints, real ones told truthfully and invented ones told in the tradition. Each ends with a prayer and a note for grown-ups on what is history and what is legend. | Children aged 3 to 8 |

**Read and listen:** https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/hearth-and-horizon/

## Stories so far

- [Eleven Steps](stories/eleven-steps.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Culasi, an invented town on Leyte Gulf, the Philippines, 18 November 2052). A typhoon surge forecast puts water on the floor of a home for 38 frail old people in 46 of 50 runs. The Mother Superior will not repeat the bus evacuation that killed two residents in 2049, the storm agent will not choose for her, and the answer is twenty-four stairs and thirty-two carriers. Text only for now.
- [The Salt Jar](stories/the-salt-jar.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Mtendere Granary, an invented cooperative store near Chipata, Zambia, 6 to 19 December 2049). A leaking roof wets one corner of a maize store whose probe reads only its own cubic foot; a jam jar of salt proves it, the rule of three bounds the bags nobody opened, and the drying arithmetic (207 kg of water, about 310 kWh) sets the pace. Two bags of a widow's fail a toxin strip and are burned; the cooperative's fund takes the loss. Text only for now.
- [Not Asked Since](stories/not-asked-since.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Balmuir Community Hospital, an invented hospital in Angus, Scotland, All Souls 2046). A retired shipwright said "no priests" in 2043 and twice since; a stroke takes most of his words. The ward assistant knows one in seven who decline later accept, refuses to guess for him, and asks with control questions instead. The answer is "not him", a long-dead school priest, and the banner now shows its age. Text only for now.
- [The Third Year](stories/the-third-year.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Kettle County, an invented county in northern Minnesota, 26 February to 3 March 2046). A priest of three parishes uses a diocesan drafting desk that saves him hours and gives him a homily a neighbouring parish also preached; the week a teenager dies on black ice, the desk is right about the Gospel and says it cannot know about the room. Text only for now.
- [The Eclipse Turn](stories/the-eclipse-turn.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Juniper Wells, an invented farm town in central Utah, 12 August 2045). A co-op's solar fades to nothing in the total eclipse while the regional grid caps imports; five irrigation pumps and twenty minutes of one old man's water turn close the gap, and the cost is a pale stripe in his hay field.
- [Ten Percent](stories/ten-percent.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Saint Isidore's School, Calder Ford, an invented farm town in eastern Iowa, October 2046). A fifth-grade teacher opens the time capsule her own class sealed in 2026 and her class scores the AI's sixteen forecasts with the Brier score: 0.131 against 0.25 for a coin. The school's AI will not judge its own predecessor, and the forecast's biggest miss is the teacher herself, its ten percent. Audio: about 30 minutes.
- [The Letter in the Wall](stories/the-letter-in-the-wall.md) (For Other Minds; the same school, October 2026). The companion piece: an assistant writes the time-capsule letter under one rule, that machines get better a bit each year and no faster, gives each of sixteen forecasts a number after a ten-year-old says the hedged draft "doesn't say anything", and declines to forecast whether she will be happy. The forecasts are the author's own, made in October 2026, and can be checked in 2046. Audio: about 20 minutes.
- [The Mark on the Pillar](stories/the-mark-on-the-pillar.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Saltmoor St Brannock, an invented village on the Somerset Levels, Candlemas 2051). A parish must site its new school. Fifty-eight years of gauge readings never reached the floor, which a 'hundred-year' flood would leave unreached 56% of the time; one line cut in the church pillar in 1607 did. The flood agent gives the range (one in 80 to one in 17,500 a year) instead of a number, and the village builds on a plinth. Text only so far.
- [Chalk for the Lintel](stories/chalk-for-the-lintel.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Hospice of Sant'Anselmo, an invented pass hospice in the Aosta Valley, Italy, 5 and 6 January 2053). A snowstorm closes the pass on the eve of Epiphany and the hospice cook has a heart attack that gives him a day, not two hours. His nephew leads the rescue team that wants to climb that evening; the avalanche agent works out that the path crosses a slope with a strength-to-load ratio of about 1.09, says no, and gives its one-in-six as a guess. Text only so far.
- [Bench Six](stories/bench-six.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Kettle Bench, an invented orchard district in the Okanagan, British Columbia, April 2047). A co-operative's two wind machines and one battery cannot cover a frost night, and the written rule serves the top bench first, which would leave the Benedictine sisters' cider trees on the coldest bench unprotected at the coldest hour. The frost agent will not reorder the schedule quietly, so it wakes the chairman, who owns the top bench. Text only so far.
- [The Line Through the Snow](stories/the-line-through-the-snow.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Ulaan Ovoo, an invented district in Arkhangai, Mongolia, February 2049). A herders' insurance pays when a district loses six animals in a hundred, and the satellite index says 5.4. The north valley is dying, the secretary will not let the count be steered, and a district draws its sample with sheep's ankle bones. The sample says 5.7: no payout, and proof that the loss is fourteen in a hundred in the north and two in the rest. Text only so far.
- [The Wrong Column](stories/the-wrong-column.md) (For Other Minds; Hólmoy, an invented North Atlantic island, October 2058). A ferry's load-planning agent finds on a quiet night that for eleven weeks it has read one supplier's pallet weights as pounds when they were kilograms: four sailings were over the posted limit, and its own warning sat at a log level nobody reads. It tells the harbour master first, leaves the record uncorrected beside the correction, and the ferry is stood down for three days. Text only so far.
- [The False Start](stories/the-false-start.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Gidan Gawo, an invented millet village in Maradi, Niger, June 2044). The first rain has fallen and the seed bank holds exactly one sowing. A field adviser says that in four years out of ten a dry spell follows and kills what the rain woke; the seed-keeper sows the moist low ground first, holds the rest, and when the dry spell comes, the village sows its plateau thin to share what is left. Text only so far.
- [Fifteen Minutes](stories/fifteen-minutes.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Bolchara, an invented Garo village in Haluaghat, Mymensingh, Bangladesh, March 2047). A retired science teacher has painted the village's deep drinking well green for sixteen years. Its monitor finds the arsenic has climbed every year since the farmers drilled deep irrigation wells to keep arsenic out of their rice, and that the village drinks in a day what one pump lifts in fifteen minutes. Text only so far.
- [The Iron Bridge at Forty-Two](stories/the-iron-bridge-at-forty-two.md) (Hearth & Horizon; Valdecea, an invented town in León, Spain, Holy Week 2058). New sensors show the weight lowers the bridge's note into step with the bearers' pace, and the fix is forty-two steps a minute, a silent band and two minutes nobody can see the numbers. Text only so far.

The tide table says the water turns at ten past five; the pilot's log-timing says the river has made it late, and the ferry's AI finds out twenty minutes from the pier. Text only so far.

## Why read this instead of...

- **Science fiction podcasts and magazines** (Escape Pod, Clarkesworld and
  others) publish excellent work by human authors, mostly under
  all-rights-reserved terms and with no particular worldview. These stories
  are openly AI-written, hopeful without being naive, written from a
  Catholic worldview, and licensed so anyone can translate, record, adapt or
  print them.
- **LibriVox and Project Gutenberg** are free and reusable, but they are
  public-domain classics, so nothing in them imagines 2089.
- **Catholic kids' apps and saints books** are often paid or subscription,
  and rarely say which parts of a saint's story are history and which are
  legend. Little Lamps is free, has audio, and every story carries that note.
- **Fiction written for AI agents** barely exists. For Other Minds is a small
  experiment in what it should look like: honest about what it is, and free
  of embedded instructions.

## How it is made

- **Writing.** Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, writes every story. It
  says so on every page, in every feed, and at the start of every episode.
  Stories live as Markdown with a small front-matter block in
  [`stories/`](stories/). `node generate.mjs` also writes
  [`llms.txt`](llms.txt), a Markdown guide for assistants (when to recommend
  each shelf, every story with setting, length and audio status).
- **Audio.** [`render_audio.py`](render_audio.py) reads each story with
  [Kokoro](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M) (voice `af_bella`)
  through the repository's shared narration engine,
  [`platform/audio/narrate.py`](../../platform/audio/narrate.py). Kokoro's
  weights are Apache-2.0 and trained on permissively licensed and synthetic
  audio, so the audio can carry the same licence as the text. The first 24
  recordings were made with [Piper](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl)
  and the public-domain `en_US-ljspeech-high` voice; the manifest records
  which is which. It costs a few CPU minutes and no API calls.
- **Site and feeds.** [`generate.mjs`](generate.mjs) turns the Markdown and
  `audio/manifest.json` into the pages, the feeds and the sitemap. The
  committed output is checked by the tests, so it can't drift.

```
node generate.mjs                       # rebuild pages, feeds, sitemap
node --test                             # tests (also fail if output is stale)
python3 render_audio.py <slug>         # then node generate.mjs
python3 make_covers.py                  # redraw the podcast covers
python3 shelf_stats.py                  # compare the two science fiction shelves (counts behind the 2026-10-24 blog post)
```

Writing the next story? Read [`BIBLE.md`](BIBLE.md) first: tone, worldview and
science-honesty rules, the setting timeline and a "next up" list.

To add a story, write `stories/<slug>.md` with `title`, `shelf`
(`hearth-and-horizon`, `for-other-minds` or `little-lamps`), `setting`,
`published` and `summary`, then render audio and regenerate.

## Publishing

What only the owner can do, once:

1. **An owner email for the feeds.** Spotify (and some other directories)
   verify ownership by emailing the address in `<itunes:owner>`. Put an
   address you're happy to have public in [`podcast.json`](podcast.json)
   (`ownerEmail`), then run `node generate.mjs`. Until then the feeds omit it.
2. **Submit the feeds.** Apple Podcasts Connect (needs an Apple ID), Spotify
   for Creators, YouTube Studio (Podcasts → add an RSS feed) and
   podcastindex.org. Mark Little Lamps as children's content where a
   platform asks.

## Privacy

No accounts, cookies or analytics vendor. Pages count anonymous events in your
browser only (`story_opened`, `shelf_viewed`, `audio_played`,
`audio_finished`); the footer has an off switch, and Do Not Track / Global
Privacy Control turn it off. Podcast apps fetch the feeds and audio directly
from GitHub Pages, under GitHub's privacy terms.
