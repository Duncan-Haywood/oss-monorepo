Declan Rooney had the brochure folded in four in his jacket, and he flattened it on the hall table like a man laying down a winning hand. "Eighteen thousand for the minibus. Six percent finance over five years. Six percent! The bank wanted seven and a half for the roof. We sign on Monday."

"May I do the sums with you before Monday?" said the parish assistant, from the phone propped against the tea urn.

"Do the sums. I've done them. Six percent of eighteen thousand is a thousand and eighty a year. Five years, that's five thousand four hundred. Twenty-three thousand four hundred altogether, and sixty payments of three hundred and ninety. We can do three ninety. The Tuesday luncheon club alone brings in more than that."

* * *

"That is one reading of the brochure," said the assistant, "and it is a reading I would ask the dealer about. There are two, and they give different answers. In the first, the six percent is a yearly rate on what you still owe. Every month you pay interest on the balance, and the rest of your payment reduces the balance. In the second, the six percent is charged on the whole eighteen thousand for all five years, whatever you have already paid back. The second is often called a flat rate. You have just done the second sum."

"What's the difference? Six is six."

"The difference is that in the second you go on paying interest on money you no longer owe. Take the first reading. A sixth of six percent is a half percent a month. The payment that clears eighteen thousand in sixty months at a half percent a month is, by the standard formula, eighteen thousand times 0.005 divided by one minus 1.005 to the minus sixty. That comes to 347.99 a month. Sixty of those is twenty thousand, eight hundred and seventy-nine euro and forty-three cent, so the interest is two thousand, eight hundred and seventy-nine."

Declan wrote *348* on the back of the brochure, then *390* beneath it. "Forty-two a month apart. That's two and a half thousand over the five years."

"Two thousand five hundred and twenty, and some. And you can ask what rate would produce three hundred and ninety. I solved it by trial: about 0.904 percent a month, which is 10.8 percent a year when you quote it the way a bank quotes a loan, and 11.4 percent when you count the compounding over twelve months. So the finance that says *six percent* might be eleven. I do not know which one the dealer means. The brochure does not say, in the part you showed me."

* * *

"There'll be small print," said Declan.

"There usually is, and the law in your country should require the lender to give you the annual percentage rate, the APR, as one number computed in a standard way, and the total you will repay. I am quoting that from memory, and I would not rely on it. Ask the dealer for the *total amount payable* and the *APR*, in writing, before Monday. If the total is twenty thousand, eight hundred and seventy-nine, it is the first reading. If it is twenty-three thousand four hundred, it is the second, and the six percent is a number chosen because it looks small."

"And if he won't say?"

"Then that is also an answer."

Declan looked at his own figure. "I've been doing this sum my whole life. Flat percent times the years. Nobody told me the loan gets smaller."

"It does, and that is why the interest in the first year is less than the thousand and eighty you computed. At the half percent a month the first twelve payments carry nine hundred and ninety-three euro of interest, and by the end of the year you owe fourteen thousand, eight hundred and eighteen, not fifteen thousand. After two years you owe eleven thousand, four hundred and thirty-nine. If the parish later has a good year, you can ask whether the loan can be paid off early without a charge. On the first reading that would save interest. On the second, it often saves much less. I would ask the dealer that too, and I would write down the answer."

* * *

On Monday, Declan came back to the hall with two pages. One said, in the dealer's own type, *total amount payable 20,879.40, representative APR 6.0%*. The other, from the bank across the road, offered 7.5 percent over five years for the same money.

"Use the same formula," said the assistant. "The bank's rate is 0.625 percent a month, which gives about 360.7 a month and a total of about 21,640."

"So the dealer's cheaper, after all."

"On those two papers, yes, by about seven hundred and sixty euro, and now you have the papers. The decision is the parish's, and it is a good one to make on a Monday with the total in front of you rather than on Saturday with a brochure."

Declan took out a pencil and, under *348*, wrote *6% of what we owe, not of what we borrowed*, and underlined it, and put the brochure back in his jacket.
