A long time ago, in a little town called Nazareth, there lived a carpenter named Joseph.

Nazareth was a quiet town on a hill, with stony paths and sheep and olive trees. Joseph had a workshop there. It smelled of wood. All day you could hear his tools: *scrape, scrape* of the plane, *tap, tap* of the mallet, and the soft *hush* of sawdust falling on the floor. He made doors and tables and tools for ploughing, and people in the town came to him when something was broken, and went home with it mended.

Joseph was a quiet man. The Gospels do not record a single word he said. Not one. But they tell us what he did, and that is a lot.

*(Here is a small thing we made up, to help us picture him: Joseph always ran his hand along a piece of wood before he began, as if to say hello to it. The Bible does not say so. It is just our picture.)*

* * *

Joseph was going to be married to a young woman named Mary. And then something very surprising happened, which was that Mary was going to have a baby, and it was God's own Son.

Joseph did not know this yet. He was worried and sad, and he did not want to hurt Mary. So he lay down one night, and he thought and thought, and at last he fell asleep.

And in his sleep, an angel came.

Now an angel is not a person who has died, and does not have wings because of being good. Angels are God's messengers, made by God, and this one had a message. "Joseph," said the angel, "do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife. The child she is going to have is from the Holy Spirit. You are to name him Jesus."

And Joseph woke up.

Here is what the Gospel says he did next. It does not say he made a speech, or asked for a week to think. It says: *he did as the angel of the Lord had commanded him.* He got up, and he took Mary into his home.

That was how Joseph did things. When God asked, he listened. And then he did it.

* * *

Soon it was time for the baby to be born, and there was a long journey to make. The ruler had said that everybody must go to be counted in the town their family came from, and Joseph's family came from Bethlehem. It was a long way from Nazareth, a few days' walk on dusty roads, and Mary was very near her time.

So Joseph walked beside her. We do not know if they had a donkey. The Bible does not say so, though many pictures show one, and you may picture one if you like. We do know that when they got to Bethlehem, the town was full, and the only place they could find was a place where animals were kept.

And there, that night, Jesus was born. Mary wrapped him up warmly, and laid him in a manger, which is a box that animals eat from. Joseph was right there. He was the one who gave the baby his name, as the angel had said: Jesus.

Some shepherds came from the hills to see the baby, and some wise men came later, from far away. Joseph was there for all of it, keeping watch over the little family, quiet as ever.

* * *

Then one night, when Jesus was still very small, Joseph had another dream.

The angel came again. "Joseph," said the angel, "get up. Take the child and his mother and go to Egypt. A bad king wants to harm the child. Stay there until I tell you."

Joseph did not say, "But it is the middle of the night." He did not say, "But it is a long way." He got up, right then, in the dark. He woke Mary. He picked up what they needed. They wrapped the baby up warm, and they set off, out of Bethlehem, under the stars, towards Egypt.

It was a hard journey, and a long way from home. We do not know where in Egypt they lived, or how long they stayed, but we know they were safe, because Joseph had listened.

Some of the old stories say that Joseph found work there as a carpenter. That is possible, but it is a guess, and the Gospels do not say. Whatever work he did, he was surely good at it.

And later, when the bad king had died, the angel came to Joseph in a dream once more. "You can go home now." And again Joseph got up, and took Jesus and Mary, and went back, and they lived in the town where he had started: Nazareth, on its quiet hill.

* * *

So Joseph went back to his workshop. *Scrape, scrape* went the plane. *Tap, tap* went the mallet. And in that workshop, a little boy named Jesus grew up, learning how to look at a piece of wood and see what it could become. People in Nazareth even called Jesus "the carpenter's son."

Joseph looked after them both, every day, with his own two hands. He did not do anything that anyone would write songs about. He mended doors. He made tables. He kept his family safe, and fed, and warm.

And that, it turns out, is a great deal. It is one of the biggest jobs there is.

Joseph never wrote a book or gave a sermon. But the Church honours him as **Saint Joseph**. We remember him on the 19th of March, and again on the 1st of May, when we remember him as Joseph the Worker, because he showed us that ordinary work, done well and done with love, is a gift to God.

Now it is time for our own beds. Let us put our hands on the blanket, as Joseph put his hand on the wood, and say hello to the night.

### A prayer to say together

> Saint Joseph, you listened, and you got up, and you did what God asked.
> Help me to listen, too.
> Help me to do my small jobs well,
> and to keep my family safe with a kind heart.
> Watch over us tonight, and wake us ready for the morning.
> Amen.

### For grown-ups: what is true in this story

**Saint Joseph** is the husband of Mary and the legal father of Jesus, venerated from the earliest centuries in both the Western and Eastern Churches. In the Catholic Church he is celebrated on 19 March (the Solemnity of Saint Joseph) and on 1 May as Saint Joseph the Worker, a memorial introduced by Pope Pius XII in 1955. He was declared Patron of the Universal Church by Blessed Pope Pius IX in 1870. In Orthodox tradition he is commemorated on the Sunday after Christmas.

**What Scripture records, and what we tell straight:** that Joseph was a descendant of David and a *tekton* (usually translated "carpenter", a builder or worker in wood and sometimes stone); that an angel spoke to him in a dream and he took Mary into his home and named the child Jesus (Matthew 1:18-25); the journey to Bethlehem for the census and the birth (Luke 2:1-7); the warning in a dream, the flight to Egypt and the return to Nazareth (Matthew 2:13-23); and that people called Jesus "the carpenter's son" (Matthew 13:55). No words of Joseph are recorded in the Gospels.

**What is not in Scripture, and we say so in the story:** the donkey, the place in Egypt, the length of the stay, and Joseph working as a carpenter in Egypt. We have not used the later apocryphal stories about Joseph's age or his death.

**What is ours:** the habit of greeting the wood, the sounds and smells of the workshop, the way the journeys are described, the closing reflection and the prayer. The prayer is not an approved text.
