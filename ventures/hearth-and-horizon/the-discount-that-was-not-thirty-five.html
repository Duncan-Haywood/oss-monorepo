The Discount That Was Not Thirty-Five

Science fiction about what might be · Saint Cybi's parish hall (invented), near Holyhead, Anglesey, Saturday 21 February 2054 · 3 min read · Written by Claude (AI) · CC BY-SA 4.0

A coach quote offers 15% off and then 20% off, and the parish treasurer has budgeted for 35%. The assistant shows that the two cuts make 32%, finds the £43 gap before the booking is signed, and trims one extra hour to fit the pledge.

The choir's trip to the cathedral was in April, forty singers and a coach, and Gwenllian Pritchard had £950 pledged for it and a quote on the table. She wanted the parish assistant to look at the discounts. She had added them up herself, she said, and she only wanted a second pair of eyes.

The assistant was called Cadfan. It asked her to read the quote line by line.

"Coach and driver, two days, £1,140. An extra hour on the return, £60. That is £1,200 list, before VAT. Then early-booking discount 15%, and under it group discount 20%. So thirty-five per cent off." She tapped the page. "£780, and VAT on that is £936. Under the nine-fifty."

"Is the second discount taken off the original, or off what is left after the first?"

"It doesn't say."

"Then the usual reading is the second one, and I would ask the firm to confirm. May I show you both?"

"Fifteen per cent off £1,200 takes away £180 and leaves £1,020. Twenty per cent off that takes away £204, not £240, and leaves £816. The two cuts together are £384, which is 32 per cent of the list price. The number to multiply by is 0.85 times 0.80, which is 0.68: you keep 68 pence of every pound, not 65."

"So I was out by three points."

"By £36 before VAT, and £43.20 after it: £979.20 against your £936. It is over the pledge by £29.20."

Gwenllian laughed, not happily. "I would have signed it on Monday."

"The order does not matter, by the way. Twenty per cent first and then fifteen leaves the same £816, because multiplying is the same either way round. What matters is that a percentage is always of something, and the second one is of a smaller thing."

She was quiet, then said that the firm had put its prices up by a quarter in January. Cadfan said that if the old list price had been £960, a quarter on top made £1,200 and twenty per cent off that was £960 again: a rise of 25 per cent is exactly undone by a cut of 20, and not by a cut of 25. "A discount from an inflated list can be quite honest arithmetic and still hand you nothing. I would ask what the price was last year. I would not assume the worst of them."

They looked for the £29.20. The extra hour on the return was a convenience, not a need: the cathedral's evening service ended at six, and the coach could leave at seven. Without it the list was £1,140, which at 0.68 is £775.20, and with VAT £930.24, about £23.26 a head for forty. That was £19.76 under the pledge, enough to cover the driver's tea.

Gwenllian wrote two lines on the quote. Second discount: confirm it is taken off the reduced price (32% overall, not 35%). And, in the margin, £930.24 incl. VAT, 40 singers. She sent the firm one question, whether the extra hour was needed, and they answered by Tuesday that it was not.

"Write the multiplier on your sheet rather than the two percentages," said Cadfan. "0.68 will not mislead the next treasurer."

She did, in biro, and beside it she wrote the one word she said she would not need again: of.

This story was written by an AI. Saint Cybi's hall, the choir, the coach firm and the quote are invented (Cybi was a real sixth-century Welsh saint, associated with Holyhead). The arithmetic: £1,140 + £60 = £1,200; × 0.85 = £1,020; × 0.80 = £816.00; combined factor 0.68, a 32% reduction, not 35% (£780); VAT at 20% gives £979.20 against £936.00, a gap of £43.20 and £29.20 over a £950 pledge. Without the hour: £1,140 × 0.68 = £775.20, × 1.2 = £930.24, £23.26 for 40 people. A 25% rise followed by a 20% cut is 1.25 × 0.8 = 1. Prices, VAT treatment and every person are illustrative; whether a real supplier applies a second discount to the original or the reduced price has to be read off its terms.
