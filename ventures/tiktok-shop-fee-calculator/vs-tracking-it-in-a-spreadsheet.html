TikTok Shop Fee Calculator vs. tracking it in a spreadsheet

A seller who prices more than one TikTok Shop listing usually ends up with a spreadsheet: a cell for the referral rate, one for the creator commission, a note somewhere that the commission is not clawed back on a return, and a second copy of the whole sheet the week the announced 1 October 2026 schedule needs comparing against today's. Each formula is ordinary arithmetic. Keeping them right — especially the commission-survives-a-return rule, which is the one detail a generic "TikTok takes 6%" page or a rushed spreadsheet formula is most likely to get backwards — is the part that gets skipped when a listing needs a price today.

What each approach actually does

A spreadsheet can reproduce every formula this calculator runs — the referral fee on the fee base, an affiliate commission held back from the seller's net but kept by the creator on a return, the facilitation fee that applies only to affiliate-sourced orders, and a break-even or target-margin price. Building it means reading the current rates off Seller University, encoding the commission-survives-a-return rule correctly, and adding a second block of formulas to hold the announced October schedule beside today's on the same order.

This calculator keeps the organic-versus-affiliate split, the return rate, and both fee schedules on one screen, already wired to the presets in the calculator. A shareable link carries the full scenario in the URL, and the fee-change page and returns page work a full example through the arithmetic this page only describes.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet do this math instead?

Yes. Every formula this calculator runs — the referral fee on the fee base, an affiliate commission that is not clawed back when the order is returned, the creator facilitation fee on affiliate-sourced orders, a break-even and target-margin price, and today's schedule against the announced 1 October 2026 one — is ordinary arithmetic you can put in a spreadsheet. Getting there means reading the rates off Seller University yourself, building a formula that keeps commission out of a refund credit without also keeping it out of the cost side, and duplicating the whole sheet to hold a second schedule next to the first.

What does a spreadsheet not do that this calculator does?

Keep the organic-versus-affiliate split, the return rate, and the fee-schedule comparison on one screen without re-deriving the sheet each time a rate changes. The easiest mistake to make by hand is netting the affiliate commission back out of a returned order's cost — it is not: the creator keeps it. This calculator applies that rule the same way every time, computes a price that holds your margin, and produces a shareable link that carries the whole scenario instead of a file to keep in sync between two people.

Is a spreadsheet ever the better choice?

Yes, when your situation has a wrinkle this calculator does not model — a bundle of several SKUs in one order, a tiered commission that changes by volume, or a fee structure from a category or plan this tool's inputs cannot represent. A spreadsheet you build yourself can represent any rule you can describe. This calculator trades that flexibility for a form that already has the right fields for one SKU moving through the organic-versus-affiliate, returns, and schedule-change decisions.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. A shareable link carries your numbers in the URL itself rather than sending them to a server, and the optional usage counter records only which features were used and the day, never your prices, costs, or creator rates.
