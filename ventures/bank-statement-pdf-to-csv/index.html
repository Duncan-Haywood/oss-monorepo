The model, and what it leaves out

  • Reading the PDF: the text layer is read directly (FlateDecode streams, compressed object streams, ToUnicode character maps) and grouped into lines by position.
  • Scans and photos (OCR): a PDF with no text layer has the picture on each page taken out (JPEG, or a Flate grey, colour or black-and-white image) and read by tesseract.js in the tab. Each word keeps its position, so amounts stay under their Withdrawals, Deposits and Balance headings; a slightly turned page is straightened before the words are put into rows; look-alike characters in figures (4.5O, 2,202:33, a lost decimal point) are fixed. Then the running balance checks every row. A figure is corrected only when the balance printed on that row is confirmed by the next row (or the closing balance), the new figure differs from what OCR read by one digit (two in a number of the same length, 1 for 7 and 3 for 8 counting half), and it moves the balance the way the row's column says. Every correction is listed and marked in the table. A row lost from the scan is never covered up this way: its amount is unrelated to its neighbour's, so the balance break is reported instead. CCITT fax, JBIG2 and JPEG 2000 scans are not read (save the scan as JPEG or PNG and choose that).
  • Rows: a transaction starts with a date (or a transaction date and a posting date), has a description, and ends with one to three amounts. A line below with no date and no amount is added to the description above it if it starts under the description; a line with no date but an amount under the table's columns is its own row on the date above (many banks print the date once a day). Text printed in the same place on every page, such as a footer or watermark, is never added.
  • Languages and formats: month names in English, French, Dutch, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese; headings and balance labels such as Solde d'ouverture, Beginsaldo, Saldo anterior or Alter Kontostand; amounts like 1,234.56, 1.234,56 and 1 234,56 $, and 1935.3 on statements that print one decimal.
  • Several statements at once: choose a year of monthly PDFs together. They are sorted by date; each must add up on its own, and each statement's opening balance must equal the previous one's closing. A missing month is reported as a gap of exactly its net movement, with the two files either side. The same statement chosen twice is counted once, and rows shared by two downloads that overlap are kept once. Scans are read one at a time.
  • Bank files (OFX, QFX, QBO, QIF): written in OFX 1.02, the version QuickBooks and Quicken read. Each transaction id is built from the date, amount and description, with a counter for identical rows on one day, so it is the same in every file that contains that row. Card statements use the credit card section with the balance owed as a negative balance, and the posting date where the statement prints one. Measured: twelve files from made-up statements read the same (rows, net amount, unique ids, balance) by three readers this tool did not write: the OFX file check on this site, and the Python libraries ofxtools and ofxparse. Not yet tested by importing into QuickBooks or Quicken themselves.
  • Several accounts in one PDF: a line naming an account, followed by a running balance that restarts, begins a new account. Each account is checked on its own: its running balance must be unbroken and its rows must match its printed deposit and withdrawal totals. Pick one account for a Xero or QuickBooks file, or download all with an Account column.
  • Bank or card: detected from phrases only card statements print (minimum payment due, payment due date, credit limit), or chosen by you.
  • Money in or out: from the running balance when the statement prints one (the sign that makes the balance add up wins), otherwise from the Debit/Credit or Withdrawals/Deposits column the amount sits under, otherwise from a minus sign, brackets or CR/DR. An amount with none of these is shown as money in and called a guess.
  • Balance check: opening + all rows = closing, in whole cents with no floating point. A gap is reported with its amount, a missing-row, wrong-side or flipped-sign hint, and the line where the running balance first breaks.
  • Also checked: the statement's own "total deposits" and "total withdrawals" lines, dates that are not real days, a missing year, and identical rows.
  • Not checked: that the file matches your bank's online record or your books, scans in languages other than English (the OCR model is English; month names in other languages may be misread), CCITT, JBIG2 and JPEG 2000 scans, handwriting, password-protected PDFs, statements whose text has no character map, currency conversion between accounts in one PDF, a description printed on the line above its amount, and bank-specific layouts nobody has tried. The import layouts for Xero and QuickBooks are written from memory of their public import help and were not tested against a live import.

Common questions

How do I convert a bank statement PDF to CSV?

Choose the PDF (or paste the text copied from it) and press Read and check. The page reads the PDF's text layer in your browser, finds each dated line and its amount, and checks that the opening balance plus the transactions equals the closing balance. Then download the CSV in the layout your accounting software wants.

Is it safe to put a bank statement into a web page?

The PDF is read inside this browser tab. Nothing is uploaded: there is no server behind the page, and a test in the code asserts the reading code contains no network calls. The one download is the OCR reader, fetched only if you press Read the scan. Many online converters upload the file to their servers; this one cannot. Your own policies still decide whether you may open bank data in a browser.

Why does my PDF say it has no text?

A scan or a photo of a paper statement is an image with no text in it. Press Read the scan (OCR) and the page reads the picture in your browser, then proves the rows against the running balance the same way; figures OCR misread are corrected only where the balances on both sides confirm them, and every correction is listed. A PDF downloaded from your bank's website has a text layer and needs no OCR, so prefer it when you can. A password-protected PDF cannot be read: open it, enter the password, and print it to a new PDF.

How do I know the converted rows are right?

The statement is proved against itself. The opening balance plus every row must equal the closing balance, to the cent, and where the statement prints a running balance each row is checked against it. If something does not add up, the page tells you by how much and what is likely wrong, such as a missing row of exactly that amount or a flipped sign.

Does it work for credit card statements?

Yes. Choose Credit card as the account type: a bare amount is a charge, a minus sign or CR is a payment or refund, and the CSV shows charges as negative amounts so it imports like a bank feed. The previous balance and new balance are checked the same way.

Which accounting software can use the CSV?

There are layouts for Xero's statement import (*Date, *Amount, Payee, Description, Reference), QuickBooks Online's three-column and four-column imports, and a generic Date, Description, Amount, Balance file that spreadsheets and most other tools accept. The same rows also download as a bank file: QBO for QuickBooks, QFX for Quicken, OFX for most other tools and QIF for older ones.

Can I convert a whole year of bank statements at once?

Yes. Choose all the monthly PDFs together. They are put in date order, each is proved against its own balances, and each month's opening balance is checked against the previous month's closing. A missing month shows up as a gap of exactly its net amount, naming the files either side. The result downloads as one CSV or one OFX, QFX, QBO or QIF file.

What does this not do?

It does not read password-protected PDFs, handwriting or non-English scans reliably, or understand every bank's layout. Statements whose columns are drawn as images, or text stored without a character map, cannot be read. It does not check the result against your bank or your books. A clean result means the rows add up to the statement's own totals, nothing more.

What happens to dates with no year?

Many statements print 01/05 with no year. The year is taken from the statement period line when there is one, or from the year you enter. Day-month versus month-day is detected from any date that can only be one way and otherwise you are warned and can choose.
