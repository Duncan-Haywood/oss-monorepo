Common questions

    
How much income tax do I pay in Scotland in 2026/27?

    
After the £12,570 personal allowance, Scottish income tax is 19% on the next £3,967, 20% up to £29,526 of income, 21% up to £43,662, 42% up to £75,000, 45% up to £125,140 and 48% above. National Insurance is the same as the rest of the UK. On £30,000 you pay about £3,451 income tax, around £35 less than in England.

    
Do I pay more tax in Scotland than in England?

    
Below roughly £32,000 to £33,000 of income you pay slightly less in Scotland, because of the 19% starter and 20% basic rates and the 21% intermediate rate. Above that you pay more, because the Scottish higher rate is 42% against 40% and starts earlier relative to income. On £50,000 the difference is about £1,500 a year, and the top rate is 48% against 45%.

    
What is the marginal rate between £100,000 and £125,140 in Scotland?

    
The personal allowance is withdrawn at £1 for every £2 over £100,000. With the 45% advanced rate that makes an effective 67.5% income tax rate on that slice, before National Insurance and loans. Pension contributions reduce adjusted net income and can bring it back down.

    
Who is a Scottish taxpayer?

    
HMRC treats you as a Scottish taxpayer if your main place of residence is in Scotland, whatever your workplace. Your tax code starts with S, for example S1257L.

    
Is National Insurance different in Scotland?

    
No. National Insurance is not devolved. Student loan Plan 4 is the Scottish plan, but Scots can be on other plans too.
