This calculator vs. working out the EITC by hand

The headline numbers — up to $8,231 with three or more children — are easy to find. The part that goes wrong by hand is the two-lookup rule, the investment income cliff, and the narrow married-filing- separately exception.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Looking the credit up from earned income only and stopping there. IRS Publication 596's own EIC Worksheet computes the credit a second time from AGI and keeps the SMALLER of the two -- so a filer with modest earned income but higher AGI (investment income under the limit, unemployment, a working spouse on a joint return) can get a materially smaller credit than an earned-income-only lookup would suggest.

What's the second most common mistake?

Forgetting the investment income limit entirely. A filer can have low earned income and qualify for a large credit by the phase-in/phase-out math alone, and still be disqualified outright because investment income (interest, dividends, capital gains, rental income) exceeds $12,200 for 2026 -- a flat disqualifier, not a phase-out.

What arithmetic mistake gets made most often?

Using the wrong phase-out threshold for the number of children. The phase-out threshold is the SAME dollar figure for 1, 2, or 3+ children ($23,890 single, $31,160 joint for 2026) -- only the 0-children category has its own, lower threshold. It is the phase-OUT RATE and the completed-phaseout ceiling that differ by child count, not the threshold where phase-out begins.

What eligibility mistake gets made most often?

Assuming married filing separately always disqualifies the EITC. It generally does -- but a filer with a qualifying child who lived with them more than half the year, who also lived apart from their spouse for the last six months of the year (or is legally separated), can still claim it as if single. A hand check that stops at "MFS means no EITC" misses this narrow but real exception.

Does this calculator know my complete tax situation?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page computes your total tax liability, verifies the qualifying-child tests, or models a state EITC. Both apply the same published federal phase-in/phase-out formula and eligibility rules to the numbers you enter; verify against IRS Publication 596 or a qualified tax professional before relying on either.
