Missed call text-back for contractors

You are under a sink or on a roof; the phone rings; you cannot pick up. The caller has a problem now. If there is no voicemail and no reply, the next plumber, roofer or electrician in the search results gets the job. A text sent within seconds says you saw the call and keeps the caller waiting for you instead of dialling someone else.

Who this is for

Owner-operators and small crews who answer their own phone and have no receptionist: trades, cleaners, mobile groomers, locksmiths, towing, pest control. Not for large call centres, and not for anyone who would use it to send marketing to people who did not call.

What the text should say

Short, from a person, with your business name, and one clear way to reply. Example (made up): "Hi, this is Acme Plumbing. Sorry we missed your call. How can we help? Reply here and we will get back to you shortly. Reply STOP to opt out." That is one billable segment. Add a booking link only if you have one that works on a phone. Check your own wording.

Rules to check before turning it on

  • Register your number. In the US, carriers expect business texting numbers to be registered (10DLC campaign or toll-free verification); unregistered traffic is filtered. This takes days; start first.
  • Say who you are, and honour STOP. Name the business in the first text; stop texting anyone who replies STOP, and do not text them again until they reply START.
  • Reply, do not market. A text answering a call the person made is a different thing from a promotion. Do not use the same number for blasts without consent.
  • Mind the hour. This agent holds night calls until 08:00 local time instead of texting at midnight.
  • Outside the US (Canada's CASL, UK PECR and GDPR, EU member states) the rules differ. Ask a local lawyer.

These are common requirements written from general knowledge, not legal advice, and the statutes were not re-read for this page.

Run it yourself, or use a hosted service

Hosted services for this exist and charge monthly per location. This one is different in one way only: the code is open and MIT licensed, so you run it on your own Twilio account and pay Twilio's per-text price rather than a subscription. If you do not want to run a server, a hosted service is the better choice, and so is the answering service your phone provider already offers. When you run it yourself you also own the setup: the number, its registration, the server and its upkeep.

Steps are on the main page; the source and the exact rules are in the repository. It has been tested against a fake Twilio only; no real call has gone through it yet, so run it in dry-run mode first and read the log.

Is it worth it? Do the sum

Missed calls a week, times the share who would reply, times the share who become jobs, times your average job. The calculator does it from your own numbers, and shows how many extra jobs a month pay for the cost. If the answer is under one job a month, it is not worth the setup.
