Common questions

Why does a second dog on the same reservation cost less than doubling the price?

Because almost none of the per-dog-day cost doubles with a second dog from the same household. The run, the feeding routine, and most of the potty-break time are already happening — a second dog adds handling effort but not a second kennel, a second cleaning cycle, or a second staff trip. Pricing it as a full second stay is the fastest way to lose a multi-dog family to a competitor who discounts it.

Why does the long-stay discount only apply past a threshold, not to the whole stay?

Because the first several nights of any stay cost the same to deliver as a short booking — there is no efficiency gained yet. The discount exists to win multi-week bookings that would otherwise go to a competitor's weekly rate, and applying it only to the nights beyond the threshold protects margin on the nights every stay has in common, short or long.

Why does the calculator separate a per-stay overhead assumption from the facility's fixed monthly costs?

They answer different questions. The per-reservation price needs a quick per-dog-day overhead estimate to fold into a quote. The occupancy section needs the facility's actual fixed costs (rent, utilities, insurance, software) and kennel/run count to find the occupancy percentage that breaks even — a number that a per-stay estimate alone cannot produce, because break-even depends on how many of your runs are actually filled, not on any one reservation's price.
