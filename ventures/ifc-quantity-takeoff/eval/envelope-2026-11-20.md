# Exterior or partition: an envelope test from the geometry, 17 public models, 2026-11-20

**Question.** The Uniformat II grouping (eval/uniformat-2026-11-20.md) put a
wall in B2010 Exterior Walls or C1010 Partitions by the `IsExternal` flag in
`Pset_WallCommon`. 274 walls in three models carry no flag and went to
"(no Uniformat element)". Walls carry most of a building's money. Can the
geometry say which walls are on the building's outside, and how far can the
authors' flags be trusted where they are set?

**Data.** The 17 models of eval/uniformat-2026-11-20.md (sparse clones of
`opensourceBIM/IFC-files` and `ThatOpen/engine_web-ifc`
`tests/ifcfiles/public/`; not committed). Two of them (HHS MEP, Mini Project)
have no walls.

**Method** (`envelope.mjs`). Each wall is read in plan as an oriented
rectangle: its body's points in world coordinates projected on the ground,
with the direction in which they are thinnest as its thickness. Curtain walls,
upright plates and members (glazing panels and mullions: tall, under 0.3 m
thick in plan, and filling half or more of their upright box, which keeps
sloped rafters out), and doors or windows that fill no wall's opening are
read the same way. From nine points along each face, at the lower of 1.2 m or
half the wall's height, five rays go out (straight and 30 and 60 degrees
either side). A ray that crosses no rectangle at that height has left the
building. A wall is exterior when one face is open at five or more of its nine
points. Walls standing beside a wall and parallel to it within a 0.3 m gap (a
brick skin and its inner leaf, a lining on a wall) are seen through, so an
inner leaf reads with its skin; the row says when a wall reads exterior only
that way.

How the result is used (`uniformat.mjs`):

- no flag: the geometry decides (B2010, A2020 below grade, or C1010), and a
  door in such a wall follows it (B2030 or C1020);
- a flag that is the same on every wall of a model (10 walls or more) while
  the geometry reads a fifth or more of them the other way says nothing, and
  is set aside for the geometry;
- any other flag is kept, and where the geometry disagrees the row's reason
  says so ("the geometry reads it inside the building, check it").

**Run.** `node eval/envelope-check.mjs <17 files> --json eval/envelope-check-2026-11-20.json`
(output `envelope-check-2026-11-20.txt`, 4,858 walls, about 80 s), and
`node eval/uniformat-check.mjs <17 files> --geometry --json eval/uniformat-check-2026-11-20-geometry.json`
(output `uniformat-check-2026-11-20-geometry.txt`). `--svg DIR` on the first
writes a plan per storey (exterior red, lining pink, partition blue, other
blockers grey, a disagreeing flag outlined green); the plans named below were
looked at that way.

The flags are often the wrong witness:

- **Every wall flagged exterior.** HHS architect (112 of 112), advanced_model
  (133 of 133) and HHS construction (48 of 48) flag all their walls exterior.
  The plans of the first two show toilet partitions (`WC Trennwand`),
  corridor walls and the stair cores well inside a curtain-wall facade. These
  two models trip the "same on every wall" rule; construction, a structural
  model whose walls are almost all on the perimeter (47 of 48 read exterior),
  does not.
- **Plasterboard partitions flagged exterior.** kleine_Wohnung flags 134
  `GK 12.5` (gypsum board) walls exterior; the plan shows them as room
  partitions. Its flags are mixed (43 interior), so they are kept and each
  row carries the geometry's doubt.
- **Core walls.** FM_ARC_DigitalHub flags its 67 stair and lift shaft walls
  (`STB 200`, `STB 250`, concrete) exterior; the plan shows the shafts in the
  middle of the floor.
- **Party walls.** duplex flags the 4 party walls between its two units, and 3
  foundation walls under them, exterior. Geometry reads them inside the
  building, which they are; whether a party wall is B2010 or C1010 is the
  estimator's call.
- **Exterior siding flagged interior.** Schultz_Residence flags 13 walls of
  type `Ext. Wall Siding` interior; geometry reads them exterior.

And the geometry is the wrong witness elsewhere:

- **Linings.** 129 walls flagged interior read exterior only through the wall
  they line: Revit's `Interior - Furring (38 mm Stud)` in dental_clinic (49)
  and Office_A (33), the inner `Ziegel` leaf in kleine_Wohnung (18). Whether
  a lining belongs to the exterior wall's element or to the partitions is a
  method-of-measurement call; the row says "inner leaf or lining".
- **Facade not modelled as walls.** S_Office reads 125 interior-flagged walls
  exterior because its facade is not in the model as walls or curtain walls
  (it has 1,711 building element proxies); 42 of them are typed
  `Partition Wall`.
- **Attic knee walls and roof-level walls.** Walls standing under a roof read
  exterior, since the roof is not a blocker (schependomlaan's `knieschot`).
- **ARK_NUS.** 218 walls flagged exterior read inside. Their type names split
  both ways: 67 are interior types (`IVA`, innervegg) and side with the
  geometry, 117 are exterior types (`YV`, yttervegg, among them 45
  `YV 18.1 136 Takhatt` roof-hatch walls and 20 `YV 7.3 410mm` clad walls)
  and side with the flag.

**A third witness: the type's name.** Where flag and geometry disagree
(1,030 walls), the wall type's own name says exterior or interior for 540:
it sides with the geometry on 228, with the flag on 213, and names a lining
the geometry reads with its exterior wall on 99. Neither witness is reliable
on its own, which is why a mixed flag is kept and the disagreement is put on
the row for a person to check rather than overruled.

## What it changed in the Uniformat grouping (17 models)

| | before (no geometry) | after |
|---|---|---|
| elements with no Uniformat element | 5,219 | 4,951 |
| at level 3 | 19,417 | 19,767 |

The 268 unflagged walls with a plan now have a code. In HHS architect and
advanced_model, 190 walls flagged exterior move to C1010 Partitions.

**Limitations.** Nobody who prices buildings has checked these placements;
the plans were judged by eye, by the engineer who wrote the test. A wall's
plan is a rectangle, so a curved wall is read by its bounding strip. The test
reads one height per wall; a wall that is exterior only above a lower roof
(a clerestory) reads by the lower part. Roofs and slabs do not block rays.

**Next.** Roofs as blockers for walls under them (knee walls); the facade
from proxies and windows where a model has no exterior walls (S_Office);
a person who prices buildings choosing whether a lining goes with its
exterior wall; frame members split B1010/B1020 by what they carry.
