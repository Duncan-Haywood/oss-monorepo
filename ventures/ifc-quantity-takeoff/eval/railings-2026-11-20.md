# Railings by what they stand on, 16 public models, 2026-11-20

**Question.** Of the 486 railings in the public models, the rules coded 77
(schependomlaan's, flagged IsExternal, to B2010) and left 409 with no
Uniformat element. Revit writes IsExternal false on every railing, so the
flag says nothing about most of them. Can the geometry say whether a railing
belongs to a stair (C2010 Stair Construction), a balcony or roof edge (B2010
Exterior Walls, where balcony walls and handrails sit) or an inside gallery
or well (C1010 Partitions, which NISTIR 6389 says covers interior balustrades
and screens)?

**Data.** The 17 models of `uniformat-2026-11-20.md` (blob-less sparse clones
of `ThatOpen/engine_web-ifc` `tests/ifcfiles/public/` and
`opensourceBIM/IFC-files`), less HHS Office's MEP model, which has no
railings. Not committed.

**Method.** `node eval/railings-check.mjs <files>` (output:
`railings-2026-11-20.txt`, each model's railings grouped by code, type name,
height and reason) and `node eval/uniformat-check.mjs <files> --geometry`
before and after (after: `uniformat-check-2026-11-20-railings.txt`). The rules,
in order (`uniformat.mjs` `findStairRailings`, `envelope.mjs`
`assignRailingSides`):

1. **Beside a stair:** some stair, flight, ramp, landing slab or stringer (or
   a part of one) has a box within 0.3 m of the railing's in plan, and the
   railing starts between that box's bottom and its top, give or take 0.3 m.
   A flight's handrails start on it; the guard round the well at its head
   starts at its top and is measured with the stair too. C2010, whatever the
   IsExternal flag says (schependomlaan's flagged landing angles,
   "bordes_hoeklijnen", are stair).
2. **Rising a storey:** a railing 3 m or more tall with no stair found beside
   it is a stair railing whose stair was not modelled (S_Office has four of
   4.6 to 5.2 m and no IfcStair). Slatted screens in ARK_NUS rise 2.3 to
   2.8 m and are not caught.
3. **Joined to one of those:** a railing that touches a railing found by 1 or
   2, within 2 m of the same stair in plan, is part of it. Revit exported
   Schultz_Residence's stair railings as 253 separate pieces (balusters, top
   rails); without this, the top rails of its landing guard read as interior
   balustrades while their balusters read as stair.
4. **Flagged IsExternal:** B2010, as before.
5. **Open or closed:** the envelope test's rays (the walls' own test), cast
   from both faces of the railing 0.5 m above its bottom against walls,
   curtain walls and upright facade pieces. One face open at five of nine
   points or more: outside, B2010. Closed on both faces: inside, C1010. Not
   read when fewer than 4 walls reach that height, nor in a model with no
   door or window (HHS Office's structural model, whose walls are not the
   envelope: its two guards read open at 9 of 9 points, while the same
   railings in advanced_model, the architect's version, read closed).

Coded railings went from 77 to 461 of 486; level-3 coverage over the 16
models from 20,187 to 20,571 elements. No author codes a railing, so the
588-code agreement (457 in these 16) is unchanged: 394 / 392 / 351 at
levels 1 / 2 / 3, before and after.

**Read by eye.** The type names agree with the calls where they say anything:
every railing named for a stair ("elevtrapper", pupils' stairs;
"rømningstrapp", escape stair; "900mm Handrail Only" in Office_A; Schultz's
stair hall) is C2010; the ones read outside are glass guards and ARK_NUS's
"Spiler bod" storage-shed slats at the building's edge. HHS construction
and advanced_model, the same building, agree on the four stair railings.

**What it got wrong or cannot see.**

- ARK_NUS's slatted "Spiler bod" screens round outdoor storage are coded
  B2010 as balcony walls. They are exterior screens; a stricter estimator
  would put them under site work (G2040) or fittings. One "Spiler1" in the
  middle of the plan reads open at 9 of 9 points, probably a courtyard.
- A guard on the floor below a flight, within 0.3 m of the flight's bottom,
  is read as the stair's.
- A full-height screen 3 m or taller in a double-height space would read as a
  stair railing by rule 2. None of these models has one.
- A railing whose geometry is not read (25) stays uncoded; most are Revit
  railings built from mapped items the reader does not resolve yet.
- B2010 for a roof-edge guard follows the level-4 list as known (parapets and
  balcony walls under exterior walls); some estimators carry roof guardrails
  with roof specialties under B3010. None of these models has one on a roof.
