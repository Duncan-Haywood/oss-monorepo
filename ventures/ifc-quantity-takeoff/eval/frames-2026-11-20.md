# Frame members into floor or roof construction, 17 public models, 2026-11-20

**Question.** UNIFORMAT II (NISTIR 6389) puts a beam, column or joist in
B1010 Floor Construction when it carries a floor and B1020 Roof Construction
when it carries the roof (columns that carry only the roof included), and
grade beams in A1010 Standard Foundations. The IFC class does not say which.
Until this change every frame member stopped at `B10 Superstructure (not told
further)`, so an elemental estimate could not price floor and roof structure
apart. How many can be told from the geometry the takeoff already reads?

**Rule** (`findCarried` and the frame case in `uniformat.mjs`). For each beam,
column, member or plate whose box was read, take the slabs and roofs whose
code is B1010, B1020 or A1030 and whose box covers the middle of the member's
plan, comes down to within 0.6 m of its top and does not lie wholly below its
top (a beam flush with the slab's top is under it). The one with the lowest
bottom is what it carries:

- a roof (B1020): B1020;
- a slab on grade (A1030): a beam is A1010, anything else B1010;
- a floor: B1010, except a beam on a below-grade storey whose bottom is at or
  under that storey's level, which bears on the ground: A1010 (a grade or
  footing beam under the ground floor).

Nothing found keeps `B10`, and the reason says whether the geometry was read.
Two cases with no geometry needed came with it: curtain panels
(`IfcPlate CURTAIN_PANEL`, 1,685 in four Revit models, most with no
`IfcCurtainWall` parent) now go with the curtain wall to B2010 as mullions
already did, and stair stringers (`IfcMember STRINGER`, 33) to C2010.

**Tried and dropped.** A fallback reading a storey named "Roof", "Dach" or
"dak" as roof construction. On advanced_model it put the 4 struts on "Roof
Level" (10.90 to 12.90 m, standing on the roof) in B1020; their author coded
them B1010. Revit files whatever stands on the roof level under that storey,
so the name says where a member starts, not what it carries. It is not used.

**Data.** The same 17 models as `uniformat-2026-11-20.md` (sparse clones of
`opensourceBIM/IFC-files` and `ThatOpen/engine_web-ifc` `tests/ifcfiles/public`,
not committed). `node eval/uniformat-check.mjs <17 files> --geometry --json
eval/uniformat-check-2026-11-20-frames.json`, output in
`uniformat-check-2026-11-20-frames.txt`; the earlier run is
`uniformat-check-2026-11-20-geometry.txt`.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| beams | 73 | 275 | 71 | 2,068 |

Most of what stays at B10 is in two models: Mini Project (2,191 steel members,
no slabs or roof modelled, so nothing to carry) and ARK_NUS (736 members
of an unnamed type with no deck over them).

**Checks.** No frame member carries its author's Uniformat code in these
models except advanced_model's 4 struts (B1010, author) and Schultz's 25
columns (B10, author). The struts have no deck over them and stay B10, which
matches the author at the level the rule tells. Schultz's columns
all carry the pitched roof ("Basic Roof: Wood Rafter 8in", ROOF slabs at
3.9 to 4.8 m) and read B1020; the author left them at B10, so that is not a
disagreement. By eye on two models:

- S_Office: 59 beams and columns on each of Ground, 1st and 2nd Floor read
  B1010, the 59 on 3rd Floor (under Roof Level) B1020: the pattern a
  four-storey frame has.
- schependomlaan: 73 of the 82 beams on "-1 fundering" read A1010 (they carry
  the ground floor "vloerstort V0" from the foundation level); 8 beams under
  the "sporenkap" (rafter roof) read B1020.

**Known misses.**

- schependomlaan's flat roofs ("plat dak", "dakvloer") are slabs typed
  FLOOR, so the slab is B1010 and so are the 19 beams under them. A slab
  name rule for roofs would fix both; it changes slab codes, so it is left
  for its own check.
- Plan cover is read from the deck's bounding box, so an L-shaped slab
  covers its whole rectangle. A member under the empty corner can be given
  that slab.
- A frame that carries a floor and the roof (a column through a mezzanine to
  the roof) takes the lower deck, B1010, which is how NISTIR 6389 counts it.
