## What the brep is

#207997 is an oblique cone frustum, 0.2 m tall, radius 0.035 m at the
bottom and 0.015 m at the top (centres 0.75 mm apart in x). Four faces:

- two plane caps, each bounded by two exact `IfcCircle` half arcs;
- two side halves, each an `IfcRationalBSplineSurfaceWithKnots`, ruled
  (`RULED_SURF`), cubic round the circumference with 4 spans and 7 control
  points, linear from cap to cap, **every weight 1**.

With all weights 1 the "rational" B-spline is an ordinary polynomial cubic,
and no polynomial follows a circle exactly. The exporter fitted one to each
half circle. The fitted curves stand up to **0.0421 mm inside** the circles
that the edges and caps describe. That is the "2e-4 of the face's size" the
bound check had seen.

## The volume three ways

`eval/off-edges.mjs` reads the volume the B-spline faces enclose without
this tool's mesher. Each side face is ruled between two horizontal curves,
so every horizontal section is the same blend of the two cap curves. Its area
is quadratic in height, and Simpson's rule on three sections is exact. Each
section's area is a shoelace sum over 40,000 points round the two cubics,
with de Boor written out in the script.

| figure | volume (m³) | against the faces |
|---|---:|---:|
| B-spline faces, exact | 0.0004131503 | 0 |
| this tool | 0.0004131534 | +0.0007% |
| circular frustum the edges describe, π h (R² + R r + r²) / 3 | 0.0004136430 | +0.1193% |
| IfcOpenShell 0.9.0, 0.05 mm | 0.0004136362 | +0.1176% |

This tool measures the faces as written, to 7 parts per million.
IfcOpenShell's figure is the circular frustum's, 0.0016% under it, as an
inscribed mesh of a circle would be. The section areas show the same thing:
0.000706016 against the circle's 0.000706858 at the top and 0.00384387
against 0.00384845 at the bottom. Each is 0.12% short.

So neither tool is wrong. They measure two different solids that the file
describes at once. IfcOpenShell meshes these faces with their vertices on the
circular edges and few or none inside, so it reads the circles. This tool
reads the surfaces. For a takeoff the difference is 0.5 cm³ on a 413 cm³
fitting. The useful outcome is that the comparison can now say which is
which on its own.

## What changed

- `Geometry.surfaceTriangles` marks each triangle of a curved face with
  how far the face's surface stands off the face's own boundary points
  (`t.off`, already measured for the 1e-3 bound check).
- `offEdges(parts)` (exported from `geom.mjs`) returns the largest such
  distance and the sum over triangles of distance times area. That sum is the
  most volume a solid fitted to the edges instead of the faces can differ by.
  On #207997 it is 1.33e-6 m³, against the 4.9e-7 m³ gap.
- An advanced brep whose bound is over 0.1% of its volume carries a note in
  the takeoff: "curved faces stand up to 0.042 mm off the edges that bound
  them (the exporter's approximation): volume read from the faces; a figure
  fitted to the edges can differ by up to 0.32%". On the two models this
  applies to 2 of 2,089 breps: #207997, and FM_ARC_DigitalHub #130735 (0.030
  mm, up to 0.72%; it agrees with IfcOpenShell within 0.003%, so there the
  bound is not reached). Volumes are unchanged.
- `eval/breps-vs-ifcopenshell.mjs` has a new reason, `surface-off-edges`.
  It applies when the gap is within that bound and is tested after the
  others. It names the face distance and the bound in the listing.

Every disagreement between this tool and IfcOpenShell on the 2,084 breps read
from the two models now has a named cause.

**Left.** The bound is loose (2.7 times the gap here), because it allows the
whole face to move by the largest distance. A tighter one would integrate the
distance round the boundary only. Nothing needs it yet. A takeoff that wants
the circular figure (the designer's intent) could use the edges' arcs where a
polynomial B-spline approximates a circle. That has not been done, because the
file's faces are the solid it states.
