      Common questions

      How do you calculate IFTA taxable gallons?

      Taxable gallons in a jurisdiction are that jurisdiction’s taxable miles divided by the average MPG of your whole fleet across the whole quarter — not the gallons you bought there. On the worked example this page opens with, the fleet ran 29,320 miles on 3,890 gallons, so its average is 7.54 MPG, and Indiana’s 6,150 miles become 815.65 taxable gallons whether the truck fuelled there or not. Computing a separate MPG per jurisdiction is the most common way a hand-built worksheet produces figures that look reasonable and reconcile to nothing.

      Why does my IFTA return say I owe money when I have a fuel credit?

      Almost always the surcharge. Indiana, Kentucky and Virginia collect part of their diesel tax as a surcharge on the return rather than at the pump, and it is charged on all your taxable gallons in that state with no credit for fuel you bought there. On the worked example the tax line across five jurisdictions nets to a credit of $91.53 — and the return still owes $479.37, because the surcharge adds $570.90 that no amount of fuel buying reduces. A worksheet that treats the surcharge as another tax rate, netted against the credit, reports $242.48 instead: a $328.42 understatement in a single quarter.

      Does buying more fuel in Kentucky reduce my surcharge?

      No, and this is the part that costs carriers money in both directions. Buying fuel in a surcharge state reduces the tax line, because tax-paid gallons are credited against taxable gallons. It does nothing whatever to the surcharge line, which is charged on taxable gallons — the fuel you burned there — regardless of where you bought it. So fuel-buying strategy moves one of the two lines and not the other, and any plan built on the assumption that it moves both will be wrong by the whole surcharge.

      What tax rates should I use?

      The ones on the official IFTA rate table for the specific quarter you are filing, which is published by the International Fuel Tax Association and republished by every base jurisdiction. This calculator deliberately ships no rate table. Rates change every quarter across 58 jurisdictions and some change mid-quarter, so a table baked into a tool would be stale within weeks and stale in the worst possible place — a filed tax return. Enter the rate and, where one applies, the surcharge rate for your fuel type, and the arithmetic is yours to check.

      What counts as taxable miles versus total miles?

      Total miles is every mile the qualified vehicle ran in that jurisdiction. Taxable miles is that figure less any miles the jurisdiction exempts — the exemptions vary by jurisdiction and typically cover things like some toll miles, fuel-trip-permit miles, and certain off-highway use. Most carriers have no exempt miles at all, so leaving the taxable column blank here means all the miles are taxable. Exempt miles come off the taxable line but stay in the fleet MPG, because the fleet really did drive them on the fuel it really did burn. What each jurisdiction exempts is its determination, not this tool’s.

      Do I still owe interest if my return nets to a refund?

      Yes, if you filed late. IFTA interest accrues to each jurisdiction you owe tax to, at one twelfth of the annual rate for each month or part of a month, and a credit due back from another jurisdiction does not offset it. That is why this calculator computes interest on the jurisdictions in debit alone rather than on the netted total, and flags the case where a return in overall credit still carries interest. The annual rate is set for the calendar year and published by the International Fuel Tax Association — enter it rather than reading one from this page, for the same reason the tax rates are inputs.

      Do I need to file IFTA if I only ran in one state?

      IFTA covers qualified vehicles operating in two or more member jurisdictions, so a quarter run entirely inside your base jurisdiction may not need a return — but most base jurisdictions require licensees to file every quarter regardless, including a zero return. This calculator flags a return with a single jurisdiction rather than assuming either way, because what your licence obliges you to file is a question for your base jurisdiction and not one arithmetic can answer.

      Is this tax advice, and can I file from it?

      No to both. It is a worksheet: arithmetic on figures you type, using rates you supply. It is not tax advice and it is not a filing — it files nothing, transmits nothing, and makes no determination about what your base jurisdiction requires, which miles a jurisdiction exempts, whether your vehicle is qualified, or what you owe. Your base jurisdiction assesses the return. Check every figure against your own trip and fuel records and the official rate table, and ask a qualified tax professional before you file from it.
