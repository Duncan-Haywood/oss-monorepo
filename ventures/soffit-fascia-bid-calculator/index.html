    Common questions

    Why is wrapping over existing soffit so much cheaper than a wood replace?

    A wrap just clads the existing fascia and soffit boards in aluminum, while a wood replace tears them off, repairs or replaces the rafter tails underneath, and installs new boards before wrapping — a different job with different labor and materials. Use “Same eave, three scopes” to see the same eave footage priced as wrap, vinyl-replace, and wood-replace so you know how much scope a per-foot special is actually assuming.

    What does the rot-linear-feet input actually change?

    It adds repair hours and lumber cost only for the footage of rafter tail or fascia board you mark as rotten — it doesn't inspect anything or estimate rot for you. You have to look at the eave (or pull a board) to know how many feet are affected before you type a number in.

    Why does going from a 1-story to a 3-story job change the price so much for the same eave length?

    Story count changes access, not the trim itself: a ranch works off an extension ladder, a 2-story typically needs pump jacks or staging, and a 3-story or steep roof needs scaffolding — each step adds real setup hours and, at the top end, equipment rental the ranch job never needed.

    Are the starting hours and material rates specific to my market?

    No, they're common ballpark figures for typical eave wrap and replace work, not a regional material price or labor standard. Coil stock, lumber, and labor rates vary a lot by area — replace the defaults in calc.mjs with your own supplier and crew numbers.
