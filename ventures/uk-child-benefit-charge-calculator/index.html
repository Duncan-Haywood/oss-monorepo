Common questions

    
What is the High Income Child Benefit Charge?

    
A tax charge on the higher earner in a household once their adjusted net income passes £60,000. It claws back 1% of the Child Benefit for every £200 over the line, so the whole benefit is gone at £80,000.

    
How do I avoid the Child Benefit charge?

    
Cut adjusted net income to £60,000 or less. Personal pension contributions and Gift Aid donations are grossed up and deducted from your income, so paying them can remove the charge entirely and move that money into your pension.

    
Should I opt out of Child Benefit payments or pay the charge?

    
If the charge is under 100%, keeping the payments and paying the charge through Self Assessment leaves you with some of the benefit. Opting out of payments avoids the bill but gives up that cash. If the charge is 100% the payments are worth nothing, so many people opt out of payment.

    
Should I still claim if my income is over £80,000?

    
Yes. Making the claim (and opting out of the payments) gives the claimant a National Insurance credit toward their state pension and registers the child's NI number.

    
Which Child Benefit rates does this use?

    
The fields are prefilled with the 2025/26 weekly rates of £26.05 for the eldest child and £17.25 for each other child. Rates are reset each April, so check GOV.UK and edit them for your year.
