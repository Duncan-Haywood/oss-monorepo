Common questions

    
What is the High Income Child Benefit Charge?

    
A tax charge on the higher earner in a household when their adjusted net income is over £60,000 and they or their partner receive Child Benefit. It is 1% of the Child Benefit for every £200 over £60,000, reaching 100% at £80,000.

    
What counts as adjusted net income?

    
Your taxable income less gross pension contributions paid under relief at source and gross Gift Aid donations. Salary sacrifice pension is already out of your taxable income.

    
Can a pension contribution remove the charge?

    
Yes. Contributions or Gift Aid donations that take adjusted net income to £60,000 or less remove the charge for that tax year, subject to your annual allowance and what you can afford to lock away.

    
Should I stop Child Benefit payments instead?

    
You can stop the payments but keep the claim. That avoids the charge and still protects National Insurance credits for a parent not working. You give up the cash while you are over the threshold.

    
Who pays the charge, the claimant or the higher earner?

    
The partner with the higher adjusted net income pays it, even if the other partner is the one who claims.
