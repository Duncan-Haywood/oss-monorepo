Questions employers ask

What is accrued PTO liability?

The cash value of paid time off employees have earned and not used: balance hours times hourly rate. Under accrual accounting many employers record it as a liability, and in some states it is a wage debt that becomes due the day someone leaves. This tool adds the employer payroll tax that a payout would also trigger.

Does my state require paying out unused PTO?

A few states treat earned vacation or PTO as wages: this tool lists California, Colorado, Illinois and Massachusetts. In most other states your written policy decides, and a policy that promises payout creates the obligation. Some states have narrower rules not listed here, so check your state labor department or an employment lawyer before relying on any answer.

Why does a cap matter?

Without a cap, a balance grows for as long as the employee stays, and so does what you owe. A cap stops accrual at a number of hours. Employees at their cap stop earning, which is the usual nudge to take time off. The tool shows who is at the cap and how many months until the rest get there.

What is a sensible target balance?

There is no legal number. Many employers aim for balances under a year or so of accrual. Type your own hours and the tool prices everything above it, which is the cash you would be sitting on if everyone used their time down to that level.
