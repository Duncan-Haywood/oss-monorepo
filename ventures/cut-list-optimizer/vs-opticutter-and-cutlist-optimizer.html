This cut list optimizer vs. optiCutter and CutList Optimizer

All three nest parts onto sheets and respect kerf and grain. The differences worth knowing are where the cutting runs, whether there is a plan tier above you, and whether the offcuts already on your rack count.

What each tool is

optiCutter and CutList Optimizer are hosted cutting-optimization services. You enter stock and parts in a web app, their servers compute the nesting, and you get cutting diagrams back. Both publish a free tier alongside paid plans, and both are established tools with real users — a woodworker or sign shop that wants saved project libraries, material catalogues, an API, or a vendor with a support desk is better served by one of them than by this page, and that is a genuine reason to pay for software.

This calculator is a single static page. The nesting runs in your own browser, in JavaScript, on the page you have already loaded. There is no account, no sign-in, no per-day calculation count, and no tier above you that a big enough job pushes you into — not as a promotion, but because there is no server doing the work and therefore nothing to meter. Nothing you type is uploaded, and once the page has loaded it keeps cutting with the connection off, which matters more in a shop with bad Wi-Fi than it sounds on a spec sheet.

No prices or tier limits are quoted for either service on this page, on purpose. Their plans and limits are theirs to change, the third-party round-ups that publish figures for them disagree with each other, and neither vendor's own site is reachable from the environment this page was written in. Rather than repeat a number we could not check, we link to their pages: read the current terms there.

What this tool actually does

Verifiable from the page itself rather than from a feature grid:

  • Sheet nesting with saw kerf and edge trim taken out of usable area, and a drawn layout per sheet.
  • Grain lock per part, so a face-grain piece is not quietly rotated to make the nesting look better.
  • On-hand offcuts placed before any new sheet is counted as bought, with the sheets avoided reported.
  • Sheet-size comparison — the same cut list costed across 4×8, 5×5 and 4×4 at your prices, because the cheaper sheet is not always the cheaper buy.
  • Board-length comparison for linear cuts, ranking common stock lengths on the same list.
  • A printable job ticket and a copy-to-clipboard plan for both the sheet and linear modes.
  • A draft kept in your own browser, so a reload does not lose the list you typed.

And what it does not do, stated plainly: no accounts, no saved project library beyond that single draft, no material or price catalogue, no edge-banding model, no API, and no support desk. Those are the things a paid service is for.

Common questions

Should I use this cut list optimizer or optiCutter or CutList Optimizer?

All three nest rectangular parts onto sheet stock and account for saw kerf and grain direction, so for a one-off panel layout any of them will give you a workable cutting diagram. The difference is the shape of the product. optiCutter and CutList Optimizer are hosted services: the optimization runs on their servers, and each publishes a free tier alongside paid plans, so a large enough job or a heavy enough week can put you above the free line. This calculator is a static page — the nesting runs in your own browser, there is no account, no plan above you, and no size at which it asks for money. It also does something neither of them is built around: you can type in the offcuts already on your rack, and it will place parts on those before it counts a new sheet as bought. If you want saved project libraries, material catalogues, an API, or a vendor with a support desk, a hosted service is the right shape and this page is not trying to talk you out of one.

Does this cut list optimizer have a free-tier limit?

No. There is no account, no sign-in, no per-day calculation count and no paid tier, because there is no server doing the work — the layout is computed by JavaScript on the page you have already loaded. The practical ceiling is your own device: a cut list of a few hundred parts lays out in well under a second on a phone. Nothing you type is uploaded, and once the page has loaded it keeps working with the connection off.
