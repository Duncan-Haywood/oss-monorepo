How it works, and what it leaves out

  • Screens = colours per location × locations. Each is burned once per job.
  • Pieces printed = pieces ordered ÷ (1 − spoilage), rounded up. Spoiled blanks are bought and printed, so they sit in the cost.
  • Hours = (screens × setup minutes + fixed setup) ÷ 60 + impressions ÷ press speed. Labor and overhead are charged on those hours.
  • Revenue needed = total cost ÷ (1 − target margin). Price each = (revenue − screen fees you charge) ÷ pieces.
  • Not modelled: artwork time, flash and cure, shipping, tax, payment fees, specialty inks. Count an underbase as a colour.

Common questions

How do I price a screen printing job?

Add the blank garments (including spoiled ones you had to buy), the screens, ink, and the press and setup time at your labor and overhead rates. Divide that cost by one minus your target margin to get the revenue the job needs, subtract any screen fees you charge separately, and divide by the number of pieces.

Why does the price per shirt drop as the order gets bigger?

Burning screens and registering the press cost about the same for 24 shirts as for 240, so on a small order that fixed setup is spread over few pieces. The price ladder shows the per-piece price at each quantity so you can set price breaks that still hold your margin.

What counts as a screen?

One screen per colour per print location. A three-colour front and a one-colour sleeve is four screens. Every piece passes under every screen, so press time and ink scale with pieces times screens.

Should I charge a screen fee?

That is your call. If you charge one, the tool takes it out of the per-piece price so your margin target still holds. If you do not, the screen cost is recovered inside the piece price, which makes small orders look expensive.

What does this leave out?

Artwork and separation time, ink waste and reclaim chemicals beyond the per-impression figure you enter, flash and cure time, shipping, sales tax, specialty inks and underbase passes (count an underbase as a colour), and payment fees.
