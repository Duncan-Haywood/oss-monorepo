Form 8949 boxes G to L for crypto: which box is each sale?

On the 2025 forms, sales of digital assets are reported in their own boxes on Form 8949, separate from stock. Which box a row goes in depends on two facts: how long you held the coins, and whether a broker reported the sale to the IRS on Form 1099-DA, with or without your basis. This page lays out the six boxes and shows how the free calculator assigns each row.

Short-term rows go in Part I and long-term rows in Part II; the totals carry to Schedule D. Check the current instructions for the form year you are filing, because box lettering and 1099-DA phase-in dates are set by the IRS and can differ from year to year.

How the calculator assigns a box

Add an optional reported column to your sell rows: basis if the exchange reported the sale with basis, nobasis if it reported the sale without basis, and leave it blank if it did not report it. Each row is then sorted into G, H or I when short-term and J, K or L when long-term, a Box column is added to the Form 8949 style CSV it exports, and totals are shown per box. Why a sale can land in a different box than you expect

  • A sale matched to several lots can be split between a short-term and a long-term row, and the two rows go to different boxes.
  • Changing the matching method (FIFO, HIFO and so on) changes which lots a sale uses, and with it the holding period and the box.
  • Coins moved in from another wallet keep their original purchase date only if the transfer is recorded; otherwise the sale shows up with no basis on record, and the calculator flags it.

Common questions

What is Form 1099-DA?

It is the information return that brokers file for digital-asset sales, starting with transactions in 2025 for gross proceeds and a later year for cost basis, under the IRS schedule. Whether you receive one depends on the platform you used and the year; confirm the current schedule with the IRS instructions.

Do I use box I and L if I got no 1099-DA?

Those are the boxes for digital-asset sales that were not reported to you on a Form 1099-DA, short-term and long-term respectively. The calculator puts a row there when its reported column is blank.

Does the calculator file my return?

No. It produces Form 8949 style rows and totals from your own CSV so you or your preparer can check them against what you file. Nothing is uploaded.
