Weekend tournament schedule maker: every division on shared fields, pools then brackets

A club tournament runs several age groups over a weekend on a handful of fields. U10s play 7v7 on small pitches, U14s need the full-size ones, and everyone wants their pool games before the knockouts. A team should not play twice in an hour or four times in a day, and the coach with two teams cannot be on two fields at once. Directors usually build that grid by hand in a spreadsheet, or pay for a tournament platform.

This page builds it in your browser. It splits each division into pools, plays a round robin in each pool, seeds a knockout bracket from the pools so poolmates meet late, and puts every game on a field at a time that keeps every rule. Then it shows the field-by-time grid for each day, each team's games and rest, and how full each field is. Nothing is uploaded.

The pool tables update, each finished pool fills its knockout places, and the winners move on through the bracket. Times and fields stay as they are.

How it decides

  • Pools: teams are listed best first and dealt into pools by snake draft (A, B, B, A...), so every pool gets a spread of strengths. "Pools of 4" picks the number of pools nearest to that size; "2 pools" sets it.
  • Knockouts: "top 4" takes four teams through. Winners are seeded above runners-up, and inside each place the order keeps poolmates apart for as long as the bracket allows (1st A v 2nd B, 1st B v 2nd A). If the number going through is not a multiple of the pools, the last places go to the best runners-up. A bracket game is never placed before every pool game of its division has finished plus the rest, nor before its feeder games.
  • Placing games: games go in pool-round order, each at the earliest time any suitable field allows: the field takes the division's size, is open and not closed, and has the turnover free on both sides; both teams have had their rest and are under the day's cap; neither team is outside its arrival or departure limit; no team sharing a coach with them is playing. Small-size fields go to the divisions that need them, and versatile fields are kept for later.
  • Many orders, the best kept: about 120 orders are tried, shuffling within rounds. The page keeps the one that places the most games, then finishes earliest, then has the shortest longest wait between a team's games, then the least waiting overall. A division that had games left out moves up the order on the tries that follow. Every result is checked again rule by rule before it is shown.
  • Room for the knockouts: a team that reaches the final plays every knockout round, so the cap counts them in. On some tries a team's pool games on the last day are held back to leave room for its semifinal and final.
  • Before the search: it warns when a group of divisions needs more field-hours than its fields are open, and when no spread of pool games and knockout rounds over the days can keep every team under the cap. A pool game takes two teams' places, so five teams who all need three games on one day cannot exist (15 places is odd).

Measured. On 160 random weekends (2 to 4 divisions of 4 to 12 teams, 4 to 8 fields of mixed sizes, one or two days, shared coaches) that passed both checks above, the page's search placed every game in 93% and 96% of them (two seeds), against 83% and 88% for a single greedy pass. It also cut the longest wait between a team's games from about 320 to 250 minutes on average. In 194 weekends that the checks warned about, no search placed every game. No result broke a rule (eval/tournament-search.mjs, eval/tournament-search.txt). The weekends were random, not real tournaments. A tighter weekend can still have a schedule the search misses, and "Try another order" can find it.

Questions

How do you schedule a tournament with several divisions on shared fields?

List the days and their hours, the fields and the sizes each takes, and each division with its game length, pool size and how many go through to the knockouts. The page splits every division into pools by snake seeding, then places every game on a field at the earliest time where the field is free (with turnover), the teams have had their rest, nobody is over the daily cap, and no coach is due on two fields at once. It tries many orders and keeps the one that fits every game and finishes earliest with the least waiting.

How much rest should teams get between tournament games?

Many youth soccer tournaments use at least an hour between the end of one game and the start of the next, and cap games at two or three a day by age group. Check your association's rules. Here the rest and the cap are set per division, and every game the page places keeps them.

How are teams seeded from pools into the bracket?

Pool winners are seeded above runners-up, and within each place the order is chosen so teams from the same pool meet as late as possible: with two pools the semifinals are 1st A v 2nd B and 1st B v 2nd A. When the number going through is not a multiple of the pools, the extra places go to the best runners-up across pools.

What if the games do not fit?

Before it searches, the page checks field time and the daily cap. It warns when a group of divisions needs more field-hours than its fields are open, or when no spread of pool games and knockout rounds over the days keeps every team under the cap. Any game it still cannot place is listed with the reason: rest, the cap, or field time.

Can a team arrive late or leave early?

Yes. Type a line like "U12 Boys/Valley United: not before Sat 11:00", "not after Sun 13:00" or "not Sun". A team name on its own applies in every division that has a team by that name.

How do shared coaches work?

List teams that share a coach on one line, like "U10 Girls/North Hills Hawks, U12 Boys/North Hills Hawks". Their games never overlap, and the turnover time is kept between them so the coach can walk from one field to the next.

Is anything uploaded?

No. The schedule is worked out in your browser. What you type is kept in this browser only so it is there on your next visit.

How do pool results decide who goes through?

Type the scores under "Results on the day". Each pool's table ranks teams by points (3 for a win, 1 for a draw unless you change it), then the games among teams level on points, then goal difference, then goals for. Many youth tournaments cap how many goals a margin counts (often 3 to 5) so nobody runs up a score: set it and a 9-0 counts as 4-0 for goal difference. When the number going through is not a multiple of the pools, the best runners-up are compared per game, so a team from a pool of three is not penalised for playing one game fewer. Teams still level on everything are flagged so you can settle it by your own rule.
