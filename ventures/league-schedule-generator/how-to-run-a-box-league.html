How to run a box league: boxes, points and who moves

A box league ranks the members of a club, splits them into small boxes, and has everyone in a box play everyone else over a few weeks. Players book their own courts. At the end of the round the top of each box goes up a box and the bottom goes down, and the next round starts. Over a season the boxes sort themselves by level, so most matches are close. Squash clubs made the format famous; badminton, tennis, table tennis and racketball clubs use it too.

1. Split the ranking into even boxes

Pick a target box size (five is the usual for squash) and use the fewest boxes that keep every box at or under it. Then share the players out so box sizes differ by at most one, with the bigger boxes at the top. Never make a box of fewer than three: two players would only meet once and nobody could finish in the middle.

2. Score every match

A common table: 3 points for a win, 1 for playing and losing (so turning up is worth something), a walkover worth 3 to the winner and 0 to the absent player. Some clubs add a point per game won to reward close losses. Rank by points; when exactly two players are level, the match between them decides; with three or more level, use game difference, then games won, then the order the box started in.

3. A worked example: 14 players, boxes of 5, one up and one down

4. Joiners, leavers and no-shows

New members usually start at the foot of the bottom box, or of a higher box if the organiser knows their level. When people leave, re-split the ranked list so the boxes stay even, which pulls a few players up a box. A player who plays none of their matches normally goes down regardless of the table, so one absent member cannot hold a box still.

Questions

How many players go in a box?

Four to six is usual; five is the most common in squash. With five, each player has four matches a round, which fits a four-week round at one match a week. When the club does not divide evenly, keep the boxes within one player of each other with the bigger boxes at the top, and never below three.

How many players go up and down?

One up and one down per box is the usual rule for boxes of four to six. Two up and two down moves players faster, which suits a new league whose first ranking is a guess. The top box has no one above it and the bottom box no one below.

What if two players finish level on points?

Most clubs use the match between the two of them. With three or more level, head-to-head usually goes round in a circle, so use game difference, then games won, and if still level the order the box started in.

What happens to a player who plays no matches?

Most box leagues move a player down if they play none of their matches in a round, whatever the table says, so a missing player does not block a box. Walkovers give the winner the full win points and the absent player nothing.
