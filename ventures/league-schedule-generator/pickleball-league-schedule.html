Pickleball league schedule: courts, start times and fair slots

A pickleball club league has two jobs: decide who plays whom, and decide which court and what time. A round robin does the first. The second is where hand-made schedules go wrong, with a court booked twice or one team stuck with the late slot all season. Eight teams is a common size: 7 weeks, 4 matches a week.

Questions

How do you make a pickleball league schedule?

List the teams, pick the night, and use a round robin so every team plays every other team once. Eight teams play 7 rounds of 4 matches. Then give each match a court and a start time so no court is booked twice, and share the early and late slots out evenly.

How many pickleball courts does a league night need?

Half the number of teams, rounded down, matches per round, so eight teams need four court-and-time slots a night: two courts at two start times, or four courts at one. Count courts times start times, not courts alone.

Is a pickleball league round robin the same as a rotating-partner round robin?

No. In a team league each team keeps its players and plays other teams. In a rotating-partner night (Americano or Mexicano) individual players change partners every round. This page covers team leagues; the Americano and Mexicano guides cover rotating partners.

How do you keep the 18:00 and 19:00 slots fair?

Count how many early slots each team gets across the season and keep the counts within one of each other. In the eight-team example every team plays the 18:00 slot three or four times out of seven.

What if a night is rained out or a court is closed?

Add the date as a skip date and the season moves past it, list the court as closed on that date, and move a rained-out round with the rainout box; the tool keeps every court and time free of clashes.
