How to run a ladder league: challenges and moving up

A ladder is a ranking that plays itself. Everyone starts on a rung. You challenge someone a few rungs above you, and if you win you take their place. Clubs use it for tennis, pickleball, squash, table tennis and darts because nobody needs a fixed fixture list: players arrange their own matches and the ranking is always current.

Questions

How does a ladder league work?

Players are ranked on rungs. You challenge someone a few rungs above you, play one match, and if you win you take their rung while they and everyone between drop one place. If the higher-ranked player wins, nothing changes. There is no fixed schedule, so it suits clubs where players arrange their own matches.

How many rungs up can you challenge?

Most clubs allow two or three rungs. The tool defaults to three, shows who each player can challenge now, and flags any result beyond the limit so the organiser can overrule it. You can also set it higher.

What is the difference between insert and swap?

Insert, the usual rule, puts the winner on the loser's rung and moves everyone in between down one. Swap only trades the two players' places. Swap is gentler on the players between but lets a player leapfrog slowly.

Is a ladder the same as a round robin?

No. A round robin schedules every pair to meet once. A ladder has no fixed fixtures: the ranking is whatever the results so far say. Use a round robin when you want a fair season, and a ladder when you want an always-open club ranking.
