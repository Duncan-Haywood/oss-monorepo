How to collect player waivers for a rec league

Most rec leagues ask every player to agree to a waiver or code of conduct before they play. The usual ways are a paper sheet at the first game, a form tool whose answers you match to rosters by hand, a dedicated waiver app on a monthly plan, or a league platform that bundles waivers with registration. The hard part is rarely the signing. It is knowing, the night before week one, who on each team still has not signed.

This guide describes the league page in this tool's source code (server.mjs). It is no longer offered for you to run on your own server, and no hosted version is live yet, so read the steps below as how the feature works rather than something you can switch on today.

How it works

  1. Paste your text. In the organiser page, under Waiver, paste the release or code of conduct your league uses. The page does not write one for you.
  2. Players sign from the league link. The league page links to the waiver. A player picks their team, types their full name, ticks that they have read and agree, and types their name again as the signature. For a minor, the parent or guardian signs with their own name.
  3. Captains sign when they enter the team. With a waiver set, the team sign-up form asks the captain to sign before the team is sent for approval.
  4. See who is missing. The organiser page matches signatures to each roster and lists who has not signed. Captains see the same list for their own team, so they chase their players instead of you.
  5. Keep the record. Download every signature as a CSV with the time, team, name, guardian, email and the version of the text agreed to. Any version's exact text can be downloaded as it was signed.

What it does not do

It does not draft legal text, check identity, collect a drawn signature or decide whether a waiver is enforceable. It keeps no IP addresses. If you need ID checks, drawn signatures or a vendor that answers for the record, a paid waiver service is the right tool.

Questions

Can I collect waivers without a paid waiver app?

Not with this tool today: its league page (server.mjs) does this, but it is no longer offered to run yourself and no hosted version is live yet. In that page: Paste your own waiver text in the organiser page and players sign from the league link with their name, a tick and a typed signature. A minor's parent or guardian signs with their own name. You download every signature as a CSV.

What happens to signatures when I change the waiver text?

Each distinct text is a version, named by the start of its SHA-256 hash and kept with the date it was first set. A signature points at the version it agreed to. After an edit, earlier signers show as signed an earlier version, so you can ask them to sign the new text.

How do I see who has not signed?

The organiser page compares signatures with each team's roster and lists, per team, who has signed the current text, who signed an older one and who has not signed. A captain's page shows the same list for their own team.

Does this write the waiver for me or make it enforceable?

No. You supply the words, ideally from your league's insurer, governing body or a lawyer. The page records who agreed to which words and when. Whether a release holds, and the age at which a player can sign for themselves, depend on where you play.
