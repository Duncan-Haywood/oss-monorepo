Bowling league schedule maker: every team rotated evenly across the lane pairs

A bowling league bowls the same night every week on the same lanes, and the round robin repeats until the season ends: a 30-week season of 10 teams plays every opponent three times and a third. The part a hand-made sheet gets wrong is the lanes. Bowlers notice when one team keeps getting the pair by the wall or the pair with the dead approach, so each team should bowl on each lane pair about as often as every other pair, and not on the same pair two weeks running.

This page builds that schedule in your browser: the round robin, real dates (skipping holidays), a lane pair for every match chosen so the counts come out even, weeks held for position rounds, and a vacancy for an odd league. Nothing is uploaded.

How the lanes are chosen, and how even it gets

Who bowls whom each week is the circle-method round robin. Each week's matches are then put on pairs as an assignment problem: the price of a pair for a match is how much it would push its two teams' counts away from even, plus a little for bowling the same pair as last week. The Hungarian method solves each week exactly given every other week, the page sweeps the season until no week improves, and then re-deals a few weeks at random and sweeps again, keeping the most even season it finds.

We measured it on 36 seasons (6 to 24 teams, 26 to 34 weeks). Every team got the most even split possible in all 36: no team bowls on any pair more than one week more than on any other, and where the weeks divide evenly every team gets every pair exactly the same number of times. A hand rotation (move every match one pair along each week) got every team even in 1 of the 36 and left some team 13 weeks off. For the hardest season lengths the page carries assignments a SAT solver found ahead of time, checked again in your browser before they are used. Some lengths cannot be made perfectly even at all (for example a season exactly as many weeks long as there are lane pairs); the solver proves it, and the page then gets as close as it can. Once the split is even, a last pass cuts the weeks a team bowls the same pair two weeks running without making any count less even: over the same 36 seasons those repeats fell from 392 to 148, and 13 seasons have none (a 12-team league over 30 weeks bowls every pair exactly five times and never the same pair twice in a row). The numbers, and the scripts that reproduce them, are in the project's eval/bowling-lanes.txt and eval/bowling-lane-table.txt.

When a schedule shows a team two off, "Try another lane deal" runs the search from a different start; the round robin itself stays the same.

Some seasons cannot be made even at all, and the page says so when it meets one. A SAT solver checked every season the search missed for leagues of 4 to 32 teams and 1 to 60 round-robin weeks, and proved 44 of them out of reach: no lane deal gives every team its even split. The most common is a season exactly as long as the lane pairs (8 teams on 4 pairs for 4 weeks, 16 teams for 8): each team would need every pair once, and the round robin's first weeks never allow it at any size from 4 to 30 teams. A 4-team league is out of reach whenever its round-robin weeks are 2, 3 or 4 more than a multiple of 6. The list is in the project's eval/bowling-bound-proof.txt.

A team that must never bowl on a pair

Some leagues have a team that cannot use one pair: the end pair by the wall where a bowler with a walker has no room on the approach, the pair nearest the bar, or the pair with the ball return that jams. Type it under "Lane limits", one team per line (Pin Pals: 7-8, Spare Me: last, Gutter Gang: ends for both end pairs). The page then never puts that team on that pair, in any week; it is a hard rule inside the search, not a fix-up afterwards. The team rotates evenly over the pairs it may use, and the other teams take the avoided pair a little more often, as evenly as the weeks allow. If the usual order of the round robin leaves a week with no deal, the page deals the teams to other places in the rotation (everyone still meets everyone as often) until every week has one: ten teams with five of them off the end pair now get a season. If no order works (in a 4-team league three teams avoiding the same pair, say), the page names the week instead of building a schedule that breaks a limit.

We measured it on 144 seasons (6 to 24 teams, 26 to 34 weeks, four kinds of limit) against the fix an organiser makes by hand: build the even schedule, then each week the team lands on its pair, swap its match with the nearest one. The search alone put every team inside its fair share in 133 of the 142 seasons where the limits can hold every week, and never broke a limit. The hand fix got every team inside its share in none of them, left 3,345 counts off it in total (the search: 24), and in 3 weeks found no swap that kept the limit at all. Of the search's 9 misses, a SAT solver proved 5 out of reach (6 teams on 3 pairs with two teams off the end pair, for example) and found that 3 others, all 20 teams over 30 weeks, have a deal the search did not find. For those and the other hard season lengths the page now carries solver answers for the common kinds of limit (one team off a pair, two teams off the same pair, one team off both ends, and a few more), worked out once and fitted to your teams by renaming: the limited teams take the round-robin slots the answer was built for, so who plays whom is still a plain round robin. With them the page puts every team inside its share in 137 of the 142 seasons, and a solver proves the other 5 cannot be done. The numbers are in the project's eval/bowling-lane-avoid.txt and eval/bowling-avoid-shapes.txt. Position rounds put 1st v 2nd on the first pair, and their teams are known only on the night, so check a limited team's pair on those weeks.

Questions

How do you make a bowling league schedule?

List the teams, pick the first league night and the number of weeks, and say where your lanes start. The page plays a round robin, repeats it until the weeks run out, and puts every match on a lane pair so each team bowls on each pair as evenly as the season allows. Download it as a CSV or copy the text for the league sheet.

How many lane pairs does a bowling league need?

One pair for every two teams: an 8-team league bowls on 4 pairs (8 lanes) every week, and a 12-team league on 6 pairs. An odd league adds a vacancy, so 9 teams still use 5 pairs and one team bowls against the vacancy each week.

How do you rotate teams across lanes in a bowling league?

Count, for every team, how many weeks it has bowled on each pair, and give each week's matches the pairs that even those counts out. A team that bowls 30 weeks on 5 pairs can have each pair exactly 6 times; the page shows each team's counts and says when a team is one or two off that.

What is a position round in bowling?

A week where the pairings come from the standings that night: 1st bowls 2nd, 3rd bowls 4th, and so on. Type the week numbers and the page holds those weeks for position rounds, shows which places bowl on which pair, and carries on the round robin after them.

What happens with an odd number of teams?

The league bowls against a vacancy (also called a blind). The team drawn against it still bowls on its pair that week, and the vacancy moves round the schedule like any other team, so every team meets it equally often.

Is my team list uploaded?

No. The schedule is built in your browser and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
