Rec basketball league schedule: courts, tip-off times and rounds

A rec or church basketball league has the same two jobs every season: decide who plays whom, and decide which court and what tip-off time. A round robin does the first. The second is where hand-made schedules go wrong, because a gym has a few courts and a few evening slots and ten teams want fair ones. Ten teams on a Wednesday night is a common size: 9 weeks, 5 games a week, every team playing every week.

Questions

How do you make a rec basketball league schedule?

List the teams, pick the weeknight, and use a round robin so every team plays every other team once. Ten teams play 9 rounds of 5 games, so all ten teams play every week. Then give each game a court and a tip-off time so no court is booked twice.

How many courts does a ten-team basketball league need?

Five games a week need five court-and-time slots. Two courts at three times give six slots, which covers it with one spare; count courts times time slots, not courts alone.

How do you keep the 6pm games fair?

Count how many 18:00 games each team gets across the season and keep the counts within one of each other. In the example every team plays exactly three of its nine games at 18:00.

Should a rec basketball league play a double round robin?

Only if you have the weeks: ten teams need 18 rounds for home and away. Most rec leagues play one round robin and use the standings to seed a short play-off.

What if the gym is closed one Wednesday?

Add the date as a skip date, list the court as closed on that date, or move the round with the rainout box; the tool keeps every court and time free of clashes.
