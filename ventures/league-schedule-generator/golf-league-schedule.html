Golf league schedule maker: tee times rotated evenly, match play scored with handicaps

A twilight golf league plays nine holes the same evening every week. Two-person teams play each other in a foursome, the round robin repeats until the season ends, and the league alternates the front and back nine. A hand-made sheet usually gets the tee times wrong. Teams notice when one of them always goes off first, or always last and finishing in the dark. Each team should have each tee time about as often as the others, and not the same one two weeks running.

This page builds that schedule in your browser: the round robin, real dates (skipping holidays), the nine for each week, and a tee time (or a starting hole for a shotgun start) for every group, chosen so the counts come out even. Then, each week, type the hole-by-hole cards and it does the secretary's sums: handicap strokes on the right holes, match play points, league handicaps and the standings. Nothing is uploaded.

How the tee times are chosen, and how even it gets

  
Who plays whom each week is the circle-method round robin. Each week's groups are then put on tee times as an assignment problem: the price of a tee time for a group is how far it would push its two teams' counts from even, plus a little for having the same tee time as the week before. The Hungarian method solves each week exactly given every other week, and the page sweeps the season until no week improves. Then it re-deals a few weeks at random and sweeps again. Last, a tabu search swaps groups within a week to bring the teams still off their share back in.

  
We measured it on 77 seasons (4 to 20 teams, 10 to 22 weeks). Every team got its fair share of every tee time in 71 of them: no team has any tee time more than one week more than any other, and where the weeks divide evenly every team has every tee time exactly as often. For 6 of those 71 the page uses tee-time deals a SAT solver found ahead of time, checked again in your browser before they are used. A hand rotation (every group one tee time later each week) got every team its share in 4 of the 77. A SAT solver proved the other 6 seasons cannot be made even at all with this round robin: a 4-team league over 10, 14, 16, 20 or 22 weeks, and 20 teams over 10 weeks. The page says so when you build one of them. The numbers, and the scripts that reproduce them, are in the project's eval/golf-tee-grid.txt and eval/golf-tee-sat.txt.

  
"Try another tee-time deal" runs the search from a different start; the round robin itself stays the same. With a shotgun start the slots are starting holes (hole 1 to 9 on the front, 10 to 18 on the back, then 1B, 2B for a second group on a hole), balanced the same way.


  
Match play with handicaps: the weekly sums

  
Under the schedule, "League nights" does what a league secretary does with the cards each week:

  
    
    
  • Strokes: in each group, low handicap plays low handicap (A v A, B v B). The higher handicap gets the difference in strokes, one on each of the nine's hardest holes by the card's stroke index. On a front nine indexed 7, 3, 15, 1, 11, 5, 17, 9, 13, a 3-stroke difference falls on holes 4, 2 and 6. Past 9 strokes, the hardest holes get a second one.
    • 
    
  • Points: each hole is worth 1 point to the lower net score, split on a halved hole. Each pairing's lower net total earns 1 more, and the team with the lower combined net earns 2. That is 22 points a match by default; set each to your league's own.
    • 
    
  • League handicap: each round's adjusted score over par for the nine, with each hole capped at net double bogey (par plus 2 plus the strokes the player gets there). By default it is 90% of the average of the last 5 rounds, rounded, up to 18. Set it to the best 3 of the last 5, say, and it uses those. A new player uses the entering handicap you type. With none, their first card sets it for that night.
    • 
    
  • Players on other tees: add the forward (or women's, or senior) tees to the course card as "Par (forward):" and "Index (forward):" lines; a tee that differs only in its stroke index needs only that line. Mark a player "Ben (forward)" in the roster. Their handicap is worked over their own tees' par. In a pairing across tees, the player whose tees have the higher par for the nine gets the difference as extra strokes, and strokes fall on the receiving player's own stroke index. Team net is each player's net over their own par, put on the main tees' par.
    • 
    
  • Two courses, or a 27-hole course: type the rotation under the schedule ("Oak front, Pine front, Oak back, Pine back", or "Red, White, Blue" for three 9-hole nines) and one card per course, each under a "Course: Oak" line; a 9-hole nine needs only 9 pars and 9 index numbers (odd or even ones, as a 27-hole card prints them). Each night is scored on its own course's card, and play-off nights carry on the rotation. Over par, a nine at a hard course counts like a nine at an easy one. Add "Rating:" and "Slope:" for every course and set of tees (per nine, or the 18-hole numbers) and the page works handicaps the World Handicap System way: each round's differential is (adjusted score minus rating) times 113 over slope, the league index is the average of the rounds counted, and tonight's strokes are your handicap percentage of index times slope over 113, plus rating minus par. With an index of 10 at 90%, that is 8 strokes on a nine rated 35.0 / 113 with par 36 and 12 on one rated 37.5 / 135. Entering handicaps are then read as a league index.
    • 
    
  • Skins: an optional side game. Each hole is a skin, won by the one player with the lowest score on it (gross, or net with a share of their handicap); a tie for low wins nothing, or with carry-over the skin rides on to the next hole. Players on different tees are compared by score against their own par on the hole. With an entry, the pot (entry times players) is shared by the skins won; a night with none won returns the entries.
    • 
    
  • Absent players and the bye: an absent player's opponent wins every hole of that pairing and its net point. For the team net, the absent player counts as par plus 3. The bye team plays the course: each player against net par with their full handicap, so they still earn points.
    • 
  

  
A worked night: Ana (5) plays Cy (2), so Ana gets 3 strokes. Ana shoots 41 and Cy 39. On net they win 4 holes each and halve one, 4.5 points each, and Ana's net 36 beats Cy's 37 for the pairing's net point: 5.5 to 4.5. Ben (12) and Dee (14) each win 2 holes and halve 5, and both net 35: 5 points each. The Hawks' team net of 71 beats the Owls' 72 for 2 more. Hawks 12.5, Owls 9.5. A test recomputes this exact night, so the page cannot drift from it.

Questions

  
How do you make a golf league schedule?

  
List the teams, pick the first league night, the number of weeks and the first tee time. The page plays a round robin, repeats it until the weeks run out, alternates the front and back nine, and gives each group a tee time so every team goes off early and late about equally often. Download it as a CSV or copy the text for the clubhouse board.

  
How are handicap strokes given in league match play?

  
The higher-handicap player gets the difference between the two handicaps in strokes, one on each of the nine's hardest holes by the stroke index on the card. A 12 playing a 9 gets 3 strokes, on the three lowest-index holes of the nine being played. Past 9 strokes, the hardest holes get a second one.

  
How is a golf league handicap worked out here?

  
From each round's adjusted score over par for the nine, with each hole capped at net double bogey. By default the handicap is 90% of the average of the last 5 rounds, rounded, up to 18 for nine holes. You can use the best 3 of the last 5, change the percentage or the cap. It is a league handicap only, not a Handicap Index.

  
How are points scored in a two-person golf league?

  
A common format, and the page's default: low handicap plays low handicap and high plays high. Each hole is worth 1 point to the lower net score (a halved hole splits it), each pairing's lower net total earns 1 more, and the team with the lower combined net earns 2. That is 22 points a match. Every number can be changed.

  
How do mixed tees work in a golf league?

  
Type the forward tees' par and stroke index on the course card and mark the players who play them. Each player's handicap is worked over their own tees' par. When two players on different tees meet, the one whose tees have the higher par for the nine gets the difference as extra strokes, on their own stroke index.

  
How are skins scored?

  
Every hole is a skin, won by the one player with the lowest score on it, gross or net. A tie for low wins nothing, or with carry-over the skin adds to the next hole. With an entry, the pot is shared by the skins won that night.

  
Can a golf league play two courses?

  
Yes. Type the rotation under the schedule, like "Oak front, Pine front, Oak back, Pine back" for two clubs taking turns, or "Red, White, Blue" for the three nines of a 27-hole course. The whole league plays the same course each night, and each night is scored on that course's own card. Add each course's rating and slope and handicaps are worked the World Handicap System way, so the same player gets more strokes at the harder course.

  
What happens with an odd number of teams?

  
One team has the bye each week, but it still plays: it keeps a tee time and its players play the course, each against net par with their full handicap. So nobody sits out a night and every team plays the same number of matches.

  
Is anything uploaded?

  
No. The schedule and the scoring run in your browser. Cards you type are kept in this browser only, so they are there on your next visit.
