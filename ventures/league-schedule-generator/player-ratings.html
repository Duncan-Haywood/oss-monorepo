Club player ratings

For pickleball, padel, tennis, squash, badminton, table tennis, darts and chess clubs. Paste a season of results, singles or doubles, and every player gets a rating that moves with each result, with a plus-or-minus that says how sure it is. Partners and score margins count. Use it to seed boxes, draw balanced teams or split divisions, and to see who is evenly matched before a league night.

How it works

How are doubles results rated?

Each player is rated as if they had played one opponent whose rating makes the gap equal to the gap between the two pairs' average ratings. Winning beside a much stronger partner moves your rating less than winning beside an equal one, and losing beside a weaker partner costs less.

What does the plus-or-minus number mean?

It is twice the rating deviation (RD): the true level is likely within that range. New players start at 1500 plus or minus 700. It narrows as they play and widens again for each rating period they sit out, so a returning player's rating moves faster.

Should scores count or only wins?

The score margin setting blends the win with the share of points won, so 11-9 counts as a narrower win than 11-2. In simulated games to 11 played rally by rally, counting points ordered players better than wins alone. On 50,000 real international football results, goal margin did not improve predictions, because goals are few. The page starts at half and half.

Whole season or Glicko-2?

For one season, "whole season at once" fits every result together, so the order games were played in does not matter and early results count as much as late ones. Glicko-2 updates one rating period at a time: use it when levels change over months, or to carry ratings from one season into the next (paste the final ratings as starting ratings).

Where ratings help most is one table across boxes or divisions. A box league's own order ranks every player in box 1 above every player in box 2. In 200 simulated 16-player box seasons, the whole-season fit (at this page's settings) matched the true order better than the box ladder in 166 seasons of 200 after eight rounds and 181 after twelve (Spearman 0.85 against 0.77, and 0.89 against 0.79). After only two rounds the ladder, which starts from the club's own seeding, was better in most seasons. When everyone plays everyone with random partners, a plain points-difference table did as well, so this page will not order an Americano night better than its own table does. What it adds there is the ± and the win chances.

What is it built on?

Glicko-2, Mark Glickman's published rating system (the one many chess sites use), checked against the worked example in his paper, and a Bradley-Terry fit with a normal prior for the whole-season method (the same 400-point scale). Players marked provisional need more games before their rating means much (RD above 110 under Glicko-2, above 200 in the whole-season fit, which counts a doubles game as a quarter of a singles game for each player).
