How it works

How is an Americano scored?

Every player is credited with the points their side scored in each game, so a strong partner never costs you anything. The table ranks by total points, then points difference, then wins. In padel the usual format is games to a total such as 24 points, where the two scores add up to 24; type one side and the page fills in the other. A score that does not add up (a game stopped by the clock) is kept and flagged.

What happens to players who sit out a round?

When the number of players is not a multiple of four, some sit out each round, and the rotation shares that out evenly. Pick how a sit-out counts: nothing, the player's own average points per game so far (the default), or half the points of a game when games go to a total. The credit is added once that round is fully scored and is shown separately in the table.

What is the difference between the Americano and Mexicano modes?

Americano fixes every round at the start so each player partners and plays against as many different people as possible; with a court for every four players and 4k or 4k+1 players, everyone partners everyone exactly once. Mexicano draws round one at random and each later round from the standings, so the top four play together on court 1, the next four on court 2, and so on.

More on each: how the Americano rotation works (whist designs) and how the Mexicano draw works.

Do the players need to install anything?

No. The organiser keeps score on one phone in the browser. The page saves the night on that phone, so a refresh or a closed tab loses nothing, and a Share link gives everyone a read-only copy of the rounds and the table as it stood when the link was made.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Names and scores stay on the device that typed them. A share link carries the night inside the link itself, so whoever you send it to can read it; nothing is stored on a server.
