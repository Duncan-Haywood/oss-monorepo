How to make a practice schedule for teams sharing fields

Game schedules get the attention, but in a youth league the practice grid is the harder puzzle. A handful of fields, permitted for a few evenings a week, are split into halves and quarters and shared by every team in the club. Some coaches coach two teams, the youngest players need to be home early, and every coach has asked for Tuesday. This page walks through a method that works by hand for a small club and is what the free practice schedule maker does for a big one.

1. Count each field in units

Decide how many teams a field holds at once. A full-size soccer field split into quarters holds four small-sided teams; halves hold two; a gym, a diamond or a single court holds one. Then give each age group a size in the same units:

TeamUsual shareUnits (field in quarters)
U6, U8a quarter1
U10, U12a half2
U14 and upthe whole field4

A practice block then holds any mix that adds up to the field's units: four U8 teams, or two U10s, or a U12 and two U8s.

2. Cut the hours into blocks

Take each window you have (Riverside, Tuesday and Thursday, 5 to 7 pm) and cut it into practice-length blocks: with one-hour practices, 5-6 and 6-7. Each block is a box to fill. Keeping practices on a fixed grid stops the half-hour fragments that nobody can use.

3. Write down the hard rules, then the wishes

Hard rules are the ones a timetable must keep: a field's units, one practice a day per team, no practice on game day, a coach with two teams never in two places, and the youngest finishing by their cut-off. Wishes are what coaches asked for: certain days, certain times, and two practices not on back-to-back days. Count one miss for each wish a practice breaks.

4. A worked example: seven teams, two fields

Riverside is permitted Tuesday and Thursday 5-7 pm and is split into three spaces; Elm School's field is two halves, Monday and Wednesday 5:30-7:30 pm. The teams, in the line format the tool reads:

Six of the seven teams get everything they asked for. U10 Storm gets Monday at Elm School for one of its two practices instead of a second Riverside night. That miss cannot be avoided. Both U6 teams must finish by 6, so they can only use Riverside's 5-6 blocks, and their shared coach puts them on different days. U8 Comets and Rockets want Tuesday and must finish by 6:30. Fit those in and Riverside has three spaces left that a U10 can use (two units each in a three-unit block): Thursday 5-6, Tuesday 6-7 and Thursday 6-7. The two U10 teams want four Riverside practices. One of them goes elsewhere, or a U8 gives up its Tuesday, and either way exactly one team misses one wish. An exact solver agrees: no timetable for these lists does better.

5. Share the misses, not just the slots

When some team has to miss, prefer the timetable where misses are spread out. Score each team by its misses squared and add them up: two teams missing once scores 2, one team missing twice scores 4. The tool searches for the lowest total and tells you, team by team, what each one missed and whether it could have done better with the fields to itself. When no team could, it says the timetable is proved best.

Questions

How do you split practice fields between teams?

Count each field in units: a field split into quarters is 4 units, into halves 2. Give each age group a size (U6 and U8 a quarter, U10 and U12 a half, U14 the whole field), cut the hours you have into practice-length blocks, and fill each block up to its units. Check that no coach is in two places and that young teams finish early enough.

How do you make practice slots fair?

Write down what each team asked for (days, times), count a miss each time a practice breaks one, and prefer the timetable where misses are spread out: two teams missing once is fairer than one team missing twice. First-come first-served hands the good slots to whoever emails first.

How many practices a week should youth teams have?

Most recreational leagues give the youngest teams one practice a week and older teams two, on days apart. Check your league's own rules and your field permits.

How do two teams share one practice slot every other week?

Mark both teams every other week. Each block then has two capacities, one for A weeks and one for B weeks, and each alternating team takes all its practices in the same set of weeks, so the two teams take turns on the same slot. A team that practises weekly fills the slot in both weeks. A coach with one team on A weeks and one on B weeks can have both at the same time, because they never meet. Number the weeks from the first week of the season so every coach knows which week is theirs. In the practice schedule maker, add every other week to a team's line, or A weeks or B weeks to fix which; the grid, CSV and calendar files say which weeks each team has, and its calendar repeats every two weeks.
