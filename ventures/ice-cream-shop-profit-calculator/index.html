What it does, and what it leaves to you

    
    
      
  • Sales: scoops = scoops per day (weekday and weekend figures) x open days x the hot or off season share x the ramp share. Sales = scoops x price + the shake uplift on the share of scoops that go into shakes or sundaes + toppings sold x topping price + pints x pint price. Average ticket = scoops per ticket x sales per scoop. Pints follow the same seasons and ramp.
    • 
      
  • Seasons: the hot season runs for the months you give, wrapping the year end; every other month uses the off-season share. An off-season share of 0 closes the shop: no staff are paid in a closed month, but rent, utilities, insurance and other fixed costs still run.
    • 
      
  • Costs: card fees are a share of sales. Cost of goods = scoops x (mix, dairy and flavoring + cone, cup and spoon) + the extra cost of shakes and toppings + pints x pint cost. Labor = staff hours per open hour x open hours x open days x wage x (1 + payroll share). Rent, utilities, insurance, other fixed costs and owner pay are monthly. Prime cost = cost of goods + labor. Operating profit = sales less all of these.
    • 
      
  • Break-even: what one scoop earns after card fees and its own cost, divided into the fixed costs and labor (less what the pint line earns), and the same with the loan payments added. Shown per open day of the year. Labor and pints are held at the planned level.
    • 
      
  • Return: start-up cost = cabinets x cost each + other equipment + square feet x build-out + the other lines. Yield on cost = full-year operating profit / start-up cost. Loan = share of start-up cost, level payments. Month by month from opening, sales ramp in a straight line, and cumulative cash starts at minus your equity. Payback is the first month it reaches zero. If you enter a sale multiple, the shop sells at that many times the last twelve months of operating profit, less the loan balance; with it blank, no sale is assumed.
    • 
      
  • Not decided: how many scoops your location will sell or how fast; zoning, health-department food, dairy and frozen-dessert licensing, hood and equipment rules, sales-tax rules; income tax and depreciation; build-out overruns and rent paid before opening; spoilage, a freezer failure, weather and staff turnover; and what a lender will actually offer. The sample figures are illustrative, not market prices or a forecast.

Common questions

    
How much does an ice cream shop make?

    
It depends on your scoop price, how many scoops you sell a day, your rent, your crew and what the mix and cones cost you. Enter your own figures; the page works out sales, cost of goods, labor, operating profit, yield on what you spend to open, the loan coverage and how long it takes to get your equity back.

    
How many scoops a day do I need to break even?

    
Two figures are shown: the scoops per open day that cover running costs, and the higher number that also covers the loan payment. They come from what each scoop earns after card fees and its own mix, cone, shake and topping cost, set against your fixed costs and the labor of your open hours.

    
What are cost of goods, labor and prime cost?

    
Cost of goods is the mix, cones, cups, shake and topping cost of what you sold, as a share of sales. Labor is wages plus payroll taxes. Prime cost is the two added together. The page shows all three for a full year so you can see what is left for rent and profit.

    
How does a hot season and an off season change the result?

    
You set the months of your hot season, how much busier they are and how much slower the other months are. A seasonal stand can set the off season to 0 to close it: staff are not paid in closed months but rent and other fixed costs still run, and the month-by-month table shows the cash low point that causes.

    
Does this tell me what sales to expect or whether I can get a permit?

    
No. It works from your own figures. Scoop volume, local prices, zoning, health-department licensing and equipment rules all depend on your location, so check them before you sign a lease.

    
Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    
No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
