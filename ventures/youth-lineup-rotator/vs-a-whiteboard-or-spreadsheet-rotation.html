Youth lineup planner vs. a whiteboard or spreadsheet rotation

Most volunteer coaches plan rotations by hand: a whiteboard, a notes app or a spreadsheet. That works well when the numbers are tidy. This page says where it stops working and what the free planner does instead.

Common questions

Is a hand-written rotation on a whiteboard good enough?

For a roster that divides evenly into the field spots it often is. It gets error-prone when the numbers do not divide (for example 11 players for 7 spots), when a child arrives late or leaves early, or when you also want to share the goalkeeper job and avoid benching anyone twice in a row. Those are the cases where a counted plan beats a remembered one.

What does the planner add over a spreadsheet?

It does the counting for you: it spreads bench turns so nobody plays more than one period more than anybody else (when nobody is absent or restricted), avoids back-to-back benching where the numbers allow, limits goalkeeper turns, and names any child it could not help. A spreadsheet can hold the same plan, but you build the rules yourself.

Can I still print it for the sideline?

Yes. The page has a printable sideline sheet, a copy-as-text option for the team chat, a CSV export, and a short note per family.

Does it remember last week so the same kids are not benched again?

Only if you paste it in. The Copy next game's roster button writes a sat: count for each child who sat, and pasting that into the next game's roster puts those children first in line to play. Nothing is stored on a server.

Is anything uploaded?

No. The planner runs in your browser with no account, no upload and no network request for your roster.
