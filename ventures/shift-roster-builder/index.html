How it is calculated

Hard rules (never broken): a person is only given a shift that falls inside one of their availability windows for the whole shift; shifts for one person never overlap and leave at least your rest gap between them; weekly hours stay at or under their maximum; days in a row stay at or under your limit. Objective: first cover as many shift-hours as possible, then minimise real payroll (wage × (1 + extras), overtime hours above your threshold at the multiplier), plus a small penalty for each hour a person is under their minimum and a quadratic penalty for hours drifting from a fair share (the balance slider). Search: a greedy build that fills the most constrained shifts first, then seeded simulated annealing with reassign and swap moves over several restarts; a fixed seed makes it repeatable. On small problems the tests compare it with an exhaustive search of every roster and it finds the same optimum; on large ones it is a strong heuristic, not a proof.

Not modelled: predictive-scheduling and fair-workweek notice rules, meal and rest breaks, minor-labor limits, split-shift premiums, skills or roles, tips, union rules, seniority, preferences beyond availability, time off requests across weeks. The rest rule does carry over from last week if you add prev: Sun 10pm to a person (when their last shift ended). Unverified: checked against exhaustive search and invented data in unit tests, not against a real operator's payroll or a scheduling law.

Common questions

How do I make an employee schedule that covers every shift?

List each shift you need covered with how many people it takes, list each person with their availability, weekly hour limits and wage, and assign people to shifts one hard rule at a time: available for the whole shift, not double-booked, enough rest since their last shift, under their weekly limit. This tool does that search for you and shows any shift it could not fill with the reason.

How does the roster builder decide who works which shift?

It never breaks a hard rule (availability, weekly hour cap, rest between shifts, consecutive days, no double-booking). Among the rosters that follow those rules it covers as many shifts as possible first, then picks the lowest payroll, counting overtime above your threshold, nudged by the hour balance slider and by each person's minimum hours. The search is a seeded simulated-annealing optimiser, so the same inputs always give the same roster.

Is the roster guaranteed to be the cheapest possible?

No. Choosing who works which shift is a hard combinatorial problem, so the tool searches rather than proves optimality. On small problems the test suite checks the result against an exhaustive search of every possible roster and it matches. On a full week it finds a strong roster quickly, and the result is deterministic so you can compare changes fairly.

What does the hour balance slider do?

At zero the tool chases the lowest payroll, which can pile hours on the cheapest people and leave others under their minimum. Raising it adds a penalty for each person's hours drifting from their fair share of the total, in proportion to their weekly limit. Payroll shown is always the real cost, not the penalised score.

What does who-is-critical mean?

For each person on the roster the tool re-builds the week without them and reports how many hours of shifts would then go uncovered. A person whose absence leaves hours uncovered is a single point of failure: one sick call and a shift is open. Use it to see where you need a backup or wider availability.

What rules and laws does it check?

It enforces the availability, weekly hour limit, rest gap and consecutive-day limit that you type in, and prices overtime above the threshold you set. It does not know your local predictive-scheduling or fair-workweek notice rules, break and meal-period rules, minor-labor limits, union terms or tip rules, which vary by place. Check the roster against the rules that apply to you.

Is my staff list uploaded anywhere?

No. Names, wages and availability are read and solved in this browser tab. Nothing is sent to a server, there are no accounts or cookies, and the tool does not store your roster. It works offline once the page has loaded.
