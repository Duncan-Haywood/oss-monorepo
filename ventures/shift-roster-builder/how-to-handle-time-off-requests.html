How many time-off requests can you grant before a shift goes empty?

Every request looks fine on its own. The trouble is the fourth one, when the people who were covering for the first three are now the ones asking. A good rule is to grant requests one at a time and rebuild the week after each, so you find the request that tips a shift into empty before you have promised it. We measured where that happens for one cafe.

What it shows

The first request is nearly free: the week still covers 216 hours and payroll moves by $2. The second costs a shift. By the fifth, more than a quarter of the week's hours are uncovered, and the payroll that "fell" is only the pay for shifts nobody is working. So the number to watch is open shifts, not cost. This is one invented example, not a benchmark, and your availability decides where your limit falls.

How to do it by hand

1. Rank requests by how few people could cover

For each requested day, count the people who are available for the busiest shift and are not also asking. Grant the requests with the most spare cover first.

2. Recheck after every approval

Each yes uses up someone who could have covered the next one, so the count drops by more than one.

3. Say no early, with a reason

Tell a person which shift would go empty. People take a specific reason better than silence. Check the rules where you operate for any notice or approval rules that apply.

Questions

How many time-off requests can I approve at once?

There is no fixed number; it depends on how many other people are available when the requester is off. Grant them one at a time and rebuild the week after each: the first request that leaves a shift open is the limit for that week. The roster builder takes an off: entry per person and shows any shift it can no longer fill.

How do I schedule around time-off requests?

Put the approved days off into each person's availability before you assign anyone, so the schedule never gives them a shift on those days, then check whether any shift went uncovered. The roster builder reads an off: entry, such as off: Sat-Sun, and re-plans the week around it.
