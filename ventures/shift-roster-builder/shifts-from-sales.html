Shifts from your sales

Paste an hourly sales export (a date-time column and a sales column). Each weekday-hour is averaged, heads needed is sales divided by your sales per labor hour (at least your minimum), and each day's headcount is cut into shifts. Copy the result into the roster builder. Square and Toast item exports work as they come: net sales are used, and voided or refunded lines are left out. Nothing is uploaded.

auto tests equal weights, half-lives of 8, 4 and 2 weeks and the last 4 weeks only on your own file (each past week planned from the weeks before it) and keeps whichever left the fewest hours short or spare. It needs 8 or more full weeks of dated sales; on a long file it takes a few seconds.

On (the default): minimum staff is needed only in hours your shop normally trades (sales on 1 in 5 of that weekday's dates or more) or sold in that day, so a plan that staffs a lunch closure or old closing hours is charged for it, and auto may pick the last 4 weeks only, which follows a change in opening hours fastest. Off: minimum staff counts as needed in every hour a plan staffs, and auto chooses among the weightings only.

Planned from the average, an hour is short about half the time. With a number here each hour is staffed for that share of its past days instead: 1 staffs for the median day, 3 for 3 days in 4 (75%), 0.5 for 1 in 3.

An extra person wanted for only an hour or two is checked against your past days before it becomes a whole shift. It goes in the cheapest shift that holds it, or is left out (and listed) when that shift's spare hours cost more than the short hours it saves, at this number or at 1 when blank. Minimum staff is always scheduled.

Test it on your past weeks

    
Each full week after the first four is planned from the weeks before it only, then compared hour by hour with what that week's sales needed. Tried for the plain average, three half-lives, the last four weeks and last week alone; with a short-cost number, each of those staffed for that share of past days too, scored as that many times the short hours plus the spare ones. Each plan is cut into shifts first, as above, so the hours added to reach the shortest shift count; with a short-cost number, a run shorter than the shortest shift is staffed only where that is cheaper than leaving it short. Needs dated rows and at least five full weeks.
