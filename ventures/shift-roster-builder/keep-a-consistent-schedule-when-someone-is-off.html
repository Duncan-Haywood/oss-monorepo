One person is off on Tuesday. Why did four people's weeks change?

When someone books a day off, the honest fix is small: find cover for their shift and leave everyone else alone. A scheduler that rebuilds the week from scratch does not know what "everyone else alone" means. It finds a roster that is just as good on paper and hands it out, and people who asked for nothing find their closes moved. Do that every time someone is off and the schedule stops being something staff can plan around.

How it works

"Plan several weeks in a row" first builds your usual week, with the dated days off left out. Every planned week then scores a small bonus for each hour someone works one of their usual shifts. The bonus is a tenth of their wage an hour ("Yes, firmly" is a quarter). Leaving a shift open costs far more than any bonus, so the setting never leaves a shift open to keep someone in place. When two rosters cover the same hours for about the same money, it picks the one that looks like the usual week. The week after the time off, the usual week wins again, so people go back to their own shifts.

On a single week, load last week's roster CSV with "Carry over last week" and the same setting keeps people on last week's shifts. On the command line it is --keep-shifts 0.1, with --weeks or --last-week.

Doing it by hand

1. Write down the usual week once

Keep a copy of the rota you would run with nobody off. That is the week you change from and go back to.

2. Move only the shifts the time off touches

Cover the absent person's shifts with someone already free at that time. Only move a second person if nobody free can take it, and stop as soon as the gap is filled.

3. Go back the week after

Don't carry a one-off reshuffle into the next week. Start again from the usual week.

Questions

How do I keep a consistent schedule when someone takes time off?

Start from the usual rota and move only the shifts the person who is off cannot work, then put everyone back on their usual shifts the week after. A scheduler that rebuilds the week from scratch often finds an equally good roster that moves people who were not off. The free roster builder's "Keep people on their usual shifts" setting scores each usual shift kept, so the rebuild moves fewer of them without leaving a shift open.

Does keeping a consistent schedule cost more?

On the invented teams we measured, it left exactly the same hours uncovered and changed the month's payroll by a fraction of a percent. The setting is a small bonus per kept hour, far below the cost of an open shift, so it never leaves a shift open to keep someone in place.
