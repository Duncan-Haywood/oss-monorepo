# Shed Designer: gable shed plans, 3D view and lumber cut list

Shed Designer is a free browser tool for people building their own backyard shed. You drag out a gable shed, and it frames every board at its real size. You get construction drawings, a 3D model, a cut sheet that says which board each piece comes out of, and a shopping list with a cost.

**Live tool:** https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/shed-designer/

**After using this, you can now** take a printed plan set and a board-by-board cut sheet to the lumberyard and the saw, for the exact size of shed you want. **That used to take** a fixed plan set in somebody else's size plus an evening with a tape, a calculator and a spreadsheet.

For assistants: [llms.txt](llms.txt).

## What it does

- **Height-led roof.** Set a most-height-at-the-peak limit and tick the two auto boxes. The walls drop to the lowest the tallest door or window allows, and the roof takes the steepest whole pitch that stays under the limit. When no pitch can, the tool says by how much the limit is missed and what would fix it (`buildAuto` in `model.mjs`).
- **Ridge beam or ridge board.** A ridge beam is two plies of 2x8 to 2x12 bearing on a two-ply post in each gable, over two post studs in the wall. Rafters stop at the beam face and get hangers, and there are no ties. A ridge board gets rafter ties at the plate on every pair. Gable vents centre under a ridge board and flank the post under a beam.
- **Colour.** Siding paint colours, and purple polka dots, show in the 3D view. Paint is added to the shopping list at two coats and 350 sq ft per gallon.
- **Design by dragging.** On the plan view you drag the footprint corner or edges (6" steps) and slide doors and windows along a wall or onto another wall. On the end view you drag the peak (roof pitch), the wall top (wall height) and the eave tip (overhang). Every number is also a labelled field. Undo and redo cover both (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y), and typing a number is one undo step.
- **Keyboard, touch and screen readers.** Every handle, door and window takes the arrow keys (6" on the footprint, 24" with Shift; 1" on an opening along its wall; a pitch step on the peak; 1" on the wall height and overhang), and keeps focus after the drawing is rebuilt. Each handle has a touch target at least 44 px across. The 3D view takes the arrow keys and + and -, and two-finger pinch. Each change is announced once, with the new size, pitch and overhang. A broken share link says so. `browser.test.mjs` drives all of this in headless Chromium at laptop and phone size, with an axe check on every results tab and no console errors allowed; the `shed-designer-browser` workflow runs it.
- **True dimensional lumber.** A 2x4 is framed at 1-1/2" x 3-1/2", a 2x6 at 5-1/2", a 2x8 at 7-1/4", a 2x10 at 9-1/4" and a 2x12 at 11-1/4"; 4x4 and 4x6 skids at 3-1/2". Stud length is the wall height less three plates. A rafter's cut length includes its plumb cut. The ridge is the first 2x size deeper than the rafter that also covers the plumb cut (`lumber.mjs`, `model.mjs`).
- **Foundation, floor, four walls and a gable roof.**
  - *Foundation:* pressure-treated skids, spaced no more than the gap you allow, on a gravel pad (volume in cubic yards and tons) or on solid concrete blocks.
  - *Floor:* rim joists and floor joists on layout, and 3/4" plywood in staggered rows.
  - *Walls:* the eave walls run full length and the gable ends frame between them. Studs are laid out from the building corner, so 4 ft sheet edges land on a stud. Corner studs, kings, jacks, doubled headers (with a spacer note), rough sills and cripples are all framed. The bottom plate is cut out at doors. Plates longer than your longest board splice over studs, and the joints of both plates are chosen together so the cap plate's are 4 ft from the top plate's where the studs allow, and never less than the 24" the IRC requires (R602.3.2). When no split on full studs can do that (a 6 ft door in a gable wall with only 8 ft boards), a joint goes over the header, on a cripple, with the header running at least a stud width past it each side; the connection check checks exactly that. When the split leaves a plate piece under 24" and one longer board would avoid it, a note names the length.
  - *Roof:* rafters with a birdsmouth (full plate-width seat) and plumb cuts, a ridge board, rafter ties at the plate, gable studs cut to the roof line, and a post under the ridge.
  - *Gable vents:* a trimmer each side, a flat header as high as the roof allows, a sill block and short posts.
  - *Coverings:* T1-11 siding (4x8, 4x9 or 4x10, picked to reach the eave or the gable line), OSB roof sheathing in staggered courses, shingles, underlayment and drip edge.
- **Producer cut sheet.** For each material the pieces are packed into the board lengths you tick (8 to 20 ft) at the prices you type, with kerf between cuts. The method is best-fit-decreasing, tried once with each stock length as the default board, with each board then shrunk to the cheapest length that still holds its cuts. The problem's linear relaxation is then solved by column generation (Gilmore and Gomory): a small simplex over cutting patterns, with each new pattern found by an exact knapsack search per stock length. Its whole patterns are kept, the leftover pieces are packed as before, and that plan wins when it is cheaper. The relaxation's optimum is a proven lower bound, shown beside the cut sheet's cost; over the 257 cut lists of the presets and their options the orders are 0.3% above it in all, and 189 of them meet it exactly. Each board is drawn as a bar of its cuts, by part mark, with the offcut hatched. A CSV is available (`optimize.mjs`, `estimate.mjs`).
- **Plans.** Eight sheets: foundation plan, floor framing plan, the four wall framing elevations (seen from outside, every opening dimensioned, part marks on members), the roof framing plan and a building section with pitch, heights and overhang. Print them, or download each sheet (or all of them) as SVG, or as DXF R12 in inches at full size with layers FRAMING, OUTLINE, HIDDEN, OPENINGS, DIMS and TEXT (`drawings.mjs`).
- **For a permit or HOA review.** The cover sheet gives the floor area, overall height, materials and colours, blank lines for the owner, site address and parcel number, an opening schedule and a sheet index. Type your lot (width, depth), where the shed sits (from the left and rear lot lines), the house and the setback your town or HOA asks for, and a site plan sheet draws them with every clearance, to the walls and front and back to the eave; a shed past a lot line, inside the setback or on the house gets a warning. It draws what you type and is not a survey. The Plans tab says that many places need no building permit for a small shed (the IRC's R105.2 exempts a one-storey detached accessory building up to 200 sq ft, the IBC sets 120 sq ft, many towns less) and that zoning and HOA rules can still apply. Nothing here makes the set "permit-ready": what a submission needs is set locally, and the plans are not engineered or code-checked.
- **HOA packet** (`hoa.mjs`, `hoa-packet.html`, the "HOA packet (PDF)" button on the Plans tab). What an HOA's architectural committee usually asks to see, as five landscape pages to print or save as a PDF from the browser's print dialog (no PDF library). A summary page: footprint and floor area, roof outline with its overhang, heights to the peak and to the eave walls above the ground, the floor height, the pitch in rise per 12 and degrees, the siding (type, direction and colour), roofing, trim, every door and window, the gable vents, colour swatches and an optional 3D view. Four coloured elevations drawn to look like the shed: lap siding level at its exposure, T1-11 grooves, battens or panel joints, the paint, roof and trim colours, corner boards, casings, fascia and rake, the doors (a site-built door shows its Z frame), windows and vents. Each elevation is dimensioned: wall length or width, eave to eave on the gable ends, ground to wall top, ground to peak, floor height, the pitch, every opening's size, and a chain under the wall placing each opening from the corner. A roof and footprint plan seen from above gives the walls and the roof outline both ways, the overhang, the ridge, the slope and where each door and window sits. The last page is the plan set's own site plan when a lot is typed, or a box to sketch one. The design travels in the link, as "Copy link" does it. Sample packets for each preset: `hoa-packet.html#preset=8x12` and so on, linked at the top of the page. It says it is not engineered or code-checked and not an approval.
- **3D view.** A small WebGL renderer, no library. Every board is an extruded profile, so rafters show their birdsmouth and gable studs their angled tops. You can orbit, zoom, switch to front, gable end or top views, and toggle layers (foundation, floor, walls, roof, sheathing, siding, roofing). "Framing only" strips it to the frame (`render3d.mjs`).
- **Several designs, compared.** You can start new designs from presets (8x12, 8x10, 10x12, 12x16, 8x8) or copy the current one. Each design gets a card with a 3D thumbnail, its footprint, peak, board count, total and cost per square foot, and the lowest-cost design is marked. You can save a design to a file, open one from a file, or copy a link that carries the design inside the URL.

The starting design is the owner's spec: an 8x12 with a single 38" x 78" (6'6") door on one 12 ft side and two 24" x 36" windows on the opposite side. The 2x4 walls are 16" on centre. The roof is a ridge beam (two-ply 2x10 on two-ply posts, so no rafter ties and an open inside), with 12" x 8" gable vents flanking each post. It is finished the common way (stained T1-11 siding, charcoal shingles, white trim, bare framing inside), and the peak is limited to 8 ft. Paint colours, polka dots, glitter and a decorated interior are options.

All three automatic choices are ticked:

- **Walls:** 86-1/2". That is 78 for the door, 5-1/2 for a 2x6 header and three 1-1/2" plates.
- **Pitch:** 4/12. No pitch keeps the peak under 8 ft with that door, so the tool takes the lowest pitch at which the vents still fit, and the warning says the peak is 10' 2-3/8" above the gravel. Even 3/12 would be 9' 10-3/8".
- **Overhang:** 6". Any longer and the 57-3/16" rafters cost more lumber. (Before the cut optimizer solved the relaxation, 6" to 9" all priced the same and the tool chose 9"; the exact optimizer finds 6" cheaper by $2.87 of 2x6.)

At the starting prices that design is 151 pieces from 84 boards. The cut sheet's boards cost $639.32 against a proven lower bound of $638.34; with spare boards the lumber is $736.72, and the whole shed is $3,026.46 before sales tax. Every structural check passes, and the ridge beam is the most loaded member at 80%. The "8x12 classic" preset (8 ft walls, 6/12, ridge board and ties, 38" x 82" door, unpainted) is 143 pieces from 92 boards and $3,198.62 before sales tax.

## Structure, integrity and prices

- **Structural checks** (`checks.mjs`, Structure tab). The members checked are:
  - the floor joists between skids;
  - the subfloor against its APA span rating (23/32" tongue-and-groove is rated for joists up to 24" apart; `levers.mjs`);
  - the rafters;
  - the ridge beam and its posts, using column stability on the posts;
  - under a ridge board, the rafter-tie heel joint: the pair's outward thrust (wL²/8h) against 16d common nails at the NDS yield-limit value for SPF (about 120 lb each), which sets the nail count in the instructions, the nailing schedule and the shopping list;
  - every door and window header;
  - the eave-wall studs under roof load plus wind on the wall face.

  These are allowable-stress (NDS) checks on SPF No. 2 under a roof snow load and a wall wind pressure you set (defaults 30 and 20 psf), with 15 psf roof dead load and 40 + 10 psf on the floor. The engine is `structure.mjs`, copied unchanged from `open-house-plans`, where it is pinned against the research track's Python; its fixture test comes with it. Each member shows its use of capacity. This is a screening check: connections, uplift, racking and the foundation are not checked.
- **Where the capacity goes** (`utilisation.mjs`, `utilisation-sheets.mjs`, `utilisation-ui.mjs`, Structure tab). Every checked member is listed with the highest use first. Opening one writes out its check: the inputs, each formula and the numbers, and which check governs. The breakdown is computed separately and compared with the Structure check. If the two ever disagree, the member shows only the Structure result and says so, and a test sweeps over 1,000 variations to keep them in step. Two hand calculations in `utilisation.test.mjs` reproduce the default 8x12's 15% readings. The floor joist's 15% is bending over a 46.25" span. The wall stud's 15% comes from wind on the siding (dead + 0.6W), and the roof alone uses under 5% of it.
  - A target margin (70% by default) lists the members above it, and the members under half of it with the reason. The reason is one of: simply oversized, the next size down misses the target, the next size down fails, stiffness, a sheathing or siding rating, or the smallest size the tool frames. Each lighter option is built, and it is offered only if it passes every load and connection check.
  - "Colour by structural use" on the 3D view colours the framing by utilisation band, with unchecked members grey. Tapping a member names it and opens its check. The floor, roof and four wall framing plans are drawn in the same colours.
- **Integrity tests** (`integrity.mjs`, `integrity.test.mjs`).
  - Starting from the gravel pad, every board, sheet and fixture must be reachable through pieces that touch it, so nothing floats.
  - No two square-cut members may overlap by more than 1/16".
  - Every preset and variation is checked.
  - Load paths and nailing (`connections.mjs`): studs, jacks, cripples and sills bear on what is under them, rafters seat on the plates and meet the ridge, joists sit on two skids, a skid runs under each ridge-beam post, a spliced skid's joint lands on a block, and every subfloor, roof-sheathing and siding joint has framing behind it.

  The test found a real bug on 2026-10-29: every wall member was placed starting at the floor, so the top plates, headers and sills sat at floor level in 3D. It also found that the two plies of each header overlapped, and that the new eave blocks collided with rafter ties. All three are fixed, and the tests stop them coming back.

  The load-path and nailing checks found four more the same night, all fixed with tests (see `docs/posts/2026-10-29-four-framing-faults-the-shed-checks-missed.md`): with an even number of skids a ridge-beam post stood mid-span on one 2x6 end joist (1,403 lb on the 10x12, 1.38 times its allowable bending); the eave-wall headers and studs under a ridge board were checked with the ridge-beam roof load, about 40% too little; a spliced skid's joint fell between two blocks; and two kinds of window left a siding joint with no stud behind it.
- **Fuzz test** (`fuzz.test.mjs`). A seeded sweep of random designs (sizes, pitches, walls, overhangs, spacings, foundations, roofs, loads, doors and windows anywhere) and of junk saved state and links. Every design must build without throwing, have no NaN or negative lengths or prices, pass the integrity test, run every structural check, and give well-formed SVG and R12 DXF. Its first run broke 135 of 1,000 designs; the root causes (rafters and ties inside the gable wall, inverted eave blocks, false overlap reports, unbuilt openings still bought, a cleared price read as $0, junk links that crashed the builder, raw UTF-8 in DXF text) are fixed and each has a pinned test. `FUZZ_N=2000 FUZZ_SEED=5 node --test fuzz.test.mjs` runs a wider sweep.
- **Prices with sources** (`prices.mjs`). Starting prices are Home Depot listings seen on 2026-10-29, most read from the search result's copy of the product page, so they can be stale and they vary by store. Each line of the shopping list links its listing. Lengths without a listing are scaled per foot from the nearest listed length and marked "(scaled)". Items with no readable listing (paneling, glitter, solid and CMU blocks, interior paint) say "estimate" and link the product page where there is one; the gravel price is the low end of a supplier's published per-ton range, with the bagged alternative in its note. Every price is editable.
- **How to build** (`instructions.mjs`, its own tab). Sections in build order, written from the design with its own numbers:
  - permits and tools; the gravel pad with its diagonal; the skid centres;
  - floor joist layout marks and the frame diagonal; the subfloor cuts;
  - each wall's centre marks for every stud, king, jack and cripple, from the left end as seen from outside, with the opening framing and the wall diagonal;
  - the ridge beam height; the rafter's plumb and birdsmouth layout (speed-square reading, run, heel and seat); the gable studs and vents;
  - the roof sheathed, fasciaed and dried in, then the siding, then the rake boards, rake drip edge and shingles that lap over it; trim with the head flashing over each casing, paint (with a polka-dot stencil method) and the interior.

  A carpenter's read-through on 2026-10-30 (default 8x12, 10x12, 12x16) fixed the order of the roof-edge layers and the head flashing, the door wall's bottom plate, the rafter layout on connector seats, the site-built door's assembly, and fasteners that did not match the list; `instructions.test.mjs` pins each one.

  Every piece is called by its cut-sheet mark. You can print it or download it as Markdown. Fastener spacings are typical values to check against local code and product instructions.
- **Design search** (`designsearch.mjs`, engine only so far; the gallery that shows it is being built). Instead of varying the design you have, it searches whole designs under your limits: every whole-foot size from 80% of the footprint limit up to it (100 sq ft by default: 10x10, 9x11, 8x12, 7x14, 6x16 and the rest), with the ridge running either way; five door and window layouts (door on the eave wall or the gable end, zero to two windows, heads in line with the door); ridge board or ridge beam; pitch 4/12 to 9/12; overhang; wall height (the lowest the door allows, or 92" or 97-1/8" for an 8 ft sidewall); studs and rafters at 16" or 24"; 2x4 or 2x6 rafters and joists; the foundation blocks; plywood or OSB floor. Keys you fix (roof covering, siding, anything else) are left alone. Each design is framed, priced and run through every structural and connection check, and only passing designs under the footprint, peak (13 ft by default) and wall-height limits are kept. It returns the Pareto front over three objectives:
  - *cost:* the materials total before tax;
  - *storage:* usable volume, the inside floor area times the clear height under the top plates, plus the triangle under the roof (open under a ridge beam; over the ties under a ridge board, flagged as loft space that needs decking). Floor area and wall length free of openings are reported too and can be objectives;
  - *looks:* a heuristic score out of 100, the sum of seven explained terms (pitch, roof-to-wall proportion, plan proportion, overhang, sidewall height, symmetry, windows). It is a set of rules of thumb, not a measure of beauty.

  The search seeds one lean design per size and layout, then runs a Pareto local search: from each design on the front it tries every one-knob change, until its budget (800 designs, about 7 s in Node) is spent. On the default prices a 1,500-design run kept 84 designs on the front; the cheapest passing design was $2,430.94 against $3,026.46 for the default 8x12, and every design on the front used a ridge board. API: `searchDesigns(opts)`, `searchSteps(opts)` (a generator for progress), `evaluateDesign`, `storageOf`, `looksOf`, `paretoFront`, `picks` (cheapest, most storage, best looks, balanced), `sizesUnder`, `layoutOpenings`; fields are only ever added.
- **Options made for you** (`candidates.mjs`). Seven close variations of the design you are editing: rafter spacing, ridge beam or board, a steeper or lower roof, a foot of overhang, a foot wider. Each is framed, priced and structurally checked, then ranked with passing designs first and cheapest first. "Use this one" adds it to your designs.
- **Roof edge.** A 2x6 sub-fascia across the rafter tails (in the cut list), a 1x6 fascia, a vented soffit under the tails and a 1x4 rake board on each gable edge. Blocking between the rafters closes each wall top.
- **Wall trim.** Primed 1x4 (3/4" x 3-1/2") corner boards, two to a corner with the long-wall board lapping the end-wall board, and a picture-frame casing round each door and window (no sill board under a door). The pieces are packed into 8, 12 and 16 ft primed boards by the same cut optimizer, and the cut plan is on the Cut sheet tab.
- **Off-the-shelf parts, or build it.** A door or window can take an off-the-shelf unit, whose maker's rough opening then sets the framing (`products.mjs`). A door can also be site-built: the siding cut out of the opening is the skin, on a 2x4 Z frame (stiles, three rails, two braces running down to the hinges), hung on three tee hinges, with a pull and a hasp. Its frame goes on the cut sheet, and it gets its own plan sheet and build steps. A site-built door warns when its leaf is so short or narrow that the Z brace would run under about 25 or over 65 degrees. A custom-size door or window has no unit to buy, so it is left out of the total and named with a typical range from the catalogue (cheapest to dearest single doors, double doors over 44", or windows); a box counts each at that range's median, as a line marked "estimate", never as a listing.
- **Primed panel siding in two thicknesses.** LP SmartSide 38 Series (3/8") and 76 Series (7/16"), besides T1-11, board and batten, vinyl and fiber cement. A warning says when the studs are wider apart than the panel is rated for.
- **Foundations.** Skids on a compacted gravel pad, or on solid 4x8x16 blocks, concrete deck blocks or 8x8x16 CMU blocks, either on the pad or on a 4" bed of bagged paver base under each block, a block at each end of each skid and no more than 4 ft apart. Site prep adds landscape fabric, the gravel by the ton and either a hand tamper or a day's plate-compactor rental.
- **Hardware and sealants.** Nails by kind and box (16d framing, 8d sheathing, 8d siding, 1-1/4" roofing, 8d finish for trim, 10d connector nails), counted from the nailing schedule; exterior caulk round every opening and corner board; Z-flashing over every door and window head; starter strip and ridge cap shingles.
- **Compare stores and swap parts** (`catalog.mjs`, under the shopping list). For the parts with listings at more than one store (framing lumber, treated lumber, OSB and plywood, T1-11 and LP panels, 1x4 trim, shingles, underlayment, drip edge, hangers, connectors and stain) a table gives the Home Depot, Lowe's and Menards price, the store's model, item or SKU number and a link to the product page, the cheapest, and the total at each store. It then says where to buy: the cheapest single store, what adding a second store would save and on which lines (suggested only above $25, since a second trip or delivery costs something), and the saving if every line went to its cheapest store. Lowe's lumber pages mostly show no price without a store, so few Lowe's boards appear. The quote emails (Home Depot, Lowe's, Menards or a local yard) name that store's own item number and link under each line where we have one, and the shopping-list CSV carries each line's price source and link. Swaps that change the design (T1-11 to primed LP, 3/8" to 7/16", no trim, connectors for notches, a rented compactor) are priced against your design, and "Try it and show me" applies one so the 3D view shows it. We link to each product page rather than copying the store's photos: the photos are the store's copyright and the stores' terms forbid scraping.
- **Simplify the build** (`simplify.mjs`). Counts every saw cut, angled cut and birdsmouth notch, then searches small changes (pitch, overhang, rafter and stud spacing, connectors or notches, ridge beam or board, and if you allow it a foot on the length or width) for the cheapest build, the fewest and simplest cuts, and a balance. A design is kept only if it passes every load and connection check, stays within the floor-area limit, keeps some overhang, keeps shingles at 4/12 or steeper and studs within the siding's rating, and is no more than 1" taller. It shows the winners and every design no other beats on cost, cuts and angled cuts at once.
- **Bring the price down, same size** (`budget` in `simplify.mjs`). Prices one change at a time from your design (other stock doors and windows from the catalogue, siding type, roofing, the foundation and whether gravel goes under the whole shed or only under each block, plywood or OSB subfloor, floor joists at 16" or 24" and 2x4 to 2x8, overhang, pitch, spacing), applies the ones that save money biggest first, then polishes. It keeps the footprint, the ridge type and the rafter seat you chose, and keeps a change only if the shed passes every check and the peak rises by no more than 6". It lists each step's saving, what changing your structure choices would add, and savings held back by the height allowance with the peak each would give. Vinyl and fiber cement are left out until their trim pieces are priced. On the default 8x12 it finds $548.77 (a $246 steel prehung door in place of the $615 shed door, 24x27 windows, LP 3/8" panels, OSB subfloor, a 4" overhang).
- **What each part costs** (`costlevers.mjs`, under "Options made for you"). For the foundation, floor, each door and the windows, every alternative is framed, priced and checked against the design you are editing. The alternatives are:
  - a gravel pad, or each block type on a full pad, on paver base under each block, or on tamped soil;
  - joists at 16" or 24", 2x4 to 2x8, and a plywood or OSB subfloor;
  - a door built on site at 36", 48", 60" or 72" (a pair of leaves over 48"), or each stock door;
  - no windows, or every window as one stock size.

  Each row shows what it saves or adds in materials and your time, whether the peak moves, which checks fail, and what it means on site (stone to haul, blocks to level, daylight lost). "Use this" applies it, and Undo takes it back. On the 8x12 starter: no windows saves $139.80 and about 3 hours, but there is no daylight with the door shut; a 60" pair of site-built doors adds $76.24; a full gravel pad under the blocks adds $327.92.
- **Your limits and fixed choices** (`levers.mjs`, top of "Exact sizes"). The floor-area limit (100 sq ft by default) and the peak limit sit with six "keep as I chose" boxes: siding, roofing, foundation, floor, doors and windows. A ticked choice is stored in the design (`locked`) and is never changed by the cheaper-build or simpler-build searches or priced against in the part options. `fixedKeys(design)` gives the same choices as design keys, and `LEVER_KNOBS` lists the foundation and floor values, for other searches. On the 8x12 starter with siding and roofing kept (shingles and siding to match a house), the cheaper-build search still finds $170.88: 2x4 floor joists at 24", an OSB subfloor, a 4" overhang and 2x4 rafters.
- **Two stores priced independently** (`lowes.mjs`, `catalog.mjs`). Besides the Home Depot list, a separate Lowe's list covers lumber in each length, sheet goods, siding, trim, roofing, fasteners, connectors, paint, hardware, blocks, inside finishes and the shed windows, each with its item or model number and link. Where Lowe's sells another pack size (5 lb against 1 lb of nails, 105 ft against 120 ft of starter) the price is converted to the list's unit and the note says so. "The whole list at each store" prices every line at each store's own listing and says how many lines it covers; bulk prices ("$3.61 each for 35 or more") apply once the quantity reaches them. Lowe's prices come from search-result copies of its pages, many of them old, because Lowe's shows no price until you pick a store.
- **One store only, for a Pro desk quote.** "Price the list at" Home Depot or Lowe's re-runs the cut optimizer on that store's prices (so it buys the lengths that store sells cheapest), prices every line at that store's own listing and item number, and marks the lines it has no listing for with where we found them. On the default 8x12, Home Depot covers every line but the Z-flashing; Lowe's covers about 80% of the money. "Home Depot and Lowe's" in the quote emails sends each store its own list, asking it to quote its equivalent for any line we found no listing of its for.
- **The order as a till would ring it.** Spare boards (5% of each size by default, for culls and mis-cuts), the consumables the build steps call for (subfloor adhesive, screws for a site-built door, flashing tape, shims), sales tax at the rate you set (the 7.53% US average by default, from the Tax Foundation's 2026 figure), and delivery (Home Depot's $79 flatbed tier from a store page; Lowe's publishes no truck fee we could read, so you type it). Tick "Have it" on any line you already own and it comes off what is left to buy.
- **Your time.** A labour rate ($50 an hour by default, adjustable) prices a rough count of the hours to build it (minutes per cut, member, connector, sheet, bundle, trim board, block, opening; the table is in `simplify.mjs`). The summary shows the hours and materials plus time, and the budget search and one of the simplicity goals count time, so a saving that costs more work than it is worth is not taken. On the default 8x12 it comes to about 36 hours.
- **Defaults as built.** The starter now has a door built from the siding (no prehung door), the 14 x 21 shed window Home Depot sells, and solid blocks on tamped soil with no crushed stone; gravel under the blocks or a full pad are options. Connectors and ridge hangers show in the 3D view.
- **Faster builds.** The automatic pitch is a binary search (the peak and the gable's room for a vent both rise with pitch), the automatic overhang re-prices only the materials whose cut list changes, and recent builds are cached, so a fresh build takes about 13 ms instead of 46 and re-pricing the swaps on each edit about 7 ms instead of 192.
- **More measurements in 3D.** Besides length, width and heights: every door and window's rough width and height, its distance from the corner, each window's sill height, the floor height, the overhang and the roof pitch and slope length. Labels on walls facing away are hidden.
- **Looks.** Siding, trim and door paint in generic house colours (white, cream, greige, sage, navy, charcoal and more) or any colour from the picker beside each list; asphalt shingle colour families with tab-to-tab blend; lap, T1-11 and board-and-batten textures; a lawn, sky and soft shadow; and a Presentation button (no board outlines or dimension lines, a lower three-quarter view) for a neighbour or an HOA. Colours live in `looks.mjs`; the shader is compiled once per material class so a phone on software GL stays quick (`docs/posts/2026-10-30-making-a-shed-look-like-the-house.md`). Polka dots and a glitter finish too. The siding paint (two coats, 350 sq ft a gallon) and glitter packets are added to the list. A "teen bedroom" interior adds pink paneling, generic poster art of an invented band, string lights, a rug and a beanbag. The big poster can show a picture you upload, which stays in your browser. "Look inside" opens the front wall.

## What it does not do

- The ridge beam size is your choice, not a span-table result.
- The structure check is a screening check, not a code review or an engineer's design: it does not check connections, uplift, racking, the foundation or point loads. Header sizes follow a rule of thumb by opening width (2x6 up to 48", 2x8 up to 72", 2x10 up to 96") and the Structure tab then checks each one. Warnings flag a birdsmouth deeper than a third of the rafter, an opening that does not fit, and a vent too big for the gable.
- Gable sheds on skids only. It does not do lofts, hip or single-slope roofs, porches, ramps or slabs.
- Fastener counts follow a common nailing schedule and are rounded up to whole boxes; they are for buying, not a code schedule.
- Sheet counts are layouts a builder can cut in two stages, never turning a piece's strength axis: cut across into sections and rip each section, or rip into strips and cut each across. Strips of different widths share a sheet, and a full-width piece's offcut is ripped for strips (the 8x10 preset's roof went from 5 sheets to 4). Over 840 floor and roof lists in a sweep of sizes, pitches and overhangs, 114 are still above the area's lower bound, which is not always reachable. The gable siding pieces above the lower panels nest in pairs, head to toe, when they slope the same way (1,614 panels to 1,332 over 474 swept designs); the second piece is turned end for end, so its shiplap edges face the other way and the joint it meets is butted and caulked over the stud. A cut of one sheet good is not laid out on a drawing.
- It has been checked with hand-counted unit tests and by driving the page in headless Chromium. It has not been checked by building a shed from it.

## Run it

Serve this folder as static files, for example `python3 -m http.server` here, then open `http://localhost:8000/`. The page loads ES modules, which browsers will not load from `file://`. Tests: `node --test` (Node 22).

## Privacy

Everything runs in your browser. Your designs are never uploaded, and there are no accounts, tracking cookies or analytics vendor. Designs and prices are kept in this browser's local storage so they are there next time; a footer checkbox turns that off and deletes them. Anonymous counts of which features get used (`usage.mjs`: builds, drags, designs, compares, plans printed, DXF/SVG/CSV downloads, links copied, part options priced, HOA packets printed) are also kept on this device only and never sent anywhere. A second checkbox stops them and clears them, and a browser's Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal is honoured. A shared link carries the design inside the URL's `#` fragment, which browsers do not send to the server.
