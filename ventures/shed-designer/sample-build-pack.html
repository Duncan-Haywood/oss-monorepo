How to build

1. Before you start

Permits, buried lines and a materials check save the most time on a shed.

  1. Ask your building department whether a 12' 0" x 8' 0" (96 sq ft) shed with a peak about 10' 3/8" above grade needs a permit, and what setbacks from property lines apply. Many places exempt small sheds; the size limit varies.
  2. Call 811 (in the US) a few days before digging so buried lines are marked.
  3. Order from the Shopping list (91 boards, 3 floor sheets, 4 roof sheets and the rest). Print the Cut sheet and the Plans; every piece below is called by its letter mark from those lists.
  4. Tools: tape, chalk line, speed square and framing square, 4 ft level, circular saw, handsaw (to finish notches and the corners of cut-outs), drill/driver, framing hammer or nailer, a string line, two step ladders, safety glasses, hearing protection and gloves.
  5. Fasteners (typical, check your code): 16d galvanized commons for framing; 8d galvanized commons for the subfloor and roof sheathing; 8d galvanized siding nails; 8d galvanized finish nails for trim; 1-1/4" roofing nails; construction adhesive for the subfloor; #8 x 1-5/8" exterior screws for the door skin; and the connectors and hangers on the list with the 10d connector nails their maker specifies.

2. Gravel under each block

  1. Mark the shed's outline with stakes and string, square it (diagonals 14' 5-1/16" each), and mark where each of the 12 blocks goes.
  2. At each mark, dig a hole 4" wider than the block (20" x 12") down through the turf and soft soil, about 4" deep plus whatever it takes to reach firm ground.
  3. Fill with paver base (14 bags of 0.5 cu ft in all) in 2" lifts, tamping each lift hard.
  4. Level each bed to the others with a long straight board or a string line: the tops of all the beds in one plane.

Check:

  • Every bed level with the others within 1/4".
  • Firm under foot: no give when you stand on it.

3. Skids

  1. Lay 3 pressure-treated 4x4 skids (mark A, 3 pieces), running the 12' 0" length of the shed.
  2. Centre them at 1-3/4", 48", 94-1/4" from the front edge of the shed (the first and last flush with the front and back edges).
  3. Set 12 solid 4 x 8 x 16 concrete blocks on the paver-base beds under the skids (4" tall), one at each end and the rest no more than 4' 0" apart, then level the blocks by adding or scraping paver base under them (not with shims on top) until every skid is level and all skids are level with each other.
  4. Each skid is one piece.

4. Floor frame

  1. Cut the two rim joists (mark B) and the 10 floor joists (mark C, 93" each) from pressure-treated 2x6.
  2. Lay the two rims side by side, ends flush, and mark both at once. Put the first joist flush with each end. Mark the rest centred at 16", 32", 48", 64", 80", 96", 112", 128" from the left end, so that 8 ft sheet edges land on a joist.
  3. Stand the joists between the rims on the marks, crowns up, and nail through each rim into each joist end with three 16d nails.
  4. Square the frame: both diagonals must read 14' 5-1/16". Push the long corner in until they match.
  5. Set the frame on the skids with the rims parallel to the skids. Toenail each joist to each skid it crosses (two 16d), or fit a framing tie at each crossing.

Check:

  • Diagonals within 1/8".
  • Frame flush with the skids at the front and back.

5. Subfloor

  1. Lay 3/4" tongue-and-groove plywood with the 8 ft edges across the joists (3 sheets). Start the first row at the front, groove facing out, in a bead of construction adhesive on each joist.
  2. Rows are 4 ft wide; 2 rows cover the 8' 0" width. Start every other row with a half sheet (48") so the end joints are staggered; every end joint must land on a joist centre.
  3. Cuts along the length: 96", 48", 48", 96" (in row order). Rip the last row to width if the shed is not a multiple of 4 ft wide.
  4. Nail with 8d galvanized commons every 6" along sheet edges and every 12" on the joists in the field, 3/8" from edges, before the adhesive skins over. Leave a 1/8" gap at butt ends.

6. Back (eave side) wall

  1. Cut the plates: bottom (mark J), top (K) and cap (L). The wall is 12' 0" long, the full length of the shed.
  2. Lay the bottom and top plates together on the deck and square a line across both for the centre of each piece, measured from the left end as you will face the wall from outside: common studs at 3/4", 16", 64", 80", 128", 143-1/4"; corner studs at 4-1/4", 139-3/4"; king studs at 29-3/4", 48-1/2", 95-3/4", 114-1/2"; jack studs at 31-1/4", 47", 97-1/4", 113"; cripples over headers at 39-1/8", 47", 97-1/4", 105-1/8"; cripples under sills at 32-3/4", 45-1/2", 98-3/4", 111-1/2".
  3. Cut 12 full studs at 80" (wall height 7' 1/2" less three 1-1/2" plates).
  4. Window 14-1/4" wide x 21-1/4" high rough opening, starting 32" from the left end of the plate: a king stud each side, a jack each side at 63-3/4", a header of two 2x4 at 17-1/4" nailed together around a 1/2" plywood spacer and set on the jacks, a rough sill at 14-1/4" with its top 44" above the floor and cripples under it, then cripples above at the layout marks.
  5. Window 14-1/4" wide x 21-1/4" high rough opening, starting 98" from the left end of the plate: a king stud each side, a jack each side at 63-3/4", a header of two 2x4 at 17-1/4" nailed together around a 1/2" plywood spacer and set on the jacks, a rough sill at 14-1/4" with its top 44" above the floor and cripples under it, then cripples above at the layout marks.
  6. Nail the wall together flat on the deck: two 16d through the plates into each stud end; nail jacks to kings every 16" staggered; header to kings with four 16d each end.
  7. Square the wall (equal diagonals of 13' 10-3/16" plate to plate), tack a diagonal brace across it, then stand it up with a helper and set it on the line flush with the edge of the floor.
  8. Nail the bottom plate through the subfloor into the rim or joists, two 16d every 16". Plumb the ends with a level and brace to stakes until the next wall is in.

Check:

  • Wall plumb at both ends and straight along the top (string line).

7. Front (eave side) wall

  1. Cut the plates: bottom (mark I), top (K) and cap (L). The wall is 12' 0" long, the full length of the shed.
  2. Lay the bottom and top plates together on the deck and square a line across both for the centre of each piece, measured from the left end as you will face the wall from outside: common studs at 3/4", 16", 32", 48", 96", 112", 128", 143-1/4"; corner studs at 4-1/4", 139-3/4"; king studs at 50-3/4", 93-3/4"; jack studs at 52-1/4", 92-1/4".
  3. Cut 12 full studs at 80" (wall height 7' 1/2" less three 1-1/2" plates).
  4. Door 38-1/2" wide x 78" high rough opening, starting 53" from the left end of the plate: a king stud each side, a jack each side at 76-1/2", a header of two 2x4 at 41-1/2" nailed together around a 1/2" plywood spacer and set on the jacks, then cripples above at the layout marks.
  5. The bottom plate runs straight across the door opening: leave it whole for now. Framing on one plate keeps the opening its width and the wall square while you nail it up and lift it; the piece in the doorway comes out after the wall is raised.
  6. Nail the wall together flat on the deck: two 16d through the plates into each stud end; nail jacks to kings every 16" staggered; header to kings with four 16d each end.
  7. Square the wall (equal diagonals of 13' 10-3/16" plate to plate), tack a diagonal brace across it, then stand it up with a helper and set it on the line flush with the edge of the floor.
  8. Nail the bottom plate through the subfloor into the rim or joists, two 16d every 16". Plumb the ends with a level and brace to stakes until the next wall is in.
  9. Keep the plate nails outside the door opening, so the piece in the doorway comes free. Once the wall is raised and the bottom plate is nailed down, cut the plate out across the door: saw flush with the face of each jack stud with a reciprocating saw, blade flat against the jack, and stop at the bottom of the plate so you do not cut into the subfloor. Knock the piece out and clean up the ends with a chisel.

Check:

  • Wall plumb at both ends and straight along the top (string line).

8. Left (gable end) wall

  1. Cut the plates: bottom (mark N), top (O) and cap (M). The wall is 7' 5" long, fitting between the front and back walls.
  2. Lay the bottom and top plates together on the deck and square a line across both for the centre of each piece, measured from the left end as you will face the wall from outside: common studs at 3/4", 12-1/2", 28-1/2", 60-1/2", 76-1/2", 88-1/4"; post studs under the ridge-beam post at 43-3/4", 45-1/4".
  3. Cut 8 full studs at 80" (wall height 7' 1/2" less three 1-1/2" plates).
  4. Nail the wall together flat on the deck: two 16d through the plates into each stud end; nail jacks to kings every 16" staggered; header to kings with four 16d each end.
  5. Square the wall (equal diagonals of 10' 1-11/16" plate to plate), tack a diagonal brace across it, then stand it up with a helper and set it on the line between the eave walls.
  6. Nail the bottom plate through the subfloor into the rim or joists, two 16d every 16". Plumb the ends with a level and brace to stakes until the next wall is in.

Check:

  • Wall plumb at both ends and straight along the top (string line).

9. Right (gable end) wall

  1. Cut the plates: bottom (mark N), top (O) and cap (M). The wall is 7' 5" long, fitting between the front and back walls.
  2. Lay the bottom and top plates together on the deck and square a line across both for the centre of each piece, measured from the left end as you will face the wall from outside: common studs at 3/4", 12-1/2", 28-1/2", 60-1/2", 76-1/2", 88-1/4"; post studs under the ridge-beam post at 43-3/4", 45-1/4".
  3. Cut 8 full studs at 80" (wall height 7' 1/2" less three 1-1/2" plates).
  4. Nail the wall together flat on the deck: two 16d through the plates into each stud end; nail jacks to kings every 16" staggered; header to kings with four 16d each end.
  5. Square the wall (equal diagonals of 10' 1-11/16" plate to plate), tack a diagonal brace across it, then stand it up with a helper and set it on the line between the eave walls.
  6. Nail the bottom plate through the subfloor into the rim or joists, two 16d every 16". Plumb the ends with a level and brace to stakes until the next wall is in.

Check:

  • Wall plumb at both ends and straight along the top (string line).

10. Tie the walls together

  1. Nail each corner: end stud to the corner stud of the eave wall, 16d every 12".
  2. Lap the cap plates to lock the corners. On the long walls the cap (mark L) stops 3-1/2" short of each end; on the end walls the cap (mark M) runs 3-1/2" past each end and sits over the long wall's top plate. Nail cap to top plate with 16d every 16", and two 16d down through each lap into the plate below.
  3. Optional: a flat steel strap tie across each corner on top of the caps adds hold, but the lap is what ties the walls.
  4. Check the box: diagonals across the top plates must match at 14' 5-1/16". Rack the walls with the braces until they do.

11. Ridge beam and posts

  1. Build each gable post from two 2x4 (mark AD, 12-1/16"), nailed face to face, and stand one centred on each gable wall's cap plate, directly over the two post studs in that wall.
  2. Nail the two plies of the ridge beam together (mark D, two 2x10 at 144"): 16d in two rows every 12", staggered.
  3. Lift the beam onto the posts with a helper. Its top sits 8' 9-13/16" above the floor; check it with a level and a tape from the floor at both ends. Toenail it to each post with four 16d, two from each side. A metal strap from beam to post adds hold where your code asks for one (none is on the list).
  4. Brace the beam level and straight with temporary braces to the floor until rafters are on both sides.

12. Rafters

Cut one rafter, test it at both ends and in the middle, then use it as the pattern for the rest.

  1. Pitch 4/12: the roof rises 4" for every 12" across, an angle of 18.4 deg. On a speed square, pivot on the pivot point and read 4 on the COMMON scale for every plumb and seat line.
  2. Ridge end: a plumb cut, marked with the square at 4 on the common scale (18.4 deg off a square cut).
  3. No birdsmouth: from the ridge plumb line measure 49" along the bottom edge (a level run of 46-1/2") and square a line across: that is the wall line, where the rafter's underside rests on the outside top edge of the wall. Each rafter sits there on a variable-pitch connector (Simpson VPA2 or equal) nailed to the cap plate; set the connector's seat to the 4/12 slope and nail as Simpson's instructions show.
  4. Tail: 8" of overhang beyond the wall, 8-7/16" along the bottom edge past the wall line, ended with a plumb cut so the fascia hangs straight.
  5. Cut length from a square end: 59-5/16" (mark F, 14 rafters, 2x6).
  6. Mark rafter centres on the top plates and the beam: the first and last flush with the gable ends, the rest at 24", 48", 72", 96", 120" from the left end.
  7. Set rafters in opposite pairs so the beam stays straight. Hang each one from the beam with a 2x6 slope-adjustable rafter hanger (on the list), bent to the roof pitch before nailing, and nail it to its connector.
  8. There are no rafter ties: the beam carries the rafters, so the inside is open to the roof.
  9. Fit the blocks between rafters on top of each eave wall (mark H, G), top edges bevelled to 4/12. The soffit is vented, so the air has to get past them: drill two 1-1/2" holes through each block and staple insect screen over them.
  10. Nail the 2x6 sub-fascia (mark E) across the rafter tails, top flush with the rafter tops, two 16d per tail.

Check:

  • Every rafter top in one plane: sight along them or run a string.
  • Pattern rafter fits at both gable ends and the middle before cutting the rest.

13. Gable ends

  1. Fill each gable with studs under the end rafter (mark AI, AH), each on a layout mark of the wall below, its top cut to 4/12 (18.4 deg). Measure each one in place and compare with the Plans sheet; the lengths on the cut sheet are to the long point.
  2. Frame the gable vents in each gable: 12" x 8" rough openings with a trimmer stud on the outer side, the post being the inner one (mark AG), a flat header (mark AE) and a flat sill block (mark AF).

14. Roof sheathing and dry-in

The fascia goes on before the drip edge, and the eave drip edge before the underlayment, so each layer laps over the one below it.

  1. Sheathe with 7/16" OSB, long edges across the rafters (4 sheets, 2 courses a side). Start at the eave with full sheets flush with the face of the sub-fascia, stagger the next course by half a sheet, and land every end joint on a rafter. Leave 1/8" between sheets. The course at the ridge is narrower than a sheet: rip its strips side by side, as many to a sheet as fit, and take them from what is left of a sheet after a short full-width piece before starting a new one (the sheet count assumes that).
  2. Nail with 8d every 6" on the edges and every 12" in the field.
  3. Fascia: nail the 1x6 fascia to the sub-fascia, its top edge tight under the sheathing, with two 8d galvanized finish nails at each rafter tail.
  4. Drip edge along the eaves, over the fascia and under the underlayment.
  5. Roll the underlayment from the eave up, each course lapped over the one below by the maker's mark, over the ridge. The roof is now dry enough to wait for the siding; the rake drip edge and shingles come after it.

15. Siding

  1. T1-11 panels, grooves vertical, 10 panel positions around the shed (see the shopping list for the panel lengths). Start at a corner, keep the bottom edge level with the top of the skids so it laps the rim joist and floor, and plumb the first panel with a level.
  2. Every vertical panel edge lands on a stud. Nail 8d galvanized every 6" on edges and every 12" on intermediate studs.
  3. Where a gable needs a second panel above the first, put Z-flashing over the lower panel's top edge before the upper one.
  4. Leave out the nails in the 8" of siding above each door and window head for now, so the head flashing can slip up behind it in the trim step.
  5. Cut the door, window and gable vent openings with a circular saw from inside, along the framing, after the panel is nailed; finish the corners with a handsaw.
  6. The corners get 1x4 corner boards in the trim step; leave the panel edges flush with the corner.

16. Rake trim and roofing

The rake boards go over the siding, and the rake drip edge over them, so they wait until the siding is on.

  1. Rake: a 1x4 board up each gable edge over the siding, its top edge tight under the roof sheathing, nailed with 8d galvanized finish nails into the end rafter.
  2. Drip edge along the rakes (gable edges), over the rake board and on top of the underlayment.
  3. Shingles: a starter strip along the eave, then courses at the exposure printed on the bundle (often 5-5/8"), staggered as the wrapper shows, four to six nails per shingle as the maker specifies. Finish the ridge with cap shingles.

17. Trim, vents, door and windows

  1. Soffit: fit the vented soffit (2 panels) to the underside of the rafter tails, from the siding to the back of the fascia.
  2. Gable vents: set each one in its opening over a bead of exterior sealant and screw through its flange; screen side in.
  3. Door and windows: follow each maker's instructions. Flash the sill and sides with the flashing tape, shim plumb and square in the rough opening, and leave the gap they specify around the frame.
  4. Case each door and window: sides first, then the head across the top lapping the sides, and for windows a sill board under.
  5. Head flashing: slide a length of Z-flashing up behind the siding over each head casing, its lip over the casing's front edge, so water runs off and not behind it. Then nail off the siding above it.
  6. Corner boards: nail the 1x4 corner boards (8) with 8d galvanized finish nails, two every 16"; on each corner the board on the long wall laps the edge of the end-wall board. The cut list is on the Cut sheet tab.
  7. Caulk: run exterior caulk where each casing meets the door or window frame and the siding, and along the inside edge of every corner board. Leave the bottom edge of the head casing and the weep gaps the window maker shows open so water can drain.

18. Build door o1 on site

  1. Cut the door skin from the siding you cut out of the opening: 38" x 77-1/2", which leaves a 1/4" gap all round.
  2. Cut the frame from 2x4s: two stiles 77-1/2" (mark T), three rails 31" (mark Z), and keep the braces (mark W) long until you scribe them.
  3. Lay the frame out flat on a flat floor: the stiles along the long edges and the rails across the top, middle and bottom between them.
  4. Braces: in each bay, hold a 2x4 corner to corner and scribe it; the ends come out about 47 degrees. The low end of each brace goes on the hinge side so the brace holds the latch side up.
  5. Run construction adhesive along the frame, lay the skin on it face up, square its edges to the frame, and screw through the skin into the frame every 8" with #8 x 1-5/8" exterior screws (on the list).
  6. Hang: three 8" tee hinges, the straps across the rails, the leaves on the casing. The casing stands 3/4" proud of the door face, so pack each strap out with a 1x4 trim offcut the length of the strap, screwed to the door, and fix the strap through it into the rail. Shim the door to keep the 1/4" gap while you screw the hinges. Then the pull and the hasp.
  7. Paint or seal the skin edges and all six faces of the door before it sees rain.

19. Ground anchors

The shed weighs about 1,790 lb (framing and sheets only, counted low). Wind on the long side would tip it at about 6 psf, so it is held down by four 48" ground anchors (Tie Down 59802): at 20 psf each windward strap pulls about 1390 lb against the kit's 3,150 lb working load in soil class 4a (loose to medium-dense sand, firm to stiff clay) or firmer. The anchors hold it down and stop it sliding; the blocks only carry its weight.

  1. Call 811 (or your local utility locator) before you dig: an auger anchor goes 4 ft into the ground.
  2. Check the soil with a soil test probe (Tie Down sells one). The 48" anchors are rated for soil class 4a (loose to medium-dense sand, firm to stiff clay) or firmer. Softer soil than that is outside the maker's rating: ask a local engineer or use concrete piers.
  3. At each corner, set the anchor out from the outer skid along the corner's diagonal, about as far out as the top of the skid is above the ground, so the strap runs down at about 45 degrees. Screw it in at a slight back angle (about 10 degrees, away from the shed, per the maker's sheet) until the head is flush with the ground; dig a hole two-thirds of its length first if the ground is hard, then backfill and tamp every 6".
  4. Wrap the strap round the skid just inside the corner block, a full turn starting over the top, as the maker's sheet wraps a beam. Run it to the anchor head, cut it 12" to 15" past the slotted bolt, and wind it on with a 15/16" wrench: four or five turns, tight, then the nut.
  5. Tie the floor frame to the outer skid at each corner with 1 H2.5A tie (4 on the list), joist to skid, every nail hole filled. Without them the anchors hold down the skids and not the shed.

Check:

  • Every strap tight, with no twist where it wraps the skid.
  • Each anchor head flush with the ground and the strap running straight to it.

20. Final checks

  1. Walk the roof edges for nails that missed rafters, and the walls for siding nails that missed studs.
  2. Check that every rafter has its connector and its hanger, with every nail hole filled with the nail its maker names.
  3. Keep the gravel clear of the siding, and slope the ground away from the pad.
  4. Re-tension the anchor straps after the first few weeks and each spring, as the ground settles.
