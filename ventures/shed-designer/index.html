    Options made for you

    Close variations of the design you are editing, each framed, priced and checked. They keep your size, doors, windows, colours and wall framing, and change one thing a builder would weigh. Pick one to add it to your designs.

    Bring the price down, same size

    Counts your time at the hourly rate you set (in the buying settings) as well as materials. Tries cheaper off-the-shelf parts and settings one at a time (stock doors and windows, siding, roofing, the foundation and its gravel, the subfloor, overhang, pitch and spacing), keeps your footprint and the ridge and rafter-seat choices you made, and keeps a change only if the shed still passes every check and the peak rises by no more than 6". It lists each saving so you can take some and leave others.

    Simplify the build

    Searches small changes (roof pitch, overhang, rafter and stud spacing, connectors or notches, ridge type) for the cheapest build, the one with the fewest saw cuts, and a balance of the two. It counts every saw cut and every angled cut and notch, and keeps only designs that pass every check.

    Compare your designs

    Every design you make is kept here with its cost at your prices. Use "+ New design" to start another, or "Copy this design" to try a change without losing the original.

    Questions

    Does it use the real size of a 2x4?

    Yes. Every board is framed at its dressed size: a 2x4 is 1-1/2 by 3-1/2 inches, a 2x6 is 1-1/2 by 5-1/2, a 2x8 is 1-1/2 by 7-1/4, a 2x10 is 1-1/2 by 9-1/4 and a 2x12 is 1-1/2 by 11-1/4. Stud lengths, plate stack heights, rafter plumb cuts and the parts list all use those numbers.

    How does it decide which board lengths to buy?

    Starting prices are Home Depot listings seen on 2026-10-29, each linked. It treats the order as a cutting-stock problem: every piece is placed into 8, 10, 12, 14 or 16 ft boards (18 and 20 ft if you tick them) to minimise cost at the prices you type, with your saw kerf between cuts. It also proves a lower bound, by solving the problem's linear relaxation, and prints it beside the result, so you can see how close the order is to the cheapest possible; often it is exactly the cheapest.

    Is the design checked for strength or against building code?

    Not by an engineer. The Structure tab runs allowable-stress (NDS) checks on the floor joists, rafters, ridge beam and posts, headers and wall studs under the snow and wind loads you set, the kind of check a span table encodes, and the tests check that no piece floats or overlaps another. It does not check connections, uplift, racking, the foundation or your local code. Many places need a permit for a shed over a certain size; ask your building department.

    Can I open the plans in CAD?

    Yes. Each sheet downloads as DXF (full size, in inches, with layers for framing, outlines, hidden lines, openings, dimensions and text) or SVG, and the whole set prints from the browser.

    Is my design uploaded anywhere?

    No. Everything runs in your browser. Your designs are saved in this browser's local storage so they are there next time (you can turn that off), and a shared link carries the design inside the link itself.
