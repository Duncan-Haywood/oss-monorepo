      Common questions

      Why does this ask how many days the traps are out?

      Because every one of those days is a return trip. Traps that are set have to be checked, and most states require it every 24 hours. A five-day raccoon removal is five more round trips with a technician in the truck, on top of the install visit and the trip back to pull the one-way door. That recurring visit is the single largest cost a flat removal fee omits, and it is why matching a competitor’s headline number loses money by day three.

      Why is height a multiplier instead of a line item?

      Because it changes how long the same footage takes, not what it costs in material. Sixty feet of soffit-to-roof line reachable from a step ladder is a different job from the same sixty feet at a two-story eave on a steep pitch in a harness, and different again where nothing but a lift will reach it. Per-foot pricing that ignores the tier is wrong by a factor, in the same direction, every time. The multiplier applies only to the exclusion and one-way-door work — it does not touch trap checks in the yard or decontamination in the attic, which take the same time however high the roof is.

      Why does the warranty reserve only cover part of the job?

      Because a callback is somebody returning to a hole that reopened, not to an attic that was already cleaned. The reserve here is taken against the work the warranty actually covers — the exclusion labor, the exclusion material and the devices — rather than against the whole invoice. Reserving against decontamination and trapping would inflate the price for exposure you do not carry; reserving against nothing at all means the first callback comes out of the profit on the next job.

      Does this tell me when I am allowed to exclude bats?

      No, and it must not be read that way. It flags that a job is scheduled in a month when the species you picked commonly has flightless or immobile young, because sealing the entry then can trap them inside — which is both an animal-welfare problem and, in many states, prohibited. The actual window is set by your state wildlife agency, varies by latitude and species, and changes. Treat the flag as a prompt to go and check with your agency before you schedule, never as a determination that any date is or is not permitted.

      Why does a bat job show no traps?

      Because bats are not trapped. They are excluded live through one-way devices that let them leave and not return, and the job is the seal plus the device removal trip, not a trapping cycle. The calculator flags a bat or bird job that has been given traps, because that is a trapping rate sheet applied to an animal nobody traps — and it usually means the visit count, which is the number that decides whether the bid is sound, was built for the wrong job.

      Is any of this uploaded?

      No. There is no account and the page makes no network requests — it runs entirely in your browser. The address, the species, your labor rate, your footage and the price worked out from them are never sent anywhere and never appear in a usage count. The optional counter records only which features were used and the day, stored on your own device, and it switches itself off for browsers sending Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

      Is this legal, wildlife-regulation, or insurance advice?

      No. It is arithmetic on the figures you type. It makes no determination about what your state’s wildlife code requires, how often traps must be checked, whether a species may be excluded on a given date, what licence or permit a job needs, or what to charge. Verify every figure and every requirement against your own costs, your state wildlife agency, and a qualified professional before sending a bid.
