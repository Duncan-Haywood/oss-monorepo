What this does not cover

  • Not a full eligibility determination. This does not check for other disqualifying coverage — a general-purpose health FSA, Medicare enrollment, being claimed as a dependent, or a spouse's non-HDHP plan can eliminate HSA eligibility even during otherwise-eligible months.
  • Family limit is a household total, not per-spouse. If both spouses are HSA-eligible under family coverage, the $8,750 family limit is split between their two HSAs by agreement; this tool computes only the household total.
  • The catch-up cannot go into a spouse's HSA. A spouse who is 55+ and wants the catch-up contribution needs their own HSA in their own name.
  • Point-in-time 2026 figures. These limits are re-indexed annually (except the $1,000 catch-up, fixed by statute); confirm the current year's figures before relying on this in a later year.

Common questions

What is the 2026 HSA contribution limit?

$4,400 for self-only HDHP coverage or $8,750 for family coverage, per IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-19. Those 55 and older who are not yet enrolled in Medicare can contribute an additional $1,000 catch-up, which is fixed by statute rather than adjusted for inflation each year.

What deductible and out-of-pocket limits does a plan need to qualify as an HDHP in 2026?

The deductible must be at least $1,700 for self-only coverage or $3,400 for family coverage, and the out-of-pocket maximum must not exceed $8,500 for self-only or $17,000 for family. A plan outside these bounds is not HSA-qualified, whatever it is marketed as.

How is the HSA limit prorated if I was not covered all year?

By whole months of HDHP eligibility: the annual limit multiplied by eligible months divided by 12. Six months of self-only eligibility in 2026, for example, gives a prorated limit of $2,200 rather than the full $4,400.

What is the last-month rule, and what is the catch?

If you are HSA-eligible on December 1 of the tax year, you may contribute the full annual limit even if you were eligible for only part of the year. In exchange, you must remain HSA-eligible through December 31 of the following year (the testing period). If eligibility lapses before then for a reason other than death or disability, the amount you contributed beyond what proration would have allowed becomes taxable income in the year eligibility lapsed, plus a 10% additional tax.
