What this does not cover

  • Regular method only. The annualized income installment method (Form 2210 Schedule AI) can lower the penalty for uneven income and is not modelled.
  • One interest rate. The IRS rate can change each quarter; this uses the single rate you enter for the whole period.
  • Withholding is treated as paid evenly on the four due dates, the Form 2210 default.
  • Farmers, fishers, waivers (disaster, retirement, disability) and state penalties are not modelled.
  • Estimate, not a bill. The IRS computes the final figure on your return; use Form 2210 and its instructions to confirm.

Common questions

How is the underpayment penalty on estimated taxes calculated?

For each of the four installments (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15) the IRS takes your required payment, subtracts what you paid by the due date, and charges the underpayment rate (federal short-term rate plus 3 points, 7% for recent quarters) on the shortfall for each day it stayed unpaid, until you paid it or the return due date. A payment is applied to the oldest unpaid installment first.

What is the safe harbor to avoid the penalty?

Pay at least the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax. If last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately), last year's tax must be multiplied by 110%. There is also no penalty if you owe under $1,000 after withholding and credits.

Does withholding count the same as estimated payments?

Yes, and it is treated as paid evenly on each due date unless you choose otherwise, even if it was withheld late in the year. That is why raising Q4 withholding can erase a penalty that estimated payments could not.

Can I reduce the penalty if my income came late in the year?

Yes. Form 2210 Schedule AI, the annualized income installment method, matches required payments to when you earned income. This calculator uses the regular method only, so it can overstate the penalty for seasonal or lumpy income.
