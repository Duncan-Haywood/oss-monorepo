How much does biohazard cleanup cost?

Less than the scene's size decides, and more than "day rate plus a per-pound hazmat fee" captures. Two costs move the price on their own terms — regulated waste billed per whole container, and PPE billed per decon cycle — and both are worked out below with this calculator's own verified numbers.

A small scene still pays close to the container's full price

A licensed regulated-waste hauler charges per container picked up, not per pound. On this calculator's defaults, a 40-pound-capacity container costs $110 to have hauled — whether it holds 40 pounds or 4. A small scene generating just 15 pounds of waste still needs one whole container, so its disposal line is $110.00, not the $41.25 a linear per-pound rate ($110 / 40 lb × 15 lb) would suggest. That gap is about 167% of the naive figure, and it does not shrink for a small job — it grows, because a small job has less other cost to absorb it.

That is a coincidence of the numbers, not a rule — a waste estimate that lands just past a container boundary (41 lb, or 121 lb) pays for a whole extra container's worth of capacity nobody uses. Estimate waste generously rather than exactly on a boundary, because rounding only ever runs one direction: up.

PPE is priced per re-entry, not per technician

Used PPE cannot be re-donned, so a technician who leaves the contaminated area for a break, to run equipment out, or across a shift change needs a fresh set to go back in. On this calculator's defaults, a 2-technician crew at one decon cycle each needs 2 PPE sets ($76.00 total). The same crew at two decon cycles each — one break, say — needs 4 sets ($152.00), exactly double, because PPE scales with re-entries, not headcount. A per-head PPE estimate misses that entire second cycle.

What this is not

This is arithmetic on the rates and estimates you type. It does not know your regulated-waste hauler's actual per-container price, your state or OSHA bloodborne-pathogen decon requirements, or whether a job is billable to insurance. Replace every default with your own hauler invoice and PPE order before quoting.

Common questions

How much does biohazard cleanup cost?

On this calculator's own verified defaults (2 technicians, 5 hours each, 55 lb of regulated waste), a job prices around $1,374.67 — direct cost $1,031.00 plus a 25% target margin. A smaller scene (1 technician, 2 hours, 15 lb of waste) still prices around $697.33, mostly because disposal and PPE do not shrink in proportion to the scene.

Why does a small scene still cost close to a minimum job fee?

Because regulated-waste disposal is billed per whole container, not per pound. A 15-pound job still needs one 40-pound-capacity container at the full per-container price ($110 on this calculator's defaults) — the same charge a 40-pound job would pay — so a per-pound estimate ($41.25 for 15 lb) undercounts a small job by a much larger percentage than a large one.

How much does a multi-day or multi-break job add in PPE cost alone?

On this calculator's defaults, raising decon cycles from 1 to 2 per technician (a break, an equipment run, or a shift change that forces re-entry) raises PPE cost from $76.00 to $152.00 for the same 2-technician crew — $76.00 more, because PPE is priced per re-entry, not per head, and used PPE cannot be re-donned.

Does a bigger waste estimate cost proportionally more?

Not exactly. Raising waste from 55 lb to 155 lb on this calculator's defaults raises disposal from $220.00 (2 containers) to $440.00 (4 containers) — exactly double, because 155 lb happens to need exactly double the containers. A waste estimate that lands just over a container boundary, rather than exactly on one, pays for a whole extra container's worth of unused capacity.
