FOR FENCE CONTRACTORS

Bid the posts and the gates, not the linear feet.

Price a fence install the way the job actually costs: posts from your spacing, heavier terminals at ends, corners, and gates, tear-out, and the extra hours clay or rock will eat. You get the job price, what it works out to per foot and per labor hour, and the number below which the job loses money.

The fence

One row per run — front vs back, or wood vs chain-link. Pick a material to start from common figures, then replace every cost and hour with numbers from your last invoice and your last timed job.

Site and tear-out

Your costs

Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off. Underestimating it — or bidding rocky dirt at a flat per-foot number — is the usual reason a fence job that looked profitable was not.
