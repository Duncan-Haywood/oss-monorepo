Common questions

What percentage should I charge?

Twenty to thirty percent is a useful starting range for many services, but the right amount depends on the price, preparation time, and how difficult the slot is to refill. This calculator shows your assumption; it does not decide for you. See the deposit, balance, and secured-cash numbers at five common percentages on a $150 service.

Is a deposit a cancellation fee?

No. A deposit is an upfront payment applied to the service total. Your refund and cancellation terms should be disclosed before booking and checked against local rules and payment-provider requirements.

How is the deposit amount calculated?

Deposit equals your service price times the deposit percentage you enter, divided by 100. The balance due at the appointment is simply the service price minus that deposit — there is no separate processing fee or markup added by this tool.

Why does the calculator ask for a booking rate?

Requiring a deposit can turn away some prospective customers who would have booked without one, so the calculator asks what share of your usual weekly bookings you expect to keep once a deposit is required. It multiplies that rate against your weekly bookings, then by the deposit, then by 52 weeks, to model the cash a deposit policy secures over a year — not just the deposit on a single booking.

What happens if a customer cancels?

The policy this tool drafts treats the free-cancellation notice window you set as the cutoff: cancellations made at least that many hours before the appointment may be refunded or transferred, while cancellations with less notice, or no-shows, may forfeit the deposit. The exact terms are yours to set and should be checked against your local rules and payment provider's requirements before you publish them.

Does this tool recommend a deposit percentage for me?

No. It is deterministic math with no built-in advice or recommended percent — it turns the price and percentage you choose into a deposit amount, balance due, and secured-cash estimate, and drafts policy wording from your inputs. The percentage decision stays yours.
