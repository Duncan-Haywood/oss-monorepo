What percentage deposit should you charge? Here is the arithmetic at five common rates.

Every service business asks this before setting a policy. There is no universally correct percentage — the ones businesses actually charge range from about 15% up to 40% or more for high-cost or high-no-show services. What changes, predictably, is the dollar deposit, the balance still due, and how much cash a year of bookings secures. See the numbers first, then decide.

Deposit and balance move in lockstep with the percentage: deposit is simply price × percent, and balance is whatever is left of the price. The number that is easy to miss is the last column — the spread between a 15% and a 40% deposit on secured cash is $21,762 a year on this one example, before any change in how many people book at all.

Two things push most small service businesses toward the low end of that range: a lower deposit is a smaller ask of someone who has not experienced the service yet, and it reads as less risky to a first-time customer deciding whether to commit. Two things push toward the high end: a service with expensive material or prep cost that is wasted on a no-show, and a customer base that already trusts the business enough that the percentage rarely changes their decision to book.

Common questions

What percentage deposit should I charge for an appointment?

There is no single correct percentage — 15% to 40% are all common in practice. The trade-off is mechanical: a higher percentage secures more cash per booking and covers more of your cost if someone no-shows, but it is also more money a new customer has to commit before they have used your service, which can lower how many people complete the booking. Pick a starting percentage, watch your actual completion rate for a few weeks, and adjust.

Does a higher deposit percentage always secure more money over a year?

Only if your booking rate stays the same, and this calculator does not assume that for you. Raising the percentage from 25% to 40% on a $150 service raises the deposit from $37.50 to $60, which — at an unchanged 93% booking rate on 12 weekly bookings — raises secured cash from $21,762 to $34,819.20 a year. If a higher deposit actually turns away bookings, you have to lower the booking-rate assumption yourself to see the real comparison; the tool will not do that adjustment for you.
