This calculator vs. Jobber

Two different shapes: Jobber is an account-based platform for running a service company's whole operation, including scheduling a storm-response dispatch and invoicing the customer once a job is priced. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this ice dam removal job cost, given its footage, crew, and minimum service charge, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

Jobber is a field service management platform used by home and trade service businesses — lawn care, cleaning, HVAC, roofing and gutter, and other seasonal or storm-response trades among them — to run client management and online quoting, scheduling and crew or driver dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client-facing hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. Jobber's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific feature list, plan names, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page. Public descriptions of that kind of field service software describe scheduling a job, dispatching a crew, and invoicing and collecting payment once a price already exists; none describe working out whether a short job's per-foot rate card actually covers a two-person crew's largely fixed dispatch cost, or reconciling a job's price against the actual on-site hours once the ice turns out thicker than the phone estimate assumed — which is what this calculator does.

This calculator does none of what Jobber does. It does not manage a customer list, schedule a crew's storm-response route, or collect a payment. It takes linear feet of roof edge, crew size and hourly pay, travel hours and miles, steamer fuel and equipment cost, an emergency/storm-dispatch premium, overhead and target margin, and a minimum service charge, and turns that into the price to quote, the break-even rate per foot for this job's specific footage, and the profit if the job goes as estimated — plus, once the crew is back down the ladder, a check of the actual on-site hours against that same breakeven, so a per-foot rate that only survives on a short job because of the minimum charge shows up clearly, instead of as a surprise on the invoice. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Jobber?

They do different jobs, and an ice dam removal contractor of any size will likely end up touching both eventually. Jobber is a field service management platform used across home and trade service businesses — including seasonal and storm-response trades — to run client management and quoting, scheduling and crew dispatch, invoicing and online payments, and a client hub, tied to one account used on an ongoing basis across every job the company runs. This calculator does one narrower thing: it takes a job's linear feet of roof edge, crew size and pay, travel, steamer fuel and equipment cost, an emergency-dispatch premium, and a minimum service charge, and turns that into the price to quote, the break-even rate per foot for that specific footage, and — once the crew is back down the ladder — the real profit and margin the job actually left. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A contractor already scheduling storm-response dispatch, invoicing customers, and taking payments across a crew or a fleet of trucks is better served by Jobber or a comparable field service platform; a contractor who wants an instant, private answer to what one job should cost before it is quoted, and whether a short job's rate card actually clears its own fixed cost, is in the right place here.
