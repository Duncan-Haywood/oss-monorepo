This calculator vs. working out the 2026 charitable deduction by hand

The arithmetic looks simple — a fixed cap on one side, a percentage on the other. The part that goes wrong by hand is which rule applies to which path, and in what order.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Applying the new 0.5%-of-AGI floor to the non-itemizer deduction too. The floor only applies if you itemize on Schedule A — the new $1,000/$2,000 above-the-line deduction for non-itemizers is not reduced by it at all.

What's the second most common mistake?

Treating the $1,000/$2,000 non-itemizer cap as a per-gift limit rather than a total-for-the-year cap. It is the maximum deduction for ALL your cash giving to public charities combined in the year, not per donation.

What arithmetic mistake gets made most often?

Forgetting the order the limits apply in. The existing 60%-of-AGI ceiling caps what could ever be deductible first; the new 0.5%-of-AGI floor is then subtracted from that capped figure. Applying the floor to your full, uncapped giving figure overstates how much the ceiling costs you.

What eligibility mistake gets made most often?

Assuming a donor-advised fund contribution qualifies for the non-itemizer deduction. It does not — only direct cash gifts to public charities qualify for the new $1,000/$2,000 above-the-line deduction.

Does this calculator know my complete tax situation?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page knows your other itemized deductions, whether itemizing beats your standard deduction overall, or the separate top-bracket itemized-deduction limitation.
