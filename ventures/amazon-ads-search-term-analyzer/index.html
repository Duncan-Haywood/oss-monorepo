The model, and what it leaves out

  • Per term: rows are combined by campaign, ad group and search term, so the same query in two ad groups stays separate (negatives are set per campaign or ad group). ACOS is spend over sales; click-to-order rate is orders over clicks.
  • Reconciles. Spend on terms with orders, plus the proven, overspent and watch tiers, adds up to your total spend, and the three tiers add up to the no-order figure.
  • Proven no-order spend means the fewest clicks n with (1 − rate)n under 0.05 at the account's click-to-order rate. Overspent is more than one average order can carry at the reference ACOS (average order value × ACOS) with none; watch is everything else.
  • Reference ACOS is your break-even if you enter it, otherwise the account's own ACOS, which can itself be unprofitable.
  • Harvest candidate: at least the minimum orders, ACOS at least 20% under the reference, and not already an exact-match keyword. It describes the report period only.
  • Not modelled: the attribution window and delayed orders, orders placed for a different advertised SKU, organic rank and halo sales, lifetime value and repeat purchases, seasonality, bid and budget changes, relevance of a term to your product, and what a negative keyword does to reach. Amazon may mask or omit some search terms.

Common questions

How do I find wasted spend in an Amazon Ads search term report?

Download the Sponsored Products search term report from the Ads console (Measurement and reporting, Sponsored ads reports, Search term, CSV) and drop it here. The tool adds up spend on customer search terms that got clicks but no orders and grades it: proven when the term had enough clicks that, at your account's own click-to-order rate, seeing no order would be chance less than 5% of the time; overspent when it spent more than one average order can carry at your break-even ACOS; watch when there is too little data. Nothing is uploaded.

Which search terms should I add as negative keywords?

The tool lists proven and overspent no-order terms, per campaign and ad group, and lets you download them as a negative exact list. Terms in the watch tier are left out on purpose: a handful of clicks with no order is not evidence. Check each one still fits your product before adding it. That is a judgement the file cannot make.

What is break-even ACOS and why enter it?

Break-even ACOS is the share of a sale you can spend on ads before you lose money: your margin before advertising. Entering it lets the tool judge each term against what a sale really pays for instead of against your account's own average ACOS, which can itself be unprofitable.

Which columns and file formats does it read?

Customer Search Term, Spend, Clicks and an orders column (7, 14 or 30 day total orders) are required. Campaign Name, Ad Group Name, Targeting, Match Type, Impressions, sales, Currency and the report dates are used when present. Column names are matched loosely, dates may be month names, month-first, day-first or ISO, and comma, tab and semicolon files in UTF-8 or UTF-16 are read. Rows that cannot be read are listed with a line number.
