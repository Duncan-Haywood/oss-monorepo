This calculator vs. a spreadsheet

A lot of landlords and tenants already reach for a spreadsheet at move-out: one column for each deduction, a subtotal, a formula that subtracts it from the deposit. That arithmetic is trivial and a spreadsheet does it fine. What it does not do on its own is flag the mistakes that actually cause disputes: an unlabeled deduction, a balance that has quietly gone negative, an interest formula entered once and never rechecked, or a statement that drifted from the sheet because it was retyped into an email by hand.

Common questions

Isn't a security deposit refund just deposit minus deductions?

The subtraction is trivial and a spreadsheet does it fine. What a plain spreadsheet does not do on its own is flag a deduction with no description, catch the case where deductions exceed the deposit (a balance owed, not a refund), or keep the copy you send the other party from silently drifting away from the number your own sheet shows — because that copy usually gets retyped by hand into an email.

Why is prorating deposit interest harder in a spreadsheet than it looks?

Interest is deposit times the jurisdiction's annual rate times days held over 365, and every deposit calculator or landlord's own sheet has to encode that formula once and then trust it every time a rate or a day count changes. Enter a rate with no day count, or a day count with no rate, and a spreadsheet cell just returns zero with no explanation — this calculator flags that combination explicitly instead of returning a silent zero.

What's actually different about the itemized statement this produces?

The statement is generated from the exact settlement figures already on screen, not retyped from them. Editing a deduction after calculating, without recalculating, disables the copy button rather than handing out a statement that disagrees with the table above it — a spreadsheet-to-email copy has no equivalent safeguard, because the retype is a separate, unchecked step.

Does this replace a landlord's own record-keeping spreadsheet?

No. A spreadsheet or ledger that tracks every tenant, every deposit, and every move-out date over years is still the right tool for that ongoing record. This page answers one settlement at a time — what does this deposit refund to, or what does this partial month prorate to — and nothing it computes is saved anywhere once you leave the page.
