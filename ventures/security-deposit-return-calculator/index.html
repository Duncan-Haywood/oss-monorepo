What this does and doesn't do

Both calculators total the numbers you enter — a deposit amount and a list of deductions, or a monthly rent and a day count. Neither one knows your state's itemized-statement deadline, deposit limit, interest rules, or what specifically counts as normal wear and tear versus damage in your jurisdiction. Treat the output as the arithmetic behind your own itemization or dispute, not a legal determination.

Common questions

How do you calculate a security deposit refund?

Add up every itemized deduction (unpaid rent, cleaning, repairs, or other charges) and subtract the total from the deposit amount. If the deductions are less than the deposit, the difference is the refund due. If deductions exceed the deposit, the difference is a balance owed instead.

What is prorated rent and how is it calculated?

Prorated rent covers a partial month, usually the first or last month of a lease. It is calculated as a daily rate (monthly rent divided by the number of days in that month) multiplied by the number of days actually occupied.

Can a landlord deduct for normal wear and tear?

Most landlord-tenant frameworks distinguish normal wear and tear (ordinary aging from everyday use, generally not deductible) from actual damage beyond normal use (generally deductible) — but the exact definition, itemization deadline, and any deposit limits vary by state and by the lease itself. Check your local law and lease before finalizing a number.

Does this calculator include interest on the deposit?

Only if you enter a rate. Some states and cities require a landlord to pay interest on a held deposit (for example New York for buildings with six or more units, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Connecticut, and Maryland), at a rate the jurisdiction sets and that changes over time. This calculator does not know whether your jurisdiction requires it or what the current rate is — enter the rate that applies to you and how many days the deposit was held, and it adds the accrued interest to the refund. Leave the rate blank if none applies.

Can I copy the itemization out to send to the other party?

Yes. After you calculate the refund, “Copy itemized statement” puts the deposit, every named deduction and its amount, the total, and the refund or balance owed on your clipboard as plain text — ready to paste into an email, a letter, or a message. It is the same arithmetic shown on this page, so the copy can never disagree with the table. The note saying this is arithmetic and not a legal determination of what may be deducted is copied along with it, because the person receiving the statement never sees this page. If your browser blocks clipboard access, the statement appears in a text box you can select and copy instead.
