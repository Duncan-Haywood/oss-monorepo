This calculator vs. the flat “8-10%” rule of thumb

“Kickstarter takes 8-10%” is the number every guide repeats. It is also a single percentage applied to the whole raise, with no line for how many backers it came from — which is exactly what changes the real number.

What each approach actually involves

The flat rule of thumb takes one number from the 8-10% range and multiplies the whole campaign total by it. One number, one multiplication, done — and the same guess for a campaign funded by 150 backers or by 1,250.

This calculator prices Kickstarter's two real fees separately: a 5% platform fee on the total, and a per-pledge processing fee that is 3% + $0.20 for pledges of $10 or more, or 5% + $0.05 for pledges under $10. Because the flat component is charged per pledge, not per dollar, the calculator asks for the total raised and the number of backers, so the average pledge size — and its effect on the real rate — is part of the answer instead of invisible.

Common questions

What is the flat “8-10%” rule of thumb this page compares against?

Picking one number in the 8-10% range that guides quote for Kickstarter's combined fees, and multiplying the whole campaign total by it. It is a one-line estimate that treats the fee as a flat percentage of the raise, with no line for how many backers it came from.

How much can the flat rule of thumb get a real campaign wrong?

On this calculator's own default example -- a $10,000 campaign -- a flat 9% guess (the midpoint of “8-10%”) prices the fee at $900.00 and the net payout at $9,100.00 no matter how many backers it came from. At the calculator's shipped default of 150 backers ($66.67 average pledge), the real fee is $830.00 and the real net payout is $9,170.00 -- the guess overstated the fee by $70.00. Spread across 1,250 backers instead ($8.00 average pledge, the micro-pledge tier), the real fee is $1,062.50 and the real net payout is $8,937.50 -- the same guess now understated the fee by $162.50. Same $10,000 raised, same flat guess, a $232.50 swing in which direction it is wrong.

Why does average pledge size change the effective rate at all?

Because Kickstarter's payment-processing fee has a flat per-pledge component ($0.20, or $0.05 under $10) on top of its percentage -- charged once per pledge, not once per dollar. More, smaller pledges spread that flat charge across more transactions, so the same total raised pays more in processing the more backers it took to raise it. A flat percentage guess has no way to see that, because it only ever looks at the total.

Does the real rate ever fall outside the quoted “8-10%” range entirely?

Yes. The calculator's own micro-pledge example above -- 1,250 backers averaging $8.00 each -- lands at a 10.625% effective rate, past the top of the range most summaries quote. A campaign built on very small pledges is not a rare edge case for something like a sticker tier or a $5 digital thank-you, and “8-10%” undersells its real cost.

Does this page assert Kickstarter's current fee schedule?

The 5% platform fee and the per-pledge processing rates (3% + $0.20 standard, 5% + $0.05 under $10) are constants lifted from Kickstarter's own published US fee schedule, not a live fetch. Confirm the current schedule at kickstarter.com/help/fees before relying on either this calculator or any rule of thumb for a real campaign.
