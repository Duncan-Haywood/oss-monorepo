# Weekly planner (periodic routing), measured 2026-11-24

Rerun: `node eval/weekly-check.mjs` (about 80 s). Engine: `weekly.mjs`; page layer: `week.mjs`; page: `weekly.html`.

## What was measured

1. **Exact check.** 40 random small weeks (6 customers, 5 days, one or two vehicles a day, a 140-minute shift so
   overtime binds, half the customers twice a week) against enumeration of every pattern choice, every split of each
   day's customers across the vehicles and every visit order. The same check runs in `weekly.test.mjs` on 45 more
   (30 with one vehicle and no shift cap, 15 with two vehicles and a cap): 85 of 85 equal the optimum.
2. **Invented service books.** One depot in a 20 x 20 mile area, travel at 30 mph with a 1.3 road factor, 15 to
   35 service minutes, a quarter of customers twice a week, an 8-hour shift, five working days, five books per size.
   Three weeks are routed by the same engine and compared:
   - *grown days*: each customer on a random allowed pattern, which is how a book looks when each new customer took
     whatever day had room;
   - *pie slice*: customers sorted by angle round the depot and cut into one sector per day of equal load, the usual
     hand method;
   - *chosen*: the planner picks the days.

Exact check: 40 of 40 small weeks equal the enumerated optimum.

## Reading it

- Choosing days is worth far more than ordering stops well inside days that were chosen badly: on the same routing
  engine, a third or more of the week's driving goes away against a grown book.
- Pie slicing is a fair hand method and gets a good part of the way; the planner still cuts 12 to 26% more and ends
  with no overtime where the slices ran up to 4 hours over a week.
- Search variance is real: five seeds on one book differ by 2 to 4% (on the page's 48-customer sample with one
  driver, travel ranged 404.5 to 426.5 minutes over five seeds while tuning). Tight shifts (88% of the week's
  capacity used on the sample) are where it sticks; a low overtime charge early in the search, rising to full by 70%
  of the iterations, was the change that helped most (5 seeds stuck at 426.5 before it).
