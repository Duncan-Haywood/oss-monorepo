Method and citation

STL (Cleveland, Cleveland, McRae & Terpenning 1990) writes the series as Y = T + S + R and finds T and S by alternating two smoothers, each a loess fit (locally weighted linear regression with tricube weights; Cleveland 1979). In each pass of the inner loop it takes the trend off; smooths each cycle-subseries (every January together, every February together, ...) with a loess window of ns cycles, extending it one cycle past each end; removes any slow drift that leaked into that pattern with moving averages of length np, np and 3 followed by a loess of window nl; and smooths what is left after taking the seasonal off with a loess of window nt to get the new trend.

Robust mode adds the outer loop: after each fit, every point gets a bisquare weight (1 - (|R| / h)²)² with h = 6 × median |R|, so points far from trend plus season count for less (or nothing) in the next fit. The defaults follow statsmodels: ns = 7, nt the smallest odd integer at or above 1.5 n_p / (1 - 1.5 / n_s), nl the smallest odd integer above the period, 5 inner passes, or 2 inner and 15 outer when robust.

The strength of seasonality is max(0, 1 - Var(R) / Var(S + R)) and the strength of trend max(0, 1 - Var(R) / Var(T + R)) (Wang, Smith & Hyndman 2006, in the form of Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice, section 4.3). A point is listed as standing out when its remainder is at least 3.5 robust standard deviations (1.4826 × the median absolute deviation) from the median remainder.

Verified by tests in the source against statsmodels 0.15.0's STL: trend, seasonal, remainder and robustness weights agree to 1e-9 or better on nine cases (the CO₂ example with the default, robust, wide-window and degree-0-with-jumps settings; daily data with planted outliers, plain and robust; hourly data with a 24-step period; a quarterly series with even windows skipped by jumps; and a short series whose seasonal window is longer than each subseries).

Limits

  • Evenly spaced steps. STL treats rows as equally spaced. Missing dates are not invented: add them as rows with an empty value and they are interpolated; otherwise a gap shifts the cycle.
  • One whole-number period. A year of weeks is 52.18, and daily data with both a weekly and a yearly cycle has two seasonalities; STL fits one. Run it twice, or remove one cycle first.
  • Additive. The seasonal swing is in the same units as the data. If the swing grows with the level (sales that double and whose December peak doubles too), decompose the logarithm of the values instead.
  • Not a forecast. The trend near the ends uses only one side of the window and moves as new data arrive.
  • Not modelled: the paper's optional post-smoothing of the seasonal component, calendar effects (trading days, moving holidays such as Easter), and the official X-13ARIMA-SEATS adjustment that statistics agencies publish.

Questions

What does STL decomposition do?

It splits a series into three parts that add back up to it: a slowly moving trend, a seasonal pattern that repeats every cycle (every year for monthly data, every week for daily data) and is allowed to change slowly, and a remainder, what neither explains. STL stands for Seasonal-Trend decomposition using Loess.

How do I seasonally adjust my data?

Subtract the seasonal component from each value. The tool does this for you: the seasonally adjusted series is trend plus remainder, so a month that is up only because it is always up at that time of year no longer looks like growth.

What period should I use?

The number of steps in one cycle: 12 for monthly data with a yearly cycle, 4 for quarterly, 7 for daily data with a weekly cycle, 24 for hourly data with a daily cycle. With a date column the tool infers it; type another number to look for a different cycle. You need at least two full cycles of data, and three or more give a steadier result.

What do the seasonal and trend windows change?

The seasonal window is how many cycles each point of the seasonal pattern is smoothed over: 7 lets the pattern change from year to year, a large number such as 35 makes it nearly fixed. The trend window is how many steps the trend is smoothed over: smaller follows turns faster, larger is smoother. Both must be odd.

When should I use robust mode?

When the series has one-off spikes or drops, such as a promotion, an outage or a data error. Robust mode runs 15 extra passes that give such points less weight, so they show up in the remainder instead of bending the trend and the seasonal pattern.

What is the strength of seasonality?

A number from 0 to 1, from Wang, Smith and Hyndman (2006): one minus the variance of the remainder divided by the variance of seasonal plus remainder. Near 1 the seasonal pattern dominates the noise; below about 0.4 there is little seasonality to speak of. The strength of trend is the same with the trend in place of the seasonal.
