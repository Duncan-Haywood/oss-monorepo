How it works

  • The model is Bradley-Terry: each option has a hidden score, and the chance you pick A over B rises with the gap between their scores. After every answer the page refits all your answers at once (Newton's method, with a Laplace approximation for the uncertainty), the same code the Quick Pick survey app runs.
  • The next question is the pair whose answer is expected to tell the most about the order: two options the model cannot yet separate. Which side each appears on is random, so screen position cannot creep into the result.
  • When to stop: "settled" is how closely the orders your answers still allow agree with the order shown (the average Spearman correlation over draws from the model). It stops at 90%. On simulated respondents who answer with ordinary noise, the order shown at that point matched their real order with a Spearman correlation of about 0.94. If many of your picks are close calls, it checks again with the model fitted to how strongly you seem to feel (an estimate from your own answers) and keeps asking until that read passes too, because a model that assumes strong likes reads noise as a settled order (on 25 simulated near-coin-flip options it had claimed 90% for an order that was right at about 0.56). Two options you find genuinely close may never separate; the rank range shows that, and you can stop at any time.
  • Full order or favourite? Full order spreads questions across the list and stops when the whole order is 90% settled; that can leave the top two close. "Be sure of the favourite" asks only among options that could still be the favourite and stops when one has a 90% chance. In simulation it took about a third more questions and named the right favourite 12 times out of 12, against 6 to 8 for the full-order stop.
  • Team results refit each person separately, then average their centred scores, so someone who answered 80 questions counts the same as someone who answered 30. The page shows how much people agree and where they split.

Privacy and usage counting

Everything runs in your browser. Your list and answers are never uploaded; we have no accounts, tracking cookies or analytics vendor. Your progress is kept in this browser's local storage so a reload does not lose it. The page counts anonymous events in local storage (lists started, picks made, results shown, codes copied, team combines), nothing about who you are or what you ranked, and the site counts one page view per load without any identifier.
