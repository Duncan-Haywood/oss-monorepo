What this does not cover

  • One priced round. The model converts every SAFE at a single equity financing and gives a rough view of the round after it. It does not track several rounds, warrants, convertible notes or secondary sales.
  • Standard terms only. Pro rata rights, MFN clauses, side letters, participating preferred, a senior new series and anti-dilution protection are not modelled.
  • The pool convention. The new pool is sized inside the pre-money, the way investors usually ask for it. Some terms treat the pool differently.
  • Simple exit maths. No option strike prices, taxes, transaction costs, debt, escrow or management carve-outs.
  • Not a cap table system. Nothing is saved or shared, so this is a planning aid and not your record of ownership. Use your lawyer's or a cap table provider's records for that.

Common questions

What is the difference between a post-money and a pre-money SAFE?

A post-money SAFE (the Y Combinator standard since 2018) fixes the percentage the investor gets: the amount divided by the valuation cap, measured after every SAFE has converted. A pre-money SAFE measures the cap before SAFE money is counted, so each new SAFE dilutes the earlier ones as well as you. With post-money SAFEs the dilution is easier to predict, and it all lands on the founders.

How does a SAFE convert in a priced round?

It converts at the lowest of three prices: the valuation cap price, the round price reduced by any discount, or the round price itself. The number of shares is the SAFE amount divided by that price. Because the round price also depends on how many shares the SAFEs and the option pool add, the calculation is circular, and this tool solves it by iteration.

Who pays for the option pool top-up?

Usually the existing holders. New investors normally require the pool to be sized in the pre-money, so the extra pool shares dilute founders and SAFE holders and not the new money. This tool sizes the pool the same way and shows what it costs you.

Does a SAFE have a liquidation preference?

The standard YC SAFE converts into shadow preferred stock that carries a preference equal to the amount invested, so it is modelled here as a 1x non-participating preference that pays out or converts to common, whichever is worth more. Your actual terms may differ.

Is this legal or financial advice?

No. It is an illustrative model of the standard YC SAFE and a plain priced round. Your documents, any side letters, pro rata rights, MFN clauses and the term sheet decide the real numbers. Have your startup lawyer check the cap table before you sign.
