FOR DECK AND OUTDOOR-LIVING CREWS

Bid the posts, the shade, and the height.

Price a residential pergola the way the trade actually loses money: post count a square-foot special cannot see, then shade, height, an attached ledger, extras, permit, and the margin you intend to keep. You get the job price, what it works out to per footprint square foot, and the number below which the job loses money.

Looking up how much to charge to build a pergola? Start here, then replace every starting figure with your own crew times.

A starting job

Pick a common call to fill the spans, attachment, shade, and height. Changing a kit never overwrites hours or material costs you have already typed.

The footprint

Width is along the house. Depth is the projection. Post spacing starts at 8 feet — a 10-foot side is two posts; a 16-foot side is three. These are starting points, not a code opinion.

Hours and counts

Post, rafter, beam, and ledger counts start from the geometry. Shade hours apply only when the job is not an open frame. Change them when the site disagrees.

Materials and your costs

Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off. A square-foot special written on the footprint never sees it — or the shade.
