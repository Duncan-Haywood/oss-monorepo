3. How to settle

Settle up

    Balances

    PersonPaidSharePayments sent − receivedBalance

    A positive balance is owed to that person; a negative one is what they owe. Balances add to zero to the cent.

    How the fewest payments are found

    Each person's balance is what they paid minus their share, plus payments they sent, minus payments they received. If k people have a balance that is not zero, any group of them whose balances add to zero can settle among themselves in one fewer payment than its size. So the fewest payments is k minus the most zero-sum groups the balances can be cut into (Verhoeff, "Settling multiple debts efficiently", 2004). Finding that cut is a hard search problem in general; with up to 20 people who are owed or owe, the page checks every subset (about a million) in your browser and gives the true minimum. Above 20 it says the answer is a good one, not a proven one.

    Every split is done in whole cents of the main currency: an expense is converted once, then cut so the parts add back exactly, with any leftover cent going to the largest remainders and, on a tie, to whoever is listed first.

    Questions

    How does it find the fewest payments?

    With k people who are owed or owe money, the fewest payments that square everyone is k minus the largest number of groups those balances can be cut into where each group adds to zero, because each group of size s settles in s - 1 payments. The page tries every way of cutting the group (dynamic programming over subsets) for up to 20 people with a balance, so the answer is the true minimum, not a good guess. Above 20 it pairs off people whose balances cancel exactly, then settles the rest largest to largest, and says the answer is not proven minimal.

    Why not just have the biggest debtor pay the biggest creditor?

    That rule, which many apps use, never needs more than one payment fewer than the number of people, but it can miss a shortcut. With balances +3, +5, +4, +6, -6 and -12, it pays the 6 from the 12 first and needs 5 payments. The exact search sees that the +6 and -6 cancel on their own and finishes in 4. The page shows both counts so you can see when it matters.

    Can people pay only the friends who paid for them?

    Yes. Pick Pay back directly. Each person repays the people who covered their share, netted per pair, so nobody sends money to someone they did not share a cost with. It takes more payments than the fewest-payments mode.

    How do I split unevenly?

    After a slash, name who shares the cost and how: Ana, Ben for equal halves; Ana:2, Ben:1 for shares; Ana=30, Ben=20 for exact amounts; Ana=60%, Ben=40% for percents; all -Ben for everyone except Ben. Leave it out and everyone shares equally. Split amounts are rounded to the cent and always add back to the bill exactly.

    Make a share link and paste it in the group chat. The whole list is inside the link itself, so anyone who opens it sees the same expenses and payments, and there is no server copy. If someone adds an expense, they send a new link. Anyone holding the link can read the list, so share it only with the group.
