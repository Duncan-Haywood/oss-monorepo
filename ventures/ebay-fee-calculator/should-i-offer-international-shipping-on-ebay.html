What this checks

Sellers search “should I offer international shipping on eBay” because worldwide looks like extra demand. The fee is a percent of the whole order, and a free-shipping listing pays the extra label too.

On the defaults — $40 item, $6 shipping, 13.6% + $0.40, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 1.65% international fee, $12 extra label — domestic keeps $21.34. Buyer-pays worldwide keeps $20.59, $0.76 less. Absorbing the extra label keeps $8.59, $12.76 less. List $40.90 or $55.05 to hold the domestic leftover.

Common questions

Should I offer international shipping on eBay?

Offer it when the leftover after the international fee — and after any extra postage you absorb — still beats keeping the listing domestic. On the calculator defaults ($40 item, $6 shipping, 13.6%+$0.40, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 1.65% international fee, $12 extra label) domestic keeps $21.34. Buyer-pays worldwide keeps $20.59. Absorbing the extra label keeps $8.59. That is arithmetic on your inputs, not advice to turn worldwide on.

What list price holds the domestic leftover?

List $40.90 when the buyer pays the extra postage, or $55.05 if you buy the extra label, to hold the $21.34 domestic leftover on these defaults. The international fee is a percent of item plus shipping, so a $12 label never costs only $12.

Is this the same as Global Shipping Program?

No. This page asks whether turning worldwide on at this list still leaves a profit. Global Shipping Program assumes an international buyer already wants the item and compares the hub label to buying the international postage yourself. Standard Envelope is cheap domestic postage versus a claim.
