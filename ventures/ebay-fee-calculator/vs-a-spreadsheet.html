This calculator vs. a spreadsheet

A seller who tracks eBay profit more than a few times usually ends up with a spreadsheet: a price cell, a cost cell, and one fee-percentage cell subtracted from the total. That formula is right for the simple case and silently wrong the moment a sale is more than the simple case — which, on eBay, is often. This page names no specific product; it compares against the generic method of tracking fees and profit in a spreadsheet or by hand.

A spreadsheet can multiply an order total by one fee rate and subtract cost of goods in a single formula, and many sellers already have one. What it does not do on its own is stack Promoted Listings and international fees on top of the final value fee when a sale carries either, or price what a typed return rate costs once the outbound label, packaging, and any unrefunded fees are counted — so a "13.6% fee" cell quietly understates the real fee bill on any promoted or international sale, and a completed-sale profit column quietly overstates what a batch of orders actually nets once some come back.

This calculator takes the final value rate, the Promoted Listings rate, and the international fee rate as separate inputs and stacks them the way eBay actually charges them, on the full order total including shipping charged. It also takes an expected return rate, return shipping cost, and the share of fees eBay refunds on a return, and reports both completed-sale profit and return-adjusted expected profit side by side — plus the exact price that hits a target margin, a target profit dollar amount, or a target ROI, solved backward rather than guessed and rechecked by hand.

What this page is not

This calculator is not a bookkeeping system and does not track listings or orders over time; a spreadsheet or a seller platform still does that job better. What it replaces is the one-off arithmetic of stacking eBay's fee types correctly and pricing a return rate on a single order — the part of a profit sheet most likely to be approximated as "revenue times one fee percentage" because modeling Promoted Listings, international fees, and returns correctly, every time terms change, is tedious enough that most sheets skip it.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet work out eBay profit?

Yes, for the simple case: revenue times one fee percentage, minus cost of goods and shipping. Most sellers already have a cell like that. It stops being right the moment Promoted Listings or an international sale add their own percentage on top of the final value fee, or a return comes back and eats the outbound label and packaging while only partially refunding fees — none of which fits a single fee-rate cell without rebuilding the formula.

Where does a hand-built eBay profit sheet usually go wrong?

Two places, on the calculator's own defaults. First, treating 13.6% as the whole fee: adding an 8% Promoted Listings rate and a 1.65% international rate on the same $40 item plus $6 shipping order lowers completed-sale profit from $21.34 to $16.91 — a $4.44 gap one flat-rate cell never shows. Second, never pricing returns: a 10% expected return rate on the base case lowers profit from $21.34 completed to $18.11 expected, because a return still eats the outbound shipping, packaging, and any unrefunded portion of the fees already paid, and a spreadsheet that only tracks completed sales has no row for that.

What does a spreadsheet not check for you?

Whether Promoted Listings and international fees are stacking on top of the final value fee rather than replacing it; what a typed return rate actually costs once the outbound label, packaging, and unrefunded fees are counted; the exact price that hits a target margin or profit dollar amount, solved backward rather than guessed and rechecked; and return on the cash actually invested, which is smaller than the invoice total because fees are deducted from proceeds rather than paid up front.

Does either one know what eBay will actually charge?

No. Both this calculator and a spreadsheet apply the fee rates you type in. Neither reproduces eBay's live, category-specific rate card — verify current rates against eBay's own Fee Calculator in Seller Center or a recent invoice before relying on either.

Is anything I enter uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculator this page links to runs entirely in your browser; nothing you type is uploaded.
