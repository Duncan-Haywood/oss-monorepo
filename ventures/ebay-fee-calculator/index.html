If some come back

A completed sale can look fine and still lose money once returns start. This assumes the item comes back into inventory — so you do not lose the sourcing cost again — and that you refund the buyer. Common questions

How do I work out what an eBay sale actually leaves?

Enter the sale price, the fee rates your own account is charged, what you paid for the item, and what shipping costs you. The order result gives net profit, margin, return on the cash you had tied up in the item, and the lowest resale price that still breaks even. The rates come from the boxes you fill in rather than a stored fee table, so the arithmetic stays right when eBay changes a category rate, or when your store tier changes what you pay.

Why does a sale that looked profitable still leave me short?

Because most of what erodes an eBay sale lands after checkout, not in the list price: the outbound label, return postage on the ones that come back, promoted-listing bills that are charged whether or not the click was the reason it sold, and the time spent packing. The “If some come back” section applies a return rate you type — the outbound label and the return postage usually stay with you even when the item resells, which is why a few returns move net profit more than sellers expect.

Should I offer a partial refund or take the return?

It depends on what the item is worth back in your stock against what it costs to get it there. “Partial refund vs taking the return” puts both against each other on the order you already entered, so the comparison uses your real postage and cost of goods instead of a rule of thumb.

Does offering free shipping cost me money?

Only if it does not sell more. Free shipping is postage you absorb into the item price, and it pays when the extra sell-through covers that. “Free shipping vs charging the buyer” asks you for the sell-through lift you actually see rather than assuming one, and shows whether it covers the postage at this list price.

Do I need an account, and does anything I type get uploaded?

No account, and nothing is uploaded. The whole calculator runs in your browser: it makes no network requests at all, your inputs are kept only in your own browser’s local storage so the page remembers them next time, and “Reset saved inputs” clears them. A shareable link carries the numbers in the URL itself, so a scenario is only shared with whoever you send that link to.

Is this eBay’s official fee schedule?

No. It is an estimate built from the numbers you supply, not a published rate card, and it is not tax, financial, or business advice. Check the fee rates against your own eBay account before you rely on a result — category rates, store tiers, and promotions all change what a given sale is charged.
