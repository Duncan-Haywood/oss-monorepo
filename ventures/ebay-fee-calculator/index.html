If some come back

A completed sale can look fine and still lose money once returns start. This assumes the item comes back into inventory — so you do not lose the sourcing cost again — and that you refund the buyer. The outbound label, packaging, other cash, return postage, and any fees you do not get back stay with you. It is arithmetic from the rate you type, not a forecast of your category or a statement of eBay’s refund rules. Why a 10% return rate can erase a thin listing.

Auction vs Buy It Now

A completed auction sale is often cheaper than Buy It Now. Auctions still win when they actually sell. Expected listing profit is the sale you already calculated — including the typed return rate — times sell-through, minus the insertion fee that is sunk either way. Partial refund vs taking the return

A buyer case is not the same decision as a return rate. Once they open it you can send a concession and let them keep the item, take the return, or refund the whole order without getting the item back. Percentage fees usually come back on the refunded amount; the outbound label does not. Should I offer a partial refund instead of taking the return?

Authenticity Guarantee vs skip-AG

Authenticity Guarantee is not the same decision as a return rate or a partial refund. You pay an authenticity fee and often an extra label to the hub; you usually sell more of the listing and see fewer “not authentic” returns. The fee is treated like a percentage marketplace fee and comes back on a return; the extra hub label does not. Rates are inputs, not eBay’s live Authenticity Guarantee schedule. Local pickup vs the label

Local pickup is not the same decision as a return rate or Authenticity Guarantee. You skip the outbound label and return postage, then pay meetup time and take a lower sell-through because only local buyers can collect. A committed buyer still ghosts. The table keeps the original sale, then drops postage and multiplies by sell-through and the no-show rate. Rates and the wage are inputs, not eBay’s pickup policy. Lot vs individual listings

A pile you could list as one lot is not the same decision as a return rate or a meetup. You keep one label, one insertion, and one listing session — or you split the pieces, pay a label and insertion on each, and take the time to write five titles. The table keeps the original lot sale, then prices each piece on its own postage and multiplies by sell-through. Rates, insertion, and the wage are inputs, not eBay’s listing policy. Standard Envelope vs a tracked label

A Standard Envelope is not the same decision as a return rate or a meetup. You save most of the outbound label, then take an item-not-received claim rate because there is no tracking. A claim is not a return: the buyer is refunded and the item is gone, so cost of goods is lost. The table keeps the original tracked sale — including the typed return rate — then prices the envelope postage and multiplies by sell-through. Postage and the claim rate are inputs, not eBay’s envelope eligibility or seller-protection policy. Is eBay Standard Envelope worth it?

Combined shipping vs two labels

Combined shipping is not the same decision as a lot listing or a Standard Envelope. The sales already closed. You ship each item on its own label, or you invoice one combined postage and pay one box. A cheap “I’ll combine them” discount can give away more shipping revenue than the second label costs. A return of the box restocks every item and eats the one outbound plus the one return label. Postage is an input, not eBay’s invoice policy. Should I offer combined shipping on eBay?

Seller-initiated offer vs waiting

Sending an offer to watchers is not the same decision as taking an incoming Best Offer. You are naming the discount. Waiting keeps the listed price and a lower sell-through; the offer drops the ticket and uses a typed accept rate. Insertion is already sunk — the listing exists. Promoted Offsite vs skip

Promoted Offsite is not the same decision as Promoted Listings Standard. Standard is a percent of a completed sale. Offsite is a bill either way — you pay the campaign even if they bounce. The extra sales still pay final value fee and, unless you zero the Offsite sale promoted rate, the same Standard percent as the listing. Spend and extra-sale counts are inputs, not eBay’s Offsite bid. Is eBay Promoted Offsite worth it?
