If some come back

A completed sale can look fine and still lose money once returns start. This assumes the item comes back into inventory — so you do not lose the sourcing cost again — and that you refund the buyer. The outbound label, packaging, other cash, return postage, and any fees you do not get back stay with you. It is arithmetic from the rate you type, not a forecast of your category or a statement of eBay’s refund rules. Why a 10% return rate can erase a thin listing.

Auction vs Buy It Now

A completed auction sale is often cheaper than Buy It Now. Auctions still win when they actually sell. Expected listing profit is the sale you already calculated — including the typed return rate — times sell-through, minus the insertion fee that is sunk either way. Partial refund vs taking the return

A buyer case is not the same decision as a return rate. Once they open it you can send a concession and let them keep the item, take the return, or refund the whole order without getting the item back. Percentage fees usually come back on the refunded amount; the outbound label does not. Should I offer a partial refund instead of taking the return?

Authenticity Guarantee vs skip-AG

Authenticity Guarantee is not the same decision as a return rate or a partial refund. You pay an authenticity fee and often an extra label to the hub; you usually sell more of the listing and see fewer “not authentic” returns. The fee is treated like a percentage marketplace fee and comes back on a return; the extra hub label does not. Rates are inputs, not eBay’s live Authenticity Guarantee schedule. Local pickup vs the label

Local pickup is not the same decision as a return rate or Authenticity Guarantee. You skip the outbound label and return postage, then pay meetup time and take a lower sell-through because only local buyers can collect. A committed buyer still ghosts. The table keeps the original sale, then drops postage and multiplies by sell-through and the no-show rate. Rates and the wage are inputs, not eBay’s pickup policy.
