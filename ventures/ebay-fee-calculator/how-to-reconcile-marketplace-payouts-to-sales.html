How to reconcile marketplace payouts to sales

The deposit that lands in your bank is not your sales. Every marketplace pays you after taking its fees, its ad charges, the labels you bought through it, and any refunds, and some of them also hold sales tax they collected for the state. Reconciling means building a bridge from the payout back to gross sales, so that every dollar between the two is named. Without that bridge, sellers usually make one of two mistakes: they report the deposits as income (and lose every fee as a deduction), or they report a 1099-K gross that includes money that was never theirs.

The bridge, on one month of eBay sales

An example month: 30 orders, $1,200.00 of items, $150.00 of shipping the buyers paid, and $96.00 of sales tax that eBay collected and remitted as a marketplace facilitator. One order was refunded in full: $50.00 to the seller's side plus $4.00 of tax. The fee figures use eBay's published US rate for most categories, 13.6% of the total amount of the sale (item, shipping and sales tax) plus $0.40 per order, with the final value fee credited on the refunded order but not the per-order fee. Your own categories and rates may differ: read them off your transaction report.

If the bridge does not close, the gap is the finding: a payout still in transit at month end, a reserve or hold, a dispute, a shipping adjustment, a store subscription taken from proceeds, or a sale paid out next month. Name it on its own line rather than forcing the totals together.

The same method on every marketplace

The bridge has the same shape everywhere; only the fee lines change. Use each marketplace's own sales or transaction report, not the bank deposits, as the starting list of sales.

A monthly routine that takes minutes, not an afternoon

  1. Export each marketplace's transaction or sales report for the month, and the bank statement.
  2. Match payouts to deposits by payout ID and date. Payouts in transit at month end are a reconciling item, not missing money.
  3. Total each report into the bridge above: gross sales, refunds, fees, ads, labels, other deductions, payout.
  4. Keep marketplace-collected sales tax on its own line, outside your sales.
  5. Save the exports. IRS Publication 583 asks you to keep records that support every income and deduction figure.

Why this matters for a 1099-K

A marketplace files Form 1099-K only above the federal threshold, which is back at more than $20,000 of gross payments and more than 200 transactions (some states set lower thresholds, and a platform may send one anyway). Either way the income is reportable, and the form shows a gross figure, not your profit. The bridge is what lets you show how that gross figure becomes your gross receipts and your deductions. How to present the difference on a return is a question for your preparer; the Schedule C instructions say that if you collected sales tax yourself, it goes into gross receipts and is deducted on line 23.

Common questions

Why doesn't my eBay payout match my sales?

Because the marketplace pays you after taking its fees, ad charges, labels you bought through it, and refunds, and it never pays you the sales tax it collected for the state. In the worked month, $1,350.00 of sales became a $923.68 payout: $201.32 of fees, $40.00 of Promoted Listings, $135.00 of labels and a $50.00 refund sit in between.

Should I report my deposits or my gross sales?

Gross sales, with the fees, ads, labels and refunds as separate figures. Reporting deposits as income hides those costs and leaves the totals impossible to tie to a 1099-K. This is a description of the method, not tax advice; your preparer decides how to present it.

Is sales tax the marketplace collected my income?

When a marketplace collects and remits sales tax as a marketplace facilitator, it never reaches your payout. Keep it on its own line of the reconciliation so a gross figure that includes it can still be tied to your sales. The Schedule C instructions say tax you collected yourself goes in gross receipts and is deducted on line 23.

Do I only need to do this if I get a 1099-K?

No. The federal 1099-K threshold is more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions, and some states are lower, but income from selling is reportable whether or not a form arrives. The reconciliation is how you know your own numbers either way.
