Are eBay promoted listings worth it?

A promoted-listings campaign takes a percentage of the same order total the final value fee does — item plus shipping. That is easy to understate if you treat “2% ads” as 2% of the item price. Here is one resale order worked at four rates, with every number visible.

The order

A $40 item with $6 shipping charged to the buyer. It cost $12 to source, $5 to post, and $1 to pack. Final value fee 13.25%, $0.30 fixed fee, no international fee. Same sale, same costs — only the ad rate changes.

Order total is $46. Two percent of $46 is $0.92, not $0.80. Eight percent is $3.68 — more than the packaging and a large share of the label. The item still makes money at 8%, but the campaign has already spent $3.68 of a $21.61 unpromoted profit.

The highest rate that still breaks even

Each extra ad point takes 1% of the $46 order, so the unpromoted net margin is the remaining budget for ads. On this order that margin is about 47%. Promote above that and the sale loses money even if the campaign produces no extra buyers — which is the conservative test. If you also want to keep a 20% net margin, the remaining ad budget is about 27%.

Whether the ads are “worth it” then depends on something this page cannot know: how many extra sales the campaign actually creates. Use the calculator to get the cash cap first, then judge lift against that number instead of guessing.
