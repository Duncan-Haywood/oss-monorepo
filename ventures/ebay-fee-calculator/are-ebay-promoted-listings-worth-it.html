What this checks

Resellers search “are eBay Promoted Listings worth it” because 2% looks cheap until it is 2% of item plus shipping. Standard takes that percent only when the listing sells. Offsite bills the campaign even if they bounce.

On the defaults — a $40 item, $6 shipping charged, 13.6% final value fee, $0.40 fixed, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, $5 return label, 10% returns, 5% Standard, one extra sale — skip expects $18.11 and Standard expects $32.08. Standard keeps $13.97 more (+$13.97) on this listing and +$279.39 across twenty listings. Extra sales have to stay at about 0.13, or the rate has to stay at about 21.87%, before Standard matches skip.

Zero extra sales flips. Keep the same 5% campaign and get no extra $40 sale. Skip still expects $18.11; Standard expects $16.04 — $2.07 less. Five percent of $46 is $2.30, not $2.00. The sale you already had paid the campaign.

Common questions

Are eBay Promoted Listings worth it?

Promoted Listings Standard is a percent of a completed sale, including the sale you already would have made. Promoted Offsite is a campaign billed even if they bounce. On the calculator defaults (a $40 item, $6 shipping, 13.6% final value fee, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 10% returns, 5% Standard, one extra sale) skip expects $18.11 and Standard expects $32.08. Standard keeps $13.97 more. Extra sales have to stay at about 0.13 before Standard matches skip. That is arithmetic on your inputs, not advice to turn Promoted Listings on.

What if Promoted Listings produce no extra sales?

Then the campaign only takes a cut of a sale you already had. On the same $40 listing, 5% of the $46 order is $2.30. After a 10% return mix that leaves $16.04 against $18.11 for skip — $2.07 less. Two percent of $46 is $0.92, not $0.80.

Is Promoted Listings Standard the same as Promoted Offsite?

No. Standard is a percent taken only when the listing sells. Offsite is a campaign spend billed even if they bounce. Extra Offsite sales can still pay Standard too if the listing is already promoted.
