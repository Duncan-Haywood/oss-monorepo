You keep one label, one insertion, and one listing session — or you split the pieces, pay a label and insertion on each, and take the time to write five titles. What this checks

Resellers search “should I break an eBay lot into individual listings” because five $14 titles look like more money than one $40 lot. Five labels, five insertions, and 40 minutes of listing time can wipe the extra you thought you were capturing.

On the defaults — $40 lot, $6 shipping charged, 13.6% final value fee, $0.40 fixed, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, $5 return label, 10% returns, five pieces at $14 each with $5 charged and a $4 label, 80% lot sell-through vs 55% single, $0.35 insertion, 8 minutes at $18/hour — the lot expects $12.09 and splitting expects $4.82. Splitting keeps $7.26 less (−$7.26) on this pile and −$43.58 across six decisions. Each single has to list at about $17.40, or sell through at about 76.5%, before splitting matches the lot.

The known-SKU case flips. Keep the same $40 pile and list five $18 pieces at 85% sell-through, store insertion ($0.00), 3 minutes each, and a $3 label. Splitting expects $41.68 against the lot’s $12.09 — $29.59 more.

It does not tell you to keep or break the lot, and it is not tax or financial advice. Common questions

Should I break an eBay lot into individual listings?

Splitting a lot adds a label, an insertion, and listing time on every piece. On the calculator defaults ($40 lot, $6 shipping, 13.6% final value fee, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 10% returns, five pieces at $14 each, 80% lot sell-through vs 55% single, $0.35 insertion, $4 single label, 8 minutes at $18/hour) the lot expects $12.09 and splitting expects $4.82. Splitting keeps $7.26 less. Each single has to list at about $17.40 before splitting matches the lot. That is arithmetic on your inputs, not advice to keep or break the lot.

When does splitting an eBay lot beat keeping it together?

When each piece is a known SKU that actually sells. On the same $40 pile, five $18 listings at 85% sell-through, store insertion, 3 minutes each, and a $3 label expect $41.68 against the lot’s $12.09 — $29.59 more.

Does listing time still cost me if a single does not sell?

This page treats listing time and insertion fees as paid on every listing, including the ones that do not sell. Completed-sale profit is only counted at the typed sell-through. Enter the minutes and wage from your last listing session; this is not eBay’s insertion-fee schedule.
