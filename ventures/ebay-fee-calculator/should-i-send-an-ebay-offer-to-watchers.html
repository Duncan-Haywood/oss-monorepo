What this checks

Card, clothing, and small-goods sellers search “should I send an offer to eBay watchers” because Seller Hub shows a watcher count and a Send offer button. A 10% coupon looks automatic. It also writes a new ticket, and a 20% “make them take it” discount can leave less than sitting at the listed price.

On the defaults — $40 item, $6 shipping charged, 13.6% final value fee, $0.40 fixed, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, $5 return label, 10% returns, 10% offer, 40% wait sell-through, 55% accept rate, forty offers — waiting expects $7.24 and the offer expects $8.25. The offer keeps $1.01 more on this listing and +$40.23 across forty offers. The offer has to be accepted about 48.3% of the time, or stay at or below about 15.9% off ($33.65), before it matches waiting.

A deeper coupon flips. Keep the same listing and send 20% off. Waiting still expects $7.24; the 20% offer expects $6.54 — $0.70 less. Waiting at 50% sell-through already beats a 10% offer ($9.05 vs $8.25). The conversion you thought you bought did not cover the leftover you gave away.

It does not tell you to send the offer, and it is not tax or financial advice. An incoming Best Offer, auction versus Buy It Now, combined shipping, and a return-rate mix are different questions. Common questions

Should I send an offer to eBay watchers?

A seller-initiated offer is a pricing decision on a listing that already exists. You either wait for Buy It Now at the listed price or send a percent-off offer to watchers. On the calculator defaults ($40 item, $6 shipping charged, 13.6% final value fee, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 10% returns, 10% offer, 40% wait sell-through, 55% accept rate) waiting expects $7.24 and the offer expects $8.25. The offer keeps $1.01 more. The offer has to be accepted about 48.3% of the time, or stay at or below about 15.9% off ($33.65), before it matches waiting. That is arithmetic on your inputs, not advice to send the offer.

When does waiting beat a seller-initiated eBay offer?

When the discount eats more leftover than the conversion lift returns. On the same $40 listing, a 20% offer at a 55% accept rate expects $6.54 against $7.24 for waiting — $0.70 less. Waiting at 50% sell-through already beats a 10% offer ($9.05 vs $8.25).

Is sending an offer the same as taking a Best Offer?

No. An incoming Best Offer is the buyer naming a price. A seller-initiated offer is you naming the discount and sending it to watchers. Auction versus Buy It Now is a listing-format choice before anyone watches. The repricing table moves the list price without a typed conversion lift.
