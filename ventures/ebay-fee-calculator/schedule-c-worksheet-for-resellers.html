A Schedule C worksheet for resellers, line by line

If you resell for profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, the year's numbers usually land on Schedule C (Form 1040), Profit or Loss From Business. The form was not written for marketplace sellers, so the same costs end up on different lines from one year to the next. This worksheet maps what a reseller actually has (payout reports, purchases, labels, mileage) onto the lines, with a worked year. It prepares numbers for you or your preparer; it is not tax advice and not a return.

Before you start

  • A reconciled year from every marketplace: gross sales, refunds, fees, ads, labels (see how to reconcile payouts to sales).
  • Cost of goods sold from inventory at the start and end of the year (see cost of goods sold for resellers).
  • Receipts for supplies, software and other costs; a mileage log if you drive to source or ship.
  • Any Forms 1099-K. The federal threshold is more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions; some states are lower. Income is reportable either way.

Postage is the line preparers treat differently: shipping you pay to receive inventory is part of its cost; shipping to buyers is a selling expense, shown here in Part V. Some preparers put marketplace fees on line 27a instead of line 10. Either way, pick one treatment and keep it from year to year so the years compare.

After line 31

  • Net earnings from self-employment of $400 or more mean Schedule SE (self-employment tax) as well as income tax.
  • Sold only a few things from your own closet, for less than you paid? That is usually not a business, and personal items sold at a loss are covered in the IRS Form 1099-K FAQs, not here.
  • Keep the reports behind every line. Publication 583 explains the records the IRS expects.

Common questions

Where do eBay and Poshmark fees go on Schedule C?

Usually line 10, commissions and fees; the Schedule C instructions note that a dealer in property enters fees paid to facilitate a sale there. Promoted Listings and other ads go on line 8. Some preparers use line 27a for marketplace fees; pick one treatment and keep it.

Is shipping to buyers part of cost of goods sold?

Shipping you pay to receive inventory is part of its cost. Shipping labels to buyers are a selling expense; this worksheet lists them in Part V, other expenses, on line 27a. Ask your preparer which treatment they use.

Do I report the 1099-K amount on line 1?

Line 1 is your gross receipts: items plus buyer-paid shipping across every marketplace. A 1099-K can show a different gross, for example one that includes sales tax the marketplace collected. Keep a reconciliation that ties the two, and let your preparer decide how to present the difference.

Do I need Schedule C if I did not get a 1099-K?

If you sell as a business, yes: the federal 1099-K threshold of more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions decides when a form is filed, not whether income is reportable. Occasional sales of personal items at a loss are a different case covered in the IRS Form 1099-K FAQs.
