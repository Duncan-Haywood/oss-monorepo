Common questions

How is workers' compensation premium calculated?

Premium is payroll divided by 100, times the rate for each class code, summed across classes to get manual premium. That is multiplied by your experience modification, adjusted by any schedule credit or debit, reduced by any premium discount, and then flat charges such as an expense constant are added.

What is an experience modification rate?

The experience mod compares your claims history over a recent multi-year window with what is expected for a business of your size and class. A mod of 1.00 is average, above 1.00 raises your premium and below 1.00 lowers it. Your rating bureau or carrier publishes the worksheet, and errors on it are worth disputing.

Is overtime pay included in workers' comp payroll?

In most states only the straight-time part of overtime is premium basis. At time-and-a-half, one third of each overtime dollar is the premium portion and is excluded, but only if your payroll records show overtime separately. Check your own state and carrier rules.

Why did my workers' comp audit bill me extra?

The policy is written on estimated payroll. At the end of the term the carrier audits actual payroll, and any difference is charged or returned at the same rates. Growth, more overtime, or uninsured subcontractors added to your payroll are the usual causes of an additional premium.

Is this an insurance quote?

No. It does the arithmetic on rates you type in from your own quote or policy. It does not look up class codes or rates, it is not insurance or legal advice, and your carrier's figures control.
