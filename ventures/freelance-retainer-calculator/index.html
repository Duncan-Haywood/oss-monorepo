Why this is different from a basic hourly-rate calculator

A $75 rate means little if only 65% of your month is billable, your tools and taxes cost $1,800, and a vague retainer absorbs unplanned revisions. This calculator makes each assumption visible so you can change it before making a promise.

Common question: how many hours should a retainer include?

Start with a scope you can deliver inside your remaining billable capacity after existing clients, then leave the buffer intact. A retainer is a commitment of access and work, not permission for unlimited requests. Put included hours, response time, and out-of-scope pricing in your agreement.

What if the client asks for a cheaper retainer?

Enter the monthly price they offered. The calculator shows the gap to your planning floor, the effective hourly rate on included hours and on buffered hours, and how many hours you can still include if you keep the floor. That is arithmetic on your assumptions, not a recommendation to accept or walk away.

How many retainers can I take this month?

Enter hours already sold to other clients. The result shows remaining billable hours, how many identical retainers fit that remainder, and the monthly revenue if you filled the leftover capacity with this same retainer. One client at 40% or more of billable capacity is flagged as a concentration observation, not as advice.
