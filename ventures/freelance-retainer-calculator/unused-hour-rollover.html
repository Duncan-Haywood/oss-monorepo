How the gift is calculated

Month 1 unused hours = included hours minus hours they actually used. Month 2 hours under unlimited rollover = new-block hours plus that leftover. Month 2 effective rate = monthly price ÷ those hours. The unpaid gift is leftover hours × hourly floor.

  • Default $1,725 / 20 hours / $75 floor.
  • Month 1 at 50%: 10 hours used, 10 leftover.
  • Expire: month 2 is 20 hours at $86.25/hr. Gift $0.
  • Cap at the 3-hour buffer: month 2 is 23 hours at $75/hr. Gift $225.
  • Unlimited rollover: month 2 is 30 hours at $57.50/hr. Gift $750.

The 3-hour buffer is the unpaid-overage break-even minus included hours ($1,725 ÷ $75 − 20). A cap there keeps a catch-up month on the floor. Unlimited rollover spends that buffer and then some.

What to put in the agreement

Write “unused hours expire at month-end” or “up to N unused hours may roll once, then expire or bill at the floor.” Do not write “hours roll over” with no cap and no expiry. A quiet first month plus a catch-up second month is the usual pattern after a kickoff delay, a vacation, or a stakeholder who was not ready.

Common questions

Should unused retainer hours roll over?

Only if the rolled hours still leave month 2 at or above your hourly floor. On the default $1,725 / 20-hour plan, a 10-hour first month plus unlimited rollover into a full second month is 30 hours for $1,725 — $57.50/hr, $750 of unpaid time. Cap rollover at the 3-hour buffer and month 2 stays at $75/hr.

How do I cap unused-hour rollover without killing the deal?

Cap rolled hours at the unpaid-overage buffer — monthly price divided by hourly floor, minus included hours. On the default plan that is 3 hours. Hours above the cap expire or bill at the floor.
