How many hours should a freelance retainer include?

The useful answer is not a universal number. It is the largest package you can deliver this month without giving one client most of your capacity.

Start from capacity, not from what the client asked for

A 20-hour retainer is cheap to promise and expensive to keep if only 65% of your calendar is billable. If you have 160 available hours and 65% utilization, you have 104 billable hours — not 160. The retainer has to fit inside that 104, after admin, other clients, and recovery.

Leave a buffer you can name

Revisions, Slack questions, and “quick looks” are the work that makes a vague retainer unprofitable. Put a scope buffer in the price and write included hours, response time, and out-of-scope rates into the agreement. Unlimited access is not a package size.

Do not sell more than half the month to one client

If one retainer uses more than 50% of billable capacity, a late invoice or a paused project becomes a cash-flow event. Quote a range — 10, 15, 20 hours — and let the client buy the size that fits both of you.
