Common questions

    
How much of my day rate do I keep through an umbrella company?

    
On a £400 day rate, 5 days a week for 46 weeks, with a £25 weekly margin and a 3% employer pension, you keep roughly 60% of what the agency pays: about £55,400 a year, or £241 per day worked. Employer National Insurance of 15% is the biggest deduction, followed by income tax and your own National Insurance.

    
What is employer National Insurance and why does it come out of my day rate?

    
Because the umbrella is your employer, it pays 15% National Insurance on your gross pay above £5,000 a year. The agency pays the umbrella one fixed rate, so employer NI, the Apprenticeship Levy, any employer pension and the umbrella margin are taken from it before your gross pay is worked out.

    
What is a normal umbrella company margin?

    
Most umbrellas charge a flat weekly fee, commonly around £20 to £30 a week, though some charge more. Ask what it covers, and check for enrolment, exit or insurance fees on top.

    
Is holiday pay included?

    
Yes. Your gross pay includes 12.07% holiday pay, which the umbrella accrues or pays with each week. It is part of what you take home over the year, so the totals here do not change whether you take it as you go or when you take leave.

    
Is an umbrella company better than a limited company?

    
It depends on your pay and whether IR35 applies. An umbrella is simple but costs you employer NI, so at higher pay a personal service company can keep more of the day rate, with accountancy costs and IR35 risk. Use the sole trader vs limited company and IR35 calculators to compare.
