Bid the doors, the drawers, and the degrease — not the door count.

A flat per-door quote treats a 10-door galley with eight drawer fronts, a greasy range wall, and a dark-to-light extra coat as the same job as ten shop-sprayed doors. Drawer fronts, face frames, hardware remove-and-rehang, and an extra coat do not scale with the door count the homeowner counts. Price those extras separately, then see the walk-away number.

    Common questions

    What does this refinishing estimate include?

    The price adds up door, drawer-front, and face-frame labor scaled by your finish method, hardware remove-and-rehang (or replacement) labor, degrease and prep hours, an extra finish coat when the color change calls for one, primer and finish material by the gallon, sandpaper and containment supplies, and new hardware when checked — plus travel and equipment, overhead, and your target margin, all from the piece counts and rates you enter, not a flat price-per-door list.

    Why does a face-frame opening cost less than a door of the same size?

    A face-frame opening is painted in place, not pulled, sanded on a bench, and sprayed in a booth, so it counts as 0.6 of a piece — not a full door — when the calculator sizes primer and finish gallons. Doors and drawer fronts, which are removed and finished off the cabinet, each count as a full piece.

    Why does on-site brush work cost so much more than shop spray for the same door count?

    Each finish method has its own labor factor: shop spray (doors off, in a booth) is the baseline, on-site spray in containment runs 28% more hours on the same doors, and on-site brush or roll runs 55% more — masking, multiple thin coats, and working around a kitchen the crew cannot fully close off all cost time that a booth does not. Switching finish method rescales door, drawer, and opening hours; it does not touch hardware or degrease hours, which take the same time regardless of how the piece gets its coats.

    What’s the difference between the refinish price and the walk-away price?

    The refinish price already includes your target margin on top of labor, materials, and overhead. The walk-away price is the total cost underneath it — doors, drawers, openings, hardware, primer, and finish — the point below which the job stops being profitable at all.

    Does this check paint adhesion or substrate condition?

    No. Hours, coverage, and material rates are ballpark shop figures you can edit, not a paint specification or a guarantee of adhesion. Whether a particular cabinet substrate needs extra degrease, deglossing, or a bonding primer beyond what you have entered is still a judgment call from the site walk — this tool prices the finish system you choose, it does not test it.
