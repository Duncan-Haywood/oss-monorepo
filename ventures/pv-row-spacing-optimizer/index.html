    How it is calculated

    Sun and sky. The sun's position comes from NOAA's solar equations at every step of the year. With a weather file, its own GHI, DNI and DHI are used; without one, the Ineichen-Perez clear sky with the Linke turbidity you enter. Diffuse light is split by Hay and Davies: the circumsolar part travels with the beam, the rest comes evenly from the sky.

    Rows. Rows are long and parallel on flat ground. The shaded share of a row's width is 1 - sin(sun elevation) / (GCR x cos(angle of incidence)), from the geometry across the rows. The sky a row's face can see past the row in front is (1 + p - sqrt(1 + p² - 2p cos tilt)) / 2 of the open sky, with p = 1/GCR. Any shadow in an electrical band removes that band's beam and circumsolar light. Trackers follow the sun and backtrack (Anderson and Mikofski, 2020) so rows never shade each other, at the cost of turning away from the sun early and late. Cell temperature uses the NOCT model; power falls 0.35% per degree above 25 C.

    Money. Capacity is the land divided by the area each kW needs (module area / GCR, plus your overhead), or your fixed MW. Net present value = energy x price over the life (with degradation), minus installed cost, O&M and land lease, all discounted. The best layout is the one with the highest NPV, or the lowest cost per MWh when none pays.

    Checked against pvlib. Sun position within 0.013 degrees of NREL's SPA, the clear sky within 0.01 W/m², the shaded fraction and tracker angles identical to pvlib's, and a year of beam and sky light on five row layouts within 0.04% of pvlib's infinite-sheds model. Not modelled: sloped or uneven ground, horizon and nearby objects, row ends, bifacial rear-side gain, snow, inverter clipping, and cable and racking costs that change with spacing (put them in the installed cost or the overhead).

    Common questions

    How far apart should solar panel rows be?

    Far enough that the energy the next foot of spacing saves from shade is worth less than the land, cable and racking it costs. That point depends on the site's latitude and weather, the tilt, the price of land and of energy, and whether the plant is limited by acres or by its grid connection. This tool simulates a year of sun for every tilt and spacing and prices each one, so the answer comes from your numbers rather than a rule of thumb.

    What is ground coverage ratio (GCR)?

    The collector width (the slant width of one row of modules) divided by the row pitch (the distance from one row to the same point on the next). A GCR of 0.4 with 4 m wide tables means a pitch of 10 m. Higher GCR fits more capacity on the land and loses more energy to row-to-row shading and to the sky the rows hide from each other.

    Is the 9 am to 3 pm winter solstice rule good enough?

    It is a safe starting point, not an optimum. It ignores the price of land and of energy and treats every latitude the same. On the two real typical-year weather files we tested, it cost 0.26 dollars per MWh at 36 degrees north and 7.21 dollars per MWh at 55 degrees north, where it spreads rows nearly three times farther apart than the cheapest layout.

    Why does the tool ask for electrical bands?

    A shadow across the bottom edge of a row costs more than its own area: the cells in a module are wired in series substrings behind bypass diodes, and a substring with a shaded cell drops out. Two standard modules in portrait make 2 bands across the table; two half-cut modules in portrait make 4; three substrings per module in landscape make 3 per module. Enter 0 for a loss that is linear in shaded area.

    Which weather file should I use?

    A typical-year file for the site: PVGIS TMY (free from the EU Joint Research Centre, worldwide), an NSRDB CSV (free from NREL, the Americas and more), a TMY3 file, or any CSV with a time column and GHI, DNI and DHI. Without a file the tool uses a clear sky every day, which overstates both the yield and the shading loss.

    Fixed tilt or single-axis tracker?

    The tool prices both on the same land and the same weather. Trackers collect more energy per kWp, need wider spacing and cost more per watt; enter the tracker premium you are quoted and compare the two results directly.

    Does this replace PVsyst, a design review or an engineer?

    No. It is a screening model for picking a spacing and tilt to take into detailed design. It assumes long rows on flat ground, no terrain or horizon, and no bifacial gain. Detailed design software, a qualified engineer and the module and racking makers' guidance decide the final layout.
