How it is calculated

Everything follows ASCE/SEI 7-16, Chapter 7, the edition adopted by the 2018 and 2021 International Building Code. The flat roof snow load is pf = 0.7 Ce Ct Is pg (Eq. 7.3-1), with Ce from Table 7.3-1, Ct from Table 7.3-2 and Is from Table 1.5-2. The sloped roof load is ps = Cs pf, with Cs read from the three panels of Figure 7.4-1 by the thermal factor and whether the surface is unobstructed and slippery; a warm roof gets the slippery curve only with the insulation Section 7.4.1 asks for. The minimum load (Section 7.3.4) and rain-on-snow surcharge (Section 7.10) are applied where their slope and pg limits say.

Drift heights come from Figure 7.6-1, hd = 0.43 ∛lu ∜(pg + 10) − 1.5, with lu no less than 20 ft, at a snow density of 0.13 pg + 14 pcf, at most 30 (Eq. 7.7-1). At a step the leeward drift uses the upper roof's length and the windward drift three-quarters of hd from the lower roof's length (Section 7.7.1); the larger governs. If the drift is taller than the clear height hc above the balanced snow, it is cut to hc and widened to 4hd²/hc, no wider than 8hc, and a drift wider than the lower roof is truncated at the far edge, not tapered. No drift is needed when hc/hb is under 0.2. Parapets and rooftop units use three-quarters of hd from the upwind roof length (Section 7.8), and none is needed for a unit whose side is under 15 ft. Unbalanced gable loads follow Section 7.6.1 and Figure 7.6-2 between 1/2 on 12 and 7 on 12. Sliding snow is 0.4 pf W per foot of eave spread over 15 ft of the lower roof (Section 7.9). Overhangs of poorly insulated warm roofs carry 2pf (Section 7.4.5).

Not modelled: the ground snow load itself (you enter it), ASCE 7-22's reliability-targeted loads and new drift equation, partial loading of continuous beams (Section 7.5), curved, sawtooth and dome roofs, drifts between separated buildings (Section 7.7.2), ponding (Section 7.11), a lower roof with its own slope or thermal factor (it is taken as low slope with the same Ce, Ct and Is), and load combinations with dead, wind or live load. Unverified: checked against hand calculations of each equation in its unit tests, not against a commercial structural program or a sealed calculation.

Common questions

How do I calculate the roof snow load from the ground snow load?

ASCE 7-16 starts with the flat roof snow load, pf = 0.7 Ce Ct Is pg: 0.7 converts ground snow to roof snow, Ce adjusts for wind exposure, Ct for how warm the roof is, and Is for the building's risk category. A sloped roof then multiplies by the slope factor Cs. With pg = 40 psf on a heated, partially exposed house in open terrain, pf is 28 psf.

Where do I find the ground snow load for my site?

From ASCE 7-16 Figure 7.2-1, a free hazard lookup by address that reports the ASCE 7-16 value, or your building department, which in many mountain states publishes its own table. Large parts of the West are case-study areas with no mapped value, so ask the jurisdiction there. This tool does not look pg up for you.

What is a snow drift load at a roof step?

Wind blows snow off a higher roof and drops it against the wall of a lower roof beside it, as a triangle. ASCE 7-16 Section 7.7 sizes the triangle from the length of the upper roof and the ground snow load. In the example on this page a 9 ft step puts 65.9 psf on the lower roof at the wall, 2.4 times the 28 psf a flat roof carries, tapering to the balanced load 7.9 ft out.

Why is the leeward side of a gable roof loaded more than the balanced load?

Wind strips snow from the windward slope and drops it on the leeward one. For a gable with 20 ft or less from eave to ridge and simple rafters, Section 7.6.1 loads the leeward slope with Is pg, the full ground snow load, and the windward slope with nothing. In the example that is 40 psf on one side against a 28 psf balanced load.

When is the minimum roof snow load used?

On monoslope, hip and gable roofs flatter than 15 degrees, Section 7.3.4 sets a floor: Is pg when pg is 20 psf or less, and 20 Is above that. It is a separate uniform case and is never added to drift, sliding or unbalanced loads.

What is the rain-on-snow surcharge?

Where pg is 20 psf or less (but not zero), Section 7.10 adds 5 psf to the balanced load on roofs flatter than W/50 degrees, W being the eave-to-ridge distance in feet, because rain soaks into snow that cannot drain off a flat roof. It applies to the balanced case only.

Does this replace an engineer?

No. It does the Chapter 7 arithmetic on the numbers you enter so you can see the loads and check a design or a quote.
