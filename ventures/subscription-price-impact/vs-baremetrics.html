This calculator vs. Baremetrics

Baremetrics is a subscription-analytics platform. You connect a live billing source — natively Stripe, Braintree, Recurly, or Chargebee, with CSV import or its API for others — and it reports MRR, churn, LTV, and related metrics computed from the subscribers you already have, on an ongoing dashboard. It is a paid product: sign-up starts a free trial, and continued use is a paid subscription, described on baremetrics.com.

That makes it the right choice for one job: watching the metrics your real, connected subscription business is already producing, over time. This page does not do that job. It has no billing connection, keeps no dashboard, and reports on nothing that already happened.

QuestionWhere to answer it
What is my actual MRR, churn rate, and LTV right now, computed from my real Stripe (or other) subscription data? Baremetrics — that is a live reporting job built on data this calculator never sees.
If I raise (or lower) the price from $X to $Y, how much subscriber churn can revenue absorb before it falls? This calculator — type in the two prices, the subscriber count, and fixed costs, get the break-even churn rate instantly, no signup, nothing connected.
I have not made the change yet and do not want to connect a billing account to test the idea — can I still see the number? This calculator — it runs entirely on numbers you type, before or without ever connecting Stripe or any other processor.
Does the price change still clear break-even once my fixed monthly costs are netted out, not just top-line revenue? This calculator's contribution column — five churn scenarios (0, 5, 10, 15, 20%) with fixed costs subtracted at each one.
What actually happened to revenue and churn in the months after I made a price change? Baremetrics, or your own billing reports — this calculator only projects forward from numbers you type in for a scenario; it stores nothing and reconciles nothing against what actually happened.

Many SaaS operators use a tool like this one to size up a price change before touching pricing, and a connected platform like Baremetrics to watch what a real subscriber base does afterward — the two are not substitutes for each other. This calculator's own churn scenarios are a planning aid, not a prediction: your own historical churn rate, wherever you track it (Baremetrics or otherwise), is a better input than the table's five representative columns.

It needs no account and no sign-in: it is a static page, the arithmetic runs in your browser, and once it has loaded it keeps working with the connection off.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Baremetrics?

They answer different questions at different points in the decision. Baremetrics is a subscription-analytics platform: you connect a live billing source such as Stripe, Braintree, Recurly, or Chargebee (or import a CSV), and it reports MRR, churn, and other metrics computed from the subscribers you actually have. It is a paid, ongoing product with a free trial to start, described on Baremetrics's own site. This page has no data connection and reports on nothing you already have. It takes a hypothetical subscriber count, current price, proposed price, and fixed cost you type in, and shows the maximum churn a specific price change can absorb before revenue falls, instantly, for free, with no signup and nothing connected. A SaaS operator deciding whether a price increase is worth trying is in the right place here; one who wants an ongoing dashboard of the metrics their real, connected subscription base is already producing wants Baremetrics.
