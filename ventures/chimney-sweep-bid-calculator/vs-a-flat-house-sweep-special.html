This calculator vs. a flat $199 house sweep special

"$199 house sweep" is not a price for a chimney — it is a price for one easy flue on a walkable roof, quietly applied to a three-story wood stove with glazed creosote.

What a flat house-sweep special is

A flat house price is the fastest way to quote over the phone: one number on a postcard, a truck wrap, or a search ad, next to "chimney sweep near me." Homeowners shop it because it is easy to compare between sweeps, and it is the number a caller holding a competitor's coupon will ask you to match on their own chimney.

The number behind it was set for one job — usually a single easy flue on a walkable one- or two-story roof with light soot, no inspection add-on, no animal — and then reused for every chimney afterward, whether or not the next roof is a third-story climb, glazed, or crawling with a Level 2 camera pass.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter each flue's type and hours, the number of stories, roof access (easy, steep, or snow/ice), creosote condition, the work being done (sweep only, Level 2, or camera), animal removal, and add-ons, along with your own wage, payroll burden, travel, and overhead. It prices the height, the roof, the creosote, and the inspection separately, and returns a walk-away price and price per flue below which the stop loses money — for this chimney, not a generic one.

Common questions

Why does a flat $199 house-sweep special get the price wrong?

Because chimney-sweep cost scales with flue type, stories, roof access, creosote buildup, inspection level, and whether there is an animal to remove — not with "a house." A one-story gas fireplace on an easy roof is a different job from a three-story wood stove with glazed creosote on a snow/ice roof. A flat house price quotes both the same and is wrong on one of them every time.

What does a flat house-sweep special usually leave out entirely?

The height, the roof, the creosote, and the inspection. A published house price is set on one configuration — usually a single easy flue on a walkable one- or two-story roof with light soot — and has no separate line for climbing a third story, working a steep or icy roof, scraping glazed creosote, doing a Level 2 or camera inspection, or removing a nest.

How much do height, roof access, creosote, and inspection actually add over the special?

On this calculator's own worked examples, at a $28 hourly wage, 32% burden, 18% overhead, and a 30% target margin: a one-story gas fireplace on an easy roof with light creosote walks away at $136.06, comfortably under a $199 special. This calculator's own shipped two-story, steep-roof, moderate-creosote default — the form's starting point, not a job picked to make a point — walks away at $193.30, just $5.70 under that same $199. A three-story wood stove on a snow/ice roof, with glazed creosote, a Level 2 inspection, and an animal removal, walks away at $366.12, $167.12 short of the same $199 special.

Is a flat house-sweep special ever the right way to quote?

For something close to the easy job it was probably priced on — one accessible flue, light creosote, no inspection add-on or animal work — a flat rate can land close, and on this calculator's own numbers even the default two-story job barely clears it. The risk is a third story, a steep or icy roof, glazed creosote, an inspection add-on, or an animal removal stacking on top of each other, because a flat house price has no way to see any of them until the sweep is already on the roof.

Does this calculator know what to charge in my market?

No. It has no data on local rates or what competitors charge. It takes the flue, stories, roof access, creosote, inspection, animal work, and travel you enter, plus your own wage, burden, and overhead, and turns them into a walk-away price for the stop you describe — the arithmetic a flat special skips, not a market survey.

Nothing here says or implies that quoting a flat house-sweep special is a bad-faith practice — it is a common, honest shortcut that works until the job stops matching the flue, height, and roof it was set on. This page describes where that shortcut breaks down, not a fault in anyone who uses it.
