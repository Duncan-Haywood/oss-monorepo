How much to charge to pave a driveway

A published $3.50-a-square-foot special treats 800 square feet of sound asphalt as the same job as 1,000 square feet with a failed base, a 4-inch street apron, and 60 feet of sawcut at the garage. It is not. Sealcoat calculators price a thin coating and crack fill. Concrete calculators price cubic yards of slab. Paving the driveway is a method, a thickness, and the extra work you have to do before the paver rolls. Quoting all of that from a per-square-foot special is how the first sawcut eats the field margin.

A job with the same square footage can cost much more

Each extra inch of thickness is mix that never appears on the brochure. On the sample rates an 800-square-foot 2-inch overlay with 20 feet of sawcut prices about $3,076 ($3.85 per square foot, 5.6 crew-hours, 9.7 tons, walk-away $2,215). The same shop’s 1,000-square-foot full-depth at 3 inches with 200 square feet of failed base and an 80-square-foot 4-inch apron prices about $6,530 ($6.05 per square foot, 18.2 hours, 20.1 tons). A quarter of that full-depth bid is extra work — demo, dump, gravel, and the apron. Matching the overlay’s $3.85-a-square-foot special on the full-depth driveway is how that job becomes a loss.

Overlay looks cheaper until the base has failed

Overlaying a failed base is a callback, not a savings. On the same 1,000-square-foot driveway the sample rates price a 3-inch overlay at about $5,287 — $1,243 less than full-depth. That gap is demo and gravel, not profit. Mill-and-overlay of that run prices about $5,787. Overlay is still the right bid on sound 800-square-foot work: milling that refresh prices about $3,437 and full-depth about $3,818 because you would be tearing out a driveway that only needed a lift. Treating every driveway as one per-square-foot number is how the method that looked cheapest on paper is not the one that keeps the margin.

A mill-and-overlay with an apron is not automatically the cheap 2-inch job

A 1,200-square-foot mill-and-overlay at 2 inches with an 80-square-foot 4-inch apron is not the 800-square-foot refresh plus 50%. On the sample rates that mill-and-overlay prices about $5,073 ($3.96 per square foot, 11.2 crew-hours, 16.4 tons). Millings are a dump ticket; the apron is extra tons at a thicker lift. A 2,000-square-foot mill-and-overlay at 2.5 inches with fabric over cracks prices about $10,801 ($5.02 per square foot, 33.8 tons). Neither number lives in a $3.50-a-square-foot special.

Load the wage, then keep a walk-away number

A $38/h hand loaded at 32% payroll burden is $50.16. Margin is a share of the price you send, not a markup on cost. The walk-away price is total cost — below that the job makes nothing before you take a dollar home. Replace every rate with your invoices before you send either number.

What this is not

This is arithmetic on the rates you type. It is not a paving specification, a code determination, a permit, an engineering stamp, or a recommendation of overlay versus full-depth. Thickness, base condition, and what the jurisdiction will accept at the street still decide the method. Verify every figure against your own takeoff.

Common questions

How much to charge to pave a driveway?

Charge from the method (overlay, mill-and-overlay, or full-depth), the square footage, new-asphalt thickness, street apron, failed-base gravel, sawcut, loaded labor, overhead, and a margin that is a share of price. A default 800-square-foot 2-inch overlay at the sample rates in this calculator prices around $3,076 — about $3.85 per square foot.

When is full-depth more than an overlay?

When the existing mat and the base have to come out. On the sample rates a 1,000-square-foot full-depth at 3 inches with 200 square feet of failed base and a 4-inch apron prices around $6,530. Overlaying that same driveway looks cheaper on paper — around $5,287 — until the failed base shows up under the new mat. The per-square-foot special never sees the demo ticket.

How much to charge for mill-and-overlay?

Milling is not an overlay and an overlay is not full-depth. On the sample rates a 1,200-square-foot mill-and-overlay at 2 inches with an 80-square-foot 4-inch apron prices around $5,073 ($3.96 per square foot, 11.2 crew-hours, 16.4 tons). Millings are a dump ticket; the apron is extra tons at a thicker lift.
