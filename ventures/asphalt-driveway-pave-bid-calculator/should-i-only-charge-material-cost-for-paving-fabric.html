Should I only charge material cost for paving fabric?

Paving fabric over cracks has a sticker price: $0.55 a square foot in this calculator's own default rates. On this calculator's fabric-over-cracks preset — a 2,000 sq ft mill-and-overlay plus a 150 sq ft apron, 2,150 sq ft of paved area — that is $1,182.50 of material. It is not what fabric should add to the price you send.

The material line and the price line are not the same number

Using this calculator's own default crew rates (16% overhead, 28% target margin) on its 2,150 sq ft mill-and-overlay preset:

Without fabric$6,274.40$8,714.45

Difference$1,371.70$1,905.14

The $1,182.50 of fabric material raises total cost by $1,371.70, not $1,182.50 — 16% overhead applies to the added material the same as it applies to everything else in the job ($1,182.50 × 1.16 = $1,371.70). Then, because price is total cost divided by (1 − 28%), that $1,371.70 of added cost turns into $1,905.14 of added price. Charging only the $1,182.50 material line — writing "+fabric, $1,182.50" on the bid instead of re-running the job through the calculator — leaves $722.64 of overhead and margin uncollected on this one job.

Overhead and margin apply to every dollar of scope, not just labor

It is easy to treat overhead and margin as something that lives on the labor and the base mix tons, and to pass a late add-on through at its raw material cost because it feels like "just materials." This calculator does not draw that line: overhead is a percentage of total direct cost, and price is total cost marked up to the target margin, whichever line the cost sits on. A material cost added after the estimate is drafted — fabric, a permit fee, extra gravel — carries the same overhead and margin share as everything already in the bid, or the bid quietly stops hitting its own target margin on the jobs that use it.

Common questions

Should I only charge material cost for paving fabric?

No. At this calculator's own default crew rates (16% overhead, 28% target margin), adding paving fabric to a 2,150 square foot mill-and-overlay costs $1,182.50 in material (2,150 sq ft x $0.55/sq ft) but should add $1,905.14 to the price. Charging only the $1,182.50 material line leaves $722.64 of overhead and margin uncollected on that job.

Where does the extra $722.64 come from?

Overhead first, then margin, in that order. The $1,182.50 of fabric material raises total cost by $1,371.70 once 16% overhead is applied on top of it ($1,182.50 + $189.20 overhead). Because price is total cost divided by (1 minus the 28% target margin), that $1,371.70 of added cost turns into $1,905.14 of added price — not the $1,182.50 the material alone cost.

Does fabric change the price on every job?

No. Fabric is its own checkbox, off by default, and adds nothing when left unchecked. It only changes the price on the square footage it actually covers — this calculator's own fabric-over-cracks preset covers a 2,000 square foot driveway plus a 150 square foot apron, 2,150 square feet in total.

Is this specific to fabric, or true of any add-on?

Any material line added after the estimate is drafted has the same gap: overhead and margin are set as a share of total cost and total price, not of the base scope alone, so a cost added without re-running the calculation is added at its raw material price — short by whatever overhead and margin that line was supposed to carry.
