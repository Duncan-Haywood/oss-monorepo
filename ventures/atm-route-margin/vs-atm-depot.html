This calculator vs. ATM Depot's Profit Calculator

One tool pitches switching a single machine to a new processor. This one prices every location on the route you already run, from the placement agreement you actually have. They answer different questions for different moments in the business.

What each one is for

ATM Depot's Profit Calculator is a free lead tool published by ATM Depot, an ATM equipment and processing vendor. It compares what a single machine currently earns to what ATM Depot's own processing and rebate would pay instead, to show how much switching could increase your take — and getting the personalized quote or the accompanying PDF guide asks for your name and email. It is aimed at someone deciding whether to move a machine's processing to ATM Depot, not at pricing a whole route of independently-placed machines from their real agreements.

This calculator does the other job: it prices every location on your route from the placement agreement you already have. It takes the location's commission split or flat placement fee, the per-transaction processor fee you already pay, an annual cost-of-capital rate on the vault cash sitting in the machine, and your own refill and drive time, and turns that into real monthly profit per location — then rolls the whole list up and checks it against a monthly income goal. A single-machine switching estimate has no field for any of that, because it is not built to answer that question.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or ATM Depot's Profit Calculator?

They do different jobs. ATM Depot is an ATM equipment and processing vendor, and its free Profit Calculator compares what a single machine currently earns to what ATM Depot's own processing and rebate would pay instead — a lead tool built to win your processing business, aimed at one machine. This calculator instead prices every location on your own route from the placement agreement you actually have: the location's commission split or flat fee, the processor fee you already pay, the cost of the vault cash sitting in the machine, and your own refill and travel time, then rolls the whole list up and checks it against a monthly income goal. If you are shopping for a new processor, their tool tells you what switching could pay. If you already know your costs and want to know which of your locations are actually worth keeping, this one does.

Does this calculator ask for my email or contact information?

No. Getting a personalized quote or the free PDF guide from ATM Depot's site asks for your name and a valid email address, because it is built to start a sales conversation. This calculator needs neither an email nor an account — it is a static page, the arithmetic runs in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere or saved when you close the tab.

Does ATM Depot's calculator account for the location's commission split, vault cash cost, or refill travel time?

Not as far as its own published page describes: it is framed around one machine's surcharge revenue and processing rebate, to show how much switching to ATM Depot could increase your take. It has no field for a location's percentage commission or flat placement fee, no cost-of-capital rate on the vault cash sitting in the machine, and no refill-visit labor, drive time, or mileage — the costs an independent route operator actually carries per stop. Those are exactly the numbers this calculator asks for, because they are what decide whether one placement is worth keeping.
