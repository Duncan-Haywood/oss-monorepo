Airbnb host bookkeeping: book bookings and fees, not payouts

Airbnb pays you what is left after its host fee, plus or minus adjustments, in batches. If you book the payouts as rental income, your books show less than your guests paid, and the fee you can deduct never appears.

Worked example: one month, three stays

The tool ships a synthetic Airbnb transaction history for June 2026: three reservations, one damage reimbursement and one payout to the bank. Read by the tool's payout reader, it splits like this:

Booked from the bank line alone, the listing shows $1,951.50 of income and no expenses. Booked from the history, it shows $1,950.00 of rental income, $60.00 of other income and $58.50 of fees: the same cash, but income and expenses that match what guests paid and what Airbnb kept. Rental income and deductible fees go on Schedule E for most US hosts, or Schedule C if you provide substantial services.

How to book it

  1. Download your transaction history from Airbnb (Account, Transaction history, CSV) and your bank statement.
  2. Import the bank statement in AI Bookkeeper, then run the payout import on the Airbnb CSV. Each payout is matched to its bank line and split into bookings, fees and adjustments.
  3. Check that the payout equals the activity. If the rows since the previous payout do not net to it, a balance carried over from before the report; the tool says so and does not split it. Download an earlier start date.
  4. Keep occupancy taxes out of income. Where Airbnb collects and remits them, they are not yours.

Questions

Why doesn't my Airbnb payout match my bookings?

Airbnb pays out the booking total less its host fee, adds or subtracts adjustments, and pays in batches. In the worked example, $1,950.00 of bookings less $58.50 of host fees plus a $60.00 reimbursement became a $1,951.50 payout.

Should I book Airbnb payouts or bookings as rental income?

Book the gross booking amount as rental income and the host fee as an expense, so income matches what guests paid you. Booking only payouts understates both income and expenses.
