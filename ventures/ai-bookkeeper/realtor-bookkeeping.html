Real estate agent bookkeeping: commission deposits against what it costs to get them

An agent's income arrives in a few large title-company deposits; the costs are many small subscriptions and fees. The bank statement is where both sit, so the month's profit starts there.

The tool ships a synthetic statement for an agent (fixtures/realtor-checking.csv): two commission deposits and seven costs. Every line gets a suggested account with no rules taught.

What the tool does for an agent

  1. Imports the bank CSV and suggests Sales for commission deposits.
  2. Files the recurring costs: MLS and association dues, lockbox keys, lead sources, CRM subscriptions and E&O insurance.
  3. Reports a P&L and a Schedule C worksheet, with a mileage log and quarterly estimated-tax dates in the tax calendar.

Rules are written from company names; no real agent's statement has been tried.

Questions

Are commission deposits all income I owe tax on?

The deposit is your share after the brokerage split and any transaction fee, so it is already net of the brokerage's cut. Book it as sales here. An agent paid as an independent contractor owes self-employment tax on the profit; the Schedule C worksheet in this tool shows the figure, and your accountant decides what applies to you.

Where do MLS, association dues and lockbox keys go?

The importer files MLS fees under taxes and licenses, NAR and board dues under education and dues, Supra lockbox keys under office, Zillow and Realtor.com leads under advertising, and E&O insurance under insurance. Move any line your accountant files differently.
