Cleaning business bookkeeping: many small deposits against supplies, fuel and leads

A house-cleaning business is paid by Zelle, a lead marketplace and card processor, in small amounts, and spends on supplies, fuel and a few subscriptions. The bank statement holds all of it, so the month's profit starts there.

What the tool does for a cleaning business

  1. Imports the bank CSV and suggests Sales for Zelle, Thumbtack and Stripe deposits.
  2. Files the recurring costs: fuel, scheduling and payroll software, lead fees and liability insurance, and leaves unfamiliar payees such as a warehouse club for your call.
  3. Reports a P&L and a Schedule C worksheet, with a mileage log and quarterly estimated-tax dates in the tax calendar.

Rules are written from company names; no real cleaning business's statement has been tried.

Questions

Are Zelle payments from clients income?

Yes, payments clients send for cleaning are sales. The importer files Zelle payments from a named person, Thumbtack payouts and Stripe transfers under Sales. Check any Zelle line that is a reimbursement or a personal transfer and move it.

Where do supplies, fuel and a lead-marketplace fee go?

Fuel goes to vehicle and fuel, scheduling and payroll tools to software, Thumbtack lead fees to advertising and liability insurance to insurance. Supplies bought at a warehouse club are left for you, since the payee alone does not say what was bought.
