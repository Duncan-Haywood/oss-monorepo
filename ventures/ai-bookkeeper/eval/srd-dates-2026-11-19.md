# Dates on US receipt photos (SRD), 2026-11-19

Receipt dates were measured on SROIE only, and SROIE is Malaysian: day-first
dates, mostly `25/12/2018`. Most of the page's users are in the US, so this run
labelled the dates on ExpressExpense's 200 US receipt photos (SRD, the same photos
the totals were measured on) and scored the reader on them.

## Labels

`srd/dates.jsonl`: four agent sessions each read 50 photos by eye, from the photos
alone with no OCR text in front of them, to one written brief: the sale date
exactly as printed (`printed`), as ISO (`date`), the shop name as printed
(`merchant`), a country guess, and a note on every judgement call. A digit the
reader was unsure of makes the date null rather than a guess. US receipts read
month first. A receipt from a day-first country (UK, Europe, India, Malaysia,
Cambodia, the UAE), or one with a first field over 12, reads day first. One
Canadian receipt with both fields 12 or under is marked `ambiguous` and read month
first.

- 184 of 200 photos carry a date. The other 16 have no date printed, a date cut
  off or blurred, or one digit a reader could not settle.
- 1034, 1041 and 1114 are the same file, byte for byte (`md5sum`). The totals
  labels (`srd/labels.jsonl`) mark 1041 as a duplicate but not 1114, so that
  receipt is scored twice there. In `dates.jsonl` both repeats carry
  `duplicate_of`. Two readers disagreed on that receipt's month (6 or 8), so its
  date is null. 1063 repeats 1053. That leaves **183 dates** and 190 shop names
  scored.
- Second reader: the session that set the brief opened three labels drawn with a
  fixed seed and checked them against the photos. All three matched (1195 Amici
  Conyers 06/10/2018, 1136 Shawarma Factory Apr 10 2019, 1147 New Delhi in Phnom
  Penh 24-Nov-16).
  A misread digit is still possible, and misses are scored as they stand.

## What the reader did (main before this run)

`node eval/srd-dates.mjs eval/srd/dates.jsonl --ocr <rapid>,<t6>,<t4>,<g6>,<g4> --settled --lead`
scores the page as it reads a photo: RapidOCR leading, then tesseract.js in both
modes on the photo and on its grown grey copy. RapidOCR here is the Python
package via `rapidocr_rows.py`; the page runs its JavaScript port.

dataset text gives 601 of 611 dates right before and after. SRD totals

## What was wrong, and the fixes (`receipts.mjs`)

1. **Month-word dates US point-of-sale systems print** were not read at all:
   `Feb23'19`, `MAR.19'17`, `Jun 28 '17`, `16Feb'19`, `25AUG'17` (an apostrophe
   year, Aloha and Micros style), `13-Jul-2018` and `24-Nov-16` (dashes),
   `Jun 26, 15` (two-digit year after a comma), `JANUARY 30,2018`, and
   `SUN JUN 24 20:49:53 2018`. `monthWordDate` rewrites each into a shape the date
   patterns read. A clock after a comma (`Jun 26, 15:30`) is not read as a year.
2. **RapidOCR glues the clock to the year and reads O as zero:** `APR04'166:09PM`,
   `Jun221807:59PM` (the apostrophe lost), `0ct011701:44PM`, `16Feb1921:31`,
   `21-Dec-20145:23:42P`, and `02MAY.21'19` with no break before the month. A run of
   digits after a month word is split only when a clock time follows it.
   `cleanOcr` turns `Jul`, `Nov` and `Dec` between dashes into `Ju1`, `N0v` and
   `0ec`, and those are put back.
3. **Tesseract reads a thermal printer's `$` as `£`**, and `£` made the receipt
   day-first: 05/11/18 became 5 November (1007), 6/02/2018 became 6 February. Now a
   pound or rupee sign counts only before an amount, a North American phone number
   (`626-337-5666`, `(678) 890-2005`, same separator twice) outweighs the sign, and
   a US state with a ZIP code (`Gardena, CA 90249`) makes the receipt month-first
   with no "check the date" note. Words still win: SROIE 435 prints
   `TEL : 607-556 1878` and `SDN BHD`, and stays day-first.
4. **A sweepstakes end date** (`Sweepstakes ends 12/31/2017`, 1090) beat the sale
   date. Dates on lines saying ends, expires, valid, until, thru, return by, redeem
   or offer now go last.
5. **A date the first reading missed** was never taken from another reading. On
   SROIE, taking a date found by the second reading alone filled 9 right and 9 wrong.
   With three or more readings, a date that at least two of the others agree on (and
   no other date as often) is now taken, with a "check the date" note. With
   tesseract's four readings this filled 13 of the dates the first reading missed,
   none wrong. The page writes it into the settled text as a `Date` line under the
   shop name.
6. **A year past next year** (`4601/07/17`, RapidOCR's misreading of `01/07/17`) was
   taken as a date. Now it is not.

## What is left (12 of 183)

- Wrong (2): 1113, where RapidOCR read `6/02/2018` as `16/02/2018` and that fixed
  the order as day-first, and 1171, an Indian receipt (`09/12/2012`, POP TATES,
  Thane) with no sign of its country in the text, read month first.
- None (10): 1001 (`04/25/15`), 1026, 1045, 1062 (`FEB 18/17`), 1115, 1118, 1156,
  1167, 1183 and 1117. In most of these no reading prints the date legibly.
  `FEB 18/17` is a shape not handled (one receipt).

## Reproduce

```
node eval/srd-fetch.mjs <photos>
node eval/sroie-tesseractjs.mjs <photos> <ocr> <node_modules>      # tesseract.js 5.1.1
node eval/srd-canvas-grow.mjs <photos> <grown>
node eval/sroie-tesseractjs.mjs <grown> <ocr2> <node_modules>
for f in <photos>/*.jpg; do python3 rapidocr_rows.py $f > <rapid>/$(basename $f .jpg).txt; done
node eval/srd-dates.mjs eval/srd/dates.jsonl --ocr <rapid>,<ocr>/psm6,<ocr>/psm4,<ocr2>/psm6,<ocr2>/psm4 --settled --lead [--misses date|merchant]
```

Tests: `receipt-us-dates.test.mjs`, 15 cases. The shapes are copied from these
receipts; the shop lines around them are made up.
