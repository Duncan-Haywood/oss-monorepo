Tutor bookkeeping: marketplace payouts against the software, materials and exam fees behind them

A tutor's income lands as payouts from a marketplace such as Wyzant or Outschool plus the odd Venmo from a parent; the costs are video and scheduling software, lesson materials and a few fees. The bank statement holds all of it, so the month's profit starts there.

What the tool does for a tutor

  1. Imports the bank CSV and suggests Sales for marketplace payouts.
  2. Files the recurring costs: meeting and scheduling software, lesson materials, exam fees, association dues and room rent.
  3. Reports a P&L and a Schedule C worksheet, with a mileage log for travel to students and quarterly estimated-tax dates in the tax calendar.

Rules are written from company names; no real tutor's statement has been tried.

Questions

Where do Wyzant and Outschool payouts go?

Payouts from tutoring marketplaces such as Wyzant, Outschool, Varsity Tutors and Preply are filed as sales. They arrive net of the marketplace's cut, so the deposits understate what students paid; the fee is not on your bank statement unless the platform sends a statement you import separately.

How are lesson materials, software and exam fees filed?

Teachers Pay Teachers and drawing-tablet purchases go under supplies and materials, Zoom, Calendly and classroom apps under software and subscriptions, and Praxis fees and teacher-association dues under education and dues. A library meeting room is filed as rent.

What about the Venmo cash-out?

It stays in the review queue. A Venmo cash-out can be a parent paying you for lessons or your own money moving, and the tool cannot tell which, so you file it. Counting all of it as income raises the profit by the same amount.
