What the tool does for a stylist

  1. Imports the bank CSV and suggests Sales for Square deposits.
  2. Files the recurring costs: supply orders, booking software, licence renewals, ads and liability insurance.
  3. Reports a P&L and a Schedule C worksheet, with a mileage log and quarterly estimated-tax dates in the tax calendar.

Rules are written from company names; no real salon's statement has been tried.

Questions

Are card-processor deposits my income?

Square and Vagaro deposits are your sales after the processor's fee, so the deposit understates what clients paid. Book the deposit as sales here; if you need gross sales and fees separately, use the payout importer. A booth renter or independent stylist owes self-employment tax on the profit; the Schedule C worksheet in this tool shows the figure, and your accountant decides what applies to you.

Where do supplies, licence renewals and insurance go?

The importer files Sally Beauty and SalonCentric under supplies, the cosmetology board renewal under taxes and licenses, liability insurance under insurance, booking software under software and Meta ads under advertising. A line it does not recognise, such as a towel laundry service, stays in the review queue for you to file.
