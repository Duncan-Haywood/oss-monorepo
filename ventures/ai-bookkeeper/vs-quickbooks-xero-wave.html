AI Bookkeeper vs QuickBooks, Xero and Wave

Hosted accounting products are accounts you pay for or sign up to, with live bank feeds and a lot around the books. This is a free tool that does one job on your own computer: turn a statement file into categorised, balanced books. It works alone, or as the step before you import into QuickBooks or Xero.

Use it with QuickBooks or Xero

  1. Do the first pass here. Import the statement, review the lines the tool is unsure about, and check the close checks and the reconciliation to the statement's running balance.
  2. Export a journal for QuickBooks Online, Xero or QuickBooks Desktop (IIF) and import it, instead of retyping. See bank statement to QuickBooks or Xero.
  3. Moving the other way? The tool can start a set of books from the account list and files your old system exports, so a switch does not begin from a blank chart.

Layouts of those imports were written from the products' documented columns and tested on hand-made files, not on a real account; try a small import first.

Who should keep the hosted product

If you send invoices and take payments through your accounting product, run payroll in it, need live bank feeds, or share the books with an accountant who works inside it, keep it. This tool is for the person who keeps books in a spreadsheet, wants a first pass before a bookkeeper or accountant, or prefers statements never leave their machine.

Questions

Is this a replacement for QuickBooks, Xero or Wave?

Not for most businesses. QuickBooks, Xero and Wave are hosted accounting products with live bank connections, invoicing, payments, payroll or tax add-ons and support, sold as accounts or services. AI Bookkeeper is a free, open-source (MIT) tool that runs in your browser or on the command line: you give it a bank or card statement file, it categorises the lines, keeps a double-entry ledger, and gives you a profit and loss, a balance sheet and close checks. It sends nothing anywhere and has no bank connection. If a spreadsheet is what you keep now, or you need the first pass before handing books to an accountant, it fits; if you invoice customers or run payroll through your accounting product every week, keep it.

Can I use it together with QuickBooks or Xero?

Yes, that is the main use. It exports journals for QuickBooks Online, Xero and QuickBooks Desktop (IIF), plus a plain CSV, so statements you categorised here can be imported instead of retyped. It can also start from the files those products export: an account list, and a chart from your old system.

What does it not do?

It has no live bank feed (you download statements yourself), no invoice sending or customer payments portal, and it does not file taxes. Payroll and sales-tax worksheets exist but are calculators for your own review, not filing services. It is not accounting or tax advice.

Why would a small business choose it?

Three reasons that can be checked: it costs nothing and has no account; your statements stay on your computer; and every categorisation is explained by the rule, payee memory or model that made it, with an AI step that is optional and never posts by itself. Whether that beats a hosted product depends on what you need from it.
