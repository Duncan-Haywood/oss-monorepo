What this does and doesn't do

Box 1a of each 1099-K is a gross figure — before refunds, fees, discounts, or any sales tax the marketplace itself collected and remitted for you are backed out, per the IRS's own General Instructions for Forms 1099-K. This worksheet backs those out, then subtracts the costs you enter (fees, cost of goods sold, shipping) to estimate net profit. It does not know about your 1099-NEC, W-2, or other income, does not determine your actual tax liability, and does not file anything. Verify every figure against the platform's own 1099-K and payout statements before relying on it.

Common questions

Why is my 1099-K higher than what I actually made?

Form 1099-K Box 1a reports the gross amount of payment-card and third-party network transactions before any adjustment for refunds, fees, discounts, or credits — that is how the IRS's own instructions for the form define it. Many marketplaces also collect and remit sales tax on your behalf as a "marketplace facilitator," and that tax often passes through the same payout and lands in Box 1a too, even though it was never your money. None of that is your taxable income; this worksheet backs it out.

Do I have to report the full Box 1a amount as income?

No — Box 1a is a gross reporting figure, not a determination of your taxable income. What you actually owe tax on is your net profit: gross receipts (after backing out amounts that were never yours, like refunds and marketplace-remitted sales tax) minus your costs, such as cost of goods sold, platform fees, and shipping you paid. This worksheet estimates that net figure; your own records and your tax preparer confirm it.

I sell on more than one platform and got more than one 1099-K — do I add them together?

Generally your total gross receipts across all platforms and payment methods count toward your business income, whether or not each individual platform issued a 1099-K. This worksheet lets you enter every platform's 1099-K as its own row and totals the reconciled result across all of them, so you have one combined figure instead of several separate ones.

Is this worksheet tax advice or a substitute for filing?

No. It performs arithmetic on the numbers you enter — it does not know your other income, does not determine your actual tax liability, and does not file anything with the IRS or any platform. Verify every figure against the platform's own 1099-K and payout statements, and treat the result as a worksheet for your own records or your tax preparer, not a conclusion.
