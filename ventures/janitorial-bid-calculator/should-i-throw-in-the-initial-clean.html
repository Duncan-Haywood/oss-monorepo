Should I throw in the initial clean to win the monthly?

Contractors search this because a facility manager who wants the building brought back from vacant or neglected usually offers a simple trade: do the deep clean, keep the monthly you already quoted. That sounds cheap if the first visit were just another Tuesday. It is not.

A takeover clean is a project, not a regular visit. Dust on vents, baseboards, behind furniture, extra restroom detail, and leftover construction grit take several regular cleans of labor plus extra chemicals. On a typical 8,000 sq ft office — $17 wage, 28% burden, 15% overhead, 20% target margin — one regular visit is 2.4 hours. A 5× takeover is 12.0 hours plus $80 of extra supplies.

Throwing it in at today’s monthly loses money in month 1. The periodic price ($1,720.00) was built on $1,376.00 of monthly cost. The takeover adds $392.29 of cost. Month 1 walk-away is $1,768.29. Keep today’s monthly and month 1 loses $48.29.

If they cancel after 30 days, that giveaway is the job. Invoice the initial and a one-month stay still leaves $442.07. Throw it in and the same 30 days finish $48.29 under water. Baking it into the monthly over 12 months is $1,760.86 — $40.86 above today — and does not help if they leave after month 1.

Common questions

Should I throw in the initial clean to win a janitorial contract?

Not at today's monthly unless you can stand to lose money in month 1. A neglected 8,000 sq ft office's 5× takeover (12 labor hours plus $80 of extra supplies) is a $490.36 one-time if it still holds a 20% margin. Throwing it into a $1,720 monthly loses $48.29 in month 1 against a $1,768.29 walk-away.

What if they cancel after 30 days?

The giveaway is then the whole relationship. On the same default office the thrown-in first month finishes $48.29 under water, versus $442.07 if the initial was invoiced as a one-time. Baking it into the monthly over 12 months is $1,760.86 — $40.86 above today's monthly — and still does not help if they leave after month 1.
