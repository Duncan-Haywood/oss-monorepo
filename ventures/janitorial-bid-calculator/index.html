FOR COMMERCIAL CLEANING CONTRACTORS

Bid the building, not the square footage.

Price a janitorial contract the way the trade actually does: each space divided by the production rate your crew really hits, then wage burden, supplies, overhead, and the margin you intend to keep. You get the monthly price, what it works out to per square foot and per labor hour, and the number below which the job loses money.

The building

One row per space type. Production rate is cleanable square feet one cleaner covers per hour — pick a space type to start from a common figure, then replace it with a rate timed from your own crew.

Your costs

Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off — the part of a cleaner’s cost that never shows up on their pay rate. Underestimating it is the most common reason a janitorial bid that looked profitable was not.
