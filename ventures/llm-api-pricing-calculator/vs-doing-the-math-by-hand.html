This calculator vs. doing the math by hand

Reading each provider's pricing page for its current rates is still on you either way -- this tool never fetches or asserts a vendor's price. What it takes off your plate is keeping fresh, cached, and output tokens priced at their own separate rates across every provider, instead of blending them into one estimate under time pressure.

What each approach actually involves

Doing it yourself means opening each provider's pricing page, noting its fresh-input rate, its cached-input rate, and its output rate, then for every provider you are comparing: multiplying your fresh input tokens by the fresh rate, your cached input tokens by the cached rate, your output tokens by the output rate, and adding the three together. That is three multiplications and an addition per provider, repeated for each one, with the fresh/cached split easy to lose track of when you are doing it in your head or on a scratch pad.

This calculator takes the same rates a pricing-page lookup gives you and the same monthly fresh-input, cached-input, and output token counts you would use by hand, and runs the per-provider arithmetic for up to four providers at once, ranked cheapest first, with the fresh/cached split always kept separate.

Neither provider's ranking flips in this example -- Provider B is cheaper both ways -- but the blended-by-hand estimate overstates Provider A's real bill by $48.60/month and Provider B's by $32.40/month, both about an 86% overstatement. That gap is not a rounding error: it is exactly each provider's cache savings figure, the dollar amount you would have earned by pricing the 18,000,000 cached tokens at the cached rate instead of the fresh rate. Blending fresh and cached input into one total silently discards that discount for every provider in the comparison, which is the exact mistake a workload built around an agentic loop is most likely to make when the arithmetic is done under time pressure -- see why cached tokens usually dominate an agentic workload's bill for why this workload's split looks the way it does.

Common questions

Why not just read each provider's pricing page and do the arithmetic myself?

You still have to -- this calculator does not fetch or assert any vendor's price, so the rates always come from you reading each provider's own current pricing page. What doing it by hand also requires is keeping three separate rates per provider straight (fresh input, cached input, output), multiplying each against your own token counts, and adding the three together -- repeated for every provider you are comparing, with no check on whether a rate got applied to the wrong token class.

What does doing this by hand usually get wrong?

The shortcut most people reach for under time pressure is blending fresh and cached input into one total and pricing all of it at the one rate they remember, which is usually the fresh-input rate since that is the headline number on a pricing page. Cached tokens are commonly billed at a fifth to a tenth of that rate, so blending them in at the fresh rate overstates the input cost by exactly the cache discount you would have earned -- see the worked example above for how large that gap gets on a cache-heavy workload.

How much does blending fresh and cached tokens actually cost you in accuracy?

It scales with how large a share of your input is cached. On a workload that is 90% cached input, blending both into one total priced at the fresh rate overstated the true monthly bill by 86% for both providers in the worked example above -- a materially wrong number for a decision that is usually about picking the cheaper of two options, not just getting a ballpark.

Does this calculator upload anything I enter?

No. Every field runs in your browser, the same as doing the arithmetic on paper -- there is no account, no server call, and no analytics vendor. The only difference is the calculator does not lose track of which rate applies to which token class.
