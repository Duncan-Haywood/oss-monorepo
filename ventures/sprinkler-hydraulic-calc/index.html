How it is worked out, and how it was checked against EPANET

1. Water supply and design

The supply is a flow test: static pressure with no flow, and a residual pressure while a measured flow runs. The design is the density over the area each head covers, the minimum pressure at any head, the hose allowance added at the source, and the safety margin you want left over.

2. Nodes and pipes

One per line. node NAME elev=FT, and k=5.6 makes it a sprinkler (add area=SQFT if it covers a different area). pipe NAME FROM TO len=FT fit=FT size=1-1/4 sch=sch40 c=120, where fit is the fittings' equivalent length and id=1.38 gives any inside diameter instead of a size. x= and y= place a node on the sketch. Sizes: 1, 1-1/4, 1-1/2, 2, 2-1/2, 3, 4, 6, 8 in schedule 10 or 40.

Questions

Is this a stamped hydraulic calculation?

No. It is an estimate for checking a layout, learning the method or sizing a bid. A licensed fire protection designer prepares the calculation the authority having jurisdiction reviews, under NFPA 13 or your local code.

Why can it do grids when hand calculations cannot?

A hand calculation walks back from the most remote head and balances each junction, which only works cleanly for a tree. This tool solves every pipe flow and node pressure at once by Newton's method (the global gradient algorithm that EPANET uses), so loops and grids need nothing special.

How do I know the solver is right?

Its tests run 59 seeded grids and trees through EPANET 2.2 and agree to within 0.00001 psi and gpm, and a single branch line matches the head-by-head hand method exactly. The how-it-works page gives the details.

What does it leave out?

It takes the heads you list as the open ones: it does not search for the most remote design area, and it does not add fittings for you (type their equivalent length as fit=). There are no pumps, backflow preventers or valves as separate items; put their loss into the supply or a pipe's equivalent length. Velocity pressure is left out throughout, which NFPA 13 permits.

Is my layout uploaded?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser. An optional anonymous counter in this browser's local storage records only that a calculation ran and the day.
