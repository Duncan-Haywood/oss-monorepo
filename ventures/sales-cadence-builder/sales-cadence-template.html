B2B sales cadence template: 8 touches over 15 business days

A sales cadence is the order of touches you make with one prospect before you stop. This one mixes four channels, because people answer different ones: email, phone (with a voicemail when they do not pick up), a LinkedIn connection note and a mailed letter. Every touch is on a business day, every call is inside the prospect's own business hours, and the last email says you will stop, and you do.

To build it for your own list with your own words, paste the list into the Sales Cadence Builder. It does the dates, the time zones and the checks, and gives you a CSV task list and a calendar file.

Step 1: the first email

Short, specific and honest. Say why you are writing to this person now (the trigger), what you do in one sentence, and ask one question that is easy to answer. Your postal address and an opt-out line go at the bottom of every cold email; CAN-SPAM requires both.

Subject: Route planning at Brightline Logistics

Hi Dana,

I saw you opened a second depot in Aurora, so this seemed like a good time to write.

I'm Sam Ortiz at RouteLoop. RouteLoop plans delivery routes for regional fleets. We help teams spend less time building routes by hand.

Is route planning something you're working on this quarter? If it is, would a 15-minute call next week be useful?

Thanks,
Sam Ortiz

--
Sam Ortiz, RouteLoop
500 Main Street, Suite 210, Boulder, CO 80302
Not the right person, or rather not hear from me? Reply "no thanks" and I won't email you again.

The call script and voicemail

Ask for permission to talk, ask one question about how they handle the problem today, and make one ask. If they do not pick up, leave a voicemail of about thirty seconds and say your number twice. For this prospect in Colorado, the builder schedules the first call for 2026-10-28 between 09:00–17:00 Denver time, inside 9:00 to 17:00 where they are.

Opener: Hi Dana, this is Sam Ortiz from RouteLoop. I'm calling about route planning. Do you have half a minute, or is now a bad time?
Why them: I saw you opened a second depot in Aurora. RouteLoop plans delivery routes for regional fleets.
Question: How are you handling route planning today?
Ask: If it's worth a look, would a 15-minute call next week work?
If not now: ask who owns route planning or when to check back, and log the answer.
Voicemail: Hi Dana, it's Sam Ortiz from RouteLoop, calling about route planning at Brightline Logistics. We help teams spend less time building routes by hand. I'll send a short email as well. You can reach me on (720) 555-0188. Again, that's Sam Ortiz, (720) 555-0188.

Rules that apply to every cadence

  • Remove people first. Take out anyone on your suppression list or marked do-not-contact, and duplicates, before you plan a single touch. In the example, 2 of 7 rows are left out for exactly these reasons.
  • Email needs a lawful basis outside the US. Canada's CASL needs consent for commercial email, several EU countries need consent even for business email, and in the UK a personal address counts as an individual. Those prospects still get calls, letters and a LinkedIn note.
  • Call in their hours, not yours. The US federal limit is 8am to 9pm in the called person's time and some states end at 8pm; 9am to 5pm their time keeps you inside all of them and inside their working day.
  • No invented proof. If you have no real result from a similar customer, ask who owns the problem instead. Never write a number or a customer name you cannot show.

Questions

How many touches should a sales cadence have?

Most B2B cadences use six to ten touches over two to four weeks. Fewer than five gives up before a busy person has seen you; more than about twelve starts to feel like pressure. The template here uses eight touches over fifteen business days and then stops.

How long should I wait between sales follow-ups?

Two to three business days between early touches and three to four between later ones. Spacing that widens over time reads as persistent rather than pushy, and keeping touches on business days means they arrive when people work.

Which channels should a cadence mix?

Email, phone and one social or physical touch. Email is cheap and easy to reply to, a call gets a decision in one conversation, a LinkedIn connection makes your name familiar, and a mailed letter stands out because so few sellers send one.

What should the last email in a cadence say?

That you will stop, and then stop. Say you will assume the problem is not a priority, that you will not follow up again, and that they can reply if that changes. It is honest, it respects their time, and it gives a busy person an easy moment to answer.
