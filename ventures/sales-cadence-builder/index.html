Questions

What is a sales cadence?

A sales cadence is the planned order of touches you make with one prospect: which day you email, call, connect on LinkedIn or send a letter, and what each touch says. A typical B2B cadence is six to ten touches over two to four weeks, mixing channels, and ending with a short note that says you will stop.

Does this tool send emails or make calls for me?

No. It plans and drafts; you send. You get a dated task list, a calendar file and a draft for every touch, and you send each email from your own mailbox, place each call yourself and post each letter. That keeps you in control of every message and inside the sending rules of your email provider and LinkedIn.

When is it legal to make a sales call?

In the United States the federal TCPA rule allows telephone solicitation between 8am and 9pm in the called person's local time, and some states, such as Florida, Oklahoma and Maryland, narrow that to 8am to 8pm. This tool schedules calls only between 9am and 5pm in the prospect's own time zone, shown in yours, and uses the overlap for a state that spans two time zones. Consumer numbers must also be checked against the Do Not Call registry.

Why did it drop the emails for some prospects?

Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) needs express or implied consent before commercial email, several EU countries such as Germany require consent even for business email, and in the UK a personal address counts as an individual who must opt in. Without a consent value in your list those prospects keep their calls, letters and LinkedIn note but no email. Add the basis in a consent column and run it again.

What does every cold email need?

Under CAN-SPAM a commercial email needs an honest subject line, a sender you can identify, your valid postal address and a clear way to opt out that you honour. The tool appends your name, address and an opt-out line to every email and blocks a draft whose subject pretends to be a reply.
