Questions people ask

Should I sell my mineral rights or royalty for a lump sum?

This tool does not tell you to. It shows what the offer is worth against the royalty checks you would otherwise keep receiving: the present value of those checks at your own discount rate, the annual return the buyer earns at the offered price, and the same comparison after tax. Mineral buyers typically need a high return to buy, so an offer well below your own present value is paying you less than the stream is worth to you.

How do mineral buyers price an offer?

Usually as a number of months of your current royalty income, or a price per net mineral acre. The tool shows both for your offer, then values the actual decline of the well so you can see what that multiple really buys.

What is the decline curve in the tool?

A modified Arps curve, the standard engineering shape for wells: steep early decline that flattens over time (hyperbolic), then a steady terminal decline. You type the first-year decline, the curve shape (b) and the terminal rate. Look at your last twelve statements to estimate the decline; a shale well often loses 30 to 60 percent in year one.

Why does the tool ask about a new well?

Unleased or undrilled acreage can add wells, and a buyer prices that in. The tool counts it as an expected value (monthly check times your chance of it happening) and shows the offer with and without it, so you can see how much of the price rests on a well that has not been drilled.

What do the oil and gas price multipliers do?

They scale every future check up or down, as if prices ran 40 percent lower or 50 percent higher than today's. The grid shows the present value across discount rates and prices so a single price guess does not decide the answer.

How is tax handled?

You type a rate for the lump sum applied to the offer minus your basis, a rate for ordinary royalty income, and the share of royalty income sheltered by percentage depletion. It is a rough comparison only. Whether a sale is capital gain or ordinary income depends on the facts, so confirm with a tax professional before signing anything.

Is this legal, tax or financial advice?

No. It is arithmetic on numbers you type in, with simplified assumptions: monthly end-of-month checks, a smooth decline curve and a constant price drift. It uses no well data or market prices. Have an oil and gas attorney review any deed or purchase agreement, and check what the offer conveys: the minerals, the royalty, the term and any reversion.
