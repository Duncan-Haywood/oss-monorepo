Send a contract link. Get a signature you can prove wasn't changed.

Paste the text, send the link. The signer reads it, types their name and ticks a consent box. You get a certificate with the document's SHA-256 fingerprint and a hash-chained audit trail that anyone can re-check. It runs on your own server, with no per-envelope fee and no account for the signer.

Questions

Is a typed-name signature legally valid?

In many places, including the US (ESIGN Act and UETA) and the EU/UK (eIDAS/ECA as a simple electronic signature), a typed name with clear intent to sign and a record of it is generally accepted for ordinary contracts. Some documents are excluded (wills, certain family-law and real-estate papers, notices a statute says must be on paper). Sign Keeper is not legal advice; check what your document needs.

Can someone alter a document after it is signed?

Not undetectably. The text has a SHA-256 fingerprint and every audit event hashes the one before it. The certificate page and /c/<token>.json recompute all of it from stored data and report any mismatch. It is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: whoever controls your server can still delete the file, so keep certificate copies.

What does it store about the signer?

The typed name, the time, the consent wording they ticked and a salted HMAC of their network address that cannot be turned back into the address. Not a raw IP, not a device fingerprint.

How is this different from DocuSign or PandaDoc?

Those add identity checks, templates, bulk send, field placement and qualified signatures. Sign Keeper does one thing: send a link, collect a typed signature with consent, keep a verifiable certificate, on a server you own with no per-envelope fee.
