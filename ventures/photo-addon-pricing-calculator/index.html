Common questions

Why does a flat add-on price not tell me whether it's worth offering?

Because the flat price is usually set by comparing to what other studios charge, not by counting what the add-on actually costs. Culling and editing an extra hour of coverage, or a second shooter's entire take, routinely runs to one to two times the shoot hours that produced it -- time that never shows up on an invoice the way a subcontractor payment does, but is just as real.

Does this tool write my client contract or tell me what to charge?

No. This calculator only prices your own add-on economics from numbers you supply. It does not do tax planning, business-entity formation, or contract review, and it does not replace a signed client contract, your own liability or gear insurance, or your accountant.

Why does my own time count as a cost when no cash actually changes hands?

Because your target hourly rate is what you have decided your time is worth, and an add-on that pays less than that rate is quietly cutting your own pay to take it. Counting only cash costs like a second shooter's fee or an album vendor's bill -- and not your own culling and editing hours -- is the most common way an add-on's real profit gets overstated.

Does anything about my add-on or comparison rows get uploaded?

No. Every field on this page runs in your browser only. Nothing is sent to a server, there is no account, and the add-on rows you type into the comparison table are never saved anywhere -- they exist only in the page while it is open. The add-on inputs above are different: those are saved locally on this device, so you don't retype them, and a shareable link can carry them to another device.
