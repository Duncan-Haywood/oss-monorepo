Grailed Fee Calculator vs. tracking Grailed sales in a spreadsheet

Most Grailed sellers who price more than a handful of pieces end up with a spreadsheet: a column for the commission, one for processing, one for what the piece cost. Since the commission became a 6% / 9% step on 20 May 2026, the formula is no longer a single multiplication, and a copied "Grailed takes 9%" cell quietly overstates the fee on everything under $120.

What each approach actually does

A spreadsheet can reproduce every rate this calculator applies: 6% under $120 with a $1.99 minimum, 9% from $120, and 3.49% + $0.49 processing. It needs a tier formula, a minimum, and a rule for whether buyer-paid shipping counts. It also means keeping the rates current when Grailed changes them.

This calculator has the tiers built in, shows the payout drop at $120, and solves backwards for the price that hits a target profit on the correct side of that step. It compares the current schedule with an international buyer and the old flat 9% for the same piece in one click.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet do this math instead?

Yes. The 6% and 9% commission tiers, the $1.99 minimum and the 3.49% + $0.49 processing fee are each ordinary arithmetic. Building it means a nested IF for the tier, a MAX for the minimum, and a separate formula to back into a target-profit price.

What does a spreadsheet not do that this calculator does?

Solve backwards across the $120 step. Because payout drops at $120, the price that hits a target profit can be below it or above it, and a single rearranged formula gets one side wrong. The calculator solves each tier and picks the lowest valid price, and it shows the dead zone for your shipping setup.

Is a spreadsheet ever the better choice?

Yes, when your situation has a wrinkle this calculator does not model, such as a bundle in one sale, a promotional credit, or a fee change since the rates here were last checked. A spreadsheet you update yourself can represent any rule you can describe.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Item prices, costs, and payouts are never uploaded, and the optional usage counter records only which features were used and the day, never your numbers.
