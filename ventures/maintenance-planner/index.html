Reliability: should you replace on a schedule?

Times between failures come from work orders marked as failures (operating hours when the asset has a meter, days otherwise). The time since the last failure counts as survived, not as a failure. The Weibull shape says whether failures rise with age; the interval shown is a 90% likelihood-ratio interval, so with few failures it is wide on purpose.

Questions

Is there a free CMMS with no per-user fee?

This one. It runs in your browser with no account: add assets, preventive maintenance plans and spare parts, open and close work orders, and export everything as a JSON or CSV file. There is no user limit because there is no server; the data stays on your computer until you export it.

How do I schedule maintenance by run hours and by date at the same time?

Give the plan both a calendar interval and a meter interval. The planner fits a usage rate to the asset's meter readings, projects the day the meter reaches the next service, and makes the plan due on whichever comes first.

What is the difference between a fixed and a floating schedule?

A fixed schedule keeps its dates from the start date whatever day the job was done, which suits inspections due on set dates. A floating schedule counts the next interval from the day the job was last completed, which suits wear items.

How is PM compliance worked out?

Each completed preventive job counts as on time when it was done no later than its due date plus 10% of the interval (or 10% of the meter interval). Occurrences that came due and were never done count as missed. Compliance is on-time jobs over all jobs.

Does preventive replacement help every machine?

No. Replacing on a schedule only prevents failures that rise with age. The Reliability section fits a Weibull distribution to each asset's times between failures, counting the time since the last failure as still running, and reports whether the shape is clearly above 1 (wear-out), clearly below 1 (early-life failures) or cannot be told from random. With planned and failure costs it also gives the replacement age with the lowest cost per day.

Where is my data stored?

In this browser's local storage on your computer, and in the files you export. Nothing is uploaded. Clearing the browser's site data deletes it, so export a copy regularly.

Not modelled, and how it compares

  • One person, one browser. There is no shared login, mobile technician app, purchase orders or approvals. Paid CMMS products such as UpKeep, MaintainX, Limble and Fiix do those, typically priced per user per month. Share the data file, or run the command-line version on a shared drive.
  • Usage rates are straight lines fitted to recent meter readings; a machine whose duty changes will be projected on its old rate until new readings arrive.
  • Reliability assumes each repair restores the asset to as good as new for the cause counted (the renewal assumption behind a Weibull fit of times between failures). Pooling different causes can mislead; give failures a cause code and read each cause once it repeats.
  • The replacement age is the classic age-replacement policy (replace at age T or on failure). It ignores inspection, condition monitoring and opportunistic replacement.
  • Statutory inspections (pressure vessels, lifts, fire systems) follow the law and the inspector, not this page.
