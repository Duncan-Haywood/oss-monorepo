FOR SOLAR INSTALLERS

Bid the roof and the service, not the watts.

A flat dollars-per-watt quote treats a 10 kW composition-roof job with a clean 200 A panel as the same job as a 10 kW tile-roof job that needs a 100 A → 200 A upgrade. Price the array, the roof difficulty, and the electrical extras separately — then see the price, the dollars per watt, and the number below which the job loses money.

The system

Start from a common residential size, then replace module count and watts with the layout you actually drew.

The roof and the service

Roof factor multiplies array labor only. Electrical extras are a fixed hour-and-material adder — they do not get cheaper on a smaller array.

Your costs

Payroll burden covers employer taxes, workers’ compensation, liability insurance, and paid time off. Matching a cheaper per-watt quote after leaving it out is how a sold job becomes a loss.
