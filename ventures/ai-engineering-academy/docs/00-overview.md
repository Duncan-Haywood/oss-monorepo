# The Constitution

This is the founding constitution of AI Engineering Academy — the document
intended to guide years of development. Where [PROJECT_BRIEF.md](../PROJECT_BRIEF.md)
is the seed (the founding vision in compressed form), this constitution is
the expanded reasoning behind it: the philosophy, the pedagogy, the
technical design, and the business strategy, argued from first principles
rather than asserted.

It exists because the educational philosophy is expected to be one of the
platform's most durable advantages, and durable advantages deserve to be
thought through before code gets written, not discovered by accident along
the way.

## How to read this

Each section stands alone and can be read independently, but they build on
each other in this order:

1. **[Philosophy and Vision](01-philosophy-and-vision.md)** — Why the
   current shape of engineering education is a product of institutional
   constraints rather than pedagogical correctness; what the platform looks
   like concretely to a learner and to the company; what has to be true for
   this to become the default way engineers are trained worldwide.

2. **[Learning Science, the AI Mentor, and the Knowledge Graph](02-learning-science-and-ai-mentor.md)** —
   The pedagogical foundations (deliberate practice, mastery learning,
   spaced repetition, transfer), a concrete mastery model, the AI mentor's
   architecture and interaction patterns, and how engineering knowledge
   across disciplines is represented as a graph that drives sequencing and
   readiness.

3. **[Curriculum and Assessment](03-curriculum-and-assessment.md)** — The
   beginner-to-world-class project progression with worked examples, why
   exams are a poor signal of engineering capability and what replaces
   them, and how a learner's body of work becomes a legible, verifiable
   portfolio.

4. **[Technical Architecture](04-technical-architecture.md)** — Simulation
   environments across disciplines, how the platform connects to real
   hardware and physical prototyping, and the software architecture of the
   platform itself, including cost-to-serve and safety considerations.

5. **[Business and Strategy](05-business-and-strategy.md)** — Industry
   partnerships, how portfolio evidence becomes a credential employers
   trust, a business model built on cost-gating rather than value-gating
   (free where it doesn't cost us money, paid only where it genuinely
   does) with a funded scholarship program and regional pricing, and
   go-to-market strategy.

6. **[Market Landscape and 10-Year Roadmap](06-market-and-roadmap.md)** —
   Researched competitive analysis (MIT, Coursera, Khan Academy, edX,
   Brilliant, Udacity, and others) explaining why none of them occupy this
   position, followed by a staged, honestly-hedged 10-year roadmap. Near-term
   priority in that file: put the already-built calculators on a public URL
   before more curriculum.

## The one metric that matters

Every section here should ultimately serve one question, stated in the
brief and repeated because it's easy to drift from under normal startup
pressure:

> After 5 years on this platform, is this person among the top 1% of
> engineers for their experience level?

Not course completions. Not engagement. Not certificates issued. If a
proposed feature, partnership, or business model decision doesn't trace
back to that question, it doesn't belong here.

## Status

This is a v1 draft, produced to establish a complete first pass across all
major dimensions of the business before writing much code. It is meant to
be argued with, revised, and deepened — not treated as settled. Open
questions and risks are flagged inline throughout rather than smoothed
over; resolving them is the next layer of work.
