Common questions

How long does it take a capacitor to fully charge?

Never — not in a strict mathematical sense. An RC charging curve is exponential, so it always leaves some gap to the supply voltage, however small. Engineers use a working definition instead: after 5 time constants (τ = R×C) a capacitor is considered fully charged, having reached 99.33% of the supply voltage. With this page's own defaults (R = 10 kΩ, C = 100 nF, 5 V supply — τ = 1 ms) that is 5 ms, at 4.9663 V.

How many time constants is "fully charged"?

By convention, five. At 1τ a capacitor has only reached 63.21% of the supply; at 3τ, 95.02%; at 5τ, 99.33%, which is close enough to call finished for almost any purpose; at 7τ, 99.91%. Each additional time constant closes most of what remains of the gap, so the returns shrink fast — going from 5τ to 7τ only buys another 0.58 percentage points, from 4.9663 V to 4.9954 V on this page's own 5 V default.

Does a capacitor ever reach exactly 100% of the supply voltage?

No, not in any finite time. The charging current is proportional to the voltage still missing — (supply minus capacitor voltage) divided by R — so as the capacitor voltage climbs toward the supply, the current driving it shrinks too, and it approaches the supply asymptotically rather than arriving. In practice, leakage, the source's own tolerance, and measurement resolution make the difference undetectable long before the math would call it finished, which is why 5τ is treated as "done."

Why does charging slow down the longer it runs?

Because the driving voltage across the resistor is the gap that's left, not the full supply. Early on that gap is nearly the whole supply, so the charging current — and the rate the voltage climbs — is at its highest; by 5τ the gap is down to 0.67% of the supply, so the current driving further charging is down to 0.67% of where it started. This page's own defaults show it directly: the first time constant closes 63.21 percentage points of the gap, but the fifth adds only 1.16 more (from 98.17% to 99.33%).
