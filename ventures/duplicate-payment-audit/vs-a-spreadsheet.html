This tool vs. a spreadsheet

Most small businesses already look for duplicate payments the same way: export the AP history, sort by vendor, and scan for two rows that look alike. That catches the easiest case — the same vendor, same amount, same invoice number, a few rows apart. It is much weaker at the cases that actually cost money: a re-entered invoice number with one digit changed, a resubmission that lands weeks later instead of days, or telling a real duplicate apart from a rent payment that is supposed to repeat.

Common questions

Can't I just sort my AP export by vendor and scan for repeats?

Sorting by vendor finds the easy case: the same vendor, the same amount, the same invoice number, sitting a few rows apart. It does not find a duplicate that only looks different because of how it was re-entered — a typo'd invoice number, a payment recorded three weeks later under a new reference, or a partial-amount resubmission — because those rows sort next to entirely unrelated payments instead of next to each other. This tool runs three separate matching passes (exact invoice match, near-identical invoice numbers, and same-amount payments within a date window) across the whole export at once, so a duplicate that a vendor-sort would scatter across hundreds of rows still gets grouped and flagged.

Why is a typo'd invoice number hard to catch by eye in a spreadsheet?

INV-40219 and INV-40129 look identical at a glance in a column of a few hundred rows, and a plain vendor sort places them wherever their own invoice number falls — not next to each other. Catching that by eye means noticing a one-character difference in a string you were not specifically looking for. This tool measures the edit distance between invoice numbers within the same vendor and amount, and flags anything within two characters of another payment as a likely re-entry of the same bill, whether or not the two rows happen to be near each other.

How does this avoid flagging my rent or a subscription as a duplicate?

It does not avoid it entirely -- a genuine duplicate and a recurring charge can look identical from amount and vendor alone -- but it does not treat them the same way. An exact invoice-number match or a near-identical invoice number is high confidence, because a real recurring bill is issued under a new invoice number each cycle. A same-amount payment with no invoice link at all, landing within your chosen date window, is labeled "review" and says explicitly to check whether that vendor is paid this amount on a regular schedule before treating it as anything else. A spreadsheet sort returns the same rows either way, with no confidence tier to tell you which is which.

Does this replace the AP ledger or spreadsheet I already keep?

No. An ongoing ledger that tracks every vendor and every payment over months or years is still the right place to keep that record. This is a one-time check you run against an export from it — did any of these payments go out twice — and it keeps no copy of your data once you close the tab.
