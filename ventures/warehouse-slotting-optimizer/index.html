Questions warehouse teams ask

How do I decide where to put each SKU in a small warehouse?

Put the SKUs that are picked most often in the bays nearest the dock or packing area, and the slow ones furthest away. This is called slotting. Enter your grid, each SKU's picks per month and how many bays it takes, and the tool shows where every SKU should live and how many feet of walking that removes. When every SKU is one bay and orders are one line, the ranking by picks is provably the best possible.

Why paste orders as well as picks per month?

Picks per month treats every pick as a separate trip. Real orders often have several lines, and SKUs that are ordered together should sit in the same aisle so one trip covers them. Paste a sample of real orders (one per line, SKUs separated by commas) and the tool measures the walk of each whole order and moves SKUs together when that shortens the routes.

What are quick wins?

A full re-slot can mean moving most of the stockroom. Quick wins start from where things are today and list the single swaps or moves that save the most, in order, with the labor to make them and the weeks to pay that back. Often a handful of moves capture most of the saving.

How accurate is the saving?

It is the walking only, on the distances and speed you enter, assuming the dock is at one corner and pickers return to it after each trip. It does not include picking, handling, replenishment, safety or ergonomics, and the time saved is only worth money if it is used for other work. Measure a real route with a tape or a wheel and enter that.

Is this business advice?

No. It is arithmetic on figures you type. Weight, reach height, hazardous or temperature-controlled goods, product families and putaway rules are yours to decide, which is why you can lock any SKU in place.

Not decided

Whether to add racking, move the dock, batch or zone picking, replenishment from reserve, product weight and reach height, hazardous or temperature rules, seasonal swings in demand, and what the saved time is worth to you. The walk is a single dock at one corner with return-to-dock routing, and a SKU is picked from its nearest bay.
