This checker vs. checking the FLSA salary threshold yourself

Checking a salary against the FLSA white-collar exemption threshold looks like comparing one number to another. It is, once you have the right number -- but which number applies depends on the employee's work state, and in one state, on the exemption category too. A manual check that reaches for a single figure and applies it everywhere gets exactly the case that differs wrong.

Common questions

If a salary clears the federal $684/week threshold, is that always enough?

No -- only in a state that has not set its own higher figure. Five states currently have: California, Washington, Maine, Alaska, and New York. An employee paid well above $684/week can still fail the applicable threshold if they work in one of those states and the state figure is higher, which is the single most common way a manual check that only knows the federal number gets the answer wrong.

Does New York's higher salary threshold apply to every exemption category?

No, and this is the detail most likely to be missed by hand. New York's higher regional figures ($1,275/week in NYC and Nassau/Suffolk/Westchester, $1,199.10/week elsewhere in the state) apply only to the executive and administrative exemptions. New York sets no separate threshold for the professional exemption, so the federal $684/week figure governs a professional-exemption classification even in New York -- while an executive or administrative employee at the identical salary and location is judged against the much higher state figure.

Why would the same weekly salary pass for one employee and fail for another at the identical company location?

Because the applicable threshold depends on the exemption category, not just the state. A $900/week employee in the NYC area classified under the professional exemption meets the threshold -- the federal $684/week figure governs, since New York sets no professional-specific figure. A $900/week employee at the same company, same location, classified under the administrative exemption instead does not meet the threshold: New York's $1,275/week figure applies to that category, and $900 is $375 short.

Does every state's higher threshold take effect on the same date?

No. California, Washington, Maine, and New York's 2026 figures are effective January 1, 2026, but Alaska's $1,120/week figure does not take effect until July 1, 2026 -- a manual check applied before that date should still use the federal $684/week figure for an Alaska employee, not the higher figure that has not taken effect yet.

If a salary meets the applicable threshold, does that make the employee exempt?

No. The salary threshold is a necessary condition, never a sufficient one. The employee must also be paid on a true salary basis (not reducible for partial-day absences or variations in the quantity of work performed) and must actually perform exempt duties under the executive, administrative, or professional duties test. This tool checks only the salary-level half of the test.
