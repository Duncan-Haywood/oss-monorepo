What's the new $6,000 senior deduction worth?

A new federal deduction applies to tax years 2025 through 2028: up to $6,000 for each taxpayer on the return who is age 65 or older — up to $12,000 on a joint return when both spouses qualify — but it shrinks once modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) clears $75,000 (single/HoH/qualifying surviving spouse) or $150,000 (joint), and it is not available at all to married filing separately. This applies the phase-out and the MFS rule and estimates the deduction.

Common questions

How much is the new senior tax deduction?

Up to $6,000 per qualifying taxpayer age 65 or older, for tax years 2025 through 2028. On a joint return where both spouses are 65 or older, that is up to $12,000 combined. It phases out at higher incomes -- see the phase-out question below.

Can I claim this if I file married filing separately?

No. Married filing separately is excluded entirely, at any income and regardless of age -- a married couple must file a joint return for either spouse to claim it. This is the SAME direction as this portfolio's own no-tax-on-tips-overtime-calculator, and the OPPOSITE of its auto-loan-interest-deduction-calculator, where MFS is explicitly eligible.

What is the income phase-out?

Each qualifying person's $6,000 is reduced by 6 cents for every dollar that modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) exceeds $75,000 (single, head of household, or qualifying surviving spouse) or $150,000 (married filing jointly). It reaches exactly $0 at $175,000 MAGI (single/HoH/QSS) or $250,000 MAGI (joint) -- the same joint zero-crossing point whether one or two spouses qualify, since each person's $6,000 phases out independently against the same household MAGI.

Do I have to itemize to claim it?

No. This deduction stacks on top of the standard deduction (and the existing extra standard deduction for age) whether you itemize or not -- it is not an itemized deduction.

Is this permanent?

No. Under current law it applies to tax years 2025, 2026, 2027, and 2028 only, and expires afterward unless a future law extends it.
