This calculator vs. doing it by hand

The new $6,000 senior deduction looks like simple arithmetic -- an amount, a phase-out, an eligibility check. The problem is that this portfolio now has three 2025 OBBBA deductions with different rules for the same two questions (does MFS qualify, does the phase-out round in steps), and a quick by-hand estimate tends to carry the wrong sibling's answer over.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake estimating this deduction by hand?

Assuming married filing separately gets a reduced amount, generalizing from this portfolio's own auto-loan-interest-deduction-calculator, where MFS is explicitly eligible with its own cap. This deduction excludes MFS entirely -- $0, at any income or age -- a married couple must file a joint return for either spouse to claim any of it.

What's the second most common mistake?

Confusing this with the existing extra standard deduction for age 65+ that has been on Form 1040 for years. This is a separate, new, additional deduction -- it stacks ON TOP of that existing age-based amount and the regular standard deduction, not instead of either one.

How does the MAGI phase-out usually get estimated wrong?

By assuming it rounds in $1,000 steps the way this portfolio's own auto-loan and tips/overtime deductions do. This one does not -- it is a continuous 6 cents per dollar of MAGI over the threshold, with no rounding step at all, so $100,001 and $100,999 of excess MAGI cost two different amounts, not the same amount.

On a joint return where both spouses qualify, does the combined $12,000 phase out as one pot?

No, and assuming so is an easy by-hand mistake. Each spouse's own $6,000 phases out independently against the same household MAGI excess. It happens to reach exactly $0 at the same MAGI either way ($250,000 joint) -- but the deduction at any MAGI between the threshold and that point is not simply double a one-spouse estimate.
