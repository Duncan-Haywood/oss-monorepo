# PhotoFlow Product Brief

## Product thesis

PhotoFlow turns a messy shoot dump into organized media, cinematic color-graded
intermediates, and ready-to-review Instagram reels, carousels, and photo posts.
The product should feel like a fast assistant editor: careful with files,
excellent at repetitive craft, transparent about decisions, and easy to override.

## Primary audience

1. **Solo photographers and videographers** who shoot often and lose margin to
   ingest, culling, grading, export, and social formatting.
2. **Creators with paid editors** who want more output per dollar, faster
   turnaround, and fewer handoff mistakes.
3. **Small studios and agencies** that need consistent social deliverables from
   weddings, events, restaurants, fashion, real estate, fitness, and creator
   shoots.

## Core customer promise

"Dump the card. Get a safe archive, graded intermediates, and a ranked set of
editable Instagram deliverables before you would normally finish organizing the
shoot."

## Jobs to be done

- Ingest from SD card, camera, phone, local folder, or SSD without losing files.
- Preserve raw/log originals while creating edit-ready proxies and intermediates.
- Detect shoot structure: scenes, takes, people, products, locations, moments,
  technical rejects, duplicates, and hero candidates.
- Apply consistent cinematic looks across mixed cameras and lighting conditions.
- Generate multiple deliverable shapes:
  - 9:16 reels with music-safe pacing and beat markers;
  - carousel narratives with cover, detail, proof, and closing frames;
  - polished single-photo exports;
  - editor handoff packages with selects, LUTs, notes, and timelines.
- Let the user review, revise, and export without needing to trust a black box.

## Differentiation

- **File safety first.** Many creator tools optimize for output; PhotoFlow should
  win trust by treating originals as irreplaceable.
- **Intermediate assets, not only final posts.** Professionals need editable
  graded photos, proxies, LUTs, sidecars, timeline XML/EDL, captions, and audit
  logs.
- **Parallel bulk quality.** The system should split ingest, proxy generation,
  scene detection, embeddings, grading, and render jobs across CPU/GPU/Neural
  Engine/cloud workers.
- **Aesthetic memory.** The app should learn per-client and per-creator taste:
  favorite looks, pacing, crop preferences, brand-safe content, and caption tone.
- **Editor-compatible output.** A strong product can coexist with human editors
  by reducing prep work and producing better review candidates.

## Business viability notes

### Packaging

- Personal/prosumer app: paid Mac app with iPhone companion.
- Pro subscription: larger batch limits, cloud acceleration, style memory,
  client workspaces, collaboration links, and render queues.
- Studio tier: shared presets, brand kits, editor handoffs, audit logs, and
  priority compute.

### Pricing pressure

The product competes against manual editing labor, not only photo apps. Pricing
can be justified when it reduces turnaround, increases output quantity, or helps
one shooter serve more clients without hiring another editor.

### Early wedge

Start with safe ingest and "first pass social package" for event/wedding/lifestyle
shoots. This is frequent, painful, visually repetitive enough for automation, and
valuable even when the final grade is revised by a human.

### Retention hooks

- Per-camera and per-client looks improve over time.
- Archive organization becomes the system of record.
- Batch history and client templates reduce setup for each shoot.
- Generated deliverables create review loops and measurable output velocity.

## Product quality bar

- Never risk originals for speed.
- Make every automated decision inspectable.
- Give users fast previews before expensive renders.
- Prefer fewer excellent deliverables over many generic ones.
- Export to standard formats so users are not locked in.
