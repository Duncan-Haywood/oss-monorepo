[The Colbert Oaks](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-colbert-oaks.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story. An AI wrote it. It is text only for
now, because the shelf's audio budget is full. It comes from the series
bible's "Next up" list. The prompt there asked for a parish oak wood,
planted in the 2080s for a roof due a century and a half later, that a
region short of timber after a storm asks for early.

## The setup

In 2084 a French village plants eight hectares of sessile oak from Tronçais
acorns. The trees are for its church roof, which a survey has given until
about 2240. In January 2216 a storm takes the roofs off farmhouses across
the Bourbonnais. The region now builds in oak, and it flies every wood with
lidar. Its survey system counts 840 trees, about 650 m³ of sawn timber,
which is about a hundred house roofs.

## Why "keep the roof trees, take the rest" fails

The carpenter's roof is 36 pairs of rafters: 129 pieces, each hewn from
one tree. With a quarter spare for defects found when the trees are opened,
that is 162 trees. The survey AI's first plan keeps the 162 best and fells
the other 678. That is 72% of the wood, about 70 roofs, and it looks like
no loss to the church.

It fails three ways, and each is a real thing about oak:

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Wind | Trees grown up in a closed wood are not built to stand alone. Leaving 20 a hectare after a storm invites the next one to flatten them. |
| Water shoots | Oak keeps dormant buds under the bark of its trunk. Sun on a suddenly bare stem wakes them, and each shoot leaves a knot in the beam. |
| The inside | Lidar measures the outside of a tree. In the story, a walk with a mallet rejects 41 of the AI's top 162 for frost cracks, shoots, twist, rot and hidden forks, which leaves 121 sound trees. The roof needs 162. |

The forester's plan takes 30% of the basal area instead: 279 trees, about
29 roofs. It leaves 561 standing and 200 future trees ringed in paint.

## The number that decides the last ten trees

A 30 × 30 cm tie beam, 10.6 m long and all heartwood, needs a tree about
62 cm across at breast height. That allows for bark, sapwood (oak sapwood
rots) and taper. Only 10 trees in the wood are that big in 2216. The region
needs exactly those trees, as 12 m ridge beams for houses that cannot be
roofed without one.

What tips the vote is growth. The 200 future trees are 50.7 to 61.7 cm now,
and the roof needs 12 tie-beam trees by 2240:

| Growth over the next 24 years | Future trees big enough for a tie beam |
|---|---|
| As before, 3.5 mm of diameter a year | 115 |
| Faster after the thinning (assumed 4.6 mm) | 200 |
| Half the old rate, like the worst dry decade | 32 |

So the ten big trees can go without starving the roof, unless the future is
worse than anything in the stumps. If it is, the carpenter has a fallback
that medieval carpenters used when no tree was long enough: the scarf joint
called the *trait de Jupiter*. Fell the whole wood now and replant, and the
roof waits until about 2373.

The village, the wood, the storm, the region's timber cell, its survey AI
and the people are invented. Tronçais, Colbert's forest reforms and the
French law that gives most pre-1905 churches to the communes are real. The
general facts are from memory and simplified, and none of it is forestry,
engineering or legal advice. The full model (a normal distribution of
diameters, a 12 m log, 55% sawn yield, 6.5 m³ a roof) is in the story's
closing note. The numbers are also in the bible's
[finished list](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/DONE.md).
