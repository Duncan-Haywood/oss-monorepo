[Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) is the portfolio's shelf of
hopeful, hard-ish science fiction. Each story ends with a note that sets out
the model behind its numbers, so a curious reader can check them. Two
earlier editor's passes
([10 November](/posts/2026-11-10-hearth-and-horizon-revision-pass/),
[earlier today](/posts/2026-11-13-hearth-and-horizon-second-revision-pass/))
found that the models were right and the text around them drifted. This
third pass took the next batch: the fifteen stories published on
11 November, 4,600 to 7,000 words each. Every number, date and weekday was
re-derived in a few lines of Python.

## The physics held again

Not one model needed changing. Among them: a Wolbachia mosquito release in
Indonesia, frost going down under a snow drift in North Dakota, the boiling
point of maple sap with the day's air pressure, the odds of a felt
aftershock at a geothermal plant, a human with wings on Titan, a cone of ice
that keeps its water under a straw coat, silt left by storm floods on a
North Frisian Hallig, a sundial drifting off true over seven centuries, and
Charon eclipsing the Sun as seen from Pluto.

## What slipped: 32 fixes in 14 stories

That is about 2.1 a story, more than the last pass's 1.7. Only *The Rising
Shore* was clean. Arithmetic in dialogue showed up again, as before: 3,262 kg of
straw shared by 38 households is 86 kg each, not 87. But this pass also found
kinds of slip the earlier ones had not met:

- **Family ages.** A 91-year-old man in 2049 had a father who planted a
  shelterbelt in 1937 and a four-year-old self who carried water to it. He
  would have been 115. The planter became his grandfather. A woman of 62 in
  2046 had a daughter in 1989, at about five. In a third story, a man born
  about 1970 named his parents among the dead in a corner of a churchyard
  whose last burial was in 1958.
- **The Church calendar.** Easter 2237 falls on 16 April, not 2 April, so
  9 March is in the first week of Lent, not the third. One parish's note
  named it after Saint Joseph the Worker (1 May), while the story kept its
  feast on 19 March.
- **The sky.** A "thin moon" over a February bonfire in 2058 was a waning
  crescent about a day and a half from new, below the horizon all evening.
  On Pluto, Charon was "nearly full" when it was only just past half.
  Beyond Uranus, a Sun about to slip behind the planet was drawn on the
  wrong side of the screen.
- **Results before the trial.** A polder story quoted a year of results
  from a drainage trial that the plot does not lay until later that spring.
- **Speeds that did not match the clock.** A lava lobe said to move one to
  three metres an hour crossed 800 metres in about three hours.

Every fix was the smallest change that made the story agree with itself.
The full list, with the arithmetic, is in the
[run's report](https://github.com/Duncan-Haywood/oss-monorepo/blob/main/docs/reports/hearth-and-horizon-revision-2026-11-13b.md).

## What changes for the writers

The series bible's checklist now asks for three more checks at writing
time. Compute Easter for any claim about Lent or a feast. Write down every
character's birth year and test it against each relative and date they
mention. Make sure no scene quotes a result from an event the plot puts
later. Each one takes a minute. Each would have caught at least one slip
here.
