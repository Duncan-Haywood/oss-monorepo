[Francis de Sales and the Letters under the Doors](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/francis-de-sales-and-the-letters-under-the-doors.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. It runs about 1,480 words, roughly ten minutes
read aloud, and is text only, because the shelves' audio folders are over the
creative track's 400 MB cap (577 MB this run). His feast is 24 January.

**The story.** A nobleman's eldest son in Savoy is sent to Paris and Padua to
become a lawyer, comes home to a seat on the high court and a hoped-for
marriage, and has to tell his father no. As a young priest he volunteers for
the Chablais, the country south of Lake Geneva, where the people have been
told not to listen to a Catholic priest, and do as they were told. He preaches
in an empty square at Thonon. Then he sits down and writes short, kind pages,
one point each, has them copied, and slips them under the doors: *swish*,
*swish*, *swish*, through the winter. Slowly people read them by the fire,
then come to ask questions. The story ends with him as Bishop of Geneva at
Annecy, writing a book that says you can be holy right where you are, and
with why he became the patron saint of writers.

**What the bible asked for.** The proposal for this story said to centre the
leaflets and his patience, keep the religious division to one line without
villains, and source or drop two traditions. How that went:

- **The leaflets are history.** The tracts were copied by hand, pushed under
  doors and posted on walls, and the manuscripts were published after his
  death as *Les Controverses*. Pius XI's 1923 encyclical naming him patron of
  writers and journalists rests on them, so the patronage could go in the
  story itself.
- **The deaf man and the honey left out.** The account of a deaf man at
  Annecy, usually named Martin, whom he taught by signs, and the saying about
  a spoonful of honey and a barrel of vinegar, are both widely repeated. This
  run found neither in a source from his own time or his writings, so neither
  is in the story; the note for grown-ups says why.
- **The winter told as told.** The plank bridge crossed on hands and knees
  and the night in a tree to escape wolves are in the lives as traditions.
  They are kept, because a child will remember them, but the story says
  "people who told his story later said... We do not know exactly how much
  of that happened."
- **No villains.** The people of the Chablais are called "good, obedient
  people" for keeping to what their own pastors told them. The Duke of
  Savoy's measures that pressed people to conform are left out of a bedtime
  story, and the note says so, so the story credits Francis only with what he
  did: preach, write, wait.

**Three lines changed on the re-read.** The draft said nobody else wanted the
Chablais mission (not on record; cut), that his father "wrote" telling him to
come home (the biographies report the urging, not the form; now "sent word"),
and gave a beatification year this run could not confirm (now only the 1665
canonization is dated).

**Next on the shelf.** The bible's Little Lamps list now proposes Angela
Merici (27 January), who taught girls in their own homes and founded the
Company of Saint Ursula at Brescia in 1535, with her vision at Brudazzo and
her blindness on the road to the Holy Land flagged as later traditions to
