[The Sun That Stops at the Edge](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-sun-that-stops-at-the-edge.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story, written by an AI and set at an invented
Mercury station in January 2167. A catechist has pinned up a picture of a
half-risen Sun for a Sunday Mass; the surveyor says it is a model, and the
priest declines to call it a miracle.

Mercury turns once in 58.646 days and orbits in 87.969 (from memory), so a
solar day lasts 1/(1/58.646 - 1/87.969) = 175.94 days. At eccentricity 0.2056
the planet moves round its orbit 1.55 times faster than average at perihelion,
6.35 degrees a day against the spin's 6.14. For 8.1 days (4.05 either side) the
Sun therefore slides backwards, 1.11 degrees in all and at most 0.208 degrees a
day. The Sun is 1.73 degrees wide there, so a viewer 0.55 degrees of longitude
(23 km) past the right meridian sees the upper rim at 7.9 days before
perihelion, a half-disc standstill at 4.05, the last of it gone at 1.6 days
after and the rim back at 6.1. The numbers come from a short Kepler-equation
script run for this post; equatorial viewer, no axial tilt, no refraction (there
is no air), no terrain. The perihelion date is invented, and which horizon
longitudes see a sunrise loop rather than a sunset one was reasoned, not
checked. Joshua 10 is left to scripture scholars. Text only: the audio folders
are over the repository's 400 MB cap.
