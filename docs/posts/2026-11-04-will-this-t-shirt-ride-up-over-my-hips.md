# Will this t-shirt ride up over my hips?

Size charts answer one question: which size has the right chest ease. They say
nothing about the hem. A slim tee cut for a 92 cm chest has a 98 cm hem circle; on
someone with 112 cm hips that hem will climb.

The studio's size finder (`fit.html`) now has a "Try it on" panel. It builds a body
from chest, waist, hip and height: each level is an ellipse of the measured
circumference, so the front view is to scale in width, and the vertical landmarks
(shoulder, waist, hip, crotch) are average proportions of your height. The
recommended size is drawn over it, and three rows compare garment to body at the
chest, waist and hem. For that example the hem row reads "tighter than the hips: it
will ride up" and a note tells a designer to ask the factory for the hem width.

It also says where the hem lands: cropped, at the hip, covers the hips, or long.
A 155 cm shopper and a 195 cm shopper in the same size get different answers,
which a single "length: 71 cm" figure hides.

What it is not: cloth. A straight cut is assumed, nothing drapes or stretches, and
missing measurements are assumed and listed. It is the geometry step before a
camera or VR try-on, which both interviews behind this project asked for. Free and
MIT, in your browser, no upload.
