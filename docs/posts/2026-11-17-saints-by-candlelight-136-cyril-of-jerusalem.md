[Cyril of Jerusalem and the Lessons Beside Golgotha](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/136-cyril-of-jerusalem-and-the-lessons-beside-golgotha.md)
is episode 136 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script runs
about 7,100 words, roughly 45 minutes at the narrator's pace. Like every
episode since 30, it is text only for now.

**Why this one.** The last three episodes followed the pilgrim Egeria
through Jerusalem, and each time the bishop she describes was "very
probably Cyril", a historians' identification she never makes herself.
The series list's top entry was Cyril in his own right. He is an easy
saint to tell, because we can hear him: a listener took down his Lent
lessons for the candidates for baptism, around 348 or 350, and they
survive.

**The best line in them is a joke.** In the introductory lecture Cyril
tells the room that some of them have come for the wrong reasons, a man
"wishing to pay court to a woman", a servant pleasing his master, and
then: "I accept this bait for the hook, and welcome you." The episode
builds its invented frame on that sentence. A young lamp-maker,
Kallistos, enrols only because the weaver's daughter has, and finds the
priest saw him coming.

**What is Cyril's, checked against the text.** Every line the episode
gives him was checked against Gifford's translation (Nicene and
Post-Nicene Fathers, second series, volume 7) as served by New Advent:
Simon the magician "baptized, but not enlightened"; "let us see what the
faithful are doing"; the Church's nets; men sitting with "a useful book"
while the young women read so quietly "that their lips speak, but others'
ears catch not the sound"; "tell nothing to him that is without";
"here is Golgotha to confute me"; the stone "which lies to this day by the
tomb"; and the sign of the Cross "before our sleep, when we lie down and
when we rise up", which gives the episode its ending. The "throne for a
King" line comes from the Mystagogical Lectures, whose author (Cyril or
his successor John) is disputed, and the episode says so aloud.

**What is the historians'.** Sozomen (book 4, chapters 5 and 25) gives the
cross of light from Calvary to the Mount of Olives, the quarrel with
Acacius over Jerusalem's rank, and the famine in which Cyril sold the
church's ornaments to feed the poor. Theodoret (book 2) gives Acacius's
accusation about Constantine's gold-threaded baptismal robe ending up on
a stage dancer, told in the episode as an enemy's accusation, and the
exile at Tarsus, where Silvanus would not silence Cyril, partly because
his people "greatly enjoyed the stranger's teaching". The 382 synodal
letter calling Jerusalem "the mother of all the churches" and Cyril a
bishop who "fought a good fight against the Arians" is in Theodoret
book 5, chapter 9.

**One thing handled with care.** Socrates tells how Julian's attempt to
rebuild the Temple stopped after an earthquake and fires, and that Cyril
had predicted it. The account carries an anti-Jewish polemic. The
episode gives the event in three sentences, notes that the pagan
Ammianus also records the fires, and says plainly that the old
historians' unkindness towards the Jewish people of Jerusalem is not
repeated. The cross of light is told as Cyril and Sozomen report it, with
a modern ice-halo suggestion labelled as a suggestion.

**Sequels left on the list:** Silvanus of Tarsus and the council of
Seleucia, and Gregory of Nyssa's visit to Jerusalem.
