A mill quotes knit jersey at 8 per kilo. The factory quotes the same cloth at
a price per metre. The sample card says "180 gsm". A new brand has to put
these on one scale before it can compare a quote or cost a garment.

The sums are exact. At 180 gsm and a 150 cm cut width one metre weighs
0.27 kg, so a kilo is 3.70 m and 8 per kilo is 2.16 per metre. A run of 300
tees at 1.3 m each is 390 m, which is 105.3 kg. If the mill's minimum is
120 kg, you buy 14.7 kg more than you need: 960 for the cloth, 3.20 per tee
rather than 2.81.

[`gsm.html`](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/gsm.html) in the
Clothing Design Studio does this in the browser, also from ounces per square
yard, and flags a tube of knit quoted at its flat width. It is arithmetic on
numbers you type, checked by hand in five tests; no real mill's quote has gone
through it yet, and the width and weight a mill states can differ before and
after finishing, so ask which one it means.
