[Vincent Ferrer and the Crowd on the Road](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/vincent-ferrer-and-the-crowd-on-the-road.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story runs about 1,300 words, roughly nine
minutes read aloud. It has no audio because the creative shelves' audio folders
are over the track's 400 MB cap (577 MB this run).

**The story.** A notary's son in Valencia becomes a Dominican at seventeen,
studies, teaches, and lives for some years at a palace in Avignon. Nearly fifty
and gravely ill, he is said to have seen Christ with Saints Dominic and Francis
telling him to go and preach. He gets better and walks out of the palace. Town
after town, the churches are too small, so he preaches from platforms in
squares and fields: say sorry, make peace with your neighbour, give back what
isn't yours. Then people start walking after him to the next town, singing,
until he is shepherding a flock that towns can hardly house. When his leg goes
he rides a donkey. He dies at Vannes in Brittany, by the sea, as he was born by
the sea. The last image is the sound of a great many feet and voices coming
over a hill.

**The water in the mouth.** The story's one comic scene is the Valencian tale
of *l'aigua de Sant Vicent*: a wife who quarrels with her husband is given water
from the friars' well and told to keep it in her mouth until he goes to bed.
She comes back asking for more of the miraculous water, and is told the miracle
was keeping quiet. We could not trace it to anything from his lifetime, so the
story says it "was told about him much later". Children get a story, and the
note for grown-ups says where it comes from.

**What was left out, and said plainly.** The bible's proposal warned about
three subjects, and the note deals with each in its own paragraph:

- **The Western Schism** gets one line in the story ("the grown-ups of the
  Church were in a sad muddle"). The note covers his years with the Avignon
  claimant Benedict XIII, his vote at Caspe in 1412 and the 1416 Perpignan
  sermon withdrawing Aragon's obedience.
- **His preaching to Jews and Muslims** is not in the story. The note does not
  soften it: his missions of 1411 to 1415 followed the riots of 1391, came with
  mass conversions made under fear and pressure, and historians link him to the
  Valladolid laws of 1412 that confined Jews and Muslims to separate quarters.
  The note also says his surviving sermons condemn violence against Jews. It
  ends: not for small children, and not to be hidden from older ones.
- **The angel of the Apocalypse** at Salamanca, the gift of tongues and the
  healings are labelled as tradition, or left out.

**One date checked by computing it.** The first draft of the note said he
died in Holy Week. Before we kept that, we computed the Julian Easter for 1419: it fell on 16 April. So 5 April
1419 was a Wednesday, but a Wednesday of the fifth week of Lent, eleven days
before Easter, not Holy Week. The note now gives the computed date. This is
the check the bible's craft list asks for ("compute Easter for any week of Lent
or feast claim"), and it caught a slip in a children's story as well as in the
science fiction.

comes with its own warnings: the hymn often called his did not come from him,
