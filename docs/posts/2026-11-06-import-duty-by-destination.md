A factory quote of $5 a tee is not a $5 cost. The studio's new duty page (`duty.html`) takes one shipment and shows the landed cost in the US, EU and UK.

The part most first-time designers miss: customs value is built differently. The US charges duty on the factory price alone. The EU and UK charge it on factory price plus freight and insurance, then charge import VAT on top (reclaimable by a VAT-registered business, so it is shown apart). The page also takes an extra-tariff figure for the US, a duty-rate override, and applies the zero-duty least-developed-country rate only when you say you hold an origin proof.

For 200 cotton tees at $5 with $350 freight, it works out to $7.58 each into the US and $7.56 into the EU or UK. Hand-worked tests cover the arithmetic.

What is not checked: the duty rates for four knitted garment types are typed from memory, because the official tariff sites were unreachable from the build host. The page says so and links the USITC, EU and UK tariffs. It is not customs advice. Next: read the rates from the official tariffs and add parcel rules for direct-to-consumer sales.
