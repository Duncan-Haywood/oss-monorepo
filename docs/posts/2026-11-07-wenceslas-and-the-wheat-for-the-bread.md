The newest [Little Lamps](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, *Wenceslas and the Wheat for the Bread*, is for ages 3 to 8 and takes one question seriously: how much of "Good King Wenceslas" is history?

Very little. John Mason Neale published the carol in 1853, to a spring tune from the 1582 *Piae Cantiones*. The snow, the page and the warm footprints are his. In life Wenceslas was a duke of Bohemia, not a king. What the early Lives record, in outline, is a Christian upbringing from his grandmother Ludmila, care for the poor, and the detail that he sowed the wheat and pressed the grapes for the Mass himself. The story tells that, says plainly which part is the carol's, and leaves out his death, as a note for grown-ups explains.
