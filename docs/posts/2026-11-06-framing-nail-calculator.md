Nails are cheap, but a framing package still needs a number: how many boxes go on the truck. Counting by hand means walking every stud end, header, jack and plate. The new page in wall-framing-takeoff does it from the same wall layout as the whole-house takeoff: end nails at every stud, king and cripple, jack and header nails, sill nails, backing, and plate nails along each wall.

Worked example from the page: a 20 ft front wall with a window and door, a 12 ft side wall and a 20 ft 2x6 back wall need 387 nails, 426 with 10% waste, 1 box of 5000 (48.00 at 48 a box). Change the schedule to three nails per stud end and it becomes 497 and 547. Both are pinned to the engine by a test and the page was loaded in headless Chromium.

Every count is a typed figure with a common-practice default, not a code table. Nail size and type, sheathing nails, hold-downs and anchors are not counted on this page. Invented walls only; no real house or engineer's schedule has checked it. Free, in your browser, MIT licensed: `ventures/wall-framing-takeoff/nail-calculator.html`.
