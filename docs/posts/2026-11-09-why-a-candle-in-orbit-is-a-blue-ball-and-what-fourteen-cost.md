[The Flame That Is a Ball](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-flame-that-is-a-ball.html) is a short story about an invented station, Wren, whose crew of fourteen wants candles for the Easter Vigil.

The numbers: without buoyancy a flame is a small dim sphere that smothers in its own exhaust, so it needs a slow draught (the physics is from memory of published experiments; the test times are invented). Paraffin (about C25H52) takes 3.45 g of oxygen per gram and yields 3.1 g of CO2 and 1.3 g of water. At assumed burn rates of 6 g/h per taper and 10 g/h for the Paschal candle, 45 minutes uses 70.5 g of wax: 243 g O2, 220 g CO2, 94 g water and about 1.2 kW of heat. Fourteen people breathe out roughly 437 g of CO2 and give off about 1.4 kW in the same time, so all the candles would add half again to the CO2 and nearly double the heat. The Paschal candle alone adds 23 g of CO2 and 128 W.
