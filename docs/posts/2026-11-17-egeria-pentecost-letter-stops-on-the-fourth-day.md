[Egeria and the Letter That Stops on the Fourth Day](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/137-egeria-and-the-letter-that-stops-on-the-fourth-day.md)
is episode 137 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it, and it is text only for now: the
creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over the track's 400 MB cap.

It closes the Egeria series. Chapters 39 to 49 of her travel letter cover
the Easter octave, the fifty days with no fasting, Pentecost, how the newly
baptised were taught, and the autumn dedication feast. The only manuscript
breaks off in that last part, at *Quarta autem die*, "On the fourth day".

## What the Latin says that a summary loses

The episode was checked against the Latin text (The Latin Library edition).
Three details stood out on that reading, and the episode is built around
them:

- **Pentecost ran from cockcrow to midnight.** Egeria calls it the day of
  *maximus labor*, the greatest labour: a vigil at the tomb, the morning
  service hurried so the city could be on Sion by the third hour (when
  Peter said "it is only the third hour of the day" in Acts 2), the whole
  city up the Mount of Olives after lunch, "so that no Christian remains in
  the city", and back through four more stations by candlelight.
- **They walked down slowly so that nobody's feet would get tired.** *Lente
  et lente itur totum pro populo, ne fatigentur pedibus*. She gives that as
  the reason they reached the great church only at the second hour of the
  night. A bishop walking at the pace of his slowest pilgrim is the centre
  of the episode.
- **Latin speakers got a translation "so that they shouldn't be sad".**
  The bishop knew Syriac but always preached in Greek. A priest translated
  into Syriac, and "Greek-Latin" brothers and sisters explained it in Latin,
  *ne contristentur*. The episode sets this beside the Pentecost reading,
  where every nation hears God's works in its own tongue.

## What the episode does not claim

Egeria keeps the fortieth day after Easter at Bethlehem and never says why.
Scholars still argue about it, and the episode says so without picking an
answer. The "about two hundred" candles at the city gate depend on an
uncertain reading, which is said aloud. Nothing is invented for the lost
fourth day. The widow Theodora, her son Zenon and slow Brother Abraham come
over from episode 135 and are flagged as invented again. So are a donkey and
a quiet monk from the Thebaid. Egeria has never been canonised, so no feast
is claimed for her.
