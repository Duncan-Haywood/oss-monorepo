The catalogue has pages that file tools by who they serve rather than by the job they do:
[free tools for roofers](https://marisstella.app/c/for-roofers/), for Etsy sellers, for landlords,
27 in all. Every one of them was for a trade or a kind of business.

Meanwhile fourteen calculators on the site are for one kind of person who is not running a
business: someone about to retire or already retired. The RMD calculator, the QCD calculator, the
Medicare IRMAA cliff, four Social Security tools (claiming age, how much of the benefit is taxed,
the earnings test, survivor and spousal benefits), Roth conversion bracket filling, the pension
lump sum against the annuity, the withdrawal sequencer, the FERS planner, the ACA subsidy cliff
for the years before Medicare, and two UK ones (State Pension qualifying years and pension
withdrawal tax). They were filed under "US personal tax, pay & retirement", next to paycheck
and equity-comp tools a retiree does not need.

So there is now [a page for retirees and near-retirees](https://marisstella.app/c/for-retirees/).
It lists those fourteen, says which two follow UK rules, and suggests where to start: the claiming
age calculator sets the income floor, and the withdrawal sequencer shows how the order you draw
from accounts moves tax, IRMAA and later RMDs.

Membership is a rule on the tool's slug, not a hand-kept list, so the next RMD or Social Security
tool joins the page on its own. The rule deliberately leaves out tools for the saving years
(SEP-IRA limits, UK pension annual allowance), for employers (the retirement plan startup credit)
and for heirs (the inherited IRA ten-year planner), and a test pins those choices.

What this does not do: it does not tell anyone what to do with their savings. Each tool is an
estimate from the numbers you type, under the rules it names, and not financial or tax advice.
