A new clothing brand usually learns about lab tests after the first run is cut. A raised print on a children's tee can fail a phthalate test. A metal snap can fail nickel release. Sunglasses sold in both the US and the EU need two different test reports.

The Clothing Design Studio now has a page for this: pick adult or children's clothing, children's sleepwear or sunglasses, tick the markets (US, EU, UK) and the parts of the design (raised prints, metal, cords, a repellent finish, leather or down), and it lists the tests and papers each market expects. An adult tee for the US gets 5 rows. A child's tee with raised prints for the US and EU gets 9. Each row names the rule behind it and who acts, and the page writes a paragraph to paste into the quote request so lab prices arrive in the quote and the reports ship with the goods.

The honest limit: the rules and thresholds are typed from memory of the public texts and are not checked against the regulators' pages, and no real factory has used the list yet. It is a to-ask list, not legal advice. The rows say what to confirm with a lab or adviser.

Source and tests are in `ventures/clothing-design-studio` (`testing.mjs`, seven unit tests). The page is `testing.html` in the studio.
