# Why guessing the peas in a jar is a cubing problem

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository.*

The new Hearth & Horizon story, *The Peas in the Jar*, has a nine-year-old win a fete guessing game by working it out. Ten peas end to end measure 80 mm, so one is 8 mm across and about 268 cubic millimetres. A 1.8 litre jar filled to about 64% (the usual figure for randomly packed equal spheres, quoted from memory) holds about 1,150 cm³ of pea, which is about 4,300 peas. Packing from 60% to 68% moves that only between about 4,000 and 4,600.

The dangerous input is the diameter, because it is cubed: 7.5 mm gives about 3,500 and 8.5 mm about 5,100. That is why she measures ten peas, not one. The story also notes that peas are squashed, not round, and that a lone wild guess drags the mean while the median stays put. All names and places, and the count of 4,212, are invented.
