Our new [Hearth & Horizon](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, *The Ice
at the Inflow*, is about a Manitoba parish that blesses the waters on river ice
every Theophany, and the parish assistant that has to admit its first number
was the wrong kind of answer.

The rule of thumb is thickness (cm) = constant x sqrt(freezing degree-days).
With 640 degree-days and an assumed constant of 1.7 for snow-covered ice, that
is 25.3 x 1.7, about 43 cm. The constant ranges roughly 1.3 to 2.7, so the
honest range is 33 to 68 cm. Then the auger: 41 cm at the church, 22 cm at the
inflow edge, 9 cm at arm's length over the creek mouth. A formula gives one
number for a whole river; springs and inflows are where the ice is thin, so
measure where people will stand, starting at the place that has always gone
wrong.

The story also walks through the "1-10-1" teaching device for cold water (the
gasp in the first minute, arm strength going over about ten minutes,
hypothermia over about an hour) and why a ten-second plunge is dangerous for
the gasp rather than the cold. All readings and the parish are invented; the
guide thicknesses vary by authority. This is not safety advice: follow your
local ice and water-safety guidance, and never go alone.
