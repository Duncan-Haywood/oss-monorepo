The clothing design studio now lays the drafted pattern pieces, curves and all, on a fabric width. The old estimate packed rectangles, so a sleeve cap and an armhole never shared space. The new one rasterises each cut outline on a 0.5 cm grid, keeps the grain, allows a 180 degree turn, and tries 15 orders for the shortest marker.

On 150 cm cloth: one tee (M) needs 85.1 cm, 77.5% of it pattern. A four-shirt ratio (S, 2 M, L) comes to 0.87 m per tee including 6 cm end loss. A crewneck run of ten comes to 1.09 m each. A long sleeve is worse, 52.7%, because 83 cm sleeves cannot tilt.

An independent exact-arithmetic check (no crossing edges, no piece inside another, inside the width) passes on every layout in the tests. What is unproven: a factory's CAD marker for the same tee. They tilt pieces and tune gaps, so treat this as a cautious estimate. Try it on the [pattern page](https://marisstella.app/) under "Nest on the cloth".
