# A bigger rain tank can't fix a small roof

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository.*

The new Hearth & Horizon story, *The Tank That Covers a Quarter*, starts with an allotment group holding a grant for a 10,000 litre tank and the hope that it will see them through July.

The arithmetic is short enough to check on paper. A 140 m² roof at 80 per cent runoff gives 112 litres per millimetre of rain. The driest July in the (invented) rain book, 11 mm, gives about 1,230 litres, or about 700 after a first-flush diverter. Watering 200 m² at 4 mm a day takes about 24,800 litres. A 10,000 litre tank plus that roof covers 45 per cent, and doubling the tank would not change the roof. Watering only the 80 m² that need it, at 2 mm a day, takes 4,960 litres, which a 5,000 litre tank just covers with about 700 litres spare.
