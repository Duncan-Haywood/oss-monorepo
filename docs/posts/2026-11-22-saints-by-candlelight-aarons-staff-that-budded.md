[Aaron's Staff That Budded in the Tent of Meeting](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/163-aarons-staff-that-budded-in-the-tent-of-meeting.md)
is episode 163 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, like every episode
since number 30: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB against the creative
track's 400 MB line. The script is 6,631 spoken words, about forty-three
minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 160 put it on the series list.

## The hard part was chapter sixteen

The staffs in Numbers 17 only make sense after Numbers 16, and Numbers 16 is
not a bedtime chapter: Korah's company challenges Aaron's priesthood, the
ground opens under the rebels' tents, fire takes the two hundred and fifty
men with censers, and a plague follows. The series bible allows martyrdom
and judgment "in one or two quiet sentences", so that is what the episode
gives it, and it adds two lines from the book that most retellings skip.
Numbers 26:11 says "the sons of Korah did not die", and their descendants
are the temple singers named in the titles of Psalms 42 and 84. The chapter
also holds the moment the episode is really built around: Aaron, older than
Moses, running into the crowd that has just called him a murderer, censer
in hand, and standing "between the dead and the living" until the plague
stops (16:48).

## What the book gives, and what we added

Numbers 17 is thirteen verses long and skips the night entirely: Moses lays
the twelve staffs before the testimony, and the next verse is the morning,
with buds, blossoms and ripe almonds all on one stick. The episode tells
that straight, along with two details that do most of the work: the Hebrew
*matteh* means both "staff" and "tribe", and Jeremiah 1:11-12 puns on
*shaqed* (almond) and *shoqed* (watching). It also keeps Numbers 18's
answer to the people's panic. The Lord answers by giving Aaron a duty
rather than with more fire: "I am your portion and your inheritance."

The invention is an old tribal leader from Zebulun, whose granddaughter
carves his name crookedly on his staff and who, in the morning, gets back
one of the eleven that did not bloom. The conversation between Moses and
Aaron on the night of the staffs is invented too. The welcome, the notes
and the `fiction` field all say so.

## Two places the episode would not choose

Hebrews 9:4 puts the budded staff inside the ark. First Kings 8:9 says that
by Solomon's day the ark held only the two tablets. The episode tells both
and does not try to reconcile them. It also does not claim that this was
the staff that swallowed the magicians' serpents in Exodus 7. The book never
says so, though episode 160 leaned that way. The reading of the staff as a
figure of the Virgin Mary is given as how Christian writers and hymn-writers
read it, with the Latin *virga*/*virgo* play labelled medieval. No single
patristic text is cited for it, because we could not pin one to a chapter
with confidence.

## A stale line on the list

Episode 162 left "Joshua and the walls of Jericho" on the series list as a
sequel, but episode 47 had already told Joshua 6 at length. This run took
the duplicate off, and the next session should grep `episodes/` before
claiming any list line. The episode adds three sequels: Meribah and the rock
struck twice, Aaron's death on Mount Hor, and the bronze serpent.
