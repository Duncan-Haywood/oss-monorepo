[Solomon and the House That Heaven Cannot Contain](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/138-solomon-and-the-house-that-heaven-cannot-contain.md)
is episode 138 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only for now, because the
creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over the track's 400 MB cap.

Episode 137 ended with the pilgrim Egeria in Jerusalem in the 380s. She
compared the dedication feast of the Church of the Resurrection with
Solomon's dedication "in the books of Paralipomenon", the old name for
Chronicles. This episode goes back to that day. It draws on First Kings 5
to 9 and Second Chronicles 2 to 7, plus Nathan's promise and David's
preparations.

## Three details the episode is built around

- **No hammer heard on the hill.** First Kings 6:7: the stone was made
  ready at the quarry, "and there was neither hammer nor axe nor any tool of
  iron heard in the house while it was being built". Scripture gives no
  reason, and the episode doesn't supply one. It notes the law against iron
  tools on the altar as one possibility, and then spends its invented scenes
  at the quarry, where all the noise went.
- **One sound.** Second Chronicles 5:13 asks the 120 priests with
  trumpets and the Levite singers to "make one sound" before the cloud
  fills the house. Anyone who has sung in a choir knows how hard that is.
- **"There is no one who does not sin."** It is said in the middle of the
  dedication prayer (First Kings 8:46), on the best day of Solomon's life.
  The same prayer also asks God to hear the foreigner who comes from far
  away. The episode connects that line to Egeria and to every pilgrim
  since.

## What the episode does not claim

Where Kings and Chronicles disagree (two thousand or three thousand baths in
the bronze sea, the bronze-worker's mother's tribe), the episode either
gives both figures or names neither. That the Temple hill is Abraham's
Moriah is presented as Chronicles' reading, taken up by later tradition. The
hidden ark is told as Second Maccabees 2 tells it, a story "found in the
records", not as a known location. The episode tells Solomon's later turning
away in First Kings 11 plainly, and leaves his end to God. Neither tradition
keeps a feast for Solomon alone, so the episode claims none. It names the
Orthodox Sundays of the Forefathers instead. The quarryman Yoash, his
daughter Tirzah and Abdi, a raftman from Tyre, are invented, and the
episode says so in the welcome and in the notes.

The Scripture is in the episode's own plain wording, not quoted from a
modern translation. The series list now offers two sequels: Solomon's dream
at Gibeon with the two mothers, and the Queen of Sheba.
