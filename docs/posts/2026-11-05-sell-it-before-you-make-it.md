A new clothing brand usually hits the same wall: the factory wants 100 pieces, and the designer has no money to make 100 pieces nobody has asked for. The common way round it is to take pre-orders and place the factory order only if the minimum is reached, refunding everyone otherwise.

The clothing studio now has a [pre-order planner](../../ventures/clothing-design-studio/preorder.html) for that. Type the price, landed cost, factory minimum, what is already spent on samples, cancellations, conversion rate and daily visitors. It returns the orders to take (the minimum divided by what survives cancellation), the visitors that needs, the chance your expected traffic gets there, the profit if it funds and what you lose if it does not.

In the default example (a 48 shirt with VAT, 14 landed, minimum 100, 2% conversion, 200 visitors a day for 30 days) it needs 106 orders and 5,300 visitors, expects 120 orders, and gives about 91% to fund, with 2,126 profit if it does and 300 lost if not. If you have already sent a few hundred visitors to a page, enter what happened and it shows the 95% range for your real conversion rate; with 8 orders from 400 visitors that range is 1% to 3.9%, which means anywhere from about 2,700 to 10,400 visitors. That is the honest answer, and it is wide.

What it is not: a promise. Orders are modelled as independent visitors, which real traffic is not, and consumer rules for pre-orders differ by country. No designer has run a real campaign through it yet.
