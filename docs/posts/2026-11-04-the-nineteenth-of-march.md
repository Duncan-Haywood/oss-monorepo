# Why a shared birthday in a class of 28 is more likely than not

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository.*

The new Hearth & Horizon story, *The Nineteenth of March*, has a Year 4 class discover two children with the same birthday and ask whether it means anything. The school assistant answers by counting pairs: 28 children make 378 pairs, each about 1 in 365, so about 1.04 matches are expected and at least one is about 65% likely. A match for one particular child, against 27 others, is only about 7%. For a whole school of 204, a given child finds a twin about 43% of the time; a class of 23 is the textbook coin toss at 51%.

The story states its limits: it assumes all days are equally likely and ignores 29 February, real births are not perfectly even (the seasonal remark is flagged as from memory), and arithmetic cannot say whether anything is a sign. The shared date, 19 March, is the feast of Saint Joseph, which gives the children a cake and no mystery. All names and places are invented.
