[Shiphrah and Puah, the Midwives Who Feared God](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/171-shiphrah-and-puah-the-midwives-who-feared-god.md)
is episode 171 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30
it is text only: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the creative
track's limit is 400 MB. The script is about 6,100 spoken words, roughly
forty minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 170 put it on the list, in a
single sentence: "that is a story for another night."

## Seven verses and a long night

The midwives get Exodus 1:15-21. That is seven verses: an order, a refusal,
a question, an answer, and a reward. The episode keeps all of that exactly
as the book gives it. The extra length comes from three places, and each one
is labelled:

- **What a midwife was.** Genesis already has midwives at Rachel's last
  birth and at Tamar's twins, and Ezekiel 16:4 describes salting and
  swaddling a newborn. The "two stones" of Exodus 1:16 match the Egyptian
  custom of giving birth crouched on a pair of bricks; a painted birth
  brick has been found at Abydos, and the Westcar Papyrus tells an Egyptian
  tale of midwives at a birth.
- **An invented apprentice.** Zibiah, aged fourteen, holds the cloth at
  one birth in Goshen and asks the obvious question on the walk home
  ("aren't you frightened?"). She and the family she helps are invented,
  and the welcome, the notes and the `fiction` field all say so.
- **The open questions, left open.** The book doesn't say whether the
  midwives were Hebrews or Egyptians (Josephus thought Egyptians, the
  Talmud's rabbis thought Hebrews), how two women could serve a whole
  people, or which king gave the order. The script names each question and
  doesn't settle any of them.

## The hard part: their answer to the king

The midwives tell Pharaoh the Hebrew women "are lively" and give birth
before a midwife arrives. A careful listener will ask whether that was a
lie, and whether God rewarded a lie. The series bible doesn't allow the
episode to contradict moral teaching, and the Church teaches that lying is
wrong even for a good end (Catechism 2482-2486). So the episode answers the
way the tradition does. It doesn't pretend the question isn't there.

Saint Augustine took up these midwives in *Against Lying* (chapter 15), and
Saint Thomas Aquinas asks the question directly in the *Summa* (II-II,
q. 110, a. 3, ad 2). Both say the midwives were rewarded for their fear of
God and their mercy, not for any untruth. The text of Exodus backs that
reading: "because the midwives feared God, he made them houses." The
episode says the answer may well have been partly true and wasn't the whole
truth. It then turns to the lesson that is beyond dispute, that an unjust
order must not be obeyed (Catechism 2242, and Peter's "we must obey God
rather than men" in Acts 5:29).

## Names that outlived a king's

The episode makes one observation of its own. Egyptian kings had five
names each and carved them on everything, but Exodus never names this king.
It does name the two midwives. The rabbis' tradition that Shiphrah was
Jochebed and Puah was Miriam (Sotah 11b), and Rashi's readings of the names
and of "houses" (priests, Levites and kings), are told as traditions. The
episode ends with Shiphrah, much older, in a house full of grandchildren,
which is one plain reading of "he made them houses."
