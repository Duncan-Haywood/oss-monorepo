[Shiphrah and Puah, the Midwives Who Feared God](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/171-shiphrah-and-puah-the-midwives-who-feared-god.md)
is episode 171 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30
it is text only: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB, and the creative
track's limit is 400 MB. The script runs 5,786 spoken words, about
thirty-seven minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 170 put it on the
series list.

## Seven verses, and the names are kept

The midwives are in seven verses, Exodus 1:15-21, and the book tells us
almost nothing about them. The episode builds on the one thing it does
stress. The king of Egypt is never named, but these two women are, and the
book names them before it names Moses. The episode saves that point for
the wind-down: the man who wanted to be remembered was forgotten, and the
two women who only did their work well were not.

The book gives the reason for what they did twice, in verses 17 and 21:
they "feared God". The script spends a scene on what that phrase means in
the Bible. It is not fright. It is knowing who God is and caring more
about what he thinks than about what the king thinks. Most of the story
is about a quiet kind of courage: nobody makes a speech, and two women
just keep catching babies, girls and boys alike.

## The hard part: was their answer true?

In verse 19 the midwives tell Pharaoh that Hebrew women are "lively" and
give birth before the midwife gets there. The book does not say whether
that was true, and it does not praise the answer. It praises their fear
of God. Christian teachers have taken the question seriously, so the
episode names two of them and quotes neither. Augustine (*Against Lying*)
held that if the answer was a lie, God rewarded the midwives' mercy, not
the lie. Gregory the Great (*Moralia* 18) was sterner: he thought an
untruth would have made their reward smaller, an earthly one, the
"houses" of verse 21. The script gives both readings and does not pick
one, as the series rule on disputed ground asks.

Two other open questions are marked as open. "The Hebrew midwives" can be
read as midwives *to* the Hebrews, and Josephus took them to be
Egyptians. And two midwives seems too few for a whole people.

## Small things from the text

- The "birthstool" of verse 16 is *obnayim*, "the two stones". The only
  other place the word appears is Jeremiah 18:3, where it means the
  potter's wheel. The episode puts the two pictures side by side.
- The other midwives in the Bible (Rachel's in Genesis 35, Tamar's in
  Genesis 38, with the scarlet thread) and the salt and swaddling of
  Ezekiel 16:4 fill in what the job was.
- Exodus 7:7 makes Aaron three years older than Moses. So Aaron was born
  in about these years, and the episode notes that someone caught him. It
  does not say who.
- The rabbis' tradition that Shiphrah and Puah were Jochebed and Miriam,
  and that the "houses" were the priesthood and the royal line (Sotah
  11b), is told as a tradition. The script then lets the two women be
  themselves, since the book gives Jochebed and Miriam names and places
  of their own.

## Fact-pass corrections

- "Six verses" became "seven": verses 15 to 21 inclusive.
- Augustine's *Against Lying* is from about 420, not "the fourth century".
- The draft said Gregory thought God was "gentle with" any untruth. That
  softened what Gregory says, so the script now gives his reading as he
  gave it.
- "The Church teaches it is always wrong to lie" became "many great
  teachers have said". The series stays off ground where Catholic and
  Orthodox teaching is put differently.
- In the draft, the invented girl agreed to "I heard none of this" when
  she had heard it. In an episode that handles truthfulness this
  carefully, that is now "not a word of this to anyone".
- "More than three thousand years" of reading the book became "thousands
  of years", because the book's date is debated.
