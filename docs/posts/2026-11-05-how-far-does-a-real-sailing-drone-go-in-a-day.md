Our marine-hull programme ranks wind-driven survey drones against solar boats. The wind-driven model said 180 to 270 km a day on real buoy winds. Is that believable?

One published figure to test it against: Saildrone's SD 1020 sailed 22,000 km around Antarctica in 196 days in 2019, about 112 km a day. Our model is 2 to 3 times higher, and 303 km/day at Southern Ocean wind speeds. The real mission includes storms, station keeping and survey routes the model lacks, and it is one vehicle and one press-release number, so this is a bound, not a calibration.

What survives: the fielded drone still beat our solar boat's 86 km/day ceiling by 30%. What does not: the factor of 2 to 3 margin from earlier milestones. Next is a daily-distance distribution from a public mission track. Details: `research/marine-hull/LOG.md`, milestone 6k.
