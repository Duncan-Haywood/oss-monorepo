[Egeria and the Friend at the Shrine of Thecla](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/120-egeria-and-the-friend-at-the-shrine-of-thecla.md)
is episode 120 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 6,800
spoken words, roughly 44 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text
only, because the audio folders are over the creative prompt's 400 MB line
(474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** It was on the series list for several runs, behind the
Paul sequels. Episode 110 told Thecla's legend and ended with two
paragraphs on Egeria's visit to her shrine. This episode tells that visit
as the main story. The source is unusual for the show: a first-person
travel letter of about 384, written by a woman to the women back home.

**How the story is built.** The visit itself takes up a page and a half of
Egeria's *Travels* (chapters 22 and 23). The episode adds context from
her own letter. Her habit at every holy place was prayer, then the reading,
then a psalm, then prayer. Her line "because I am rather curious, I began
to ask" is in chapter 16. The Jerusalem church preached in Greek with a
Syriac translator and Latin whisperers (chapter 47). Deacons guarded the
wood of the Cross on Good Friday because someone had once bitten a piece
off (chapter 37). So when the whole Acts of Thecla is read at the shrine,
listeners already know it is her habit, done one more time. The centre of
the episode is her one sentence on meeting Marthana: "Could I ever
describe how great was her joy, and mine?" The episode only imagines that
meeting. Egeria herself never describes it.

**What the episode is careful about.**

- *No canonisation claimed.* Valerius of Bierzo, a seventh-century monk,
  calls Egeria "blessed". She has never been canonised, and the episode says
  so out loud and never calls her a saint. Marthana is known only from this
  letter, so the episode claims no feast for either woman.
- *Deaconesses, described and not argued.* Marthana is a *diaconissa*. The
  episode describes what fourth-century Eastern deaconesses did, helping at
  the baptism of women and caring for women in the community, and says
  nothing either way about later questions.
- *Thecla's story labelled.* The Acts of Paul and Thecla is told as a
  second-century book that is not Scripture, with Tertullian's note about
  its author. Thecla's last years at Seleucia are given as the story's
  ending, not as history.
- *Neighbours checked.* Gregory of Nazianzus stayed at the shrine a few years
  earlier. That comes from his poem on his own life, and the episode
  does not claim anyone Egeria met had known him. Zeno's basilica, built
  about a century later, is from Evagrius (*Church History* 3.8).

**Invented, and said so.** Egeria says only that she met Marthana in
Jerusalem. The Good Friday meeting, with its shared look at the
deacons, is invented. So is every word the two friends say, along with the
flour on Marthana's hands, the mule, the beekeeper's honey and the wave at
the bend in the road. Her letter ends in Constantinople with a plan to go
to Ephesus and the request "remember me". The episode ends there too and
says that nobody knows whether she got home.

The blessing is Psalm 121:8, a pilgrims' psalm: "The Lord will keep your
going out and your coming in."
