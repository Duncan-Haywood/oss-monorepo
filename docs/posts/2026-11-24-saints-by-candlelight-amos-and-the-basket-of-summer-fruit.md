[Amos and the Basket of Summer Fruit](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/175-amos-and-the-basket-of-summer-fruit.md)
is episode 175 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, like every episode
since number 30, because the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the
creative track's line is 400 MB. The script is 7,510 spoken words, about
forty-eight minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 155, the wise woman of
Tekoa, put it on the series list.

## Leaving the wilderness

The last ten episodes stayed with Moses and the people in the wilderness.
This one jumps about five hundred years ahead to the town of Tekoa, on the
edge of the same kind of desert. The reason is the list: the next-up entries
had grown into a long run of Exodus and Numbers stories, and Amos was one of
the oldest left from outside them.

## The job title is the story

Amos 7:14 is the line people quote: "I am no prophet, nor a prophet's son;
but I am a herdsman, and a dresser of sycamore figs." The episode spends a
whole scene on the second half of that line. A biblical sycamore is a fig
tree (*Ficus sycomorus*, the tree Zacchaeus climbed in Luke 19:4). It does
not grow well at Tekoa's height. First Chronicles 27:28 puts David's
sycamores in the lowland Shephelah, so the script calls Amos's travel to the
groves a sensible guess, not something the book says. Theophrastus
(*Enquiry into Plants* 4.2.1) says the fruit would not ripen unless it was
scraped with iron claws. The basket scene comes back to that picture: a man who nicked
hundreds of small figs by hand, so he knew what an honest measure was, sent
to people who "make the ephah small and the shekel great" (Amos 8:5).

Amos 1:1 uses a different word for him, *noqed*. It appears only once more
in the Old Testament: Mesha of Moab in Second Kings 3:4, who paid a tribute
of a hundred thousand lambs. The script says the word may mean a breeder,
not a hired boy, and that nobody can be sure.

## What the episode would not decide

The book does not say which words Amos spoke at Bethel and which he spoke at
Samaria or elsewhere. The script says so out loud. It then gives the
oracles against the nations (chapters 1 and 2) as a picture of a crowd that
cheers through Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon and Moab, until Judah and
then Israel. The basket of summer fruit (8:1-2) is told as what the book
calls it, a vision. The invented boy carries a real basket of figs up the
road, but nothing in the story turns the vision into that basket. The
pun is explained for the ear: *qayits*, summer fruit, and *qets*, the end.

Amos's hard word to Amaziah (7:16-17) is about the priest's own family. The
episode says it was given and not softened, and points to the book without
reading it out at bedtime. His death is told in one quiet sentence and
labelled as the later tradition of the *Lives of the Prophets*, not
Scripture.

## A check the re-read changed

The first draft said Samaria fell "within about forty years after the days
of Jeroboam". Jeroboam son of Joash died about 746 BC and Samaria fell in
722, so that was too loose. The finished line counts from Amos's preaching
instead, about 760, which fits. The same pass took out a "Dr." before
Martin Luther King, because the series rules ban abbreviations that a voice
reads badly. The episode uses Amos 5:24 as the line people know even when
they do not know it is his.

## What changed on the list

The finished entry moved to `DONE.md` with its sources. Three sequels were
added: Hosea, Amos's northern contemporary; Jonah son of Amittai and his
word to Jeroboam about the borders (Second Kings 14:25), which the episode
mentions; and Uzziah's towers and cisterns (Second Chronicles 26).
