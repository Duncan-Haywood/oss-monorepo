[Simeon and the Pillar on the Hill at Telanissos](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/095-simeon-and-the-pillar-on-the-hill-at-telanissos.md)
is episode 95 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 6,400
spoken words, roughly 40 minutes. Like every episode since 20, it is text only
for now, because the audio folders are still over the repository's 400 MB cap
(423 MB today).

The show's bible had Simeon on its "next up" list for a while, with a note
asking for more saints' lives after a long run of Scripture episodes.

## A source written while he was still up there

What makes Simeon a good fit for a show about honesty is his main source.
Theodoret, bishop of Cyrrhus, wrote chapter twenty-six of his *Religious
History* (the *History of the Monks of Syria*) around 444, about fifteen
years before Simeon died. He had visited him himself, and he writes like a
historian who expects readers to doubt him. Most of the episode's details
come from that chapter:

- the snowy day when the shepherd boy couldn't take the sheep out, went to
  church, heard the Beatitudes and asked an old man what "pure in heart"
  meant;
- the dream of digging a foundation and being told four times to dig deeper;
- the monastery at Teleda asking him to leave because his palm-fibre rope was
  a bad example to the younger monks;
- the priest Bassus sealing him in a hut for Lent with ten loaves that stayed
  uneaten;
- the country bishop Meletius telling him that his own will was chain enough,
  and the twenty-odd bugs that came out from under the leather when the smith
  cut it off;
- pilgrims from Persia, Armenia, Georgia, Spain, Britain and Gaul, and small
  pictures of him at workshop doors in Rome;
- pillars of six, twelve, twenty-two and then thirty-six cubits;
- Theodoret's defence of the pillar using the prophets' strange signs (Isaiah
  barefoot, Jeremiah's yoke, Ezekiel lying on his side), and a king who puts
  a new image on the coinage so people look at the coins again;
- the Bedouin crowd that nearly flattened Theodoret trying to get his
  blessing, until Simeon shouted at them from above;
- the companion who counted 1,244 bows one evening and then gave up. That is
  the cold open.

The episode tells the dream, the loaves and the bugs as Theodoret's report.
The story of the desert fathers ordering him down to test his obedience comes
from Evagrius, who wrote about 130 years later, and it is labelled that way.

## What the episode would not do

The other two early Lives, the Greek one by Antonius and the Syriac one, have
harsher material, including the story of his mother coming to see him. The
episode leaves those out and says so in the notes. It also makes no claim
about the size of the platform at the top, because the sources don't give
one. The episode opens and closes by saying plainly that the Church asks
nobody to copy Simeon, and that Simeon didn't either: what he taught the
crowds twice a day was ordinary honesty, fairness and prayer.

The one invented character is Habib, a boy from the village at the foot of
the hill. He carries water up the ladder, grows up, and eventually sends his
own son up with the jug. The welcome, the notes and the front matter all
say he is invented. His job is to stand in for the real people of Telanissos
who did that work for thirty-five years and whose names nobody wrote down.
