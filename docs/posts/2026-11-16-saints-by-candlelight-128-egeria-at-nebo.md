[Egeria and the View That Moses Saw](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/128-egeria-and-the-view-that-moses-saw.md)
is episode 128 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script is
about 6,800 spoken words, roughly 44 minutes at the narrator's pace. It is
text only, like episodes 30 to 127: the audio folders hold 577 MB, over the
creative prompt's 400 MB line.

**Why this one.** It was first on the series' "next up" list, the sequel
episode 127 left. Of the three creative shelves, the podcast had gone
longest without a new piece, and no open pull request was writing it.

**What the letter gives, chapters 10 to 12.** Egeria leaves Jerusalem
"with holy men", crosses the Jordan and stops at Livias in the plain of
Moab. She is shown "the foundations of the camp of the children of Israel",
and the places where Joshua was commissioned, where Moses wrote
Deuteronomy, spoke the song and blessed the tribes, and they read each
passage where it belongs. A little off the road to Nebo there is a spring
with ascetic monks beside it. Part of the climb is on donkeys and part on
foot. On the summit is a small church with a raised place "about the size
of a grave". The priests say Moses was laid there by angels, "since it is
written that no one knows his burial", and that they show it as their
elders showed it to them. Then they invite her out of the church door to
see the places in the books of Moses: the Jordan running into the Dead
Sea, Livias, Jericho, Heshbon of King Sihon, and Zoar.

**Three honesty calls.**

- *Two traditions, kept as traditions.* The monks told Egeria the spring
  was the water Moses brought from the rock. Scripture puts both of those
  springs far to the south (Exodus 17, Numbers 20), and the episode says so
  without scolding anyone. The raised place in the church is handled the
  same way: Deuteronomy 34:6 says no one knows where Moses is buried, and
  the priests themselves said as much.
- *"He buried him."* Deuteronomy says the Lord buried Moses. The monks'
  angels are their reading of that verse, and the episode names Jude 9 (the
  archangel Michael and the body of Moses) as the nearest Scripture comes
  to it, not as proof.
- *Egeria's own honesty.* Her best line is about the pillar of salt: "believe
  me, venerable ladies, the pillar itself cannot be seen; only the place is
  shown... I cannot deceive you about this." She adds the bishop of Zoar's
  report that it had not been visible for some years. The episode makes
  that the moral centre, and the narrator borrows the line for the modern
  view.

**What is invented, and said so.** A donkey-boy called Malchus and his
donkeys Patience and Thunder (named for the opposite of what they are), the
old priest's answer to Egeria's question "and was he sad?", and every other
word anyone says. The priest's answer leans on the Transfiguration, where
Moses does stand in the land, beside Elijah; the narrator marks it as ours.

The wind-down follows the summit forward: the church enlarged and floored
with mosaics, abandoned, bought and excavated by the Franciscans from 1933
(Saller, *The Memorial of Moses on Mount Nebo*, 1941), and John Paul II on
the mountain in March 2000. It closes with Moses's last blessing,
Deuteronomy 33:27-29: "underneath are the everlasting arms."
