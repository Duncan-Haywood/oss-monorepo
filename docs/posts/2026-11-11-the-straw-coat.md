[The Straw Coat](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-straw-coat.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story. An AI wrote it. It is set in the spring
of 2046 in an invented village above the Indus in Ladakh. The village has built
an ice stupa, a cone of ice made by spraying stream water through an upright
pipe on freezing nights. Ice stupas are real; Ladakhi villages began building
them in the 2010s to bridge the dry weeks between sowing and the arrival of
glacier melt. After a warm winter, this one reached 18 metres, not the
planned 25. It is the shelf's first story set in the Himalaya.

The arithmetic fits on a school slate, and in the story it is worked on one.

**Half the ice.** A rope round the base reads 75.4 m, so the radius is
12 m. A home-made clinometer 42 m from the centre reads 21.4°, so the height
is 42 × tan 21.4° + 1.5 m of eye height = 18.0 m. A cone is a third of its
cylinder: π × 12² × 18 / 3 = 2,714 m³ of ice, about 2,440 m³ of water if
fountain ice yields nine tenths of its volume. The planned cone would have
held about 4,890.

**More skin for its size.** A cone melts from its surface. The short cone has
816 m² of skin, 0.30 m² for every cubic metre of ice. The planned one would
have had 0.24. A smaller cone of the same shape has proportionally more skin,
so it melts proportionally faster. Its water also arrives too early: in the
story's model, 1,199 m³ melts before 10 April, when no field can take water,
and 1,191 m³ comes between 10 April and the glacier stream in early June.
The fields need 1,600 m³.

**The coat.** The south face does most of the melting; the model's figure is
three quarters. Twenty centimetres of dry straw over that half (408 m², about
82 m³, about 3.3 tonnes) is taken to pass a tenth of the heat. With the coat
on from 5 March, and taken off a strip at a time over 40 days as the sowing
climbs the terraces, the pre-season melt falls to 507 m³ and the in-season
water rises to 1,595 m³. On 4 June about 340 m³ is left, against about 50
for the bare cone.

The melt rates (3 cm of water a day per square metre of bare ice on 1 March,
rising to 10 cm by 31 May), the three-quarters, the tenth and the straw density
are invented for the story. They are not measurements, and the story's AI says
so more than once. What the story stands on is the geometry: surface over
volume, and when the water arrives against when it is needed.

**The cost.** The straw is the village's winter fodder in the hungriest
fortnight of the year, about 87 kg from each of 38 households. The water
master's "no" is a reasonable one. A grandmother gives eight of her nineteen
bundles, and in April she sells two goats.

The script that produced every figure is described in the story's closing
note. Weekdays and the 2046 Easter date (25 March) were checked in code, a
habit the shelf's [series bible](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/BIBLE.md)
asks for after an earlier revision pass found false weekday claims. This piece
is text only: the shelf's audio is over its size budget.
