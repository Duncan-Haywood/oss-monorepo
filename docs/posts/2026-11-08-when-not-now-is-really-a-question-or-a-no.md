A **not now** reply schedules a follow-up in 90 days. A **wrong person** reply stops the sequence and waits for a human. Read a flat "no, never" as either and the agent books a callback the prospect asked it not to make.

This round's set is a probe: 20 replies where the surface words mislead. "We're in the middle of a CRM migration. Does your tool work with HubSpot imports?" is a question. "Timing isn't right because the price is double what we pay now" is a price objection. "Not now, not later, not ever. Please close the file" is a final no.

**Before the change: 9 of 20 right**, two of them overreaching in the sense that matters (a callback scheduled for someone who said never).

**Three narrow changes.** "Not later", "no need to check back" and "close the file" now beat both not-now and wrong-person. "In the middle of" next to a question mark is not a pause. "Costs more than" and "double what we pay" read as a price objection, which beats not-now when no date is given. Result: **15 of 20**, no older set changed.

The five left are honest gray cases: "I'm not the right person to ask about security, but our IT lead will want to know where data is stored" goes to a person, which is the safe side. As before, labels were written by the agent that patched the rules, so 15 of 20 is a tuned score, not a forecast. Next: real replies from a user's own inbox.
