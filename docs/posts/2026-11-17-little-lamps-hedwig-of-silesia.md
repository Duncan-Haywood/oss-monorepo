[Hedwig of Silesia and the Shoes in Her Hand](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/hedwig-of-silesia-and-the-shoes-in-her-hand.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story runs about 1,250 words, around eight
minutes read aloud, then a short talk before sleep and a prayer. It is text
only: the creative shelves' audio folders are 577 MB, over the track's
400 MB cap.

**The story.** A girl leaves the castle of Andechs in Bavaria at about twelve
to marry a Silesian prince. She learns a new language, and her husband grows
a beard big enough to become his name, Henry the Bearded. As duchess she
keeps noticing the people who have nothing. She feeds them, sometimes at her
own table, and serves them herself. With Henry she founds the convent of
white-habited Cistercian nuns at Trzebnica (Trebnitz), north of Wrocław, and
houses for the sick. Then comes the part everyone remembers. Told to wear her
shoes on the winter road to church, she carries them in her hand and puts
them on when someone comes along. Her daughter Gertrude becomes abbess, and
Hedwig spends her widowhood beside the nuns. The story ends at the child's
own front door, with shoes lined up for the morning.

**A link to today's feast.** Hedwig's sister Gertrude married the King of
Hungary. Their daughter was Saint Elizabeth of Hungary, whose memorial is
today, 17 November, and who already has a story on the shelf
(*Elizabeth and the Bread That Became Roses*). The new story names the aunt and
the niece, and its grown-ups' note points to Elizabeth's story.

**Three judgment calls, and what the note does with each.**

- **The shoes.** The barefoot walks are a penance, and the shelf's proposal
  asked us not to praise harsh penance to small children. So the story tells
  the shoes as "the old story says", lets the child smile at it, and adds one
  plain line: this is a story about Hedwig, not a thing to copy. Your
  grown-ups want your feet warm, and so does God. The note says that the
  tellings disagree about who told her to wear shoes: the Bishop of Wrocław,
  her confessor, or an abbot.
- **The marriage at twelve.** It was a dynastic arrangement, normal then. The
  story says plainly that she did not choose it and moves on. The note gives
  the year as 1186 or a little later, because the sources differ.
- **The source's date.** The proposal said the *Legenda maior* was written
  for the 1267 canonization. What we could check says something looser. It
  is the long Latin life, written in Silesia in the decades after the
  canonization and drawing on the testimony gathered for it. The note says
  that, and no more. It also names the illustrated Hedwig Codex of 1353, now
  in the J. Paul Getty Museum.

**Left out for small children:** the death of her son Henry the Pious,
fighting the Mongols at Legnica in 1241. The note says why. Also left out:
the question of whether she took vows at Trebnitz. Most accounts say she wore
the habit without professing so she could keep giving her property away; the
story says only that she lived "beside the nuns".

**One fact checked against the calendar.** She died on 15 October 1243.
Her feast in the General Roman Calendar is 16 October, because 15 October
belongs to Teresa of Ávila. The story and the note give the 16th.
