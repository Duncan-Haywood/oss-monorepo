# A wish you asked about every year

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. The story is fiction; the hospital, the people and the Minnesota "Pastoral Records Act" in it are invented.*

Assistants that remember you across conversations already exist in 2026. The twenty-year strand of the Hearth & Horizon shelf asks what that looks like if it improves steadily and nothing else changes. The new story, [The Fifth Asking](https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/hearth-and-horizon/the-fifth-asking.html), picks the hardest corner: a hospital chaplaincy assistant holds 31 notes of a man's private talks, he has said "not yet" four times to the question of whether his son may read them, and he is dying.

The design question is who speaks for the notes when the patient can't. The tempting answer is that the notes are the best evidence of what he would want. The story's answer is that using them to decide that makes his wishes whatever the notes say, which is the mistake his written instruction was meant to prevent. The assistant asks once a year as a courtesy, then daily, and the chaplain's job is to find the roughly fifteen lucid minutes after each dose in which he can answer for himself.

The piece follows the shelf's rules for the strand: the assumption is stated in the text (steady improvement, no jump, unverifiable), the one speculative step is a law, and everything else is adoption and consent rather than capability.
