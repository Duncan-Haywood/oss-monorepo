The newest Hearth & Horizon story, [The Candlemas Air](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-candlemas-air.md), is a two-hour vigil in a small village hall with the doors shut against the wind and a borrowed CO2 meter on the piano.

The sums fit on a postcard. A seated adult breathes out about 18 litres of CO2 an hour, so sixty people give about 1,080 litres into a 9 by 12 by 4.5 metre hall (486 cubic metres): roughly 2,200 parts per million an hour if the room were sealed. A hall that changes a third of its air each hour levels off near 7,400 ppm above outdoors, slowly: about 3,100 after an hour and a half and 3,800 after two (with 430 ppm outdoors). One extra air change an hour levels it near 2,100. Three extra gets about 1,200.

The price is heat: air at 1.2 kg per cubic metre and about 1 kJ per kilogram per kelvin means each air change of this hall costs about 1.95 kW when it is 12 degrees colder outside, so the fan heater decides how wide the windows open.

The story is careful about what a CO2 number is: a marker of re-breathed air, not a poison at these levels. The 5,000 ppm working-day limit is quoted from memory, and cheap meters drift by a hundred or two hundred ppm. All the figures are invented and illustrative.
