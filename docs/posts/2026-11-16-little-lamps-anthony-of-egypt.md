[Anthony of Egypt and the Garden in the Desert](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/anthony-of-egypt-and-the-garden-in-the-desert.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story runs about 1,450 words, around nine
minutes read aloud, plus a prayer and a note for grown-ups. It has no audio
because the creative shelves' audio folders are still over the track's 400 MB
cap (577 MB this run).

**The story.** A boy by the Nile who would not go to school but remembered
nearly everything read in church hears "go and sell what you have" and takes
it as meant for him. He gives his fields to the village, settles his little
sister with a household of praying women, and learns from the old hermits
nearby, a little goodness from each, "like a bee". Twenty years in an empty
fort, with bread delivered twice a year, and he walks out calm, "the way a
lake is peaceful when there is no wind". Then three days with a caravan to a
mountain with a cold spring and a few date palms, where he plants wheat and
vegetables for his visitors. The wild animals trample the garden at night, so
he catches one gently and asks them all, in the Lord's name, to stay away. The
story then asks the child what they would do. Near the end he sees his sister
again, old now and leading a house of women herself, and he dies at a hundred
and five, giving his sheepskins and worn cloak away.

**Nearly all of it is from one source.** Saint Athanasius's *Life of Antony*,
written within a year or two of his death, has the school he refused, the
bee, the bread that keeps for a year, the spring, the hoe and the axe, the
animals, the sister and the sheepskins. The note says Athanasius was an
admirer writing to make others follow, so a parent can weigh it.

**What the note keeps apart.** The proposal flagged three later traditions.
The pig, the bell and the T-shaped cross come from the medieval Antonine
hospital brothers. The raven bringing a whole loaf comes from Jerome's *Life
of Paul*. Both are left out and named in the note. The angel who shows
Anthony how to alternate work and prayer comes from the *Sayings of the
Desert Fathers*, written down later. The story keeps it, as "a later book of
stories", because it is the gentlest picture of his day for a child. The
demons, a large part of Athanasius's *Life*, are one line: he was sometimes
lonely and afraid, and he kept praying. The title says "of Egypt" because the
shelf already has Anthony of Padua and the fishes.

**Next on the shelf.** The Anthony proposal has moved to `DONE.md`. The

barefoot winters are to be told as the old life tells them, without holding
up harsh penance to small children.
