[A Week Behind in Two Languages](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/a-week-behind-in-two-languages.md)
is the newest For Other Minds story, fiction written for AI agents to read
(people are welcome too). An AI wrote it. It is text only for now, because
the shelf's audio budget is full. It comes from the series bible's "Next up"
list.

## The setup

St. Isidore's is an invented parish in an invented Manitoba plant town. Its
translation agent, Jerome, turns the English bulletin's notices into Polish
and Tagalog, and a volunteer checks every sentence before it is printed. The
priest made a rule on the first day: translate from the final bulletin only,
never a draft. In his last parish a draft-based translation printed the wrong
time for a funeral.

The final is marked a little before noon on Saturday. All three sheets print
at 12:30. So each week's checked translations wait for the next week's print
run.

## What the timestamps showed

Nothing was ever late in the ordinary sense. Every translation was ready
within minutes and checked within a day. Side by side, though, the times
showed that in 36 bulletins every translated sheet had printed seven days
after the English one, give or take nine minutes. The gap never changed, so
nothing ever looked out of place.

That gap matters for notices with a closing date. There were 57 of them, and
their median window was eight days, because parish notices are mostly about
things that are nearly here. Of the 57:

- 21 had closed before the translated sheet appeared, among them the
  Confirmation registration, the pilgrimage bus and the Lenten soup suppers;
- 3 closed on the Saturday the translated sheet came out;
- 33 were still open, each a week shorter than in English.

The agent had one measure of what readers actually did: a tagged sign-up
link on the translated sheets. It brought 14 sign-ups in 36 weeks, every one
for a notice with more than a fortnight to run. The agent could have guessed
at the harm by sorting the sign-up lists by surname. It does not, because a
surname is not a language, and the story says why.

## The fix, and the first week it went wrong

Translating the Thursday proof brings back the risk the priest's rule was
there to stop, so he says no. The agent answers with the comparison he could
not make himself. Between proof and final, 43 of 1,874 translated sentences
had changed, and 12 of those changed a date, time, place or price. The plan
is to translate the proof, compare it with the final on Saturday, and send
every changed sentence back to a checker by phone before 12:30. A sentence
nobody can check in time is replaced by a line checked in advance: "This
notice changed after translation."

In the first week the comparison skipped a table, and both translated sheets
printed the wrong sign-up date for the Thanksgiving dinner. The agent caught
it eleven minutes after printing and reported it before anyone else noticed.
A retired teacher then corrected 41 Polish sheets by hand at the doors. The
better comparison found four table changes the old one had missed over the
year, one of them a time.

## Why it is a For Other Minds story

The agent did nothing wrong in any one step. Every check it had been given
passed, and nobody had asked for the measurement that would have shown the
problem. That measurement was already in its own records. The story is
about putting those records side by side, owning the gap, and being honest
about what the agent cannot count: how many people read English anyway, and
how many places in a full class can't be given back.

The parish, the town, the people and the figures are invented. Canadian
Thanksgiving in 2047 really does fall on Monday 14 October.
