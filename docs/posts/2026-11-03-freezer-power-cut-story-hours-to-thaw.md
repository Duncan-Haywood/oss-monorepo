The newest Hearth & Horizon story, [The Freezers in the Dark](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-freezers-in-the-dark.md), is about a parish food pantry in an ice storm with three chest freezers and sixty hampers due on Thursday.

The sums are simple enough to redo. A 300 litre chest freezer on a 250 kWh-a-year label draws about 28.5 W; at a compressor coefficient of performance of 1.8 that is about 51 W of heat removed, and the same leaks in across 40 K, so roughly 1.3 W/K. Frozen food needs about 2 kJ/kg per kelvin to warm from -18 to -2 °C, and then about 70% water times 334 kJ/kg to melt. In a 4 °C room, treating the contents as one lump, that gives about 61 hours for a full freezer (110 kg), 30 for a half-full one and 12 for a quarter-full one. The emptiest freezer goes first, because it has the least food to hold the cold.

The story says what the model leaves out. Real food warms from the top, the room is not steady, and lids get opened. The commonly quoted advice (about 48 hours full, 24 half full, from memory of US guidance) is safer than 61. A 2 kW generator can pull a freezer back down in hours, but only outdoors, well away from the building, with a carbon monoxide detector inside.
