The owner interviewed a designer who wants to start a unisex clothing line: shirts carrying classical and devotional paintings, saints and angels, printed so they stand off the fabric "like 3D paintings", on fabric that looks expensive even when it isn't. The designer asked for two things above the rest: to describe changes out loud and by pointing, and to know what every shirt costs. "I'm not good at marketing" came up too.

The [Clothing Design Studio](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/) is the first slice of that, and it runs in a browser.

**Talking to it needs no AI.** "Make it cream, add a halo, embroider it in 3D and move it up a bit" is split into four clauses and each becomes one edit: garment colour, a motif, 3D puff embroidery, a 1 cm move. Every change is listed with the words that made it and can be undone. Anything the parser does not know ("frobnicate the shirt") is reported as not understood, never guessed. That makes it free to run for everyone. A model is an option for freer speech ("make it feel like an old church painting"), but the model only rewrites the sentence into the same commands, so it cannot do anything a typed command could not. It uses your own key or a model on your own computer.

**Three versions, one change each.** "Show me three versions" gives a different colourway, a different size and placement, and a different decoration method side by side, and "go with the second one" picks one. Each differs from your design in one way, so you can tell what you are choosing.

**Raised prints are drawn, and their limits are named.** High-density ink, puff print and 3D puff embroidery are drawn with depth. The studio also says when they are a poor fit: a detailed painting usually prints better flat, with the raised ink kept to an outline or a halo.

**The cost comes from your supplier's quote, not ours.** The studio ships no real prices. Its example sheet uses round placeholder numbers, labelled as placeholders everywhere they appear. With those numbers, 100 heavyweight tees with a raised front print and a printed back cost €21.27 each landed. At €45 including 20% VAT, card fees leave €14.58 a shirt: a 38.9% margin, not the 53% that 45 against 21 seems to promise. With €500 of launch spend, break-even is 74 sales, and only 90 are expected to sell. [The full sum is here](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/how-much-does-it-cost-to-make-a-t-shirt.html). These are illustrative figures, not market prices.

**The factory gets a tech pack.** Every placement is measured from the shoulder high point. The garment drawing is to scale for that reason: a size-M tee at 51 cm across and 72 cm long, which a test checks. A quote request draft lists the exact questions to ask. A launch kit drafts the product description, captions, hashtags and a store import file from the design's own facts, so it never says "organic" unless the fabric is organic.

Not built yet: VR (the interview's favourite), a multi-brand shop with commissions, ad campaigns, and turning an uploaded painting into a printable colour separation. They are on the [roadmap](https://github.com/duncan-haywood/oss-monorepo/blob/main/ventures/clothing-design-studio/ROADMAP.md). The next real test is the designer's own artwork and a real quote going through it.
