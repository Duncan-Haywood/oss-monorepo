[Paul and the King Who Said Almost](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/123-paul-and-the-king-who-said-almost.md)
is episode 123 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script is
about 8,400 words, roughly 54 minutes at the narrator's pace. It is text
only, like every episode since 20, because the audio folders are still over
the creative prompt's 400 MB line (474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** It was first on the series list and picks up where episode
122 stopped: Felix recalled, Paul left in prison, a new governor arriving.
Acts 25 and 26 are the last courtroom before the voyage, and they hold two
of the most quoted lines in Acts: "I appeal to Caesar" and Agrippa's
answer, which the King James Version renders "Almost thou persuadest me to
be a Christian."

**What the episode leans on.**

- *Festus is quick where Felix was slow.* He goes up to Jerusalem three days
  after arriving and hears the case the day after he gets back (25:1, 25:6).
  The episode sets that beside the two years of "not tonight" in episode 122.
- *A governor with nothing to write.* 25:26-27 has Festus say outright that
  he has "nothing definite to write to my lord" and that sending a prisoner
  without charges is unreasonable. The episode builds its one invented
  figure, a secretary called Decimus with a blank wax tablet, on that
  admission. He is labelled as invented in the welcome, the notes and the
  front matter.
- *"A certain Jesus, who was dead, whom Paul said was alive"* (25:19). The
  whole creed, said by a puzzled official over dinner. Festus calls it a
  question of their *deisidaimonia*, the word that can mean religion or
  superstition. Paul used the same word family at Athens (17:22), and the
  episode points that out.
- *The goads.* The voice on the road says "it is hard for you to kick
  against the goads" only in this third telling of the conversion (26:14).
  It is a Greek proverb, found in Aeschylus and Euripides, and Paul is
  speaking to an educated room. The episode reads it as sympathy, not
  rebuke.
- *What "almost" means.* *En oligō* (26:28) probably does not mean "almost".
  Most modern translations read it as "in a short time" or "with so
  little". The Vulgate has *in modico* and the Douay-Rheims "in a little".
  The episode gives the old reading and the newer one, mentions the hymn
  "Almost Persuaded" that grew out of the KJV wording, and leaves open
  whether Agrippa was joking, being sharp or wavering, because Luke does
  not say. Paul's reply, "except for these chains", is the emotional centre.
- *Who the Herods were.* Agrippa II and Bernice are the brother and sister
  of Drusilla from episode 122, and children of the Agrippa who died in
  Caesarea's theatre (Acts 12). From Josephus: Agrippa chose the high
  priests, and there is the story of his new dining room that looked down
  into the temple courts. The priests built a wall to block the view,
  Festus ordered it pulled down, and Nero's wife Poppaea saw that it stayed
  up.

**Checked against the sources.** The adversarial reread changed nine things
before the pages were built. "The only time in the whole Bible" the word
*akroatērion* appears became "the New Testament", since the claim was not
checked against the Septuagint. Felix being "remembered for taking bribes"
became "for his cruelty, and for hoping for bribes", because Acts 24:26 is
the evidence and it reports a hope. Agrippa "kept the keys of the temple's
money" was cut back to "a say in the temple's affairs", which is as far as
the Josephus passages clearly go. Bernice "married twice and widowed once"
was wrong: Josephus has both of her husbands so far dying, so it now says
widowed twice. The tribunes "each in charge of about a thousand men" now
says only that the title meant "commander of a thousand", because real unit
sizes varied. Luke's "we" starts again when the voyage is decided (27:1),
not when they board. Paul's death is "some years later", not "many". The
Josephus source line now includes book nineteen, chapter eight, for the
silver robe. And a line saying "as we have said before on this show" was
cut, since this run could not confirm that an earlier episode said it.

**Kept gentle.** Josephus repeats gossip about Bernice. The episode says so
once and leaves it there. Her later years are told through the brave thing
she did, standing barefoot before the governor Florus to beg for
Jerusalem (*Jewish War* 2.15), and through Titus sending her away
"against his will and against hers" (Suetonius, *Titus* 7). Paul's death is
one clause.
