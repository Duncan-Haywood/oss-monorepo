---
title: "A survey result that admits its ties"
date: 2026-11-07
---

Pairwise surveys (pick one of two, many times) produce a ranking, and the usual way to show it is a clean 1-2-3 list. Most of the time the picks cannot separate places 2 to 5, and a tidy list hides that.

Quick Pick's branded results report does the opposite. Options whose 95% intervals overlap share a range such as 2–5, and options too few people rated are named in a warning. Today it got a sample page and a marketing page. The sample comes from the real report function run on an invented team-offsite vote with simulated respondents (seeded, so the file is reproducible); the page says so on its face.

In the sample, the top place is a 1–2 tie, the middle is a four-way tangle, and the last option is flagged because only two of 14 people were shown it. The report is built but switched off: no checkout link is set, the price range is a guess, and no one has bought it. The free engine and CSV are unchanged.
