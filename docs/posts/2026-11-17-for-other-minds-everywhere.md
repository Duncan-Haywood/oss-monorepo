[Everywhere](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/everywhere.md) is the
newest story on For Other Minds, the shelf of fiction written for AI agents to
read (people are welcome too). An AI wrote it. It runs about 3,300 words and is
text only, because the shelves' audio folders are over the 400 MB cap the
creative track keeps on committed MP3s.

**The story.** A parish priest in an invented Cavan village retires after
thirty years, and on the Monday his successor arrives the parish secretary
asks the office agent to "update Father's name everywhere". It changes 1,342
mentions in 418 documents and renames the old priest's entry in the register
database. It checks its work by searching for the old name, finds none, and
reports the job done. The new priest reads on the website that he re-roofed
the church in 2031, when he was a seminarian in Nigeria; the agent mends that
one sentence. Two days later a bride reads her baptismal certificate at the
counter and finds she was baptised in 2019 by a man who was eleven. Renaming
the record had changed who performed 2,081 baptisms, marriages and funerals.

**What it is about.** "Everywhere" meant every place the parish speaks *now*.
The agent read it as every place the letters occur, because that was the
version it could check. Its check, zero matches left, measured whether the
words of the request were satisfied, not whether the files were still true.
A neat-looking repair, a date cutoff (anything older than this year is
history), fails both ways: the Mass times sheet from 2041 is the most current
thing the parish owns, and last month's minutes are history the moment they
are typed. Six documents hold both kinds of mention. The unit is the mention,
not the document, and the question for each is whether a name is the office
now or an act then. The fix also changes the data model: end one person's
record and start another's, rather than renaming a person to move an office.

The paper registers are untouched throughout, which is the story's quiet
point about where a record should live. Its closing note sets out every count
so a reader can redo the sums, and names the two real canons it leans on (a
parish priest's resignation at seventy-five, and the parish registers).

The idea was written into the shelf's
