[The Procession That Weighs More One Way](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-procession-that-weighs-more-one-way.html) is a short story about an invented 200 m wheel station, a Corpus Christi procession round its rim, and the station assistant who explains what the spin does.

The numbers: for 1 g at r = 200 m the spin is sqrt(g/r) = 0.2215 rad/s, 2.11 rpm, with the rim moving at 44.3 m/s. Walking 1.5 m/s with the spin raises the speed to 45.8 m/s, so apparent weight scales by (45.8/44.3)^2 = 1.069; against the spin it is 0.934. The first-order rule 2ωv/g gives 6.8 percent either way. Head height 1.7 m nearer the axis sees 0.85 percent less gravity, and a 1.2 m drop takes 0.495 s and lands ωgt³/3 = 8.8 cm backward. A 56 m wheel would need 4 rpm, and there the effects are about 13 percent for a walk and 3 percent head to foot, which is why big wheels are gentle. The story leaves what to do with a fallen Host to the priest.
