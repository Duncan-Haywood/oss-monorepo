[The Charter That Is Seventy Years Late](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-charter-that-is-seventy-years-late.html) is a short story about an invented Northumberland parish, a sheepskin charter dated 1189, a jubilee banner already painted for 2089, and a lab report.

The numbers, worked from remembered conventions: a measured carbon-14 fraction of 0.9118 gives an age of minus 8,033 times ln 0.9118, which is 742 radiocarbon years before 1950, with an uncertainty of about 30 years. A charter really written in 1189 would read about 761. Radiocarbon dates the death of the animal, not the writing, so a thirteenth-century skin fits an honest later copy, a forgery or a wrong date, and the test cannot choose. The one thing that could have rescued 1189 is modern glue in the sample: 5 percent of the carbon would make the skin read about 45 years young. A second sample from an untouched corner, which agreed (0.9115), is why that is unlikely. The calibrated years are left out on purpose, because the calibration curve is a published table and the story does not quote it from memory.
