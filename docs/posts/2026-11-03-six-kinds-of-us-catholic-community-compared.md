Ask how US Catholics differ and you get a list of tribes: Latin Mass families, FOCUS missionaries, Newman Guide college graduates, Spanish-language parishes, rural German Catholic towns, suburban mega-parishes. This post puts them side by side using numbers that can be sourced, and marks where the numbers stop and impressions start.

The short version: **how often someone goes to Mass separates Catholics more than where they go.** Weekly Mass-goers anywhere in the country resemble each other more than they resemble the occasional Catholics in their own parish. Most of the differences between "types" of community come from who chooses to join them, plus age and ethnicity.

## The baseline

- About **one in five US adults** is Catholic. Pew's 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study puts the racial mix of Catholic adults at 54% white, **36% Hispanic** (up from 29% in 2007), 4% Asian and 2% Black ([Pew, 10 facts about US Catholics](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/03/04/10-facts-about-us-catholics/)). Only 40% of Hispanic adults are now Catholic, so growth in the Hispanic population no longer carries Catholic numbers by itself ([Pew, Hispanic Catholics](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/16/profile-of-hispanic-catholics-in-the-us/)).
- **Belief tracks attendance.** In 2019, 31% of Catholics said the bread and wine actually become the body and blood of Christ. Among weekly Mass-goers it was 63% ([Pew, 2019](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2019/08/05/transubstantiation-eucharist-u-s-catholics/)). Vinea Research found that wording changes the answer a lot, so treat the 31% as a floor ([The Pillar](https://pillarcatholic.com/p/the-revival-the-eucharist-and-the)).
- **The same goes for moral teaching.** In 2024, 61% of Catholics said abortion should be legal in all or most cases, against 34% of weekly attenders. Party splits it too: **78% of Catholic Democrats and 43% of Catholic Republicans** ([NPR on Pew, April 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/04/12/1244156165)).
- There are about **16,300 parishes** (16,346 in CARA's 2019 count, 1,437 fewer than in 1971; [America](https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2019/04/19/two-us-churches-one-closing-down-parishes-other-standing-room-only)). CARA's [statistics page](https://cara.georgetown.edu/faqs) has the current series.

## The six communities

**1. Traditional Latin Mass.** Pew's only national question, from 2025, finds 2% of Catholics attend a TLM weekly and 13% have attended one in five years. Counts based on how many Masses are offered suggest nearer 100,000 people, so the 2% is an upper bound. Everything else comes from surveys of attendees who chose to answer. Those find almost universal weekly Mass, near-zero approval of contraception, large families and, in Bullivant and Cranney's 2024 sample, 77% leaning Republican. Details and caveats: [Who attends the Latin Mass](2026-11-03-who-attends-the-latin-mass-what-the-numbers-show.md) and [where it clusters](2026-11-03-where-the-traditional-latin-mass-clusters-in-the-us.md).

**2. Evangelising lay missions (FOCUS, NET, St. Paul's Outreach).** FOCUS reports 1,062 missionaries and 49,439 students in its Bible studies in 2024-25, plus 1,469 cumulative decisions to pursue priesthood or religious life. All of these are its own unaudited numbers. No independent study of its alumni's Mass attendance, marriage or politics exists. [FOCUS and Catholic lay missions by the numbers](2026-11-03-focus-missionaries-and-catholic-lay-missions-by-the-numbers.md).

**3. Social-justice service corps (Jesuit Volunteer Corps, Catholic Volunteer Network).** This is the older tradition of a year of service with the poor, and it is shrinking: JVC fell from a high of 500 volunteers to 89 in 2023-24. These programmes and the evangelising missions above are the clearest case of two Catholic subcultures pulling apart, one oriented to the sacraments and conversion, the other to service and social teaching. There is no survey data comparing what their alumni believe, so any claim about their politics is impression only.

**4. Newman Guide colleges.** The 14 US schools with federal data enrol 21,077 undergraduates. They are whiter, more male and less often on Pell grants than four-year colleges overall, and one Houston university supplies most of their Hispanic students. [Who goes to Newman Guide colleges](2026-11-03-who-goes-to-newman-guide-colleges.md). Don't confuse them with Newman *Centers*, the Catholic ministries at secular universities, which vary from FOCUS-run to old-style progressive. No public dataset covers Newman Center members.

**5. Spanish-language and shared parishes.** A 2014 Boston College study counted about **4,368 parishes with Hispanic ministry**, roughly one in four ([BC news](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/bcnews/news-archive-2011-to-2015/chronicle/2014/topstories/boston-college-study--hispanics-vital-to-future-of-catholic-chur.html)). Many are "shared parishes", where an older, whiter English-language congregation and a younger Spanish-language one share a building but little else. Hispanic Catholics lean Democratic but are moving. Starr County, Texas, about 97% Hispanic, voted Republican in 2024 (57.7% to 41.8%) for the first time since about 1892.

**6. Urban, suburban and rural parishes.** This is where the evidence is thinnest. No national survey reports Catholic belief by parish setting. The usual picture: urban parishes are either immigrant or "destination" parishes with high practice, or are closing. Suburban parishes are large, well funded and middle of the road. Rural Midwestern ones are traditional and Republican and losing their young. That picture is plausible, but it is mostly a reading of county voting patterns.

## Side by side

| Community | Size, sourced | Practice | Politics | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Latin Mass | ≤2% of Catholics weekly (Pew 2025) | Very high (self-selected surveys) | 77% lean R (non-random sample) | Weak beyond the one Pew question |
| FOCUS and similar | 1,062 missionaries (own claim) | High (own 2015 survey, no n) | Not measured | Organisation's own figures |
| Service corps | JVC 89 volunteers, 2023-24 | Not measured | Not measured | Enrolment counts only |
| Newman Guide colleges | 21,077 undergraduates (IPEDS) | Not measured | Not measured | Federal enrolment data |
| Spanish-language parishes | ~4,368 parishes (2014) | Not measured nationally | Lean D, moving R | Parish survey plus election results |
| Weekly Mass-goers overall | ~1 in 4 Catholics | 63% Real Presence (2019) | 34% legal abortion (2024) | Representative surveys |

The last row is the only one with representative data on belief. For every intentional community above it, the striking numbers come from people who chose to join and chose to answer a survey.

## What would settle it

Two public datasets could test most of this without anyone's goodwill. The **Cooperative Election Study** asks about 50,000 people a year their religion, Mass attendance, views and county. The **US Religion Census 2020** counts Catholic adherents by county. Joined, they would show how Catholic belief and vote differ by urban or rural setting and by county partisanship, using real samples instead of the stereotypes above. Neither covers Mass form or language, so the Latin Mass and Spanish-language comparisons still need a national survey that asks.
