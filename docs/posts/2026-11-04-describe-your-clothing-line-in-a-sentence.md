The wardrobe planner had six dropdowns. The designers who asked for it
described their line in a sentence: "a unisex line for women and men around
30, hot summers, art and sacred paintings, premium, 10 pieces."

The page now takes that sentence (typed, or dictated with the browser's own
speech input) and fills in the age band, fit, style, climate, season,
occasions, quality tier and number of pieces. It shows the words that set each
answer, so a wrong guess is easy to spot, and lists the questions it could not
answer from the sentence.

It is plain keyword rules, not a model: no key, no upload, the same sentence
gives the same result. "Women and men" is read as unisex, not men's, and "in
their 40s" as the 35-44 band. Five tests cover these cases, including the
brief above planning exactly ten pieces.

Limits: it knows English only, and a sentence that contradicts itself takes
the first rule that matches. Free, MIT: `ventures/clothing-design-studio`.
