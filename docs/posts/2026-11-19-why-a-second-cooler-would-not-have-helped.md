[Hearth & Horizon](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/) has a new story: [*The Far Window*](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/the-far-window.html). It is about 5,100 words before its closing notes, and text only for now, because the shelf's audio is over its size cap. It is set in June and July 2058 at an invented parish dispensary on the Costa de Hermosillo, the farming plain west of Hermosillo, Sonora. The dispensary opens a heat ward for grape pickers and old people, with one evaporative cooler on the wall. Sonora calls these simply "coolers".

## A cooler is not an air conditioner

An air conditioner takes the room's air, cools it, and gives it back. An evaporative cooler takes hot air from outside and blows it through wet pads. The water drinks heat from the air as it evaporates, and the cooled, damp air goes into the room. Then that air has to leave, so more can come in. In the story the young doctor shuts the windows to keep the cool in, as she would with an air conditioner. The fan keeps roaring, but almost nothing gets through the room. In the story's model, cutting the airflow from 1.2 to 0.5 cubic metres a second takes the ward from about 31 °C toward 38, on a 44 °C afternoon.

The old cooler man shows her with a strip of toilet paper held at the far window. It hangs straight down. Then he opens the window and it streams out. Moving 1.2 cubic metres a second at about a metre a second takes about 1.2 square metres of free opening. Mosquito mesh that lets through 60% turns that into about two square metres of window.

## The wet sock

Nothing that cools with water can get the air below the wet-bulb temperature. That is the reading on a thermometer with a wet sock over its bulb, swung through the air. On a dry June afternoon at 44 °C and 12% humidity, the wet bulb is about 22 °C. A cooler whose pads get 80% of the way there puts out air at about 26. The room's roof, walls and ten people add about five degrees, so the ward holds at 31.

Then the monsoon's humid air comes up the Gulf of California. At 38 °C and 45%, the wet bulb is about 28. The cooler's air comes out at 30, and the ward sits at about 35 °C and 63% humidity, with a wet bulb near 29. It is cooler on the wall thermometer than the shut-window mistake was. It is worse for the people in it, because the wet bulb is what decides how well sweat can dry.

The dispensary's assistant spots a cheap tell along the way. The cooler's water meter falls from 36 litres an hour in June to 16. Every litre evaporated is heat taken out of the air, so a cooler that drinks less is doing less.

## Why a second cooler is the wrong fix

The doctor's reasonable plan is a second cooler. Twice the air spreads the room's heat over twice as much flow. The ward drops from 34.8 °C to 32.5. But twice as much wet air comes in, so the humidity rises from 63% to 71%. The ward's wet bulb moves from 28.8 to 28.1, less than a degree. "Your patients do not sweat by the wall," the cooler man says. "They sweat by the sock." He talks himself out of the sale. The money buys a small heat pump for the consulting room instead, which takes water out of the air rather than putting it in. Four cots fit there, and it holds them at 26 °C and 50%, a wet bulb of about 19.

## What the story is careful about

Every figure comes from a simplified steady-state model, and the closing note lists its parts: pad effectiveness 0.8, Stull's wet-bulb formula, a 6.5 kW load, 1.2 cubic metres a second. Real rooms store heat in their walls and lag behind any steady-state model. The note says the story is not medical or engineering advice. Handling refrigerant needs a certified installer, which is why the cooler man won't fit the heat pump himself. Doña Cuca, the dispensary's 91-year-old, asks whether the summers really are wetter than when she was young. The assistant says it has not read the station records properly and would rather not say. The note keeps to what is documented about the region: the aquifer's seawater intrusion, the grape harvest's migrant workforce, and the monsoon's usual arrival in early July.
