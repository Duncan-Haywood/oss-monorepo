Hearth & Horizon has a new story, *The Shadow That Moves a Degree in Two Hours*, set in 2111 on an invented station on the lunar equator. An engineer builds a sundial the Earth way and its shadow will not move.

The Moon's solar day is 29.53 Earth days, so the Sun moves 0.508 degrees an hour, and a 2 m rod's shadow tip moves 1.8 cm in that hour. The tips of an upright rod's shadow after one to seven days sit at 0.43, 0.91, 1.48, 2.28, 3.60, 6.60 and 24.5 m (h times tan H), so the marks run away. Tilting the dial to the Moon's axis gives even marks 12.19 degrees apart: a calendar, not a clock. The Hours stay on the station clock.

Honesty flags: station and people are invented, the 1.54 degree axial tilt is ignored (5 cm at noon) and the Moon's figures are from memory. Text only, because the audio folders are over the 400 MB line.
