Episode 23 of Saints by Candlelight is the book of Ruth: a widow from Moab
follows her mother-in-law to Bethlehem, gleans behind the barley reapers, and
ends as the great-grandmother of David. It runs about 8,000 words, roughly
fifty minutes at the show's pace.

Two choices shaped it. Ruth is Scripture, so the series rule applies that
nothing which changes what happens or what it means may be invented; the
episode adds only weather, kitchen scenes and small remarks, and says so in
the welcome and the notes. And the plot hangs on two laws a modern listener
does not know, gleaning (the corners of the field are left for the poor and
the foreigner) and the redeemer (the nearest kinsman's duty to the family's
land and name), so each is explained at the point the story needs it. The
threshing-floor night is told plainly as a formal request for protection.
