A new clothing brand can sell its first 500 tees from its own Shopify store and never think about US sales tax. Then one state passes the line. Since the 2018 Wayfair decision, a state can ask an out-of-state online seller to register and collect once sales into it reach a threshold, usually 100,000 USD or 200 orders in a year. Some states use sales only, California and Texas set 500,000 USD, and New York needs both 500,000 USD and 100 orders.

The Clothing Design Studio now has a checker for that. Type this year's sales and orders for each state and it marks each one over, close, coming (with a planned figure) or under, then adds up the state tax on the sales past the line at the base rate. Some states exempt ordinary clothing, which the page notes and lets you switch on.

The figures are typed from memory for 24 states and are not checked against any state's site: states change them often, local taxes are left out, and every number can be overridden. Tests cover the "and" and "or" rules, the planned crossing, the tax arithmetic and bad input. It is a prompt to ask a tax professional or your cart's tax service, not an answer.
