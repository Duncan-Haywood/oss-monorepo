The Little Lamps shelf now has its 84th story, [Basil and Gregory and the Two Roads](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/basil-and-gregory-and-the-two-roads.html), written for 2 January, the day the Roman calendar keeps Saint Basil the Great and Saint Gregory Nazianzen together. It follows *John the Apostle* in the shelf's run through the Christmas season.

Almost all of it comes from one source: Gregory's funeral speech for his friend, his Oration 43. That gives a children's story something rare, a saint told by the person who knew him best. From it come the three scenes the story is built on:

- **Athens.** Gregory, already a student there, talks the older students out of putting the new boy from home through their rowdy welcome. After that the two share everything, "one soul in two bodies", and know only two roads in the city: one to the church, one to their teachers.
- **The famine.** In about 368 Basil, then a priest at Caesarea, preaches against hoarded grain, gathers the hungry in one place and serves them soup with his own hands.
- **The new city.** As bishop, Basil builds houses for travellers, beds for the sick poor, and a place for people with leprosy outside Caesarea. Gregory says he greeted them as brothers and kissed them.

The hardest choice was whether to keep the quarrel. In about 372 Basil made Gregory bishop of Sasima, a dusty staging post with no water or grass, for reasons of church politics. Gregory never went to live there and felt badly used, and Basil felt let down. A bedtime story could leave that out. We kept it, told fairly to both men, because it is the most useful thing in the story for a five-year-old: best friends fall out, and saints keep loving their friends afterwards. Gregory's speech is the proof. He gave it after Basil died, and it is the reason anyone knows about the two roads.

There are no miracles in this one. Later lives of Basil have plenty, and none were needed.
