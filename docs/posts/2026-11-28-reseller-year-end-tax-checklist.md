Most of a reseller's Schedule C is decided by what happens in a few weeks around December 31.
Sales and fees are already in the platforms' reports. The rest is a count of the shelf, a few
records only you have, and two dates. Here is the order to do it in. Seller Books, our
bookkeeping app for resellers, does most of it from your imported reports, and is open on a free

## Before December 31

**1. Mark what left the shelf without selling.** An item you kept for yourself, gave away, lost
or threw out is no longer stock, and Schedule C Part III needs the year it left. Give each one a
date. Kept items come off purchases (line 36); donated and lost items stop counting as stock on
hand. Whether and when to write an item off is a question for your preparer. The free
[reseller inventory spreadsheet](https://marisstella.app/seller-books/reseller-inventory-spreadsheet)
has a Status and a Date Removed column for exactly this.

**2. Decide on December's equipment.** A camera, label printer, shelving or laptop is equipment,
not inventory. Under the de minimis safe harbor, a business without an audited financial
statement can deduct an item or invoice of $2,500 or less in the year it is bought, but only if
the return carries a statement titled "Section 1.263(a)-1(f) de minimis safe harbor election".
Over $2,500, bonus depreciation, which is 100% for equipment bought after January 19, 2025, can
still deduct the whole cost in the year you start using it, through Form 4562. Buying in December
or January moves the deduction a year. It never makes a purchase free.
[Home office and equipment](https://marisstella.app/seller-books/home-office-and-equipment) has
the rules and worked numbers.

**3. Note the months you used the storage space.** Under the simplified home office method, the
deduction is $5 a square foot on at most 300 square feet, averaged over the 12 months, and a month
counts when the space was used on 15 or more days. Write down when the bins moved in.

## On December 31

**4. Count the shelf at cost.** Cost of goods sold is not what you spent on stock this year. It is
opening inventory, plus purchases, minus what is still unsold at the end of the year. That last
figure, the cost of everything on the shelf on December 31, is line 41. Items bought in December
and sold in March count in the year they sell, so a big Q4 sourcing trip does not lower this
year's tax. A count you can show item by item is far stronger than a guess. Lots and bin hauls
bought for one price need their cost split across the items in them:
[cost of goods sold for resellers](https://marisstella.app/seller-books/cost-of-goods-sold)
works through lines 35 to 42.

**5. Close the mileage log.** 2026 has two business rates: 72.5 cents a mile for drives from
January 1 to June 30, and 76 cents from July 1 to December 31. Each trip takes the rate for the
day it was driven. Sourcing trips to thrift stores and estate sales count, and so do post office
runs with orders.
[Mileage deduction for resellers](https://marisstella.app/seller-books/mileage-deduction) has the
log's columns and an example.

## In January

**6. Pay the fourth estimated payment by January 15, 2027.** It covers September 1 to December
31. Resellers often make most of their profit in the fourth quarter, and the annualized method
(Form 2210, Schedule AI) can lower the earlier quarters' payments when that happens.
[Quarterly estimated tax](https://marisstella.app/seller-books/quarterly-estimated-tax) works an
example both ways.

**7. Check opening inventory against last year's return.** This year's line 35 should equal last
year's line 41. If it does not, IRS Publication 334 says: "You must explain any difference in a
schedule attached to your return." Find the difference before your preparer does.

**8. Match each 1099-K, or the lack of one.** For federal reporting a platform files a Form 1099-K
when its payments to you for the year are over $20,000 and more than 200 transactions. Some states
are lower, and a platform may send one below the line. Box 1a counts gross payments, before fees
and refunds, so it will be higher than your sales. Check it month by month against your own
records. No form does not mean no income, and personal items sold at a loss are not income either
way. [Why your 1099-K is higher than your sales](https://marisstella.app/seller-books/1099-k).

## What to hand your preparer

- Sales, fees and shipping by platform for the year (each platform's own reports)
- The closing inventory list at cost, and last year's line 41
- Items that left without selling, with dates
- Equipment bought, with dates and prices, and whether you are making the de minimis election
- The mileage log and the storage space's months and square feet
- Each 1099-K received, and the estimated payments made
