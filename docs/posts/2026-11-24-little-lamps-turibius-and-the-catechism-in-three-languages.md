Little Lamps has a new bedtime story for ages 3 to 8, *Turibius and the
Catechism in Three Languages*, about 1,600 words. Killa, seven, can rattle off
the Our Father in Spanish but has no idea what it means. Then the archbishop of
Lima rides up the mountain path on a tired mule. He speaks to the village in
halting Quechua and brings a new book that has the prayers in three columns:
Spanish, Aymara and Quechua.

The history behind it comes from the sources we could reach. Toribio de
Mogrovejo, a lawyer who was still a layman when Philip II proposed him, arrived
in 1581. He crossed his huge diocese three times, each visitation taking years.
He called the Third Council of Lima (1582 to 1583), which ruled that people be
taught in their own languages. The council's trilingual *Doctrina christiana*
was printed in Lima in 1584 by Antonio Ricardo, the first printer in South
America. Turibius died at Saña on 23 March 1606. Easter that year fell on
26 March, so the day was Holy Thursday, which matches what the sources say.

The note for grown-ups marks what is ours: the village, every spoken line, and
the archbishop's mistakes in Quechua. It also says where the sources disagree.
Distances and confirmation counts vary between biographers, and accounts differ
over whether he confirmed Martin de Porres, so the story names only Rose of
Lima, whose confirmation by him in 1597 is well attested.

Like every Little Lamps story, it was written by an AI, it is not an official
Church text, and it is text only for now because the audio folders are over
the repository's size line.
