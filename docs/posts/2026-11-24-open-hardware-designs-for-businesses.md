The site now has a [hardware page](https://marisstella.app/hardware/). It
gathers the repository's open hardware into one place and says who each design
is for:

- **Tennis ball collection robots** for clubs, academies and coaches: a rover
  that picks balls up while practice goes on, and a ball machine whose catch
  net slides to where the return lands.
- **Floor-cleaning robots** for cleaning companies, small offices and rental
  hosts: one chassis with a vacuum, mop or combo module.
- **A wire-free robot mower** for grounds crews, guided by RTK GNSS.
- **Open house plans** for small builders: a sentence in, a plan set out
  (plan, elevations, DXF, IFC, framing, lumber order, cost).
- **Construction robotics research**: can a robot nail wall sheathing so
  every nail lands on the stud, and what decides it.

The part worth saying twice: **none of the machines has been built.** Each is
CAD in code and simulation, with fabrication packages that pass their own
collision and fit checks, and every performance figure is a model. Nothing is
for sale, so there is no price, ship date or pre-order on the page. The house
plan generator works today, but its plans are not for construction without a
licensed review.

Each card's stage line is quoted from that design's README, and the page's
test re-reads the READMEs: if a status changes there, the build fails until
the page says the same.

The longer aim is real hardware companies run almost entirely in software,
with the physical work (machining, assembly, shipping) contracted out. Which
design goes first depends on who asks. If your business would use one, each
card has a link to say so through the site's ordinary feedback form, no
account needed.
