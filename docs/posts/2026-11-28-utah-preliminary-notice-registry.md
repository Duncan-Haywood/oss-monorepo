In most states with a preliminary notice, the subcontractor mails a letter to the owner and the
general contractor sends nothing. Utah does it differently. The notice is **filed with the State
Construction Registry**, and on a private job the statute makes no exception for the GC: "A person
who desires to claim a construction lien on real property shall file a preliminary notice with the
registry no later than 20 days after the day on which the person commences providing construction
work on the real property" (Utah Code § 38-1a-501(1)(a)). The cost of skipping it: "A person who
fails to file a preliminary notice as required in this section may not claim a construction lien"
(§ 38-1a-501(1)(e)).

## Late, and too late

A late filing still counts for something. You may file after the 20 days, but you "may not claim a
construction lien for construction work the person provides to the construction project before the
date that is five days after the preliminary notice is filed" (§ 38-1a-501(1)(c)(ii)). Work from
your first day up to the fifth day after filing falls outside the lien, so file the day you notice
the miss.

The hard stop is the notice of completion. An owner, GC, lender, surety or title company can file
one with the registry at final completion (§ 38-1a-507), and after that a preliminary notice "has
no effect if it is filed more than 10 days after the filing of a notice of completion"
(§ 38-1a-501(1)(d)).

## Why the GC's filing matters to every sub

Every construction lien on a Utah project "relates back to, and takes effect as of, the time of the
first preliminary notice filing" (§ 38-1a-503(1)), which is usually the GC's. That filing also helps
the subs below it: a sub whose notice links to the original contractor's notice by its entry number
has substantially complied (§ 38-1a-501(2)(b)). Ask the GC for the number on day one.

One filing covers all your work under one original contract, even for more than one sub above you
(§ 38-1a-501(1)(b)). A second original contract on the same project needs its own.

## Government jobs

On a government project the notice is for subcontractors only. A person with a contract with the
owner and a laborer paid wages are exempt (§ 38-1b-202(1)(a)). The date is the later of 20 days after
you start and, if you started before the project's notice of commencement was filed, 20 days after
that filing. If the notice of commencement came more than 15 days after you began, you need not file
at all to keep your rights (§ 38-1b-202(8)). A late notice takes effect five days after filing
(§ 38-1b-202(4)). The claim itself is on the payment bond; the
[state bond claims page](https://marisstella.app/pay-app-guides/state-payment-bond-claims.html) has
Utah's steps.

## After the notice

The lien is recorded with the county recorder within 180 days after final completion of the
original contract, or 90 days after a notice of completion, "but not later than 180 days after the
date on which final completion of the original contract occurs" (§ 38-1a-502(1)(a)). Copy it to the
owner by certified mail within 30 days, and sue to enforce within 180 days of recording
(§ 38-1a-701(2)). One more detail: Utah's general time rule carries a deadline that ends on a
Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday to the next working day (§ 68-3-7). The page names the weekday,
doesn't check holidays, and tells you to plan on the unmoved date.
