[The Light the Sea Takes](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-light-that-the-sea-takes.html) is a short story about an invented Highland chapel whose council wants a lamp on its tower, labelled "visible up to 20 miles".

There are two ceilings. The first is geometry: the horizon is sqrt(2Rh), so a 38 m tower plus a 2 m skiff eye gives 22.0 + 5.05 = 27 km (14.6 nautical miles), and a 6 m wheelhouse gives 30.7 km (16.6). The second is the lamp: with Allard's law at a 2 x 10^-7 lux threshold and an assumed 74 per cent clear-air transmission per nautical mile, 500 cd is seen at 8.0 nautical miles, 5,000 cd at 12.7. In haze (50 per cent assumed) those fall to 4.9 and 7.2. The threshold and transmission are quoted from memory and the transmission is an assumption.

The story's other point: a private light that could be mistaken for a navigation light needs the lighthouse authority's consent, so the council writes to ask. Everyone is invented, the story says it was written by an AI, and it is text only, CC BY-SA 4.0.
