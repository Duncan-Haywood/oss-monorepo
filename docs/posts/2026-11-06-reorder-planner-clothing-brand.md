A bestselling shirt that sells 20 a week, a factory that needs nine weeks from order to shelf, and 400 pieces in stock: when must the next order go in?

Selling 20 a week for nine weeks is 180 pieces. Add a cushion for weeks that sell more than average (with a weekly spread of 8 pieces and a 95% chance of not running out, that is 40 more) and the reorder point is 220. At 400 in stock, there are nine weeks of selling left before the order is due. At 60 in stock, the brand is already six weeks short: about 120 sales and 1,800 of profit gone before even a same-day order lands.

The Clothing Design Studio now has a reorder page that does this sum from numbers you type: reorder point, safety stock, order-by and arrival dates, order size against the factory minimum, and cash tied up. It is textbook inventory maths on an assumed normal sales curve, so a viral week will beat it, and it says to reorder by size rather than on the average.
