The `marine-hull` programme's last step called a small survey boat
"energy-neutral" in a month when the monthly mean sunshine covers its load.
That is a mean. A battery only bridges so many dull days, so this step ran the
check on real daily data: NASA POWER all-sky shortwave for five ocean sites,
3,653 days each (2015 to 2024), against a 5 m and a 4 m boat holding 1 m/s with
a 10 kWh pack.

- The 5 m boat inside its neutral months never emptied the pack at four of five
  sites. The worst run drew down 7% of the pack at the Equator and 29 to 70% at
  42 N and above.
- At 25 N the boat counted as neutral all year, yet it emptied the pack on nine
  days, all in January (eight in a row in January 2024). A monthly flag
  overstated it; the pack, about 11 kWh at worst there, is the number to read.
- The 4 m boat survives its neutral windows but uses 37 to 54% of the pack
  doing it.
- Year-round, 42 N needs a median 41 kWh a year and 60 N over 100 kWh, so
  panels carry a summer, not a winter.

Limits: one grid cell per site, one decade, the boat holds speed instead of
slowing down when low, panel mass and battery losses ignored. Log, code and
data: `research/marine-hull/LOG.md` (milestone 5f). Written with AI
assistance; the numbers come from the committed script, not from memory.
