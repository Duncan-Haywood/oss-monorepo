"Catholics have big families" was true once. In the General Social Survey (GSS), non-Hispanic white Catholics born 1925-44 had about 0.21 more children than white Protestants of the same age. For those born 1945-64 the gap is 0.04, which is statistically zero. For those born 1965-80 it has reversed: Catholics have 0.28 fewer children (95% interval -0.48 to -0.09).

That is the first result of a new open research programme, [`research/catholic-demography`](https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/research/catholic-demography/). It compares Catholic subpopulations on the GSS 1972-2024 file, weighted, with intervals that take the survey's cluster design into account. Other findings from the first pass:

- **Practice predicts family size, the label doesn't.** Catholics who attend Mass weekly have about 0.5 to 0.6 more children than other Catholics, among both white and Hispanic Catholics. This is a correlation measured after the fact, not a cause.
- **White Catholics are leaving faster than Hispanic Catholics.** Of non-Hispanic whites raised Catholic, 66% of those born 1945-64 still identify as Catholic. For those born 1965-80 it is 63%, and for those born 1981-96 it is 55%. Among Hispanics the figures are 74%, 76% and 71%.
- **Hispanic Catholics don't attend Mass more.** Weekly attendance was the same as white Catholics' in 2010-18 and 6 points lower in 2021-24. Hispanic Catholics are more likely to read the Bible literally and about 25 to 30 points less likely to be Republican.
- **The Church is changing shape.** Hispanics made up 31% of US Catholics in 2000-09 and 37% in 2021-24. About one Catholic in four was born abroad. Converts have been a steady 7 to 10% of Catholics in every decade.

Limits: the GSS asks about Hispanic origin only from 2000, and it was English-only until 2006. Since 2021 most interviews have been done online, so part of the drop in weekly attendance (24% of Catholics in 2010-18, 18% in 2021-24) may come from that change in method. The GSS also puts the Catholic share of adults at 21.6% in 2024, higher than Pew's 19%. The next steps check these points, then build a full accounting of how much retention, conversion, births and immigration each contributed to the change. The proposal, hypotheses (including the one that failed) and code are in the repository. Written with AI assistance.
