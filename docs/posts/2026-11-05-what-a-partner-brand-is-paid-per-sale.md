The designer who asked for the clothing studio wants other brands on the same site for a commission. The money in that arrangement is easy to get wrong in a spreadsheet: is the commission on the price shoppers see or on the price without VAT, who pays the card fee, and what happens to the commission when a sale is refunded?

The studio now has a [partner-brand page](../../ventures/clothing-design-studio/partners.html) that answers those. At a 61 EUR shelf price with 22% VAT and 20% commission, the net is 50, the commission 10 and the brand is paid 40 (the shop carries the 2.13 card fee and keeps 7.87). If the brand carries the fee instead it is paid 37.87 and the shop keeps the full 10. You can also type the payout a brand wants and get the shelf price that delivers it. Paste a period's sales and it makes a statement per brand, with refunds taking their commission back, and a CSV to send.

What is unproven: the conventions. They are common ones, written into the file's header, but who is the seller of record and so owes VAT depends on the contract and the country, and no real brand has run a payout through it. The tests check hand-worked cases, not an accountant's books.
