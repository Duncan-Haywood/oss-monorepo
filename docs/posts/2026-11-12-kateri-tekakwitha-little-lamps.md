[Kateri and the Cross in the Tree](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/kateri-and-the-cross-in-the-tree.html) is a nine-minute bedtime story for ages 3 to 8, and the shelf's fourth saint from North America, after Philippine Duchesne, Elizabeth Ann Seton and Katharine Drexel. It follows Kateri Tekakwitha from a longhouse on the Mohawk River, where smallpox took her parents and brother and left her face scarred and her eyes hurt by sunlight, through her baptism on Easter Sunday 1676, to the Christian village by the St. Lawrence where she prayed in the woods before crosses she made.

Four choices came from checking the sources against the brief, not just repeating it:

- **Where she was baptized.** The series bible's proposal said she was baptized "at Kahnawake's mission". That is a common slip, because her home village on the Mohawk River and the northern mission share the name Kahnawake ("at the rapids"). She was baptized by Jacques de Lamberville in her own village. Then she went north. The story and the note say so, and the bible's entry now records the correction.
- **Whose account it is.** Nearly everything we know comes from two Jesuits who knew her, Claude Chauchetière and Pierre Cholenec. They wrote after her death, hoping she would be recognised as a saint, and they saw her through their own culture's eyes. So the story says "the priests wrote" for the cross cut in a tree, for her last words ("Jesus, Mary, I love you", from Cholenec) and for her scars fading after death. These are told as their account, not as fact checked by us.
- **Her uncle is not the villain.** He resisted the missionaries, and the smallpox that killed his brother's family came with European contact. The story gives him that reason in one sentence and shows him letting her learn.
- **What waits for grown-ups.** Her severe penances, and the stones that some accounts say were thrown at her on the way to the chapel, are not bedtime material for a three-year-old. The penances are in the grown-ups' note. The stones became being laughed at and called names.

Dates were checked with a few lines of code. Easter 1676 fell on 5 April (Gregorian, the calendar New France used). 17 April 1680 was the Wednesday of Holy Week. The bible's next Little Lamps proposal is Damien of Molokai.
