A small machine shop that takes Department of Defense work and only ever sees Federal Contract Information (purchase orders, delivery schedules, part numbers) needs CMMC Level 1. That level is short: the 15 safeguarding requirements of FAR 52.204-21(b)(1). Level 1 has no score, so a shop can't post 104 out of 110 the way it can at Level 2. Under 32 CFR 170.15 every requirement has to be met. Under 170.21 there is no plan of action and milestones (POA&M) to put off a gap. The shop repeats the check every year and a senior official affirms it in SPRS.

The new [CMMC Level 1 self-assessment checklist](https://marisstella.app/machine-shop-guides/cmmc-level-1-self-assessment.html) does that check in a browser tab. Each of the 15 requirements gets met or not met and a line on how the shop meets it. The scope, CAGE codes and assessment date go at the top, and the page downloads a worksheet that carries the date of the next annual check. One requirement marked not met turns the result red. It stays red until that requirement is fixed, because the rule allows nothing else.

**Checking the clause text.** The session that built the page couldn't reach acquisition.gov or ecfr.gov through its network proxy. GSA publishes the FAR's source files on GitHub, and that host was reachable, so the 15 texts on the page come from GSA's `52.204-21.dita` file. A script compared all 15 to it and found no difference. A test pins a hash of the texts, so a later edit fails until someone checks it against the clause again.

The check paid off at once. Item (xv) as written from memory said "periodic and real-time scans of the information system". The clause says "periodic scans of the information system and real-time scans of files from external sources". Those are two different things. The system gets periodic scans, and real-time scanning applies to files from outside as they are downloaded, opened or run. That wording is what an assessor will hold the shop to.

**Fifteen requirements, 59 objectives.** A second session the same day could reach ecfr.gov and acquisition.gov, so it read the rule itself. [32 CFR 170.15(c)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-A/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-170/subpart-D/section-170.15) says a Level 1 self-assessment "must be performed using the objectives defined in NIST SP 800-171A" for the NIST requirement each one maps to, reading FCI wherever an objective says CUI. Its Table 2 gives the mapping. Requirement (ix), visitors, access logs and keys or badges, maps to three NIST requirements, one per phrase. The 15 requirements carry 17 NIST numbers and **59 "determine if" objectives**, and those objectives are what the self-assessment is judged on. Take (xii), "correct flaws in a timely manner". It becomes six objectives: the shop sets how quickly flaws are found, reported and fixed, then meets each of those deadlines. A shop with automatic updates but no stated timeframes fails three of the six.

The page now lists every objective under its requirement, and the shop marks each one met, not met or N/A. A "set all" control still marks a whole requirement in one go. The same reading settled N/A. [32 CFR 170.24(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-A/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-170/subpart-D/section-170.24) says an objective that does not apply "is equivalent to the same assessment objective being assessed as MET". The rule's own example is separating public-facing systems when nothing in scope is public. So the page now has an N/A choice, and the reason goes in the evidence note. Every row of Table 2 matched the mapping the first session had transcribed, and the 15 texts on acquisition.gov matched the GSA copy word for word. Two independent copies that agree are a better check than either one alone. The downloadable worksheet now has one row per objective.

**The dates the affirmation rule sets, and a record that keeps them.** The affirmation is the step shops most often lose track of, because nothing reminds them. [32 CFR 170.22(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-A/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-170/subpart-D/section-170.22) says the affirming official "must affirm the continuing compliance" of the company "after every assessment, including POA&M closeout, and annually thereafter", and that affirmations "are entered electronically in SPRS". Each one carries the official's "name, title, and contact information". The self-assessments behind them run on their own clocks. A Level 1 self-assessment is repeated "on an annual basis" (§ 170.15(a)(1)). A Level 2 self-assessment is repeated "every three years" (§ 170.16(a)(1)). A Conditional Level 2 status, the one a shop gets with a POA&M, has to be closed out with results in SPRS "within 180 days" of its status date, or it expires. Take a Level 2 shop with a Conditional status dated March 2, 2026 that affirms on March 5. Its closeout is due by August 29, 2026, its next affirmation by March 5, 2027 at the latest, and its next full self-assessment by March 2, 2029. The 2029 date holds even after the closeout. The rule counts the three years from the Conditional status date, and a closeout doesn't restart that count. The rule texts were read on eCFR on 2026-11-25. The workspace in development now keeps a record of each affirmation: who affirmed, on what date, for which level and status, and the score entered in SPRS next to the score the workspace computed that day. If the two differ, the record shows it. From those records it works out the dates above. A dashboard card turns amber 60 days before a date and red once it has passed. The dates are reminders worked out from what the shop recorded. They are not a ruling on its status, and the workspace still submits nothing to SPRS. It sends no email yet, so the reminder lives on the dashboard, and in the shop's own calendar: the Affirmations tab downloads the dates as an `.ics` file that Outlook, Google Calendar and Apple Calendar import. Each date becomes an all-day event with alerts 60 days and 7 days ahead, and each event quotes the section of 32 CFR 170 it comes from. An event keeps the same identifier when the dates move, so importing a fresh file after the next affirmation moves the event in calendars that respect that, rather than adding a second one (some still add a copy). The file holds the company name and the dates, not the affirming official's name or contact. A shop on the free Level 1 plan can read every Level 2 requirement and objective in the workspace. The page says plainly that recording them needs the Level 2 plan, so nobody fills in a form that then refuses to save.

**One training record for two auditors: ISO 9001 next to CMMC.** Many of the machine shops that need CMMC also carry ISO 9001, or are told by a prime to get it. The two ask for some of the same paper. A training matrix with dated records is what an ISO auditor checks under clause 7.2 (competence), and it is also evidence for NIST SP 800-171 requirement 3.2.2, "Ensure that personnel are trained to carry out their assigned information security-related duties and responsibilities." An internal audit programme serves ISO 9.2 and is evidence for 3.12.1, "Periodically assess the security controls in organizational systems". Corrective action records serve ISO 10.2, and the plan of action they feed is 3.12.2. Customer drawings held under ISO 8.5.3 (property belonging to customers) are often CUI too, which brings in 3.8.1 (protect media containing CUI). The workspace now has an ISO 9001 tab that lists the 47 requirement clauses of ISO 9001:2015, 4.1 to 10.3. A shop marks each clause as a gap, partly in place, in place or not applicable. A file already attached to a related CMMC requirement shows up under the clause without a second upload. Any other file can be linked to a clause. Two rules come from how certification audits go. A clause marked not applicable needs a reason, because the scope statement has to give one; build-to-print shops usually exclude 8.3, design. A clause marked in place with no file attached gets flagged, because the auditor's first question is "show me". The tab also downloads a gap report.
