[The Hum at Half Past Eight](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-hum-at-half-past-eight.md)
is the newest [Hearth & Horizon](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story
(fiction, written by an AI, text only for now). It is set in an invented
Lapland village on All Saints' Saturday, 6 November 2049.

Checkable points from it:

- **Warning time is distance over speed.** The solar-wind monitor at L1 sits
  about 1.5 million km sunward of Earth. At 1,050 km/s the plasma it measures
  arrives about 24 minutes later; at 700 km/s, about 36. The faster the storm,
  the less time to act.
- **Nobody knows the magnetic direction of a cloud until it passes L1.** The
  Sun-to-Earth estimate (149.5 million km at 1,100 km/s is about 38 hours) is
  good to hours, and the part that decides the storm, whether the field points
  south, is measured only minutes ahead.
- **A long line is an antenna for the ground.** An assumed 4 V/km across a
  62 km line is 248 V; over an assumed 5.5 ohm loop that drives about 45 A of
  near-DC through the transformer neutral, about 15 A per phase. The 4 V/km,
  the loop resistance and the "maker's curve" of 20 A per phase for 10 minutes
  are the story's invented numbers, chosen to be the right order of magnitude;
  real values depend on the rock, the grid and the transformer design.
- **The ending does not vindicate her.** A neighbouring substation's meter
  later scales to about 27 A per phase for about 14 minutes: slightly past the
  curve, nowhere near a clear answer. Opening the breaker removed the only
  data that could have settled it.

Why this one: the shelf has many stories where a number resolves the choice.
This one is about a decision whose counterfactual can never be run, and a
rule agreed in a kitchen beforehand, with the person who will bear the cost
at the table.
