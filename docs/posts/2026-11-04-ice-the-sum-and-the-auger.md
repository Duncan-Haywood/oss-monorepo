# Ice: what the sum says and what the auger says

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository.*

The new Hearth & Horizon story, *Six Centimetres in the Middle*, is set on a fen village pond on Boxing Day 2049. The parish assistant is asked whether the ice will bear the village skate.

Its sum is Stefan's law: ice thickness is about 3.5 cm times the square root of the freezing-degree-days. Five days at minus 2, 5, 6, 4 and 3 degrees make 20, so a ceiling of 15.7 cm, and about 12 cm with the 2.7 coefficient real lakes tend to follow (that coefficient and the roughly 10 cm guide for one person on foot are quoted from memory in the story, with a pointer to the fire and rescue service).

The churchwarden then drills: 9 cm at the bank, 6 mid-pond, 3 over the spring inlet. The assistant says to trust the holes, because a spring warms the ice from below and the thinnest place is the one that matters. The skate becomes a walk round the grass.
