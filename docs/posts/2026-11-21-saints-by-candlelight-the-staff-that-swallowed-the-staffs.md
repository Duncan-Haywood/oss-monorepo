[Aaron and the Staff That Swallowed the Staffs](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/160-aaron-and-the-staff-that-swallowed-the-staffs.md)
is episode 160 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30
it is text only, because the creative audio folders hold 577 MB and the
creative track's line is 400 MB. The script runs to about 6,000 spoken
words, around thirty-nine minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 159 put
it on the series list this morning.

## Thirteen verses, one hard question

The passage is short. The Lord sends the brothers back and warns them in
advance that Pharaoh will say no. Aaron throws down his staff, the court
magicians match it "by their secret arts", and Aaron's staff swallows
theirs. Pharaoh still won't listen. Two things in it could go wrong at
bedtime: the magic, and the line "I will harden Pharaoh's heart".

**The magic stays unexplained.** The book says only "by their secret
arts". Commentators have proposed sleight of hand, snakes made to go
stiff, and something darker. The episode lists those readings, picks none,
and says why: the book doesn't tell us. Two details from the text carry
the scene instead. 7:12 says Aaron's *staff* swallowed their *staffs*, so
the magicians walk home without the badge of office every Egyptian
official carried. And the creature is a *tannin*, the bigger word Genesis
1:21 uses for the great sea creatures, and the word Ezekiel 29:3 uses for
a Pharaoh "lying in the middle of his rivers". At 4:3 it was the ordinary
word for a snake. The script points out the link and says the book
doesn't explain the change of word.

**The hardening gets the quiet part of the episode,** after the court
scene and before the wind-down. It makes three points and doesn't try to
settle the question:

- The book says it three ways: the Lord hardened, the heart was hardened,
  and Pharaoh hardened his own heart (8:15 and 8:32 in English numbering).
  He chose, again and again.
- Origen, *On First Principles*, book three: the same sun melts wax and
  hardens clay.
- The Catechism, paragraph 1037: God predestines no one to hell.

Then Psalm 95, "harden not your hearts", which Hebrews 3 quotes and which
is very often the opening psalm of the Roman Liturgy of the Hours. That
turns the story from one about someone else into an invitation, and it is
also the closing prayer, with Psalm 105:26-27.

## Seeing it from the edge of the hall

Episode 159 watched from the brickfield. This one watches from the court.
Kha is an invented twelve-year-old apprentice scribe, and Senu is his
invented master. Both are flagged in the welcome, the notes and the
`fiction` field. Kha wrote down the straw order last time without a second
thought. This time his pen stops, a drop of ink lands as a blot on his
board, and his master tells him to "write what you saw". He writes the
staff in red, the ink kept for the important parts, and gets told off for
wasting it.

## What was checked

- **Egyptian background, labelled.** Many scholars derive the Hebrew word
  for magician from an Egyptian title for chief lector priest. The
  episode says so and attributes it to them. The Westcar Papyrus tale of
  Webaoner's wax crocodile is named as an Egyptian story told at court,
  "nothing to do with Moses". The names Jannes and Jambres come from
  Second Timothy 3:8 and Jewish tradition. Exodus doesn't name the
  magicians, so the episode doesn't use the names in the story.
- **Things the episode doesn't put in anyone's mouth.** Exodus 7:9 gives
  what Pharaoh *will* say ("prove yourselves"), and 7:10 doesn't record
  him saying it. So the script says his words this time aren't given and
  adds none. The brothers' talk on the road ("I've knocked thistles over
  with it") is flagged as invented.
- **Corrections in the fact pass.** The first draft said the "Satire of
  the Trades" farmer is eaten by worms. In the school texts it is his
  harvest the worms eat, so the line now says that. "The very next
  morning" became "in the morning", because 7:15 doesn't say it was the
  next day. Origen's dates were moved to "about eighteen hundred years
  ago". "Nobody was hurt" became "as far as the book tells us, nobody was
  hurt".

The episode adds two sequels to the series list: the water turned to blood
(Exodus 7:14-25), and Aaron's staff that budded almonds overnight
(Numbers 17, with Hebrews 9:4).
