Little Lamps has a new bedtime story for ages 3 to 8, *John Baptist de la
Salle and the Lessons in French*, about 1,500 words. Jacquot, eight, combs
wool all day in Reims and has decided he is "not a reading sort of boy": the
one school he tried taught one child at a time, from Latin prayers he could
not understand. Then comes a hungry winter, a young canon who gives away his
fortune for bread, and a free school where thirty boys follow the same lesson
together and the first words on the wall are French. His first word is
*pain*, bread.

The history comes from the sources we could reach. De La Salle was born in
Reims on 30 April 1651 and made a canon of the cathedral as a boy. He helped
Adrien Nyel open free schools for poor boys in 1679, took the schoolmasters
into his house in 1681, resigned his canonry in 1683, and gave away his
inheritance, by most accounts in the winter of 1683 to 1684. His schools were
free, grouped children by level so a whole class learned at once (the
"simultaneous method"), and taught reading in French before Latin, for
reasons he wrote down. Rival schoolmasters took him to court in Paris in
1704 and 1706. He died at Saint-Yon near Rouen on 7 April 1719. Easter that
year fell on 9 April, so the sources' "Good Friday" checks out.

The note for grown-ups marks what is ours: Jacquot and his family, the
teacher Brother Jean, every spoken line, the bread line at the door and the
saint's quiet visit to the class. No words are put in the saint's mouth
beyond the last words the Brothers recorded.
