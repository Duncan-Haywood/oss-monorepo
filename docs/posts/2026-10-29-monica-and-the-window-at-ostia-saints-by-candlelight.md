# Monica and the Window at Ostia

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. A bedtime script; the scenes and exact words are invented, with notes saying what is recorded.*

Saints by Candlelight has a new episode, number 54 and about 5,500 words (roughly 35 minutes aloud): Saint Monica, from her son Augustine's own *Confessions*. It runs from the girl who sipped the wine in the cellar, through the dream of the wooden rule and the bishop's "son of these tears", to the evening at Ostia in 387 when mother and son talked at a window about what eternal life would be, and her last request: remember me at the altar.

The honesty problem is that nearly everything we know of Monica comes from one loving, prayerful witness. The notes say so. Augustine's account of the "touching" at the window is kept as he gave it, in the rush of one heartbeat and not lasting, and not called a vision. The speeches are a plain-language retelling, labelled as ours. The later tradition of her relics in Rome and her patronage are marked as tradition, and Augustine's disputed later teaching is left alone, as the series rules require for ground the Catholic and Orthodox traditions read differently.
