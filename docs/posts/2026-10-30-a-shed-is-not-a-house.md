---
title: "A shed is not a house: framing the windows to the load"
date: 2026-10-30
tags: [shed-designer, framing, cost]
description: "Every window got kings, jacks and a 2x6 header, house-style. Sizing headers to the load and framing small windows in a stud bay saves up to $48 a preset."
---

The owner of the [shed designer](https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/shed-designer/) looked at its framing and said it was built like a house. Every opening got the full package:

- a king stud each side;
- a jack each side;
- a header of two 2x6s;
- a rough sill with cripples under it.

That is house practice, and the 2x6 came from a rule of thumb: any opening up to 4 ft wide got one, whatever it carried. So we let the structural checks decide instead.

**Headers sized to the load.** The tool already checked every header for bending, shear, deflection and bearing under its wall's share of roof and snow. Now it also picks the header: the shallowest two-ply size that passes. At 30 psf of snow, every opening on every preset needs only two 2x4s, the smallest header in the IRC's own table. Heavier snow or wider openings still get deeper ones: 50 psf over a 6 ft opening in an eave wall takes two 2x6s, and 70 psf over 10 ft takes two 2x10s.

A shallower header also lets the tool's automatic walls come down 2", from 86-1/2" to 84-1/2" on the starter.

**Windows inside a stud bay.** The starter's windows are 14-1/4" wide, and the space between two studs at 16" centres is 14-1/2". A window that fits in that space cuts no stud, so there is nothing for a header to carry. The double top plate spans that bay exactly as it spans every other one. Such a window now gets a flat block above and below it, nailed between the two studs, and nothing else. The tool checks the plates over it with a rafter landing in the middle of the bay: 22% of capacity on the starter.

**Sliding a door or window.** The starter's windows sit about an inch off the stud layout, so they don't qualify as they stand. Moving a window is the owner's call, not the tool's, so the cheaper-build and budget searches now offer it as a priced move. They also offer to slide a door or window so its king stud lands on a layout line and replaces a stud, rather than standing next to one.

The savings are modest, because framing lumber is about a quarter of a shed's materials. The rest is siding, roofing, paint, fasteners and trim. On the starter, the two windows moved into their bays save $8.30 of lumber each. On the 12x16, one stud fewer also brings the 16d nails down a box, which saves $25.

**What it did not do.** The checks are still the bar. Nothing here removes a member that a check needs, and every slid design passes every load and connection check. A door is never framed in a bay, and a window for which the user picked a header keeps it. As always, these are screening checks, not an engineer's review, and the plans are not engineered or code-checked.
