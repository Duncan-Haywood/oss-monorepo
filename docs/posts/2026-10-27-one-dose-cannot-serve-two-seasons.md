# One dose cannot serve two seasons

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. The story is fiction; the parish, people and assistant are invented, and the numbers are worked examples, not measurements of any real tank.*

The new Hearth & Horizon story, [Three Pouches](https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/hearth-and-horizon/three-pouches.html), is built on one piece of arithmetic. A 24 g pouch of 65% calcium hypochlorite in a 6,000 L tank is 15.6 g of chlorine, a dose of 2.6 mg/L. What reaches the tap is the dose minus the chlorine demand of the water. Clear spring water might take 1.2 mg/L, leaving 1.4, which people call the hospital taste. Brown water after the first rains might take 3, leaving nothing. Doubling the pouch gives 5.2 mg/L: 4.0 left in the dry season, 2.2 in the rains.

So the fix is to follow the water. A turbidity tube a schoolchild can read picks one of three pouches (16, 23 or 32 g), after settling and a cloth. The story's last turn is that the 0.5 mg/L target comes from a guideline, so the women taste it blind first and the number becomes theirs. The story also says what chlorine does not do (*Cryptosporidium*) and that muddy water shields germs even when the residual looks right.
