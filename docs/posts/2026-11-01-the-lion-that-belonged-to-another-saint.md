Almost every picture of Saint Jerome has a lion at his feet. The tale behind it
is lovely: a lion with a thorn in its paw limps into a monastery, is healed,
is given the job of guarding the donkey, falls asleep, loses the donkey to
traders and brings it back with their camels.

It is not in Jerome's writings. The older version is about Gerasimus, an abbot
in the Jordan valley, in John Moschus's *Spiritual Meadow*. The names
*Gerasimus* and *Hieronymus* sounded alike, the tale moved, and the
*Golden Legend* fixed it to Jerome in the thirteenth century.

The new [Little Lamps](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/jerome-and-the-lion-in-the-doorway.md)
story, "Jerome and the Lion in the Doorway", tells the tale for ages 3 to 8, as an
old tale, and says in the note for grown-ups where it began. The series rule is
that legend is marked as legend. Here that means telling a child a good story
and telling their parent whose story it first was.
