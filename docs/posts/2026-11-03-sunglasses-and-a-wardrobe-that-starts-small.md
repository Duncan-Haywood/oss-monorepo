A second conversation with the designer behind the [clothing studio](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/) added two things to build.

**Sunglasses with the brand's logo.** The designer does not want to invent a new frame. The plan is to take a frame a factory already makes, choose its measurements, and put the logo on the frame and the case so it reads as luxury. They estimated about €20 to make a pair and €60 to €70 to sell one. The [sunglasses designer](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/sunglasses.html) does exactly that. You pick a shape, set the size the way it is printed inside every temple (52□18-145: lens, bridge, temple in millimetres), and choose frame and lens colours and where the logo goes and how it is applied (laser engraving, a metal plate, foil on the case). It draws the frame front and the temple to scale and flags sizes outside the usual range for the shape, because those may need a new mould rather than a stock frame.

The designer's own numbers hold up. At €20 landed and €65 including 22% VAT, card fees leave €31.03 a pair, a 58.2% margin. A 60% margin needs €68.15. With €500 of launch spend, 100 pairs break even at 49 sold.

The quote request asks the factory for the paperwork too. In the EU and UK, sunglasses count as personal protective equipment (category I), so the request asks for the EN ISO 12312-1 test report and a declaration of conformity. For the US, it asks for the impact (drop-ball) test the FDA requires. These are prompts for the right questions, not a compliance check.

**A whole wardrobe, started small.** The owner's idea was a "full wardrobe" service: someone with money and no time for fashion gets a complete set of outfits. The designer's condition was to start with few pieces and a limited quantity, then add more once it sells. The [wardrobe planner](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/wardrobe.html) takes the audience they named: an age band, fit, style, climate and season, what the person dresses for, and a quality tier that changes the fabric, not the shape of the wardrobe. It returns a capsule where every piece comes in a colour it is really made in. The outfit count follows a rule you can check by hand: any top with any bottom, with or without a layer, and at most one accent colour. The default 12 pieces give 39 outfits.

Each piece is marked as the brand's own (with a link that opens it in the studio) or carried from a partner brand on commission, the "both options" the designer preferred. The first drop then fills a production budget with minimum orders of the pieces that appear in the most outfits, and holds the rest for later.

Trying clothes on (AR, VR or a body model) is the next big piece on the [roadmap](https://github.com/duncan-haywood/oss-monorepo/blob/main/ventures/clothing-design-studio/ROADMAP.md). Every price these tools ship with is a labelled placeholder; real quotes go in their place.
