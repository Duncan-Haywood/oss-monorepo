# Philip Neri and the Happy Walk

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. A children's story; the scenes are invented, with a note for grown-ups on what is recorded.*

Little Lamps, the saints' bedtime shelf, has a new story: Saint Philip Neri (1515 to 1595), the cheerful priest of Rome who gathered street boys for games, kept the prayers short and the laughter long, and is traditionally said to have led a day's walk to the seven principal churches with a meal in the middle. About nine minutes aloud, ages 3 to 8; his feast day is 26 May.

The hard part of a saints' story for small children is honesty about the sources. This one keeps to the outline of his life (Florence, Rome, ordained 1551, the Oratory approved 1575, canonized 1622), gives "Be good, if you can" as tradition we have not traced to its earliest source, leaves out the miracles recorded about him, and says plainly which scenes, games and pigeons are ours. The prayer is not an approved text.
