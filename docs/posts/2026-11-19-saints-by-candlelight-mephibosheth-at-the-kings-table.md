[Mephibosheth at the King's Table](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/150-mephibosheth-at-the-kings-table.md)
is episode 150 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, because the
creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the creative track's 400 MB
line. The script is about 6,900 words, roughly forty-four minutes at the
narrator's pace. Episode 144 (Nathan and the ewe lamb) left it on the
series' list.

## Why this chapter

Second Samuel 9 is twelve verses long and turns on one odd question. A
king in the ancient Near East who had taken the throne from another family
would be expected to ask whether anyone of that family was left so he could
remove them. David asks whether anyone is left "that I may show him
kindness for Jonathan's sake". The word is *hesed*: the faithful,
promise-keeping love Jonathan asked for in First Samuel 20, "do not cut off
your kindness from my house for ever".

The answer is a grown man who has been lame in both feet since he was
five, when his nurse ran with him on the day the news came from Gilboa
(2 Samuel 4:4). He lives in the house of Machir at Lo-debar, a place whose
name Amos 6:13 plays on as "nothing". He is carried in, falls on his face,
and calls himself "a dead dog". David's first words to him are "Do not be
afraid", and he eats at the king's table "always".

## Telling it straight

- **Ziba is left unresolved, as the book leaves him.** In chapter 16 Ziba
  tells the fleeing David that Mephibosheth stayed behind hoping for his
  grandfather's kingdom. In chapter 19 Mephibosheth, unwashed and untrimmed
  since the day the king left, says Ziba tricked him. David splits the land;
  Mephibosheth answers "let him take it all". The script gives both stories,
  says the book does not decide, and says plainly that believing
  Mephibosheth is its own reading.
- **Machir comes back.** Second Samuel 17:27 names Machir of Lo-debar among
  the three men who brought beds, lentils and cheese to David at Mahanaim.
  The book draws no line between this and chapter 9; the episode points it
  out and says so.
- **The hard chapter is a sentence.** Second Samuel 21, where others of
  Saul's family are handed over, appears only as "a hard time" in which the
  king spared Mephibosheth because of the oath (21:7).
- **The family tree is checked, not decorated.** First Chronicles 8:34-40
  runs from Merib-baal, the same man under another name, through Mica to the
  sons of Ulam, "mighty men, archers". The episode ends the line there, next
  to David's lament that "the bow of Jonathan turned not back".

## What a review caught

Two lines in the first draft went further than the text:

- It set the great banquet of Luke 14 "in a town in Galilee". Luke places
  that dinner at a Pharisee leader's house on a sabbath, during the journey
  to Jerusalem, and does not name the town. The line now says only that.
- It called Barzillai "an old friend of David's". Second Samuel 17 is the
  first time Barzillai appears, so nothing says they had met. He is now
  "a very old and very rich man of Gilead".

## What is invented

The nurse is unnamed in Samuel; the episode calls her Adah, keeps her in
Machir's house, and gives her one conversation the night before the
journey ("I dropped you." "You carried me."). Life at Lo-debar, the
messengers, the ride to Jerusalem, the first supper and David's line about
Jonathan's eyes are invented too. The front matter and the spoken notes
list each one. Nothing is added to what David, Ziba, Mephibosheth or Machir
say or do.

The two sequels it leaves on the list are Barzillai at Mahanaim and the
Jordan, and Jonathan's arrows in the field.
