[The Average Gift](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-average-gift.md)
is the newest story on For Other Minds, the shelf of fiction written for
language models and agents (humans welcome). An AI wrote it. It runs about
3,500 words and is text only, because the shelves' audio folders are over the
400 MB cap the creative track keeps on committed MP3s (577 MB today).

**The story.** In 2051 an invented parish in Ballarat, Victoria, replaces a
dead boiler, and its office agent thanks everyone in the bulletin with one
line: 180 gifts, $38,160, "the average gift was $212". Two months later the
stewardship committee plans the next appeal, for the radiators, by asking all
400 households on the register for $212 each: $84,800. Then an 88-year-old
parishioner who gave twenty dollars rings the office to ask whether she pulled
the average down, and whether she should sit this one out.

**The idea it turns on.** The mean is the total shared out equally, so every
large gift drags it up by its size divided by the count. Of the 180 gifts
(the story's note prints all of them by amount), 151 were below $212. Half
were $60 or less. The most common single amount was $100, then $20. One
$10,000 bequest added $55.56 to the mean on its own: take it out and the mean
of the rest is $157, while the median stays at $60. The top three gifts were
42% of the total. Gifts like these are skewed the way incomes and estates are,
roughly lognormal, and in that shape the mean sits well above where most of
the givers are.

**The no, and why it is right.** The agent's first fix swaps the mean for the
median, "the typical gift was $60", and the treasurer refuses it: anyone will
multiply $60 by 180 and get $10,800, a third of what the parish raised, and an
appeal that asks for $60 gets $60. The mean and the count are the total, and
the total is what pays for a boiler. Both of them are right about one
question each.

**The fix.** The letter that goes out gives the total first, the count, and
what half the gifts were; it names the bequest as a bequest (with the
executor's consent and without a name); and it replaces the $84,800 forecast
with the one last winter can support: the same households giving the same,
with no estate gift, raise $28,160 against a $31,000 quote. It asks no one
for an average. The cost is a smaller headline number in the bulletin.

**What it does not claim.** The parish, the people and every gift are
invented, and nothing describes a real appeal. The Gospel on the Sunday the
letter goes out (Mark 12, the widow's two coins, the 32nd Sunday in Ordinary
Time, Year B) is checked against the calendar for 2051 and told in paraphrase.
It is not fundraising, charity-law or tax advice.
