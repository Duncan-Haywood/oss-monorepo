[Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) is the portfolio's shelf
of hopeful, hard-ish science fiction. Its newest story,
[*The Step That Goes Up*](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-step-that-goes-up.html),
is set on an invented Atlantic island in 2049, the week its only cable
breaks four kilometres down. The story rests on one measurement puzzle.
This post sets it out, because it works outside the story too.

## Two distances that add up and still disagree

A repeatered submarine cable is watched from the shore by sending light
down the fibre and timing the faint glow the glass scatters back. That
gives a distance along the cable to a break. In the story the island
measures 318.7 km and the far station measures 330.7 km. They add to
649.4 km, which is exactly the cable's length as measured end to end in
2045. So both ends see one break.

A length of cable is not a place, though. It becomes a place only through
the route position list, the record of where each metre went down when the
cable was laid. That list says the cable is 640.0 km long. Somewhere there
are 9.4 km of cable that no surviving record explains.

- If the extra lies between the island and the break, the break is at
  318.7 − 9.4 = **309.3 km** on the list.
- If it lies beyond the break, the break is at **318.7 km**.
- Spread evenly, 318.7 × 640.0 / 649.4 = **314.1 km**, the middle.

The two extremes are about nine kilometres of seabed apart. The
repair superintendent in the story picks the middle, which caps her error
at about 4.8 km of cable. It is the right call on the evidence she has, and
the story treats it that way. A wrong cut is not a disaster, but it costs a
day of picking up cable from the bottom and kilometres of spare that the
next repair in the zone might need.

## Where 9.4 km of cable can hide

The likeliest home for unexplained length is an old repair. When a cable
is mended in deep water the last joint is made on deck, between two ends
lifted from the bottom, and the extra cable is lowered afterwards as a
loop on the seabed. Published descriptions put that loop at roughly two to
three times the water depth. In the story, a 2031 repair in 3,880 m of
water inserted 10.5 km (2.7 times the depth) and removed 1.1 km: a net
9.4 km. The cable changed hands after a bankruptcy, and the repair records
did not come with it.

## The fingerprint: a step that goes up

The story's 2031 repair joined the island's cable to spare cable from
another maker, through two "universal" joints. When two fibres with
different core sizes are spliced, a one-way reflectometer trace shows a
small apparent *gain* going into the smaller fibre, and an exaggerated loss
coming out. Technicians call it a gainer. Averaging the two directions
gives the joint's true loss.

So an inserted section of a different fibre has a signature: from either
end, a step up going in. The island's monitoring assistant had both steps
in its 2045 baseline (+0.11 dB at 199.8 km, −0.29 dB at 210.3 km, invented
figures). Neither matched anything on the list, both were below the action
threshold, and it filed the first as an instrument artefact, because glass
cannot amplify light. The far station's trace showed the matching step up
at 439.1 km from its end, which is 210.3 from the island. The two
directions together put each joint's true loss at about 0.09 dB.

The repair lay between the island and the break, so the far station's
figure was the one to use.

## What the story is about

The numbers are the plot's skeleton. The story itself is about the retired
jointer who made both 2031 joints, who was told a hard diagnosis in October
and no longer trusts her own memory, and the notebook she lent to a parish
exhibition in 2039 and forgot. It is also about a monitoring assistant
admitting that in 2045 it wrote down what it could explain and not what it
could not. Her answer when the superintendent asks whether she is sure is
the line the story was built around: "I am sure of what I wrote."

## How it was checked

As with every story on the shelf, the closing note sets out the model so a
reader can check it: the sums above, the spare-cable budget (24 km aboard,
11.8 used, 12.2 left against about 7 had the ship cut in the middle), every
weekday (25 January 2049 is a Monday, 2 February a Tuesday), and the
jointer's age against each dated event. The gainer figures, the grapnel
times and the island are invented, and the note says so. The engineering
is simplified from general published practice, and the story says it is
fiction, not cable-repair guidance.
