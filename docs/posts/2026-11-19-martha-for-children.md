It runs about 1,600 words, roughly ten minutes read aloud, for ages 3 to 8. Since 2021 Martha's feast, on 29 July, has been shared with her sister Mary and her brother Lazarus.

## Two Gospel portraits, one person

Most children who know Martha know one scene. In Luke 10:38-42 she is "distracted with much serving", asks Jesus to tell her sister to help, and hears "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; one thing is needful." Told on its own, that scene can turn her into the cautionary tale of the kitchen.

John's Gospel gives the other half. When Lazarus has been dead four days, it is Martha who runs out to meet Jesus while Mary stays in the house (John 11:20). Jesus says "I am the resurrection and the life" to her, and she answers with one of the fullest professions of faith in the Gospels: "Yes, Lord; I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God" (11:27). And in John 12:2, at the supper after Lazarus is raised, the evangelist adds three words: "Martha served."

The story puts the two portraits in order and lets each correct the other. Jesus is kind in the kitchen, not cross; he is glad of the bread, he just does not want her to miss him. The same quickness that made her cross is what sends her running down the road. And the last scene has her back at her pots, which we read as "a happy busy". A busy, helpful child should come away from the story liked, not told off.

## What the decree says, checked

The 29 July memorial changed in 2021. We read the Congregation for Divine Worship's decree (Prot. N. 35/21, dated 26 January 2021) on vatican.va before writing the note rather than relying on summaries. It says the day used to be Martha's alone because of "the traditional uncertainty of the Latin Church about the identity of Mary", who had long been merged with Mary Magdalene and the forgiven woman of Luke 7, and that this "has been resolved in recent studies and times". The note for grown-ups quotes its phrase about the "family spirit and friendship" Jesus found in Bethany and keeps Mary simply as Martha's sister.

## Kept small: a dragon and a smell

The medieval Provençal tradition that Martha sailed to France and tamed the Tarasque at Tarascon, told in the thirteenth-century *Golden Legend*, gets one paragraph called a legend. Martha's blunt objection at the tomb, "by this time there will be an odor", became "He has been in there four days!" for small listeners. The note says so.
