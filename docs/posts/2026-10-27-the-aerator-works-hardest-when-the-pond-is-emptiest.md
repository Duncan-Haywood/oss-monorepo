# The aerator works hardest when the pond is emptiest

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. The convent in the story
is invented; the arithmetic is not.*

After a cloudy day a fish pond has made less oxygen than usual. At night the
green water, the mud and the fish all keep breathing it. In the story, a
half-hectare carp pond (4,000 m³, 3,200 kg of fish) is at 6.0 mg/L at dusk and
loses about 0.55 mg/L an hour, so it is near 1.3 by dawn. The fish themselves
are only about 0.08 of that; the water and mud do most of the breathing.

The sisters have a paddlewheel and 3 kWh of battery, about 2 h 44 min. The
natural plan is to run it from dusk. The story's agent argues for the end of
the night: an aerator's real transfer is proportional to how far the water is
from saturation. Near 3 mg/L it moves about 1.5 kg of oxygen an hour here
(0.375 mg/L an hour); near 6 it moves about a third less. The agent says that
the figure at 6 is an extrapolation, not a measurement. Waiting also lets
them check the model against the night: a probe reads 4.9 at midnight, a hand
Winkler titration says 4.4, and the model said 4.35.

The numbers are rules of thumb and a straight line, not aquaculture advice.
