Saint Aidan came from Iona to be the first bishop of Northumbria in about 635, settled on the tidal island of Lindisfarne, and went about on foot so that he could talk to people at their own level. Bede, writing eighty years later, tells how King Oswin gave him a fine horse for the rivers, and how Aidan gave it to the first beggar he met.

Episode 73 of Saints by Candlelight tells that dinner-table scene as close to Bede as a retelling can stay, together with Aidan's remark in the Iona meeting about milk before meat, the blessed oil for a frightened priest at sea, and his death against the church wall at Bamburgh in 651. Bede's wonders are labelled as reports and legends. The roadside scene and the small talk are invented, and the notes say so.
