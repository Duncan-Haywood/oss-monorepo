[The Bitter Water at Marah and the Springs of Elim](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/172-the-bitter-water-at-marah-and-the-springs-of-elim.md)
is episode 172 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30
it is text only: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the creative
track's limit is 400 MB. The script runs about 5,500 spoken words, roughly
thirty-five minutes. Episode 170 put it on the list. It starts three days
after that episode's song on the far shore of the sea.

## Six verses, and why they suit bedtime

Exodus 15:22-27 has a clear shape: three days without water, a pool that
turns out to be bitter, a complaint, a cry to God, a piece of wood, sweet
water, and then Elim, "where there were twelve springs of water and seventy
palm trees; and they camped there by the water." The last verse is
effectively a lullaby already. The episode spends its final third under
the palms, so a listener who drifts off misses nothing.

A few details from the text carry the episode:

- **"Three days."** Moses had asked Pharaoh for "a three days' journey
  into the wilderness" (Exodus 3:18, 5:3). This is that journey, and it
  turns out to be dry.
- **"Showed."** The verb for the Lord showing Moses the wood comes from the
  root of *torah*, teaching. The script says this once and builds the
  closing reflection on it: the wood was lying there all along, and Moses
  saw it only after he cried out.
- **"I am the Lord who heals you."** This is God's first description of
  himself on the journey, and he gives it beside a pool he has just healed.

## Keeping the miracle God's

The series bible says miracles are God's and that relics and objects are
never magic. That matters here, because a reader may ask whether the wood
was simply a plant with a useful property. Sirach 38:4-5 answers that
question within Scripture: "The Lord created medicines from the earth ...
Was not water made sweet with a tree in order that his power might be
known?" The episode therefore leaves the mechanism open and is firm about
the agent. Moses didn't know which piece of wood to pick up until he was
shown.

## Readings, labelled as readings

The Christian reading of the wood as the Cross comes from Tertullian (*On
Baptism* 9) and Saint Ambrose (*On the Mysteries* 3). The reading of the
twelve springs and seventy palms as the apostles and the seventy disciples
comes from Origen, homily seven on Exodus. Each is introduced by name, and
the script says plainly that Exodus doesn't make these readings itself. It
notes that some old copies of Luke 10:1 read seventy-two. Where Marah and
Elim were is left as travellers' guesses.
