The newest [Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, [The Pot on the Cold Stove](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-pot-on-the-cold-stove.html), is set in a County Clare parish hall in 2049. A cook has left twenty litres of soup on a cold stove to cool before the fridge, as her mother did, for a Confirmation lunch.

The hall's assistant works a rough cooling curve. Twenty litres holds about 80 kJ per degree; a lidded pot loses perhaps 4 W per degree (a guess), so it takes about 5.6 hours to shed most of its heat. Off the heat at 90 °C at five, it reads 53 °C at nine (it does), passes 63 °C at about 7:40 pm and is still about 32 °C at six in the morning, all hours inside the 8 to 63 °C range where bacteria grow. Stirred in running 11 °C tap water, at a guessed 40 W per degree, the same pot falls from 53 to 21 °C in about 49 minutes, inside the two-hour rule on the hall's kitchen board.

The assistant will not call the soup safe or ruined. It says what is still possible, what the water costs, to reheat to 75 °C, and to put a plain sandwich beside the frail. Guidance differs by country and every figure is illustrative.
