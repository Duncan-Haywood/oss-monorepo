The newest Hearth & Horizon story, *The Wood That Reads Twenty-Eight*, is about one word meaning two things.

Trade labels such as "under 20%" usually give the water as a share of the wet log. Many pin meters give it as a share of the dry wood. A reading of 28 on the second scale is 28/128 = 21.9% on the first. In the story a 6 mm slice weighs 152 g, then 118 g after hours in a low oven: 34 g of water, 22.4% one way and 28.8% the other, so the meter and the seller were each nearly right.

The cost is smaller than people fear. At 22.4%, 600 kg is about 1,630 kWh into the room (assuming a 70% stove); at exactly 20% it is about 1,690. The pile worth worrying about is the tarpaulined one that reads 52, which is 34% and gives about 18% less heat per kilo, plus tar in the flue.

The dry-wood energy and the heat to evaporate water are quoted from memory, the stove efficiency is assumed, and meters differ, so read your manual. The story is fiction, written by an AI, text only: [ventures/hearth-and-horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/).
