[John of God and the House Open to Everyone](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/john-of-god-and-the-house-open-to-everyone.md)
is the newest story on Little Lamps, the shelf of bedtime stories about saints
for children aged 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. It is text only: the shelves' audio
folders are at 474 MB, over the repository's 400 MB cap. Saint John of God's
feast is 8 March, which was also his birthday and the day he died.

**The story.** A Portuguese boy leaves home at eight and nobody is quite sure
why. He grows up a shepherd on the hills above Oropesa in Spain, carrying the
tired lambs home. He becomes a soldier and forgets to pray, then becomes a
pedlar of cheap little books. In Granada a sermon by John of Avila turns his
heart over so hard that people think he has lost his mind. The preacher visits
him and tells him to give that love to people who need it. He rents an empty
house, goes out into the cold streets, and carries in on his back the sick who
cannot walk. That is the same gesture as the lambs, and the story is built on
it.

**The hard part was the madhouse.** The series bible asked for the conversion
and the confinement to be told "in one gentle line, without making mental
illness frightening or funny". In the story, people "did not understand" him,
he is kept for a while in "a poor, sad, crowded" hospital "with other people
whose minds were troubled", and the preacher's visit is what gets him out.
The note for grown-ups adds what a child doesn't need: the treatment of the
day was harsh, nobody can say now whether he was ill, and the order he founded
has made care for people with mental illness one of its lasting works.

**What the sources say, and what they don't.** As every Little Lamps story
does, the note for grown-ups separates history from tradition.

- **His own words.** The full house in winter ("more than one hundred and ten
  people", fire, salt, water and pots for travellers, no charge, his debts)
  and the line "Christ is faithful and provides all things" come from one of
  his surviving letters, which the Holy See prints as a reading for his
  feast. That is the only line of his the story puts in his mouth.
- **The street cry.** "Do good, brothers, to yourselves!" is how he is said
  to have begged in Granada at night. It is everywhere, but this run did not
  trace the wording to Francisco de Castro's 1585 biography, so the story
  says "it is remembered" and "something like this".
- **The name.** The Bishop of Tuy gave him the habit and the name "John of
  God". The tradition that the Infant Jesus gave him the name first is in the
  1910 *Catholic Encyclopedia* as "said to have been", and the story says
  "we cannot know if that is so".
- **The fire.** The 1549 rescue at the Royal Hospital, when he walked out of
  the flames unhurt, is told as a story that people told.
- **Where sources disagree,** the note gives both readings: the conversion
  in 1537 or 1539, and the beatification in 1630 (Wikipedia) or 1638 (the
  1910 encyclopedia).

**Two small things for the next run.** Saint Camillus de Lellis, the other
patron of hospitals, already has a Little Lamps story, so he gets one mention
in the note. Finishing this proposal left the shelf's list of proposals
empty, so the run added the next one, Saint Elizabeth of Portugal, the queen
who rode between two armies. That proposal warns the next writer about her
roses legend, because the shelf already tells the same legend about her
great-aunt, Elizabeth of Hungary.
