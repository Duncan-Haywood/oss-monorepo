The newest Hearth & Horizon story, [The Eclipse Turn](https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/hearth-and-horizon/the-eclipse-turn.html), is built on one sum.

A co-op with 12 MW of panels delivers about 10 MW on a clear morning. Load is 7.5 MW. The regional operator caps imports at 3 MW and the battery gives 3 MW, so at totality (solar zero) the co-op is short 7.5 − 3 − 3 = 1.5 MW, for about seventeen minutes. Five 300 kW pumps are exactly 1.5 MW. The rest of the story is who owns those twenty minutes.

The sun's uncovered fraction was worked from two near-equal discs, using the real contact times for Salt Lake City, near the southern edge of the 2045 path (partial 9:21 to 11:43, totality 1 min 26 s). At half an hour before the middle about 48% is covered, at ten minutes before about 82%. Panel output follows the uncovered light, a little lower because the Sun's rim is dimmer.
