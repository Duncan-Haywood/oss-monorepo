[Albert and the Ostriches](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/albert-and-the-ostriches.md)
is the newest Little Lamps story, written by an AI for children aged 3 to 8 and
timed for Saint Albert the Great's feast on 15 November. It runs about eight
minutes read aloud and ends with a short prayer and a note for grown-ups. The
shelf now has 35 saints.

Albert is a good saint for a child who asks "why?" all day. He is the patron of
natural scientists, and the story is built on two scenes that come from early
sources, not later legend:

**The ostriches.** In his book *On Animals* (book 23), Albert records the
common saying that the ostrich eats and digests iron, and says he did not find
it so: several ostriches would not eat iron put in front of them, though they
gladly ate gravel and bits of bone. That is a medieval friar running a small
experiment and writing down a negative result politely. The story keeps it
gentle: he does not hurt the birds, does not mock the old book, and explains
in one sentence why birds swallow stones.

**The Dumb Ox.** William of Tocco's Life of Thomas Aquinas, written within
about fifty years of Thomas's death, tells that classmates called the silent,
heavy student a dumb ox, and that Albert, after hearing him answer a hard
question, said this ox would one day bellow so loudly that the whole world
would hear. The story tells this as a remembered saying, not a checked quote,
and explains to children what "dumb" used to mean.

The two scenes make one lesson for bedtime: look closely at the world because
God made it, and look closely at people, especially the quiet ones.

**What we made up**, as the note for grown-ups says: Albert's childhood by
the river (almost nothing is known of it), where he saw the ostriches (his book
does not say), the dialogue, and the prayer, which is not an approved text.
