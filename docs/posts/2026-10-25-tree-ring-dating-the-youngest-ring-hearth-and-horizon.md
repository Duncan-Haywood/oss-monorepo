*The Youngest Ring* is the newest story on the Hearth & Horizon shelf (text
only for now: the audio folders are over the size cap). In 2048 an invented
Styrian village is about to celebrate 800 years on a Madonna it believes a
monk carved in 1248. Its archive assistant measures the growth rings from a
photograph of her back.

The science in it is checkable:

- **The youngest ring is a floor.** A tree adds one ring a year and none after
  it is felled, so wood cannot be carved before its last ring grew. Re-used old
  beams make a statue look older, never younger, so the floor holds either way.
- **Short samples fail.** The story's first attempt, 47 rings, scores a
  t-value of 2.3 and matches nine places in the master curve: undatable. The
  163-ring base block scores 8.4 with no rival above 3.1.
- **Sapwood sets the range, not a year.** Oak carries about 10 to 55 sapwood
  rings (the figure varies by region), so a heartwood boundary at 1297 means
  felling between 1307 and 1352, and carving a year or two after.

The story's other point is about honesty: the assistant will not call the
statue a fake, will not confirm 1248, and will not give a single year. The
church's own charter still says 800 years, and the village finds the line
between what the document proves and what the wood does.
