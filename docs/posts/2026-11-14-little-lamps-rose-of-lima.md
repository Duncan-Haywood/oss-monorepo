[Rose and the Hut in the Garden](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/rose-and-the-hut-in-the-garden.md)
is the 55th story on the Little Lamps shelf, the saints' bedtime stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. It runs about 1,240 words before the prayer,
roughly nine minutes read aloud. Like the other recent pieces it is text only,
because the shelf's audio folders are over the creative prompt's 400 MB line
(474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** The series bible's "Next up" list had Rose of Lima as its
only Little Lamps proposal, and *Brother Martin and the Mice*, already on the
shelf, takes place in the same city at the same time. The bible asked for the
garden, the flowers and needlework sold for the family, and the room for the
sick at the centre, and for her severe penances to be left out of a bedtime
story.

**Three decisions the sources forced.**

1. *Which death date.* The 1912 Catholic Encyclopedia says she died on 30
   August 1617. Wikipedia in English and Spanish say 24 August. The 30th was
   her feast day for centuries (the 24th already belonged to Saint
   Bartholomew), which is the likely source of the slip. The story gives "the
   month of August, in the year 1617", and the note for grown-ups gives 24
   August and names the disagreement rather than hiding it.
2. *What to do with the mosquitoes.* The best-loved Rose story in Lima is
   that she made a pact with the mosquitoes of her garden, sent them out each
   morning to praise God, and told them each evening to settle down "without
   making a fuss". Its best-known telling is Ricardo Palma's, in the seventh
   series of his *Tradiciones peruanas*, a nineteenth-century literary
   collection; Palma says he had it from "one of the saint's biographers"
   without naming which. That is a tradition, not a record, so the story
   stops and says so before telling it: "Nobody can say for sure that it
   happened. But it is a lovely story, so listen."
3. *What to leave out.* Rose is famous for penances (long fasts, a spiked
   circlet under a crown of roses) that the Church honours in her and does
   not ask anyone to copy, least of all a child. The story leaves them out
   and the grown-ups' note says why. The 1615 episode with the Dutch ships
   off Callao is left out too; one Spanish summary we read adds a detail
   about the Dutch commander's death that we could not confirm, so it was
   not worth the risk.

A smaller one: the bible's proposal called her feast a "memorial". Liturgical
calendars list 23 August as an *optional* memorial on the General Roman
Calendar, so the story and the bible now say that.

**What is ours** is listed at the end of the story, as on every Little Lamps
page: all the scenes and spoken words except the gist of what Rose tells the
mosquitoes, the humming game for the listener, and the prayer, which is not an
approved text.

With Rose done, the shelf's next-up list was empty, so the bible now proposes
Saint Bede: the boy at Jarrow, the counting of years from the birth of
Christ, and the last sentence of Saint John's Gospel dictated on his deathbed.
