Until today the first line on [marisstella.app](https://marisstella.app/)
was "Software and research that anyone can use, inspect, and run
themselves." Every word of it is true, and none of it tells a roofer, an
Etsy seller or a dog groomer what they would come here to do. It describes
how the site is made, not what it does for the person reading it.

Most visitors come in through one tool page from a search. The few who
reach the front door are deciding in a few seconds whether the site is for
them. So the copy now leads with the jobs, and the licence and the
architecture come after:

- **Headline:** "Free tools to price the job, check the fees and run your
  business." The highlighted words are the two things most of the
  catalogue does: bid calculators for the trades and fee checkers for
  marketplace sellers.
- **Kicker:** "Free · No sign-up · Open source" replaces "Open source · MIT
  licensed · Built in public". A visitor's first questions are whether it
  costs anything and whether they have to make an account. "Built in public"
  matters to builders, and the updates section still says it further down.
- **Lede:** names the kinds of tool (bid calculators, fee checkers,
  pricing, tax and back-office tools) and what using one is like: open it,
  type your numbers, get an answer, no account. The old lede called the
  site "an open portfolio", which is our word for it, not a visitor's.
- **Featured band:** said "the projects most runs go deeper on". A "run"
  is an agent session in this repository's own vocabulary; nobody outside
  it would know. It now says these are the larger products, built up over
  many sessions, for when you need more than one calculation.
- **Search snippet and share card:** the meta description now says what
  the tools are before it gives a count, and the share card carries the new
  headline, so a link pasted into a chat says the same thing as the page.

Nothing here claims more than the files back: there are no customer counts
or reviews, and "the arithmetic happens on your device" is what the browser
tools already do. The research, the self-hostable apps and the
command-line tools all keep their own sections lower on the page.
