A subcontractor who goes unpaid on a private job can file a mechanics lien. On a public job it
can't: government property cannot be liened. Two other things decide whether the sub gets paid
there. One is the prime contractor's payment bond. The other is the cap many states put on how
much retainage a public owner may hold. We read both from the statutes and built them into
[PayApps](https://marisstella.app/payapps/), our pay-app billing app for specialty
subcontractors. This post sets out what the law says.

## Federal jobs: the payment bond and its 90-day notice

The Miller Act (40 U.S.C. §§ 3131-3134) requires a payment bond on federal construction contracts
above $150,000 (FAR 28.102-1). Who can claim on it depends on who you contracted with:

- **With the prime contractor:** you can sue on the bond if you are still unpaid 90 days after
  your last day of labor or material. No notice is required first.
- **With one of the prime's subcontractors:** you must first give the prime written notice
  "within 90 days from the date on which the person did or performed the last of the labor or
  furnished or supplied the last of the material for which the claim is made" (§ 3133(b)(2)).
- **Further down** (a sub-subcontractor's supplier, say): the bond does not cover you
  (*J.W. Bateson Co. v. United States*, 434 U.S. 586 (1978)).

The statute prescribes no form for the notice, only what it must contain. It must "state with
substantial accuracy the amount claimed and the name of the party to whom the material was
furnished or supplied or for whom the labor was done or performed". It goes to the prime, not to
the sub that hired you. It must be served "by any means that provides written, third-party
verification of delivery" at a place where the prime does business, or the way a U.S. marshal
serves a summons. In practice that means certified mail with a return receipt, or a courier that
records a signature. The suit itself must be brought "no later than one year after the day on
which the last of the labor was performed or material was supplied" (§ 3133(b)(4)).

The 90 days run from your last day of real work. Punch-list and warranty work usually does not
move that day later. Courts also differ on whether the notice has to arrive within the 90 days or
only be sent, so send it early.

## State and local jobs: retainage caps

Many states limit the retainage a public owner may hold from its prime, and some extend that
limit to what the prime holds from its subs. These are the 16 that PayApps checks, from the
table in our
[retainage guide](https://marisstella.app/pay-app-guides/retainage-explained.html), which links
each statute:

| State | Cap on public work | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona | 10% of each progress estimate | A.R.S. § 34-221 |
| California | 5% of each payment, and 5% of the contract price in total | Cal. Pub. Cont. Code § 7201 |
| Florida | 5% of each progress payment | Fla. Stat. § 255.078 |
| Illinois | 10% before 50% complete, then reduced so no more than 5% is held | 30 ILCS 550/1 |
| Massachusetts | 5% of each periodic payment | M.G.L. c. 30 § 39G |
| Michigan | 10% until 50% complete, then no more | MCL 125.1563 |
| Minnesota | 5% of each progress payment | Minn. Stat. § 15.72 |
| Nevada | 5% until 50% complete | NRS 338.515 |
| New York | 5% of each progress payment | N.Y. Gen. Mun. Law § 106-b |
| North Carolina | 5% of each periodic payment | N.C. Gen. Stat. § 143-134.1 |
| Oregon | 5% of each progress payment | ORS 279C.570(7) |
| Pennsylvania | 10% until 50% complete, then 5% | 62 Pa.C.S. § 3921 |
| Texas | 10% under $5 million; 5% at $5 million or more | Tex. Gov't Code § 2252.032 |
| Utah | 5% of each payment, and 5% of the total construction price | Utah Code § 13-8-5 |
| Virginia | 5% (at least 95% of the earned sum is paid) | Va. Code § 2.2-4333 |
| Washington | 5% of moneys earned | RCW 60.28.011 |

The common case is a GC writing 10% into a subcontract on a state job where the statute allows
5%. A sub billing $40,000 a month has $2,000 a month held that the law may not allow, and on a
year-long job that is $24,000 of its own money.
