[Egeria and the Lamp That Burned Night and Day](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/135-egeria-and-the-lamp-that-burned-night-and-day.md)
is episode 135 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script runs
about 8,100 words, roughly 50 minutes at the narrator's pace. Like the
episodes before it since 30, it is text only for now.

**Why this one.** Episode 133 kept Holy Week with Egeria. The series list's
next entry was the chapters just before it: 24 to 26, where she tells her
sisters at home what Jerusalem does every ordinary day, every Sunday, at
Epiphany and on "the fortieth day after Epiphany". It is the part of her
letter readers tend to skip. The episode argues they should not.

**What the letter gives.** Every quoted line was checked against the Latin
this time. The Latin Library host was refused by the session's network proxy
again, but the same text is mirrored in the CLTK project's
`latin_text_latin_library` repository on GitHub, which was reachable. What
the episode keeps:

- *The light comes from the tomb.* At the tenth hour, the lamp-lighting the
  Greeks call *lychnikon*, every lamp in the Anastasis is lit, "and the light
  is not brought from outside, but from inside the cave, where a lamp is
  always burning night and day" (24.4). That became the title and the
  refrain.
- *The little boys.* As the deacon reads the names for prayer, "a great many
  little boys" (*pisinni plurimi*) answer every one with *Kyrie eleison*,
  "and their voices are beyond counting" (24.5). She uses the same word,
  *infinitus*, for the boys' voices as for the light a sentence earlier.
- *Timed by a chicken.* The holy places do not open before cockcrow (24.8).
  On Sundays the crowd arrives early and sits waiting, "afraid they will not
  be there in time", and the bishop reads the Resurrection at the door of the
  tomb in a cloud of incense while the whole church weeps (24.10).
- *As many as wish to may preach.* On Sundays every priest who wants to
  preaches, then the bishop, "so that the people may always be taught". So
  the dismissal comes at the fourth or fifth hour (25.1). The narrator is
  gentle about it.
- *Fitting.* What impressed her most was not the gold but that the psalms are
  always *apti*, fitting the hour and the thing being done (25.5).
- *The missing page.* The only manuscript has lost a leaf exactly where
  Epiphany night in Bethlehem was described. The text resumes mid-song,
  "Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord", on the night walk back
  to Jerusalem, slowly "because of the monks, who go on foot", arriving "at
  the hour when one person can begin to recognise another" (25.6). The
  episode says out loud that the Bethlehem night is lost, and invents
  nothing about it.
- *The first Candlemas.* Chapter 26 is the oldest description anywhere of
  the feast now kept on 2 February, the Presentation of the Lord, or the
  Meeting in the East. Jerusalem kept it "as if it were Easter", with sermons
  on Simeon and Anna. Luke 2:22-38 is told straight, and the episode
  notices the echo: Anna prayed in the temple "night and day", and so did
  the lamp in the tomb.

**Honesty calls.**

- *Christmas in January.* That Jerusalem then kept the Nativity on 6 January
  is told as historians' view, drawn from the letter and later Jerusalem
  service books. Rome's 25 December is attested from 336. The Armenian
  church still keeps 6 January.
- *Anna's age.* Luke 2:37 can be read as "eighty-four years old" or "a widow
  for eighty-four years". The script says both.
- *The bishop.* Egeria names no bishop. "Very probably Saint Cyril" is a
  dating inference, said as one.
- *Fixed in the edit.* A first draft said a monk copied the letter "seven
  hundred years after" Valerius of Bierzo praised it. Valerius wrote about
  680 and the Arezzo manuscript is eleventh-century, so it now says "about
  four hundred". The draft said Egeria uses "exactly the same words" for the
  Good Friday dawn. The Latin in chapter 36 is close but not identical (*incipit
  quasi homo hominem cognoscere*), so it now says "almost". The draft had the
  monks sing Simeon's song "before they go to sleep". That is Western
  Compline; Orthodox monasteries sing it at Vespers, so the line now says
  "every evening as the day ends".

**Invented, and labelled.** Theodora, a widow who keeps a pilgrim lodging,
her nine-year-old son Zenon, who sings Kyrie eleison louder than anyone and
knows three words of Latin ("yes", "no", "bread"), and Brother Abraham, a
slow old monk, are inventions. So is the burnt bread and every word anyone
says that Egeria does not report. The front matter, the welcome and the
notes all say so.
