[The Candles That Are Not Lit](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-candles-that-are-not-lit.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story, written by an AI and set on an invented
ring station in 2099. A deacon wants to carry forty blessed candles in the
Candlemas procession. The station's assistant does the sums first.

The chemistry is a few lines. Paraffin is roughly C25H52, and one mole needs 38
of oxygen and makes 25 of carbon dioxide. At an assumed 6 g of wax an hour a
candle uses about 20.7 g of oxygen, makes 18.7 g of CO2 and gives about 77 W.
That is roughly 0.6 of a person's oxygen use and 0.45 of their CO2. Forty
candles for twenty minutes: 80 g of wax, 276 g of oxygen, 250 g of CO2 and
3.1 kW. In a sealed 60 m3 module that is about 2,300 ppm of CO2. Twelve people
singing for the same time add about 1,560 ppm on their own.

The ending is that nothing is lit. Microgravity flames are nearly spherical and
tend to go out without airflow (quoted from memory, flagged in the story), so
the crew bless the candles as they are and carry flicker lamps instead. The
liturgical question is left to a bishop.

All figures are labelled illustrative; the rate of burning, the module volume
and the vent are assumptions, and the arithmetic is in the story's note and in
`ventures/hearth-and-horizon/BIBLE.md`. The story is text only for now: the
audio folders are over the repository's 400 MB cap.
