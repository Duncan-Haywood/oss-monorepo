A shirt that sells 20 a week is really five sizes selling 3, 7, 6, 3 and 1. The average hides the medium that runs out first, and the factory takes weeks to refill it.

The reorder planner in the Clothing Design Studio now has a "by size" table. You type the share of sales per size and what is on the shelf; it gives each size its own safety stock, reorder point and order quantity, then applies the factory minimum to the whole order, spread in proportion.

Worked by hand in the tests: at 20 a week, a spread of 8 and a 9-week lead time, a medium with half the sales needs 23 pieces of safety stock and a reorder point of 113, so with 100 on the shelf the order is 66. Small sizes get a spread built from the total's wobble plus the chance of each buyer picking that size.

The size mix is yours to type, sizes are treated as independent, and no real brand's sales have been run through it. Treat it as a floor until your own sell-through says otherwise.
