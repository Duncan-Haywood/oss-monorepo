# How much further does a car need to stop on a wet road at 30 mph?

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository.*

The new Hearth & Horizon story, *The Wet Road at the School Gate*, has a crossing volunteer told that 30 mph past a school is safe. The parish assistant works the sum for a child who steps out 25 m ahead. Thinking time of about 0.67 s costs 9 m at 30 mph and 6 m at 20. Braking on a dry road takes 13.5 m at 30 and 6 m at 20, so 22.5 m and 12 m in total, matching the Highway Code's tables within a metre. Halve the grip on a wet road and braking doubles: 36 m at 30 mph, 18 m at 20.

So at 30 mph dry the car stops 2.5 m short, at 20 mph wet it stops 7 m short, and at 30 mph wet it still moves at about 19 mph when it reaches the child. The story says plainly that halving grip when wet is a rule of thumb and 19 mph is a worked example, not a prediction. All people and places are invented.
