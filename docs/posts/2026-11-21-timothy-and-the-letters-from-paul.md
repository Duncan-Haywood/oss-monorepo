[Timothy and the Letters from Paul](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/timothy-and-the-letters-from-paul.html)
is the newest Little Lamps story, written for 26 January, the memorial of
Saints Timothy and Titus, the day after the Conversion of Saint Paul. An AI

note for grown-ups follows it. It is text only for now, because the audio

## A saint you meet in other people's letters

Timothy never speaks in the New Testament. Everything we know about him
comes from what Paul (and Acts) says about him. For a children's story that
turns out to be a gift. The story is about being trusted, and other people's
letters are where trust shows. Paul writes to Philippi: "I have no one like
him." He writes to Corinth: "When Timothy comes, see that you put him at ease
among you." And to Timothy himself: "Let no one despise your youth."

The story is built on three of those moments. The first is a faith handed
down by his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice (2 Timothy 1:5). It became
the story's picture of a lamp passed from hand to hand, and its bedtime
question: who handed it to you? The second is an errand alone, back to the
frightened Christians of Thessalonica, and a return with good news, which
Paul answered with a letter that still opens "Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy".
The third is the old Paul in prison asking him to "come before winter" and
bring the cloak left at Troas. Small children understand wanting your coat,
your books and your friend.

## What the re-read changed

The story was checked against the verses once more before it was finished,
and that check caught three lines that said more than the sources do.

- **The big handwriting.** The first draft had Paul signing "one of the
  letters" to Timothy in large letters. That detail is real, but it is in
  Galatians 6:11 and 2 Thessalonians 3:17, not in the letters to Timothy.
  The story now says Paul often took the pen himself at the end, and the note
  gives the verses, including Romans 16:22, where the scribe Tertius names
  himself.
- **How many believed at Lystra.** "Lots of people" became "some of the
  people". Acts says disciples were left behind there, not how many.
- **Thessalonica.** Acts and 1 Thessalonians tell the errand in slightly
  different orders. Acts has Timothy waiting in Beroea and rejoining Paul in
  Corinth. The letter has Paul sending him from Athens. The story follows the
  letter and ends the errand in Corinth, and the note shows both accounts.

Some things are left for the grown-ups' note. One is the dispute over who
wrote the Pastoral Letters. Others are Timothy's circumcision (Acts 16:3),
his own imprisonment (Hebrews 13:23), and the late *Acts of Timothy*, which
says a crowd killed him at a pagan festival in Ephesus. The child hears that
last one in a single gentle sentence, marked as an old story.
