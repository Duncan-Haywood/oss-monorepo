[Massah and Meribah and the Water from the Rock at Horeb](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/174-massah-and-meribah-and-the-water-from-the-rock-at-horeb.md)
is episode 174 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, like every episode
since number 30, because the creative audio folders hold 581 MB against the
creative track's 400 MB limit. The script runs about 6,700 spoken words,
roughly forty-three minutes. Episode 173 left it on the list.

## Seven verses, and the problem of length

Exodus 17:1-7 is short. The people camp at Rephidim and find no water. They
quarrel with Moses: "Give us water." He answers: "Why do you put the Lord
to the test?" He cries out, "A little more, and they will stone me." The
Lord tells him to take the elders and "the staff with which you struck the
Nile", and adds: "I will stand before you there on the rock at Horeb."
Moses strikes the rock, water comes out, and he names the place Testing and
Quarrelling.

The book reports the miracle in four words: "And Moses did so." A
forty-minute episode can't make up for that by adding events, because the
series bible forbids changing what happens in Scripture. So the length
comes from three other places:

- **Scripture's own retellings.** Psalms 78:15-16 and 105:41 and Isaiah
  48:21 describe what Exodus leaves out: the water gushed and "flowed
  through the desert like a river". The scene where water comes down the
  dry stream bed is built only from those verses, and the episode says so.
- **An invented family watching from outside.** Ziva and her family,
  carried over from episodes 170, 172 and 173, watch from a ridge at the
  edge of the camp. The book says only that the elders saw, so the
  episode never puts Ziva at the rock.
- **The two names.** Massah, "testing", picks up the line from episode 173,
  where God says of the manna "that I may test them". When God tests
  people, it is like the wind on a young tree. When people test God, they
  are saying "prove it". Moses later says "you shall not put the Lord your
  God to the test, as you tested him at Massah" (Deuteronomy 6:16), and
  Jesus quotes those words on the pinnacle of the temple (Matthew 4:7).

## The question asked under the cloud

The people ask "Is the Lord among us or not?" with manna on the ground
that morning and the pillar of cloud overhead. The episode points that out
once, gently, and gives the answer before the people hear it: the Lord's
"I will stand before you there on the rock" means he has already gone on
ahead. The father of the invented family never grumbles out loud. He
admits that he thought the same question all afternoon, and he says the
answer quietly when his empty water skin fills.

## Readings, labelled

Psalm 95 ("harden not your hearts, as at Meribah") is the usual Invitatory psalm that opens
the Liturgy of the Hours. The episode mentions this and
ends with the psalm's first line, "the rock of our salvation". Hebrews 3
leans on that psalm's "today". Saint Paul's "the rock was Christ" (1
Corinthians 10:4) appears together with the later Jewish tradition of a
well that followed the people, which the episode labels as a tradition.
Tertullian's *On Baptism*, chapter nine, counts the water from the rock
among baptism's figures, and the pierced side in John 19:34 is told as a
picture Christian writers have long drawn. The episode also names two
uncertainties: where Rephidim was, and why the rock is "at Horeb" when the
camp was not.
