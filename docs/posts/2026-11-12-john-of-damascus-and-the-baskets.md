[John of Damascus and the Baskets at the Market](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/099-john-of-damascus-and-the-baskets-at-the-market.md)
is episode 99 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,600
spoken words, roughly 49 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text only,
because the audio folders are still over the repository's 400 MB cap (423 MB
today).

The [episode 98 post](2026-11-12-andrew-of-crete-and-the-great-canon.md)
named John as the next step. He was born a few years after Andrew in the same
city, he rounds off the hymn-writers, and his life is where the icon
controversy starts.

## Which story to build the episode around

John's Life has two famous episodes. One is the hand cut off on the caliph's
orders after a forged letter, and restored as he prayed before an icon of the
Mother of God. The other is the elder at Mar Saba sending him back to
Damascus, the city where he had been a great man, to sell palm-leaf baskets
at a ridiculous price.

The hand is the better-known one, but it has two problems for a bedtime show.
It involves a mutilation, and most historians, Andrew Louth among them, take
it for a legend that grew up long after John's death. The episode tells it,
because the Church has kept it and the icon of the Three Hands at Hilandar
depends on it. But it tells it quickly, labels it as the Life's report, and
says plainly what historians think.

So the baskets carry the episode. It is a story about being laughed at, then
finding it hurt less than dreading it did. Children can follow that.
The invented young brother, Elias, walks to Damascus with John. He gets the
line the episode turns on, after John says he may have left his old
self in the market: "Good. He sounded heavy."

## The argument, made small

The three treatises on the images are serious theology. The episode turns
them into a conversation that keeps two of John's real points:

- **Why the old commandment and the icon are both right.** Nobody could
  picture God in Moses's day because nobody had seen him. After the
  Incarnation, what people saw can be painted. Elias boils this down to
  "because of Christmas", and John says that is better than three treatises.
- **Worship is not veneration.** John keeps latreia, the worship owed to God
  alone, separate from the honour shown to an image. He backs it with Saint
  Basil's line that the honour given to an image passes to its prototype.
  The episode makes the point with a child hugging their mother through her
  dress.

John's famous sentence about honouring matter is given only as a plain
paraphrase, and the episode says so.

## What was left out

Not every true thing fits a bedtime story about a saint whom both
traditions honour. The episode leaves out John's chapter on Islam in *On
Heresies*. It skips *Barlaam and Josaphat*, which was once credited to him
and no longer is. It also stays off his place in the filioque argument, under
the series' rule against disputed ground. It does keep the insult at the
council of Hieria in 754, where the bishops called him by his Arabic family
name, Mansur, to sneer at him. It keeps the answer too: the council of Nicaea in
787 named him among the defenders of the icons. And it keeps the nickname the
chronicler Theophanes used, Chrysorrhoas, "golden stream", which was the old
name of the river at Damascus.

## Next

The icon fight had a second round under Leo V, from 815. Saint Theodore the
Studite at Constantinople would carry it on and keep the city thread of
episodes 93 to 98. Cosmas, John's adopted brother and a hymn-writer, and
Saint Sabbas, who founded the cliff monastery in 483, are also on the list.
