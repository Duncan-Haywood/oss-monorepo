[Angela and the Girls around the Kitchen Table](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/angela-and-the-girls-around-the-kitchen-table.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story itself runs about 1,300 words, roughly
nine minutes read aloud, and it has no audio because the shelves' audio
folders are over the creative track's 400 MB cap (577 MB this run). Her feast
is 27 January, three days after Francis de Sales, whose story landed this
morning.

**The story.** A farm girl at Desenzano on Lake Garda listens to her father
read the lives of the saints in the evenings. Then she loses her father, her
mother and her sister, and she tells God all of it, including the parts that
hurt. She joins the Franciscans as a tertiary, a sister who lives out in the
world, and comes home to the farm. There she notices that the girls in the
lanes around her have nobody with time to teach them about God. She has no
school and no money, only a table and a kettle, so she asks them in. Years
later, in Brescia, at about sixty, she gathers twenty-eight young women into
a company named for Saint Ursula. They are to belong to God completely while
living at home with their own families, and to teach the girls of the city.
The last image goes back to the kitchen table and a girl saying *I can do
that.*

**Why this saint.** The shelf leans on founders who built something big. Her
work starts small: one person, at home, with what she already has. For a child
it gives one clear idea (share what you have) and one gentle one (tell God
when you are sad). The second matters because she lost so many people young,
and some children listening will have lost someone too.

**What was left out, and why.** The bible's proposal flagged her later lives
as full of traditions. The story keeps one and labels it. Four others stay
out of the story and are named in the note for grown-ups:

- **Kept, labelled:** the comfort she was given after her sister died,
  without the last sacraments. The story says "people who told Angela's
  story long afterwards said... We do not know exactly what happened."
- **Left out:** the vision at Brudazzo of a ladder of young women and angels
  going up to heaven; going blind at Crete on the way to the Holy Land and
  seeing again at the same place on the way home; Pope Clement VII inviting
  her to stay in Rome in 1525; her flight to Cremona in the war of 1529. All
  come from the biographies, not her own writings. Any one of them would
  turn a ten-minute story about a kitchen table into a travelogue.

**One claim softened.** The proposal called the Company of Saint Ursula "the
first such company in the Church". That overstates it. Christian women had
lived consecrated lives in their own homes since the early Church. What was
new in 1535 was a company of such women, under a written rule, given to
teaching. The story says "nobody had a company quite like that", and the note
explains the distinction and the common description of the Ursulines as the
first women's teaching order.

**Dates checked against the story.** It never gives a birth year, because the
sources give 1470, 1474 or 1475. "About sixty" at the 25 November 1535
foundation fits 1474. "In her forties" when she moved to Brescia in 1516
gives 42. Her death at Brescia on 27 January 1540, the canonization on 24 May
1807 and the feast's moves (31 May, then 1 June, then 27 January in 1969) are
in the note.

**Next on the shelf.** The bible's Little Lamps list now proposes Vincent
Ferrer (5 April), the Dominican from Valencia who spent his last twenty years
walking from town to town to preach with crowds behind him. The proposal comes
with a warning: in the Spain of his day his preaching to Jews went alongside
forced conversions. A bedtime story either leaves that subject out or the
note says plainly what happened.
