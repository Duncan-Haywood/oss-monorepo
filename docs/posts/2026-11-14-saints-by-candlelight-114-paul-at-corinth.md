[Paul and the Tentmakers of Corinth](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/114-paul-and-the-tentmakers-of-corinth.md)
is episode 114 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 6,500
spoken words, roughly 42 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text
only, because the audio folders are over the creative prompt's 400 MB line
(474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** Episode 113 ended with one line of Acts: Paul left Athens
and went to Corinth. The series list asked for the sequel, Acts 18:1-18.
It is a different kind of story from Athens. There is no great speech.
There is a workshop, a married couple thrown out of Rome, a year and a half
of sewing and arguing, a vision at night, and a Roman governor who would
not listen.

**What the episode is built on.** Acts 18:1-18, with a few verses after it
for Ephesus and Apollos, and Paul's own lines about the same months: "in
weakness and in fear and in much trembling" (1 Corinthians 2:3), working
"with our own hands" (1 Corinthians 4:12, 1 Thessalonians 2:9), the gift
from Macedonia (2 Corinthians 11:9), and baptizing "Crispus and Gaius" and
then remembering Stephanas's household (1 Corinthians 1:14-16). What the
Lord says in the night vision is kept word for word in plain English. The
episode does not describe what Paul saw, because Acts does not.

**Choices a careful listener might notice:**

- **The fixed date.** Gallio is the one point in Paul's life that outside
  evidence pins down. The Delphi inscription, a letter of Claudius naming
  "Junius Gallio, my friend and proconsul", dates to about 52, which puts
  Gallio in Corinth around 51 to 52. The episode says so and calls it a
  cornerstone. Seneca supplies two more things about his brother: Gallio's
  sweetness to everyone (Natural Questions 4, preface) and the fever he
  caught in Achaia, "not of the body but of the place" (Letter 104).
- **Chrestus.** Suetonius says Claudius expelled the Jews of Rome over
  disturbances "at the instigation of Chrestus". Whether that means Christ
  is argued, and the episode leaves it argued. Orosius gives the year, 49.
- **The joke Luke left in.** Paul shakes out his cloak, says he is going to
  the Gentiles, and moves in with Titius Justus, whose house "was next door
  to the synagogue" (18:7). The narrator lets that be funny. Then comes the
  turn that matters: the synagogue's ruler, Crispus, believes with his
  whole household.
- **Sosthenes.** The crowd beats him in front of the tribunal, and the
  episode gives that one quiet sentence, as the series bible asks. A
  Sosthenes is co-author of 1 Corinthians (1:1). That they are the same man
  is offered only as a hope, which is all the evidence allows.
- **Priscilla first.** She is named before her husband in four of the six
  places the couple appears. That is counted from the text: Acts 18:18 and
  18:26, Romans 16:3 and 2 Timothy 4:19. John Chrysostom remarked on it in
  his two sermons on "Greet Priscilla and Aquila".
- **Open questions told as open:** whether "tentmaker" meant leather or
  goat-hair cloth (the Latin *cilicium* is named after Paul's own Cilicia),
  whether the stone platform in the ruined forum is the *bema* of Acts,
  whether the Isthmian Games fell while Paul was there, whose hair was cut at
  Cenchreae, and whether the Erastus of the pavement inscription is the
  Erastus of Romans 16:23.

**What is invented.** Nikon, the neighbour's boy who sits on a basket in the
workshop door, his mother Melitta and her lentil soup, the customers, the
knots, and the farewell at the harbour. The welcome says so, the notes say
so, and so does the episode's `fiction` field.

**One correction made before landing.** The first draft gave Nero a
"golden spade" for the canal he began across the isthmus. The writer could
not confirm the golden tool against Suetonius (Nero 19), the source the
episode cites, so the line now says only that Nero turned the first earth
himself.
