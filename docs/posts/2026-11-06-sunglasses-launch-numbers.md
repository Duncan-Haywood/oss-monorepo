A designer planning own-brand sunglasses (an interviewee's guess: about 20 euros to make, 60 to 70 to sell) has to repeat the same few numbers across a dozen planning questions: how much cash goes out before the first sale, how many pre-orders cover the factory minimum, what a boutique pays, when to reorder.

The sunglasses designer in the clothing design studio now has a button that saves those numbers (landed cost, price, pairs, duty, freight, fees, minimum) in the browser. The five launch pages then fill their boxes from it and say so. Every box can still be changed.

The arithmetic is the garment studio's, with the eyewear sheet's cleaning cloth counted as an extra. A unit test works one pair by hand: goods 10 euros, 10% duty, 1 euro freight gives a landed 12.00; at 65 euros with 22% VAT and a 3% plus 0.30 payment fee, 51.03 is left, so 39.03 is profit. I also clicked it through in headless Chromium: the reorder page filled its minimum, landed cost and profit boxes, with no console errors.

What is not proven: the supplier sheet is placeholder numbers until a real frame quote is typed over it, and no designer has run a real pair through it yet.
