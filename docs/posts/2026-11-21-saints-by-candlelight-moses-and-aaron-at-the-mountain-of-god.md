[Moses and Aaron at the Mountain of God](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/158-moses-and-aaron-at-the-mountain-of-god.md)
is episode 158 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30 it
is text only: the creative audio folders hold 577 MB, over the creative
track's 400 MB line. The script is about 5,600 spoken words, around
thirty-six minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 157 left it on the series
list the day before.

## A gap between two episodes

Episode 37 told the burning bush and stopped at Jethro's "go in peace".
Episode 157 told the well of Midian forty years earlier. The verses between
the bush and Pharaoh's court, Exodus 4:14-31, are usually a bridge people
cross quickly. This episode tells them from Aaron's side, because the text
gives him three things a bedtime story can rest on:

- **"I know that he can speak well."** The Lord says it to Moses about
  Aaron (4:14) at the moment Moses asks him to send someone else. The
  episode's most tender invented scene has Moses repeat it to Aaron by the
  fire. It adds no event: verse 28 says Moses told Aaron *all* the words, so
  this one was among them.
- **"When he sees you he will be glad in his heart."** The promise comes
  before the narrative tells of Aaron's own call (4:27). The episode reads
  that as God arranging both ends of the meeting, and says "whichever way
  round it happened", since the participle "he is coming out" could also
  mean Aaron was already on the road.
- **"He met him at the mountain of God and kissed him."** Five or six words.
  The book names no tears and no speeches, so neither does the script when it
  is on the book's ground; the feelings live in the invented parts, flagged
  as such aloud each time.

## What it refused to tidy up

Two things a gentler show might skip are named in a sentence each. The
lodging place (4:24-26) is "in the Bible, and we are not hiding it. We are
simply leaving it for daylight"; it now sits on the series list as its own
episode, to be told carefully with the main readings named. And the
believing elders of 4:31 do not stay believing: chapter five's straw and
chapter six's "broken spirit" come straight after. The wind-down also
gives the golden calf one plain line, because Hebrews 5:4's "called by
God, as Aaron was" only means something if the man was not perfect.

## What was checked

The ages (Moses eighty, Aaron eighty-three) are Exodus 7:7; Aaron's
hundred and twenty-three years at his death are Numbers 33:39, which a
first draft wrongly credited to the "book" in general and Numbers 20 in
the sources. Chapter five's complainers were the Hebrew foremen, not the
elders, and the draft was corrected. Elisheba, Amminadab, Nahshon and the
four sons are Exodus 6:23; Miriam as Aaron's sister is Exodus 15:20 and
Numbers 26:59. For Aaron's feast the episode follows the series' rule:
he is remembered among the holy forefathers in the Orthodox weeks before
Christmas, and no calendar date is given that we cannot vouch for. Where
the mountain stands is left open, with Egeria's south Sinai mountain named
as the pilgrims' tradition.

Invented and flagged: how the word reached Aaron, Miriam filling the water
skin, a shepherd lad who knows the way, the brothers practising the
message on the walk back ("he said deliver, so we say deliver"), and the
fire. The closing prayer is a plain rendering of Psalm 133, the oil running
down the beard of Aaron.

It leaves two new lines on the list: Moses and Aaron before Pharaoh and the
bricks without straw (Exodus 5), and Aaron and Hur holding up Moses' hands
at Rephidim (Exodus 17).
