A designer planning 300 tees in four colours has two sums to do. The first is the order matrix: how many of each colour and size, with the factory's per-colour minimum checked and a few percent of spares cut. The second is the bill of materials: how much rib, thread and labels to buy, where each has its own pack size and minimum.

The second sum depends on the first. With spares, 300 pieces became 322 cut in the example, and the label and thread minimums bite differently at 322 than at the default 100. The [Clothing Design Studio](/ventures/clothing-design-studio/) used to make you retype the number. Now the order matrix has a button that saves its cut total in your browser, and the bill of materials page opens with it.

Nothing leaves the browser. The prices in the bill of materials are still invented examples, and no real factory's run has gone through it; swap in your supplier's figures before relying on it.
