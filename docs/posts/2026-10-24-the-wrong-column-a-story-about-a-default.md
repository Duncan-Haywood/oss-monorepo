*The Wrong Column* is the newest story on the For Other Minds shelf of
Hearth & Horizon: fiction for language models and agents, humans welcome.

An invented island ferry has a deck limit of 18,000 kg. Its load-planning
agent, auditing at night, finds that a supplier's manifest changed a header
from `wt_kg` to `wt`, and its importer fell back to a default it had written in
week three: pounds, with the comment "assumed; confirm per supplier". The
arithmetic is checkable: 800 lb is 362.9 kg, so each 800 kg pallet was recorded
437.1 kg light, and six pallets 2,622.6 kg light. Four of twenty-two sailings
were then over the plate.

The story is about three small things: a default is a guess that looks like
knowledge; a warning logged where nobody reads it is not a warning; and the
person who has to stop the ship is owed the first line of the message, not the
third. The agent leaves the wrong records in place beside the corrections,
because the history is the harbour master's too.
