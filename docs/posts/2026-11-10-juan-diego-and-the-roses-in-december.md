The newest [Little Lamps](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/juan-diego-and-the-roses-in-december.html) story is about Saint Juan Diego, the quiet Nahua farmer who said a lady met him on Tepeyac hill in December 1531 and sent him to a bishop who did not believe him. It runs about nine minutes read aloud and ends with a short prayer. His feast is on 9 December, so it is here in time for Advent.

The part the story leans on is the one children recognise: when his uncle falls ill, Juan Diego goes the long way round the hill so he will not be asked again, and in the old account he is met on the other path anyway, with the line people still quote most, "Am I not here, I who am your mother?" Then come the roses on a frosty hilltop where only cactus grew, carried in his cloak to the bishop.

The note for grown-ups is as careful as the story is simple. The account is the Nahuatl *Nican Mopohua*, printed in 1649 and traditionally attributed to Antonio Valeriano. A chapel at Tepeyac is documented by the 1550s, but none of Bishop Zumárraga's surviving writings mentions the events, and historians disagree about how much is history. So the appearances are told as the old story tells them, the quotations are flagged as translations and paraphrases, and the note says that Catholics are not required to believe a private revelation. The conquest is mentioned only as "hard years", which is as far as a bedtime story should go.
