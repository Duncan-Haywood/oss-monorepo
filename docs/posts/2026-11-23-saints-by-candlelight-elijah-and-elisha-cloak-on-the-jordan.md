[Elijah and Elisha and the Cloak on the Jordan](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/168-elijah-and-elisha-and-the-cloak-on-the-jordan.md)
is episode 168 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30,
it is text only: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the creative
track's limit is 400 MB. The script is 7,102 spoken words, about
forty-five minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 162 left it on the list,
and episode 12 had already given the passage a single paragraph. This one
gives it the whole night.

## A chapter that tells you the ending first

Second Kings 2 opens with its conclusion: "When the Lord was about to take
Elijah up to heaven by a whirlwind" (2:1). There is no suspense about what
happens. The suspense, such as it is, is whether Elisha will stay behind.
Elijah asks him to three times, at Gilgal, at Bethel and at Jericho. Three
times Elisha answers with the same oath, "As the Lord lives, and as you
yourself live, I will not leave you" (2:2, 4, 6). At Bethel and at Jericho
the companies of prophets tell him that his master will be taken "from
over your head". Both times he says the same thing: "Yes, I know it; keep
silent" (2:3, 5). The episode is built on that repetition. It keeps every
spoken line as the book gives it and adds no lines of its own to
Elijah's or Elisha's part, except on the road where the book is silent.
Those additions are labelled.

## Details kept because they are in the text

- **"Went down to Bethel"** (2:2). Bethel stands far above the Gilgal
  near Jericho, so the verb is a problem if that is the Gilgal of Joshua's
  twelve stones. The narrator says that out loud: some scholars place this
  Gilgal in the hills north of Bethel, and nobody knows for certain.
- **The double portion** (2:9). The episode reads it through Deuteronomy
  21:17, where the firstborn heir gets twice any other son's share. Elisha's
  cry of "My father, my father!" (2:12) supports that reading. It is
  presented as a reading.
- **"The chariots of Israel and its horsemen."** King Joash says the same
  words over the dying Elisha in 2 Kings 13:14. The episode uses that echo
  to close the arc.
- **The fifty men** stood "at some distance" (2:7). The book says nobody
  but Elisha saw Elijah taken, so the invented character on the slope
  sees only what the text allows.
- **The salt in a new bowl** (2:19-22). The men of Jericho open with a
  compliment, "the situation of this city is pleasant", before they get
  to the bad water. Elisha's words begin "Thus says the Lord, I have
  healed these waters". The episode leans on that phrasing for the show's
  rule that saints pray and God acts.

## Labelled as tradition or left out

- The spring known today as Ein es-Sultan, or Elisha's Spring, is given as
  an old identification, not a proved one.
- The link to the Ascension (Acts 1:9-11) and the Malachi to John the
  Baptist to Transfiguration line (Malachi 4:5-6, Luke 1:17, Luke 9:30-31)
  are given as the Church's readings.
- 2:23-25, the boys and the bears on the road to Bethel, is left out. The
  notes say so: it is a hard passage that we have left for another time.

## What is invented

There are two invented characters, flagged in the welcome, the notes and
the `fiction` field. Ezer is a nineteen-year-old in the Jericho company:
one of the fifty, and one of the searchers who looked for three days
(2:16-18). His grandmother Adah carries water from the sour spring and
supplies the new bowl. The closing blessing is Sirach 48:11, which is
addressed to Elijah: "Blessed are those who saw you, and those who have
been adorned in love; for we also shall surely live."
