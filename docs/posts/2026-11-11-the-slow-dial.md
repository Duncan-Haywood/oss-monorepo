[The Slow Dial](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-slow-dial.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story. An AI wrote it. It is set in 2702, which
puts it in the band of the shelf's brief (20 to 1,000 years ahead) that had
only two stories so far; the other 282 are all set before 2240.

The town is Görlitz, which really does lie on the fifteenth meridian east, the
line Central European Time is counted from. A sundial there should agree with
the clock once the equation of time is applied. The one on the (invented)
church of Saint Hyacinth has been about twenty-five minutes slow for as long as
anyone remembers, and the old stonemason asked to replace it finds his new
dial slow by the same amount.

**Why.** Tides drag on the sea floor and slow the Earth's spin; ancient eclipse
records put the long-term lengthening of the day at about 1.8 milliseconds a
century. Until recently leap seconds held clock time to the turning Earth. In
2022 the General Conference on Weights and Measures resolved to let the gap
grow past 0.9 seconds by or before 2035, and what replaces leap seconds was
left open. The story assumes nothing ever does. Then the gap grows with the
square of the time:

| Year | Clock ahead of the Sun (model) |
|---|---|
| 2051 | under a second |
| 2702 | about 24 min 24 s |
| 2750 | about 28 min |
| 2800 | about 32 min |
| 2900 | about 41 min |

The model is 0.5 × 1.8 ms × 36,525 days × T², about 32.9 s × T² for T
centuries since 2035. In 2702 each day is about 12 ms longer than the clock's
86,400 seconds, which adds about 4.4 s a year. Decade-to-decade wobbles from
the Earth's core make any single year's figure uncertain by a minute or more.

**A second, smaller drift.** The equation of time depends on when Earth is
closest to the Sun, and that date creeps later through the calendar by about a
day every sixty years: 4 January in 2051, about 16 January in 2702. A 2050s
table is out by up to 1.9 minutes in 2702. On the story's test day, 20 May,
it is 53 seconds out, so the old book's noon is 25 min 17 s early.

**The choice the plot turns on.** The committee, reasonably, wants the new dial
cut to agree with the clock. The apprentice works out that a dial shifted to
the clock is right on the day it is cut and about eight minutes slow a century
later, so they would be making the next slow dial on purpose. They cut it true
to the Sun instead, with a table for the clock whose rows for 2750 onwards are
left blank for whoever is there to fill them in.

The geometry is the ordinary vertical south dial: at 51.15° N the style makes
38.85° with the wall, and each hour line sits at atan(cos 51.15° × tan h) from
the noon line. Every figure is listed in the story's closing note, worked with a
short script; the note also says which parts are invented. The text is CC BY-SA
4.0. There is no audio for this one: the shelf's audio folder is over the
400 MB cap that keeps the published site under GitHub Pages' limit, so new
stories land as text until the audio moves somewhere else.
