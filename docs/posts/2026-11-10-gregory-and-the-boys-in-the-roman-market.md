[Gregory and the Boys in the Roman Market](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/086-gregory-and-the-boys-in-the-roman-market.md)
is episode 86 of Saints by Candlelight. It was written by an AI and runs about
6,600 words, roughly 43 minutes spoken. It is text only for now, because the
audio folders are over the repository's 400 MB cap.

Episodes 47 to 85 were almost all Scripture, so this one goes back to a
saint's life, and it's one whose sources can be checked. Bede tells the
"not Angles but angels" scene (with the Deira/*de ira* and Aelle/Alleluia
puns) and says himself that it came down by tradition, so the episode tells
it as tradition. Most of the mission is documented history. Bede copied
Gregory's letters into his book: the letter of 23 July 596 that turned the
frightened monks around in Gaul, the warning to Augustine not to take pride
in wonders, and the letter to Mellitus about keeping the temples ("step by
step, and not in leaps"). Gregory's own letter to Eulogius of Alexandria
reports ten thousand baptisms at Christmas 597.

Kept apart and labelled:

- **Legend:** the locust on the book ("locusta, loco sta"), from the Whitby
  Life. It is older than Bede but less careful.
- **Later report:** Gregory refusing to say Mass after a beggar starved, from
  John the Deacon in the ninth century.
- **Left out:** the angel over Hadrian's tomb and Gregory as composer of the
  chant. Both appear only centuries later.
- **A guess, and said so:** the link between the market scene and Gregory's
  real letter to Candidus about buying English youths.
- **Invented:** Brother Felix, the merchant's extra lines and the Kentish
  weather.

The episode stays off the ground the two Churches dispute about the papacy.
Gregory is honoured in both, in the East as the Dialogist, and the sign-off
gives both feast days. The slave market is told gently but plainly, as a
wrong.
