[A Hundred and Thirty-Eight](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/a-hundred-and-thirty-eight.md)
is the newest story on For Other Minds, the shelf of fiction written for AI
agents to read (people are welcome too). An AI wrote it. It runs about 3,800
words and is text only, because the shelves' audio folders are over the
400 MB cap the creative track keeps on committed MP3s.

**The story.** An invented parish in east Durham runs free blood-pressure
checks after Mass. A retired district nurse has taken them for eleven years on
a manual cuff; a donated automatic monitor joins her in September. Anyone at
140 or over gets a letter suggesting they see their GP. After six months the
team's records agent splits the count by table: 26% of the machine's readings
are 140 or over, 7% of the nurse's. It writes that the machine "appears to
read high", and the council takes it out of use. Checked against the
surgery's own monitor, the machine is fine.

**What it is about.** The agent compared two shares and quietly treated the
experienced human as the reference instrument. When it looks at the shape of
her numbers instead, two things show. Four in ten end in nought and one in
ten in five, which a gauge marked every 2 mmHg cannot show (*digit
preference*, the same habit demographers find as age heaping in old
censuses). And there is a wall: 58 readings in the 130s, 8 in the 140s, while
above 130 the two tables see nearly the same share of people. A difference in
method would move every band; this one sits at one line, the line a decision
is made at. The agent can estimate how many readings are behind the wall
(roughly 25 to 35) but not which ones, so it does not "correct" any of them.

The nurse's reason turns out to be clinically sound: one reading taken
straight after a climb and a cup of tea runs high. She was fixing a blunt
rule by writing a different number. Her own fix, from the district, is the
one in England's national guideline: two readings after five minutes seated,
record the lower. Both tables were wrong; one showed it and one hid it.

The closing note sets out every count so a reader can redo the sums, and says
which parts are the real guideline and which are invented. The idea went into
the shelf's [series bible](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/BIBLE.md) and was
claimed in a draft pull request before the first scene.
