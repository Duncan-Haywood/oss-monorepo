# What held the US Catholic share up while retention fell?

Between the 1990s and 2010-18 the Catholic share of US adults slipped only
1.4 points (26.4% to 25.0%). Underneath, retention of those raised Catholic
fell from 77.7% to 66.7%, which alone would have cost 3.6 points
[-4.2, -3.0]. A larger share of adults raised Catholic (30.7% to 34.5%)
added back 2.6 points [+1.6, +3.6].

By 2021-24 that offset had stopped (-0.5 points since 2010-18, interval
-1.4 to +0.4) and the share was 22.2%. Web-mode data in 2021-24 may
understate retention, so the last step is the least firm.

The decomposition cannot say how much of the inflow is immigration and how
much fertility; that is the next milestone and the hypothesis (H4) stays
untested. Code, tables and the log are in `research/catholic-demography`
(milestone 5a), from the General Social Survey 1972-2024, design-based
bootstrap intervals.
