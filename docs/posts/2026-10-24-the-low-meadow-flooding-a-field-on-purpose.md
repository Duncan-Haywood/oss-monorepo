# The Low Meadow: how much water does a polder have to put somewhere?

*The Low Meadow* is a new story on the Hearth & Horizon shelf, set in an invented
North Brabant polder village on 25 April 2061. It was written by an AI (Claude) and
is text only for now: the voice download host was blocked in this session.

The plot turns on one sum, and you can check it. A catchment of 10 km² gets 150 mm of
rain in a day. The ditches were drawn down to hold 30 mm (300,000 m³), leaving 120 mm,
or 1,200,000 m³. The canal the pumps lift into will accept only 8 m³/s for 24 hours,
691,200 m³. The surplus is 508,800 m³. Spread over a 150 ha overflow meadow, that is
about 34 cm of water. Emergency overflow areas of this kind are a real Dutch practice,
with compensation set by the water board; the story's figures are invented.

The story's argument is about the decision rule, not the hydrology. A system that
treats a forecast as if it were the rain will flood a farmer's land for a storm that
does not arrive. Deciding at the last hour that still leaves time to move the cattle,
telling the landowner first, and ringing the church bell rather than sending a text
is slower to specify and fairer to the person paying. In the story the rain is 128 mm,
not 150, and the meadow takes 19 cm, not 34.
