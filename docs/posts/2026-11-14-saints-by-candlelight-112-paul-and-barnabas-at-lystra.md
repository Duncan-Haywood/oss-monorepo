**Why this one.** Episode 110 told Saint Thecla of Iconium from the
second-century *Acts of Paul and Thecla*, which is legend, not Scripture.
The series list asked for its Scripture companion: Acts chapter fourteen,
where Paul and Barnabas preach at Iconium, flee to Lystra, are taken for
Zeus and Hermes, and then are stoned. Telling the two side by side lets a
listener hear the difference between the canonical account and the later
romance set in the same town.

**What the episode is built on.** One chapter of Acts (13:51 to 14:28), with
cross-references Paul supplies himself: "once I was stoned" (2 Corinthians
11:25) and "what befell me at Antioch, at Iconium and at Lystra" (2 Timothy
3:11). Timothy of Lystra comes from Acts 16:1-3 and 2 Timothy 1:5.

**Choices a careful listener might notice:**

- **Did the apostles understand the crowd?** The shouting is "in
  Lycaonian" (14:11), and Paul and Barnabas only act "when they heard of
  it" (14:14). Many readers take that to mean someone had to translate. The
  episode tells it that way and says in the notes that it is a reading, not
  Luke's statement.
- **Why Zeus and Hermes?** Ovid's tale of Baucis and Philemon, where Jupiter
  and Mercury visit the Phrygian hills in disguise and punish the towns that
  shut their doors, is the usual background scholars cite, along with local
  inscriptions honouring the two gods together. The episode offers both as
  background and claims no more than that such a tale was told in that
  region.
- **The speech starts with the weather.** Acts 14:15-17 is one of the two
  speeches in Acts to people with no Jewish Scripture (Athens in chapter 17
  is the other). It appeals to rain, harvests and "food and gladness". The
  episode keeps every clause in a plain paraphrase and spends a scene on
  why it is gentle: it tells the crowd they were right to be grateful and
  wrong only about whom to thank.
- **The stoning is two sentences.** The series rule is no graphic suffering
  at bedtime. The weight goes on the circle of disciples standing round him
  and on Paul walking back *into* the town.
- **Fair to everyone.** The men who came from Antioch and Iconium are named
  as Acts names them. The episode also says plainly, before any conflict,
  that Paul, Barnabas and the first believers in every town were Jews
  themselves, and that this was an argument inside a family.
- **The turn back at Derbe.** From Derbe the road home through the Cilician
  Gates to Tarsus was short. Acts says they turned round instead and
  revisited every town that had thrown them out, to appoint elders and say
  "through many hardships we must enter the kingdom of God". The episode
  credits the geography point to "people who know the roads", because Luke
  does not make it.

**What is invented.** The fig-seller, where the healed man sat, the rented
room, the oxen keeping their garlands for the evening, the night farewell in
Lystra and most small talk. That Timothy stood in the circle outside the
gate is a guess, and the episode says so twice.

**Feast days.** Barnabas on 11 June and Paul (with Peter) on 29 June, in both
the Catholic and Orthodox calendars.
