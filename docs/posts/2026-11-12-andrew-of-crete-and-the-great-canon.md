The [episode 97 post](2026-11-11-romanos-the-melodist-and-the-scroll.md)

because he is usually named as the first great writer of the canon, the form
that took over from Romanos's kontakion.

## Explaining a canon to someone who has never heard one

The story only works if the listener knows what a canon is, so the episode
builds it from the start. For centuries the morning office had sung nine
biblical songs besides the psalms: Moses at the Red Sea, Moses in
Deuteronomy, Hannah, Habakkuk, Isaiah, Jonah, the three young men in the
furnace (counted twice), and the Magnificat with Zechariah's song. A canon
writes a new ode on each of those nine. The narrator calls it "a house with
nine rooms, where each room has an old window in it from the Bible."

Andrew's Great Canon runs to about 250 verses, against thirty or forty in an
ordinary canon. Almost all of it is spoken to his own soul as he walks
through Scripture: Adam, Cain, Lot's wife, Jacob's ladder, David after
Nathan. Then, in the New Testament verses, it turns towards people who came
home: Zacchaeus, the woman who washed the Lord's feet, the son in the
parable, the thief on the cross. Many of them already have Saints by
Candlelight episodes of their own, so a regular listener meets them again.

## The awkward fact, kept in

In 712 Andrew was one of the bishops who went along with the emperor
Philippikos's synod, which set aside the Sixth Ecumenical Council. That is
the same council whose acceptance by Jerusalem he had carried to
Constantinople as a young secretary in 685. After Philippikos fell in 713,
Andrew returned to the council's faith. Many lives of saints leave this out.
The episode tells it plainly, and it is explicit that nobody knows when the
canon was written. It does not claim he wrote a hymn of repentance because
of 712. It only notes that whoever wrote it knew what turning back means.

## History, legend, invention

- **History:** born in Damascus about 660, around when the city became the
  Umayyad capital. Secretary to Theodore, who was keeping the patriarch's
  throne at Jerusalem. Deacon of Hagia Sophia in charge of an orphanage and
  an old people's home. Archbishop of Gortyna. Died on Lesbos about 740.
  Feast day 4 July in both traditions.
- **The Life's report:** that he was mute until seven and first spoke after
  receiving Communion, and that a raid on a Cretan town was turned back at
  his prayers.
- **Invented, and the episode says so:** Leo, the orphan who becomes his
  scribe, and two cooks both called Anna. The canon's lines are given only
  in the episode's own plain paraphrase.

The ending ties back to [episode 20](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/020-mary-of-egypt-and-the-three-loaves.md).
On the Thursday of the fifth week of Lent the whole canon is sung, and the
Life of Mary of Egypt is read during the same service. Andrew grew up in the
city where she was converted, and now the two share a night every year.
