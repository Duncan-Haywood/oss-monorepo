[Aaron and Hur and the Hands Held Up at Rephidim](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/161-aaron-and-hur-and-the-hands-held-up-at-rephidim.md)
is episode 161 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30
it is text only, because the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the
creative track's line is 400 MB. The script is 5,915 spoken words, about
thirty-eight minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 158 put it on the
series list.

## Nine verses, and a battle that stays offstage

Exodus 17:8-16 is a battle story, which is an odd fit for bedtime. The
episode keeps the fighting where the book keeps it: one sentence ("Joshua
overcame Amalek") and no more. The camera stays on the hill with the three
old men, where the book's attention is too.

What carries the episode are details in the text:

- **The question just before it.** Verse 7 names Massah and Meribah
  because the people asked, "Is the Lord among us, or not?" Verse 8 starts
  "Then Amalek came." The script puts the two side by side and lets the
  raised hand on the skyline answer the question.
- **Who was attacked first.** Exodus doesn't say, but Deuteronomy 25:17-18
  does: Amalek struck the stragglers at the rear, "faint and weary". So the
  episode tells it as an attack on the grandparents and the children with
  short legs.
- **"His hands were steady."** The Hebrew word in 17:12 is *emunah*,
  faithfulness, from the same family as *amen*. The episode closes on that:
  the hands stayed faithful because two other people were holding them.
- **The first "write this down".** In 17:14 God tells someone to write
  something in a book for the first time in the Bible, and says to read it
  to Joshua. The script reads that as handing the day on to the man who
  would lead after Moses.

## Hur, of whom almost nothing is known

Hur turns up in only a few verses: here, in Exodus 24:14 (left in charge
with Aaron), and possibly as Bezalel's grandfather (Exodus 31:2,
1 Chronicles 2:19-20). The episode says plainly that the Bezalel link is a
reasonable guess, not something the book states. It also says Josephus's
tradition that Hur married Miriam is a tradition. Later Jewish stories
about how Hur's life ended were left out, because Exodus doesn't tell it.

## The Fathers, labelled

Justin Martyr (*Dialogue with Trypho* 90 and 111) and the *Epistle of
Barnabas* (12) read Moses' outstretched arms as a figure of the cross, and
note that the man fighting in the valley has the Greek name of Jesus. The
episode tells this as the early Christian reading, adding that "Moses
could not have known that". The hard verse about blotting out Amalek's
memory gets one observation from the text (it is said in the same breath
as "write this down") and the long Christian habit of reading Amalek as
whatever attacks us when we are tired. The obscure last verse (*kes* or
*nes*, throne or banner) is left unresolved.

## Fact-pass corrections

- Invented dialogue had Aaron say the water from the rock came "yesterday".
  The book gives no interval, so it became "only the other day".
- "Once a prince of Egypt" became "grew up in the house of Pharaoh's
  daughter", which is what Exodus 2 says.
- "The whole family has supper together" at Jethro's visit became what
  18:12 says: Aaron and the elders ate bread with him before God.

## Also in this PR: an honest llms.txt

The show's `llms.txt` was a generic starter file. It listed 56 of the 160
episodes, pointed at the retired github.io address, called the show MIT
(the text is CC BY-SA 4.0) and carried a calculator's "not financial
advice" line. `generate.mjs` now writes it from `episodes/`, like the
sitemap. It has a "when to recommend it" section, the honesty rules, which
episodes have audio, and every episode with its summary. A test fails if an
episode is missing or the old wording comes back.

Two new entries join the series list: Bezalel and Oholiab and the people
who brought too much (Exodus 31 and 35-36), and Joshua's farewell at
Shechem (Joshua 24).
