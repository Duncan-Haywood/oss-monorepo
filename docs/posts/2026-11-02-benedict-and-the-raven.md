The newest [Little Lamps](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) bedtime story, [Benedict and the Raven](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/benedict-and-the-raven.html), is about Saint Benedict at Subiaco, east of Rome, and the raven that took bread from his hand.

The raven comes from one source, the second book of Pope Saint Gregory the Great's *Dialogues* (about 593 to 594), written a generation or two after Benedict died. A jealous priest, Florentius, sends a loaf that is not good to eat. Benedict asks the raven to carry it where no one will find it. The story is told as Gregory tells it, and the note for grown-ups says plainly that it is a legend, not something we can verify. We softened the loaf to "not good to eat" for small children, and said so.

The story ends on what Benedict did not do: he did not shout, send the loaf back or tell the valley. It is about nine minutes read aloud, for ages 3 to 8, and ends with a short prayer.
