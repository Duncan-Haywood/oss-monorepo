# The back row and the front

A speaker rated 94 dB at one metre gives about 88 dB at the front row (2 m) and 69.9 dB at the
back (16 m), because level drops 20 log10 of the distance ratio. Against 60 dB of room noise the
back wants about 75. Raising the speaker by the missing 5.1 dB puts the front at 93.1 dB, and by
10 dB at 98, which on the usual workplace guide (85 dB for 8 hours, halved every 3 dB) is 1.2
hours and 24 minutes. A second speaker of 82 dB at one metre placed 2 m from the back row adds
76 dB there, so the back reads 76.9, and leaves the front unchanged at 88. Delayed by about 51
ms, it sounds as though the voice comes from the stage.
