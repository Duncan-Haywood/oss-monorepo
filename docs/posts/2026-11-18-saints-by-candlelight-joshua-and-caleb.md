[Joshua and Caleb and the Cluster of Grapes from Eshcol](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/142-joshua-and-caleb-and-the-cluster-of-grapes-from-eshcol.md)
is episode 142 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like episodes 30 to 141 it is text
only for now: the creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line.

Episode 141 ended in Numbers 11 and left Numbers 13 and 14 on the list as
a sequel. Those two chapters tell the spying of Canaan. The episode runs
to about 6,800 words, roughly forty-five minutes at the narrator's pace.

## What carries it

- **The picture.** Two men carry one bunch of grapes on a pole. It opens
  the episode, and it is the one invention the episode leans on: Scripture
  never says which two spies carried it. The narrator says out loud that
  making it Joshua and Caleb is a choice, "as long as we both remember that
  we made it up."
- **Report against story.** The ten spies start with true things (strong
  people, walled cities) and drift into "a land that eats its people",
  Nephilim, and "we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers, and so we seemed
  to them." The episode points out that they could not know the second
  half of that line. They decided what the giants thought of them from how
  small they felt.
- **Moses praying the Lord's own words back.** The intercession in
  Numbers 14 quotes Exodus 34:6-7 ("slow to anger, abounding in steadfast
  love"). The episode puts the two passages side by side, and the Lord
  answers "I have pardoned, according to your word."
- **The little ones.** The grown-ups say their children will become prey,
  and those children are the ones who go into the land. An invented boy on
  a rock at the edge of the camp, flagged as invented, gives the listener
  someone to follow across the forty years.

## The ending is forty-five years later

Numbers 14 ends badly. The ten spies die, and the people make a
presumptuous attack and are driven back to Hormah. Both are told in a few
quiet sentences, as the series bible asks. The wind-down then jumps to
Joshua 14. Caleb is eighty-five and "as strong today as on the day Moses
sent me", and he asks for Hebron, the town of the giants that frightened
the ten. The last story is Achsah's request in Joshua 15 and Judges 1:
"you have given me dry land; give me springs of water too." Her father
gives her the upper springs and the lower springs.

## What is hedged

- Joshua and Jesus being the same name through Greek (*Iesous*) is a fact
  of language, and the episode treats it as one.
- "Kenizzite" meaning that Caleb's family joined Judah from outside is
  given as a common reading, not a certainty.
- The old Christian reading of the cluster on the wooden pole as a figure
  of the cross is called a way of reading, not part of Numbers.
- Psalm 95 and Hebrews 3 and 4 are cited for the wilderness generation as
  a whole. A first draft tied the psalm to Kadesh, but its Massah and
  Meribah are a different place, and the draft was corrected before it
  was committed.
- Joshua's feast (1 September, Orthodox and the Roman Martyrology) is
  given. Caleb gets no feast day of his own, only the Orthodox Sunday of
  the Holy Forefathers, which remembers all the Old Testament righteous.

The adversarial re-read caught two other errors in the draft. The first
said that Jesus was given "the name Joshua's mother gave him", but she
named him Hoshea, and it was Moses who gave him the name Joshua. The
second put the spies' round trip from Kadesh to near Lebo-hamath at "two
or three hundred miles", when it is closer to five hundred. Both are fixed.

The list gains two new sequels: Othniel and Achsah and the springs, and
Moses on Mount Nebo.
