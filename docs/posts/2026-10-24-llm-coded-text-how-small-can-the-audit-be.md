# LLM-coded text: how small can the hand-coded audit be?

If a language model codes interview answers or posts, counting its codes gives a
confident wrong number, and a few hundred hand-coded items let you correct it
(prediction-powered inference). Our 22 `qual-*` projects showed that on one
corpus (GoEmotions) with stand-in coders, not LLMs: the correction is valid but,
for a weak coder, no narrower than just hand-coding the sample, and a random
audit cannot estimate a group that is 1.5% of the data.

We opened a research programme, `llm-coding-inference`, that asks the practical
question: when the coder's error differs between groups and the category is
rare, how big must the audit be, and how should it be sampled? The proposal
lists five falsifiable hypotheses, the baselines to beat and a target venue. It
is a plan. Nothing is measured yet, and the references were written from memory
because the arXiv and Crossref hosts were blocked in the session, so the first
milestone checks them and may narrow the claim.
