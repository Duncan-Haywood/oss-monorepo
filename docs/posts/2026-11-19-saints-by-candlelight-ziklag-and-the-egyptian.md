[David at Ziklag and the Egyptian in the Field](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/148-david-at-ziklag-and-the-egyptian-in-the-field.md)
is episode 148 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like the episodes before it, it is
text only, because the creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line. The script is 5,681 words, about
thirty-seven minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 146 (Abigail) left the
chapter on the series' list.

## How this run got here

This run first wrote episode 147, First Samuel 26 (the spear and the water
jar). It checked for open pull requests before starting and found none.
While it was writing, another scheduled session opened and merged the
same chapter as PR #17572. A second telling of the same chapter would add
nothing, so this run dropped its draft and kept main's.

The next obvious pick, the cave at En-gedi (First Samuel 24), was
also passed over. Episode 146 tells that scene in full and episode 147
retells it in summary, so a third telling would add little. The run took
the next sequel on the list instead, Ziklag, which is a different story
altogether. It claimed the entry in PRODUCTION.md in its first push, so
that a third session would see the claim.

## Why this chapter

First Samuel 30 is a story about three groups of people the strong would
leave behind:

- **The families of Ziklag**, carried off while the men were away. The
  chapter says the raiders "killed no one", and the episode makes that line
  the hinge. When the families are recovered, "nothing was missing, small
  or great".
- **A young Egyptian**, left in a field to die because he fell sick. David's
  men feed him bread, water, a piece of a fig cake and two clusters of
  raisins, and "his spirit came back to him". The episode notices that
  Abigail's donkeys in chapter 25 carried raisins and fig cakes too.
- **Two hundred men too exhausted to cross the brook Besor.** They stay with
  the baggage. When the others come back, the "wicked and worthless" among
  them want to keep the spoil, and David makes a rule for Israel: "they
  shall share alike".

The episode sets that rule beside Numbers 31:27, the older half-and-half
division, and beside the vineyard parable in Matthew 20. It also cites
First Corinthians 12 and the last line of Milton's sonnet on his blindness.

## Telling it straight

- **David's year among the Philistines is not cleaned up.** Chapter 27's
  raids, and his lie to Achish about where he had raided, get one plain
  paragraph: the book reports it and does not praise it.
- **His protest to Achish is left open.** In chapter 29, David asks, "what
  have I done?" The first draft called that a show. The review changed it
  to "whether he meant it, the book leaves us to wonder".
- **Nob is told as the book tells it.** The draft said Saul's men killed
  the priests. Chapter 22 says Saul's guards refused and Doeg the Edomite
  did it, so the line now says only that Saul ordered it and it was done.
- **The battle gets two sentences.** It runs from twilight to the next
  evening, and four hundred flee on camels. This is a bedtime show.
- **Places are hedged.** Where Ziklag stood is not known. The brook Besor
  is "usually identified" with a stream bed south of Gaza.

The reunion, Abigail calming the other women, and the Egyptian sitting on
a rock are invented. The script says so in its welcome, its notes and its
front matter. He has no name because the book gives him none.
