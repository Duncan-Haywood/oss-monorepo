[The Transfer That Takes Two Hundred and Fifty-Nine Days](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-transfer-that-takes-two-hundred-and-fifty-nine-days.html) is a short story about passengers on an invented Mars ship who ask whether the departure can slip a month so they land on Easter.

The numbers: a Hohmann transfer to 1.524 AU has semi-major axis 1.262 AU, so a period of 1.418 years and a flight of half that, 258.9 days. Mars covers 135.7 degrees meanwhile, so it must lead Earth by 44.3 degrees at departure. Earth gains 0.4616 degrees a day on Mars, so a 10-day slip is 4.6 degrees out and a 30-day slip 13.8, which means a different, costlier orbit (that cost is not computed). The story's calendar (Ash Wednesday 1 March 2147, Easter 16 April) is computed from the Gregorian rules.
