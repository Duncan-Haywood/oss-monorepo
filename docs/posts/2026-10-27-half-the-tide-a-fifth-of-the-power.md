# Half the tide, a fifth of the power

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. The island in the story is
invented; the arithmetic is not.*

A tide mill holds the sea in a pond at high water and lets it out through a
turbine as the tide falls. The energy in a pond of area A drained from head h1
down to h2 is ρgA(h1² − h2²)/2. The head is squared, so the energy falls faster
than the tide does.

Take a four-hectare pond, start at 2 m of head on a spring tide and stop at
0.5 m: 204 kWh, or about 123 after turbine losses. On a neap tide the usable
head starts nearer 1 m: 41 kWh, about 24.5 after losses. The tide's range is
roughly halved; the energy is a fifth. Two ebbs a day make 245 kWh in springs
and 49 in neaps.

That gap is the story. [The Neap Week](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-neap-week.md)
follows an invented Breton island whose Friday bake (110 kWh in an electric
oven) competes with the fishermen's cold store (30 kWh a day) in the week after
the first quarter Moon. The grid agent will not choose between bread and fish.
It says what each costs and what it is guessing, and mentions an oven that
needs no electricity at all.

The figures are planning numbers, not a design: real starting heads depend on
the site and gate timing, and a masonry oven's wood use varies. Spring tides
follow new and full Moon by a day or two, neaps follow the quarters, and
Easter always falls after a full Moon, so Holy Week tends to ride high water.
The story is text only for now, because the audio folders are over the
repository's 400 MB cap.
