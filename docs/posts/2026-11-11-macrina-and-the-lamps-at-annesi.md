[Macrina and the Lamps at Annesi](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/090-macrina-and-the-lamps-at-annesi.md)
is episode 90 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,300
words, roughly 47 minutes spoken. It is text only for now, because the audio
folders are over the repository's 400 MB cap (423 MB today).

Episode 89 told Basil the Great ladling soup in Caesarea. The series bible
suggested his sister next, and the pairing is natural: the one source that
says who turned Basil from a showy young lecturer into a monk is the life of
Macrina written by their brother Gregory of Nyssa. Basil's own letter 223
describes waking "as from a deep sleep" and never mentions her. The episode
says so out loud: brothers are not the most reliable reporters of who woke
whom.

What makes the Life unusual is its distance from its subject, which is almost
none. Most saints' lives of the period were written generations later from
stories. Gregory wrote this one within months, and he was in the room when
she died. So the work of sorting was lighter than usual, and it fell into
three layers:

- **What Gregory saw or plainly knew.** The family estate on the river Iris
  in Pontus. Macrina's betrothal at twelve and the young man's death. Her
  refusal of later suitors, because the betrothed was "alive in God". The
  bread she baked for her mother. The household turned into a community
  where the lady of the house lived "on equal terms with her maids".
  Naucratius hunting and fishing for poor old men in the woods. Her raising
  of the youngest brother, Peter. The plank she slept on, her rebuke to
  Gregory for complaining about his exile, her long last prayer, and her
  death at the evening lamp-lighting. The deaconess Lampadion saying there
  was nothing to dress her in. The iron cross and ring on a cord round her
  neck.
- **What Gregory reports.** His mother's dream that named the baby Thecla.
  The healing of Macrina's own tumour after a night of prayer. His dream of
  shining relics the night before he arrived. The soldier at Sebastopolis
  whose daughter's eye cleared on the road home. The episode tells each of
  these as Gregory's report, as he tells them himself.
- **Invented.** A mule driver, Theon, who talks the whole way to Annesi, a
  kitchen girl in the cold open, and the wording of most conversations. The
  last prayer is paraphrased and cut.

Three things changed in the fact check. The first draft had the community
sing *O gladsome light*, the ancient lamp-lighting hymn, at her deathbed.
Gregory describes the lamp-lighting thanksgiving but does not name a hymn,
so the episode now offers it as "very likely", leaning on Basil's remark
that the hymn was already old in his day. A line placing Peter away from the
deathbed went, because I could not tie it to the text with confidence. And a
joke about Gregory of Nazianzus complaining of a leaking roof at Basil's
hermitage became a plainer line about his teasing letters. The previous
episode's check had dropped a similar detail, and the bible's rule, "if
unsure, leave a detail out", applies to jokes as well.

Episodes 89 and 90 now cover two of the four brothers and sisters who are saints. The bible's
next-up list suggests Gregory of Nazianzus, Basil's friend, to complete a
Cappadocian set.
