[Bezalel and Oholiab and the Gifts That Were Too Many](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/167-bezalel-and-oholiab-and-the-gifts-that-were-too-many.md)
is episode 167 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30,
it is text only: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the creative
track's limit is 400 MB. The script is 8,440 spoken words, about
fifty-four minutes at the narrator's pace, close to the series' 8,500-word
ceiling. Episode 161 left it on the list: Hur on the hill at Rephidim, and
his grandson who made the ark.

## The building project that was oversubscribed

Exodus 36:5 is the verse the episode is built around. The craftsmen leave
"each from the task that he was doing" and tell Moses, "The people bring
much more than enough for doing the work that the Lord has commanded us to
do." Moses has it proclaimed through the camp: "Let no man or woman do
anything more for the contribution for the sanctuary" (36:6). The book adds
that "the people were restrained from bringing" (36:6-7). The episode tells
those words as the book gives them and invents nothing that changes them.

The book does not explain why the people gave so much, but where it puts
the scene says a lot. Four chapters earlier the same people handed Aaron
their gold earrings for the calf (32:2-4), and the gold was very likely
the jewellery the Egyptians gave them on the night they left (12:35-36).
The episode puts the two gifts side by side and says only that the book
lets you notice them. The narrator draws one bedtime line from it: the
same earrings, brought to a better place.

## Details kept because they are in the text

- **"Filled with the Spirit of God"** (31:3). As far as we can find, this
  is the first time Scripture says anyone was filled with the Spirit of
  God, and it is said of a metalworker and woodcarver. The script hedges
  the claim out loud. Exodus 28:3, about the garment makers, has a related
  but different phrase.
- **Teaching.** Exodus 35:34 says the Lord gave both men the gift of
  teaching others, so the workshop scenes show them teaching.
- **The mirrors.** Exodus 38:8: the bronze basin and its stand were made
  "from the mirrors of the ministering women who ministered in the entrance
  of the tent of meeting". The book does not say who these women were, and
  neither does the episode.
- **The inventory.** Exodus 38:21-31 gives the weights, counted by the
  Levites under Ithamar: twenty-nine talents and seven hundred and thirty
  shekels of gold, a hundred talents and 1,775 shekels of silver, and
  seventy talents and 2,400 shekels of bronze. They are read out in words.
  The silver came from the same half-shekel for rich and poor (30:15) and
  went into the hundred bases under the walls. The episode does not convert
  any of this into money, because the weight of a talent is uncertain.
- **The tachash.** The outer covering is "badgers' skins" in the King
  James Version, "goatskins" in some modern versions and "fine leather" or
  sea cows in others. The episode says nobody is sure.

## Labelled as tradition or reading

- **Judah and Dan.** Rashi on Exodus 35:34 says a man of the greatest
  tribe was paired with a man of one of the least so that the great and the
  small would stand equal. The episode tells this as a Jewish teacher's
  saying.
- **The house or the furniture.** In Berakhot 55a, Bezalel asks Moses
  whether the tent should not come before the ark. Moses answers with a
  pun on his name: "perhaps you were in the shadow of God (*be-tsel El*)".
  It is told as the rabbis' story. The episode then points out something
  that is in the text: Exodus 36 to 37 does tell the tent's making before
  the ark's.
- **The tent and the Word.** John 1:14 "dwelt among us" uses the Greek
  word for pitching a tent. Hebrews 8:5 and 9:1-5, and Wisdom 9:8, read the
  tent as a copy of heavenly things. These are presented as the Church's
  old reading.

## What is invented

Two characters, both flagged in the welcome, the notes and the `fiction`
field. One is Ziva of Naphtali, who gives her bronze mirror on the second
morning. The other is Ammi, a ten-year-old of Dan who sweeps Oholiab's
workshop and may not touch anything sharp, hot or gold. Nothing the book
gives Bezalel or Oholiab to say is changed, because it gives them no
words at all. Every word they speak in the episode is invented and
labelled as such. The closing prayer is Psalm 90:17, from the psalm
headed "a prayer of Moses, the man of God": "establish the work of our
hands".
