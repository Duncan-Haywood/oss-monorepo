[Bruno and the Seven Stars](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/bruno-and-the-seven-stars.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story runs about 1,600 words, around eight
minutes read aloud, then a short talk before sleep and a prayer. It is text
only: the creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over the track's
400 MB cap.

**The story.** Bruno is the most famous teacher at the cathedral school of
Reims, the sort of man people bow to in the street. One evening in a garden
he and two friends promise to leave all that behind for God, and of the
three, he is the one who keeps it. He and six companions walk south to
Grenoble, where Bishop Hugh has just dreamed of God building a house in a
lonely place and seven stars showing him the way. The children count the
travellers with him: seven. Hugh leads them up into the Chartreuse, where
they build little wooden houses round a spring and get up in the night to
sing by candlelight. Bruno sends the bishop, who loves visiting too much,
back down to his people. His old pupil, now the Pope, calls him to Rome; he
ends his life in the forests of Calabria, writing home that God writes on
the hearts of the brothers who cannot read. The last image is a child
counting stars up to seven.

**Why this saint.** The shelf's bible proposed him this morning, and no open
pull request had taken him. For Other Minds was the shelf due this session,
but an open pull request was already writing for it, so this run took the
next shelf on the list.

**What the sources carry, and what is ours.** The grown-ups' note sorts each
scene:

- **The garden is Bruno's own.** It comes from his letter to Raoul le Verd,
  written from Calabria, which recalls the vow made in the garden beside
  Adam's house and gently reminds Raoul he has not kept it. The dialogue is
  ours.
- **The dream is Guigo's.** The seven stars come from the Life of Saint Hugh
  written by Guigo, fifth prior of the Chartreuse, soon after Hugh's death in
  1132. The story says "Guigo wrote", and Hugh counting the men at the door
  is our dramatisation.
- **The letter about the lay brothers survives.** We paraphrase Bruno's
  line that God writes on their hearts with his finger, rather than quoting
  a translation.
- **One scene is hedged.** Bruno sending Hugh home is commonly told from
  Guigo's Life of Hugh, but this run could not reach the Latin (the two
  hosts it tried were blocked from the session), so the story says "people
  tell" and the note says why.

**Left out on purpose:** the late legend of a Paris doctor who spoke from
his coffin and frightened Bruno into the cloister. It is not in the early
sources, and the story keeps to Bruno's own letter. The Order's boast
"never reformed because never deformed" is a motto, and stays out too.

**Next on the shelf.** Frances of Rome is still waiting, and this run added

thirty relatives and friends; the proposal points at the scene where his
youngest brother says it is not a fair share to be left the earth while the
others take heaven.
