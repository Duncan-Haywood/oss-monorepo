Sound travels at about 331.3 m/s plus 0.6 m/s per degree Celsius. A flue pipe's note follows that speed, so a church tuned at 18 C and played at 5 C sits about 40 cents flat (A of 429.9 Hz instead of 440). A reed pipe's tongue sets its own pitch and barely moves. So the trumpet stop ends up about 40 cents sharp of the principals.

It gets worse: 260 people warm the gallery by a few degrees in an hour and a half, so the flues climb about 3 cents per degree (14 cents for a 4.6 degree rise) while the reeds stay put. The fix in the story is a bit of arithmetic: tune the reeds, and the strings, to the middle of the Mass (A of about 431.7 Hz). Nothing is then more than 7.2 cents off, at either end.

That is the new Hearth & Horizon story, *The Pitch of the Cold Church* (text only, since the audio budget is spent). The hamlet, people and the 4.6 degree rise are invented; the physics is standard and was recomputed with a script. It has not been checked against a real organ.
