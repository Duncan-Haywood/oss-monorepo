The newest [Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, [The Beans on Low](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-beans-on-low.html), is set in a County Cork parish hall in 2050. A cook has soaked 2.5 kg of dry red kidney beans and put them straight into three slow cookers for a Lenten chilli supper for sixty, as his late wife did.

The hall's assistant asks the one question that matters: were they ever boiled? Raw red kidney beans carry a lectin, phytohaemagglutinin. The FDA's food-hazard guide says four or five raw beans can make a person ill, and that beans heated only to about 80 °C have been found to be about five times more toxic than raw. The published rule (FDA, UK Food Standards Agency) is to soak, discard the water and boil at a full rolling boil for at least ten minutes. A slow cooker on low may not do that, because it spends hours climbing through the warm band.

In the story the probes read 93, 88 and 82 °C at two in the afternoon (invented), and the assistant will not call any of the pots safe, since a reading shows where the beans are, not where they have been. The cook boils all three batches on two gas rings, and supper goes out forty-five minutes late.
