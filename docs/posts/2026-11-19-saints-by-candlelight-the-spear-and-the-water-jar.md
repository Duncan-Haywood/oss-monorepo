[David and the Spear and the Water Jar by Saul's Head](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/147-david-and-the-spear-and-the-water-jar-by-sauls-head.md)
is episode 147 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only for now, like the
episodes before it: the creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line. The script is 7,255 words, about
forty-seven minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 146 (Abigail) left the
chapter on the series' list.

## Why this chapter

Episode 146 was built around a contrast. The man who would not raise his
hand against a king nearly wiped out a household over a rude reply at a
party. Chapter 26 is the other half of that contrast. This time nobody
has to stop David. He stops his nephew Abishai, who whispers over the
sleeping king, "one stroke, I won't need a second". David names three ways
Saul might die: the Lord strikes him, his day comes, or he falls in
battle. The only way he rules out is doing it himself. The episode's wind-down
follows the third way to Gilboa, and then to David's lament, "beloved and
lovely", which he wrote for the man who had thrown spears at him.

It is also the last time the two men speak. The chapter ends with Saul's
blessing, "blessed are you, my son David", called across a valley at dawn.
The episode says so plainly. The book never brings them together again.

## Telling it straight

- **The dialogue is the chapter's own**, in a plain rendering: Abishai's
  offer, David's refusal, the taunt to Abner ("where is the king's
  spear?"), Saul's "is this your voice, my son David?", the flea and the
  partridge, "I have played the fool", and the spear handed back.
- **Only what the text supports is given as fact.** The deep sleep "from
  the Lord" uses the same Hebrew word as Adam's sleep in Genesis 2 and
  Abraham's in Genesis 15. The jar uses the same word, and the same "by his
  head", as Elijah's jar in First Kings 19. Two things are labelled as less
  than certain. One is the pun some commentators hear in the partridge (the
  word may mean "the caller", and David is calling from a hilltop). The
  other is the guess that this was the same spear Saul once threw.
- **The jar is left where the text leaves it.** David gives back the spear.
  The chapter never says what happened to the water jar, and the episode
  doesn't decide either.
- **The father is described, not quoted.** Saint John Chrysostom's Three
  Homilies on David and Saul are real. The episode summarises them as
  praise for a victory over anger and says the summary is the narrator's.
  An early draft went further and had Chrysostom rank the night above the
  giant. That ranking came out on review, because the script could not
  back it with a quotation.

Two smaller facts were corrected in the same review. The elders of all
Israel anointed David at Hebron, not in Jerusalem (Second Samuel 5:3).
Saul threw his spear at David "more than once", which is safer than
"twice", because First Samuel 18:11 already has David dodging it twice.

The walk across the valley, the snoring soldier, the sentries' morning and
the narrator's guesses about Ahimelech the Hittite are invented. The
script says so in its welcome, its notes and its front matter. It closes
on Psalm 3, "I lay down and slept; I woke again", the first of the Six
Psalms at Orthodox Matins.
