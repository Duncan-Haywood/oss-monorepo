The new Hearth & Horizon story, *The Plate on the Door Pillar*, starts from a number that sits on every van and is rarely read: the payload. A seventeen-seat minibus plated at 3,500 kg and weighing 2,550 kg empty can carry 950 kg of people and bags. That is 56 kg a seat, less than most adults, so a full van is over before anyone brings a suitcase.

The story's choir of fifteen, at an assumed 78 kg each plus 14 kg bags, comes to 1,380 kg: 45 per cent over the payload, 12 per cent over the plated gross. The more interesting sum is the way down. Dropping 740 m with 3,930 kg is 28.5 MJ, 3.1 MJ more than at the plated mass, and an 8 per cent grade pulls with about 3,080 N, or 34 kW at 40 km/h for fourteen minutes. If the brakes took it all, 28 kg of discs would need to rise by about 2,200 K, which is why the rule is the driver's old one: down in the gear you went up in.

The fix in the story is dull and sound: eleven people with light bags (924 kg), the heavy luggage and four walkers by car, and a trip over a weighbridge to check the guess. The weighbridge reading, the disc temperatures and the parish are invented, and the physics ignores drag, cooling and engine braking, so it explains why the descent matters rather than predicting fade.
