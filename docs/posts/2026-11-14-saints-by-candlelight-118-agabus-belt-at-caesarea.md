[Agabus and the Belt in the House at Caesarea](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/118-agabus-and-the-belt-in-the-house-at-caesarea.md)
is episode 118 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,900
spoken words, roughly 50 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text
only, because the audio folders are over the creative prompt's 400 MB line
(474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** Episode 117 left Paul's ship sailing out of Miletus, and
the series list asked for the next stretch: Acts 21:1-16. In it Paul is
heading towards trouble he has been told is coming, and at every stop people
who love him beg him not to go.

**The line it is built around.** Acts 21:14: "And when he would not be
persuaded, we ceased, saying, The will of the Lord be done." The company
says it, not Paul. The episode treats this as the bravest sentence in the
chapter: they have done everything love can do to stop him, and then they
let him go.

**What the episode is careful about.**

- *The Spirit puzzle stays open.* In Acts 20:22 Paul goes to Jerusalem
  "bound in the Spirit". In 21:4 the disciples at Tyre tell him "through the
  Spirit" not to go. The episode gives the usual reading: the Spirit showed
  them the danger, and their love drew the conclusion. It names other
  readings too, and settles none of them.
- *Agabus's hard line, kept and framed.* "So shall the Jews at Jerusalem
  bind the man that owneth this girdle." The episode keeps the line as it
  stands. It then says plainly that Agabus, Paul, Philip and most of that
  room were Jews, so this was one family warning another, and that in the
  end it was the Romans who put the chains on Paul (21:33).
- *No words for the daughters.* Luke says Philip's four daughters
  prophesied, and he records nothing they said. The episode invents their
  household jobs and says so. It gives them no prophecies. What became of
  them comes from Polycrates, Proclus and Papias, quoted by Eusebius
  (*Church History* 3.31 and 3.39), and these writers seem to confuse Philip
  the evangelist with Philip the apostle. The episode says so.
- *Background labelled by source.* The fallen Colossus comes from Pliny
  (*Natural History* 34.18: few people could get their arms round its
  thumb). The popular picture of it standing astride the harbour is called a
  later mistake. The smell of the dye-works at Tyre is from Strabo
  (*Geography* 16.2.23). Saint Nicholas's birth at Patara is labelled
  tradition, and Mnason's village on the road is in Codex Bezae only.

**A pattern, offered as ours.** Agabus's first prophecy, of the famine
under Claudius (Acts 11:28-30), ended with a collection carried to
Jerusalem "by the hands of Barnabas and Saul". His second prophecy finds Paul
carrying another collection to Jerusalem. Luke does not point this out, and
the episode says the observation is its own.

**Invented, and said so.** Elissa is a dyer's daughter of Tyre who leads
the travellers to the church and gives Luke a murex shell on the beach.
Paul thinking of Barnabas as Cyprus passes on the left is invented too.
None of it changes what Luke reports.
