The hood is where hoodie patterns go wrong at home: it is cut by eye and then
does not meet the neckline. The Clothing Design Studio's pattern page now drafts
a hoodie, and the hood's neck edge is not eyed. It is made as long as the body's
front neck plus back neck, one side, and the page prints both numbers so you can
see they agree (for a size M, 23.13 cm each).

The draft is the crewneck body and sleeve, a two-piece hood (cut two, one
reversed) and a kangaroo pocket wider at the bottom with slanted hand openings.
The face opening carries a deeper allowance for a drawcord casing. Pieces come
as 1:1 SVG, a DXF for a plotter, and a tiled PDF for a home printer, and nest
onto the cloth: four size M hoodies on 150 cm cloth need 5.76 m nested against
6.17 m by rectangles.

What it is not: the ratios are typical drafting proportions typed from practice,
not checked against a real hood block or a sewn sample, and there is no rib at
the hem or cuffs. Cut a test garment first. Next up is a real hoodie measured
against the pieces.
