The newest [Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, *The Sack by the Lychgate*, is set in a Cumbrian churchyard in January 2054. A volunteer wants to empty a 25 kg sack of rock salt on a 60 m² path before a minus six night. The parish assistant does the sum: salt lowers the freezing point by about 1.86 degrees per mole of dissolved particles per kilogram of water, so holding a 0.5 mm film at minus six takes about 43 g a square metre, 2.6 kg for the path, while the sack is 417 g a square metre. Ice three millimetres thick needs six times as much, which is a job for a spade, not salt.

The point of the story is the unknown: nobody has measured the ice. So the fix is a freezer bag, a dustpan and a kitchen scale: scrape 0.1 m², weigh it, and the weight says whether to salt or chip. The parish, people and readings are invented, the end note lists every figure and its limits (real brines depart from the ideal formula, and salt's effect on stone is from memory), and a real path deserves real advice.
