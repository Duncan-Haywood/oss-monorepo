Shift Roster Builder now has a page for small shops. It takes an invented
four-person boutique with 19 floor-shifts a week (one person on the floor on
weekdays, two on Thursday and Friday late-night shopping, three on Saturday)
and shows the builder's real output.

The roster covers 17 of the 19 for $1,445 with no overtime. The two it cannot
fill are the second person on each late-night evening. On Thursday the people
who could work are already on another shift; on Friday one more would break
the 10-hour rest rule. Turning the rest rule off covers 18 of 19 for $1,496,
which shows what that cover costs: someone closing late and opening early.

The point for a shop owner is that the gap shows up as a hiring or opening-hours
decision before the week is posted. The page's numbers are pinned by a test
that re-runs the builder on the same inputs, so they can't drift from the tool.
The example is invented, and the builder is a planning aid: it does not model
local scheduling-notice, break or minor-labor rules.

The page is at `ventures/shift-roster-builder/retail-staff-schedule.html`, and
the tool is free and runs in the browser.
