[Moses and Aaron and the Bricks Without Straw](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/159-moses-and-aaron-and-the-bricks-without-straw.md)
is episode 159 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, like every episode
since number 30: the creative audio folders hold 577 MB, over the creative
track's 400 MB line. The script is about 6,600 spoken words, around
forty-two minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 158 put it on the series
list this morning.

## A bedtime story where things get worse

Most episodes of the show end well inside their own chapter. This one
can't. Exodus 5 is the chapter where obeying God makes everything worse
straight away: Pharaoh's "who is the Lord?", the order to stop supplying
straw while keeping the brick count the same, the foremen beaten for the
shortfall, and their curse on Moses and Aaron at the palace gate. The
script says so at the start ("tonight is the evening where everything gets
worse first") and doesn't rush the reader out of the hard part, because the
chapter doesn't.

For bedtime, the beating gets one quiet sentence, the house rule for
violence. The weight goes to three things the text itself puts there:

- **Moses' prayer of complaint** (5:22-23). "Why did you ever send me?"
  stays in the Bible unedited, and the Lord answers without rebuking him.
  The episode puts it next to the lament psalms (13, 44) and leaves it
  there: you may say this to God.
- **"I have heard the groaning"** (6:5), and the promises in 6:6-8, from
  "I am the Lord" to "I am the Lord" with five "I will"s in between. The
  episode mentions the Jewish custom of linking the Passover cups to these
  promises. It describes the custom and doesn't explain it.
- **"Because of their broken spirit and their cruel slavery"** (6:9).
  The book doesn't blame the people for not listening. The script points
  out that the Hebrew is close to "shortness of breath", and that this was
  Pharaoh's aim in his own words: keep them busy "so they pay no attention
  to lies" (5:9).

## Seeing it from the brickfield

The book names none of the Israelite foremen, so the episode invents one,
Hanan, and his ten-year-old daughter. It flags both in the welcome, the
notes and the `fiction` field. They add nothing to what Moses, Aaron,
Pharaoh or the Lord say or do. Their job is to stand where 5:14 and 6:9
stand: Hanan bows his head at the first gathering, is beaten for the count,
can't let the message in the second time, and still carries it home in his
head the way a foreman carries the day's count. His daughter counts the
promises: "That's four." The episode ends on that count, and the stars.

## What was checked

- **Brickmaking.** The script describes Nile mud, straw trodden in to stop
  the bricks cracking as they dry, open wooden moulds and sun drying, and
  points to the brickmaking paintings in the tomb of the vizier Rekhmire at
  Thebes (TT100). It says plainly that the painting shows Egyptian
  brickmaking, not the Israelites.
- **Words.** Pharaoh's "you make them rest" (5:5) uses the same root as
  *sabbath*. The episode ties that to Deuteronomy 5:15, which gives the
  sabbath's reason as "you were a slave in Egypt". 6:12's "uncircumcised
  lips" becomes "clumsy lips" in the plain rendering, and the script says
  the Hebrew is stranger.
- **Dates and names.** As in earlier Exodus episodes, the Pharaoh isn't
  named and no date is given, since the book gives neither. The ages come
  from Exodus 7:7, the four hundred and thirty years and the "night of
  watching" from 12:40-42, the basket from Psalm 81, and Stephen's
  retelling from Acts 7:34-36.

The closing prayer is a plain rendering of Psalm 81: "your hands were set
free from the basket". The episode adds one new line to the series list:
Aaron's staff in Pharaoh's court and the start of the plagues (Exodus 6:28
to 7:13).
