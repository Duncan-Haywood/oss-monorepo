[Hugh and the Swan of Stow](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/hugh-and-the-swan-of-stow.html) is a nine-minute bedtime story for ages 3 to 8 about Saint Hugh of Lincoln, whose feast is kept in England on 17 November.

Most saints' animal stories on this shelf (Kevin's blackbird, Brendan's whale) were written down centuries after the saint died, so the story says "the old story says" and the notes call them legend. Hugh's swan is different. It comes from the *Magna Vita Sancti Hugonis* by Adam of Eynsham, Hugh's own chaplain, who says he saw the bird himself: a big swan at the bishop's manor at Stow that drove off the other swans and hissed at the servants, but took bread from Hugh's hand, tucked its head into his sleeve and guarded him while he rested.

So this story can say more plainly what happened, while the grown-ups' note still lists what we added: the kettle hiss, the wobbly walk up the path, and one line of dialogue marked "perhaps". Adam reads the friendship as a sign of God's favour, and the story gives that as his reading. We left out the episode Adam links with Hugh's death, because it is too sad for bedtime, and we cut two details we could not check against the text before publishing.
