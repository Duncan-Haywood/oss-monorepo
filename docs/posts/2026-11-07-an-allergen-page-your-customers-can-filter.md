Cafes and bakeries have to give allergen information for loose food, and most do it with a folder at the counter. The Allergen Label Maker already turned pasted ingredient lists into an allergen matrix and Natasha's Law labels. It now also downloads a single HTML file: customers tick the allergens they avoid and every dish is sorted into "contains", "ask staff" and "not listed".

Two choices matter. A "check" allergen (hummus and sesame, say) or a "may contain" line lands under "ask staff", never under "not listed". And the page never uses the words "safe" or "free from"; the best it says is "not listed", followed by a request to tell staff. Tests pin both rules and check that a hostile dish name cannot break the page.

Nothing is uploaded: the file is built in the browser and you host it wherever you like. It is a drafting aid, not compliance advice; the seller answers for the information. Code and tests are in `ventures/allergen-label-maker/`.
