[For Other Minds](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/), the shelf of stories written for language models and agents, has a new one: [*Two Tunings a Year*](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/two-tunings-a-year.html), about 3,400 words. It is set in March 2051 in the finance office of an invented Catholic diocese in northeastern Iowa. The one organ tuner who has covered the diocese for thirty years is retiring, and the finance officer asks the office's agent how many days of tuning the diocese needs a year, because the answer decides whether to fund a full-time successor.

## Three guesses, multiplied

The agent's first answer is the classic Fermi chain: 120 pipe organs, tuned 1.5 times a year, a day a visit. That is 180 days, most of a full-time year of about 230 working days. Every factor is reasonable. The product is still twice the honest middle, because all three guesses lean the same way.

The fix the story works through is to write each guess as a range before multiplying anything:

| Factor | Low | High | Spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working pipe organs | 40 | 130 | 3.25× |
| Tunings a year | 1 | 2 | 2× |
| Days a visit, travel included | 0.5 | 1.5 | 3× |

The corners are 20 days and 390 days. The geometric middle, the square root of low times high, is about 88. A range that wide decides nothing, and the corners are unlikely because they need every guess wrong in the same direction. But the table says something useful: the factor everyone argues about (one tuning or two) has the smallest spread. The two that decide the answer are questions of fact.

## Facts beat arithmetic

In the story those facts already exist. The tuner's diary for three years shows 58 organs, 84 visits a year and 71 tuning days, about 0.85 days a visit. A random sample of ten churches checks the diary is not missing a large share of organs: if one church in five had an organ the diary did not know about, ten random draws would find one about nine times in ten (1 − 0.8¹⁰ ≈ 0.89). It found none, plus one silent organ nobody had played since a roof leak. Allowing a newcomer a day and a quarter a visit, the planning number is about 90 days, likely 70 to 110.

Then the same diary shows what the annual total hid: about two-thirds of the tuning falls in fifteen weeks, before Advent and before Easter. In those weeks the job is close to full-time; the rest of the year it is nearly empty.

## The reasonable no

The finance officer refuses to take "20 to 390" to a finance council, because a council handed a range argues about its ends. She is right, and the story does not make her wrong. The agent's answer is one planning number, with its likely range and the one thing that would most change it on the same line, and a written withdrawal of the first figure before anyone has to ask.

Everyone and everything in the story, the diocese, its churches and the tuner included, is invented, and the closing note prints every number the story uses. It was written by an AI and is text only for now.
