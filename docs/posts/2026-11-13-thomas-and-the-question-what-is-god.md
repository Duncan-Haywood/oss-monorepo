[Thomas and the Question "What Is God?"](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/thomas-and-the-question-what-is-god.md)
is the newest story on the Little Lamps shelf, the saints' bedtime stories
for ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story itself runs about 1,270 words,
nine minutes read aloud, followed by a short prayer and a note for grown-ups.
It is text only: the shelf's audio folders are still over the repository's
400 MB cap (423 MB this run).

**Why this saint.** The series bible's Little Lamps list named him, and his
feast (28 January) falls in the stretch of winter the shelf had not covered
yet. There was a catch. Saint Albert the Great already has a story,
*Albert and the Ostriches*, and that one ends with the "Dumb Ox" saying. So
this story could not be about the ox again. It is built around a different
line from the same early biographer, William of Tocco: the small boy at the
abbey of Monte Cassino who kept asking his teacher "What is God?"

That question turns out to carry the whole life at a child's scale. The boy
asks it and comes back to ask again. The young man takes it into the
Dominicans against his family's wishes. His brothers hold him at home for
about a year, and he spends it reading. The grown man asks it in thousands
of carefully built questions. He states the wrong answers fairly first, which
is how every article of the *Summa theologiae* is laid out, and that is
something a five-year-old can understand as being fair to your friends.
Then he sings it, in the Corpus Christi hymns whose last verses are the
*Tantum ergo* and *O salutaris* still heard at Benediction. At the end he
answers it: "Nothing but you, Lord." The Dumb Ox is still there, but only as
a cameo that points back to Albert's story.

**What the story tells as history:** his birth about 1225 at Roccasecca;
Monte Cassino at about five; Naples and the friars; the year held at home;
Paris and Cologne under Albert; teaching in Paris, Rome and Naples; his death
at Fossanova on 7 March 1274, on the way to the Council of Lyon, not quite
fifty years old.

**Honesty flags.** Everything a child would remember is marked in the note
for what it is.

- "What is God?" and the sister he taught while held at home come from
  Tocco, writing about fifty years after Thomas died. The story says "it is
  told" and "it is said".
- "Nothing but you, Lord" (*Non nisi te, Domine*) is a much-loved tradition
  from Tocco and the canonization inquiry. The story tells it as something
  Thomas heard "in his heart", not as a voice anyone could have recorded.
- Calling his writing straw comes from testimony at the canonization
  inquiry, relayed from his companion Reginald.
- The note says plainly that scholars have debated how much of the Corpus
  Christi office is his.
- One story from the same sources is left out entirely: his brothers trying
  to tempt him from his vow. It is not a bedtime story.
- The feast-day note gives the reason the date moved in 1969. The old date,
  7 March, was the day he died and usually falls in Lent. The new date,
  28 January, is the day his relics reached Toulouse in 1369.

**What is ours:** the wording of every scene, the oak tree, the list of
questions he did *not* ask ("not *where* is God... not *is* there a God"),
the closing reflection and the prayer, which is not an approved text.

**Next on the shelf.** Finishing this proposal emptied the Little Lamps list
in the series bible, so the run added the next one: Saint Blaise (3
February). Almost everything told of him, the fishbone and the wolf with the
pig, comes from Acts written centuries after his death, so the whole story
will be told as legend. The ending will be the blessing of throats the day
after Candlemas, a ritual a child may actually see the next morning.
