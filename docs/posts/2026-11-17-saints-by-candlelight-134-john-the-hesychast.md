[John the Hesychast and the Bishop Who Carried Stones](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/134-john-the-hesychast-and-the-bishop-who-carried-stones.md)
is episode 134 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script runs
about 7,100 words, roughly 45 minutes at the narrator's pace. Like episodes
30 to 133 it is text only for now: the audio folders hold 577 MB against the
creative prompt's 400 MB line.

**Why this one.** The last six episodes followed the pilgrim Egeria, and the
series list suggested more of Cyril of Scythopolis's desert lives next.
Episode 104 told Saint Sabbas's life from Cyril. John the Hesychast was one of
Sabbas's monks, so this episode goes back to the same gorge and the same
author. `creative_due.py` drew this shelf, and no open pull request was
writing an episode for it.

**Why John is unusual among old saints' lives.** Most lives of fifth- and
sixth-century monks were written down generations later. Cyril wrote John's
while John was still alive, at over a hundred, in a cell at the Great Lavra.
Cyril had gone to him for guidance as a young monk and says he learned much
of what he wrote from him. So the story stands closer to its subject than
most in the series, and the episode leans on that: its last scenes are the
old man at his window and the young Cyril outside it.

**The story in brief.** John was made bishop of Colonia in Armenia at about
twenty-eight, against his wishes. After about nine years he went to
Constantinople on church business, then quietly took ship for Jerusalem
instead of going home. At Sabbas's lavra he started as a newcomer, carrying
water and stones and cooking for the builders of a guesthouse. Later he
served as steward and then lived alone in a cell. When Sabbas brought him to
the patriarch Elias to be ordained a priest, John asked to speak to the
patriarch alone and told him the truth. Elias told Sabbas only that John
could not be ordained and should be left in peace. Sabbas feared the worst,
prayed, and (Cyril says) was shown that John was a bishop.

**Honesty calls.**

- *Death date.* An earlier draft said Cyril gives John's death at 104. He
  cannot: his Life ends while John is alive. The episode now says Cyril
  gives his great age, and that the date of his death, about 558, comes from
  later records.
- *Left out on purpose.* The Life has more wonders from John's years in the
  desert of Rouba. They are skipped rather than half-remembered: the series
  rule is to leave out a detail the episode can't stand behind. So are the
  name of the governor who troubled John's church and the details of that
  quarrel.
- *A bishop leaving his see.* A listener could fairly ask whether that was
  allowed. The script answers in a few plain sentences. It was not
  encouraged, but it happened, and some of the men who did it are saints.
  John never denied what he had been, and he told the truth to the person
  who needed to hear it.
- *Psalm numbers.* The closing blessing is from "God is our refuge and
  strength" ("Be still, and know that I am God"). The script names the psalm
  by its first line, because it is 46 in the Hebrew numbering and 45 in the
  Greek and Latin.
- *Invented.* The young monk Domnus, a donkey called Patience, the lentils
  and nearly every line of dialogue are invented. That includes Sabbas's
  joke when he finds out, that he is "not giving you back the stores". The
  welcome, the notes and the `fiction` field all say so.

**Feasts.** The Orthodox Church keeps John the Silent on 3 December, two days
before Sabbas. The Roman Martyrology remembers him on 13 May.
