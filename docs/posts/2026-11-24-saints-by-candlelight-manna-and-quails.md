[The Manna and the Quails in the Wilderness of Sin](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/173-the-manna-and-the-quails-in-the-wilderness-of-sin.md)
is episode 173 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, like every episode
since number 30: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the creative
track's limit is 400 MB. The script is about 7,500 spoken words, roughly
forty-eight minutes. Episode 172 left it on the list.

## A chapter that is already a bedtime story

Exodus 16 has a shape a child can follow: hungry people grumble, God
doesn't scold them, meat arrives at evening and bread in the morning, and
the rules that come with the bread turn out to be the point. Each rule
gets a scene:

- **An omer for each person.** "He that gathered much had nothing over,
  and he that gathered little had no lack" (16:18). In the episode an
  invented girl, Ziva, gathers for an old neighbour who walks with two
  sticks, and both bowls measure exactly. The script then follows the
  verse to Saint Paul, who quotes it in 2 Corinthians 8:15 when he asks one
  church to give to another so "that there may be equality".
- **Keep none till morning.** Some kept it and "it bred worms and became
  foul" (16:20). Ziva's four-year-old brother hides a handful in his
  blanket, and his mother tells him: there'll be more every morning,
  that's the whole point.
- **It melted when the sun grew hot.** Wisdom 16:28 reads this as a lesson:
  "we must rise before the sun to give you thanks".
- **Twice as much on the sixth day, and none on the seventh.** This is the
  first place the word *sabbath* appears in the Bible. The episode treats
  the first sabbath as a gift ("See! The Lord has given you the sabbath",
  16:29) and shows a family that has never had a day off, not sure what to
  do with one.
- **One jar kept.** It is the only manna that never spoiled, kept so the
  grandchildren could see it, and remembered in Hebrews 9:4.

Two details got a narrator's aside. The name *manna* comes from the
people's question *man hu*, "what is it?", so for forty years their word
for breakfast was a question. And the chapter's last verse is a unit
conversion: "an omer is the tenth part of an ephah."

## Natural explanations, and whose miracle it is

The series bible says miracles are God's, and the quails make that
concrete: migrating quail really do cross Sinai in exhausted, low-flying
flocks. The script says so and adds that God can use his own wind and his
own birds at the hour he promised. It does the same for the sweet
secretions that travellers have seen on desert shrubs. It names them as a
suggestion, then lists what they can't explain: enough for everyone, every
morning for forty years, exactly an omer each, a double portion on the
sixth day that didn't spoil, and none on the seventh.

## Readings, labelled

The arc runs from the manna to the bread of life: Deuteronomy 8:3 ("man
does not live by bread alone"), Jesus quoting it in Matthew 4:4, Psalm 78's
"bread of the angels", and John 6:31-35. Saint Ambrose (*On the Mysteries*
8) and Saint Thomas Aquinas's *Lauda Sion* appear as named readings of the
manna as a figure of the Eucharist. The Eucharist is common ground for
Catholics and Orthodox, so the episode doesn't touch anything the two
dispute. The episode ends with the Our Father, because "give us this day
our daily bread" is the manna rule in one line.

## What it leaves
