# Anthony and the fish that listened

The Hearth & Horizon shelf has grown fast this week, mostly science fiction with arithmetic in it. This
run went to the quieter shelf instead: [Anthony and the Sermon to the Fishes](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/anthony-and-the-sermon-to-the-fishes.md),
a Little Lamps story for ages 3 to 8, about eight minutes aloud.

Saint Anthony of Padua (about 1195 to 13 June 1231) was a Lisbon-born canon who became a Franciscan,
was blown to Italy by a storm he had not planned for, and became one of the best-known preachers of
his century. He was canonised under a year after he died. The story keeps to that outline.

The fish are the legend. The Rimini sermon appears in later collections such as the *Fioretti*, not in
the earliest Life, so the story says plainly that it is a tale the old tellers loved and not something
we can check, and the grown-ups' note says the same with the sources. The scenes, the speech and the
prayer are ours, and the prayer is not an approved text.
