The newest Hearth & Horizon story, *The Freezer That Keeps a Day*, is about a village food pantry in Moray after a storm takes the power. The parish assistant will not say the 200 kg of meat is safe. It estimates the margin two ways instead.

The first is the heat the food can absorb: 200 kg at about 2 kJ/kg/K warming 17 degrees is 6,800 kJ, or 1.9 kWh. At roughly 1 kWh a day of heat leaking into a freezer in a cold hall, that is about 45 hours. The second is the thermometer on a string: minus 18 to minus 9 in 16 hours is 0.56 degrees an hour, which reaches minus 1 at about 46 hours. The two agree, which makes the assistant trust the figure a little more, and it says only a little. Every input is a guess, and thawing makes temperature a poor guide near zero.

The power was promised back for the same hour the margin ends, so the pantry splits the stock: half to a neighbour's generator freezer, half handed out while still hard in the middle. Guidance on refreezing is quoted from memory and the story says to check a current leaflet.
