[John of the Cross and the Songs in the Dark](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/john-of-the-cross-and-the-songs-in-the-dark.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. It runs about 1,510 words, roughly ten minutes
read aloud, and is text only, because the shelves' audio folders are over the
creative track's 400 MB cap (529 MB this run). His feast is 14 December, four
weeks away, so it is up in time for Advent.

**The story.** A weaver's son in a village on the plains of Castile loses his
father as a baby and grows up hungry. In Medina del Campo he is apprenticed to
one trade after another and is no good at any of them, until a hospital for
the very poor takes him on and it turns out what he is good at is looking
after people. He becomes a very small, very quiet Carmelite friar. Teresa
writes that he is small in size but great in the sight of God, and he starts
the first reformed friars' house in a farm building too low to stand up in.
Then comes the one hard scene: friars who opposed the reform shut him in a
dark cell in Toledo for nine months. He makes songs in his head, a kind
guard brings him paper, and one summer night he climbs out of a window on a
rope of knotted blanket strips. The ending is quiet: his letter's line "where
there is no love, put love", and the bell at midnight in Úbeda.

**The two traditions the bible flagged.** The proposal for this story named
two things to source or leave out:

- **The pond.** The small boy who falls into a pond and will not take a
  beautiful lady's hand because his own is muddy comes from the
  seventeenth-century lives. The retellings differ on who pulled him out (a
  farmworker with a goad, an old man with a staff), and nothing from his own
  time records it. It stays in, because it fits the boy, but the story tells
  it as "an old story... so we cannot be sure exactly how it happened", and
  the note for grown-ups says the same.
- **"A friar and a half."** Teresa's joke that with Antonio de Heredia and
  John she had a friar and a half turned up in no source this run could
  check, so it is left out. Her letter of 1568, saying he was small in
  stature but great in the sight of God, is well attested, and the story uses
  that instead, paraphrased.

**One line removed on the re-read.** The first draft had "a grown-up friend
said he hardly came up to her shoulder". Nobody is on record saying that, and
the bible forbids putting invented words in a real person's mouth, even
harmless ones. It now just says he was not even five feet tall.

**One saying held loosely.** "Where there is no love, put love, and you will
draw out love" is genuinely his, from a letter of 1591 to a Carmelite nun at
Segovia. The editions number and date the letters differently, so the note
names the year and the recipient's house, not the letter number. For small
listeners it ends "find love".

**What was softened.** The Toledo imprisonment included harsh treatment. The
story says only that the room was small, cold and nearly dark, that the
friars "were wrong to do that", and that he never spoke unkindly of them
afterwards, which every early life reports.

**Next on the shelf.** The run proposed Saint Francis de Sales (feast 24
January): the young priest who slipped leaflets under the doors of people who
would not come to hear him preach. The proposal lists the traditions about him
that need a source before a story can use them.
