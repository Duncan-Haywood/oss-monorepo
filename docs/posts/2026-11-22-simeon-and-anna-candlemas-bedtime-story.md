## A bedtime prayer that was already there

Most Little Lamps stories end with a short prayer we wrote, labelled as not an
approved text. This one has a better ending ready made. Simeon's words when he
takes the child, "Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace"
(Luke 2:29-32), are the Nunc dimittis. It is the Gospel canticle of Night
Prayer, said every night of the year by people who pray the Liturgy of the
Hours. So the story tells children something true and useful: the prayer the
old man said at the end of his long wait is the one the Church says at the end
of every day. The closing prayer we wrote echoes it, and the note points to the
real one.

The story's theme is waiting. It opens by asking a child what they have waited
for, a birthday or Christmas or a granny at the window. Then it gives them two
people who waited longer than anyone they know.

## What Luke does not say

The passage is only nineteen verses, and much of what people "know" about it is
not in them. The note for grown-ups marks each piece:

- **Luke never says Simeon was old.** The tradition draws it from his words
  about being let go in peace. The story gives him white hair and a stick and
  says in the note that those are ours.
- **Anna's age reads two ways.** The Greek of Luke 2:37 can mean she was now
  eighty-four, or that she had been a widow for eighty-four years, which would
  put her past a hundred. The story says "a very, very old lady" and leaves the
  number to the note.
- **The doves were the poor family's offering.** That comes from reading Luke
  2:24 against Leviticus 12:8, which allows two birds "if she cannot afford a
  lamb". Luke does not say it outright.
- **The candles are later.** The oldest account of the feast, by the pilgrim
  Egeria in Jerusalem in the 380s, describes the procession and the sermons on
  Luke 2. It does not mention candles. The story says only that people carry
  candles today, and why: Simeon called the child "a light for revelation to
  the Gentiles".

Simeon's words to Mary about a sword piercing her soul (2:35) are kept, but
gently: her heart would hurt one day "because she loved him so". The Cross is
left to the note.
