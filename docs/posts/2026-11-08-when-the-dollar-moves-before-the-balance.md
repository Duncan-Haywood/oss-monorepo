title: "What if the exchange rate moves between the deposit and the balance?"
date: 2026-11-08

description: "A new calculator shows what a factory currency move does to cost and profit, and the move that wipes the profit out."
---

A designer pays a factory 30% up front and the rest weeks later, in dollars, while selling in euros. Take a 10,000 USD order, a 0.92 EUR per USD deposit rate, 2,000 EUR of freight and duty, and 20,000 EUR of expected revenue. At today's rate the cost is 11,200 EUR and the profit 8,800. If the dollar is 10% stronger when the balance falls due, the 7,000 USD balance costs 7,084 instead of 6,440, so the profit drops by 644 EUR.

The new [factory currency risk page](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/fx.html) tabulates moves from -10% to +20%, the move at which profit reaches zero (here a 136.6% rise, so currency is not the main risk on those numbers), the price rise per piece that would cover each move, and a typed forward quote compared with paying at spot.

Limits: moves are scenarios you pick, not a forecast. There is no rate feed, forward terms are typed in, and revenue is assumed to be in your own currency. The tests are hand sums; no real designer's order has been run through it.
