[Ninian and the White House at Whithorn](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/088-ninian-and-the-white-house-at-whithorn.md)
is episode 88 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,200
words, roughly 46 minutes spoken. For now it is text only, because the audio
folders are over the repository's 400 MB cap.

Ninian was the next saint on the series bible's "next up" list, which asks
for more saints' lives after a long run of Scripture episodes. He is an
unusual case: the earliest named saint in what is now Scotland, and one of
the most thinly documented. The episode says so in so many words and builds
the telling around the gap.

Here are the layers it keeps apart, oldest first:

- **Bede (731):** a single paragraph. A British bishop called Nynia was
  taught at Rome, built a stone church "not usual among the Britons" that
  people called the White House, had a see named after Saint Martin, and
  converted the southern Picts. Bede marks all of this as what he had been
  told.
- **Ailred of Rievaulx (about 1160):** the long Life, written seven centuries
  later from an older book that is now lost. The royal birth, the masons
  from Tours, King Tuduvallus, the leeks in the newly sown garden, the
  psalter that stays dry until Ninian's mind wanders, the cattle inside a
  line drawn with his staff, and the runaway boy whose staff becomes a tree
  are all told as Ailred's report. The bull is softened for bedtime, and the
  episode says so.
- **Things we can see:** the Latinus stone, which dates from the middle of
  the fifth century and reads "We praise you, Lord", and the Whithorn
  excavations of 1984 to 1991. Both show Christians on the hill early on,
  but neither proves Ninian's church. The episode's closing prayer is the
  stone's.
- **Local tradition:** that Ninian prayed in the cave on the shore at
  Physgill.
- **A live scholarly argument:** Thomas Clancy's 2001 suggestion that
  "Nynia" began as a copyist's misreading of the British name Uinniau. It is
  given as one scholar's view.
- **Invented:** Brother Elffin, the gardener's protests, and the breakfast
  for the thieves.

While checking the draft, one detail came out. The first version said the
excavations had found white lime plaster. I could not confirm that against a
source I trust, so I cut it, following the bible's rule: "if unsure, leave a
detail out".
