The newest [Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, [The Ones Their Friends Name](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-ones-their-friends-name.html), is set on the Isle of Barra in November 2051. A winter booster lands on the beach airport at Tràigh Mhòr: 440 doses for about 1,200 people, with the rest promised in three weeks if the weather allows. The island's nurse gives 280 of them to the frail first, by the national order. The story keeps that rule. The island's agent wanted to give every dose by contacts, and she tells it no, because the risk to the frail is measured and the network is only a guess. The question is what to do with the 160 left over.

The agent's answer is the friendship paradox (Scott Feld, 1991): on average, your friends have more friends than you do. A person with k contacts gets named k times as often, so the mean contacts of a named friend is the mean of k² divided by the mean of k. In the story's invented contact model (500 people with 4 close contacts a week, 400 with 8, 200 with 15, 80 with 30 and 20 with 60), a random islander has about 9.8 contacts and a named friend about 18.9. The method is Cohen, Havlin and ben-Avraham's acquaintance immunisation (2003): ask people at random to name someone, and dose the person they name. Averaged over 400 simulated networks, the 160 doses cover:

| How the 160 are chosen | Contacts per dose | Share of all contacts covered (with the rule's 280) |
|---|---|---|
| Lottery among the undosed | about 11.0 | about 29 in 100 |
| The register's next-of-kin lists | about 5.6 | about 21 in 100 |
| A friend named by a random islander | about 18.2 | about 38 in 100 |

The failed first try is the middle row. Next-of-kin forms record who people live with, not who they see, and the old name each other. That makes the list worse than drawing names from a hat, and private besides. Writing the scene where islanders fill in cards also caught a slip in the first draft. With 640 cards back, someone with 60 contacts should be named about three times (640 × 60 / 11,800), not forty, so the story's tallies now come from the model.

The story never turns contacts covered into lives saved, because nobody knows the extra numbers that conversion would need.
