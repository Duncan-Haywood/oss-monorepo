Hearth & Horizon has a new story, *Sixteen Sunrises and One Bell*, set in 2099 on an invented ring station 420 km up. A newcomer wants to pray Lauds at dawn and finds the station has about fifteen and a half of them a day.

The numbers are the plot. The orbit takes 92.8 minutes, the Earth's shadow covers 38.7 per cent of it (36 minutes of night when the orbit crosses the shadow squarely), and the sunrise pattern slides 47.6 minutes against the clock each day before nearly repeating every second day. The crew settle on the ship's clock for the Hours and let the next sunrise be a sign rather than a timer. The odds of a sunrise landing inside a three-minute Benedictus come to 3.2 per cent, one morning in thirty-one.

Honesty flags: station, crew and assistant are invented; the shadow is treated as a sharp edge and the zero-beta case is the one worked; how a station should keep the liturgical day is left to a bishop. Text only, because the audio folders are over the 400 MB line.
