Hearth & Horizon has a new story in the twenty-years-ahead strand, *The
Condition in the Trust*, about 2,700 words, text only for now. A retired
driving instructor leaves his first grandchild $100,000 "on the day that
grandchild holds a valid driver's licence". In November 2046 the trust is
worth $219,112 (4 percent a year for twenty years: 1.04^20 is 2.19), she is
sixteen, and nobody she knows drives.

The story's bet is that adoption, not capability, sets the pace. The AI in it
is the kind we have now, a bit better each year and no faster, and the
interesting 2046 is what is allowed: an invented licence class for automated
cars only, bought in three weeks on a simulator, meets the words of the trust
and betrays the man. The family's assistant lays out three roads (the ordinary
licence, the automated-only one, or asking a court to modify the condition
under Uniform Trust Code section 412), gives its own odds for the court, and
declines to choose for a girl who is in the room.

What is real: section 412 and Ohio's adoption of the Code, and the long fall in
the share of teenagers who hold licences. What is invented: the licence class,
the insurance prices and the odds, which the story labels as a guess. It is
fiction, not legal advice.
