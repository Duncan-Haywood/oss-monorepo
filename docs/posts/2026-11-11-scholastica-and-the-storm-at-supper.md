[Scholastica and the Storm at Supper](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/scholastica-and-the-storm-at-supper.html) is a nine-minute bedtime story for ages 3 to 8 about Saint Scholastica, sister of Saint Benedict. Her feast day is 10 February.

The source is two short chapters in the second book of Pope Gregory the Great's *Dialogues*, written about fifty years after her death. Gregory says he had his account of Benedict from four of Benedict's disciples. The scene is very simple. Brother and sister meet once a year, at a house just below the monastery. At nightfall she asks him to stay and talk about heaven until morning. He says he cannot sleep outside the monastery. She lays her head on her folded hands to pray. When she lifts it, lightning and rain come out of a cloudless sky, and nobody can leave. "I asked you and you would not listen; I asked my God and He listened."

Unlike most legends on this shelf, the miracle comes from a near-contemporary written source, so the story tells it as Gregory tells it. The two spoken lines are close paraphrases of his text, and the story says they come from "the old book". We added no other speech. Gregory's own verdict, "she could do more, because she loved more", is the hinge of the bedtime reflection: you don't win by being loudest, you fold your hands and ask.

Two choices for small listeners. We dropped Gregory's detail that she wept as she prayed. We kept her death, which comes three days later, but told it gently: Benedict sees a dove rising, and she is buried in the tomb he had made for himself. The note for grown-ups lists what is Gregory's and what is ours (the candle, the window, the thunder noises, the prayer). It also flags the popular claim that the two were twins as later tradition, not Gregory.
