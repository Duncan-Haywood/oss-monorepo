[Francis Xavier and the Little Bell](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/francis-xavier-and-the-little-bell.html) is an eight-minute bedtime story for ages 3 to 8, in time for his feast on 3 December.

Most of it is history told in outline: the castle in Navarre, the room in Paris shared with Peter Faber and Ignatius of Loyola, the year-long voyage, and his death off the coast of China. Its centre is a scene the early Lives remember and his own letters support. He walked the streets of Goa ringing a small bell to call the children in, taught them the prayers by repetition, and sent them home to teach their families. So the children who learned also became teachers, and the story ends by asking the child to do the same with a brother, a sister or a grandparent.

The crab that came out of the sea carrying his lost crucifix is told aloud as a story, not as fact. The note for grown-ups says where it comes from: testimony given decades later for his canonization. It also names what was left out, including the colonial setting of the Portuguese missions, which older children can talk about.
