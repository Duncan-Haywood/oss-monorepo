Total screen time is barely growing. What is changing is where it goes. Put the main 2025-26 measurement reports side by side and four patterns stand out: short vertical video takes the biggest share, YouTube is taking over the living-room TV, serialised "microdramas" are pulling ahead of streamers on phones, and AI chatbots are the fastest-growing kind of screen time.

A note on sourcing first. We read these figures through coverage of the reports (Sensor Tower's *State of Mobile 2026*, DataReportal's *Digital 2026*, Nielsen's *The Gauge*, Pew Research Center's December 2025 teen survey), not the paywalled originals, and the coverage does not always agree. Each figure below names its source, and where the sources disagree we say so.

## How much time

Estimates of daily phone time run from about **3.6 hours** (one summary of Sensor Tower's 2025 data) to **4.5 hours** (DataReportal's 2026 figure, as quoted by Countly). The vendors measure different things: app sessions against all phone use, and different countries and platforms. So read the range as "three and a half to four and a half hours", not as a precise number.

The split is easier to trust. Sensor Tower puts social and messaging apps at **more than 60% of all time spent in mobile apps** in 2025: about 2.5 trillion hours, or over 90 minutes per user per day, up about 5% on 2024.

## Which apps

**TikTok** ranked first worldwide in 2025 for total time spent, downloads and in-app revenue (Sensor Tower). DataReportal puts the average Android user at about **1 hour 37 minutes a day** in the app.

**YouTube** gets slightly less time per user on Android (about 1 hour 25 minutes, DataReportal) but the largest total share of social-video time, because so many more people use it. Its bigger story is on the TV. Nielsen's Gauge, which counts only TV screens, gave YouTube a record **15.8% of all US TV viewing in August 2026**, up from 12.7% in January. That is far ahead of Disney (9.3%) and Netflix (7.7%). Streaming as a whole reached 48.5% of US TV viewing in June. Nielsen changes its method from September 2026 on, so compare later months with care.

**ChatGPT** grew fastest. Sensor Tower reports its time spent up **426%** in 2025, enough to make it the second most-downloaded app worldwide. Sensor Tower forecast about 43 billion hours across generative-AI apps for 2025. That is small next to the 2.5 trillion hours on social apps, but no other category grew at triple-digit rates.

**Teens.** Pew's December 2025 survey of 1,453 US 13- to 17-year-olds found about three-quarters use YouTube daily. Roughly one in five say they are on YouTube "almost constantly", and about the same share say so for TikTok. News reports of the exact YouTube figure vary. About a third of teens say they are almost constantly on at least one of the five big platforms, a share Pew says has held steady for several years. In the same survey, Black and Hispanic teens were more likely to report almost-constant use.

## What kind of content

1. **Short vertical video is the default format.** One industry roundup estimates short-form video takes 58% of social media time. Reels reportedly make up about half of time on Instagram, up from 37% in 2024. In China, short video against long-form online video runs about 8 to 2 by time spent. These are the softest numbers in this post, because they come from marketing roundups, but they all point the same way.
2. **Microdramas are the surprise.** Apps such as ReelShort sell soap-opera serials in one- to two-minute vertical episodes. Sensor Tower counts 5.78 billion extra hours on these apps in 2025 and about $2.98 billion in in-app revenue. Its Q4 2025 data, and separately Omdia, find that US users now spend more daily mobile viewing time per user in microdrama apps than in Netflix, Disney+ or Prime Video. That comparison covers phones only, where the streamers are weakest.
3. **Creator video is replacing TV programming.** YouTube's share of the TV screen means podcasts, commentary and long creator videos are now watched where network shows used to be.
4. **Conversational AI is a new kind of screen time.** A chat is interactive and comes when the user asks, unlike a feed that keeps serving the next item. Its growth rate stands out even though its total is still small.

## What it adds up to

Total time is moving slowly, so most of the change is a reshuffle. Time is shifting from posts by friends to recommendation feeds, from TV networks to YouTube creators on the same TV, from full-length shows to tiny serial episodes, and, at the edges, from scrolling to chatting.

## What we could not pin down

- **A common basis.** No single source measures social, video, microdrama and AI time the same way. Sensor Tower covers apps on phones, Nielsen covers TV screens in the US, and Pew records what teens say about themselves. The shares above cannot be added together.
- **AI's share of total time.** We found growth rates and a forecast, but no measured share of daily time.
- **Primary figures.** Several of the numbers come through secondary coverage. Anyone citing them should check the original reports.
