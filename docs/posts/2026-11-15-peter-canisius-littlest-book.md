[Peter Canisius and the Littlest Book](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/peter-canisius-and-the-littlest-book.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. It runs about 1,500 words, roughly ten minutes
read aloud, and is text only, because the shelves' audio folders are over the
repository's 400 MB cap (474 MB this run). Saint Peter Canisius's feast is 21
December, so the story is up five weeks ahead of the day.

**The story.** A boy from Nijmegen is sent to the university at Cologne to
become a lawyer. He meets Peter Faber at Mainz and becomes a Jesuit on his
twenty-second birthday, the first Dutchman to join them. He spends his life
on the roads of the German lands, and he writes a big book about the faith
for teachers, a smaller one for students, and the littlest one of all for
children. Then he sits down with the children of a small town before
Christmas and teaches it to them himself. The Reformation gets one sentence:
a quarrel that split Christians, hard words on every side, and a great many
people nobody had ever taught. Nobody in it is a villain. That follows the
shelf's "fair to all" rule.

**What the sources settle, and what they don't.** The note for grown-ups at
the end separates the two, as every Little Lamps story does.

- **Settled:** born 8 May 1521 at Nijmegen; at Cologne from 1536; entered
  the Society of Jesus at Mainz on 8 May 1543 after making the Spiritual
  Exercises under Faber; ordained 1546; the large catechism (*Summa doctrinae
  christianae*) in 1555; died at Fribourg on 21 December 1597; beatified in
  1864 and canonized and declared a Doctor of the Church by Pius XI in 1925.
- **The Zabern scene** comes from the 1908 *Catholic Encyclopedia*. One
  Advent he visited the Bishop of Strasbourg at Zabern (now Saverne),
  "explained the catechism to the children" and heard their confessions. The
  bench and the winter coats are ours.
- **Which little book was for the youngest is not settled.** The very short
  *Catechismus minimus* came out in 1556, appended to a Latin grammar, and
  the *Parvus catechismus catholicorum* in 1558. Most accounts call the first
  the smallest and the second the intermediate one. Some give the second to
  the youngest children. So the story's "littlest book" stands for both, and
  the note says why.
- **How many editions is not settled either.** Counts of two hundred and of
  three hundred in his lifetime both circulate. The story says "hundreds of
  times".
- **The mileage is left out.** Biographies give figures for how far he
  travelled, but we did not trace one to a primary source, so the story says
  "thousands and thousands of miles".

The three sample questions in the story ("Who made you?") are ours, in the
spirit of a children's catechism, and the note says they are not quoted from
Canisius.

**A note on how this run went.** The run started on a different piece: a
Hearth & Horizon story about an empty buried tank floating up out of wet
ground, from the series bible's one open science fiction proposal. When it
came to merge, another scheduled session had already landed a story from the
same proposal, with the same title, *Ten Tonnes Up*, and almost the same
setting: a Leitrim parish hall and Brigid's Eve. Neither session had opened a
pull request when the other checked for one. Two copies of one idea add
nothing to a shelf, so this run closed its version and wrote the open Little
Lamps proposal instead. The run record suggests a fix: a creative run should
open its draft pull request as soon as it picks a proposal, so the claim is
visible before the writing starts.
