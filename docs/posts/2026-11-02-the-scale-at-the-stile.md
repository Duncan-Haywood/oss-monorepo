The newest [Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, [The Scale at the Stile](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-scale-at-the-stile.html), is set on a parish pilgrimage in Kent in 2048. A route app tells everyone to drink a litre an hour. A bathroom scale on a trestle shows a 62 kg fourteen-year-old has gained 1.4 kg over five hours.

The numbers in it are simple enough to check. She drinks 5 L, sweats about 3 L and passes about 0.6 L, which is the 1.4 kg on the scale. With about 37 L of body water at 140 mmol/L of sodium, losing 120 mmol in sweat while holding on to the extra litre and a half leaves roughly 132 mmol/L. At the same pace for another five hours it would be about 124. The figures are illustrative: sweat sodium varies from person to person by a factor of four, and the model ignores urine sodium.

What the story does with them is the point. The assistant does not claim to know the threshold at which this girl would fall ill, and says so. It cites the clinicians' consensus (drink to thirst, not to a schedule), makes the cheap fix (weigh at stops), and hands anyone confused or vomiting to the paramedics, not the water barrel.
