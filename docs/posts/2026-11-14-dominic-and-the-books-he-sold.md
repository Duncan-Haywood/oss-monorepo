[Dominic and the Books He Sold](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/dominic-and-the-books-he-sold.md)
is the newest story on the Little Lamps shelf, the saints' bedtime stories
for ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story runs about 1,240 words, nine
minutes read aloud, followed by a short prayer and a note for grown-ups. It
is text only: the shelf's audio folders are over the repository's 400 MB cap
(474 MB this run).

**Why this saint.** The series bible's Little Lamps list named him next, and
yesterday's story, about Thomas Aquinas, already had the white-and-black
friars in it without ever saying who began them. Dominic is often pictured
with a Rosary, a dog and a star, and the story leaves the first out (see
below).

**The two scenes it is built on.** Both come from the earliest sources, and
both work at a child's scale:

- **The books.** Jordan of Saxony, who knew Dominic and succeeded him as
  Master of the Order, writes that the student at Palencia sold his books,
  annotated in his own hand, to feed people in a famine. A brother at the
  1233 canonization inquiry in Bologna remembered the reason Dominic gave:
  he would not study on dead skins while men were dying of hunger. The story
  spends a page on why that cost so much. Every book was copied by hand on
  parchment, and these had years of his own notes in their margins. That is
  the part a child can feel: giving away the thing you love most, because
  someone is hungry.
- **The innkeeper.** On a royal embassy in 1203, Dominic's host at Toulouse
  followed the Cathar teaching that the material world is evil. Jordan says
  Dominic talked with him through the night and won him back. The story
  makes the method the point. He sat down, listened, and explained slowly
  and kindly that God made everything good. The ending ties it back to his
  mother's dream of a dog carrying a torch: he carried a light by sitting at
  a table, not by shouting.

**Honesty flags.** The note for grown-ups marks each of these:

- The dream of the dog with the torch is Jordan's report of a saint's
  mother's dream. The story says "it is told".
- The "dead skins" saying is a remembered saying from testimony, given in
  substance. The story softens "men" to "people".
- The Rosary given by Our Lady to Dominic is a fifteenth-century tradition,
  chiefly from Alan de la Roche, and is not in the early sources. It is
  in the note, not the story, with the reason the two are so often joined.
- "Seed piled in a heap goes bad, seed scattered grows" is a traditional
  saying attached to the 1217 dispersal of the brothers. It is given as a
  saying, not a quotation.
- The feast date: he died on 6 August, which is the Transfiguration, so his
  feast was kept on 4 August from 1558. The 1969 calendar moved it to
  8 August.

**What is ours:** the wording of every scene, the students' question, the
book "as costly as a house" (a fair picture of what a manuscript cost, not a
figure from the sources), the passage on what he told people, the closing
reflection and the prayer, which is not an approved text.
