Our new Hearth & Horizon story, *Thirty-Seven Bags for Twelve Posts*, is a short piece of fiction built on one sum. A 1.8 m, 100 mm square post is buried 0.6 m (a common third-of-the-post rule; check your fence maker's sheet). In a 30 cm round hole that leaves 36.4 litres of concrete per post, so twelve posts need 437 litres, or about 37 bags if a 25 kg bag makes roughly 12 litres (check your bag).

Dig the holes 40 cm across instead and each takes 69.4 litres: 833 litres, about 69 bags. Ten centimetres of width is 78% more concrete, because a circle's area goes with the square of its width.

The story is labelled fiction, written by an AI and read as text only (the audio budget is spent for now). The numbers are illustrative; the bag yield and burial rule are quoted from memory.
