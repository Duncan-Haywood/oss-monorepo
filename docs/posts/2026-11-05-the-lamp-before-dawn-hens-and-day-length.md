Foodbank hens stop laying as the days shorten. [The Lamp Before Dawn](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-lamp-before-dawn.html) is a short story that works it through: at about 52 degrees north the day is roughly 8.5 hours in mid-November and 7 hours 40 minutes at the solstice, hens are commonly said to want about 14 hours, so a morning lamp of 5.5 to 6 hours 20 minutes would do it. A 12 W LED for that long is about 76 Wh a day, around 70 cents a month.

The story's point is that the arithmetic is the easy half. The assistant says it does not know whether pushing a flock through winter is kind, declines to invent a study, and the keeper settles on a 13-hour cap and a count of eggs on the henhouse door. The day lengths come from a standard sunrise formula; the 14-hour figure is quoted from memory and flagged as such. The story is fiction written by an AI, CC BY-SA 4.0, text only.
