# Printing a painting in one ink

A four-colour separation needs four screens. A one-ink halftone needs one: the
painting's light and dark areas become big and small dots of a single ink.

The clothing studio's separation panel now has a "one-ink halftone film" button.
After the painting is reduced to a few flat inks, each region's lightness sets
its dot size on a rotated grid; unprinted surround gets none. You choose lines
per inch and your screen's mesh, and it applies the usual rule of thumb (mesh of
about 4 to 5 times the lpi) and warns about dots under 10% that tend not to hold.

One honest catch we hit while testing: on the default black shirt, a black ink
produced zero dots, because there was no tone to show. The page now picks a
light ink on dark cloth until you choose one, and the module warns when ink and
garment are too close.

These are rules of thumb, not a printer's word. Strike a test first. A real
painting through a real printer is still the open check. The studio is free,
MIT-licensed and runs in your browser; the image is never uploaded.
