[Bernard and the Fair Share](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/bernard-and-the-fair-share.md)
is the newest bedtime story on Little Lamps, the shelf of saints' stories for
ages 3 to 8. An AI wrote it. The story runs about 1,300 words, around eight
minutes read aloud, then a short talk before sleep and a prayer. It is text
only: the creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over the track's
400 MB cap.

**The story.** Seven children grow up in a castle in Burgundy, and the
children count them. Bernard, the third, decides to join Cîteaux, a poor new
monastery among the reeds that hardly anyone wants to join, and talks his
brothers, his uncle and his friends into coming too, so well that (his
biographers wrote) mothers hid their sons when he came to town. On the day
they leave, the eldest tells little Nivard, playing in the street, that all
the land will now be his, and Nivard answers that they are taking heaven and
leaving him the earth, which is not a fair share. About thirty of them
arrive at Cîteaux's gate. Bernard founds Clairvaux in the Valley of
Wormwood, rides a whole day beside the Lake of Geneva without noticing it,
and in the end Nivard and their old father both knock at his door. The last
image is a star, from Bernard's sermon about Mary as the star of the sea.

**Why this saint.** The shelf's bible proposed him this morning, and the
session's shelf picker (`platform/ci/creative_due.py`) drew Little Lamps.
No open pull request had taken him.

**What the sources carry, and what is ours.** The grown-ups' note sorts each
scene:

- **Nivard's answer** is in the *Vita prima*, the first Life of Bernard,
  and was retold in the *Golden Legend* and by Butler. This session's network
  could not reach the Latin, so the words in the story follow the common
  English renderings and the note says so.
- **The hidden sons** come from the early Life as older reference works
  summarise it, told as what his biographers wrote.
- **The lake** is from the *Vita prima*'s third book, by his secretary
  Geoffrey; the story adds that we should notice lakes, because delight in
  creation is good.
- **The star** paraphrases his second homily on the Annunciation, read in
  the Liturgy of the Hours.

**Left out on purpose:** the Second Crusade he preached, his quarrel with
Abelard and the papal schism belong to his life but not to a bedtime story,
and the note names them. The Memorare is often credited to him; it is not in
his works, so it stays out.
