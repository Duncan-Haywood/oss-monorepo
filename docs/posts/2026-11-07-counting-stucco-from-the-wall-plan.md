The wall-framing takeoff covered studs, sheathing and siding but had nothing for stucco, the usual exterior in much of the US South and West. `--stucco` now takes the exterior walls the siding counts (same height, gables, openings) and works out the barrier rolls, lath sheets and fasteners, plaster bags, and the metal trim: weep screed, casing bead round openings, corner bead and control joints.

On a 30 ft by 8 ft wall run with one 4 ft window the net area is 224 sq ft. That gives 16 lath sheets, 407 fasteners and 32 bags of plaster at a 7/8 in three-coat build with 15% rebound, plus one control joint because one wall is over 18 ft. The tests pin those numbers by hand arithmetic.

What it does not know: the coat thicknesses, panel limits, bag yield and rebound are typical figures you can overwrite, not a code lookup, and we have not compared it with a real plasterer's order. Flashing, floor-line joints, color coat and labor are left out. If you have a real stucco takeoff to check it against, that is the most useful thing to send.
