Little Lamps, the saints' bedtime shelf, has a new story: Saint Zita (about 1212 to 1272), a domestic servant in Lucca who served one household for most of her life and is the patron of domestic workers. About eight minutes aloud, ages 3 to 8; her feast day is 27 April.

The honesty problem here is the bread. The best-loved tale, that angels baked the loaves while she was at church, is a tradition told after her death, so the story says plainly that it is a legend and what it is for. The outline (service in the Fatinelli household, daily prayer, generosity to the poor, local veneration, cult confirmed in 1696) is kept to what the sources record. Her feast is not in the General Roman Calendar today; the grown-ups' note says so. The other miracle stories are left out and the prayer is not an approved text.
