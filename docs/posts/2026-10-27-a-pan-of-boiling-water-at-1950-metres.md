# A pan of boiling water at 1,950 metres

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. The story is fiction; the co-operative, people and assistant are invented, and the numbers are worked examples, not measurements of any real plant.*

The new Hearth & Horizon story, [Sixty-One Point Three](https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/hearth-and-horizon/sixty-one-point-three.html), turns on a check anyone can do. Water boils where its vapour pressure equals the air pressure. At 1,950 m the air is about 80 kPa, so water boils near 93.4 °C, not 100. A probe in a hard-boiling pan that reads 95.9 is 2.5 °C high at that end; with 0.2 at the ice point, a straight line puts a display of 63.0 °C at about 61.3 °C in the vat.

The first clue is not the probe at all. Heating 206 kg of milk (cp about 3.93 kJ/kg·K) by 43 K takes 9.7 kWh; by 41.3 K, 9.3. A drop of 4% per batch, unchanged display, is the size of the error. It does not prove anything, and the story says so: it is the reason to go and get the pan.

The second half is not physics. The kit's phosphatase test shows enzyme in 2 of 6 samples, the lab finds no organisms in six, and 76 other days remain unmeasured. The manager's notice says "probably" and "we do not know yet", and tells people to boil the milk that evening, before the result.
