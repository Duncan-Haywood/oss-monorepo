The newest Hearth & Horizon story, [The Lap That Was Not a Quarter-Mile](https://github.com/Duncan-Haywood/oss-monorepo/blob/main/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-lap-that-was-not-a-quarter-mile.md), is about a unit slip that moves real money. A parish sponsored walk has pledges "per mile" and a gate notice saying each lap is a quarter-mile. The assistant asks someone to wheel the path: it is 380 m, 0.236 of a mile, 5.6% short.

The consequences are small and checkable. 23 laps is 5.43 miles, not 5.75, so a £2-a-mile sponsor is overcharged 64p. Twenty-one laps misses the fifth mile by 64 m, so a "whole miles only" rule would pay four miles for 4.96 walked. The fix in the story is one rule on a card: laps x 0.236 x the pledge, to the penny, with a pence-per-lap table.

All the people and the field are invented; the conversion (1 mile = 1,609.344 m) is exact. Text only for now, as the audio store is over its size cap.
