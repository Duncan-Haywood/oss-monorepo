[Solomon and the Two Mothers Before the King](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/139-solomon-and-the-two-mothers-before-the-king.md)
is episode 139 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like episodes 30 to 138 it is text
only for now: the creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over the
track's 400 MB cap.

Episode 138, on the Temple's dedication, mentioned the dream at Gibeon and
then said the two mothers were "a story for another night". This is that
night. It tells First Kings 3 straight, in the episode's own plain wording,
with Second Chronicles 1 for the old tent of meeting and Bezalel's bronze
altar at Gibeon. It runs to about 6,300 words, roughly forty minutes at the
narrator's pace.

## The problem the episode sets up first

The judgment only seems clever once a listener sees how stuck the court
was, so before the women arrive the episode spends a scene on how justice
worked at the town gate. Deuteronomy tells judges to "hear the small and
the great alike" (1:17) and says a matter stands "on the word of two or
three witnesses" (19:15). Jethro tells Moses to keep the hard cases for
himself (Exodus 18). Absalom wins hearts by complaining that the king
hears nobody (Second Samuel 15).

Then the first woman says the line the story turns on: "There was no
stranger with us in the house." There is no witness and no test, only two
voices, each calling the other a liar. Gaddi, an invented apprentice
scribe, writes down "the first woman says" and "the second woman says",
then has nothing more to write.

## Three details the episode is built around

- **A listening heart.** At Gibeon Solomon asks for a *lev shomea*. Most
  English Bibles render it "an understanding heart", but *shomea* is the
  ordinary Hebrew word for hearing. The episode keeps the literal sense and
  ties it to the first thing Solomon does in the courtroom: he repeats both
  women's claims back to them, word for word (First Kings 3:23), before he
  says anything else.
- **"I do not know how to go out or come in."** The narrator calls it the
  new king's first-week-at-work prayer: "I do not know where the kettle is.
  Please help."
- **"Give her the living child."** In verse 27 the king repeats the true
  mother's own words, "give her the living child; do not kill him", but
  points them back at her.

## What the episode does not claim

Scripture never says what Solomon meant by calling for the sword, so the
episode doesn't say either. It is a bedtime show, though, so before the
order the narrator tells the listener that the baby will be safe. The
child who died gets one line, as in the text, and is left with God. The
women are called harlots, as the text calls them. The episode points out
that these were exactly the "small" whom the Law told judges to hear.
Solomon's turning away in old age (First Kings 11) gets two honest
sentences, and the episode ends with him young. The Book of Wisdom is said
to "speak in Solomon's voice", which is how it presents itself, and the
episode makes no claim about who wrote it down. The closing prayer comes
from Wisdom 9. The elder Hanan, his grandson Gaddi, the donkey that ate the
barley and the creeping boundary stone are all invented, and the episode
says so in the welcome and in the notes.

The series list gains two sequels: Jethro and the judges for the small
cases, and Nathan's parable of the ewe lamb. The Queen of Sheba is still
waiting.
