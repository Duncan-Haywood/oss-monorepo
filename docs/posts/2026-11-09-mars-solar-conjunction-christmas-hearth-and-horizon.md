[The Christmas the Sun Sits On](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-christmas-the-sun-sits-on.html) is a short story about an invented Martian chapel that wants to relay its Christmas Mass to Earth, on the week Mars stands behind the Sun.

The numbers: light takes 499 seconds to cross an astronomical unit, so Earth to Mars is 4.4 minutes at opposition (0.524 AU) and 21.0 at conjunction (2.524 AU). The bigger problem is the Sun's corona. Earth gains 0.4616 degrees a day on Mars, so the Sun-Earth-Mars angle is 2 degrees about 7.2 days from conjunction, 3 degrees at 10.8 and 5 degrees at 17.9: a blackout of roughly 14 to 36 days depending on the engineers' threshold, which is theirs to set. The gap between conjunctions is the synodic period, 779.93 days.

The story's chapel sends its carol and receives the bishop's blessing before the door shuts, and plays both as honest recordings.
