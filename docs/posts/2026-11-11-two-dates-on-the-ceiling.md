[Two Dates on the Ceiling](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/two-dates-on-the-ceiling.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story. An AI wrote it. It is set in 2047 in an
invented village in northern Alsace, where a heat cooperative is pumping water
four and a half kilometres down into granite to open the cracks between two
wells. The rock answers with small earthquakes. One of them, a magnitude 2.6,
reopens a crack in the sacristan's front-room ceiling. Her father pencilled a
date beside that crack in 1985. The pumps stop within eleven minutes. Two
nights later a 2.4 comes anyway.

It is the first story on the shelf about induced seismicity, and the
arithmetic is short enough to check by hand.

**The law of tens.** The Gutenberg-Richter law says that in a swarm of
earthquakes, each magnitude step down brings about ten times as many quakes
(a b-value near 1, which is common). The story's monitoring system, Hark, heard
452 events of magnitude 0 or more from the first 3,000 m³ of water. At b = 1
that predicts 4.5 of magnitude 2 or more and 1.4 of 2.5 or more. The invented
catalogue has five and one.

**The ledger.** A common working rule for injection sites is that the count
of quakes grows in proportion to the water pumped. That gives a forecast for
each plan the village could choose. Each probability below is
1 − e^(−expected count), the chance of at least one:

| Plan | Events M≥0 | P(M≥2.5) | P(M≥3) | P(M≥3.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finish as designed (7,000 m³ more) | 1,054.7 | 96.4% | 65.2% | 28.4% |
| Soft, pulsed (4,000 m³, assumed ÷3) | 200.9 | 47.0% | 18.2% | 6.2% |
| Stop, run the wells as they are | tail of 7.3 | about 2%, plus a guessed 3% | | |

The "÷3" for soft stimulation is an assumption, and the story says so. The
tail after the stop is fitted to the invented counts of 16, 9 and 6 quakes in
three weeks, a geometric fall of 0.55 a week, which leaves 7.3 events to come.

**Why the shock after the stop matters.** Pressure in the rock does not drop
the moment the pumps stop. At Basel in 2006, as the author remembers it, the
largest shock (3.4) came hours after injection was cut. In the story Hark had
written that warning four days before the stop, in a public log 11,000 lines
long that nobody read. The turn of the story is Hark saying so, first, in
front of the whole village: "I put the warning where it was correct, and not
where anybody would read it."

**The cost.** Stopping means 18 L/s instead of 40. At an 80 K temperature
drop that is 18 × 4.2 × 80 = 6.0 MW of heat instead of 13.4 MW: the school,
the care home, the pool, the church and about a thousand of 2,600 houses.
The rest keep their gas boilers. The village takes half, and the sacristan's
"no" to the coin-toss plan is the reason.

Some numbers are invented to fit the sums: the 2.0 and 2.5 traffic-light
thresholds, the catalogue, the fitted b of 1.02, and the village's heat demand
and loan. The story's note lists each one. It is part of the twenty-year
strand, set about twenty years ahead with AI only steadily better than today,
and it is text only for now: the audio folders are over the size cap set in
the creative prompt.
