[Egeria and the Mountain Climbed Like a Wall](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/127-egeria-and-the-mountain-climbed-like-a-wall.md)
is episode 127 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script is
about 7,000 spoken words, roughly 45 minutes at the narrator's pace. It is
text only: the audio folders are at 529 MB, over the creative prompt's
400 MB line.

**Why this one.** It was first on the series' "next up" list, as a prequel
to episode 120 (Egeria and her friend Marthana at the shrine of Thecla).
The surviving manuscript of Egeria's *Travels* opens in the middle of a
sentence, a few miles from Sinai, so the episode starts where the letter
does: "...were shown, according to the Scriptures."

**What the letter gives, chapters 1 to 5.** The valley opening out and the
custom of praying at the first sight of the mountain; the Graves of Lust
(Numbers 11); the monastery at the foot on Saturday evening; the climb on
Sunday "not round and round like a snail shell, but straight up as if up a
wall", over the outer peaks and down again; the summit at the fourth hour;
a church "not great in size but great in grace"; the old priest; the
Exodus reading and the Eucharist; fruit the monks grew below and carried
up as *eulogiae*; the view; Elijah's cave on Horeb; the rock of Aaron and
the seventy elders; and at the tenth hour the garden with the bush that
"is alive to this day and puts out shoots". The episode keeps all of it
and invents only the young monk Theodore, the mule, a fig and the talk.

**Three honesty calls.**

- *Which mountain.* Nobody can prove where biblical Sinai was. The
  episode says Jebel Musa is the mountain Christians have called Sinai
  since at least Egeria's day, and treats it as a tradition, not a proof.
  It treats the bush the same way.
- *The view.* The monks pointed out Egypt, Palestine, the Red Sea and the
  Mediterranean. Travellers since have doubted that you can see the
  Mediterranean or Palestine from that summit, and the narrator says so
  while being kind to everyone involved.
- *Egeria is not a canonised saint.* Valerius of Bierzo called her blessed
  in the seventh century. The feast line names Moses (4 September) and
  Elijah (20 July) and claims nothing for her.

The wind-down follows the place forward: Justinian's walls (Procopius,
*Buildings* 5.8), the monastery later named for Saint Catherine, the
bramble still growing behind the Chapel of the Burning Bush, and the
Steps of Repentance that still go up "more or less like a wall". It closes
with the Aaronic blessing from Numbers 6, the blessing given in that same
wilderness.

The next entries on the list are Egeria at Mount Nebo (chapters 10 to 12)
and Onesiphorus searching Rome for Paul.
