The Clothing Design Studio grew a set of launch planners: cash flow, pre-orders, wholesale, reorder and a time-and-action calendar. Each had its own boxes with placeholder numbers, so a designer who had just costed a shirt in the studio had to retype the landed cost, price, run size and fees five times, and a typo in one page quietly disagreed with the others.

Now the studio saves its result in the browser and each planner starts from it. A 100-piece tee at 45 EUR with a landed cost of 19.82 arrives in the cash-flow page as 100 pieces, 16.92 from the factory, the freight and duty as costed, and the money kept per sale after VAT and fees. A line at the top says which boxes were filled and from what; any box can be changed and the studio is not touched.

Nothing leaves the browser. The numbers are only as good as the supplier sheet behind them: if it is still the placeholder example sheet, the note says so. Tests rework the goods price, freight and net-per-sale by hand, and a browser run went from the studio through all five pages without errors. Sunglasses and wardrobe drops do not feed the planners yet.
