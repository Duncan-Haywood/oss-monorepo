A new clothing brand's first compliance surprise is usually the sewn-in label.
The [label checker](../../ventures/clothing-design-studio/labels.html) in the
Clothing Design Studio takes the fibre content as typed and returns the wording
for the EU or the US.

What it does differently per market: the EU uses "elastane" and "polyamide or
nylon" and allows fibres under 3% to be grouped as "other fibres", translated
into eight languages; the US uses "spandex" and "rayon" and groups fibres under
5% as "other fiber", except spandex. It flags brand names such as Lycra and
shares that do not add to 100. It then lists the other items: country of origin
and an RN number or maker name for the US; maker name, postal address, a
product identifier and, for makers outside the EU, a responsible person under
the general product safety regulation.

It is a summary of 16 CFR 303 and 423, Regulation 1007/2011 and Regulation
2023/988, not legal advice. The translated fibre names come from memory of the
Annex I tables and are marked for a native reader to confirm. Unproven: a real
factory's label through it. Ten unit tests cover parsing, grouping, names,
checklists and care.
