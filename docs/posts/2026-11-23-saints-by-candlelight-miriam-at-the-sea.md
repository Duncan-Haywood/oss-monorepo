[Miriam at the Sea and the Women with Tambourines](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/170-miriam-at-the-sea-and-the-women-with-tambourines.md)
is episode 170 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30
it is text only, because the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the
creative track's limit is 400 MB. The script is 7,680 spoken words, about
fifty minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 164 put it on the series list.

## Two scenes eighty years apart

Miriam gets very few verses. In Exodus 2 she is not even named: she is
"his sister", standing among the reeds. In Exodus 15 she has two verses, a
tambourine and one line of song. The episode tells both in full and lets
the gap between them do the work: a girl of about ten, then a woman of
about ninety. The script says outright that the ninety is a guess. It is
worked out from Exodus 7:7 (Moses 80, Aaron 83) and the usual view that
the sister was the eldest child.

Three details in the text hold the episode together:

- **The same verb.** In 2:4 the sister "took her stand" at a distance, and
  in 14:13 Moses tells the people to "stand firm and see the salvation of
  the Lord". Both are the hitpael of *yatsav*. The book doesn't point this
  out; the episode does, in one sentence, and the invented talk between
  Miriam and a girl on the beach grows out of it.
- **The same word for the basket.** *Tevah* is used only for Noah's ark
  and for this papyrus basket. Both are daubed with pitch and both carry
  someone God is keeping safe.
- **The same word for the reeds.** *Suf* is the reeds by the Nile in 2:3
  and the "sea of reeds" in 14 and 15. The script keeps the sea's location
  an open question and names the main candidates without picking one.

## What is tradition, and labelled as such

Four later traditions are used, and each is introduced as a tradition:
the rabbis' story that Miriam foretold her brother's birth and stood by
the river "to know what would become of her prophecy" (Megillah 14a,
Sotah 12b-13a); Rashi on Exodus 15:20, that the women packed their
tambourines because they trusted God would give them something to sing
about; the name Bithiah (from 1 Chronicles 4:18, via Megillah 13a); and
Josephus's Thermuthis. The Christian reading (1 Corinthians 10:1-2 on the
sea as baptism, the Easter Vigil's third reading with the song of Moses as
its response, the first of the Orthodox biblical odes, Revelation 15:3) is
presented as a reading, not as something the text says.

The destruction of Pharaoh's army stays in the book's own register: one
plain sentence and no description. The cruel order in Exodus 1:22 gets
two sentences, and then the story moves on, as the book does.

## A continuity thread

Episode 164 made up Ziva, a woman of about fifty-five who had danced
behind Miriam at the sea as a girl of fifteen and played her tambourine
at Miriam's grave in Kadesh. This episode is her prequel: she grabs the
drum her mother made on the night they leave Egypt, without knowing why,
and it is the drum Miriam lifts on the beach. Every word Ziva and Miriam
say is flagged as invented, in the welcome, the notes and the `fiction`
field.

## Fact-pass corrections

- "One of the saddest sentences in the Bible, and only ten words long"
  about Exodus 1:8 was wrong in English (twelve words) and in Hebrew, so
  the word count went.
- The first draft had Aaron meet Moses and Miriam "look up" in the same
  moment. Exodus 4:27 puts the meeting at the mountain of God, in the
  wilderness, so the scene now has the brothers come back to Egypt
  together first.
- "The Sunday before Christmas" for the Orthodox commemoration of the
  forefathers became "the Sunday of the Forefathers, in the weeks before
  Christmas". The Sunday right before Christmas is the Sunday of the Holy
  Fathers.
- "The first song sung at the morning service" became "the first of the
  nine biblical songs on which the hymns of the morning service are
  built". That is what the odes of the canon actually are.
