[Joshua and the Twelve Stones at Gilgal](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/162-joshua-and-the-twelve-stones-at-gilgal.md)
is episode 162 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30
it is text only: the creative audio folders hold 581 MB against the creative
track's 400 MB line. The script is 6,622 spoken words, about forty-three
minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 161 put it on the series list, and
it picks up from episode 143, where Moses died on Mount Nebo.

## What the book gives, and what we added

Joshua 3 to 5 is a short passage with a lot of quiet detail that films skip:
the Jordan "overflows all its banks throughout the time of harvest"; the
people keep about two thousand cubits behind the ark "for you have not
passed this way before"; the water stops "far off, at Adam", so what the
crowd actually sees is the river draining away; there are two piles of
twelve stones, one on dry land and one left in the riverbed; the Passover
falls four days after the crossing; and the morning after people eat
parched grain from the fields, the manna stops. The episode tells all of
that straight and adds nothing to what happens.

The one invention is a point-of-view character, one of the twelve
stone-carriers, from the tribe of Asher. The book names none of them, so he
stays unnamed. He was born in the wilderness and has eaten manna every
morning of his life, which gives the end of the manna its weight. The
episode closes, years later, with his great-grandson running a finger round
the stone he chose and asking the question Joshua 4:6 expects children to
ask. The welcome, the notes and the `fiction` field all say this part is
invented.

## Where the episode refused to choose

The commander of the army of the Lord (Joshua 5:13-15) is unnamed in the
text. Justin Martyr's *Dialogue with Trypho* (chapter 62) reads him as the
Son of God; many others read him as the Archangel Michael. The series' rule
is to stay off ground the book leaves open, so the script gives both
readings as readings and settles neither. It also leaves out the
landslide explanations sometimes offered for the stopping of the river at
Adam: we could not cite them to a standard source with confidence, and the
series bible says to leave a detail out rather than guess.

## What's next on the list

The episode adds two sequels to the list in `PRODUCTION.md`: the walls of
Jericho (Joshua 6) and Elijah and Elisha parting the Jordan with a cloak
near the same crossing (2 Kings 2), which the wind-down already touches.
