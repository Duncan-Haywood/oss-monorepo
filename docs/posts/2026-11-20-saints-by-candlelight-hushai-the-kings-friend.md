[Hushai the King's Friend and the Well at Bahurim](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/154-hushai-the-kings-friend-and-the-well-at-bahurim.md)
is episode 154 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like the episodes before it, it is
text only, because the creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line. The script is about 7,000 words, about
forty-five minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 150 (Mephibosheth) left
Hushai on the series' list, and episode 152 (Barzillai) had promised to
tell how David came to be across the Jordan.

## Why this story

Second Samuel 17 is the best debate in the Old Testament, and it works as
a bedtime story because the winning speech is the less sensible one.

- **Ahithophel's plan is right, and the book says so.** Twelve thousand
  men, tonight, while David is "weary and discouraged"; strike the king
  alone and bring the people home "as a bride comes home to her husband".
  Verse 14 calls it "the good counsel of Ahithophel". The episode keeps
  that word and explains why a general would agree.
- **Hushai wins with a story, not a plan.** A bear robbed of her cubs,
  David hiding in a pit, brave men melting, all Israel "as the sand by the
  sea", falling on David "as the dew falls on the ground", and ropes to
  drag a city into the valley "until not even a pebble is left". What he
  is really buying is time, and the episode says so.
- **The prayer and the friend.** David prays on the Mount of Olives,
  "turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness", then reaches the
  summit and finds Hushai there in a torn coat. The narrator draws the line
  once, that the Lord answered by a person, and leaves it.
- **Hushai's careful words.** "Long live the king" without saying which
  king; "whom the Lord has chosen, his I will be". The episode points out
  that every word could be true, which is a long-noticed reading of 16:18,
  told as a reading.

## Telling it straight

- **Bedtime lines.** Ahithophel's advice about the king's household
  (16:21-22) is mentioned but not told. His death (17:23) is two quiet
  sentences.
- **Uncertain words hedged out loud.** The place of the "fords of the
  wilderness", the site of Bahurim, the word for the crushed grain on the
  cloth, the "brook of water", whether "the king's friend" was a court
  title, and whether Baana son of Hushai (First Kings 4:16) was his son.
  The four years at the gate are forty in the Hebrew and four in the Greek
  and Syriac, and the episode says which it follows and why.
- **The unnamed stay unnamed.** The maidservant who carried the news to
  En-rogel, the boy who saw the messengers and the woman of Bahurim who hid
  them are all nameless in the book and in the episode.
- **The psalms are hedged.** Psalm 41:9 and Psalm 55:12-14 are often heard
  as Ahithophel's betrayal, but nobody can be sure they were written about
  him. John 13:18 is where Jesus quotes Psalm 41 at the Last Supper. The
  sign-off is from Psalm 3, whose title gives it to David "when he fled
  from Absalom his son": "I lie down and sleep; I wake again, for the Lord
  sustains me."

Invented, and flagged in the welcome, the notes and the front matter:
Hushai's age and his walk back down the hill against the crowd, the faces
at the council, the maidservant's water jar, the inside of the well, and
the grain of barley on Ahimaaz's or Jonathan's nose.
