[Elizabeth Ann and the School for Everyone](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/elizabeth-ann-and-the-school-for-everyone.html) is an eight-minute bedtime story for ages 3 to 8, the shelf's second American saint after Philippine Duchesne.

The story follows her from New York, where her father was the port's health officer and died of yellow fever caught tending sick immigrants, through the cold month in the Livorno quarantine station and her husband's death at Pisa, to the Filicchi family who took her in and the Catholic faith she brought home. It ends at Emmitsburg, Maryland, where in February 1810 she opened a school that took paying pupils and girls who paid nothing. The thread a child can hold is one we drew from her father's work and say is ours: when people are in trouble, you go to them.

One choice shaped the writing. Seton left a large body of letters and journals, and a lot of sayings circulate under her name that are not hers. The shelf's rule is never to put invented words in a real person's mouth, so this story quotes her not at all, and the note for grown-ups says so and points to the published editions of her writings.

Two details were checked against overclaiming. She is often called the first American saint, but Rose of Lima was born in the Americas and canonized three centuries earlier, so the story says she was the first person born in the land that became the United States to be canonized. Her feast on 4 January is in the proper calendar of the United States, not the General Roman Calendar, and the story says "in the United States".

The note also gives what the bedtime version leaves out: two of her daughters died young at Emmitsburg. The series bible now proposes Katharine Drexel next.
