The newest Hearth & Horizon story, *The Pipe That Goes Flat in December*, is about an organ that was in tune in September and sour by Advent. Speed of sound goes as the square root of absolute temperature, so ordinary flue pipes drop about three cents per kelvin of cooling: 30 cents between 16 C and 6 C, with A 440 sounding at 432.3 Hz. Reed pipes are set mostly by a brass tongue and stay put, so the two beat at about eight times a second.

The twist is that retuning the reeds in the cold church only moves the clash: once the church warms to 12 C the flues rise 18 cents and the reeds are 4.6 Hz flat. The fix is to settle the service temperature first and tune to that.
