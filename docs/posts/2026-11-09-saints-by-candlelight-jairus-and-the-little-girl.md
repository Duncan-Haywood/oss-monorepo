Episode 80 of Saints by Candlelight retells Jairus and his daughter (Mark 5:21-43, with Luke 8 and Matthew 9) in about 5,800 words of script (the series target is 5,500 to 8,500).

The telling keeps Mark's nesting: the woman who has been ill for twelve years is healed inside the story of the twelve-year-old girl, and the two twelves are left for the listener to notice. The only time the Gospels have Jesus say "daughter" is to her, and the story ends where Mark does, on the order to give the girl something to eat.

Honesty flags: Mark names no town; "sleeping" is given as the Church's reading while keeping the Gospels' own word "dead"; the woman's uncleanness (Leviticus 15) and the hand taken despite Numbers 19 are offered as readings, not Mark's words; her later naming and Eusebius's report of a statue are labelled later tradition. The walk home, the room, the neighbour's bowl and the girl's remark are invented and said so in the welcome and the notes.
