[Reuel and the Seven Daughters at the Well of Midian](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/157-reuel-and-the-seven-daughters-at-the-well-of-midian.md)
is episode 157 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30 it
is text only for now: the creative audio folders hold 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line. The script is about 6,300 spoken words, a
little over forty minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 140, about Jethro's
advice to Moses, left it as "a story for another night", and the series list
had carried it since.

## Twelve verses, and one question doing the work

Exodus 2:15-22 is short. Moses flees Pharaoh, sits by a well, stands up for
seven sisters when shepherds drive them off, waters their flock and is
invited to supper. Most retellings hurry to the burning bush. The episode
slows down on three small things the text already holds, so nothing had to be
added to what anyone in Scripture does:

- **"How is it that you have come so soon today?"** Reuel's question (2:18)
  only makes sense if the girls were usually late, so the bullying at the
  well was the daily routine, not a bad morning. The episode builds its
  middle on that one inference and says in the notes that it is one.
- **"He even drew water for us."** The sisters are more surprised that a man
  who looks like the court of Egypt hauled the bucket himself than that he
  faced down the shepherds. Acts 7:22 ("instructed in all the wisdom of the
  Egyptians") makes the contrast sharp without inventing anything.
- **"Why did you leave the man?"** The father's rebuke (2:20) is the turn of
  the story and the reason the title names Reuel, not Moses. The episode
  connects it, as a narrator's wondering and not as a claim, to the Law's
  later "love the stranger as yourself, for you were strangers in Egypt"
  (Leviticus 19:34), and to the name Gershom, which the book glosses as
  "I have been a sojourner in a foreign land".

## The hard verse

Before the well, Moses kills an Egyptian who is beating a Hebrew (2:12). A
bedtime show could skip it, but the flight makes no sense without it, and
the series bible says Scripture is told straight. The episode gives it one
plain sentence, no detail, and notes that the book "does not stop to say
whether it was right or wrong", which is accurate and leaves the judgement
where the text leaves it.

## What was checked

The verb in 2:17 usually rendered "helped" is from the root *yasha*, to
save, the root of the names Joshua and Jesus; the episode says so because it
is true and it matters to the scene. The ages (forty at the flight, forty
years in Midian) are Stephen's in Acts 7, and the script attributes them to
him, since Exodus gives none. The three names for Moses' father-in-law
(Reuel here, Jethro later, Hobab "son of Reuel" in Numbers 10:29) are named
and left unsettled, as in episode 140. Midian is placed in north-west
Arabia as the common view, with the caveat said aloud.

Invented and flagged: the road from Egypt, the six sisters the book does
not name, the bucket lessons, the lentils at supper and the last talk by the
tent. The closing prayer is a plain rendering of Psalm 146:5-9, which ends
"the Lord watches over the strangers".

The episode leaves two new lines on the series list: Aaron coming out to
meet Moses at the mountain of God (Exodus 4:27-31), and the night on the
road back to Egypt (4:18-26), which will need care.
