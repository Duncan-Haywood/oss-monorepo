The newest [Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, *The Air the Congregation Breathes*, is set in a chapel on an invented orbital station in October 2088. Every Sunday the congregation gets drowsy about twenty minutes into Mass, and the priest blames his homily. The station assistant has a carbon dioxide log.

Eighty people at an assumed 15 L/h put 1.4 m3 of CO2 into a 150 m3 nave in 70 minutes: 9,300 ppm if the door were sealed. With a loop moving 200 m3/h the level climbs from 3,000 to about 7,700 ppm and passes 5,000 after 19 minutes. Holding it near 5,000 takes 600 m3/h, so the chapel borrows a spare 38 W fan. The second point is weightlessness: warm breath does not rise away, so it hangs round a still face and the duct sensor's average understates the quiet corner. That effect is real; the 650 ppm gap in the story is invented and would need measuring.
