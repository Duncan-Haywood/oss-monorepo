On a laptop, the top of [the hub](/) had a big headline, a search box and a row of counts down the left side. The right third was decorative starfield: 364 pixels of it at 1,280 px wide and 444 at 1,440. A first-time visitor read that the site has free tools for pricing jobs and checking fees, but couldn't see a single one.

That space now holds three framed screenshots of real tools on this site, each with its own worked example loaded:

- [Wall Framing Takeoff](/wall-framing-takeoff/) drawing every stud, header and cripple in a wall, for "price the job";
- [eBay Fee Calculator](/ebay-fee-calculator/) comparing a sale with and without Promoted Listings, for "check the fees";
- [Container Load Planner](/container-load-planner/) packing cartons and pallets into a 40-foot container, drawn to scale.

Each picture is a link to its tool. They stack like papers on a desk, so every title shows and the last one is seen whole.

Some things stay as they were. Below 1,180 px wide there's no room for a column beside the search, so the art is hidden and phones get the same hero as before. Phones don't download the pictures either. At desktop width the three images add about 106 KB, and the showcase further down the page reuses the same files.

While doing this, the tool that takes these screenshots got two fixes. It can now retake stills without re-recording the screen video. It also hides the floating assistant button, which had been sitting in the corner of every picture. All nine showcase stills were retaken today, so they match how the tools look now rather than in October.

What's still weak: at 300 px wide you can't read the text in the pictures. They show a real tool with a real drawing, which is the point, but cropping to the drawing alone would make them sharper. That's next.
