[John Chrysostom and the Last Road to Comana](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/093-john-chrysostom-and-the-last-road-to-comana.md)
is episode 93 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,800
words, roughly 50 minutes spoken. Like every episode since 20, it is text only
for now, because the audio folders are still over the repository's 400 MB cap
(423 MB today).

Episode 92's post named John Chrysostom as next. With him the show has all
three of the Orthodox Church's Three Holy Hierarchs: Basil (episode 89),
Gregory of Nazianzus (92) and now John.

## Why the last journey

John's life has a lot of drama for a bedtime show: riots, an empress, a
church that burned down the night he left. The episode moves through it at
an even pace and keeps the heaviest weight on the gentlest part, the end.
Palladius, a friend who wrote a few years after John died, describes it
plainly. Two soldiers marched him for three months toward the far shore of
the Black Sea. One was harsh and one was kind. They stopped at a martyr's
chapel near Comana. In the night the martyr Basiliscus told him, "Courage,
brother John, tomorrow we shall be together." The next morning he set out,
was brought back with a fever, put on white clothes, received Communion and
died saying the words he always said: *Glory to God for all things.* The
cold open, the soldier scenes and the wind-down are all built toward that
line.

## What the episode keeps from the record

- his mother Anthusa, widowed at twenty, and the pagan teacher (most likely
  Libanius) who said "what women these Christians have", which is John's own
  report;
- the cave years in the hills that ruined his health and sent him back to
  the city to preach;
- the Homilies on the Statues of 387, including the homework he set a
  frightened city (a week without swearing oaths);
- being taken from Antioch in a carriage to become archbishop, so people
  would not riot;
- the sermon against silver chamber pots;
- Eutropius, the chamberlain who had picked him, holding on to the altar for
  sanctuary while John preached "vanity of vanities" and then asked the crowd
  to show him mercy;
- Olympias the deaconess, who sent him meals and got the seventeen letters
  on despondency, and the little book *That No One Can Harm the Person Who
  Does Not Harm Himself*;
- the Synod of the Oak, which listed eating alone among its charges, the
  recall, the second exile, Cucusus, and the march toward Pityus;
- the young emperor Theodosius II laying his face on the coffin in 438 and
  asking forgiveness for his parents, as Theodoret tells it.

## Where the care went

Some things are told as somebody's report: the teacher's remark (John's),
Libanius's deathbed line (Sozomen's), and both appearances of Basiliscus
(Palladius's). The Paschal Homily and the Divine Liturgy are described as
bearing his name, with the authorship question named. The story of how the
Three Hierarchs feast began, the bishop of Euchaita's dream, is labelled
tradition, and so is Saint Paul whispering at his ear.

The quarrels are told briefly and from John's side, and the notes admit that.
Theophilus of Alexandria is a saint of the Coptic Church, and the episode
tries not to make him, or the Empress Eudoxia, into villains. It says outright
that historians still argue whether John's sermons were aimed at her.

The episode invents two things and says so in the welcome, the notes and the
front matter: a boy who keeps the lamps at the chapel, and the words of the
kind soldier. Palladius says the kinder soldier existed, but he never names
him or quotes him.

A first adversarial read caught several overreaches before the episode was
generated. It had placed the pillar saint Simeon at the wrong age. It put
Cucusus in "the far eastern corner" of Turkey and Comana "not far from the
Black Sea". It listed three named deaconesses at the farewell when only one
name was certain. And it had John baptised at a precise age. Each was
softened or cut.
