[John Chrysostom and the Last Road to Comana](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/093-john-chrysostom-and-the-last-road-to-comana.md)
is episode 93 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 8,000
words, roughly 50 minutes spoken. It is text only for now, because the audio
folders are still over the repository's 400 MB cap (423 MB today).

With it the show has all Three Holy Hierarchs of the Orthodox Church: Basil
(episode 89), Gregory the Theologian (episode 92) and now John. The series
bible asked for John's last journey "told gently", so the first half moves
quickly through Antioch and Constantinople and the second half slows down on
the road into exile.

## Where the story comes from

John is well documented by ancient standards. His friend Palladius wrote a
defence of him within a few years of his death. The church historians
Socrates and Sozomen covered his years as bishop about forty years later
(Socrates is the cooler of the two), and Theodoret describes the return of
his body. Best of all, more than two hundred of John's own letters from exile
survive, including seventeen to the deaconess Olympias, so most of the road
to Cucusus is told in his own words.

Things in the episode that come from those sources:

- his mother Anthusa, widowed at about twenty, asking him beside the bed where
  she gave birth to him not to leave her a widow a second time (John tells
  this in *On the Priesthood*);
- the riot of the statues in Antioch in 387 and the Lent sermons he preached
  while the city waited for the emperor's verdict;
- the governor inviting him to a martyr's shrine outside the gate and putting
  him in a carriage to the capital, so Antioch could not riot;
- the fallen chamberlain Eutropius clinging to the altar while John preached
  "vanity of vanities" over him and refused to hand him over;
- his farewell to Olympias and the deaconesses in the baptistery, and the
  fire that burned the great church the same night;
- the night near Caesarea when he was sent away at midnight, without
  torches for fear of raiders, and his mule threw him on the stones;
- the forced march toward Pityus, the martyr Basiliscus in the night ("Take
  courage, brother John, tomorrow we shall be together"), the white
  clothes, and the last words, "Glory to God for all things";
- Theodosius II, Eudoxia's son, laying his face on the coffin in 438 and
  asking forgiveness for his parents.

## The judgment calls

The middle of John's life is a quarrel with an empress and a rival
bishop, and a bedtime show has to tell it without taking sides it cannot
prove. Theophilus of Alexandria is honoured as a saint in the Coptic Church,
and the episode says so and treats him fairly. The famous "Herodias" sermon
is left out: the old historians disagree about what John said, and the
sermon that survives under that title is thought not to be his. What
frightened the palace into recalling him after his first exile is told as
the sources leave it: one says an earthquake, another an unnamed disaster.
The soldiers at the Easter baptisms get one sentence.

The Divine Liturgy and the Easter sermon read in Orthodox churches carry
John's name. The episode calls that a traditional attribution, not proof.

## What we made up

Palladius says one of the two guards on the last march was kinder than the
other, in secret. He gives no name. The episode gives him one, Theodore, and
a few small scenes: helping John up on the dark road, sitting by the door at
Cucusus, holding his cloak over John's head in the rain. It says three times
that Theodore is invented: in the welcome, when he first appears, and in the
notes. John's words to the deaconesses and in his letters are our own plain
paraphrase, not a published translation.
