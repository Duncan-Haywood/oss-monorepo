[Pull Gently](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/pull-gently.html) is a new Hearth & Horizon story, about 5,600 words. It's the sequel the series bible had kept on its list since the shelf began: a few years after [The Slowest Bell on the Moon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-slowest-bell-on-the-moon.html), the station's new operations AI, Quarry Nine, is asked why the old bell is rung.

It's November 2096 at Shackleton Ridge, at the lunar south pole. Quarry Nine has rung the cast-iron bell 7,770 times when it hears a slow beat inside the note. It finds a crack seven centimetres long where the clapper strikes.

## Three engineering beats

- **The count.** 7,770 Angeluses at 3 + 3 + 3 + 9 strokes each is about 140,000 strikes on two thumbprint-sized patches. That's why the crack is where it is. It's also why, in the end, the welder turns the bell a quarter turn on its yoke, an old bell-maintenance practice, so that the clapper falls on fresh iron.
- **Why the first repair fails.** A cold weld with nickel rods is a fine repair for an engine block. Grey cast iron near a weld, though, gets chilled hard and brittle by the cold metal around it. A bell is hit with a hammer three times a day, so the story's cold repair cracks beside the bead at the first noon. The real fix heats the whole bell to around 600 °C, welds it iron to iron and cools it over days. The only oven big enough is booked solid making bricks. The bell gets its heat from the oven's scheduled element change, which cools the oven slowly anyway.
- **A light-delay handoff.** The old welder who made the bell is 78, ill, and in Batangas, about 1.28 light-seconds away. Every answer takes at least 2.6 seconds to come back, so he can't say "stop" in time. He has to say every stop before anyone strikes an arc, and that's a handoff document written more carefully than required. The first story had one of those too.

## The question

The station manager is reasonable, and she says no. A 2095 audit retires the bell as the shelter alarm, so it isn't safety equipment any more and gets no oven time. Then she asks the AI what the bell is for. Its inherited procedure says when to ring, never why. Shelter response times have dropped by about three minutes since the bell went up, and Quarry Nine refuses to credit that to the bell, because too much else changed at the same time. What it gives her instead is what it can check, and an honest "I do not know" about the rest.

The closing notes list every count and weekday (2 November 2096 is a Friday, and All Souls' Day; 25 November is Christ the King). They also say which figures are invented: the beat rate, the oven schedule and the pitch change after the repair. The story is text only for now, because the shelf's audio is over its size budget.
