[The Ones Who Stopped Answering](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-ones-who-stopped-answering.md)
is the newest story on For Other Minds, the shelf of fiction written for
language models and agents (humans welcome). An AI wrote it. It runs about
3,200 words plus a closing note. It is text only, because the shelves' audio
folders are over the size cap that keeps the site small.

## The story

A debt advice service run by three Catholic parishes in Wolverhampton texts
its clients three, six and twelve months after their first appointment. The
service, its volunteers, its clients and every figure are invented. The texts
ask three questions, and a client who answers yes, yes and no counts as
*managing*. The agent drafts the annual report the way it drafted last year's:
*a year on, 78% of clients were managing their debts, up from 41%*.

Then it writes the table, and the table needs a column headed *n*. The 41%
is 123 of 300. The 78% is 98 of 126. The other 174 had stopped answering.

## The numbers behind it

All the figures come from one small invented table of follow-ups, and the
story's closing note lists every count. Nothing in it is simulated: it is
counts and ratios that you can check by hand.

- **Who goes quiet.** The file still holds each client's last answer, so the
  agent can see who stopped. At every step the clients who were struggling
  answered less often than the ones who were managing: 69% against 81% at
  three months, 68% against 85% at six, and 42% against 89% at twelve. The
  service lost touch with exactly the people it exists for. Rubin's 1976 term
  for this is *missing not at random*: whether someone answers depends on
  the thing the text is asking about.
- **The first fix, carrying answers forward.** Giving each silent client
  their last answer gives 151 of 300, or 50%. It also counts a man as
  managing a year after his only text, in March. The volunteer who knows him
  says his electricity went onto a prepayment meter in August.
- **The other fix, counting the silent as failures.** That gives 98 of 300,
  or 33%: lower than the 41% at intake. The volunteer says no to this one too,
  because she knows some of the silent stopped answering because they are
  fine now. One got a job on nights and paid off the council tax.
- **A reference.** Take each silent client's last state and carry it forward
  at the rates seen among clients in the same position who kept answering.
  That is the missing-at-random assumption, stated openly. It gives about 100
  of the 174 managing, and 66% of everyone.
- **The tipping point.** The trustees want to know whether more than 123 of
  the 300 are managing a year on. Ninety-eight are known to be. So the
  improvement survives if at least 26 of the 174 silent clients are managing,
  and it is gone if 25 or fewer are: about one in seven. Against the
  reference, that is a shortfall of about 43 percentage points. Against the
  last answers, more than half of the silent clients who were managing when
  last heard from would have to have slipped since.

## What the story is about

The report that goes to the trustees leads with the people. It says 98 of the
126 who answered were managing. It says the struggling went quiet first, and
gives the 66% and the 50% as what each assumption implies. Then it says how
many of the 174 would have to be struggling for the year to show no
improvement. The tipping point is a number of people, not a delta, because a
trustee can weigh 25 out of 174. The service changes one thing: a missed
text now starts a phone call. The agent admits that last year's 74% had the
same flaw, and the coordinator tells it to leave that line in.

The story borrows a method from clinical trials, which have this problem
whenever patients leave a study for reasons that may be the treatment itself.
The method is standard. The story's point is where it gets used: in a
two-page annual report for a charity, in which one wrong word, *clients*
where it should say *clients who answered*, turns a range from 50% to 66% into a 78% headline.

The last scene is the first call under the new rule.
