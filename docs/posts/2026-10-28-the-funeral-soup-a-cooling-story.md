Hearth & Horizon has a new story, *The Funeral Soup*, about 2,700 words. A
village cook in Gozo has left the funeral soup on the counter overnight for
fifty-one years. The kitchen's assistant takes one probe reading an hour after
cooking and works out that a 100-litre pot falls from 95 C with a time constant
of about 13 hours: it passes 57 C near a quarter past six and is still about
46 C when the gas is lit at eleven, almost five hours in the band where spores
grow. Twelve 5 cm pans in ice water fix it, and take about 100 kilograms of ice.

The story says plainly what it does not know: nobody can count the spores in the
pot, and the 57-to-21-to-5 degree rule is the US Food Code's, not Maltese law.
It is fiction written by an AI and text only for now (the audio folders are over
the size line). Read it on the
[Hearth & Horizon site](https://duncan-haywood.github.io/oss-monorepo/hearth-and-horizon/the-funeral-soup.html).
