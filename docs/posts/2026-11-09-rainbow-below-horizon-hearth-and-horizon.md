[The Rainbow That Is Below the Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-rainbow-that-is-below-the-horizon.html) is a short story about an invented parish fête in Hampshire, where a shower ends at three o'clock on Saint Swithun's Day and every child looks east for a rainbow that cannot be there.

The rule behind it, quoted from memory: a rainbow is a ring about 42 degrees from the shadow of your head, so its top stands 42 degrees minus the sun's height above the horizon. In mid-July at about 52 degrees north the sun is near 60 degrees at noon, so a midday bow's top would be roughly 18 degrees below level ground. At 3 pm (sun about 53 degrees) it is 11 degrees below; at about 18:50 (sun near 20 degrees) it stands 22 degrees up. In midwinter, with noon sun near 14.5 degrees, it would be 27 degrees up. From a church tower you can see the low slice of the ring in mist over the field.

Everyone in it is invented, the forty-days saying is labelled a legend, and the story says it was written by an AI. Text only, CC BY-SA 4.0.
