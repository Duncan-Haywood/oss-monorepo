[Abigail and the Loaves on the Mountain Path](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/146-abigail-and-the-loaves-on-the-mountain-path.md)
is episode 146 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like the episodes before it, it is
text only for now, because the creative shelves' audio folders are over
the creative track's 400 MB line. The script runs to about 7,500 words,
which is roughly fifty minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 144 (Nathan
and the ewe lamb) had left the chapter on the series' list.

## Why this chapter

First Samuel 25 comes between the two nights when David spares King Saul:
the cave at En-gedi in chapter 24 and the sleeping camp in chapter 26.
Between them, a rich farmer named Nabal insults David at shearing time,
and David sets out with four hundred men to kill every male in Nabal's
house. The man who would not raise his hand against a king nearly
massacres a household over a rude answer at a party. The episode builds
around that contrast. It tells the cave scene in full before Nabal's
reply, so a listener can hear how far David has fallen by the time
Abigail meets him on the path.

## Telling it straight

The series bible says Scripture is told straight, and the episode follows
that:

- **Abigail's speech is given almost whole** (verses 24 to 31), in a
  plain rendering, with the narrator stepping in between parts to point
  things out. She takes the blame first. She uses her husband's name
  (*nabal*, "fool") against him. She talks to David as if God has already
  held him back. And she ends on the line that carries the story: when he
  is king, this must not be "a weight on your heart".
- **Nabal's death is left to God, as the text leaves it.** "The Lord
  struck Nabal" is quoted. The episode says plainly that it does not know
  what "his heart died within him" describes medically. It also points out
  that nobody in the story lifts a hand against him.
- **David's other marriages are named, not glossed.** Verses 43 and 44 come
  straight after the proposal. The episode gives them one plain paragraph
  and points to Matthew 19 and "the beginning".

The bakehouse, the head baker counting loaves, and the narrator's guesses
about Abigail's donkey and her later years in Hebron are invented, and
the script says so in its welcome, its notes and its front matter.
Traditions are labelled as traditions. The Talmud (Megillah 14a) counts
Abigail among seven prophetesses. Her phrase "the bundle of the living"
still appears on Jewish gravestones.
