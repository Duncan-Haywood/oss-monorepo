A clothing brand that sells at 50 and keeps 20 per order after cost, fees,
shipping and returns cannot pay more than 20 to win a customer, unless that
customer comes back. At 0.80 a click and 2 in 100 clicks buying, a customer
costs 40: ROAS 1.25 against a break-even of 2.5. Cut the click to 0.40, or
double the conversion, and the first order pays for itself.

The Clothing Design Studio now has this as `adspend.html`. It takes the profit
per order before ads (from the returns calculator), click price, conversion,
repeat orders in a year and a monthly budget. It returns the cost of a customer,
break-even ROAS with and without repeat orders, the most a click may cost, the
least conversion that works, what the budget buys, and what happens if
conversion is a third worse or half again better.

It is arithmetic on typed numbers, checked by hand in seven tests. It is not a
forecast: click prices rise with spend, repeat orders are a guess until a year
of real ones exists, and no real brand has run its numbers through it yet.
