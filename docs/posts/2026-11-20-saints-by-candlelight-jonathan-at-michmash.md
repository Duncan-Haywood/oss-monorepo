[Jonathan and the Armour-Bearer at Michmash](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/153-jonathan-and-the-armour-bearer-at-michmash.md)
is episode 153 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like the episodes before it, it is
text only, because the creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line. The script is 6,362 words, about forty-one
minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 151 (Jonathan and the arrows) left
this chapter on the series' list. It is the story of Jonathan before David
arrives.

## Why this chapter

First Samuel 14 has two halves, and the episode keeps both:

- **The climb.** The Philistines camp at Michmash, across a gorge from
  Saul's six hundred men. Jonathan slips away with his armour-bearer, asks
  for a sign ("if they say, come up to us, we will go up"), and climbs the
  crag "on his hands and his feet". The camp panics, and the chapter sums
  up the morning as "So the Lord saved Israel that day". The episode makes
  Jonathan's own line its hinge: "nothing can hinder the Lord from saving,
  by many or by few". It also points out that his "it may be" is a long
  way from a boast.
- **The honey.** Saul's oath ("cursed be the man who eats food until
  evening") starves the army. Jonathan hasn't heard it, so he dips his
  staff in a honeycomb, and "his eyes became bright". The lot falls on
  him that night and he answers "Here I am. I will die." The people answer
  back: "he has worked with God this day". They ransom him. The episode
  tells the half people skip in full: the meat eaten with the blood, and
  Saul setting up the great stone and building "the first altar he ever
  built". Saul doesn't come out of it a cardboard villain.

## Telling it straight

- **The armour-bearer has no name, so the episode doesn't give him one.**
  The narrator says why. His line, "I am with you, heart and soul", gets
  as much weight as Jonathan's.
- **The uncertain words are hedged out loud.** The meanings of the crag
  names Bozez and Seneh are guesses. The measure of the first skirmish
  ("half a furrow" in a plot of land) is hard Hebrew. In verse 18 the
  Hebrew has Saul call for the ark and the Greek has the ephod.
- **The pim.** First Samuel 13:21 gives the Philistine blacksmiths'
  charge for sharpening a ploughshare. The word appears once in the Bible.
  Its meaning, about two-thirds of a shekel, came from stone weights
  inscribed *pym* that turned up in excavations. The episode tells it as a
  story about a monopoly on repairs.
- **The battle stays short.** About twenty men fall in the first skirmish,
  in one sentence, as the book has it. This is a bedtime show.
- **The 1918 story is labelled.** Vivian Gilbert's memoir *The Romance of
  the Last Crusade* (1923) says a British brigade major read this chapter
  by candlelight before an attack on Mukhmas, and that a company used
  Jonathan's route. The episode tells it as one officer's account that
  can't be checked in every detail. This run could only find it in later
  retellings, such as Werner Keller's *The Bible as History*, not in
  Gilbert's own text, so the details stay loose.

Invented, and flagged in the welcome, the notes and the front matter: the
armour-bearer's thoughts and his mother, the plant on the cliff, the
breakfast smoke, the bees, and the small talk on the way to the pass.
