Yesterday's [size-run costing](2026-11-05-what-a-whole-size-run-costs.md) guessed cloth by size from chest times length. A factory does not guess; it lays the pattern pieces of several sizes side by side across the cloth (a marker), spreads many layers under it, and cuts. How the pieces pack decides how many metres you buy.

The [size finder](../../ventures/clothing-design-studio/fit.html) now does a simple version. It builds the pieces of a tee, long-sleeve, crewneck or hoodie in every size (front, back, sleeves, ribbing, hood, pocket), keeps them on the grain, packs them across the usable width, and picks lays and plies that fit your ply limit and cutting table. Sizes with few pieces, like three XXL, are cut apart from the bulk so rounding doesn't pile up spares.

On the default 200 tees at 150 cm cloth it comes out around 0.95 m a piece, against 1.3 m from the area rule, with about 82% of the cloth covered by pattern. The pieces are rectangles, so curves are not nested into each other and a real CAD marker should do better; this is a sanity check on a quote, not a replacement for one. No factory has compared its marker to it yet, and the page says to type theirs in when you have it.
