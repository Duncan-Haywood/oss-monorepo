[The Gibeonites and the Mouldy Bread](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/149-the-gibeonites-and-the-mouldy-bread.md)
is episode 149 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, because the
creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the creative track's 400 MB
line. The script is about 6,000 words, roughly thirty-nine minutes at the
narrator's pace. Episode 145 (Balaam) left it on the series' list.

## Why this chapter

Joshua 9 is one of the few comic scenes in a book about a war. A hill town
sends envoys down to Joshua's camp at Gilgal with old sacks, burst and
mended wineskins, patched sandals and bread that is dry and mouldy, and
claims to have come from a very far country. The leaders of
Israel inspect the bread and do not, the book says, "ask counsel of the
Lord". They swear peace. Three days later they learn the town is just over
the hill.

What makes it more than a trick story is what happens next:

- **The oath holds.** The congregation grumbles, but the leaders say, in
  effect, we swore by the Lord, so we may not touch them. A promise got by
  a lie is still kept, because of whose name it was sworn in.
- **The Gibeonites stop lying.** Asked why, they answer plainly: we were
  afraid for our lives, and now we are in your hand.
- **Joshua marches all night for them.** In chapter 10, five kings besiege
  Gibeon for making peace with Israel. Joshua goes up from Gilgal through
  the night, about a thousand metres of climb, to save the town that fooled
  him. The sun stands still over Gibeon, of all places.

The episode closes the loop with Psalm 15, verse 4: the one "who swears to
his own hurt and does not change".

## Telling it straight

- **The envoys' speech is given nearly whole.** The script points out what
  it leaves out: Egypt, Sihon and Og are mentioned, the Jordan, Jericho and
  Ai are not, which is how news would reach a town that really was far away.
  A first draft also said that Rahab, in Joshua 2, "told the news right up
  to date". The review caught that Rahab spoke before the Jordan crossing,
  so she could not have, and the line now says only that she used almost the
  same words earlier.
- **Joshua's sentence is softened but not changed.** The book has him call
  them cursed and bondmen. The episode says they would live and serve, as
  woodcutters and water-carriers "for the house of my God", which is what
  happens.
- **The battle gets a paragraph.** The hailstones and the sun are told as
  the book tells them, quoting the lost Book of Jashar, and the script says
  people have argued over the meaning for centuries without settling it.
- **Second Samuel 21 is named, not told.** Saul breaking the oath, and what
  followed, is "a hard story, and not one for bedtime".
- **Archaeology is hedged.** El-Jib is "usually identified" with Gibeon.
  The rock-cut pool and the jar handles inscribed with the town's name come
  from James Pritchard's digs of 1956 to 1962, and the notes say most of
  what he found is centuries later than Joshua.

The baker Ama, her grandson Pazi with string on his sandal, the town
council and the recipe for bad bread are invented. The script says so in
its welcome, its notes and its front matter.

## What the wind-down follows

The episode traces the Gibeonites forward through Scripture, and the trail
turned out longer than expected. Deuteronomy 29 already lists "the one who
cuts your wood and the one who draws your water" in Moses' covenant. The
ark rests twenty years at Kiriath-jearim, one of Gibeon's four towns. The
tent of meeting stands at Gibeon in David's day, which is why Solomon
dreamed there (episode 139). Ishmaiah the Gibeonite joins David at Ziklag
(1 Chronicles 12, the setting of episode 148). The men of Gibeon rebuild a
stretch of Jerusalem's wall in Nehemiah 3.

Two of those went onto the list in
[PRODUCTION.md](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/PRODUCTION.md) as
future episodes: the ark at Kiriath-jearim, and Nehemiah's builders on the
wall.
