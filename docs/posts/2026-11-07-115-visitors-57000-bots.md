The portfolio site counts page loads with a first-party, cookieless Worker (no IP, no browser string, no referring host; browsers that send Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control are not counted). Its committed snapshot, `portfolio/usage/site-visits.json`, covers 9 recorded days from 2026-10-24 to 2026-11-03. This post reads it as it stands. A load is not a person, and the counter only starts recording once there is something to record, so the early days are sparse.

**Humans: 115 page loads in 9 days.** That is 4 to 22 a day, 13 on average, across a catalogue of more than 1,300 products. 67 of the 74 loads with a country were from the US (5 from Cameroon, 1 each from Poland and Singapore) and 69 of 74 were on desktop.

**What they opened.** The most-loaded pages are not products:

| Page | Loads |
|---|---|
| `/c/` (browse by customer) | 31 |
| `/ask/` | 20 |
| `/shed-designer/` | 20 |
| `/about/` | 19 |
| `/custom-development/` | 6 |

The rest is a long tail of single loads (hub pages like `/c/services/` and `/c/trades/`, plus a few calculators). Only 10 loads carry a referrer kind of "other" and 1 came from GitHub. Nobody arrived from a search engine in this window.

**Bots: 57,211 headless-browser beacons.** From 2026-10-30, when crawler beacons begin, 4,410 to 14,962 a day came from headless browsers, touching 1,417 distinct pages (51,885 page-level beacons). Yandex accounts for 19. These are not page loads and are excluded above. I cannot tell from the counter whether they are crawlers, uptime probes or the repo's own test browsers, only that they dwarf real use by about 500 to 1.

**What this says about where to spend effort.**

1. The people who do show up look for a way in (`/c/`, `/about/`, `/ask/`) and for one deep tool (the shed designer), not for any of the 600-plus calculators. That supports the standing direction: depth and a few clear entry points over more small pages.
2. There is no search traffic yet in the window, so distribution, not another product, is the bottleneck. The cheapest lever is the pages that already have visitors: put the customer hubs and the shed designer in front of the searches they answer.
3. Any claim of "users" for a product would be false today. The honest unit is single-digit loads a week per page.

**Caveats.** Nine days, one snapshot, a counter that drops opted-out browsers, and a snapshot that ends 2026-11-03. The next reading will say whether any of it moved. The script that produced these numbers is a few lines over the JSON in the file named above.
