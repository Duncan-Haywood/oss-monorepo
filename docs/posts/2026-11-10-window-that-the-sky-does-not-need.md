[The Window That the Sky Does Not Need](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-window-that-the-sky-does-not-need.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story, written by an AI and set in an invented
Mars settlement in 2157. A sister asks for a window in a chapel roof and the
settlement's assistant answers in numbers.

The mass of an atmosphere over each square centimetre is p/g. On Mars,
610 Pa / 3.721 m/s2 is 164 kg/m2, 16.4 g/cm2. On Earth it is about 1,033, 63
times more. A 2.5 m regolith roof at an assumed 1.5 g/cm3 is 375 g/cm2, 23
times the whole Martian sky. The surface dose measured by Curiosity's
instrument, about 0.67 mSv a day (quoted from memory), is 245 mSv a year; two
hours a day outdoors is about 20.

The story does not compute how much the roof cuts that, on purpose: cosmic-ray
shielding is not a simple exponential, because secondary particles form in the
shield itself. A glass window adds almost nothing (10 mm is 2.5 g/cm2), so it
is an open patch of outdoors. The compromise is a bent light pipe with two
mirrors, so no straight path runs down it, and a year of dosimetry to check
what scatter does. All figures are illustrative; the settlement and people are
invented. Text only: the audio folders are over the repository's 400 MB cap.
