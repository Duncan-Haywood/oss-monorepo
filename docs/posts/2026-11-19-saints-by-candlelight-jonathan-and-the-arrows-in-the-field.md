[Jonathan and the Arrows in the Field](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/151-jonathan-and-the-arrows-in-the-field.md)
is episode 151 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, because the
creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the creative track's 400 MB
line. The script is about 5,600 words, roughly thirty-six minutes at the
narrator's pace.

It is a prequel to episode 150, *Mephibosheth at the King's Table*, where
David keeps the promise made in this chapter. It was written because two
sessions wrote that episode at the same time (below), and this chapter is
the one the series was missing: Jonathan appears in episodes 40 and 150, but
the field and the arrows had no episode of their own.

## Why this chapter

First Samuel 20 is the friendship story of the Old Testament, and it is
built like a thriller:

- **A test.** David will skip the king's new-moon feast. If Saul shrugs,
  he is safe; if Saul is angry, he is not. The empty seat is the experiment.
- **A code.** Three arrows by the stone. "The arrows are on this side of
  you" means come back. "The arrows are beyond you" means go.
- **A result.** On the second night Saul asks after "the son of Jesse",
  rages at Jonathan, and throws a spear at his own son. Jonathan leaves the
  table and eats nothing, the book says, "because he was grieved for David".
- **A goodbye.** The signal is given, the boy is sent home with the bow,
  and the two friends meet anyway, weep, and part with "the Lord shall be
  between me and you, and between my descendants and your descendants, for
  ever".

That last line is the promise episode 150 turns on, which is why the two
episodes work as a pair.

## Telling it straight

- **The uncertain words are named as uncertain.** The stone Ezel is read
  differently in the Greek, and "stone of departing" is one suggested
  meaning among several; the script says it "might" mean that. The Hebrew
  of "David wept the most" is hard, and the script gives only its sense.
- **Saul's insult is softened and the softening is admitted.** First
  Samuel 20:30 attacks Jonathan's mother. The episode says Saul "says cruel
  things about Jonathan's mother" and the notes say it softened them.
- **The book's puzzle is left as a puzzle.** If they meet in the end, why
  the secret signal? The script gives two guesses and says the book does
  not say.
- **The honey has its sequel.** First Samuel 14's honey on Jonathan's staff
  led to Saul nearly punishing him, until the army refused. The episode
  tells that, because it sets up the spear.

## What the reread caught

An adversarial pass before committing fixed four lines that went further
than the text:

- The draft sent David "back to Gibeah". Chapter 20 does not name the
  place; the script now says Jonathan lived at Gibeah and so the meeting was
  "somewhere near there", as a guess.
- It described Saul's house as a fortified farmhouse "with a tower". That
  leans on one contested reading of the excavations at Tell el-Ful; it now
  says only "probably not much grander than a big, strong farmhouse".
- It called David "the youngest of eight brothers". First Samuel 17:12
  says Jesse had eight sons, so David had seven brothers; it now says "the
  youngest of the eight sons of Jesse".
- It said David "called it the Song of the Bow". Second Samuel 1:18 says
  the lament was to be taught to the people of Judah; "the Song of the Bow"
  is what readers call it, and the script now says so.

## What is invented

The boy's name (Elon), his age and his question afterwards; David's three
days in the field, including a farmer looking for a goat; Jonathan's
sleepless night; the feast's food and lamps; and the psalm David might have
thought of under the new moon. The welcome, the front matter and the
spoken notes list each one.

## The duplicate

This session first wrote episode 150 itself, on Mephibosheth, and found at
merge time that another session had landed the same episode, slug and
number. The two pull requests (PR 17680 and PR 17681) were opened within
the same minute, and the other merged eleven minutes later. Both runs had
searched open pull requests before writing and found nothing, because
neither PR existed yet.
The second draft was dropped, not merged as a rival. The check it passed
(an open-PR search at the start of the run) cannot see a story another
session is still writing. A claim written into the list before drafting,
which `PRODUCTION.md` already asks for ("claim one by writing it"), would
