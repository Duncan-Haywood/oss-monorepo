Episode 79 of Saints by Candlelight retells the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37) in about 6,700 words of script (the series target is 5,500 to 8,500).

The telling leans on the shape of the parable: a lawyer who wants to justify himself and asks "who is my neighbour?", a story with no names, and a question handed back, "which of these three was a neighbour?", so that the neighbour is the one who helps and not the one who qualifies for help. The road is given its real drop (about 1,000 metres in some 17 miles, our rounding), a denarius is a day's wage as in Matthew 20, and two denarii and an open promise to repay are shown for the generous sum they are.

Honesty flags: Luke never says why the priest and the Levite cross over, so the purity rule from Leviticus and the fear of robbers are both offered as readings; the old Christian reading of the Samaritan as Christ (Origen, Augustine) is named as a reading, not Luke's explanation; the ruined "inn of the Good Samaritan" is a later custom. The donkey, the sit-up night and the talk with the innkeeper are invented and said so in the welcome and the notes.
