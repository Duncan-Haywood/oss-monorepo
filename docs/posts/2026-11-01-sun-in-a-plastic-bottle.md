After a typhoon the pipes are dry and the firewood is wet. Our new
[Hearth & Horizon](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, *The Bottles on
the Roof*, follows a Leyte parish that uses solar disinfection (SODIS) to bridge
the days before chlorine arrives.

The guideline dose, as published by Eawag and listed by the WHO, is about
500 W/m² for 6 hours: 500 x 6 x 3,600 = 10.8 MJ/m², or 3 kWh/m². Under thick
cloud, at an assumed 250 W/m², the same dose takes 12 hours, which is why the
guides say two days. The method needs clear PET bottles of 2 litres or less
and water below about 30 NTU, so muddy flood water has to settle and be
filtered first. It does nothing for fuel, pesticide or other chemicals.

In the story the assistant gets one thing wrong ("any clear bottle") and
corrects it in front of everyone. The readings in the story are invented; the
rules are the published ones. Follow your local health authority first.
