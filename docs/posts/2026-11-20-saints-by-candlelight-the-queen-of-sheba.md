[The Queen of Sheba and the Hard Questions](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/156-the-queen-of-sheba-and-the-hard-questions.md)
is episode 156 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since number 30 it is text
only for now: the creative audio folders hold 577 MB, over the creative
track's 400 MB line. The script is about 5,900 words, roughly thirty-eight
minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 138 promised it in passing ("a story
for another night") and the series list had carried it since.

## The problem with this story

First Kings 10:1-13 is thirteen verses, and the centre of it is missing. The
queen "came to prove him with hard questions", Solomon "told her all her
questions", and the text records not one of them. Later writers filled the
gap with riddles. The episode does not, because the series bible forbids
adding to what a figure in Scripture says. The narrator says plainly that
the questions are lost and offers only the *kinds* of thing anyone might
ask, as the narrator's guesses.

That left a hole to fill without inventing anything, and the text itself
fills it:

- **"All that was in her heart."** Verse 2 does not say she put her riddles
  to him; it says she spoke with him of everything in her heart. The episode
  turns on the difference between a test and a real question, and ties it
  to episode 139's listening heart (First Kings 3:9).
- **What she saw, not only what she heard.** Verse 5 lists the table, how the
  servants sat, the ministers' clothes, the cupbearers, and last of all the
  way up to the house of the Lord. The episode reads the list as wisdom that
  runs through a whole household, ending at prayer.
- **"The half was not told me"** and **"Happy are your men"**: the visitor
  praises the servants, not the gold, and then blesses the Lord who made
  Solomon king "to do justice and righteousness".
- **The almug-wood aside.** In the middle of the gifts the chapter stops to
  say the rare wood went into harps for the singers. The narrator enjoys the
  digression as one of their own.

## Telling it straight

- **Sheba hedged.** Usually Saba in what is now Yemen; no inscription yet
  names a queen in Solomon's century, and most of what is known of Saba is
  later. The Assyrian records of Sabaean tribute and of Arab queens are
  named as two centuries later.
- **Numbers hedged.** A talent's weight is unknown; "about thirty kilograms"
  is given as a common guess, so the hundred and twenty talents become
  "around three and a half tonnes". Verse 5's "ascent" or "burnt offerings"
  is given both ways, since Kings and Chronicles differ.
- **Legend labelled.** Josephus's name for her (Nikaule, queen of Egypt and
  Ethiopia), the later Jewish riddles and the Ethiopian *Kebra Nagast*
  (Makeda and Menelik) appear only in the wind-down, as tradition and
  legend, with respect for what the last means to the Ethiopian Orthodox
  Church.
- **No feast day claimed.** She has none. The sign-off points instead to the
  Roman-rite Mass of the Epiphany, which reads Isaiah 60 (camels, gold and
  frankincense from Sheba) and sings Psalm 72 (the gold of Sheba).

## The ending

The wind-down runs from Psalm 72 and Isaiah 60 to the wise men's gold,
frankincense and myrrh in Matthew 2, and then to Matthew 12:42, where Jesus
holds her up to a crowd that wants a sign: she came "from the ends of the
earth" for Solomon, "and something greater than Solomon is here". For a
bedtime show that is the gentlest possible moral: bring your hard
questions, the quiet ones too.

The episode leaves two sequels on the list: Hiram of Tyre's cedar rafts
(First Kings 5) and the ships of Tarshish and the ivory throne (First Kings
10:14-29), to be told with Deuteronomy 17's warning to kings about gold.
