# Do Hispanic Catholics leave the Church less than white Catholics?

Of US adults raised Catholic, born 1945-64 and interviewed 2000-2024, 66% of
non-Hispanic whites are still Catholic. For those born 1981-96 the figure is 48%
(interval 42 to 53%). Among Hispanics the same three cohorts read 74%, 76% and 70%.

The numbers come from the General Social Survey, 7,155 respondents aged 30 or
over, with design-based intervals and a logistic model whose standard errors
respect the survey's strata and clusters. A cohort-by-ethnicity interaction is
significant (Wald p = 0.015). First-generation immigrants retain more than
third-generation Catholics at equal cohort and ethnicity (odds ratio 1.41), which
accounts for most of the Hispanic level advantage but not its flatter trend.

Caveats: the youngest cohort is only seen up to age 43, the Hispanic youngest
cell has 385 people, and the 2021-24 surveys were mostly web interviews, which
earlier work in this programme found lowers reported attendance.
