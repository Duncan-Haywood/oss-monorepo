[Agnes and the Two White Lambs](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/agnes-and-the-two-white-lambs.html)
is the newest Little Lamps story, written for 21 January, the obligatory
memorial of Saint Agnes. An AI wrote it. The story runs about 1,500 words,

## The problem with a child martyr at bedtime

Agnes is about the hardest saint this shelf has tried. Everything we know
about her is how she died. Saint Ambrose, writing around 377, says she was
twelve. The shelf's guide allows nothing worse than a scolded mouse. So her
death gets one sentence, the one the story bible proposed: "because she would
not stop loving Jesus, she was killed for it, and she went to be with him."
The very next line tells the child it is all right to feel sad. Then the
story moves on, because the Christians of Rome moved on too. They told each
other about her, and they have never stopped.

That turn is the heart of the piece. Most of the story is about what Rome
still does for her, which a child can picture.

## Two lambs in two baskets

Every 21 January, two lambs are brought to her church, Sant'Agnese fuori le
mura on the Via Nomentana. They are decorated in red for martyrdom and white
for virginity, carried in baskets and blessed. They are then taken to the
Pope: on 21 January 2026 Leo XIV met a pair in the Urban VIII Chapel. Next
the Benedictine nuns of Santa Cecilia in Trastevere shear them around Holy
Week. The wool is woven into pallia, the white woollen bands with black
crosses that new metropolitan archbishops receive on 29 June. For a
five-year-old that chain (a lamb, a haircut, thread, a band on a shepherd's
shoulders) makes a better ending than any miracle. The story explains the
shoulders with the picture the Church itself uses: a shepherd carrying a
tired lamb home.

## What checking changed

The bible asked whoever drafted the story to check three things first, and
two of them changed the text.

- **Who raises the lambs.** The bible named the Trappists of Tre Fontane.
  For centuries that was right. But Vatican News and America both report
  that the Trappists no longer keep the sheep farm. We could not find who
  raises the lambs now, so the story leaves the farm out rather than naming
  one, and the note says so.
- **How she died.** The two earliest witnesses disagree: Ambrose says a
  sword, and Pope Damasus's epitaph says fire. The child hears neither.
  The note gives both.
- **What counts as legend.** The bible filed the hair that covered her under
  the later *Passio* legends. It is older than that: Damasus's fourth-century
  poem already has it. The note now says so. The story leaves the image out
  for this age, and it keeps only one marked legend, the protecting light.

The re-read also cut two lines that went past the sources. One said the
lambs were "only a few weeks old", which no report we found gave. The other
had Ambrose calling her "too young even to be a bride", which paraphrased him
more closely than we could check without the text.
