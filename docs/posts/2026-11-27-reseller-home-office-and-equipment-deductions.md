## The storage room: line 30

A home office normally has to be used only for the business. Storing inventory is an exception.
[IRS Publication 587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587) lets a seller deduct a storage space
that is not used only for the business when all of these hold: you sell products at wholesale or
retail, you keep the inventory at home for that business, home is the business's only fixed
location, you use the space regularly, and it is a separately identifiable space suitable for
storage. The basement corner of bins can count even if the family walks through it. Whether yours
qualifies is for you or your preparer to decide.

The [simplified method](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/simplified-option-for-home-office-deduction)
is $5 a square foot, with at most 300 square feet counted in any month. That caps it at $1,500
for a full year. A space used for part of the year is averaged over all 12 months, and a month
counts when the space was used on 15 or more days. Two limits catch people out. The deduction can
never be more than the profit before it (line 29). And under this method, the part that does not
fit is lost, not carried forward
([IRS FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/faqs-simplified-method-for-home-office-deduction)).
The regular method, on Form 8829, can carry it forward.

An invented example:

| | |
|---|---|
| 150 square foot storage room, used from April (9 months) | 112.5 sq ft average |
| Line 30 at $5 a square foot | $562.50 |
| The same year with $400.00 of profit on line 29 | $400.00 |
| Lost under the simplified method | $162.50 |

## The camera: the de minimis safe harbor

A camera, label printer, shelving or laptop is equipment you keep and use. It is not a supply and
it is not inventory. Under the de minimis safe harbor in the
[tangible property regulations](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tangible-property-final-regulations),
a business without an applicable financial statement (an audited one, for example) can deduct an
item or invoice of $2,500 or less in the year it is bought. You elect it every year by attaching a
statement titled "Section 1.263(a)-1(f) de minimis safe harbor election" to the timely filed
original return. Without the statement, the safe harbor does not apply. Anything over the limit
is depreciated, or expensed by election, on
[Form 4562](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-4562), which feeds line 13.

| Invented purchase | Where the worksheet puts it |
|---|---|
| Camera for listing photos, $899.00 | Line 27a, its own row |
| Thermal label printer, $189.99 | Line 27a, its own row |
| Laptop, $2,799.00 | Form 4562, then line 13 |

Stock you buy to resell never goes on this line. It is inventory, and it goes through cost of
goods sold.

## What Seller Books does with it

You save the storage space once for the year: its square feet, the whole home's square feet, and
the months you used it. The worksheet then works out line 30 and holds it to line 29, and says so
when the limit applies. The quarterly estimates take line 30 month by month to each period's end.
Equipment has its own expense category. Purchases at or under the limit land on line 27a with a
reminder about the election statement. Anything over it is named for Form 4562 and left out of
the totals, with a warning, so nothing is deducted twice. A large office or supplies expense that
may really be equipment is flagged.

The regular method (Form 8829) and the depreciation itself are not built. Your preparer does
those.
