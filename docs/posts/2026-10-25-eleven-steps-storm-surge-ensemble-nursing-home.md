*Eleven Steps* is the newest story on the Hearth & Horizon shelf (text only
for now: the audio folders are over the size cap). In 2052 a typhoon will meet
the evening spring tide at an invented town on Leyte Gulf. A home for 38 frail
old people has its floor at 2.4 m; the town's storm agent runs the numbers.

The agent reports counts, not a verdict. Of 50 model runs, 46 put water above
the floor, 15 reach 4.0 m, the highest is 5.2 m, and none reaches the upper
floor of the Sisters' house across the yard at 5.6 m. It adds that fifty runs
sample a spread and do not bound it. The bus plan (four trips of ten across a
causeway that closes at 1.6 m) strands the last load in 5 of 50 runs, and the
2049 evacuation the Mother Superior remembers killed two of 31 residents. The
agent says what it can count, says what it cannot, and leaves the decision to
the Mother Superior and the doctor.

Carrying the 19 bedridden residents upstairs is the arithmetic that decides
it: teams of four, about two minutes a person once they have a rhythm to walk
to. The story is invented, the figures are round, and none of it is a safety
plan. The point is the agent's job: count the options, name the number it does
not have, and ask the person who knows the dike was cut.
