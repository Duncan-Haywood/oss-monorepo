[Camillus and the Red Cross](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/camillus-and-the-red-cross.html) is an eight-minute bedtime story for ages 3 to 8. A boy from the hills of the Abruzzo grows taller than his father's door and becomes a soldier. He loses everything he owns at cards and dice, his shirt included by the usual account, and takes work carrying stones for the Capuchin friars at Manfredonia. In 1575 he turns to God on the road. Turned away from the friars because of a sore leg that never healed, he goes to the hospital of San Giacomo in Rome, where he becomes the man who tells the attendants to put "more heart into your hands". His Ministers of the Infirm wore a red cross and vowed to nurse the sick even in plague. He is the patron of the sick, of hospitals and of nurses, and his feast is 14 July.

The series bible proposed him as "a good, gentle children's turn": a saint whose sin was gambling and wasting what he had, not anything a four-year-old needs protecting from. Writing the story came down to three choices.

- **The losing is the comedy, and then it isn't.** Children understand "just one more game" very well. The list goes money, sword, coat, shirt, and it ends with the tallest young man in the street standing there with nothing. The story leaves the joke there and moves to the sore leg, which is where his life turns.
- **The "no" belongs to the friars.** The bible's craft checklist asks for a reasonable "no" in the middle. Here it is real history: the Capuchins twice took him as a novice and twice sent him away, because a friar went barefoot and his leg kept reopening. That refusal sends him to the hospital, so it reads as kind, and it is.
- **The nursing is told as verbs.** Bringing water, washing faces, feeding people who can't lift a spoon, turning pillows, opening windows, sitting with someone frightened at night. Those are the things a child can do for someone who is poorly, and the ending asks them to.

**What we could not check.** This session's network reached no outside reference (Wikipedia, New Advent and vatican.va were all blocked), so the grown-ups' note was written from the standard accounts of his life, and it says so. Each detail that rests on tradition is marked as tradition: the shirt, his mother's age, the donkey on the road, and the two sayings, which are given as paraphrases from his order's rule and tradition, not quotations. Saint Philip Neri, his confessor for a time, was cut from the story because the accounts of how far Philip backed the new congregation differ. The note also says plainly that the Camillian red cross has no link to the International Red Cross beyond the image. The one checkable date was checked in code: from 25 May 1550 to 2 February 1575 is 24.7 years, so the story says "nearly twenty-five".
