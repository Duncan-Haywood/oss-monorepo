Small clothing brands write "eco-friendly", "sustainable" and "carbon
neutral" without thinking. In the EU those are the phrases the new
consumer-protection rules go after first. Directive (EU) 2024/825 changes the
unfair-commercial-practices law so that, in member states from 27 September
2026, broad environmental words need recognised top environmental performance
behind them, and a claim that a product is carbon neutral because the maker
buys offsets is banned outright. Sustainability badges must rest on a
third-party certification scheme or a public body, and a future promise such
as "net zero by 2030" needs a public plan with an independent check.

The Clothing Design Studio now has a
[green-claims checker](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/greenclaims.html).
Paste a product description or a hangtag and it lists the phrases that fall
under those rules, says which are banned in that form and which only need
proof, and suggests wording that says the same true thing, such as "shell:
100% GOTS-certified organic cotton" instead of "eco-friendly". Nothing leaves
the browser.

What it is not: a lawyer. It is a short English phrase list, each country
wrote its own version of the directive, and the UK and US have separate
rules. A clean result means only that no listed phrase was found. The rules
were read from the directive's published summary, not checked with counsel.
Nobody outside the project has run real copy through it yet, so the false
positives and misses are unmeasured.

It sits in the "sell it" stage of the studio's launch path, beside the label
checker and the French textile eco-contribution page.
