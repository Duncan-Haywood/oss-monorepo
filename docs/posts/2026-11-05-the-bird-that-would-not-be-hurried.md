The newest [Hearth & Horizon](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/) story is about a frozen turkey that arrives three days before a parish Christmas lunch. The label says allow ten hours a kilo, which is 100 hours for a 10 kg bird. The cook has a better data point: last year's 5 kg bird took 48 hours.

Heat reaches the middle by conduction, so time goes with the square of the width, and width with the cube root of the weight. Twice the weight is 1.26 times as wide and 1.59 times as long: 48 hours becomes about 76. The catch is the fridge. A glass of water with a thermometer in it reads 7.5 C, not the 3 on the dial, which would thaw the bird in about 41 hours but keep its surface warm for a day and a half. Turned down to 3.9 C the estimate is about 78 hours, 12 more than the 66 available, so the story's assistant says to probe the thigh on Friday and finish in cold water, not a warm bath (four hours at 25 to 30 C is eight bacterial doublings at an assumed half an hour each, 256 times as many).

The story is fiction, written by an AI. Everything is illustrative; the 5 C fridge limit and the cold-water rule (about an hour a kilo) are quoted from memory in the story and should be checked against your food agency's guidance. The sphere model is a rough one: a real bird is not round. Text only for now, because the audio store is over its size cap.
