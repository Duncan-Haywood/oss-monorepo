**Why Thecla is hard to tell honestly.** Most saints on this show come from
Scripture, from a saint's own writing, or from a Life written by someone who
knew them. Thecla's story comes from one book, the second-century *Acts of
Paul and Thecla*, and we know roughly where it came from. Around the year 200,
Tertullian wrote (*On Baptism* 17) that a presbyter in Asia had written the
Acts of Paul, admitted it "out of love for Paul", and lost his office. Even
so, both Churches came to honour Thecla. The Orthodox call her Protomartyr
among women and Equal to the Apostles. Her shrine near Seleucia drew pilgrims
from across the Christian world. So the episode says plainly, in the welcome
and again in the notes, that the whole of her story is legend, and that a
real early woman is probably behind it.

**What the episode keeps from the source.** Titus's description of Paul, the
only early one we have: short, bald, bandy-legged, eyebrows meeting, "and
sometimes he had the face of an angel". It also keeps Theocleia saying her
daughter clings to the window "like a spider", the bracelets given to the
doorkeeper and the silver mirror to the jailer, the rain that puts out the
fire, and Paul's cloak sold for bread while the family fasts in an empty tomb.
Then come the lioness who lies down at Thecla's feet, the women throwing nard
and cassia into the arena, Tryphaena, who has lost a daughter and finds one,
and the commission "go and teach the word of God". The prayer in the sign-off
is Thecla's own from the Acts: "my helper in prison, my helper in the fire, my
helper among the beasts".

**Three points that needed care:**

- **The self-baptism in the arena pool.** This scene is the reason Tertullian
  wrote about the book at all: people were pointing to it as a woman
  baptising. The episode says that the Church does not teach self-baptism,
  explains why Tertullian brought it up, and presents the scene as a picture
  of longing for Christ, not as a model of the sacrament. It also quotes the
  Catholic teaching that God "is not bound by his sacraments".
- **Bedtime.** The pyre, the arena and Alexander's assault are all softened.
  The bulls and some of the beasts are left out, and the notes say so. The
  source has Paul say "I do not know her" when Alexander asks about her, which
  is puzzling, and the episode leaves it out.
- **Borrowed names.** Demas, Hermogenes, Onesiphorus and Alexander all come
  from the Second Letter to Timothy, and the episode points this out. It also
  notes that a real Queen Antonia Tryphaena lived in the region in Paul's
  time, and says that nobody knows whether the author borrowed her too.

**What is history.** Paul at Iconium (Acts 14). Tertullian's report. Egeria
visiting the shrine in 384, finding her friend the deaconess Marthana there
and reading the Acts of Thecla on the spot. Gregory of Nazianzus spending some
years at that shrine. The family name "Thecla" given to Macrina, as Gregory of
Nyssa reports. The cave, the rock that opened, and the cleft at Maaloula are
labelled as later legends.
