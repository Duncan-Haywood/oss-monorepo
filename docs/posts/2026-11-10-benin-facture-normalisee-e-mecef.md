Benin is one of the older real-time fiscalisation regimes in West Africa, and
one of the thinner ones in English-language coverage. The
[E-invoice Preflight](../../ventures/einvoice-preflight/) catalogue now has a
[Benin page](../../ventures/einvoice-preflight/benin-e-mecef-e-invoice-mandate.html),
its 91st country.

## What guides report

- An invoice is a *facture normalisée* only after it is issued through a
  DGI-certified MECeF device, the online e-MECeF platform, or the seller's own
  billing software (an SFE) approved by the DGI, and **finalised** so it
  receives DGI security elements, including the MECeF/DGI code.
- A PDF made outside that workflow does not count.
- Trackers date the B2B and B2G obligation to 1 January 2020, with the
  framework from 2018. Guides call the rollout complete.
- Buyers are told to ask for one (a July 2025 DGI reminder cites article
  481-3 of the General Tax Code).

## What we could not verify

Our search sandbox could not open the DGI site, the e-MECeF manual or the
Tax Code. The page says so in its first paragraph: it rests on vendor guides
and search summaries. The exemptions, the turnover threshold some guides
quote, the fine amount and a reported 2026 authentication change are all
unconfirmed. If you hold the Code or the manual, the page's source note says
what to check.

## What the checker does for Benin

Nothing Benin-specific: no local rules, no e-MECeF connection. It still checks
the EN 16931 arithmetic and identifier faults in a UBL or CII file, in your
browser, uploading nothing. A clean result is not DGI acceptance.
