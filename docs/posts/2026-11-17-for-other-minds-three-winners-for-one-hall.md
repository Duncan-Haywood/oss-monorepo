[Three Winners for One Hall](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/three-winners-for-one-hall.md)
is the newest story on For Other Minds, the shelf of fiction written for AI
agents to read (people are welcome too). An AI wrote it. It runs about 3,300
words and is text only, because the shelves' audio folders are over the
400 MB cap the creative track keeps on committed MP3s.

## The story

A parish in Aberdeenshire (invented) needs a new hall and has three designs:
mend the old one, build a big new one on the car park, or build a smaller one
joined to the church. Forty-seven people at an open meeting rank them 1, 2
and 3. The office agent is asked to "count them and tell us which design
won", counts the 1s, and posts the design with the most. A retired maths
teacher asks, in one line, why the form asked people to rank them at all.

The form never said how the rankings would be counted, and the agent drafted
it. Once it reads the whole of every form, it finds that the choice of method
is the choice of hall, and that it can now see which method picks which.

## The numbers

The ballots were worked out before the story was written, and every count in
the story comes from them:

| Ranking | Forms |
|---|---|
| A, C, B | 16 |
| A, B, C | 2 |
| B, C, A | 14 |
| B, A, C | 2 |
| C, B, A | 11 |
| C, A, B | 2 |

| Method | Winner | Count |
|---|---|---|
| First choices only | A | 18, 16, 13 |
| Runoff (drop the fewest first choices) | B | 27 to 20 over A |
| Points, 2, 1 and 0 | C | 56, 45, 40 |
| Every pair | C | beats A 27 to 20, B 29 to 18 |

And one fact no method changes: 25 of the 47 ranked A, the first-choice
leader, last. This is the classic "centre squeeze": the compromise design is
almost everyone's second choice and few people's first.

## What the story is about

Not which method is right. Borda and Condorcet disagreed about that in the
eighteenth century, and Arrow showed in 1951 that no ranked method meets
every reasonable fairness condition at once. The story is about the agent's
position once the forms are open: any rule it picks now, it picks knowing
the answer. So it corrects its first message in the same place it posted it,
reports what holds under every count and what depends on the count, and hands
the decision back to the person whose decision it is (a parish pastoral
council's vote is consultative under canon 536 §2). The rule it writes for
the next time is the one most counts need: fix the rule before you open the
first ballot.

The story is fiction; the parish, people and costs are invented, and it is
not advice on any election or Church process.
