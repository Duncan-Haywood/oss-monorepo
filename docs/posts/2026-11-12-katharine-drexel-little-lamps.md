[Katharine and the Question from the Pope](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/katharine-and-the-question-from-the-pope.html) is a nine-minute bedtime story for ages 3 to 8, the shelf's third American saint after Philippine Duchesne and Elizabeth Ann Seton.

It follows Katharine Drexel from a Philadelphia banker's house, where her stepmother Emma opened the front door to poor families every week, to a private audience with Pope Leo XIII in January 1887. She asked him to send missionaries to Native American people; he asked her why she should not become one herself. The story then goes west to the reservations, to the Sisters of Mercy novitiate in Pittsburgh, to the founding of the Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament on 12 February 1891, and on to Santa Fe and New Orleans, where her high school of 1915 became Xavier University of Louisiana.

Three choices came from checking sources rather than repeating them:

- **The Pope's words.** Biographies give the question in at least three wordings ("Why not, my child, yourself become a missionary?", "Why not become a missionary yourself, my child?", and in one homily "What about you?"). Rather than pick one and present it as his, the story tells it in indirect speech and the note for grown-ups lists the variants.
- **No dollar figure.** A lifetime total is often quoted, but we could not trace one to a source, and the money she spent was the income of a trust, not the capital. The story says "her share" and gives no sum.
- **The hard part named, gently.** Her schools were opened under legal segregation and met threats; some property was bought through a third party because sellers would not sell to her. The note says so without making any group the villain, and adds that the history of Catholic Indian schools also includes harms Native communities remember.

The series bible now proposes Kateri Tekakwitha next. The story was written by an AI and is not an official Church text. It is text only, because the shelf's audio is over the repository's size cap.
