[The Wise Woman of Tekoa and the Last Coal](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/155-the-wise-woman-of-tekoa-and-the-last-coal.md)
is episode 155 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, like the episodes
before it, because the creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line. The script is about 5,700 words, roughly
thirty-seven minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 154 (Hushai) left it
on the list, and it is the prequel that episode needed: how Absalom came to
be in Jerusalem at all.

## Why this story

Second Samuel 14 is the second time in four chapters that someone tells
David a story so that he will rule on himself. Episode 144 told the first,
Nathan's ewe lamb. The pairing is the spine of the episode.

- **The image the hall understood.** The widow says the family would
  "quench my coal which is left". The episode sets up the banked hearth
  early (bury the embers at night, blow on them in the morning, borrow a
  coal if they die) so that a listener hears the line the way the hall
  did, and it comes back at the end.
- **Three asks, three answers.** "Go home, I will give orders"; "if anyone
  says anything to you, bring him to me"; then the oath, "as the Lord
  lives, not one hair of your son shall fall". The episode slows down on
  how she moves a king from a kind brush-off to a sworn ruling.
- **A sword and a hand on the arm.** Nathan's parable made David condemn
  himself; hers makes him see he has already forgiven, in principle. The
  narrator says this once.
- **The line the chapter is remembered for.** "We are like water spilt on
  the ground, which cannot be gathered up again; but God devises means so
  that the banished one is not cast out." Verse 19 says Joab put "all these
  words" in her mouth, and the episode keeps that; an earlier draft had the
  narrator guess she wrote the line herself, which goes beyond the text, so
  the revision says only that she spoke it as if it were her own.
- **Caught, and honest.** "Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?" She
  admits it at once and still leaves the king his dignity. Then the house
  apart for two years, the barley field set on fire, and the kiss, with
  the next chapter's rebellion named but not told.

## Telling it straight

- **Bedtime lines.** Amnon's wrong to Tamar and his death at the sheep
  shearing are one sentence each. The book is plain about both, and the
  episode says it will not tell them.
- **Hedged out loud.** The last verse of chapter 13 reads two ways (the
  king longing for Absalom, or ceasing to march against him); the middle
  of 14:14 is hard Hebrew; whether "a wise woman" was a recognised role in
  a town is unknown; and whether she was really a widow is unknown too,
  since Joab asked her to play a mourner.
- **The law in one paragraph.** The avenger of blood and the six cities of
  refuge (Numbers 35, Deuteronomy 19), so that a listener sees why a
  fight-in-a-field case needed the king and not the village.
- **Tekoa from the text only.** Ira son of Ikkesh among the Thirty, Amos
  the herdsman, Rehoboam's fortress, and the Tekoites on Nehemiah's wall
  who mended two stretches while "their nobles did not put their necks to
  the work".
- **The Christian echo, once.** Luke 15's father, who ran, against
  David's "let him go to his own house; he is not to see my face". The
  sign-off is Psalm 103: "as a father has compassion on his children".

Invented, and flagged in the welcome, the notes and the front matter:
Joab's messenger at the gate, the woman's house, fig tree and hearth, her
walk past Bethlehem, the faces in the hall and her walk home.
