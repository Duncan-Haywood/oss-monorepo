[Sabbas and the Wild Ass at the Dry Stream](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/104-sabbas-and-the-wild-ass-at-the-dry-stream.md)
is episode 104 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,400
spoken words, roughly 48 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text only,
because the audio folders are still over the repository's 400 MB cap (423 MB
this run).

After Ignatius in the Basque country, this one goes back a thousand years and
east to the Judaean desert. Saint Sabbas (439 to 532) founded the Great Lavra
in the Kidron gorge in 483. It is Mar Saba, and monks have lived in it almost
without a break since. Episode 99, about John of Damascus selling baskets,
was set in the same monastery, so the two episodes now point at each other.

**Why this saint.** The series list had named him for weeks. He also has an
unusually good source. Cyril of Scythopolis wrote the Life about twenty-three
years after Sabbas died, from interviews with monks who knew him, and he
gives his dates and names his witnesses. Cyril also tells us that Sabbas
blessed him as a small boy in his father's house, and the episode ends its
story there.

**The scenes Cyril gives, told as his:**

- the boy who picked an apple early, stamped on it, and (Cyril says) never ate
  one again;
- Euthymius turning away an eager eighteen-year-old because a lavra is no
  place for the young, then later calling him "the young old man";
- fifty palm-leaf baskets a week, about one every two hours;
- a wild ass digging in the dry stream bed at night, showing him where the
  water ran under the gravel (the episode's title and its closing image);
- the patriarch answering monks who wanted a new abbot by ordaining Sabbas in
  front of them;
- a lion dragging him out of its cave by his cloak, and Sabbas offering to
  share, since they were made by the same hands;
- palace doorkeepers taking the seventy-two-year-old for a beggar, and the
  emperor asking which of the visitors was Sabbas.

**Honesty flags.** The oven, the pillar of fire over the cave church, the wild
ass and the lion are labelled Cyril's stories, neither proved nor dismissed.
Several details from memory were cut on the adversarial re-read because I
could not tie them to the source with confidence: a deathbed quotation, a
count of the monasteries he founded, the lion cave's location, and a claim
that visitors are still shown "the wild ass's well". Zenon, a young monk with
a water jug, is invented and the episode says so. The fifth- and
sixth-century quarrels over how to speak of Christ, which took Sabbas to
Constantinople twice, get one sentence and no sides, under the series' rule
to stay off disputed ground. The relics coming back from Venice in 1965, sent
by Pope Paul VI to an Orthodox monastery, is the kind of fact the show likes
to end on.
