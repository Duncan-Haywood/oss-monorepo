[The Sun at Three Degrees](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-sun-at-three-degrees.md)
is the newest story on the Hearth & Horizon shelf, written by an AI: about
5,000 words, text only for now. A drill engineer at a camp 18 km out in Titan's
dunes is dying of heart failure and asks for a priest for the first time in
forty-one years. The settlement's young people fly everywhere on strap-on
wings. It is night, the night lasts eight Earth days, and the safety rule says
nobody flies until the Sun is three degrees up.

The story is built on three pieces of arithmetic a reader can check.

**Flying is easy on Titan.** The power to hold a wing in level flight goes as
weight to the power 1.5, divided by the square root of the air's density.
Titan's gravity is 0.138 of Earth's and its air, nitrogen at 1.47 bar and about
94 K, is a bit over four times as dense as ours at sea level. For the same wing
and the same body, that comes to about 0.025 of the power, roughly a fortieth.
That is why human-powered flight on Titan is an old idea in popular science,
and the story's one speculative step is a settlement where teenagers learn it at
school.

**Carrying a passenger is not.** The same scaling bites the other way. A tandem
rig with two adults in heated suits weighs about 2.14 times a solo one, so for
the same wing it needs 2.14^1.5, about 3.1 times the power. The pilot can hold
that for about 25 minutes; the crossing takes 75. So the old priest cannot be
flown out, and the sacristan, who is an extraordinary minister of Holy
Communion, goes alone with the pyx. Church law allows that for Viaticum
(canon 911 §2). It does not allow her to anoint or absolve, and the story does
not pretend it does.

**Cold shrinks the wrong part.** The ground route needs a crawler with a new
bearing housing, and the print shop only has aluminium. From room temperature
to Titan's surface, aluminium alloy contracts about 0.4% and bearing steel about
0.2%, so a 120 mm bore made to the old steel drawing closes about 0.23 mm more
than the bearing it holds, ten times a normal press fit. The first test seizes;
the second housing is bored a quarter of a millimetre big so it fits once it is
cold.

The waiting is astronomy. Titan keeps one face to Saturn, so its solar day is
about 15.97 Earth days and its Sun moves about 0.94 degrees an hour across the
sky. At 7° north in the northern winter the Sun comes up at about 0.85 degrees
an hour, so "three degrees up" is about three and a half hours after a sunrise
nobody can see through the haze. The figures came from a short script with
inputs from memory; the story's closing note lists them all.

The faith side is handled the way the shelf's
[series bible](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/BIBLE.md) asks: the priest
refuses to hear a confession over the radio, because the Church does not accept
one, and tells the dying man what the Catechism (paragraph 1452) says about
perfect contrition instead. Nobody gets a miracle and nobody breaks the rule.
The sacrament gets there in time, the priest does not, and the ending counts
both.
