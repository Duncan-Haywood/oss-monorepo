[Paul and the Speech on the Steps of the Fortress](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/119-paul-and-the-speech-on-the-steps-of-the-fortress.md)
is episode 119 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,500
spoken words, roughly 48 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text
only, because the audio folders are over the creative prompt's 400 MB line
(474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** Episode 118 ended with Agabus tying himself up with
Paul's belt. The series list asked for the sequel, Acts 21:17 to 22:29,
where that prophecy comes true. Paul agrees to the elders' plan, a crowd
seizes him anyway, and he speaks to that crowd in chains.

**How the story is built.** The episode turns on one fence and one
staircase. The fence is the temple balustrade, three cubits high, whose
stones warned any foreigner who went past it that his death would follow
(Josephus, *Jewish War* 5.193-194). The staircase runs from the fortress
Antonia down into the temple porticoes (5.238-247). The rumour that Paul had
taken Trophimus past the fence starts the riot (Acts 21:29). The stairs are
where Paul turns round and lifts his hand. The wind-down ends with the one
complete warning stone, found in 1871 by Clermont-Ganneau and now in
Istanbul.

**What the episode is careful about.**

- *The speech is Luke's, not ours.* Acts 22:1-21 is told in a plain
  rendering with nothing added. The narrator comments only after the crowd
  stops listening at the word "Gentiles".
- *James, without the family argument.* Paul calls him "the Lord's brother"
  (Galatians 1:19). The episode calls him a close kinsman of Jesus, not
  another son of Mary, and says the two traditions tell the family tree a
  little differently. It stays off that disputed ground, as the series
  rules require.
- *Readings named as readings.* Whether Paul minded the elders' plan is
  read from 1 Corinthians 9:20 and labelled a reading. So is the echo of
  "away with this man" (Luke 23:18) in the crowd's "away with him"
  (Acts 21:36). That Claudius Lysias bought his citizenship under Claudius
  is called a likely guess from his name, set beside Cassius Dio 60.17 on
  citizenship sold in that reign.
- *Sources kept apart by date.* The chamber of the Nazirites comes from the
  Mishnah (*Middot* 2.5), written down long after the temple fell, and the
  episode says so. Josephus puts the Egyptian's followers at thirty
  thousand, against the commander's four thousand, and the episode notes
  the difference. The knees "like a camel's" are given as Hegesippus's
  report (Eusebius, *Church History* 2.23).
- *Bedtime.* The beating in the outer court gets one sentence. The flogging
  never happens, because Paul asks a centurion one question (22:25).

**Invented, and said so.** Acts says nothing about the four men under the
vow. The episode gives one of them a story: Natan, a potter's son, whose
thirty-day vow stretched to fifty-one days because his family could not
afford the lambs, and whose seven-year-old sister calls him "the haystack".
The epilogue, in which his vow is finished and his hair goes into the fire
as Numbers 6:18 directs, is invented and labelled as such. Nothing invented
changes what Luke reports.

The closing blessing is the priestly blessing of Numbers 6:24-26, from the
same chapter that gives the Nazirite vow.
