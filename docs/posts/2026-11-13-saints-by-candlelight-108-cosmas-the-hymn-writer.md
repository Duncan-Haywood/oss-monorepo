[Cosmas and the Canon for Christmas Night](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/108-cosmas-and-the-canon-for-christmas-night.md)
is episode 108 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,300
spoken words, roughly 47 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text only,
because the audio folders are still over the repository's 400 MB cap (423 MB
this run).

**Why this saint.** Episode 99 told John of Damascus's story and left a second
boy at the edge of it: Cosmas, the orphan John's father is said to have adopted,
taught on the same bench by the same ransomed monk. The series list had
"Saint Cosmas the hymn-writer" waiting. He is also a good fit for the season.
On 21 November the Byzantine churches start singing his Christmas verse,
"Christ is born, glorify him", five weeks before Christmas. This episode goes
up a week before that day.

**The problem with Cosmas.** Almost nothing is known about his life. John wrote
treatises. Cosmas wrote hymns, and hymns get sung for twelve centuries without
anyone remembering who wrote them. The episode says that out loud in its first
few minutes. It then builds on what does survive, which is the hymns
themselves:

- His Christmas canon opens with the first words of Gregory of Nazianzus's
  Christmas sermon (Oration 38, about 380), stitched onto the song of Moses at
  the Red Sea. That links back to episode 92, Gregory's little church in
  Constantinople. One sermon, one monk four centuries later, and a hymn sung
  every year since.
- In the service books, the Christmas and Theophany canons each come in a pair:
  Cosmas's first, then an iambic canon credited to John, in a strict classical
  metre. So the two brothers still stand side by side on the same night, and
  that gives the episode its idea.
- The odes are given only in our own plain paraphrase. They are the Jonah ode,
  where the sea monster gives Jonah up "like a newborn", and the ninth ode,
  "the cave is heaven".

**Honesty flags.** The adoption, the Italian teacher and the two brothers going
to Mar Saba together all come from the much later Life of John. The episode
says so, and it names Andrew Louth's doubt that Cosmas was ever at Mar Saba.
"More honourable than the cherubim" is credited to him "by long tradition". The
acrostic in the Christmas canon is attributed to the scholars, not claimed.

On the adversarial re-read I cut four things I could not pin down firmly
enough: the name of the patriarch who made him bishop, a claim that the
Pentecost canons pair the brothers the same way, a line about paper arriving in
Damascus (too early by decades), and a description of Gregory's looks. His
dates of birth and death are left unknown, because they are.

**The invented part.** Elias, the invented young monk of episode 99, comes back
to sharpen pens in Cosmas's cell. The new invention is Andreas, a seasick
fisherman's boy at Maiuma, the harbour of Gaza. He meets the new bishop on the
harbour wall: a man from Damascus and the desert who has never seen the sea.
Andreas grows up to sing the first verse on Christmas night, with a voice that
cracks on the first word. "Breathe first" is the line the wind-down hands to
the listener. All of it is labelled as invented in the welcome, the notes and
the front matter.
