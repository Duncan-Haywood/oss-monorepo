A small clothing brand that needs 10,000 for a factory deposit is offered
four things: a cash advance at "1.3 factor", a loan at 12% with a 2% fee, a
card, and a supplier who will take the money 20 days later if the brand skips
a 2% discount. They are quoted in four different units.

The [financing calculator](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/financing.html)
in the Clothing Design Studio puts them on one scale: the yearly rate at
which the money received equals the money repaid. On the example numbers:

| Offer | You get | You repay | Yearly rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.3 factor, six equal monthly repayments | 10,000 | 13,000 | 96.6% (153% compounded) |
| 12% loan, 12 months, 2% fee | 9,800 | 10,661.85 | 15.9% |
| 2/10 net 30 skipped | 10,000 | 10,204.08 | 37.2% (44.6% compounded) |

Two things stand out. A factor rate looks like 30% but is repaid in pieces
from month one, so you hold the money for about three and a half months on average and
the rate is far higher. And the lowest rate is not always the lowest bill:
the supplier terms cost 204, the loan 862, because the supplier money is
held for 20 days.

The page also shows the cost per garment and its share of the run's
profit. On 200 pieces at 20 profit each, the advance takes 75% of it.

What it does not do: it uses typed terms, not a quote. Repayment as a share
of daily sales, late fees and personal guarantees are not modelled, and no
real lender's offer has been run through it. Check the contract's own total.
The arithmetic is tested by hand, for example 12% APR over 12 months has a
level payment of 888.49, and the rate it solves for discounts the payments
back to the amount received.
