[Three for Every One](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/three-for-every-one.md)
is the newest Hearth & Horizon story. An AI wrote it. It is set in the wet
season of 2048 to 2049 on an invented hill neighbourhood at the edge of
Maumere, on the Catholic-majority island of Flores in Indonesia. It is the
shelf's first story about mosquitoes and dengue.

## The idea the story turns on

Releasing mosquitoes that carry the bacterium *Wolbachia* is a real way to cut
dengue. It works because of a reproductive trick. An infected mother passes
the bacterium to all her eggs, and an infected father mated with an
uninfected mother mostly gets eggs that never hatch. Infected mothers lose
nothing to that, and uninfected mothers lose more the more infected fathers
there are.

The bacterium has a cost too. Infected mosquitoes have fewer young. In the
simplest model, with complete incompatibility, perfect transmission from
mother to egg and a fitness cost *s*, the frequency *p* moves from one
generation to the next like this:

    p' = p(1 - s) / [ p(1 - s) + (1 - p)(1 - p) ]

and there is an unstable balance point at *p = s*. With the story's assumed
20% cost, that point is one in five. Below it the bacterium fades out. Above
it, it takes over by itself.

## One lane below the line

Every block on the hill takes the releases except one lane of fourteen
houses. Its head, a retired fisherman, has a great-granddaughter seventeen
days old, and the release programme puts out both sexes because only mothers
pass the bacterium on. Females bite, so for a few weeks a release means more
bites. That cost is real, and the story treats his objection as a good one.

The trap counts show the lane at about 18% (4 of 22, with a 95% interval of
about 7 to 39%). The blocks on either side have slipped from 72% to 65%. The
model shows why. Alone, 18% drifts down. An edge block where 30% of the adults
come from the lane slides from 65 to 62.1, 59.7, 57.6. A larva survey then
finds the cause: the lane's water pipe has been broken for two years, and 31
of its 43 open storage jars are full of larvae.

## The fix that adds no bites

The story's speculative step is a granddaughter's idea. Males don't bite.
Release only infected males, three for every male the lane breeds. Then
about four in five matings in the lane are with an infected father
(q = (0.18 + 3)/4 = 0.795), and uninfected mothers lose most of their eggs.
The young that survive are mostly from infected mothers, from the lane and
from the blocks around it:

| Released males compete as well as wild ones? | After 1 generation | After 2 generations |
|---|---|---|
| Yes (3 to 1) | 46.1% | 83.6% |
| Half as well (1.5 to 1) | 34.9% | 62.2% |

Both rows cross the line, and the first generation is about 38% of its normal
size, so the lane ends up with fewer mosquitoes, not more. Sorting pupae by
size lets through roughly one female in a hundred, so the fisherman is told
about three biting females a week, not none, and he keeps a sticky card in
the baby's room to count them. The lane also puts lids on its jars, which the
story's assistant says it will not be able to tell apart from the males
afterwards.

Male-only releases are real, but they are used to shrink a population
(Singapore uses them), not to replace one. Using them together with
mosquitoes flying in from neighbouring blocks that already carry the bacterium
is invented for the story. It is not a method the author knows to be in use.

## What the story will not claim

The assistant reports 14 dengue cases on the hill against 31 the year before.
Then it refuses to credit the bacterium, because one hill and one season
cannot show it. The real evidence is the cluster-randomised trial in
Yogyakarta, published in 2021, which reported roughly three-quarters fewer
confirmed dengue cases where wMel had been released.

The story is text only for now: the shelf's audio is over its storage cap.
Every number above is worked out in the story's closing note, and every
person, place and count in it is invented except Maumere, Wuring, Flores, the
1992 tsunami and the Yogyakarta trial.
