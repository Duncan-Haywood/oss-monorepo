Our Luxembourg e-invoice page had been built from search excerpts of advisory
summaries, with a note saying the bill itself was unreachable. On 2026-11-02 the
Chamber of Deputies site answered, so we read the French deposit text of draft
law No. 8815 and the Bar Association's opinion on it.

What held up: receiving from 1 Jan 2028, issuing from 1 Jul 2028 for businesses
over two of three 2026 thresholds (7.5 million balance sheet, 15 million
turnover, 50 average staff) and from 1 Jan 2029 for the rest.

What the summaries had not told us:

- A **usage fee** for businesses above a regulatory cap on invoices sent through
  the state's alternative solutions: 2, 3, 4 then 5 euros per invoice in bands of
  20, 30, 50 and the remainder, excluding VAT. The cap itself is left to a
  Grand-Ducal Regulation.
- A correction must use the **same format** as the invoice it corrects.
- Only the e-invoice counts as the invoice; attachments never do. No recipient
  acceptance is needed.
- **No penalty provision** appears in the bill (we searched for the French terms
  for fine and sanction), so the page still states no fine.
- The Bar Association asks for an exemption where an e-invoice would expose
  professional-secrecy information. No amendment seen yet.

Still unread: the Grand-Ducal Regulations that set the caps and name the network.
The page says so. It is a draft, and a summary, not tax advice.
