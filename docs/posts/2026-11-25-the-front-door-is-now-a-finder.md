Until today the home page of this site was a catalogue. Under the headline
sat a tool count, a scrolling marquee of tool names, a grid of category
tiles, tiles by type ("self-hostable apps", "command-line tools"), a
featured row, a showcase, a research band and the latest posts. Every one of
those was true, and together they answered the wrong question. A roofer or a
machine shop owner does not arrive wanting to know how many calculators
exist. They arrive with one problem.

The owner put it plainly on 2026-11-25: "not so much clutter on home page we
wanted target marketing sales pipeline not a catalog of random stuff", and
"easy navigation to find your profession and your problem".

## What the page asks now

Two questions, in the hero:

1. **What kind of business do you run?** Five cards for the businesses
   with a product in the works (construction, manufacturing, online
   sellers, EU e-invoicing, nonprofits), then a row of fourteen more:
   restaurants, home services, beauty, trucking, freelancers and the rest,
   plus "something else".
2. **What is hardest for you right now?** The problems listed are the ones
   each product is built around, in the customer's words: getting the
   monthly pay application right before the general contractor's cutoff,
   turning an RFQ's drawings into a priced quote, knowing your real profit
   after every platform's fees.

Each problem links to the product built for it, labelled live or early
access. Only one product, Grant Autopilot, is live today; the page reads
that from the same file the products page uses, so it cannot say "live" of
anything that is not. The fourteen businesses without a product yet say
so plainly: their problems lead to the free tools on their own page, and a
link asks which job to build for them first. "Something else" lists every trade page, the
assistant at /ask/, and the form for describing a problem nobody has built
for yet.

It works without JavaScript: the choices are radio buttons and the panels
appear through plain CSS, so a browser with scripts off still gets the
whole flow.

## What moved off it

The catalogue is still there, one link away at /c/, and the search box sits
below the products for anyone who needs one number rather than a product.
Research, writing, guides and updates are a single row of links at the
bottom. The "deploy your own copy" pillar and the "no accounts or sign-up"
promise are gone: self-deploy was retired on 2026-11-24, and the products
now have accounts.

## What we will watch

Each business's problem panel is marked up for the site's own first-party
funnel counter: a view is counted when a visitor picks that business, and a
click on each problem is counted against its product. Usage on
this site has mostly been unmeasured; this is a place where a reading will
tell us which product to finish first.
