The question we hear most from finance teams is not "what is EN 16931" but "what do I have to be ready for, and when". Our mandates hub answers it country by country, which is the wrong shape for someone who sells into six countries and wants one timeline.

So the free E-invoice Preflight site now has a [deadlines page](../../ventures/einvoice-preflight/e-invoicing-deadlines-2027.html): 18 dated milestones in 14 countries between 2 November 2026 and the end of 2027, grouped by quarter. The near ones: the Dominican Republic's small-taxpayer wave on 15 November 2026; the Philippines' first deadline on 31 December 2026; and a crowded 1 January 2027 with Denmark, Norway, Poland, Slovakia, Spain (Verifactu software, postponed) and the UAE all on or around it.

Two design choices worth saying out loud. First, the page is generated from the same JSON as the hub table, the calendar feed and the countdown, and a test fails if it goes stale, so a date fixed on a country page flows through. Second, only full day-month-year dates are listed. "From 2027" for Germany's issuing duty is real but has no day, so it stays in the hub table instead of being guessed onto 1 January.

Dates here are summaries of public guidance, some from secondary sources, and the country pages say where sources disagree. It is not tax advice. Subscribe to the `.ics` feed if you would rather have the dates land in your calendar.
