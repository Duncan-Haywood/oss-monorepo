The newest Hearth & Horizon story, [The Three Blank Pages](https://github.com/Duncan-Haywood/oss-monorepo/blob/main/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-three-blank-pages.md), is about printing a stapled booklet. A parish history runs to 37 pages, and an A4 sheet folded once carries four A5 pages, so 37 / 4 = 9.25 means ten sheets and a 40-page booklet with three blanks.

The pages on one sheet aren't neighbours. For sheet k of ten, the front is pages (42 − 2k) and (2k − 1) and the back is 2k and (41 − 2k): the outer sheet is 40|1 and 2|39, the centre one 22|19 and 20|21. The story's fix is a 12-page dummy folded from scrap paper (12|1, 2|11; 10|3, 4|9; 8|5, 6|7), which also tests a printer's booklet setting. The three blanks become a map, a source list and a "tell us what we got wrong" page. At 120 copies it is 1,200 sheets either way: three reams, whether 36 or 40 pages.

Everyone is invented; the arithmetic is exact apart from a guessed millimetre of creep. Text only for now, as the audio store is over its size cap.
