[Egeria and the Garden Where John Baptized](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/129-egeria-and-the-garden-where-john-baptized.md)
is episode 129 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script is
about 6,800 spoken words, roughly 44 minutes at the narrator's pace. It is
text only, like episodes 30 to 128: the audio folders hold 577 MB, over the
creative prompt's 400 MB line.

**Why this one.** `platform/ci/creative_due.py` drew the podcast for this
session, no open pull request was writing an episode, and the first
suggestion on the series' list was the sequel episode 128 left: chapters
13 to 16 of Egeria's letter.

**What the letter gives.** Monks from the land of Uz, visiting Jerusalem,
make her want to see the tomb of Job, "if one can call it a toil, when a
person sees their wish fulfilled". The road is eight staging posts. In a
valley of vines and water she finds a village called Sedima, with a small
hill and a church on top that the people call, in Greek, "where
Melchizedek". The priest says this was Salem. Egeria remembers John 3:23,
"John also was baptizing at Aenon near Salim", and asks where that is. Two
hundred paces, he says, and walks her to a spring in an orchard called the
garden of Saint John. Every Easter the village's baptisms happen there, and
the newly baptized walk back to the church with candles, singing psalms.
He gives her fruit from the garden as a blessing. Further on are Tishbe,
Elijah's town, and the brook Cherith with a hermit beside it. At Carneas, a
desert monk had been shown where to dig and found a cave and a stone
carved with Job's name. A tribune was building a church over it, and it
was not finished when she came.

**Three honesty calls.**

- *Salem.* The priest was sure his village was Melchizedek's city. Many
  readers, Jewish and Christian, take Salem to be Jerusalem (Psalm 76:2).
  The episode says so and leaves the question open, as Egeria did.
- *The Roman Canon.* Melchizedek's bread and wine are in the Roman Mass's
  great prayer. Egeria worshipped in Jerusalem's Greek liturgy and her own
  western rite, so the script doesn't claim she heard those words. It dates
  the prayer by Ambrose, who quotes it in *On the Sacraments* within a few
  years of her journey.
- *Leaving details out.* A draft line gave Carneas an older name and named
  the provinces on its border. I wasn't sure enough of either to put them
  in the letter's mouth, so both came out. So did a line saying the church
  was finished later, which the letter doesn't say.

**What is invented, and said so.** The donkey-boy Malchus comes back from
episode 128, this time with a very tall, very quiet cousin called
Zacchaeus. The donkeys, the cold-water complaint, the joke about who
brings the hermit his supper, and every word anyone says apart from the
letter's are invented too.

The Scripture is told straight: Genesis 14, Psalm 110 and Hebrews 7 for
Melchizedek; John 3:22-30 for "he must increase, but I must decrease",
said at a spring; 1 Kings 17 for Elijah and the ravens; and Job from the
ashes to the whirlwind to the three daughters with names. The episode
closes with Job 19:25, "I know that my Redeemer lives."
