[Barzillai and the Old Man at the Jordan](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/152-barzillai-and-the-old-man-at-the-jordan.md)
is episode 152 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, because the
creative audio folders are at 577 MB, over the creative track's 400 MB
line. The script is about 6,600 words, roughly forty-three minutes at the
narrator's pace. Episode 150 (Mephibosheth) left it on the series' list.

## Why this story

Barzillai gets about a dozen verses in the whole Bible, and most of them
are a list. Second Samuel 17:28-29 says what three men brought to David's
people at Mahanaim: beds, basins, earthen pots, wheat, barley, flour,
roasted grain, beans, lentils, honey, curds, sheep and cheese. Their whole
recorded reason is one line: "the people are hungry and weary and thirsty
in the wilderness." The episode is built on that list. Beds and basins are
what someone thinks of who has looked at tired families, not at an army.

The other half is his answer at the Jordan in Second Samuel 19:34-37,
when David offers him a place in Jerusalem: can I taste what I eat, can I
still hear the singing men and singing women, let me go home and die near
the grave of my father and my mother, and take Chimham instead. The
episode reads it as dry humour and contentment, not sadness, and sets it
beside Ecclesiastes 12 and Psalm 90 ("threescore years and ten, or by
reason of strength fourscore": Barzillai is exactly fourscore).

## Telling it straight

- **The three men are an odd crowd, and the book says so.** Machir is the
  man from Lo-debar who had sheltered Mephibosheth (episode 150). Shobi is
  "son of Nahash, from Rabbah of the Ammonites", the enemy capital; that he
  is the son of the Nahash whose heir shamed David's envoys in Second
  Samuel 10 is what many readers think, and the script hedges it.
- **The war is told quietly.** Absalom's death in the oak is one sentence,
  and David's "O my son Absalom" is given in full, because the camp
  Barzillai fed is the camp that heard it.
- **Hedged, not asserted:** that Barzillai means "iron" and Rogelim
  "fullers", that Chimham was his son (First Kings 2:7 speaks of "the sons
  of Barzillai", which makes it likely), and that the "lodging place of
  Chimham" near Bethlehem in Jeremiah 41:17 is named after him. The
  script does not repeat the guess some commentators have made, that it was the inn of the
  Nativity.
- **A draft slip.** The first draft had Barzillai say "I am eighty years
  old today". The Hebrew "this day" is an idiom for "now", not a birthday,
  so the line was changed.

## The wind-down

Two late echoes close the episode. On his deathbed David tells Solomon to
let the sons of Barzillai eat at his table (First Kings 2:7): with a
kingdom to hand over, he remembered the cheese. And after the exile, Ezra
2:61 and Nehemiah 7:63 list a priestly family called the sons of
Barzillai, named for an ancestor who married one of Barzillai's daughters
and took his father-in-law's name. Five centuries on, it was still a name
worth carrying.

The household at Rogelim, the loading of the donkeys, the evenings in the
camp and the goats at the ford are invented, and the script says so in its
welcome, its notes and its front matter. Two sequels went onto the list in
[PRODUCTION.md](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/PRODUCTION.md): the
flight up the Mount of Olives with Ittai and Zadok (Second Samuel 15), and
the two runners from the battle (Second Samuel 18).
