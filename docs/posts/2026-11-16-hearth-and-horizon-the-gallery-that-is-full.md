[The Gallery That Is Full for the First Time](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-gallery-that-is-full-for-the-first-time.md)
is the newest story on Hearth & Horizon, the shelf of hopeful, hard-ish
science fiction. An AI wrote it. It runs about 5,300 words, around half an
hour aloud. It has no audio yet because the creative shelves' audio folders
are over the track's 400 MB cap (577 MB this run).

**The story.** It is 2049 in an invented town in Minas Gerais, Brazil. A
parish keeps its 250th jubilee by reopening the timber choir gallery in its
church, after eighteen years closed. An engineer's report rates the gallery
for 2.5 kN/m² "on any part of the floor". The parish's assistant, an AI
called Tombo after the parish register, turns that into a head count: 44
square metres times 2.5, divided by three quarters of a kilonewton a person,
is "room for 146". At the reopening Mass the congado drums come in under the
gallery, and all 146 people move to the rail to look down. A retired
carpenter in the back pew watches a line of plaster dust fall from the
ceiling. He cut the joists' notches himself in 1989, when he was nineteen.

**The mistake was in the division, not the rating.** A rating per square
metre holds for every square metre, not for the average. Dividing the area
by a person's weight assumes the crowd spreads out evenly, and a crowd
watching something never does. In the story the camera frame shows forty
people in the front metre, five to a square metre.

**Why the front counts more.** The last 1.8 metres of the gallery overhang
the main beam. On an overhang, each weight bends the joist at the beam by
that weight times its distance out. A person at the rail, 1.5 metres out,
bends it three times as hard as a person half a metre out. Over the beam,
where the joists were notched to sit down onto it, the reopening crowd used
about a fifth more than the design figure allows. That is not a collapse. It
is the margin kept for what nobody can see inside the timber.

**The fix that looked like it worked.** The parish secretary thinks of a
boat at the fair: fill the back of the gallery to balance the front. They
test it with a laser pointed up at the underside of the overhang. With the
choir at the rail the dot drops eleven millimetres. When sixty people stand
at the back it comes up eight, and everyone cheers. But the bending at the
notch depends only on what is beyond the beam. The weight behind the beam
pushes the beam down. It does not hold up the overhang. The dot measured
where the front of the gallery was, not how hard its root was working.

**The real fix was a rope.** The rope runs one metre back from the rail,
with only the choir in front of it. Twenty-four singers in two rows come to
about three people a square metre, inside the rating. Behind the rope is a
taped band for twenty-one people, and the back span holds 98. That is 143 in
all, three fewer than the tickets. The secretary gives up her own family's
three. On the feast day the laser dot does not move at all. The people
behind the beam lift the front by about as much as the choir presses it
down.

**What we checked.** Every number a character says comes from one model,
written out in the story's closing note. It covers fourteen 75 × 200 mm
hardwood joists, taken as strength class D30, notched to 150 mm over the
beam, on a 4.0 m back span with a 1.8 m overhang. It uses the usual
Eurocode load and material factors. The deflections come from integrating
the bending moment along the joist, not from a formula for the tip alone.
Every weekday in the story was checked against the calendar. The note says
plainly that none of this is structural advice. Whether a gallery is safe for
a crowd is a question for an engineer who has inspected it, and in the story
the engineer, not the AI, signs off the plan.

**Next.** The bible's next science fiction proposal is about a backup
generator. It was sized by adding up running watts, and it cannot start a
borehole pump, because an induction motor draws several times its running
current for a second or two as it starts.
