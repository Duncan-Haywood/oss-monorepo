[Jethro and the Judges for the Small Cases](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/140-jethro-and-the-judges-for-the-small-cases.md)
is episode 140 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like episodes 30 to 139 it is text
only for now. The creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, which is over
the track's 400 MB cap.

Episode 139 put the judges at Jerusalem's gate down to "the oldest piece of
management advice in the Bible", Jethro telling Moses to hand the small
cases to others, and left that story for another night. This episode tells
it. It follows Exodus 18 closely, in the episode's own plain wording, and
runs to about 6,300 words, roughly forty minutes at the narrator's pace.

## What the chapter actually says, and what it doesn't

Exodus 18 is short, and most of it is a family visit. Jethro hears the
news, brings Zipporah and the two boys across the desert, and sends word
ahead: "I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you." Moses goes out,
bows, kisses him, and "they asked each other how they were". Moses tells
him everything, and Jethro blesses the Lord ("now I know that the Lord is
greater than all gods"). Then he eats bread with Aaron and the elders
"before God". The advice comes only on the second day. The episode keeps
that order. The reunion and the meal carry as much weight as the
management lesson.

The advice is given nearly verbatim, because it is the heart of the story,
and three of its phrases shape the episode:

- **"Both you, and these people with you."** Jethro is worried about the
  queue as well as about Moses. The invented people in the line are there
  to make that visible: a goat that ate one sandal, two sisters fighting
  over a cooking pot they carried out of Egypt, a tent peg, and a boy with
  a question about manna on the sabbath.
- **"If God so commands you."** The old priest offers counsel and sends it
  to God. He doesn't give an order.
- **"Moses listened."** The man who spoke with God at the bush takes advice
  from his father-in-law and acts on it. The episode treats that as the
  point to remember.

Deuteronomy 1:9-18 has Moses' own retelling and his charge to the new
judges, "hear the small and the great alike". That ties this episode back
to the gate in Jerusalem in episode 139.

## What the episode does not claim

The Bible calls Moses' father-in-law Reuel in Exodus 2 and Jethro from
Exodus 3 on, and a Hobab turns up in Numbers 10. The episode names all
three as the text has them and leaves the puzzle to the scholars. The book
doesn't say when or why Moses sent Zipporah and the boys back, so neither
does the episode. Some readers ask whether the visit came before or after
the Law was given at Sinai. The episode tells it where the book puts it and
mentions the question in the notes. One fact comes from outside the Bible:
the synagogue names the Torah portion that opens with this chapter, and
holds the Ten Commandments, *Yitro*, after Jethro. Jethro has no feast day.
Moses is remembered on 4 September in the Orthodox calendar and in the
Roman Martyrology. The closing prayer is Psalm 90, which the Psalter calls
"a prayer of Moses, the man of God", ending on "establish the work of our
hands".

The journey, Jethro's talk with his grandsons, the people in the queue and
the next morning in the camp are invented. The episode says so in the
welcome, in the notes and in its `fiction` field.

The series list gains two sequels: the seven daughters at the well of
Midian, where Moses first came to supper, and the seventy elders of
Numbers 11 with Eldad and Medad. Nathan and the ewe lamb is still waiting.
