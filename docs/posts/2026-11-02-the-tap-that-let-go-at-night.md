An invented Peruvian village's tap fitting bursts for the third time, always between midnight and five. The committee's assistant works out why: 75 metres of fall is about 7.4 bar with the taps shut, and a busy hour loses about 20 metres to friction in 2 km of narrow pipe, so the fittings (rated 6.3 bar) see the most pressure when nobody is using water.

A break-pressure tank halfway down halves the standing head to about 3.7 bar, leaving about 5 bar even with the pulse from a slammed tap. A borrowed gauge also shows the phone's map was out by 3 metres. Figures are illustrative; the story does not claim the water is safe to drink.
