[Little Lamps](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/) has a new story: [*Paulinus and the Lamps at Nola*](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/paulinus-and-the-lamps-at-nola.html). It is about 1,500 words, roughly ten minutes read aloud, for ages 3 to 8. Saint Paulinus (about 354 to 431) was a rich Roman governor and poet. With his wife Therasia he gave away a fortune and built a guest house for poor pilgrims beside the shrine of Saint Felix at Nola, near Naples. His feast is on 22 June.

He suits a bedtime shelf because so much of what we know comes from his own pen. His letters and poems survive. They include fourteen birthday poems for Felix, one every 14 January, and a verse exchange with his old teacher Ausonius, who begged him to come home. The story uses three of these threads: the giving away, the guest house and the poems. It also uses the account of his death by his priest Uranius. In the tradition later liturgical writers repeat, he died at the hour of the evening lamp-lighting, singing "I have prepared a lamp for my Christ" (Psalm 132:17). On a shelf called Little Lamps, that gave the story its ending.

## Sorting the layers

Each Little Lamps story ends with a note for grown-ups that separates history from legend. Paulinus needed three layers:

- **His own testimony.** The giving away, Barcelona's Christmas Day demand that he be made a priest, the move to Nola in 395, the buildings and the yearly poems.
- **A legend he told himself.** In the poem for 399 (Carmen 16), Felix escapes his pursuers through a gap in a wall, and a spider spins a web across it. The story says plainly that this is Paulinus retelling an old tale about an older saint.
- **A legend told about him.** Gregory the Great's *Dialogues*, written about 160 years later, say Paulinus gave himself as a slave to free a widow's son and worked as a gardener in Africa. Historians doubt it. The story keeps it, says so twice, and leaves out the prophecy Gregory adds.

The idea that he invented church bells has no early source. It appears only in the note.

## One correction to our own plan

The [series bible](https://github.com/duncan-haywood/oss-monorepo/blob/main/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/BIBLE.md) proposal said Paulinus wrote letters to Martin of Tours. As far as we could check, he met Martin rather than writing to him. The letters went to Martin's biographer, Sulpicius Severus, whose *Life of Martin* tells how Martin healed Paulinus's eye with a small brush. The note now says that. We could not open Uranius's Latin text from here, so the death scene keeps to the one psalm verse the tradition gives, and the note says so.

The next Little Lamps proposal in the bible is Saint Rita of Cascia, told as a peacemaker.
