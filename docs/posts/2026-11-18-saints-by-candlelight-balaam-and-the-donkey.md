[Balaam and the Donkey That Saw the Angel](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/145-balaam-and-the-donkey-that-saw-the-angel.md)
is episode 145 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only for now, like the
episodes before it: the creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over
the creative track's 400 MB line.

Episode 143 (Moses on Mount Nebo) left Numbers 22 to 24 on the list. The
script runs to about 7,700 words, around fifty minutes at the narrator's
pace.

## The hard part: the main character is not a saint

Every earlier episode has been about someone the Church honours. Balaam
is not one of them. Numbers 31:16 blames him for the sin at Peor, and
Second Peter 2:15 remembers him as a man "who loved the wages of doing
wrong". A bedtime show of holy people still has good reason to tell his
story: it is Scripture, it has one of the Bible's only two talking
animals (the other is the serpent in Eden), and it contains the star prophecy the Church reads in Advent. The
episode handles that in three ways:

- **It says so plainly.** The front matter says Balaam has no feast day
  in either tradition. The wind-down tells the end of his story in a few
  quiet lines, not hidden: "Knowing the truth is not the same as loving it.
  Balaam knew. The donkey simply obeyed."
- **It keeps the puzzle as a puzzle.** In Numbers 22:20 God tells Balaam to
  go, and in 22:22 God is angry because he went. The episode gives both
  common readings (he went still hoping for the fee; he was let go so he
  could be stopped) and the angel's own words, that "your way is reckless",
  and does not choose between them.
- **The miracle is God's.** The series bible says saints pray and God
  acts. The script says "the Lord opened the mouth of the donkey" and adds
  that the book never says she learned to talk.

## What carries it

- **Threes.** The donkey turns aside three times, Balaam strikes her
  three times, and Balak complains that he has been blessed "these three
  times". The narrator points out the echo once and leaves it there.
- **A servant who cannot see.** Numbers 22:22 says two servants went with
  Balaam, and nothing more. The episode names one of them Zakkur, a
  fifteen-year-old whose job is the donkey, and has him kneel beside her
  when his master falls on his face before an angel neither of them can
  see. He, his name and everything he does are flagged as invented in the
  welcome, the notes and the `fiction` field.
- **Pisgah.** Balak's second try is on the top of Pisgah (Numbers 23:14),
  the summit of the mountain Moses climbs in Deuteronomy 34:1, the
  previous episode. The narrator uses it for the episode's one gentle
  thought: the camp below goes about its day not knowing it is being
  defended.
- **The star.** The fourth oracle ("I see him, but not now") leads into the
  Magi. The episode gives Origen's reading that the Magi knew Balaam's
  prophecy (*Against Celsus* 1.59-60) as an interpretation, not as
  something Matthew says.

## Checked before it landed

The re-read changed several lines. The draft placed the Advent reading "in
the week before the third Sunday"; the Lectionary gives Numbers 24:2-7 and
15-17a on the Monday after it, and the line now says so. A line dating
the Magi "some thirteen hundred years" after Balaam came out, because the
date of the Exodus is disputed and the show's Moses episodes give no date.
A claim that both traditions read the star prophecy was cut from the front
matter, because only the Catholic reading was checked. Balak's feast
now happens at Kiriath-huzoth (22:39), not "at home". And a line saying
Balaam's oracles about Moab and Edom simply "came true" now says that many
readers see them fulfilled in David's wars. The Deir Alla inscription,
found in 1967 in the Jordan valley, is named with its usual eighth-century
BC date and the caveat that it is broken and tells a different story.

The episode leaves one sequel on the list: the Gibeonites and the mouldy
bread (Joshua 9).
