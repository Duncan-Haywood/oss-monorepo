---
title: "Which board to buy as a spare: Shed Designer's new rule, measured"
date: 2026-10-30
tags: [shed-designer, construction]
description: "Spare boards now come in lengths set by the pieces, not the cut plan: one that holds the longest piece, the rest the commonest. What it costs, on 18 sheds."
---

Shed Designer adds a few spare boards to every order (5% of each material's boards by default) for the warped one at the yard and the one you cut wrong. Until today every spare came in the material's most common board length in the cut plan. That had two problems.

First, the cut plan decided the spares. When the optimizer found a cheaper way to cut the 2x6s, the most common 2x6 length changed and the spare got dearer. On the default shed that ate the whole $3.06 the better plan saved, plus another $0.60. Second, the spare often couldn't stand in for the piece that went wrong. Four 8 ft 2x4 spares don't help with a mis-cut 12 ft top plate. (On the default shed that spare is a 14 ft 2x4, because the listed 14 ft board, $8.32, costs less than the 12 ft one, $8.46.)

The new rule takes the lengths from the pieces instead. One spare comes in the cheapest ticked length that holds the material's longest piece, so any piece can be cut again. The rest come in the cheapest length that holds its commonest piece, which for 2x4s is the stud.

What it does to the bill, at the listed starting prices:

| Design | Lumber before | Lumber after |
|---|---|---|
| Default 8x12 | $736.72 | $731.93 |
| 8x12 classic | $742.55 | $745.27 |
| 10x12 | $860.77 | $863.18 |
| 12x16 | $1,156.85 | $1,181.40 |
| 8x8 lean | $548.42 | $539.92 |

Across the 18 designs measured (the presets plus the twelve size pages), spares cost $1,816.50 in all, against $1,620.43 before. That is about $11 a shed. In return, every material now has a spare long enough for its longest piece. I also nudged lumber prices by up to 5% at random, ten times per design. The spare's length changed in 39 of those 180 runs, against 65 before, and the changes that remain come from prices crossing, not from the cut plan.

The 12x16 costs the most extra ($24.55) because its longest 2x4, 2x6 and 2x8 pieces all need 16 ft boards. Set spares to 0% if you'd rather buy them at the yard. These are screening estimates from listed prices, not a quote, and the tool has not been checked by building a shed from it.
