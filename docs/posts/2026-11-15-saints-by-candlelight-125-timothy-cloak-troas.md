[Timothy and the Cloak Left at Troas](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/125-timothy-and-the-cloak-left-at-troas.md)
is episode 125 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. The script is
about 7,200 words, roughly 46 minutes at the narrator's pace. It is text
only, like every episode since 20, because the audio folders are still over
the creative prompt's 400 MB line (474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** It was first on the series list, and it follows on from
episode 124, which ended where Acts ends: Paul under house arrest in Rome,
"unhindered". Second Timothy is the letter the Church has always taken as
Paul's last. It comes from a harder prison. Onesiphorus has to search Rome
to find him, he is "chained like a criminal", and he writes "I have finished
the race". Then, straight after that, comes a shopping list: bring Mark,
bring the cloak I left with Carpus at Troas, the books, "and above all the
parchments", and come before winter.

**The problem: the letter doesn't say what happened next.** Scripture never
says whether Timothy went or got there in time. Making up an arrival and a
reunion would add a scene to Scripture. So the episode does three things:

- It tells the letter straight, in plain paraphrase with nothing added, from
  Timothy's side. The story is him reading it at Ephesus, from "I remember
  your tears" and Lois and Eunice to "Only Luke is with me".
- It labels the journey as imagined *inside the story*, not only in the
  notes: "Everything from here until I tell you otherwise is our own
  imagining." The route is a real one: the coast road to Troas, the two-day
  crossing to Neapolis of Acts 16:11, Philippi, the Via Egnatia through
  Thessalonica (where Demas went), Dyrrachium, Brundisium and the Appian
  Way.
- It stops the journey near the Three Taverns, short of Rome, because "this
  is where the Scriptures stop too". The wind-down gives what is actually
  known: Hebrews 13:23 ("our brother Timothy has been set free", author
  unknown, wondered about and not claimed as fact), Eusebius on Timothy at
  Ephesus, and the much later festival martyrdom, labelled late.

**What the episode leans on.**

- *"Get Mark … he is very useful to me"* (4:11), set beside Acts 13:13 and
  15:37-39. Mark left at Perga, and Paul and Barnabas split over him. The
  man who said "never again" sends for him by name at the end.
- *The cloak.* *Phailonēs* is the Roman *paenula*, the round,
  hole-for-the-head travelling cloak. Historians of vestments trace the
  chasuble and the Orthodox *phelonion* (the same word) to that ordinary
  garment. The episode is careful to say "not Paul's coat, the same kind of
  coat".
- *The closed sea.* Vegetius (4.39) gives roughly 11 November to 10 March
  as the months the sea is shut. That is why "before winter" matters.
- *Linus.* Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* 3.3.3) himself ties the Linus of 2
  Timothy 4:21 to the Linus who was given the care of the Roman church. The
  episode states that and argues nothing from it.

**Checked against the sources.** The adversarial reread changed six things
before the pages were built. The biggest was Chrysostom. The draft had him
reading the cloak as a sign of Paul's poverty. A check of his tenth homily
on 2 Timothy showed he says something else. The word may mean a garment
or, "as some say", a bag for the books. Paul wanted the books so they could
be left "in the hands of the faithful". He wanted the cloak "that he might
not be obliged to receive one from another". The episode now quotes that
reading, which turns out to fit Paul better than the draft did. The other
five fixes:

- 2 Timothy 3:11 says Timothy "followed" Paul's persecutions at Lystra, not
  that he "saw" them.
- Martial wrote a few years after Paul, not "at this time".
- Vegetius's dates were given exactly, and he is placed centuries later.
- A lighthouse at Brundisium nobody can source was cut.
- Timothy was "in Troas that week" of Eutychus's fall (Acts 20:4-5),
  without the claim that he watched it.

**Named in the notes.** Many modern scholars think the Pastoral Letters
were written in Paul's name after his death. The Church has always read the
letter as Paul's, and the episode tells it that way and says so.
