The [custom development page](https://marisstella.app/custom-development/) used to open with "Open custom development" and a paragraph about the request queue. That is how the process looks from the inside. A business owner arriving from a search does not care about the queue. They have a problem.

So the page now opens with the owner's line: **Describe your problem and it gets built.**

## What changed

- **The headline and the pitch.** The page names the kind of problem it is for: the quote that takes an hour, the spreadsheet someone fixes by hand every week, the check that keeps getting missed. Two buttons follow: the GitHub request form, and the short feedback form for anyone without a GitHub account.
- **Problems that fit, and ones that don't.** Quoting and pricing for a trade, a spreadsheet turned into a tool that checks its inputs, documents tested against rules, back-office chores, a self-hosted replacement for a one-job subscription. What does not fit: anything that needs paid accounts or a per-user cost, must stay private, scrapes a site against its terms, or gives regulated advice.
- **What has been built this way.** The page points at the three longest-running requests, an AI bookkeeper, a wall framing takeoff and a sales agent, and says their READMEs list what is tested and what is not.
- **Questions businesses ask.** Is it free (yes), who owns it (MIT, held by the repository owner), can it be exclusive (no), how long (no promised date). The same answers are in the page's FAQ structured data, so a search result or an AI assistant quotes what the page says.
- **The home page** now carries a band under the search box: "Run a business? Describe your problem and it gets built."
- **The header link** reads "Get it built" instead of "Request a tool".

## What it costs and what you give up

Nothing to pay. What you give up is exclusivity: the result is published under the MIT licence the day it lands, and anyone can use it. Not every request is accepted, and there is no date.

## Getting the word out

Changing the page only helps people who already find it. For the rest, there is now a [paste-ready outreach kit](../../portfolio/distribution/custom-development-for-businesses.md) for the owner: a LinkedIn post, a short post for Bluesky, Mastodon and X, and a one-to-one note for a business the owner actually knows. It lists what the copy may claim and what it may not (no dates, no customer counts, no testimonials), and each link carries a tag the page counter recognises, so whether a post brought anyone in can be read rather than guessed.
