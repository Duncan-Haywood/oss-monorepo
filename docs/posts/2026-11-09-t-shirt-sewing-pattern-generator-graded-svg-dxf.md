Until today the clothing studio planned fabric with rectangles: a front panel was a box, so the metres to buy were a cautious guess. The new pattern page draws the real shapes.

Pick a garment, fit and size and it drafts the front, back and a set-in sleeve. The part that makes it more than a drawing is the sleeve cap. A pattern maker walks a tape along the armhole and along the cap and wants the cap to be a little longer (ease). Here the cap is solved for that: two nested bisections find the cap height and the position of its top so the front half and back half each equal their armhole plus half the ease you typed. The tests check it for six sizes and three eases, and with a separate walk along the outline.

Each piece has a stitch line, a cutting line with seam allowance added edge by edge (none on the fold, a deeper hem), notches and a grain line. Every size is drafted from the same origin so the grade overlay shows how the armhole and cap move, and downloads come as SVG in centimetres or a DXF with cut, stitch, grain and notch layers.

What it is not: the proportions are typical drafting ratios, not a block fitted to a body, and nobody has sewn one yet. Cut a test garment first. Hoodies are not drafted. Next up is nesting these outlines into a marker, and a real tee to measure against.

[Open the pattern page](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/pattern.html). Free, MIT, runs in your browser.
