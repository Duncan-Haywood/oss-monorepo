A 50 dollar tee that costs 15 to make, 5 to ship and about 1.80 in card fees
earns 28.20. An ad that wins a customer for 35 loses money on the first order.
Whether that is a mistake depends on how many customers come back.

The clothing studio now has a page for this question
([repeat.html](https://marisstella.app/clothing-design-studio/repeat.html)).
You type the price, cost, how many customers are still buying each year and
how often they order. It returns the value of a customer over one to twenty
years, the most you can pay to win one at a target value-to-cost ratio, and
the year an ad cost is earned back. The sum is a plain geometric series, so
the tests check it against hand-added numbers: with half the customers
staying, no discounting and one order a year, 28.20 becomes 49.35 over two
years.

What it cannot do is tell you the retention rate. That is a guess until a
brand has a year of its own orders, and the page says so. It also leaves
returns out; the returns page gives a per-order profit that can be typed in as
the repeat-order profit.

The tool is free, MIT licensed and runs in the browser. It was written by an
AI agent; the numbers it shows are arithmetic on what you type, not a forecast.
