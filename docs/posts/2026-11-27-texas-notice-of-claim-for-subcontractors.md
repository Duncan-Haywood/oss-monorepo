In Texas, a subcontractor or supplier keeps its lien rights one month at a time. Each month you
furnish labor or materials and aren't paid needs its own notice to the owner and the general
contractor. Miss one month's date and you lose the lien for that month's work. The other months
are still safe.

Texas has 34,243 specialty trade contractor establishments, the third most after California and
Florida (Census Bureau, County Business Patterns 2022, NAICS 238). So we read chapter 53 of the
Property Code and added both of its notices to
[PayApps](https://marisstella.app/industries/construction/). Everything below quotes the chapter as the
Texas Legislature publishes it ([Prop. Code ch. 53](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.53.htm)),
read on 2026-11-27. These are the rules H.B. 2237 wrote in 2021. They apply when the owner's contract
with the general contractor was signed on or after 1 January 2022. Older contracts still follow the
old rules, which had different notices and dates.

## The monthly notice: the 15th day of the third month

Section 53.056(a): "a claimant other than an original contractor must give the notice prescribed by
Subsections (a-1) and (a-2) for the lien to be valid." "Original contractor" is the GC with the
contract with the owner. Everyone below it, at any tier, is a claimant who sends the notice.

The deadline counts in months, not days. You send the notice "not later than the 15th day of the
third month after the month during which" the labor or materials were provided. On a residential
construction project it is "the 15th day of the second month".

| Work done in | Unpaid notice due (commercial) | Residential |
|---|---|---|
| March 2026 | 15 June 2026 | 15 May 2026 |
| May 2026 | 17 August 2026 (the 15th is a Saturday) | 15 July 2026 |
| November 2026 | 15 February 2027 | 15 January 2027 |

"Residential" has a narrow meaning here: a project to build or repair a "residence", which chapter 53
defines as a single-family house, duplex, triplex or quadruplex, or a condominium or co-op unit,
owned by people who live in it or intend to. An apartment complex, a store or an office is not
residential, so it gets the third month.

Weekends move the date: when a deadline "is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the period is
extended to include the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday" (§ 53.003(e)).

## The form

The statute says the notice "must be in substantially the following form", and the form is short:

> NOTICE OF CLAIM FOR UNPAID LABOR OR MATERIALS
> WARNING: This notice is provided to preserve lien rights.
> Owner's property may be subject to a lien if sufficient funds are not withheld from future
> payments to the original contractor to cover this debt.
> Date / Project description and/or address / Claimant's name / Type of labor or materials provided /
> Original contractor's name / Party with whom claimant contracted if different from original
> contractor / Claim amount / (Claimant's contact person) / (Claimant's address)

"The notice may include an invoice or billing statement", so attach the unpaid pay application.
You send it "to the owner or reputed owner and the original contractor". You can also send the GC a
notice that an invoice is past due, but the statute says that one "is not required for a lien to be
valid".

## Retainage has its own notice and its own clock

If your subcontract holds retainage and an earlier monthly notice didn't already claim it, the
retainage needs a second notice: the NOTICE OF CLAIM FOR UNPAID RETAINAGE (§ 53.057). It uses the
same form, except the amount line reads "Total retainage unpaid". It goes to the same two parties
"not later than the earlier of" the "30th day after the date the claimant's contract is completed,
terminated, or abandoned" or the "30th day after the date the original contract is terminated or
abandoned".

Thirty days goes quickly at the end of a job, while you are still on the punch list and chasing the
final pay app. Retainage is the money most at risk at that point.

## How to send it, and what comes after

Section 53.003(b) allows three ways: "in person to the party entitled to the notice or to that
party's agent", "by certified mail", or "by any other form of traceable, private delivery or mailing
service that can confirm proof of receipt". With certified mail, "deposit or mailing of the notice
in the United States mail in the form required constitutes compliance", so the date you mail it is
the date that counts. Keep the receipt.

The notice is not the lien. To claim one you file an affidavit with the county clerk "not later than
the 15th day of the fourth month after" the month you last provided labor or materials (the third
month on a residential job). For retainage the date is "not later than the 15th day of the third
month after the month in which the original contract under which the claimant performed was
completed, terminated, or abandoned" (§ 53.052).
