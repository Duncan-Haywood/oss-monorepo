A Texas subcontractor or supplier who is owed money doesn't get one lien deadline per job. It gets
one for every month of unpaid work. Since H.B. 2237 rewrote Chapter 53 of the Property Code, the
rule is short: "For all unpaid labor or materials provided, the claimant must send a notice of claim
for unpaid labor or materials to the owner or reputed owner and the original contractor" (Tex. Prop.
Code § 53.056(a-1)). Without it, the lien isn't valid: "a claimant other than an original
contractor must give the notice prescribed by Subsections (a-1) and (a-2) for the lien to be valid"
(§ 53.056(a)).

Texas has 34,243 specialty trade contractor establishments, the third most of any state (Census
Bureau, County Business Patterns 2022, NAICS 238). Most of them bill monthly on pay applications,
and an unpaid pay app is exactly what this notice is for. So we read the statute and built it into
[PayApps](https://marisstella.app/industries/construction/). Everything quoted below is from
Chapter 53 as the Texas Legislature publishes it
([statutes.capitol.texas.gov](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.53.htm)), read on
2026-11-27.

## The deadline: the 15th of the third month (second if residential)

The notice must be sent:

- "for projects other than residential construction projects, not later than the 15th day of the
  third month after the month during which" the labor or materials were provided;
- "for residential construction projects, not later than the 15th day of the second month after the
  month during which" they were provided (§ 53.056(a-1)).

A "residential construction project" is the construction or repair of a "residence" under a contract
with its owner, and a residence means "a single-family house, duplex, triplex, or quadruplex or a
unit in a multiunit structure used for residential purposes" owned by one or more adults and "used or
intended to be used as a dwelling by one of the owners" (§ 53.001(8)-(10)). An apartment building for rent, a clinic or a warehouse is not one, so most
subcontract work runs on the third-month clock.

Work done in each month of 2026 on a commercial job:

| Work in | Notice by | Day |
|---|---|---|
| January | April 15 | Wednesday |
| February | May 15 | Friday |
| March | June 15 | Monday |
| April | July 15 | Wednesday |
| May | August 15 | **Saturday** |
| June | September 15 | Tuesday |
| July | October 15 | Thursday |
| August | November 15 | **Sunday** |
| September | December 15 | Tuesday |
| October | January 15, 2027 | Friday |
| November | February 15, 2027 | Monday |
| December | March 15, 2027 | Monday |

On a weekend the statute gives you the next business day: "if the deadline or the last day of the
period is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the period is extended to include the next day that
is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday" (§ 53.003(e)). Don't rely on it. February 15, 2027 is
a Monday and also Presidents' Day, and whether a given day counts as a legal holiday is a question
for your attorney, not a calendar. Send by the 15th and the question never comes up.

## Pay applications cross months, so use the earliest one

A pay app for October 26 to November 25 bills work from two months. October's work is due January 15
and November's is due February 15. The notice has to reach the owner by the earliest date for any
work it claims, so the safe rule is that the period's first month governs. One notice sent by January
15 can claim the whole application.

Each month has its own date. Sending June's notice on time doesn't rescue March's if March's date
has passed, and a notice that claims only March's amount doesn't cover April's. If two applications are unpaid, check two
dates.

## The form

The statute says the notice "must be in substantially the following form" (§ 53.056(a-2)), and it is
short:

> NOTICE OF CLAIM FOR UNPAID LABOR OR MATERIALS
>
> WARNING: This notice is provided to preserve lien rights.
>
> Owner's property may be subject to a lien if sufficient funds are not withheld from future
> payments to the original contractor to cover this debt.
>
> Date, Project description and/or address, Claimant's name, Type of labor or materials provided,
> Original contractor's name, Party with whom claimant contracted if different from original
> contractor, Claim amount, (Claimant's contact person), (Claimant's address)

"The notice may include an invoice or billing statement" (§ 53.056(a-3)), so attach the pay
application the amount comes from.

## How to send it, and what to keep

Section 53.003(b) allows three ways: "in person to the party entitled to the notice or to that
party's agent", "by certified mail", or "by any other form of traceable, private delivery or mailing
service that can confirm proof of receipt". Certified mail counts when you mail it: "deposit or
mailing of the notice in the United States mail in the form required constitutes compliance with the
notice requirement" (§ 53.003(c)).

Keep the date and the method. If you ever file the lien affidavit, it must include "a statement
identifying the date each notice of the claim was sent to the owner and the method by which the
notice was sent" (§ 53.054(a)(8)).

## Retainage has its own notice

Retainage that the monthly notices didn't claim needs a second form, the "NOTICE OF CLAIM FOR UNPAID
RETAINAGE" (§ 53.057(a-2)), sent "not later than the earlier of: (1) the 30th day after the date the
claimant's contract is completed, terminated, or abandoned; or (2) the 30th day after the date the
original contract is terminated or abandoned" (§ 53.057(a-1)). That clock starts when your own
contract is finished, not your last pay app, and it is 30 days, not months.

## Which jobs this covers

H.B. 2237 says: "The changes in law made by this Act apply only to an original contract entered into
on or after the effective date of this Act", which is January 1, 2022. A job whose prime contract is
older is under the earlier rules, with different notices. Public jobs are under the Government Code's
payment bond rules, not this lien chapter.
