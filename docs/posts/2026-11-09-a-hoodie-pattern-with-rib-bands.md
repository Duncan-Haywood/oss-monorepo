A hoodie drafted without rib bands looks right on screen and is wrong on the
cutting table: the real garment has a stretchy band at the hem and each wrist,
cut shorter than the opening it joins so it grips.

The clothing design studio's pattern drafter now has a checkbox for that. With
it on, a crewneck or hoodie gets a hem band and two cuff bands. Each band is a
strip folded along its length, cut to a chosen share of the opening (0.85 by
default, because rib stretches) and twice the finished height (6 cm finished).
The body and sleeves are cut shorter by the band height, so the finished
garment is the same length as before, and the page prints the opening, the band
length and the finished length.

Honest limits: the 0.85 ratio and the 6 cm height are typical figures typed in,
not measured from a real rib, and the bands are not yet in the nesting marker.
The check is arithmetic: band length against an independent read of the front
hem width at three sizes. The next step needs a real hoodie to compare.
