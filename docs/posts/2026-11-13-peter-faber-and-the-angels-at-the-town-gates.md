[Peter Faber and the Angels at the Town Gates](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/105-peter-faber-and-the-angels-at-the-town-gates.md)
is episode 105 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 7,400
spoken words, roughly 48 minutes. Like every episode since 20 it is text only,
because the audio folders are still over the repository's 400 MB cap (423 MB
this run).

It follows episode 103, about Ignatius, from the other side of the room. Peter
Faber (1506 to 1546) was a shepherd boy from Villaret in Savoy. At Sainte-Barbe
in Paris he roomed with Francis Xavier. In 1529 the 38-year-old Ignatius moved
in with them, and Faber was the one who went over each day's Aristotle with
him. Ignatius in turn spent about four years helping Faber through his
scruples. Faber was the first of the group to be ordained, so at Montmartre
on 15 August 1534 he said the Mass at which the seven made their vows.

**Why this saint.** The series list named him as the next episode. His source
is rare for a saint: the Memoriale, a private spiritual diary he kept from
1542, never meant for anyone else to read. In it he notes that, entering a
town, he greeted the guardian angel of the place and the angels of the people
he had to deal with, especially the difficult ones. He also lists the people
he prayed for by name, and Luther and Melanchthon are on that list. That
habit gives the episode its title, its cold open and its ending.

**What the episode tells as history:** the tears to be sent to school, the
promise he made in a field at about twelve, the tutoring, the ordination and
Montmartre, his leading the companions across the Alps to Venice in the
winter of 1536, his work at the meetings of Worms and Regensburg, the
Exercises he gave Peter Canisius at Mainz in 1543, Portugal and Spain, his
death in Rome at forty on his way to the Council of Trent, his beatification
in 1872, and his canonisation by Pope Francis on 17 December 2013, the Pope's
own birthday.

**Honesty flags.** The six days without food in the snow during his first
retreat are labelled as the early Jesuit accounts' story, not his own. On the
adversarial re-read I cut three details I could not tie to a source with
confidence: a frozen Seine, the claim that his mother had died before his
1533 visit home, and the exact way the tutoring was arranged. His advice to
Laínez on dealing with the reformers (charity first, start from what you
agree on) and Canisius's "if he is a man and not an angel" letter are given
in paraphrase, not as quotations. The episode does not claim to know where
his bones lie in the Gesù. Konrad, a carter's boy who drives him through the
rain and thinks he is a bit odd, is invented, as are the inn and the men
mocking priests at the next table, and the episode says so. The Reformation
gets a few gentle paragraphs and no sides, under the series' rule to stay off
disputed ground. The sign-off prayer is the old "Angel of God".
