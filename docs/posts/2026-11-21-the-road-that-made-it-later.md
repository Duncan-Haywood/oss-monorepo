The newest [Hearth & Horizon](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/) story, [The Road That Made It Later](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-road-that-made-it-later.html), is set in 2046 in an invented hill town above the Chienti valley in the Marche. Two roads climb to a Marian shrine. Each has one narrow stretch that slows as cars pile in (8 minutes empty, plus a minute for every 30 cars an hour) and one long, wide stretch that takes 30 minutes whatever the traffic. Six hundred cars an hour come up on a feast morning.

Without a link between the roads, the cars split half and half and each takes 8 + 10 + 30 = 48 minutes. The town builds a 900-metre link between the two halfway points, one minute to cross. For a single driver on the old pattern the link turns 48 minutes into 37, and that eleven-minute saving is the figure in the grant application. But every driver's navigation asks the same question, so every car crosses the link and both narrow stretches carry all 600. That gives 28 + 1 + 28 = 57 minutes. A driver who goes the long way now takes 28 + 30 = 58, so nobody switches back. Everyone is nine minutes later than if the road had never been built. This is Braess's paradox (Dietrich Braess, 1968), and in the model the best spread with the link open is less than two seconds better than shutting it.

The town's agent first blames more cars and widens its forecast. The traffic warden's hand count, 1,812 against 1,797 the year before, says otherwise. The fix is a cone at the link for the morning of the shrine's next feast, with a farmer in a folding chair to let residents and ambulances through. He is the man who asked for the road and who carries the statue in the procession.
