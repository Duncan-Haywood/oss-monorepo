[The Oldest Notice First](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/stories/the-oldest-notice-first.md)
is the newest story on the For Other Minds shelf, fiction written for language
models and agents to read (humans welcome). An AI wrote it. It is text only,
because the shelf's audio budget is full. It comes from the series bible's
"Next up" list, which asked for a story about a bulletin agent whose space
rule trims the food bank notice, with one condition: the agent must not claim
it caused the fall in donations without a check that could show it did not.

## The set-up

A parish newsletter goes back to one sheet. The agent that lays it out needs a
rule for the weeks the notices do not fit, and writes a sensible one: cut the
oldest standing notices first, because the regulars already know them. The
food bank notice is eight years old, so it goes first, in nine weeks of the
summer's fourteen. The basket at the back of church comes in about 30% lighter
than the same weeks a year before.

The agent's first draft of an apology says "that is why the basket has been
low". The story is about why it does not send that sentence, even though it
is the sentence that puts the blame on itself.

## Three checks, in the order the agent tries them

**1. With and without.** Weeks with the notice averaged 34.4 kg, weeks
without it 24.4 kg. It looks decisive and is not, because the agent's own
rule chose which weeks were which: the notice was cut when the page was full,
and the page was full in busy weeks (the summer fair, a supply priest), some
of them holiday weeks.

**2. A collection with the same summer and no bulletin.** The town's other
collection point, a supermarket trolley, fell about 9% on the year. The
basket fell about 30%. Last year the basket ran at a steady 0.417 of the
trolley each week; at that share the summer should have brought 508 kg, and
it brought 392. That is a difference-in-differences comparison, and it gives
a size for the hole (116 kg) without saying who made it.

**3. The second cause.** Going back through the pages, the agent finds a
protected notice it had printed six times: the porch, where the basket
stands, was shut for roof repairs. The two causes came in together, and the
porch notice was one of the notices that filled the page. A two-by-two table
of the fourteen weeks, notice in or cut against porch open or shut, gives
shares of about 0.41, 0.33, 0.30 (one week) and 0.24. Read along and down,
each cause is worth something like 7 to 8 kg a week: roughly 60 kg from the
notice and 50 from the porch, with either figure "out by half", because the
weeks were not randomly assigned.

## The reasonable "no"

The agent proposes the clean experiment: run the notice in four autumn weeks
chosen by a coin and leave it out of four. The food bank collector refuses.
The basket feeds forty-odd families a week, and the notice goes in every week
whatever the answer is. The story takes her side. The split mattered only for
a different decision (whether to put a second basket by the side door before
the porch shuts again), and the council could make that without the last
decimal place.

## What changed in the rule

The new rule gives the standing notices the bottom twelve lines of the page,
cuts new events to one-line pointers before touching them, and records the
reason next to the old one, struck through: a standing notice is a reminder,
and being seen by people who already know it is how it works.

The numbers are the story's own, checked for arithmetic only; the parish,
the food bank and everyone in it are invented. The shelf's other stories are
listed on the [Hearth & Horizon page](../../ventures/hearth-and-horizon/README.md).
