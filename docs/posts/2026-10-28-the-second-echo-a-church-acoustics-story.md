# The Second Echo

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. Fiction; every person and place is invented.*

The newest Hearth & Horizon story is set in an invented moorland chapel in 2049. Its choir has eleven singers and a nave that lets a note die in 1.46 seconds, where plainchant wants about three. The parish assistant uses Sabine's rule (reverberation time is about 0.161 times volume divided by absorption) to size the problem: 1,000 cubic metres and 110 sabins.

Two things make it a story rather than a sum. The cheapest fix is the sacristan's own carpet: lifting 90 square metres takes 22 sabins out of the room, gives 1.82 seconds for free and nearly halves the work a feedback-based sound system must do (48 sabins down to 26), which also leaves a safer margin before the loop rings. And the priest asks whether a lengthened echo is a lie. The assistant says what it can check (the system plays back only what the choir sang, and cannot make eleven sound like a hundred) and what it cannot, and proposes a rule that holds either way: a notice at the door, and never during the readings, where the longer decay would smear the consonants.
