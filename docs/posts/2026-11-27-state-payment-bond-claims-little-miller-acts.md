A subcontractor on a school, a county road or a state university building cannot file a mechanic's
lien. The building is public property. What protects you instead is the payment bond the prime
contractor had to buy, under the state's own bond law (a "little Miller Act", after the federal
one). The bond pays when the prime or a sub above you does not. It pays only if your notices go out
on time and you sue before the limit, and each state counts those dates its own way.

We read the bond statutes of eight states on each legislature's own site on 2026-11-27 and put them
in a new free guide,
[state payment bond claims on public jobs](https://marisstella.app/pay-app-guides/state-payment-bond-claims.html).
The guide links every statute it summarises.

## What the eight statutes say

| State | If you contracted with one of the prime's subs | Everyone | Suit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Texas (Gov't Code ch. 2253) | notice to the prime by the 15th of the 2nd month after each unpaid month | notice with a sworn statement to the prime and the surety by the 15th of the 3rd month | before one year from mailing the notice |
| Florida (§ 255.05) | notice within 45 days of starting; sworn notice of nonpayment within 90 days of your last day | none (laborers exempt) | within 1 year of your last day |
| New York (State Fin. Law § 137) | notice to the contractor within 120 days of your last day | none | within one year of the owner's completion and acceptance |
| Virginia (§ 2.2-4341) | notice to the contractor within 90 days, registered or certified mail | none | within one year of your last day |
| Washington (RCW 39.08.030) | suppliers to a sub: notice within 10 days of the first delivery | notice filed with the public body within 30 days of acceptance | not in these sections |
| Arizona (§ 34-223) | preliminary twenty-day notice; ninety-day notice to the contractor | none | within one year of your last day |
| North Carolina (§§ 44A-27, 44A-28) | notice of claim within 120 days; notice of public subcontract for claims over $20,000 | none | the later of one year from your last day or from final settlement |
| Ohio (§ 153.56) | notice of furnishing on work over $30,000 | statement to the sureties within 90 days of acceptance | within one year of acceptance |

## Three things that catch subs out

**Texas counts every month.** Chapter 2253 gives each month of unpaid work its own notice date. A
sub that waits for the job to end to send one notice has usually already lost the early months.

**Some clocks start at acceptance, not at your last day.** In Washington the notice goes to the
public body within 30 days after it accepts the work, and in Ohio the statement to the sureties is
due within 90 days of acceptance. Acceptance is the public body's act, often a board vote, so ask
for the date instead of guessing it.

**The start-of-job notices matter.** Florida, North Carolina, Ohio, Arizona and Washington reward a
notice sent when you start. Sent late, it limits what you can recover or ends the claim.
