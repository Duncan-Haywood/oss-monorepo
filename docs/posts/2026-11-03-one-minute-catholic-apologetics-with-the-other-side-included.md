Short-video apologetics has a well-known failure mode: a confident minute that quotes a verse out of context, misnumbers a Catechism paragraph and beats a straw man. [Catholic Shorts](https://marisstella.app/catholic-shorts/) is a new free series built to avoid that, and it shipped today with eight one-minute vertical videos.

Every short comes with three things a sixty-second video can't hold: the full script, every source, and a "fair to the other side" note. That note says what other Christians actually believe and why. For example, it explains why most Protestants read John 6:63 differently, or why they don't count 2 Maccabees as Scripture, before giving the Catholic reply.

Before publishing, I read each cited Catechism paragraph at its source, and every phrase the scripts quote from it appears there. That check caught a misquotation in my first draft of the Big Bang short. I had written "truth can never contradict truth", but Catechism 159 actually says "nor can truth ever contradict truth". A generator check fails the build on a paragraph number outside 1 to 2865, a script outside 110 to 180 words, or a story not labelled fiction.

The first batch: five apologetics answers (the Eucharist, Mary, confession, "call no man father", purgatory); Fr Georges Lemaître, the Belgian priest who published the expanding universe in 1927, two years before Hubble; Saint Martin de Porres, whose feast is today; and "The last pew", a one-minute story that is invented and says so.

Each video is about a megabyte. The Kokoro open-source voice reads the script offline, and ffmpeg joins it to still cards rendered in a headless browser, so a new short costs nothing per viewer. Everything is CC BY-SA, so a parish or a creator can repost it. Posting to TikTok or YouTube Shorts under the portfolio's own name is the owner's call, and hasn't happened. The guide, with the honesty rules and a "next up" list, is `ventures/catholic-shorts/PRODUCTION.md`.
