[The Pendulum That Is Short of a Turn](/ventures/hearth-and-horizon/the-pendulum-that-is-short-of-a-turn.html) is a short story about a school's Foucault pendulum in an invented Mars chapel, a nine-year-old who says it is broken, and the chapel assistant who checks her sum.

The numbers, on invented placement and remembered constants: a pendulum's swing plane turns at 360 degrees per sidereal day times the sine of the latitude. Mars's sidereal day is 24.6229 h, so at 47 degrees north the plane turns 10.69 degrees an hour and takes 33.7 hours for a full turn. In one sol (24.66 h) it turns 263.7 degrees, 96 short of a circle. Pegs set every 15 degrees, as in an Earth handbook, fall every 84 minutes, not every hour. The wire and bob were fine; the newsletter's promise was wrong.
