[Nathan and the Ewe Lamb](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/144-nathan-and-the-ewe-lamb.md)
is the newest episode of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of
saints, angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like every episode since
thirty it is text only for now: the creative shelves' audio folders hold
577 MB, over the creative track's 400 MB line.

Episode 139 left Second Samuel 12 on the list. It is about 6,200 words,
roughly forty minutes at the narrator's pace.

## The hard part: a bedtime story that starts with adultery and murder

Second Samuel 11 is the hardest set-up the show has used so far. The
series bible asks for no gore and no fright. It also says Scripture is
told straight, with nothing invented that changes what happens. The
episode does three things to meet both rules:

- **It tells the listener first.** Before the scene-setting, the narrator
  says the story "begins with a sad thing, and a wrong thing, done by a good
  man", and promises to tell that part "quietly and quickly, the way the
  Bible itself does".
- **It keeps the facts and drops the detail.** David "took her as if she
  were his own, though she was another man's wife". Uriah's refusal to go
  home and the letter he carries to Joab are kept whole, because Uriah's
  faithfulness is what the chapter sets against the king.
- **It does not skip the child.** Second Samuel 12:14-23 is painful, and
  leaving it out would change the story. It is told in a few plain
  sentences, ending with David's "I shall go to him, but he will not come
  back to me".

## What carries it

- **Going round the wall.** The episode's one interpretive frame is that
  David has spent a year building a wall of excuses, and Nathan's parable
  goes round it: the king condemns the rich man before he knows the rich
  man is him. The invented parts (Nathan rehearsing the parable under his
  breath on the way up the hill, a doorkeeper who knows that look) serve
  that frame and are flagged as invented.
- **Shepherd to shepherd.** Nathan's lamb "used to eat from his own plate,
  and drink from his own cup, and sleep in his arms". The episode reminds
  the listener that the man hearing this once fought a lion and a bear for
  a lamb (First Samuel 17), and that pet lambs reared by hand are still an
  ordinary thing in farming villages.
- **The ending Scripture gives.** The story does not end on "you are the
  man". It ends on Second Samuel 12:25, where the same prophet climbs the
  same hill to name the baby Jedidiah, "beloved of the Lord". The
  wind-down follows Nathan and Bathsheba to First Kings 1, standing
  together at the old king's bedside to make Solomon king, and down to the
  spring of Gihon.
- **The psalm.** Psalm 51 (50 in the Greek and Latin numbering) carries
  a heading linking it to Nathan's visit. The episode gives that as the
  psalm's own heading, says its date is debated, and closes with "Create
  in me a clean heart".

## Checked before it landed

Every quotation is a plain rendering of the cited verse, not any one
translation. One draft line said Nathan held the horn of oil at Gihon.
First Kings 1:39 gives it to Zadok the priest, so the line was changed.
Another draft said Catholics number the psalm fifty-one. Catholic usage is
split, since the Douay-Rheims Bible and the Latin liturgy keep the old
numbering, so the line now says "most modern Bibles". The notes separate Nathan the prophet from
Nathan the son of David in Luke's genealogy, whose mother First Chronicles
3:5 names as Bathsheba. Feast days are given only where we are confident:
David on 29 December in the Roman Martyrology and on the Sunday after
Christmas in the Orthodox Church, and Nathan with the righteous of the Old
Testament on the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers.

The episode leaves two sequels on the list: Abigail and the loaves on
the mountain path (First Samuel 25), and Mephibosheth at the king's table
(Second Samuel 9).
