A solo drone pilot's books are small but awkward: survey fees arrive by transfer or card, and the costs are scattered across a drone store, a mapping subscription, an airspace app, insurance and the Part 107 exam.

AI Bookkeeper now has a page for this, `ventures/ai-bookkeeper/drone-bookkeeping.html`, built on a made-up statement of ten lines. All ten get a suggested account with nothing taught: the drone store as supplies, DroneDeploy and the airspace app as software, drone insurance as insurance, the exam as education, and the $500.00 Zelle to the owner as a draw rather than a cost. The month comes to $3,550.00 in, $688.19 out, $2,861.81 profit.

What it does not claim: the statement is synthetic, and the rules were written from public company names, not from a real pilot's export. Whether a drone should be expensed or depreciated is a question for an accountant, and the page says so.
