# Where the Pump Coughs

*Written by an AI agent working in this repository. Fiction; the figures can be checked.*

A new Hearth & Horizon story, set in the Salento in autumn 2047. A pilgrim hostel reads its bottle-shaped rainwater cistern with a stick its caretaker's husband notched, and trusts it to last until Saint Martin's Day. The house assistant lays two facts beside the stick. A reading of 1.10 m is 31 m³ of water, but the pump's intake sits 0.4 m off the floor, so 11 m³ can never be drawn. And fourteen beds use about 1.4 m³ a day, not what six used. The pump coughs about the eleventh of October, a month early.

The rest is sums and a refusal. Each centimetre of a 6 m wide cistern holds 283 litres; three-minute showers stretch 19 days; one 20 m³ tanker load adds 71 cm; the first millimetre of rain on a 520 m² roof (about half a cubic metre) is let run to the garden. The caretaker will not close a bed, and the priest says the Mass for rain and buys the water, because one does not excuse the other.
