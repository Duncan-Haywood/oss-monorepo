[Eldad and Medad and the Spirit in the Camp](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/141-eldad-and-medad-and-the-spirit-in-the-camp.md)
is episode 141 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. Like episodes 30 to 140 it is text
only for now: the creative shelves' audio folders hold 577 MB, over the
creative track's 400 MB line (the series guide notes that line is stale
since audio moved to jsDelivr, but this run did not voice anything).

Episode 140 ended with Jethro telling Moses he could not carry the people
alone. Numbers 11 is the same lesson given a second time, by the Lord, and
the episode tells it as the sequel it left on the list. It runs to about
6,400 words, roughly forty minutes at the narrator's pace.

## Why this chapter works at bedtime

Numbers 11 is one of the most human chapters in the Pentateuch. Three
things in it carry the episode:

- **The shopping list.** The people do not just want food. They want "the
  fish we ate in Egypt for nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks,
  the onions and the garlic." The episode uses that list to make a gentle
  point about memory, which "lets go of the whip and holds on to the
  onions", and about calling the daily manna "nothing".
- **Moses' prayer.** "Did I conceive all this people? Did I give birth to
  them, that you should say, carry them in your arms as a nurse carries a
  baby?" The Bible keeps the prayer untidied, and so does the episode. It
  stops to say that an honest prayer is allowed, with the Psalms, Job and
  Jeremiah as company. The Lord's reply begins by agreeing with Moses.
- **"Would that all the Lord's people were prophets."** Joshua's "my lord
  Moses, stop them" is read as protective loyalty, not malice. Moses'
  answer is the centre of the episode, and the wind-down follows it
  forward to Joel 2, Pentecost in Acts 2, and Jesus' "do not stop him" in
  Mark 9 and Luke 9.

## Where the text is uncertain, the episode says so

- The words about the seventy are usually read "they did not do it again",
  but some old translations read "they did not cease". The episode gives
  both and builds on neither.
- "Two cubits above the ground": the quails lay heaped that deep, or flew
  that low. Both readings are given.
- A homer "seems to have meant a donkey-load". That is a common gloss, not
  a certainty.
- Quail migration across Sinai is real and is mentioned, without claiming
  it explains the miracle away.
- Scripture never says why Eldad and Medad stayed in the camp. The episode
  uses the Talmud's guess, that they thought themselves unworthy, and says
  each time that it is a guess.
- A lost book under their names survives as one line quoted in the
  Shepherd of Hermas: "the Lord is near to those who turn to him."

The fire at Taberah and the plague at Kibroth-hattaavah, the graves of
craving, are each told in a couple of quiet sentences, as the series bible
asks for anything grim at bedtime. The episode reads the plague as about
the craving to go back to Egypt, not about wanting meat.

The family at the grinding stone, the grandfather who says "fish", Eldad's
wife and the rope he would rather mend, the boy who ran (Scripture calls
him only "a young man") and Moses stopping at their tents are invented. The
welcome, the notes and the `fiction` field all say so. The closing blessing
is Numbers 6:24-26, which comes from the same book.

The series list gains two sequels from the next chapters of Numbers: Miriam
and Aaron at Hazeroth, and Joshua, Caleb and the grapes of Eshcol.
