[Paul and the Nephew Who Walked into the Fortress](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/121-paul-and-the-nephew-who-walked-into-the-fortress.md)
is episode 121 of Saints by Candlelight. An AI wrote it. It runs about 8,300
words of script, roughly 50 minutes at the narrator's pace. Like every
episode since 20 it is text only, because the audio folders are over the
creative prompt's 400 MB line (474 MB this run).

**Why this one.** It was first on the series list, the direct sequel to
episode 119, which ended with Paul safe for the night in the Antonia
fortress. Acts 22:30 to 23:35 is one of the most tightly plotted stretches
of the book. The council meeting falls apart in about a minute. More than
forty men swear an oath with a deadline built into it. A relative of Paul
appears, the only time Acts or the letters mention his family at all. And a
Roman officer sends 470 men to escort one prisoner out of the city before
midnight.

**What the episode leans on.**

- *The apology.* Paul calls the high priest a "whitewashed wall" (an echo of
  Ezekiel 13), then withdraws it as soon as he learns who he is, quoting
  Exodus 22:28 against himself. The episode gives the usual explanations
  for why he did not recognise Ananias (long absence, an irregular meeting,
  poor eyesight from Galatians 4:15 and 6:11, irony) and says Luke settles
  none of them.
- *The commander's letter.* Claudius Lysias writes that he rescued Paul
  "having learned that he was a Roman." That reverses the order of events
  in chapters 21 and 22, where he found out at the whipping post. Luke prints the
  letter without comment. The episode points this out and leaves it as
  Luke does.
- *The detail that carries the episode.* The commander "took him by the
  hand" (23:19). That one gesture is why the episode imagines the nephew as
  young. Luke's *neanias* and *neaniskos* allow it but do not fix an age,
  and the notes say so.
- *Hard words left hard.* The word translated "spearmen"
  (*dexiolaboi*) has no earlier attestation and its meaning is unknown. The
  episode says that rather than picking one. Forty miles to Antipatris in a
  night is a very long march for infantry, and the episode offers that as a
  question, not a solution.

**Checked against the sources.** The draft said the Lord's words in 23:11
were "eleven words in the Greek". A recount found sixteen, so the line now
says "one sentence". The escort's distance was first given as forty miles
for the whole trip. The total is more than sixty: about forty to Antipatris
and about twenty-five more to Caesarea. Ananias's greed (tithes taken from
the threshing floors) and his death in 66 come from Josephus. His death is
told in one quiet clause, as the series bible asks. Tacitus supplies
Felix's "power of a king with the mind of a slave". The Pilate stone from
Caesarea's theatre (1961) closes the wind-down. The fate of the forty men
is not in Acts. The episode notes that the Mishnah (Nedarim 3) lists "vows
of constraint" among those the sages released, and says outright that
nobody knows whether these men used it.

**Invented, and said so.** How and where the nephew heard of the plot, his
age, his walk to the fortress, his talk with Paul, the horse, the moon on
the road and the sea wind at Caesarea. The road by Beth-horon is offered as
a guess. The blessing borrows the Lord's "take courage".
