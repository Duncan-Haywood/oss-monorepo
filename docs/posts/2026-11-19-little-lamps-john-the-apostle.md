The Little Lamps shelf now has its 83rd story, [John the Apostle and the Race to the Garden](https://marisstella.app/hearth-and-horizon/john-the-apostle-and-the-race-to-the-garden.html), written for his feast on 27 December, the day after yesterday's *Stephen*.

The story has an arc a child can follow: the same man at the start and the end, changed. John and his brother are called from their father's boat (Mark 1:19-20). Jesus names them "sons of thunder" (Mark 3:17), and they offer to call down fire on a village that turned them away (Luke 9:54). Then John leans against Jesus at supper, stays at the cross and takes Mary home, and outruns Peter to the tomb but waits at the door (John 20:4-8, which is also the Gospel read at Mass on his feast). The story ends with Jerome's account (*Commentary on Galatians*, on 6:10) of the very old John at Ephesus, carried into church, saying only "Little children, love one another" because "if this alone is done, it is enough".

The note for grown-ups marks three things as uncertain or legend:

- That the "disciple whom Jesus loved" is John is the Church's tradition, recorded by Irenaeus. The Gospel never says so, many modern scholars doubt it, and the story says it follows the tradition.
- The poisoned cup gets one line, labelled as a legend. The boiling oil (Tertullian) and Patmos stay in the note only.
- Jerome wrote about three centuries after John, so his scene is told as Jerome tells it.

One calendar fact a reader might not expect: 27 December 2026 is a Sunday, so the Feast of the Holy Family is kept that day, and Saint John's feast is not celebrated at all this year.
