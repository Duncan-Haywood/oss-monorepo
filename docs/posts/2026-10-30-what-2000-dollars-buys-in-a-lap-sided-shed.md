Here is the brief: a shed under 100 sq ft, with painted lap siding and asphalt shingles to match a HardieBoard house, for about $2,000. The [shed designer](https://monorepo-projects-site.duncan-andrew-haywood.workers.dev/shed-designer/) searches thousands of designs for the cheapest one that passes every structural check. With those finishes fixed, the cheapest it finds is a 7x12 at **$2,878** of materials before tax, at our dated store prices. So we went looking for the missing $878.

**Step 1: cheaper ways to build the same shed.** The "Find a cheaper build" search changes one thing at a time and keeps a change only if every check still passes. On the 7x12 it takes five steps:

| Step | Saves | Total |
|---|---:|---:|
| Roof 4/12 instead of steeper | $52 | $2,826 |
| 4" overhang | $21 | $2,806 |
| Floor joists at 24" | $20 | $2,786 |
| Door slid 2.5" so a king stud lands on a layout line | $4 | $2,782 |
| Rafters at 24" | $51 | $2,731 |

That is $147. The flatter roof and the short eave cost some looks, which is why the gallery's search had left them alone.

**Step 2: savings that give something up.** The search never makes these on its own, because they are choices about the shed rather than better ways to build the same one:

- **No gable vents** saves $40. No code asks a shed for roof vents, but vents let summer heat and damp out.
- **No spare boards** saves $45. The order becomes exactly what the cut list uses, so one miscut means another trip to the store.

With both, the 7x12 is **$2,647**. That is still $647 over.

**Where the money is.** The walls alone take about $860:

- the fiber cement planks ($531);
- the OSB sheathing they are nailed over ($140);
- the house wrap and its cap nails ($100);
- two coats of paint ($92).

Neither lap product braces a wall on its own, so the tool always sheathes under it (IRC Table R602.10.4 lists structural panels, not siding). Swapping the HardiePlank for LP's engineered-wood lap made it $34 dearer at our prices, not cheaper.

**Step 3: the size that fits.** The new budget path takes the same design and cuts a foot at a time, choosing whichever side saves the most per square foot given up, until it fits. For $2,000 the lap-sided shed comes out as a **6x7 at $1,976**: 42 sq ft, with every check passing. A separate design search, with lap and shingles fixed and footprints down to 48 sq ft, agrees. Its cheapest designs were:

| Footprint limit | Cheapest design |
|---:|---|
| 48 sq ft | 6x6 lean-to, $1,925 |
| 64 sq ft | 6x7 lean-to, $2,101 |
| 80 sq ft | 8x6 gable, $2,096 |
| 100 sq ft | 8x8 gable, $2,473 |

Much of a small shed's cost is fixed: a door, a starter strip and a ridge cap, whole boxes of nails, a tamper. That fixed cost does not shrink with the floor.

**So, honestly:** at today's prices, $2,000 buys a lap-and-shingle shed of about 40 sq ft, or an 8x10 in T1-11 panels for about $2,450 after the same steps. For a 7x12 or larger in lap siding, plan on $2,650 to $2,900 of materials, plus tax.

The shed designer now has a budget box beside "Find a cheaper build". It shows:

- each step's running total, and the step that reaches your number;
- the opt-in savings, with what each one gives up;
- the largest size of your own design that fits, which you can add to your designs with one click.

These are estimates from dated store listings, not quotes. The designs are not engineered or code-checked.
