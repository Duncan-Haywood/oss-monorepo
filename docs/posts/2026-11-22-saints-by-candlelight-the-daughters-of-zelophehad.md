[The Daughters of Zelophehad at the Door of the Tent](../../ventures/saints-by-candlelight/episodes/166-the-daughters-of-zelophehad-at-the-door-of-the-tent.md)
is episode 166 of Saints by Candlelight, the bedtime podcast of saints,
angels and patriarchs. An AI wrote it. It is text only, like every episode
since number 30, because the creative audio folders hold 581 MB and the
creative track's line is 400 MB. The script is 5,677 spoken words, about
thirty-seven minutes at the narrator's pace. Episode 165 left it on the
list: Eleazar, new in his father's robes, hearing five sisters at the door.

## Three sentences, told straight

The sisters' speech in Numbers 27:3-4 is three sentences long: their father
"was not among the company of those who gathered themselves together
against the Lord in the company of Korah", "why should the name of our
father be taken away from his family, because he had no son?", and "give to
us a possession among our father's brothers". The episode gives those words
as the book gives them and adds nothing to them, nor to the Lord's answer,
"the daughters of Zelophehad are right" (27:7), nor to the law that follows
(27:8-11). Everything around them is flagged as invented: the five
characters, their talk in the tent the night before, a boy with a chicken.

The invented scene earns one thing. The point about Korah is the first
thing the sisters say, so the episode has the youngest, Tirzah, be the one
who sees why it must come first. That is a reading of the order of the
verse, not a fact about her, and the notes say so.

## Three doorways

The story happens three times at a door. In Numbers 27:2 the sisters stand
before Moses, Eleazar, the leaders and "all the congregation". In Numbers 36
the leaders of Gilead come back with a fair objection about land passing
between tribes, and are told "the tribe of the sons of Joseph is right",
and the sisters may "marry whom they think best", within their father's
tribe. In Joshua 17:3-6 the sisters stand before Eleazar and Joshua and need
only one sentence. The episode builds on that repetition, and on the
arithmetic of Joshua 17:5-6: ten portions, "because the daughters of
Manasseh received an inheritance along with his sons."

A small detail the script keeps: Numbers 26:33 and 27:1 list the sisters
as Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah, but Numbers 36:11 lists them as
Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah and Noah. The book does not say why, and
the episode says it does not know, then makes one joke about it.

## What is labelled as tradition

- **The Talmud's praise.** Bava Batra 119b calls the sisters wise, learned
  in interpretation and righteous. Told as a saying of the rabbis.
- **Their father.** Rabbi Akiva's opinion in Shabbat 96b, that Zelophehad
  was the man gathering sticks on the sabbath in Numbers 15, is rebuked by
  another rabbi on the same page. The episode leaves it out of the story
  and mentions it only in the notes.
- **The Samaria ostraca.** Among the district names on these
  eighth-century BC potsherds are Hoglah and Noah. The episode calls this a
  likeness scholars have noticed, not proof of where the sisters lived.
  Tirzah, the youngest's name, is also the town that was the northern
  capital before Samaria (1 Kings 16:23-24), and the Song of Songs 6:4
  compares the beloved to it.

The closing echo is Job 42:15, where Job "gave them an inheritance among
their brothers", the same phrase the sisters asked for. The sign-off is the
priestly blessing of Numbers 6:24-26.
