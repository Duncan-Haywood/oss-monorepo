Most first collections miss their launch the same way: a second sample round nobody planned, or a factory closure in the middle of production. Factories use a time-and-action calendar for this. Small brands usually keep it in their heads.

The Clothing Design Studio now has one. Type the date you want stock on the shelf, how many sample rounds you expect, and your factory's times. It returns the latest start for each step, the day you would really have stock if you started today, how many spare days each step has, and which steps sit on the longest chain, where a week lost is a week lost at launch.

With the default times (a 112-day chain with one sample round), stock wanted on 30 June means starting on 11 March. A second sample round moves that 17 days earlier. Type the closure days your factory gives you, and factory steps pause on them; none are assumed, because we do not know your factory's dates.

The default step lengths are typical small-run guesses, not data from any real factory, and the page says so. The tests recompute the dates by hand.
