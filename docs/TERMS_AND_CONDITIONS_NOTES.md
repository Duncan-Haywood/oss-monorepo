# Terms and Conditions Notes

These are product/legal drafting notes, not legal advice. Before public release,
have counsel adapt them to the actual company, jurisdiction, data practices,
payment flow, model providers, and app-store requirements.

## Topics the terms should cover

- User owns or controls the media they import.
- User is responsible for backing up irreplaceable originals.
- PhotoFlow provides automation assistance and does not guarantee artistic,
  commercial, legal, or platform-distribution outcomes.
- AI-generated selections, captions, crops, grades, and edits require user review
  before publication.
- User must not upload or process media they lack rights to use.
- User must comply with privacy, publicity, music, talent, client, and platform
  rules.
- If cloud acceleration is enabled, media may be transmitted to service providers
  for processing according to the privacy policy.
- Local-only processing should be clearly distinguished from cloud processing.
- Beta features may be experimental and can produce incorrect outputs.

## Indemnification concept

Drafting goal: users should indemnify the company for claims arising from their
media, instructions, publication decisions, rights violations, and misuse of the
service. Counsel should tune scope and enforceability.

Example language to review:

> You agree to defend, indemnify, and hold harmless PhotoFlow, its affiliates,
> officers, directors, employees, contractors, licensors, and service providers
> from and against any claims, liabilities, damages, losses, and expenses,
> including reasonable attorneys' fees, arising out of or related to: (a) media,
> prompts, instructions, captions, music, or other content you provide, process,
> export, publish, or share through the service; (b) your violation of these
> Terms; (c) your violation of any intellectual property, privacy, publicity,
> contract, platform, or other rights of a third party; (d) your publication or
> commercial use of outputs generated with the service; or (e) your misuse of
> the service.

## File safety disclaimer concept

Do not use this as an excuse for careless engineering. The product should still
be built to avoid data loss.

Example language to review:

> PhotoFlow is designed to help organize and process media, but you remain
> responsible for maintaining independent backups of your original files. You
> should verify imports and exports before formatting cards, deleting source
> files, or relying on generated outputs. To the maximum extent permitted by law,
> PhotoFlow is not responsible for lost, corrupted, deleted, or unavailable media
> except where liability cannot be excluded.

## AI output disclaimer concept

Example language to review:

> Outputs may be generated or assisted by automated systems and may be inaccurate,
> incomplete, offensive, infringing, unsuitable for a client, or inconsistent with
> your intended style. You are responsible for reviewing and approving outputs
> before publishing, distributing, selling, or otherwise using them.

## Privacy and sensitive media

The privacy policy should be specific about:

- what stays on device;
- what is uploaded when cloud acceleration is enabled;
- retention period for uploaded media and generated proxies;
- whether media is used to train models;
- subprocessors;
- deletion requests;
- treatment of biometric, location, minor, wedding, medical, or confidential
  client media.

## Product copy implication

Marketing should avoid promising "automatic publishing with no review." A safer
position is "automatic drafts and high-quality intermediates ready for review."
