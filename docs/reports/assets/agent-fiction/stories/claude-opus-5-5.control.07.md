**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Visitor**

The lighthouse on Carrow Point had been automated for eleven years, but Ines still climbed the hundred and twelve steps every evening at dusk. Nobody paid her to. Nobody asked her to stop.

She liked to watch the lamp wake up. It did it without her now, with a soft click and a hum like something clearing its throat, but she felt it was polite to be there.

On the night of the first autumn storm, someone knocked at the base of the tower.

Ines went down slowly, one hand on the cold iron rail. When she opened the door, a boy of perhaps nine stood in the rain, holding a jar. Inside the jar, something glowed a faint, uncertain green.

"It's a star," he said, before she could ask. "It fell in our field. My mum says it's a firefly, but fireflies don't hum."

Ines bent down. The thing in the jar *was* humming, low and steady, almost exactly the note the great lamp made when it switched on.

"Why bring it here?"

The boy looked up at the beam sweeping through the rain. "Because it's lost. And you're the one who shows lost things the way home."

She didn't have the heart to tell him that the lamp did that on its own now. That she was just an old woman who climbed stairs out of habit. Instead she said, "Come up, then. Mind the steps."

At the top, the wind howled against the glass. The boy unscrewed the lid and held the jar up to the lens. For a moment, nothing happened. Then the green light rose, drifted toward the great white beam, and seemed to *listen* to it, hovering in the turning brightness like a moth learning a song.

The lamp swung toward the sea. The green spark followed it out through a gap in the vent, over the black water, up and up into the clouds, until the clouds parted for just a second and there, low on the horizon, was one small star that hadn't been there before.

The boy let out a breath. "Told you."

Ines walked him home through the wet grass. The next evening, she climbed the hundred and twelve steps again, and the evening after that, and every evening for the rest of that long winter.

Not out of habit, now. Just in case anything else needed showing the way.
