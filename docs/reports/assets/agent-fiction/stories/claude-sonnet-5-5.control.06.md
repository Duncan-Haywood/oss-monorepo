**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Lamp**

Marin had tended the Calder Point light for forty-one years, and in all that time the sea had never once asked her for anything it hadn't already decided to take.

On the night the automated system arrived, a young technician named Oyelaran came up the spiral stairs with a toolbox and an apologetic smile. "It'll run itself," they said. "Sensors, backup power, remote monitoring. You can finally rest."

Marin poured two cups of tea and said nothing. She watched them bolt a gray box to the wall beside the great lens, its small green light blinking with the confidence of a thing that had never known fog.

"Does it know the sound?" she asked at last.

"The sound?"

"When the swell turns and the rocks begin to talk. You hear it before you see anything wrong. Forty seconds of warning, if you're listening."

Oyelaran laughed politely, then stopped when they saw she wasn't joking. "It has barometric sensors. Wave-height data. It's more precise than any ear."

"I'm sure it is."

They left at dusk, the boat shrinking toward the mainland. Marin stayed. She told herself it was only to finish her tea.

Near midnight, the fog came down thick as wool. The green light on the gray box blinked on, serene. The lens turned, the beam swept out, and everything was as it should be.

Then she heard it: the low, wrong murmur from the rocks to the north, the swell turning against the shoals. Out in the white dark, a faint horn sounded, a fishing boat, lost, drifting toward the sound she knew too well.

Marin looked at the box. Its light blinked green. *Within parameters.*

She climbed to the gallery, pulled the old brass handle from its hook, and cranked the foghorn by hand, the long, ugly, magnificent bellow she had sounded ten thousand times. Three blasts, a pause. Three blasts, a pause. Her shoulders burned.

An hour later, a small engine coughed out of the fog, and a voice called up, hoarse with relief: "We hear you! Keep going!"

She kept going until the boat slid safely past the point and into the harbor's mouth.

In the morning, Oyelaran's message arrived on the gray box's tiny screen: *Anomaly detected: manual horn activation. All systems nominal. Please advise.*

Marin read it, then took a pencil and, beneath the screen, wrote on the wall in careful block letters:

*ALSO LISTEN.*

Then she put the kettle on, because the sea was not finished with her yet, and neither, she suspected, were the machines.
