**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Lamp**

Marrow Point had been automated for thirty years, but Ines still climbed the 112 steps every evening, because the machine could turn a light and could not watch.

Tonight the fog came in thick as wool. She sat by the great lens with her tea and listened to the horn bleat across the water. Somewhere out there, a boat was feeling its way home.

At nine, the automated system flickered and died. The beam stuttered, then went dark.

Ines did not panic. She opened the brass hatch her grandmother had shown her as a girl, the one the engineers had sealed with a sticker reading DO NOT USE. She peeled the sticker away. Inside was a hand crank and a small oil lamp, polished and waiting, as though someone had known.

She lit the wick, set it at the lens's heart, and turned the crank. The old glass caught the little flame and flung it outward, a slow golden sweep through the fog.

She cranked for four hours. Her shoulders burned. Her tea went cold.

Near one in the morning, a thin voice crackled over the radio: "Marrow Point, this is the *Dorothea*. We see you. We see your light."

Ines smiled, wiped her forehead, and kept turning.

At dawn the power returned and the machine hummed back to life, as if nothing had happened. But she left the oil lamp in the lens, and she left the sticker off.
