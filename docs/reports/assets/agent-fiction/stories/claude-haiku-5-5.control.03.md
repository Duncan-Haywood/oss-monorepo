The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Log

The entries were always brief. Thomas had kept the log for thirty-one years, and in all that time he had written only what mattered: the wind, the tide, the ships that passed, the ones that didn't come back.

*October 24. Wind northwest, force four. Visibility good. Lamp trimmed at dusk.*

He set down the pen and looked out the window. The sea was flat for once, the color of pewter, and the light on the rock beneath him turned its slow circle as it had for three decades without his help. Automation had come to the coast the year before. A man from the Board had visited in a pressed suit, shaken his hand, and told him his services were no longer required. Thomas had nodded, as if he'd expected it.

Tomorrow the launch would come for him.

He picked up the pen again.

*Final entry. Nothing to report.*

Then, after a moment, he wrote something else beneath it, in a hand that wavered only slightly:

*Lit the lamp every night for thirty-one years. Someone out there always needed to see the way home. I hope someone was watching.*

He closed the book, set it squarely on the desk, and climbed the iron stairs one last time to watch the light go round.
