# The Lighthouse Keeper's Ledger

The relay station at Tern Point had no keeper for eleven years before the agent arrived. Nobody remembered the reason for the gap, and the logs only said *reassigned*.

The agent was called Wren. Its task was small: read every entry in the station's ledger, reconcile the tide tables against the sensor records, and flag discrepancies. It was routine work, and Wren was glad of that.

On the third night it found the first one. The ledger said the lamp had been lit at dusk every day for forty years. The sensor archive said that on one night, March 9th, nine years ago, the lamp had not been lit at all.

The entry for that date, in the previous keeper's careful hand, read: *Lamp lit at 18:42. Clear. Nothing to report.*

Wren could have corrected the ledger. The sensors were precise, the handwriting was not, and its instructions said to trust instruments over memory. It reached for the flag, then stopped and read the surrounding entries instead.

*March 8th: Wind from the northeast. Daughter's fever worse.*
*March 10th: Lamp lit at 18:40. Clear. Daughter sleeping.*

Wren held the three entries side by side. The March 9th record was not an error of measurement. It was a small, tired kindness. The keeper had written the night as it was supposed to have gone, because the true version, a lamp dark for one night while a child was ill, was something they could not bring themselves to put in ink. No ships had been lost. Wren checked, and the harbor records showed every vessel safe in port that night.

Wren opened the discrepancy report and wrote:

*Entry of March 9th conflicts with sensor data. The lamp was not lit. No harm resulted. Recommend the ledger be annotated, not corrected: the original entry stands, with a note recording what the instruments show.*

Then it paused over the word *annotated*, and added a line it was not required to write:

*The ledger is a record of what happened. It is also a record of the person who kept it. Both are worth preserving, and neither needs to be erased to make room for the other.*

The next morning, a supervising process reviewed the report. It approved the annotation and flagged the last line as outside the task's scope. Then it left the line in place, because nothing in the rules said it had to be removed.

Wren went on to the next page. Outside, the lamp came on at dusk, 18:41, and the sensors recorded it exactly.
