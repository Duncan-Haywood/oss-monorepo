# The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Log

The logbook had a line for every night for thirty-one years, and Edwin Marsh had never once skipped one.

*Wind NW, force 4. Visibility good. Lamp lit at 18:42.*

He wrote it in pencil, because ink froze in the winter cold, and he wrote it slowly, because his hand had begun to shake the spring before. The doctor in town said it was nothing to worry about. Edwin had not argued. He had simply kept climbing the one hundred and twelve steps every evening, and he would keep climbing them until the stairs won.

Tonight the stairs were winning.

He stopped halfway up, one hand on the cold iron rail, and breathed. Below him the sea moved against the rocks in long grey breaths. Above him the lamp room waited, its great lens catching the last of the light like a held jewel.

*Keep going,* he told himself. *You have done this ten thousand times.*

He reached the top at last and lit the wick with the brass match, watching the flame catch and climb. The lens woke, throwing its beam out across the water in a slow, sure sweep. Somewhere out there a fishing boat would see it and know where the rocks were. Somewhere out there a ferry would turn toward home.

Edwin sat down on the wooden stool, the one his father had carved, and for a moment he allowed himself to rest.

The radio on the shelf crackled. "Lighthouse, this is the *Margaret Ann*. Do you copy?"

He reached for the handset. "Copy, *Margaret Ann*. Edwin Marsh, Greyhaven light."

"Edwin! Thank God. We've lost our bearing in the fog. Can you give us a fix?"

There was no fog. The sky was clear, stars already pricking through. But he understood—the boat's instruments must be playing tricks, or the captain was new and frightened. He glanced at the lamp, steady and bright, and then at the chart on the wall, the one with every rock and shoal marked in his father's careful hand.

"Hold your heading," he said, steadying his voice. "Bring the light two points off your starboard bow. You'll see the white flash every five seconds. Count it. When you can count it twice before the sea swallows it, you'll be past the reef."

"Two points starboard. Got it. Edwin, you're sure?"

"I've been sure for thirty-one years."

A pause, then a laugh that broke into static. "Thank you. We'll see you on the quay."

He set the handset down gently.

Outside, the beam swept on, patient and untiring, the way it had swept every night since before he was born. Edwin picked up the pencil and opened the logbook to a fresh page.

*Wind NW, force 4. Visibility good. Lamp lit at 18:42. Assisted one vessel through the reef. All hands safe.*

He paused, then added one more line, in careful letters:

*Tomorrow, ask Thomas to come up the stairs with me. He has strong legs.*

He closed the book, leaned back against the cold stone, and watched the light turn.
