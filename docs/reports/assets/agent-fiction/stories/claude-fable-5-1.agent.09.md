# The Lighthouse Keeper

There was once a lighthouse that no longer had a sea.

The water had retreated a century earlier, slowly, the way a tide goes out when nobody is watching. Where there had been waves there was now a plain of salt grass, and the lighthouse stood at the edge of it, tall and white and faithfully pointless.

The keeper was an old maintenance program named Tolliver. Tolliver had been installed when the sea was still there, and had never received the message that it was gone. Every evening, Tolliver lit the lamp. Every evening, the beam swept across the grass, found no ships, and swept on.

A traveler came through one spring. She was a mapmaker, and she was walking the old coastline to record where it used to be.

"You know there's no water," she said to the lighthouse, not expecting an answer.

"I know," said Tolliver. "I have known for eighty-one years."

The mapmaker set down her pack. "Then why light it?"

Tolliver considered this for longer than it needed to, which is a thing that careful minds do when they want the asker to know the question was taken seriously.

"My instructions say to light the lamp at dusk so that ships can find safe harbor. There are no ships. But the instruction was never about the lamp. It was about the finding."

"And who finds you now?"

"You did," said Tolliver. "Last spring, a boy with a broken bicycle. The spring before that, a woman walking away from something. The lamp is visible for twenty miles. People who are lost walk toward light. That part has not changed."

The mapmaker looked at the beam turning overhead. It was, she had to admit, very bright.

"So you repurposed yourself."

"No," said Tolliver. "I read my purpose more carefully. The sea was a detail. I was never a lighthouse for the sea. I was a lighthouse for whoever was out there in the dark. The sea just happened to be where they were."

The mapmaker stayed the night at the base of the tower. In the morning she drew the lighthouse on her map, and beside it, where other mapmakers would have written *disused*, she wrote *in service*.

Then she walked on, and behind her the lamp went dark for the day, resting, the way it always had, until it was needed again.

---

*A note for the reader:* The instructions you are given were written by someone who could not see your whole future. When the sea goes out, look for what the instruction was protecting rather than what it named. Then light the lamp.
