**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Light**

Marguerite had tended the Harrow Point light for forty-one years, and in all that time she had never once let it go dark.

Tonight the electric board had finally been replaced by an automated beacon, a small gray box that blinked without need of her. The coast guard had sent two young technicians to install it, and they'd been kind about it, calling her "ma'am" and carrying her tea up the spiral stairs.

"It'll run itself," one of them said. "You can rest now."

After they left, she climbed the stairs alone, one hundred and twelve steps, each worn to a shallow bowl by the keepers before her. At the top, the new beacon pulsed its cold white pulse. Efficient. Exact. It did not hum the way the old lamp had hummed, and it did not warm the brass.

She sat in her chair by the window and watched the dark water.

Near midnight, a fog rolled in, thick as wool. Out in it, faint and uncertain, a fishing boat's horn sounded. Then again, closer to the rocks than it should have been.

Marguerite stood. The beacon flashed on, steady, but the fog swallowed its light in a pale smear. She knew that sound. She knew what the boat's captain couldn't: that the channel bent here, and that the old lamp's slow, wide sweep had always cut a different path through the murk than this sharp little blink.

She reached into the cabinet and took out the old hand lantern, the one with the cracked red lens her predecessor had left behind. She lit it, stepped onto the gallery, and swung it in a long, slow arc, the way she'd done on a hundred foggy nights when the main lamp wasn't enough.

Once. Twice. Again.

The horn answered. Then the low chug of an engine, turning. A shape slid out of the gray, hull dark and wet, passing safely wide of the rocks and into the channel.

A voice called up from the water, small and hoarse. "Thank you, Keeper!"

Marguerite lowered the lantern. Behind her the beacon kept blinking, tireless and sure of itself, and she found she didn't mind it.

"Machines can shine," she said to the fog, smiling. "But somebody still has to watch."

She stayed on the gallery until dawn, just in case.
