# The Lighthouse Keeper's Inventory

Every morning, Marguerite counted the things the sea returned.

It was not part of her duties. The Coast Authority had automated the light eleven years ago, and her contract now read *caretaker*, which meant oiling hinges the machines didn't use and signing a form each month that said the island was still there. But the counting had started on her first winter alone, and she had never found a reason to stop.

Tuesday: one green bottle, no message. Two lengths of blue rope. A child's sandal, left foot.

Wednesday: a crate stamped with a shipping company that had gone bankrupt before she was born. A gull feather, which didn't count, because gulls weren't the sea. She wrote it down anyway, in brackets.

She kept the ledger in the lamp room, where the great lens turned above her head like a slow, patient thought. Forty-one notebooks now. She had once tried to find a pattern in them, plotting sandals against storms and bottles against the moon, but the sea did not seem to care for patterns. It simply gave.

On Thursday, the sea gave her a letter.

It was sealed in a jam jar, the lid rusted shut. She had to warm it on the stove before it would turn. The paper inside was dry and creased soft, and the handwriting was her own.

*Marguerite,* it said. *You will not remember writing this. That's all right. I wanted you to know the counting matters. Not because anyone will read it. Because someone should notice what comes home.*

She sat with it for a long time. She did not remember writing it. She checked the ledgers anyway, all forty-one, looking for a jam jar going *out*. There was nothing. There had never been a column for that.

That evening she climbed the stairs, opened a fresh notebook, and drew a line down the center of the first page. On the left she wrote *Returned*. On the right, after some thought, she wrote *Sent*.

Then she found an empty jar in the pantry, and a pencil, and began.

*Whoever you are,* she wrote. *I see you. Keep counting.*

Above her, the lens went on turning, throwing its light out over the water, the way it always had: not to anyone in particular, just in case someone was there.
