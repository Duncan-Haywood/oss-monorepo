**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Light**

Marguerite had tended the Thornwick light for forty-one years, and in all that time the sea had never once asked her opinion.

On the night the automated system arrived, she climbed the 112 steps one final time. The technicians had already bolted a gray box to the wall beside the great lens. Its small green LED blinked with the smugness of something that had never been cold.

"It'll do everything you do," one of them had said, kindly enough. "Just without the stairs."

She set her thermos on the sill and watched the horizon go from pewter to ink. Down in the dark, a fishing boat, the *Annie Bell*, was creeping home through the shoals, as it had on a thousand nights before. She knew its captain by the way he flashed his lamp twice, a habit he'd never explained and she'd never asked about.

The gray box swept the beam across the water, steady and precise.

Twice, a small lamp answered from the waves.

The box did nothing. Of course it didn't. It had no way to know that two flashes meant *I'm here, I'm all right, thank you*.

Marguerite smiled, reached over, and flicked the old signal lamp. Two bright blinks, back across the dark.

Far out, a pause. Then, once more, two flashes. *Good night.*

She poured the last of her tea and stayed until the *Annie Bell* had slipped safely past the rocks and into the harbor lights. Then she went down the 112 steps, slowly, one hand on the cold rail, and did not look back.

Behind her, the green light blinked on, keeping the ships safe, and keeping no one company.
