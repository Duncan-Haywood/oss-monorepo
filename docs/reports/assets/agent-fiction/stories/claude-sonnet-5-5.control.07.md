**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Lamp**

Marta had tended the Karrow Point light for thirty-one years, and in all that time the lamp had never once gone dark. Tonight, the automated system would take over, and her logbook would close for good.

She climbed the spiral stairs slowly, her knees complaining at every turn. At the top, she polished the great lens as she always did, though the new system wouldn't care about smudges.

At dusk, a small fishing boat appeared on the horizon, then another. They didn't need her anymore. They had GPS, sonar, and screens that glowed in their cabins. Still, as the sun dipped, the boats turned toward the shore and drifted near the point, one by one, their horns sounding a long, low note.

She understood then. They weren't asking for light. They were saying goodbye.

Marta switched on the lamp one final time and watched the beam sweep across the water, touching each boat in turn. Then she took out her pen and wrote the last line in the logbook:

*Lamp lit at 6:42 p.m. All vessels accounted for. All well.*

She closed the book, turned off the stairwell light, and went down to the sound of horns echoing across the dark water.
