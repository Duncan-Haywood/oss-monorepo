# Hearth & Horizon revision pass, 2026-11-13 (third pass)

A creative-track run took the next batch the second pass named
(`hearth-and-horizon-revision-2026-11-13.md`, "For the next run"): the 15
science fiction stories published on 2026-11-11, 4,600 to 6,700 words each.
Five reviewers each took three stories. They re-derived every number, date
and weekday in Python and fixed each slip with the smallest text edit. The
run then re-checked the fixes that went beyond a single figure: Easter 2237,
the Biike moon phase, the dune count, the lava speed and a birth year.

**Result: 32 fixes in 14 of the 15 stories, about 2.1 a story.** Only *The Rising Shore* was clean. As in the
second pass, the physics models held: the Wolbachia release model, Stefan's
frost-depth equation, Clausius-Clapeyron for syrup boiling points,
Gutenberg-Richter for induced quakes, Titan flight power, a cone of ice
melting under straw, sediment per storm flood on a Hallig, the
equation-of-time drift of a sundial over 700 years, and Charon's eclipses of
the Sun seen from Pluto. The slips were in the text around the models. This
pass found more kinds of slip than the last one: family ages that do not
fit, a liturgical calendar, a moon phase, and plot timelines that quote a
trial before it was laid.

## Left alone, with reasons

- *Two Dates on the Ceiling*: a council vote of eleven to four suits a commune
  of 500 to 1,499 people, and Wolfsmatt has 2,600 houses. Absences could
  explain it, and the reviewer was not sure of the seat rule.
- *The Stones in the Nave*: "twenty-six marked graves" could be 23 stones if
  the four *Sæbjörg* men share one slab. That depends on how a shared slab is
  counted.
- *The Slow Dial*: perihelion moves "about a day every sixty years" in the
  story; its own dates give one day per 50 to 54 years, and the real rate is
  about one per 58. "About" covers it.
- *The White Clay*: a priest named Father Tomasz Wrona appears in 2058, and so
  does the Titan priest of 2193 in *The Sun at Three Degrees*. They are
  different people, probably a deliberate nod. If it is not one, rename one
  of them.
- *The Bell Tax*: "taught for three hundred years" at Mechelen means the
  carillon tradition; the school itself dates from 1922.
- *The Rising Shore*: the model integrates to 1.726 m of land rise over 250
  years against the text's "about one point seven five"; the note's later
  figures follow 1.75. "Four centuries of fishing boats" at a quay the shore
  left within about two may be fine if the quay predates the chapel.
- *The Rule for After Rain*: Pete Haugen at "every meeting for fifty years"
  of a cooperative founded in 2033; the growers may have met before it.

## For the next run

- Every story on the shelf from before 2026-11-11, about 270 of them, has
  had only the weekday test. Three passes have now found 1.3 to 1.7 slips a
  story (2.1 here), so the same pass, batch by batch, is still worth
  a run.
- The new kinds of slip suggest three cheap checks at writing time, now added
  to BIBLE.md's checklist item 8: compute Easter for any liturgical date
  claim; compute every character's birth year and check it against the
  dates of each relative they mention; and do not let a scene quote results
  from an event the plot places later.
