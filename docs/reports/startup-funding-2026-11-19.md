# What tech startups build and what VCs fund (November 2026)

The owner asked: "what does it seem to be that tech software startups do at
this point. make a report on it. what start ups are getting funded by the
vcs?" This is the answer. A short version for readers is the blog post
[What venture capital funded in 2026](../posts/2026-11-19-what-venture-capital-funded-in-2026.md).

**How it was made.** Five research passes on 2026-11-19 covered funding
totals, AI model and infrastructure rounds, AI application startups, hard tech
(defense, robotics, fintech, energy, bio) and how early-stage startups operate.
A sixth pass wrote this report from their notes. The backbone is the Q3 2026
PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor and the HBS/INSEAD working paper, both read in
full; this run re-checked the PitchBook headline figures against the PDF
($515.8B, 82.7%, 8,625 first financings, $108.5B and $23.8B raised by funds).
Crunchbase, Carta, Menlo Ventures, CB Insights and news pages were read
through search and fetch tools. Several returned 403 to this container, so
some figures come from outlets quoting them, and the text says so.

**How to read the numbers.** Trackers scope "venture" differently, so global
totals disagree (Crunchbase $679B, CB Insights $698B, KPMG $728.3B through
Q3); each figure is given with its source. Figures marked "my arithmetic" or
"my calculation" are derived here, not published. "In talks" means a reported
negotiation, not a closed round. Rounds that appeared only on aggregator sites
were left out or labelled. One claim found during research, an opinion
piece's statement that Nvidia's CEO resigned, could not be verified and is
not used.

**Disclosure.** This report was written by Claude, an Anthropic model.
Anthropic is one of the companies it covers; its figures here come from the
cited filings and press, not from inside knowledge.

## Summary

As of 19 November 2026, almost every funded tech startup is an AI company of one of three kinds: a builder of the model-and-compute stack, an AI-native application that sells finished work (shipped code, resolved support tickets, drafted contracts) rather than software seats, or a "physical AI" and hard-tech company in defense, robotics, power or space. Venture investors put a record **$679B** into startups worldwide in the first three quarters, already far above 2025's full-year **$440B** ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-global-startup-funding-ai-billion-dollar-rounds-exits-data/); [Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/)), yet the money is spread more narrowly than at any time this decade: AI took **80% of global dollars in Q1 and 64% in Q3**, the top 10 US deals took a record **68% of US dollars** ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)), and OpenAI and Anthropic alone took 43% of the first half. Below the giants there are fewer rounds at higher prices, with Carta's seed round count down 27% year on year in Q3 even as the median seed pre-money valuation reached $19.7M ([The Fund CFO on Carta](https://thefundcfo.substack.com/p/389-higher-vals-fewer-deals-carta)). The startups themselves run about a quarter smaller than comparable non-AI companies, charge by usage or by outcome, and report growth SaaS never saw (Cognition nearly doubled its run-rate revenue to about $900M between May and September), alongside thin margins, loosely defined ARR and heavy dependence on the labs they compete with. Outside AI software, VCs are funding defense, robotics, power for data centers, prediction markets and stablecoin payment rails, while pulling back from legacy SaaS, general fintech, conventional biotech and carbon removal. The strain is visible: AI venture dollars fell in every quarter of 2026 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/state-of-venture-q326/)), exits beyond a handful of giants remain scarce, and Anthropic's record IPO on 18 November valued it at about $1.5T, below the roughly $2T it reportedly sought months earlier ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/18/investing/anthropic-ipo-price-stock)), which makes the next two quarters a test of whether this concentration reflects conviction or crowding.

## A record $679B went to fewer, larger bets

Every major tracker calls 2026 the largest venture year on record; they differ only on how large, because each defines its scope differently. Crunchbase counts $679B globally through Q3, KPMG (on PitchBook data) $728.3B and CB Insights $698B, while for the US alone PitchBook-NVCA counts **$515.8B, about 44% above 2021's full-year record of $357.9B** and 1.6 times 2025's $319.7B ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-global-startup-funding-ai-billion-dollar-rounds-exits-data/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/10/ai-megadeals-dominate-venture-capital-as-investors-pour-billions-into-fewer-startups.html); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/state-of-venture-q326/); [PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). The year was front-loaded. OpenAI's $122B round made Q1 the biggest quarter ever and Anthropic's $65B made Q2 the second biggest; Q3 then fell 25% on Crunchbase's global count (about 40% on PitchBook's US count) while still running about half again above Q3 2025. October added $59B, up 45% year on year, lifted by a $15.9B DeepSeek round ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/)).

Concentration defines the year. Four companies took 65% of global Q1 funding ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/record-breaking-funding-ai-global-q1-2026/)), US deals of $100M or more were about 5% of Q3 deal count but 75% of Q3 dollars, and J.P. Morgan, writing in the PitchBook-NVCA report, describes capital "concentrated within fewer deals than at any point this decade" ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). Removing the giants reveals the rest of the market. By my own arithmetic on the trackers' figures, US funding excluding the top 10 deals is about **$165B year to date**, and global funding excluding the OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI and Waymo rounds (about $253B) is roughly **$425B**, about the size of all of 2025. The rest of the market is therefore growing at a healthy, slightly-better-than-2025 pace, not doubling. The fall in AI's share of US dollars, from 88% in Q1 to 66% in Q3, reflects the frontier-lab rounds winding down rather than waning interest, because AI's share of US deal count held near 45% all year ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)).

Early-stage investing shows the same pattern of fewer rounds at higher prices. On Carta's platform in Q3, seed rounds fell 27% year on year to 426 while seed dollars rose 21%, Series A rounds fell 22%, median pre-money valuations reached **$19.7M at seed and $63.3M at Series A**, about a third of Series A rounds were bridges, and down rounds fell to 12.8% of priced rounds, a four-year low ([The Fund CFO on Carta](https://thefundcfo.substack.com/p/389-higher-vals-fewer-deals-carta)). PitchBook adds that the median US seed deal *shrank* from $3.4M to $3.0M, the only stage to do so, while median Series D+ pre-money valuations doubled to $1.72B; AI companies command a premium at every stage, with an AI Series A median pre-money of $78M against $60.3M overall and a 2.2x median step-up between rounds, the steepest since ChatGPT launched ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). At the same time, US first-time financings hit a record 8,625 deals through Q3, beating 2021's full-year 8,507, which PitchBook credits to AI "lowering barriers to entry and driving a rise in new company formation." The two datasets cover different universes and should not be blended, but together they describe what J.P. Morgan calls "a barbell approach": many small first checks, then heavy doubling-down on the few companies whose revenue materializes. Those companies move fast, as Carta's median seed-to-Series A gap shortened to 664 days from a peak of 869.

The investor side is concentrating too. US VC funds raised $108.5B across 699 funds through Q3, 38.6% more than in all of 2025, but funds of $500M or more took 78.1% of the capital and Andreessen Horowitz alone raised **$23.8B, more than a fifth of the total**. First-time funds raised just $4.9B across 81 funds, and the number of emerging firms closing a fund fell from 927 in 2022 to 211 ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). Fewer independent seed and Series A managers means fewer potential leads, a plausible contributor to the falling Series A round count. Geography also shifted once the US frontier-lab rounds slowed: the US share of global dollars fell from 83% in Q1 to 57% in Q3, Europe posted its strongest quarter in four years at about $25B (75% of it AI, led by Mistral), and China passed $20B in October for the first time since June 2018 on DeepSeek's round ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-global-startup-funding-ai-billion-dollar-rounds-exits-data/); [AI Weekly on Crunchbase data](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/europe-q3-vc-jumps-77-yoy-to-25b-ai-captures-75-share); [Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/)).

### Exits rewarded the giants and punished 2021-era unicorns

2026 produced the largest exits in history, almost all involving a few names. SpaceX acquired xAI in February, went public in June raising about $75B at roughly $1.77T, and in Q3 completed a **$60B all-stock purchase of Anysphere, the maker of Cursor**; the two acquisitions are the largest M&A deals ever involving venture-backed companies ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/); [PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). On 18 November Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above its $70–78 range, raising about $81.3B in the largest IPO ever at roughly $1.5T fully diluted, against $965B in its May private round, and seven strategic partners including Google and Nvidia agreed to buy a further $26B of non-voting stock at the IPO price ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-prices-ipo-83-per-share-1-5-trillion/)). OpenAI has ruled out a 2026 listing and is expected to debut in 2027 ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). AI-infrastructure M&A also landed in Q3, led by Nvidia's $12.9B purchase of Hugging Face, AMD's roughly $8.2B deal for World Labs, Stripe's roughly $7.5B acquisition of OpenRouter and Salesforce's $3.6B purchase of Fin, Intercom's support agent ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-north-america-startup-funding-falls-ai-exits-data/)).

Beneath the headlines the window is barely open. Only 18 US VC-backed companies went public in Q3, 12 of them in healthcare, PitchBook says the window "has not reopened," and VC-backed IPOs trailed the Morningstar growth index by 15% to 38% over their first 30 to 120 days ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). Excluding SK hynix's $26.5B US offering, Q3 US IPO proceeds were just $6.2B ([Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/121864/updated-renaissance-capitals-3q-2026-us-ipo-market-review)), and year to date the US has seen 124 IPOs, 36% fewer than in 2025, even as proceeds rose more than sixfold to $230.3B ([Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/Stats)), a sign that a few huge deals carry the totals. Unicorns from the 2021 boom are selling at deep discounts: Bending Spoons bought Airtable for $1.3B against an $11.7B peak and agreed to buy Miro for $1.4B against $17.5B. The US now holds a record 992 unicorns worth $5.7T combined, and limited partners have put in $202B more than they received back since 2022 ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). That missing liquidity is the engine of megafund concentration, because LPs short of distributions re-up with the firms they already trust.

## Labs, chips and inference clouds took about half of AI dollars

The frontier labs account for most of 2026's totals. OpenAI closed **$122B at an $852B post-money valuation** on 31 March, led by SoftBank, while disclosing more than $2B of monthly revenue, more than 900M weekly users and an ad business above $100M ARR six weeks after launch ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/03/31/openai-just-closed-record-breaking-122b-funding-round-brings-value-852b/)); in early November Reuters and TechCrunch reported an $11B first close of a further round at $1.25T, not yet confirmed by OpenAI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/openai-reportedly-raises-an-additional-11-billion-at-a-1-25-trillion-valuation/)). Anthropic raised a $30B Series G at $380B in February and a **$65B Series H at $965B** on 28 May, when its run-rate revenue passed $47B ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/financial-accounting/anthropic-finalizes-30-billion-funding-at-380-billion-value); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)). xAI raised $20B in January before SpaceX absorbed it on 2 February at about $250B ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/xai-raises-20b-series-e-20260107); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/02/elon-musk-spacex-xai-ipo-trillion)). By my arithmetic, these three companies' rounds equal roughly 48% of all AI venture funding in the first nine months, and at those marks Anthropic was valued at about 20 times run-rate revenue and OpenAI at about 35 times. PitchBook cautions that Anthropic's announced profitability excludes model training costs and partner revenue sharing, "both of which are likely large drags on true positive net income" ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)).

A second tier of $1–16B rounds went to regional champions. Mistral raised €3B at about €21B (roughly $24B) in a Samsung-led round in September, the largest European venture round ever, and targets $1B ARR by year-end ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/08/mistral-ai-raises-record-3-billion-in-samsung-led-funding-round); [Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/news-mistral-24b-valuation-europe-sovereign-ai-emea-france/)). DeepSeek took outside money for the first time in June, about $7.4B at roughly $52B from investors including Tencent and CATL, and Crunchbase counts a further $15.9B at $90B in October ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-17/deepseek-reaches-52-billion-valuation-in-round-backed-by-tencent-catl-102465358.html); [Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/)). Moonshot AI raised $2B at $20B in May ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets)) and $3.5B more in Q3 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/10/ai-megadeals-dominate-venture-capital-as-investors-pour-billions-into-fewer-startups.html)), and Cohere announced in October an initial close of a round targeting up to C$7B at C$35.7B, led by Germany's Schwarz Group with the Government of Canada participating and pitched as sovereign AI ([Light Reading](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/cohere-wraps-initial-close-of-new-funding-round-to-fuel-sovereign-ai-efforts)).

"Neo-labs" founded by star researchers or billionaires raised enormous sums, often with no product. Jeff Bezos's Prometheus raised **$12B at $41B** to build an "artificial general engineer" for physical design and manufacturing ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/bezos-ai-startup-prometheus-raises-12b-at-41b-valuation/)); Yann LeCun's AMI Labs raised a $1.03B seed at $3.5B pre-money for world models ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/03/10/yann-lecuns-new-startup-ami-labs-raises-1-03b-train-world-models/)); Humans& raised a $480M seed at $4.48B when it was three months old with about 20 staff and no product ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/humans-a-human-centric-ai-startup-founded-by-anthropic-xai-google-alums-raised-480m-seed-round)); and Safe Superintelligence, which has released nothing, received an Nvidia investment that Reuters and Bloomberg put at about $5B, an amount the companies have not disclosed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/27/ilya-sutskevers-safe-superintelligence-partners-with-nvidia-to-scale-its-ai-research/)). Pedigree is not a blank check, though: Thinking Machines Lab's attempt to raise at $50–60B collapsed after three co-founders returned to OpenAI ([Startuphub.ai](https://www.startuphub.ai/news/figure-mira-murati-company-financial-breakdown-2026-06-02)), and in November it was reported near a $5B round at a $40B pre-money valuation ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/170334-thinking-machines-lab-nears-5b-round-at-40b-valuation-led-by-accel/)).

### Suppliers and lenders fund much of the boom

Much of the headline money is circular or borrowed. By my arithmetic, corporate and strategic investors supplied roughly $125B of the $187B raised in the two largest lab rounds: Amazon's roughly $50B into OpenAI (partly contingent on an IPO or AGI milestone), Nvidia's $30B, SoftBank's $30B (financed with a $40B bridge loan), and about $15B of Anthropic's Series H that hyperscalers had committed earlier ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/openai-secures-another-110-billion-in-funding-from-amazon-nvidia-and-softbank-171006356.html); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/softbank-40-billion-bridge-loan-openai); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)). Nvidia appears on nearly every lab's cap table, had committed more than $40B of AI equity by May on a CNBC tally ([Let's Data Science, restating CNBC](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/nvidia-40-billion-ai-equity-investments-2026-openai-corning-iren)), and is guaranteeing up to $105B of lease obligations on an Ohio data center built for OpenAI ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/whos-paying-whom-the-circular-money-machine-behind-the-ai-boom-or-bubble-293938)); Jane Street is simultaneously Crusoe's $13B customer and Etched's lead investor and first customer ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/crusoe-3-9-billion-series-f-30-9-billion-valuation-spark); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/etched-700m-series-d-21-billion-jane-street)).

The physical build increasingly runs on debt. AI-related bond issuance has passed $250B ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/bonds/bond-issuance-backing-ai-investment-tops-250b-testing-limits-voracious-investor-demand)), Nebius raised about $10B in convertibles during 2026, and CoreWeave carried about $35B of debt at mid-year ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nebius-secures-575bn-in-senior-notes-offering); [Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/news-coreweave-3-billion-convertible-debt-ai-infrastructure/)), with its 2031 bonds yielding above 16% in October according to Reuters as cited in a Fortune commentary ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/18/ai-bubble-financing-nvidia-coreweave-circular-deals-credit-mark-minevich/)). Nearly half of fund managers in a BofA survey named hyperscaler debt as the likeliest trigger of a systemic credit event ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/ai-data-center-debt-has-climbed-to-the-top-of-wall-streets-credit-risk-watchlist/)), and Sequoia's David Cahn estimates 2026 AI infrastructure spending at $1.5T, requiring about $3T of revenue to justify ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/can-ai-answer-the-3-trillion-question/)). Nvidia's Jensen Huang calls the circular-financing charge "simply ridiculous" because the stakes are small relative to Nvidia's business ([InvestingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/nvidia-s-huang-rejects-circular-financing-claims-as-investment-scrutiny-grows/)). Both views hold at once: the 2026 venture total overstates independent risk capital, while equity totals understate the true scale of the build.

## Applications sell finished work, and coding is the $34B leader

Menlo Ventures' enterprise spending data gives the clearest picture of what application startups actually sell. Enterprises spent **$77.3B on AI applications in 2026**, of which coding took $34.0B (up 7.7x, 44% of the total), workplace chatbots $25.0B, customer support $2.7B and industry-specific applications $10.1B, with healthcare 37% of the vertical pool and legal second ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-enterprise-ai/)). Startups hold 61% of application spending once the frontier labs' own products are excluded, but in practice the labs compete head-on: Claude Code and Codex took about $24B, or 70% of coding spend, up from about $1B a year earlier, while every other coding vendor combined grew 2.9x to $10.1B. The labs are simultaneously the coding startups' main competitor and their main supplier.

That tension explains the category's shape. Cursor went from $1B ARR in November 2025 to $3B in May 2026, according to Bloomberg figures compiled on a Wikipedia page that carries a reliability warning, and was then bought by SpaceX for $60B ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursor_(company)); [PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). Cognition, maker of Devin and owner of Windsurf, lifted run-rate revenue from $492M in May to about $900M in September and raised more than $2B at **$48B**, while The Information projects about $800M of 2026 cash burn, much of it Nvidia compute leases ([Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/news-cognition-devin-2b-funding-48b-valuation/)). Lovable raised $400M at $13.3B in August ([Lovable](https://lovable.dev/en/blog/series-c)) after reporting $400M ARR with just 146 employees in March ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/lovable-says-it-added-100m-in-revenue-last-month-alone-with-just-146-employees/)), and Replit raised $400M at $9B while saying it is on track for $1B ARR ([CFOtech](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/replit-raises-usd-400m-hits-usd-9bn-ai-valuation)). Poolside, after a $2B raise fell apart, took a $6B license fee plus $1B of equity at $12B from Nvidia ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonmarkman/2026/08/24/nvidia-pays-poolside-6b-to-license-its-model-factory-and-109-workers/)). Cursor ending up with a compute owner and Poolside with a chip maker suggests that owning compute, more than owning distribution, is becoming the decisive asset in coding.

The multiples tell their own story: coding tools and labs price at roughly 20–50 times revenue, while support agents price at 80–100 times, a premium for the category that best fits outcome-based selling. Customer service is the one horizontal agent category that reached venture scale. Sierra raised $950M at $15.8B in May, led by Tiger Global and GV ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/sierra-raises-950m-at-15b-valuation-eyes-transformation-beyond-customer-support/)); Decagon raised $250M at $4.5B and ran an employee tender at the same price ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/04/decagon-completes-first-tender-offer-at-4-5b-valuation/)); and Salesforce bought Fin for about $3.6B ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-north-america-startup-funding-falls-ai-exits-data/)). The category pioneered outcome pricing, with Fin charging $0.99 per successful resolution and Sierra blending outcome and consumption billing ([Fin](https://fin.ai/learn/fin-vs-sierra)). Enterprise search kept compounding as Glean announced $300M ARR in May ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glean_Technologies)), whereas first-generation "AI SDR" outbound-sales startups such as 11x and Artisan have no verified 2026 mega-rounds after 2025 disputes over churn and ARR claims ([usagepricing.com](https://usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/11x-2025-03-24-inflated-arr-claims); [The VC Corner](https://www.thevccorner.com/p/artisans-playbook-how-an-ai-sdr-startup)).

Vertical AI money concentrates in legal, healthcare and finance. Legal is the most crowded relative to customer spend: Harvey raised $200M at $11B in March and **$550M at $15.5B** in September ([Harvey](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-dollar550m-at-a-dollar155b-valuation-to-help-legal-teams-own-their-intelligence)), and Stockholm's Legora raised $600M at $5.6B across March and April after passing $100M ARR ([Legora](https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-raises-550-million-series-d-to-fuel-us-growth); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/egora-nvidia-atlassian-600m-series-d)), giving two companies a combined $21B valuation against $1.8B of total legal AI spending. In healthcare, OpenEvidence, a clinical search tool for doctors that partly monetizes through advertising, went from $12B in January to $15B in September ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenEvidence)). Finance AI is rising fastest from a small base: Rogo went from $750M in January to about $2B in April and was reported in talks at $3.75B with $77M ARR ([Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/rogo-technologies-nears-3-75-billion-valuation-with-new-fund/)), while Ramp raised $1.85B in October and says its ARR has reached $2B ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/)).

Consumer AI is winner-take-most. In a16z's October ranking, ChatGPT leads web, mobile and spending, only 4.5% of eligible US consumers pay for ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude (double the share a year earlier), the top 10% of payers generate about half of all spending, and the list had the fewest first-time entrants in its seven editions ([a16z](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/)). Consumer venture money went mainly to creative tools that also sell to businesses, such as ElevenLabs' $500M at $11B in February ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/series-d)) and Chinese video generator Kling AI's $3B in Q3 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/10/ai-megadeals-dominate-venture-capital-as-investors-pour-billions-into-fewer-startups.html)), while AI devices and companion apps raised only small rounds like Nirva's $33M for a listening pendant ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/nirva-raises-33m-for-a-pendant-that-only-listens-to-you/)).

### Traditional SaaS loses ground, but less than its stock prices imply

Public software stocks fell into what Forbes called a "SaaSpocalypse" in February, and software now trades at a lower forward price-to-earnings ratio than the S&P 500 for the first time ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/petercohan/2026/02/06/saaspocalypse-now-ai-is-disrupting-saas---but-not-all-software-is-doomed/); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-saas-rout-of-2026-is-even-worse-than-you-think-for-the-first-time-ever-software-now-trades-at-a-discount-to-the-sp-500/)). Menlo, however, finds only 9% of incremental AI spend ($12.6B) has moved from SaaS budgets to AI-native vendors, about a two-point growth headwind on a $600B-plus application software market, with "limited evidence" that enterprises are replacing Salesforce, Workday or NetSuite ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-enterprise-ai/)). The damage is sharpest for undifferentiated horizontal tools and thin "wrappers" around someone else's model; the head of Google's startup organisation warned in February that LLM wrappers and aggregators have their "check engine light" on ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/21/google-vp-warns-that-two-types-of-ai-startups-may-not-survive)).

## Defense, robots and power drove a second boom outside software

After AI software, the clearest 2026 winners are what StepStone's Brian Borton calls "bits and atoms": energy, power, compute, industrials, aerospace and defense ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf)). In Q3 alone, aerospace, robotics, data centers, semiconductors and energy each raised more than $10B globally ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-global-startup-funding-ai-billion-dollar-rounds-exits-data/)), PitchBook notes some investors are diversifying into "AI-safe" sectors insulated from AI disruption, and KPMG's Carolina Oliveira observes that "AI isn't really a standalone investment theme anymore" ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/10/ai-megadeals-dominate-venture-capital-as-investors-pour-billions-into-fewer-startups.html)).

### Defense tech beat its annual record within five months

Crunchbase counted $14.6B of defense funding in the first five months of 2026, already past 2025's record $9.6B ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/defense-tech/anduril-5b-valuation-vc-funding-record-data/)), and PitchBook's broader defense-tech category set a record **$37.9B across 269 deals in Q3**, up 117.5% on Q2 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/17/pentagon-defense-tech-shield-ai-bureaucracy/)). Anduril raised $5B at $61B in May after revenue doubled to $2.2B in 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/13/anduril-raises-5b-doubles-valuation-to-61b/)); Germany's Helsing raised $1.8B at $18B in July, Europe's largest defense-startup round ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/13/european-defence-ai-leader-helsing-secures-18b-series-e-at-18b-valuation/)); Saronic raised $1.75B at $9.25B for autonomous ships ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/physical-ai-funding-startups-robotics-aerospace-h1-2026/)); and Shield AI's valuation climbed from $12.7B in March to $28.8B as it raised $2.5B to buy radar maker Echodyne ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/17/pentagon-defense-tech-shield-ai-bureaucracy/)). The tailwind is a proposed FY2027 US defense request of about $1.5T, a 42% increase, though it is a request rather than law and $350B of it depends on a reconciliation bill that has not passed ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/fiscal-year-2027-topline-fact-sheet.pdf)). The money is barbelled toward late-stage giants: defense pre-seed and seed deals fell to 41 in Q3, the fewest since 2016 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q3-2026-defense-tech-vc-first-look)), and even Anduril's CEO has said the sector could be in "a bit of a bubble" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/17/pentagon-defense-tech-shield-ai-bureaucracy/)).

### Physical AI nearly quadrupled, with China ahead in robotics dollars

Crunchbase counts $47.4B for robotics, autonomous vehicles, aerospace and drones in H1 2026, nearly four times H2 2025, though Waymo's $16B round at $126B made up almost a third of it ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/physical-ai-funding-startups-robotics-aerospace-h1-2026/)). Robotics alone raised $9.9B in Q3, more than double a year earlier, with China ($13.2B year to date) narrowly ahead of the US ($12.1B) ([Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/robotics-startup-funding-q3-2026-physical-ai)). Robot "brain" companies command the richest prices, with Physical Intelligence reported in March to be in talks for about $1B at more than $11B ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-robotics-lab-in-talks-to-nab-1-billion-at-11-billion-value)), and Walden Robotics raised a $300M seed ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-north-america-startup-funding-falls-ai-exits-data/)). Unitree's Shanghai IPO, raising about $905M at a $9B valuation, was the year's marquee humanoid exit ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-global-startup-funding-ai-billion-dollar-rounds-exits-data/)). Valuations remain hard to pin down: PitchBook estimates Figure at $91.8B, yet its last confirmed priced round, in September 2025, was at $39B ([PitchBook-NVCA](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-PitchBook-NVCA-Venture-Monitor.pdf); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/figure-ai-raises-1b-in-series-c-funding-toward-humanoid-robot-development/)).

### Climate money became power-for-AI money

Climate-tech venture reached $26.1B in H1, up 55% and the best first half since 2022, but 34% went to low-carbon data-center developers against 3% a year earlier, while carbon-management equity fell 61% and deal count fell 25% ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/07/16/ai-power-binge-delivers-best-half-since-2022-for-climate-tech-venture-funding/5272401)). Firm, always-on power won. Sequoia led a $1B Series B for nuclear startup Valar Atomics, valued at about $6B by Bloomberg ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/sequoias-shaun-maguire-leads-1b-round-for-nuclear-startup-valar-atomics/)); Commonwealth Fusion Systems raised $1B and Helion $500M at $15.5B ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/15/every-fusion-startup-that-has-raised-over-100m/); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/helion-funding-round-grows-to-500m-amid-uncertainty-over-fusion-startups-microsoft-timeline/)); Axiom raised $2B for data-center power and grid capacity in October ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/)); and Fervo Energy's $1.89B Nasdaq IPO gave geothermal a public exit ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-12/geothermal-energy-developer-fervo-raises-1-89-billion-in-us-ipo)). Space rode SpaceX's public mark, with Stoke Space closing the first tranche of a $1B Series E ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/spacex-challenger-stoke-space-raises-1b-as-it-gears-up-for-first-orbital-flight/)). AI-for-science platforms drew software-style money before clinical proof: Isomorphic Labs raised $2.1B led by Thrive Capital ([Isomorphic Labs](https://www.isomorphiclabs.com/articles/isomorphic-labs-announces-series-b-investment-round)) without having publicly named a clinical candidate ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/isomorphic-labs)), even as conventional biopharma venture fell to $6.7B in Q3, down about a third from Q2 ([DealForma](https://dealforma.com/biopharma-therapeutics-and-platforms-venture-funding-q3-2026-review/)).

### Fintech shrank except for prediction markets and stablecoin rails

Overall fintech venture fell to $10.6B in Q3, down 21% year on year, across 687 rounds, the fewest since Crunchbase's dataset began in 2018 ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-fintech-startup-funding-deal-count-fell-ai/)), and CB Insights finds mega-rounds were 4% of fintech deals but 61% of its dollars ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/fintech-trends-q3-2026/)). The money pooled in two niches. In prediction markets, Kalshi raised $1B at $22B in May and reported about $4B of annualized revenue in July, more than 80% of it from sports, while Polymarket took $600M from ICE at $15B ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/08/13/kalshi-in-talks-with-sequoia-wellington-for-usd750-million-fund-raise-at-usd40-billion-valuation)); Crunchbase lists another $1B Kalshi round in October ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/)), though reports of a $40B price remain unconfirmed. Stablecoin infrastructure, energized by the GENIUS Act, drew strategic buyers such as Mastercard, which agreed to acquire BVNK for up to $1.8B ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/04/mastercard-rain-stablecoin-startup-institutional-customers-partnership/)), and Forerunner's Kirsten Green already warns of overfunding there ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-fintech-startup-funding-deal-count-fell-ai/)). Crypto venture overall is flat, on pace for about $19.4B in 2026 against $20.3B in 2025 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/crypto-vc-investment-q3-2026-galaxy/)), while cybersecurity was steady and acquisition-led, with Q3 funding of $5.26B, the most since Q1 2022, and agent security the hottest early-stage niche ([Pinpoint](https://pinpointsearchgroup.com/cyber-security-vendor-funding-report-q3-2026/)).

## Leaner teams sell outcomes, but their revenue figures need a discount

### AI-native startups run about 25% smaller

The best systematic evidence on how AI-era startups are built comes from two 2026 working papers. Kim and Koning (INSEAD and HBS) find AI-native startups are **25% smaller** than comparable non-AI startups, with a 13% greater share of engineers and about 15% lower shares of both entry-level staff and managers, yet "valuations are comparable" ([HBS Working Paper 26-090](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/26-090_96f92aa0-37d9-4789-beaa-5c0cb87a4032.pdf)). Gupta, Qian, Simintzi and Sun find that startups most exposed to generative AI cut junior and implementation roles within two quarters of ChatGPT's release, then scaled faster and moved through financing rounds more quickly ([NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f232872.pdf)). Anecdotes run far ahead of these averages: Lovable's $400M ARR on 146 employees implies about $2.7M per person ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/lovable-says-it-added-100m-in-revenue-last-month-alone-with-just-146-employees/)), and Forbes puts AI-native firms at $2–4M of revenue per employee against about $300K for public SaaS ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/paulbaier/2026/03/31/ai-native-firms-lead-in-revenue-per-employee/)), but no verified one-person billion-dollar company exists. Solo founders are more common, at 36% of startups founded on Carta in 2025, yet solo-founded companies raised only about 20% of priced rounds ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026/)). Startup hiring stays depressed, with 8% fewer hires in H1 2026 than in H1 2024 ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/startup-compensation-h1-2026/)), even as equity grants for AI/ML engineers at small startups rose 59% from January 2024 to February 2026 ([Carta](https://carta.com/sg/en/data/equity-comp-execs-ics/)).

### YC batches tilt toward agent plumbing, agent-run services and hardware

Y Combinator's 2026 batches show what new founders choose to build. YC ran four batches this year, growing to about 235 companies in Summer 2026, the largest in its history ([New Economies](https://www.neweconomies.co/p/y-combinator-summer-26-batch)), and 71% of the Fall 2026 directory carries an AI tag, rising to 84% once company descriptions are counted ([Presenc.ai](https://presenc.ai/research/yc-fall-2026-batch-ai-startups)). The mix moved during the year from "agent applications" in Spring toward three groups in Summer: infrastructure for agents (identity, payments, memory, evals and routing between models), agent-native service firms that perform accounting, legal work or ERP implementation themselves rather than selling software to the firms that do, and industrials, robotics and defense, whose share of the batch rose from 12.8% to 23% ([New Economies](https://www.neweconomies.co/p/y-combinator-summer-26-batch)). YC's Winter 2027 requests for startups ask for space access, an American robot supply chain, AI for city halls, data-center supply chains, financing for the physical buildout and companies that "own your intelligence" with specialized models, and contain no classic SaaS ideas ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)). The labs have also moved into the accelerator itself: Sam Altman offered every Spring 2026 company $2M of OpenAI tokens for an uncapped SAFE, prompting warnings that OpenAI could copy what it sees ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/sam-altman-makes-mic-drop-offer-to-every-y-combinator-startup)).

### Pricing shifts from seats to usage, outcomes and owning the service firm

Among the top AI-native consumer web products, 84% sell subscriptions, 64% usage credits and 14% ads, and OpenAI reports a $1B annualized ad run rate for ChatGPT ([a16z](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/)). In enterprise the template is selling completed work, through per-resolution pricing in support and "forward-deployed" engineers who embed with customers to codify their workflows, as Harvey does by placing legal engineers inside Am Law 100 firms; Foundation Capital warns that outcome pricing breaks down when results vary from customer to customer, which is why most AI SDR tools stay usage-priced ([Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/ideas/the-4-6t-services-as-software-opportunity-lessons-from-the-first-year)). The most aggressive version buys the service business outright: General Catalyst has committed $1.5B to AI-native companies that acquire accounting firms, IT managed-service providers and call centers, and together with Thrive Holdings more than $4.5B is committed to the model, according to secondary trackers whose performance figures are self-reported ([aioapex](https://aioapex.com/en/blog/general-catalyst-is-spending-15-billion-buying-accounting-firms-and-bolting-on-a-muauk9j3); [Capital Founders](https://www.capitalfounders.io/playbooks/ai-enabled-roll-ups/chapters/capital-and-players/)). Paul Graham's September essay captures the strategy, urging founders to route money and tokens through themselves, price by usage for fast-deciding customers, and "err on the side of having APIs" because agents are becoming users ([Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/powerful.html)). New distribution surfaces such as the ChatGPT app directory, built on the Model Context Protocol, opened in December 2025 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-now-accepting-chatgpt-app-submissions-from-third-party-devs-launches)), but no hard data yet shows how much revenue they deliver.

### Thin margins and loose ARR make high multiples riskier

AI-native gross margins are commonly benchmarked at 50–60% against about 80% for classic SaaS, because startups pay the labs for inference ([Charles Rubenfeld](https://charlesrubenfeld.substack.com/p/blame-competition-not-compute-for)). TechCrunch documented in May that startups routinely present contracted-but-not-yet-billing ARR as ARR, with one VC seeing it run 70% above the real figure, count free pilots, and annualize a single strong month, which is especially misleading under usage pricing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/how-vcs-and-founders-use-inflated-arr-to-kingmake-ai-startups/)). Demand is also concentrated: Ramp's index shows a record 56.8% of businesses paying for AI but a median spend of just $16.32 per employee per month, with the top 1% of spenders generating about 80% of revenue for the major AI firms ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/data/ai-index)), and a16z finds only 13% of people paying for one AI product pay for another ([a16z](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/)). Revenue that is usage-based, concentrated and loosely defined makes a 20–50x run-rate multiple considerably riskier than the same multiple on contracted SaaS revenue.

## Conclusion

The 2026 venture market is two markets reported as one. The first is a public-market-scale financing of perhaps a dozen labs and compute builders, funded increasingly by their own suppliers, sovereign investors and lenders, with exits measured in trillions; it behaves more like infrastructure project finance than venture capital. The second is a normal-sized but pricier venture market growing a little faster than 2025, where round counts fall, bridges rise and capital crowds around companies whose revenue appears quickly. Headline records describe the first and say little about whether a typical founder can raise. Within applications, the year's sharpest strategic lesson is that compute ownership is becoming the moat: Cursor sold to SpaceX, Poolside licensed to Nvidia, Fin sold to Salesforce, and the labs captured 70% of the coding market they also supply. The application companies holding value own a workflow, a proprietary data loop or the service business itself rather than an interface on someone else's model, and the unreliability of the revenue figures that justify their prices, many self-reported or tracker-estimated rather than audited, is itself one of the year's findings.

The next two quarters turn on liquidity. Anthropic's listing is the first public price for a trillion-dollar AI lab. If it trades well, it opens a path for Databricks, Crusoe, Lambda and the inference clouds and could return cash to LPs who have been net payers since 2022, easing the megafund concentration that starves emerging managers. If it trades poorly, the private marks below it (labs without products, inference-chip makers with little trailing revenue, support agents at 80–100 times ARR) lose their reference point just as AI venture dollars have fallen three quarters running and AI-infrastructure credit already prices in default risk. Either way, the diversification into defense, robotics and power looks durable, because it rests on government budgets and physical demand for electricity rather than on the next model release.
