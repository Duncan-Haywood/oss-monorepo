    How it decides, and what it leaves out

    • Syrup: sap gallons times Brix over 86 (the rule of 86). Sap per tap is the biggest input and varies year to year; the season table shows 60% and 130% of what you enter.
    • Fuel: the heat to bring sap to a boil and evaporate its water (about 1,150 BTU a pound) divided by the evaporator efficiency, priced per unit of fuel. Check it against the cords or gallons you actually burn.
    • Labour: hours per tap, plus boiling hours from your evaporator rate, plus bottling. Shown before and after paying yourself the wage you enter.
    • Equipment: tubing wears out over 10 years and the evaporator over 15 here, spread evenly per season.
    • Reverse osmosis: concentrates the sap to the Brix you enter before boiling, which cuts fuel and boiling hours; the unit, membranes and power are charged against it.
    • Not decided: grading, licensing, labelling and food-safety rules for selling syrup in your state, taxes, land, sap quality over the season, vacuum pumps and their power, and the cost of capital. Prices and costs here are your inputs, not market quotes.

    Common questions

    How much money can you make per tap of maple syrup?

    It depends on sap per tap, sugar content, your sales channel and what you count your own time as. Many small sugarmakers net a few dollars a tap before labour and far less after it. This page models a whole season: sap to syrup by the rule of 86, fuel, supplies, equipment wear, fees and labour, then reports profit per tap, per hour worked and per gallon.

    What is the rule of 86 for maple syrup?

    Divide 86 by the sugar content (Brix) of the sap to get the gallons of sap needed for one gallon of syrup. Sap at 2 percent needs about 43 gallons; sap at 3 percent about 29. A higher Brix cuts boiling time and fuel directly, which is why sugar content matters more than tap count for cost.

    Is reverse osmosis worth it for a small sugarbush?

    It removes water from the sap before the evaporator, so you burn less fuel and boil fewer hours. Against that you pay for the unit, membranes and power. The page compares the season with and without it from your own numbers and gives a payback in years; units tend to pay at larger tap counts and with expensive fuel.

    Should I count my own labour?

    Show both. Profit before labour is what the operation earns for you; profit after labour at a wage you pick shows whether it beats paying someone, and the per-hour figure tells you what an hour in the sugarhouse is actually worth.
