Should I charge for Etsy personalization?

Sellers search for “should I charge for Etsy personalization” because an $8.00 name, color, or size add-on looks like free margin. Transaction and payment fees sit on the higher ticket. Extra thread, ink, or a gift box is cash out. Twenty minutes at $18/hour is $6.00 you will not get back.

Open the Etsy profit calculator, keep your shop fees, and enter the upcharge and extras for the request in front of you. The table below uses the calculator defaults: $30 item, $5.00 shipping charged, 6.5% transaction, 3% + $0.25 payment, $6.00 postage, $4.00 materials, $8.00 personalization, $1.50 extra materials, $6.00 extra time.

The $8 add-on loses against the standard sale

Selling as listed keeps $21.23. Taking the custom request keeps $20.97. Personalization keeps $0.26 less (−$0.26). Extra fees on the higher ticket are $0.76. Extra cash out is $7.50. Across 12 custom orders that is −$3.12. The upcharge has to reach about $8.29 before the upgrade beats the standard sale.

Ignore time and the same $8 keeps more

If you treat extra time as $0.00 and still spend $1.50 on materials, the same $8.00 personalization keeps $26.97 — $5.74 more than the $21.23 standard sale (+$5.74). The break-even upcharge falls to about $1.66. That is why a shop that “doesn’t count time” thinks every custom request is free money.

Charge $12 and the upgrade wins

A $12.00 upcharge on the same extras keeps $24.59 — $3.36 more than selling as listed (+$3.36), or $40.32 across 12 custom orders. Raise Offsite Ads to 15% on the original $8.00 add-on and extra fees become $1.96, the upgrade loses $1.46 (−$1.46), and the break-even upcharge rises to about $9.93.

If they would not have bought the standard listing

That is extra demand. On the default $8.00 request the personalized order still leaves $20.97 versus $0.00. Take it when that path is still a profit, even if the upgrade loses against a standard sale you would have made anyway.

Common questions

Should I charge for Etsy personalization?

On the worked example — a $30 listing, $5 shipping charged, 6.5% transaction plus 3%+$0.25 payment, $6 postage, $4 materials, an $8.00 personalization, $1.50 extra materials, and $6.00 extra time — selling as listed keeps $21.23. Taking the custom request keeps $20.97, which is $0.26 less. Extra fees on the higher ticket are $0.76. The upcharge has to reach about $8.29 before the upgrade beats the standard sale.

Does Etsy take fees on a personalization add-on?

Transaction, payment-processing, and Offsite Ads percentages sit on item plus shipping, so they rise with the upcharge. The listing fee and the payment fixed fee do not change. Enter the rates from your last payout; this page does not claim Etsy's live schedule.

What if the buyer would not have bought the standard listing?

That is extra demand. On the same defaults the personalized order still leaves $20.97 versus $0.00 if you decline. Take the request when that path is still a profit, even if the upgrade loses against a standard sale you would have made anyway.
