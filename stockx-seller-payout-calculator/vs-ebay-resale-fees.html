StockX vs. eBay: the same sale, both fee structures

Every rate below is an illustrative example, not either marketplace's published fee schedule — both are inputs you should replace with your own seller dashboard numbers. What the worked example is useful for is the shape of the two fee structures: StockX tiers its seller fee by your own Seller Level and Fast Shipping enrollment and bills payment processing as a separate line, while eBay bundles processing into one final value fee and leaves outbound shipping to the buyer entirely up to the seller to arrange, rather than a flat verification-center drop. Not affiliated with eBay.

The item

A pair that sells for $200 on either platform. It cost $120 to acquire, plus $2 in packaging either way. On StockX, illustrative numbers: a 9% seller fee (no Fast Shipping), a 3% payment processing fee, and $6 to ship to a verification center. On eBay, illustrative numbers: a 13.25% final value fee plus a $0.30 per-order fee (payment processing already bundled in), and $12 to ship the pair directly to the buyer. Same pair, same price, same acquisition cost — only the platform and its fee structure change.

The structural difference matters more than the single-sale gap. StockX splits its cost into a seller fee that falls as your account's Seller Level rises, plus a flat processing percentage, plus a short hop to a verification center. eBay folds processing into one final value fee that does not depend on any seller-level ladder, but hands the seller the entire outbound shipping cost of getting the item to the buyer's door — which is usually farther, and pricier, than a verification-center drop. On this illustrative example, StockX nets about $9 more.

What would close the gap

Holding every rate fixed, eBay would need to sell this pair for about $210 — roughly $10, or about 5%, more than the $200 asked on StockX — to net a similar profit. Whether eBay's audience for a given pair actually clears a higher price than StockX's bid/ask market is a judgement about that specific listing, not something either fee schedule can answer.

What the fee comparison cannot tell you

Worth being direct about, because it is the honest half of this page. Fees are one input into which platform nets more on a given pair — they are not the only one:

  • Neither platform's current fee schedule is quoted here. StockX's seller-level bands and eBay's category-specific final value fee both change over time, and neither publishes a static rate an outside page can read live. Replace every rate above with the line on your own seller dashboard or payout statement before trusting the comparison.
  • Seller level, category, and shipping distance are yours, not a constant. A seller further along StockX's level ladder, or enrolled in Fast Shipping, pays less than the 9% used here. eBay's final value fee rate varies by category, and the $12 shipping figure above is a guess — a buyer across the country costs more to reach than one three states over.
  • Neither platform's authentication process, audience, or typical sell-through speed is compared here. StockX authenticates every sale itself before it reaches the buyer; eBay does not, outside whatever category-specific authenticity program it offers at the time. This page prices one hypothetical sale on each platform's fee structure — it says nothing about which one sells a given pair sooner, or what a buyer will pay for the same item on each.

Common questions

Is StockX or eBay cheaper for selling sneakers?

On the illustrative worked example here — a $200 pair, sold at a mid seller tier with no Fast Shipping, against an illustrative 13.25% eBay final value fee plus a $0.30 per-order fee and the seller paying to ship direct to the buyer — StockX nets about $9 more: $48.00 profit against $39.20. eBay bundles payment processing into its final value fee rather than charging it separately, but that is usually outweighed by the seller shipping straight to the buyer instead of to a nearby verification center, so the gap and even its direction depend on your own seller level, category fee rate, and shipping distance.

Why does eBay have no separate payment processing fee here?

Since eBay moved sellers onto its own managed payments, the final value fee already includes payment processing — there is no second processing line the way StockX and GOAT charge one on top of their seller fee or commission. That is a genuine structural difference, not a rounding choice: eBay's one fee does more of the work StockX splits across two.
