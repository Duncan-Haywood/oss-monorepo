This calculator vs. QuickBooks Online invoicing

Two different shapes: QuickBooks Online is paid bookkeeping and invoicing software you sign up for and use as an ongoing system of record. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — given this invoice's terms and how your client actually runs payments, when does the cash land, and does an early-pay discount or factoring beat waiting.

QuickBooks Online, made by Intuit, sends and tracks invoices, lets you set payment terms per customer or invoice, reconciles bank feeds, and produces the books an accountant or tax filing needs, as a paid ongoing subscription with signup and setup. QuickBooks' own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's plan names or figures, so no dollar amount for QuickBooks appears anywhere on this page — only its well-documented identity and category (invoicing and bookkeeping software sold as a subscription) are stated.

This calculator does not send an invoice, track a client's balance, or touch your books, and it stores nothing on a server. It takes an invoice amount, payment terms, and how your client's accounts-payable run actually works (weekdays only, twice a month, once a month), and turns that into the due date, the date cash actually lands once that run is accounted for, what 30/60/90 days of delay costs at your carrying-rate assumption, whether a 2/10 Net 30 discount or a factoring fee still beats waiting, and how exposed a project is under a given deposit or milestone schedule. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

What this page is not

This calculator is not a replacement for QuickBooks Online, or for invoicing and bookkeeping software generally. It does not send an invoice, does not track a client's running balance, and does not produce books for an accountant or a tax filing. Most freelancers and small firms that invoice regularly get different value from each: QuickBooks Online (or a comparable platform) for sending invoices, tracking balances, and keeping the books as an ongoing subscription, and a private calculator like this one for an instant, no-login answer to when a specific invoice's cash actually arrives and whether an early-pay or factoring offer is worth taking before you set the term.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or QuickBooks Online?

They do different jobs and most freelancers who invoice regularly will end up using both. QuickBooks Online, made by Intuit, is paid bookkeeping and invoicing software: it sends the invoice, tracks who owes what across every client, reconciles bank feeds, and produces the books an accountant or tax filing needs, sold as an ongoing subscription with a signup and setup step. This calculator does one narrower thing before or alongside that: starting from an invoice amount, payment terms, and how your client actually runs payments (weekdays, twice a month, once a month), it works out the due date, the date cash actually lands once that payment run is taken into account, what a 30/60/90-day delay costs at your carrying-cost assumption, whether a 2/10 Net 30 discount still beats waiting, whether factoring still beats waiting after the fee, and how much of a project stays unpaid under a given deposit or milestone schedule. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, sends nothing you type anywhere, and does not send the invoice itself. A freelancer or small firm that already needs one system tracking every client, every invoice, and the books behind them is better served by QuickBooks Online or a comparable accounting platform; someone who wants an instant, private answer to when this invoice's cash actually arrives and whether an early-pay or factoring offer is worth it is in the right place here.

Does QuickBooks Online already do this?

QuickBooks Online lets you set payment terms (Net 15, Net 30, due on receipt, or a custom term) per customer or per invoice, and it tracks which invoices are open and how many days overdue they are. What it does not do is model when your client's specific payment run actually pays that due date, price a 2/10 early-pay discount or a factoring fee against your own cost of carrying the cash, or turn a project's billing schedule into a day-by-day unpaid-exposure figure. This calculator is not a replacement for QuickBooks Online's invoice tracking; it is the arithmetic step before you set the term or decide whether an early-pay or factoring offer is worth taking.
