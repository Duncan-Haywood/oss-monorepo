    Why chasing an invoice yourself so often loses

    Chasing recovers the most cash of any path when it works — you give up no discount and no commission. What sinks it is that the hours are certain and the payment is not. On the defaults, three hours at $85 is $255 spent whether or not the client ever pays, which drops an 85% chance at $1,188.95 down to $755.61. A 10% settlement paid in five days at 80% odds is worth $861.16.

    That is why the chase-hours break-even is the most useful number on the page. At these defaults it is about 1.8 hours: one focused call and a firm email is worth it, six weeks of polite follow-ups is not. If you can make the chase cheap — a scheduled reminder, a statement, one call — the arithmetic changes in its favour, and you can watch it change by typing fewer hours.

    What these numbers are not

    The odds are your estimate of this client, not a prediction — the calculator has never met them. Carrying cost is the rate you type, not a late fee and not interest you can invoice; use the main calculator for late-fee scenarios. Whether a late fee is permitted, what a collection agency may do, and what a settlement means for a balance you still intend to pursue are legal questions that vary by jurisdiction and by the contract you signed. This page does not answer them, and it does not tell you to settle, escalate, or walk away.

    Common questions

    Is it worth chasing a late invoice yourself?

    It depends on how many of your own hours it takes, because those hours are spent whether or not you get paid. On the calculator defaults — a $1,200 invoice 15 days overdue, 85% odds of recovery, $85 an hour — chasing recovers the most cash of any path if it works, but three hours of your time costs $255 and drops it to $755.61. It only gets ahead of a 10% settlement if you can close it in under about 1.8 hours.

    How much do collection agencies take?

    Commissions on small commercial invoices are commonly quoted in the 25-50% range, and the calculator defaults to 30% over 90 days at 45% odds. On a $1,200 invoice that is worth $355.63 — behind every other path. It gets more interesting on a large invoice from a payer unlikely to settle: at $20,000 with 55% odds, collections leads and stays ahead up to about a 65% commission. Enter the commission you were actually quoted.

    Should I offer a discount to settle an overdue invoice?

    The calculator prices it rather than recommending it. On the defaults a 10% settlement paid in 5 days at 80% odds is worth $861.16, which is $37.73 more than waiting. The break-even is about 13.9% — past that, waiting is worth more on these odds. This is arithmetic from numbers you type, not a recommendation to settle.
