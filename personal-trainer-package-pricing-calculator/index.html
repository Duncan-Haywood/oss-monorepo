    Your package protects revenue. Does it protect your take-home pay?

    A discounted prepaid session package guarantees you get paid even if a client no-shows. If a missed session also earns a free makeup rather than being forfeited, you still work that hour eventually — and if your gym or studio charges a flat per-session fee rather than a percentage cut, that fee does not shrink with your discount, so it takes a bigger bite out of a discounted package session than a full-price one. This calculator compares your package to plain pay-per-session pricing on net take-home pay per hour, after your venue's cut, so you know which one you are actually choosing.

    Common questions

    Does a prepaid client package actually protect my income?

    It protects your revenue: the client has already paid, so a no-show does not cost you that session's fee. It does not automatically protect your net take-home per hour. If your package promises a makeup for a missed session, you still work that hour eventually — the discount you gave to sell the package is paid on every hour once all of them are delivered, and if your gym or studio charges a flat per-session fee, that fee is owed on the makeup too.

    Why does a flat facility fee hurt a discounted package more than full price?

    A flat fee is the same dollar amount no matter what you billed the client, so it is a bigger percentage of a discounted package session than of a full-price single session. A $12 fee is 16% of a $75 session and nearly 21% of a $58 package-equivalent session — your own discount and the gym's flat cut compound against the same hour. A percentage revenue-share cut does not do this: it takes the same share of both, so it scales the comparison down without changing which side wins.

    So should I stop offering packages, or stop training at a commission gym?

    This calculator does not tell you what pricing or venue arrangement to run — that depends on what keeps clients booking with you and what space is available. It tells you the arithmetic consequence of the numbers you enter so the tradeoff (guaranteed revenue vs. protected hourly take-home) is a choice you make on purpose.

    What no-show rate should I use if I don't track one?

    Use your best honest estimate from recent memory, or run the comparison at a couple of different rates (for example 5% and 20%) to see how sensitive the result is. The point is the relationship between your discount and your no-show rate, not a precise historical figure.

    Is this scheduling software or tax advice?

    No. It applies the numbers you enter to one representative block of sessions — it does not track which client owes a makeup, does not schedule anything, and is not tax, legal, or financial advice.

    I already compare these in a spreadsheet — why use this instead?

    A spreadsheet catches the revenue comparison fine. It usually misses the hours a makeup guarantee still owes on a missed session, and it rarely models a flat facility fee eating a bigger share of a discounted rate than of full price. See this calculator vs. a spreadsheet for a worked example.
