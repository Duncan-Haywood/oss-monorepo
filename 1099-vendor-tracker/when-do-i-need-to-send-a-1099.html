When do I actually need to send a 1099?

For payments made in 2026 and later, the answer to "did this vendor cross the threshold" changed for the first time since 1954. A vendor who was reportable at $650 in 2025 is not reportable at $650 in 2026 — and a spreadsheet built around the old $600 line will flag vendors that no longer need one, or worse, hide the habit of checking altogether.

What actually changed

The general threshold: $600 to $2,000. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) raised the Form 1099-NEC / 1099-MISC nonemployee-compensation reporting threshold to $2,000 for payments made on or after January 1, 2026. It will adjust for inflation starting with payments made in 2027, so treat $2,000 as the 2026 figure specifically, not a number fixed forever.

The corporation exemption did not change. A C-corp, S-corp, or LLC taxed as a corporation is still generally exempt from 1099-NEC reporting regardless of how much you paid it — except an attorney's own fees, which are reportable no matter how the firm is organized. That exception is older than the threshold change and the threshold change did not touch it.

Some related categories follow their own separate rules. Gross proceeds paid to an attorney (a settlement, for example) go on 1099-MISC box 10 under section 6045(f), which kept its own $600 threshold. Medical and health-care payments also stay reportable to a corporation. Both are different calculations from the general threshold above.

Common questions

What changed about the 1099 threshold in 2026?

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) raised the Form 1099-NEC / 1099-MISC nonemployee-compensation reporting threshold from $600 to $2,000 for payments made on or after January 1, 2026 — the first change to that number since 1954. It will adjust for inflation starting with payments made in 2027.

Does the higher threshold mean I never have to send a 1099 to a corporation?

The corporation exemption is a separate, older rule the threshold change did not touch. Payments to a C-corp, S-corp, or an LLC taxed as a corporation are generally exempt regardless of amount — except an attorney's own fees for services, which are always reportable no matter how the firm is organized.

What about a payment I made to an attorney that isn't for the attorney's own services?

Gross proceeds paid to an attorney — for example, a settlement routed through counsel — follow a different rule, 1099-MISC box 10 under section 6045(f), with its own $600 threshold that did not move with OBBBA. That is a separate calculation from the general nonemployee-compensation threshold this page and the tracker describe.
