This tracker vs. Track1099

Track1099, an Avalara product, is a well-known paid service for the back half of 1099 season: collecting W-9s from vendors electronically and e-filing 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC forms with the IRS and participating states, priced per form, described on track1099.com. It is the tool a business reaches for once it already knows its full vendor list and is ready to file.

That is a different job from the one this page does. This tracker has no W-9 collection portal, no e-filing, and no per-form fee. It exists for the step before that: working out, from what you already know about each vendor, who actually needs a 1099-NEC under the 2026 $2,000 threshold, who is exempt as a corporation, and whose W-9 is still missing — with nothing to sign up for.

QuestionWhere to answer it
I have my full 1099 list finalized — how do I collect any outstanding W-9s electronically and e-file everything with the IRS and states? Track1099 or a comparable paid e-filing service — collection and e-filing are jobs this page does not attempt.
Which of my vendors actually crosses the 2026 $2,000 threshold, and which ones are exempt because they're a corporation? This tracker — enter each vendor's entity type, category, and year-to-date total, and get the threshold and exemption applied with no signup.
Which vendors on my list still don't have a W-9 on file, so I know who to chase before the deadline? This tracker — the review queue sorts a missing W-9 above everything else and marks it done the moment you record one received.
I need audit-trail e-filing confirmations and state-by-state submission. Track1099 or a comparable paid service — this page has no accounts and files nothing; it produces a CSV you can hand to whichever service you use.

This tracker is deliberately the opposite shape from an e-filing subscription: nothing to sign up for, nothing billed per form, and nothing typed here ever leaves your browser. That makes it useful for narrowing down a vendor list before you pay anyone to file it — and the wrong tool for the actual e-filing step, which is exactly the job a service like Track1099 is built for.

It needs no account and no sign-in: it is a static page, the arithmetic runs in your browser, and once it has loaded it keeps working with the connection off.

Common questions

Should I use this tracker or Track1099?

They answer different questions. Track1099, an Avalara product, collects W-9s from vendors and e-files 1099s with the IRS and states for a per-form fee, described on Avalara's own site. This tracker does not file anything and never will — it exists for the step before that: figuring out, from what you already know about each vendor, who actually crosses the 2026 $2,000 threshold, who is exempt as a corporation, and whose paperwork is still missing. A business that already knows its full 1099 list and wants to e-file it should use Track1099 or a comparable service; a bookkeeper still working out who is even on that list is in the right place here first.
