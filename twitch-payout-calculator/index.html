    The dashboard number isn't your payout. Take-home is.

    Common questions

    Does a subscription bought on mobile pay me less because of Apple or Google's cut?

    No, as of Twitch's October 2024 pricing change. Twitch charges more for a mobile subscription specifically to cover the app store's in-app-purchase fee, so your payout per sub is the same whether the viewer bought it on desktop or in the iOS/Android app. Enter your web tier price here either way — see the worked explainer.

    Why is the revenue split 50% for some streamers and 60% or 70% for others?

    Everyone starts at 50% of subscription revenue. Affiliates and Partners who sustain 100 Plus Points for three consecutive months move to 60% under Twitch's Plus Program, and those who reach 300 Plus Points move to 70%. Plus Points come only from paid recurring subs — 1 point per Tier 1, 2 per Tier 2, 6 per Tier 3; gifted and Prime subs earn none. This is the one number that actually changes your payout per sub — read your own current split off your Twitch dashboard rather than assuming one.

    Why don't Bits follow the same split as subs?

    A cheered Bit is worth a flat $0.01 to the streamer no matter what the viewer paid for the Bits bundle or what the streamer's subscription split is — Twitch's own cut is already built into what a viewer pays for Bits before the streamer ever sees the cheer. So Bits income is not reduced by a 50% or 70% split the way sub revenue is.

    Does this tool estimate ad revenue?

    No. Twitch publishes no standard ad CPM a static calculator could model honestly, and ad payouts vary by campaign and channel. Enter any ad revenue, sponsorship, or other income as the single "other monthly income" figure instead — this tool only computes the parts with documented, deterministic mechanics: subs and Bits.

    Does anything about my numbers get uploaded?

    No. Every field on this page runs in your browser only. Nothing is sent to a server and there is no account. Your inputs and target are saved locally on this device so you don't retype them, and a shareable link can carry them to another device. Usage counting only tallies anonymous feature events, and you can turn it off with the button above.
