    What it does, and what it leaves to you
    
    
      
      
    • Keep the renewal date: the old amount covers the days before each change and each new amount the days from it, every segment as days / days in the period of that amount, rounded half up to the cent. The balance is the sum of the segments minus what was billed.
      • 
      
    • Restart the period: credit = billed amount x unused days / days in the period; new charge = the full new amount; the next renewal is one period length after the change date.
      • 
      
    • Tax is the entered percent of the balance, rounded half up, and is negative for a credit.
      • 
      
    • Not decided: whether you refund, credit or keep the unused part, your provider's rounding or per-second proration, trials, usage charges, discounts and coupons, tax rules for credit notes, and currencies with other minor units.

    Common questions
    
    
    How do I prorate a subscription upgrade in the middle of a billing period?
    
    
    Split the period at the change date. The old amount covers the days before it and the new amount the days from it on, each as a share of the period's days. Add the two, subtract what the customer already paid, and the difference is the charge or credit. Paste your dates and amounts and the page does exactly that and shows every line.
    
    
    Should the renewal date move when someone upgrades?
    
    
    Both are common. Keeping the renewal date charges only the difference for the days left. Restarting the period on the change date credits the unused old time and bills a full new period from today. The page has a mode for each so you can see what the customer would pay under either.
    
    
    How is a cancellation or downgrade refund calculated?
    
    
    Enter the change date and the new amount; 0 means cancelled. The unused days at the old amount come back as a credit. Whether you refund it, keep it as account credit or keep it at all depends on your terms, which this page does not decide.
    
    
    Does it match Stripe, Chargebee or Paddle?
    
    
    It uses plain day-count proration rounded half up to the cent. Billing providers can prorate by the second, round differently or apply their own rules, so check the figure against the provider's preview before you rely on it.
    
    
    Is my billing data uploaded anywhere?
    
    
    No. The arithmetic runs in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
