    Questions people ask

    What is the difference between carryover basis and stepped-up basis?

    If you give someone an appreciated asset while you are alive, they take over your cost basis (IRC section 1015) and owe capital gains tax on all the growth since you bought it when they sell. If they inherit it at your death, their basis resets to the fair market value on the date of death (IRC section 1014), so the growth during your lifetime is never taxed.

    Should I put my child on the deed to my house?

    Usually not for tax reasons alone. A lifetime gift of the house gives your child your low basis, loses the home sale exclusion on their share, and can be a Medicaid look-back transfer. A transfer-on-death deed, a revocable trust or a life estate deed (where your state allows it) can move the house at death and keep the step-up. This tool prices the tax side so you can take the comparison to an attorney.

    When does gifting beat inheriting?

    When the estate is large enough that the federal estate tax (40% above the exemption) outweighs the capital gains tax the recipient will pay, so moving future growth out of the estate saves more than the lost step-up costs. Below the exemption, which is 15,000,000 per person in 2026, inheriting nearly always wins for appreciated property.

    Do I need to file Form 709?

    A gift to one person above the annual exclusion (19,000 in 2026) needs a gift tax return, even if no tax is due because it just uses lifetime exemption. A gift of a share of a house to each of two children is tested per recipient.

    What if the property has lost value?

    A gift of loss property uses the lower fair value at the date of the gift for any loss the recipient later claims, so a loss cannot be passed on. An inheritance resets basis down to the date-of-death value. Either way the paper loss is not usable, so selling before is the case to ask a professional about.
