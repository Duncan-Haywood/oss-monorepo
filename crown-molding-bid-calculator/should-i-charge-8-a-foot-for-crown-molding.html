Should I charge $8 a foot for crown molding?

A homeowner brings you a published “$8 a foot installed crown” Facebook special. It reads like a safe number for any run. Priced at this calculator's own default crew rates — a $42 wage, 32% burden, 15% overhead, 20% target margin — it does not cover the walk-away price on any of the four jobs the calculator ships with, and the gap between the cheapest and the most expensive is not small.

Same footage, thirteen times the gap

A per-foot number moves in a straight line with linear feet: it cannot see grade, profile, ceiling shape, or how you reach the crown. The walk-away price moves with all four. 80 lf of paint-grade simple cove in a flat 8-ft room copes nothing and climbs no scaffold. The same 80 lf of stain-grade built-up in a two-story cathedral foyer with an LED cove is coped insides, two stacked pieces of stock, a spring-angle change, a scaffold ticket, and an LED channel — none of which a per-foot dump ever carried.

This is a scope question, not a footage question

The trap is not "the run got longer." It is "the run stayed the same length and the owner is asking for stain-grade built-up in a foyer instead of paint-grade cove in a bedroom, and the number I quoted from never had that scope in it." A carpenter who matches the Facebook special on footage alone — without re-checking grade, profile, ceiling, and height — is the one who finds out the copes and the scaffold ate the whole margin.

Common questions

Should I charge $8 a foot for crown molding?

No, not at professional crew rates. Under this calculator's own default crew rates ($42/hour wage, 32% burden, 15% overhead, 20% target margin), $8 a foot does not cover the walk-away price on any of its four example jobs — not even the cheapest one. On 80 linear feet of paint-grade simple cove in one flat 8-ft room, $8/ft ($640.00) is $542.95 under the $1,182.95 walk-away price.

Why is the gap so much bigger on some jobs than others?

Because $8 a foot is quoted from the linear footage alone, and the walk-away price is not. On the identical 80 linear feet, swapping paint-grade simple cove in a flat room for stain-grade built-up crown in a two-story cathedral foyer with an LED cove raises the walk-away price from $1,182.95 to $7,710.87 — the same $640.00 quote goes from $542.95 short to $7,070.87 short, thirteen times worse on the same footage.

Does a bigger run mean a smaller gap?

No. 180 linear feet of paint-grade colonial ($1,440.00 at $8/ft) is $1,862.54 short of its $3,302.54 walk-away price, and 120 linear feet of stain-grade colonial in a tray ceiling ($960.00 at $8/ft) is $3,987.07 short of its $4,947.07 walk-away price. The shortfall tracks grade, profile, ceiling, and height, not linear feet alone.
