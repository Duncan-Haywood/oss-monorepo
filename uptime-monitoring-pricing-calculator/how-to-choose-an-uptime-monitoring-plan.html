How to choose an uptime monitoring plan

Put your monitor count, check interval and status-page need into the calculator to price UptimeRobot, Better Stack, Hyperping, Pingdom and self-hosted Uptime Kuma side by side. These are the questions to settle before you trust the answer.

Why does the cheapest uptime monitor change as I add monitors?

Plans are tiers, not per-unit prices. UptimeRobot's free plan covers 50 monitors at five-minute checks, but 60-second checks need a paid tier, and past 100 monitors you jump to the next one. Better Stack adds a per-monitor fee after its included monitors, while Pingdom steps through fixed 10, 50, 100 and 500-check plans. The cheapest provider at 10 monitors is rarely the cheapest at 120, which is what the crossover table shows.

Which check interval do I actually need?

Match it to how long an outage may go unnoticed. A five-minute check can miss a four-minute outage entirely and delays the alert by up to five minutes; 60 seconds is the usual paid default and 30 seconds or faster costs the most. If your SLA promises 99.9 percent monthly (about 43 minutes of downtime), a 3 or 5 minute interval is usually fine; for payment or login flows pay for 60 seconds or less.

Should I self-host Uptime Kuma?

It is free software and a small VPS runs hundreds of monitors, so it is cheapest on paper. The catches: the monitor must not live on the infrastructure it watches, you maintain and patch it, and you need a second independent way to alert you when the monitor itself dies. Many teams self-host for volume and keep one hosted monitor watching the self-hosted one.
