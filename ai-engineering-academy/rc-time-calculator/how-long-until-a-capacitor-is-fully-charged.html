    Common questions

    How long until a capacitor is fully charged?

    There is no exact answer, because an RC charge never mathematically reaches 100% — it only gets closer and closer, forever. What people usually mean is one of a few conventions stated in time constants (τ = RC). On this page's defaults (10 kΩ, 100 nF, 5 V supply, so τ = 1 ms): 4τ reaches 98.17%, 5τ reaches 99.33%, and 10τ reaches 99.995%, indistinguishable from full for almost any purpose. Pick your own R, C, and supply above to see the real numbers for your circuit.

    Why doesn't a charging capacitor ever reach exactly 100%?

    Because the rate it charges at is proportional to how far it still has to go. The voltage follows V(t) = Vs(1 − e^−t/RC): every additional time constant closes 63.2% of whatever gap is left, not a fixed amount of voltage. Closing 63.2% of a gap can shrink that gap forever without ever making it zero, the same way repeatedly halving a distance never quite reaches the wall — it is arithmetic, not a limitation of any particular circuit.

    Is 5 time constants really "fully charged"? How close is that?

    5τ reaches 1 − e^−5 = 99.326% of the supply — a common convention, but a convention, not a law. On this page's own defaults that is 4.9663 V out of a 5 V supply, 3.37 mV short. Whether that is close enough depends entirely on what the circuit does next: 0.67% off is nothing for an indicator LED and can be a real error for a precision ADC reference.

    How do I find the time for a different threshold, like 99.9%?

    Use the target-percent tool above rather than reading the fixed table: it inverts the same equation, t = −RC × ln(1 − P/100), for any P you choose. On this page's defaults, 99.9% takes 6.9078 time constants — 6.9078 ms — noticeably longer than the 5τ convention for one more '9' of precision, which is the general shape of an exponential tail: each added 9 costs roughly one more time constant (ln 10 ≈ 2.303τ per decade).

    Does this apply to discharging too, not just charging?

    Yes, symmetrically. A discharging capacitor follows V(t) = V0 × e^−t/RC, closing 63.2% of the remaining voltage every time constant on the way down to zero instead of up to the supply — same τ = RC, same never-quite-zero asymptote, same 5τ/99.33% convention, just approaching from the other direction. The parent RC calculator's own discharge mode uses the identical formula this page's table is built from.
