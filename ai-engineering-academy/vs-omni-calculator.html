These calculators vs. Omni Calculator

Omni Calculator answers one formula at a time across thousands of unrelated topics. These six tools are a narrow, dependency-free electronics toolkit that goes one step past the formula: it snaps a design to a resistor or capacitor you can actually buy, and flags the ones a real chip can't run.

What each is for

Omni Calculator is a large, well-known general calculator website with a catalog spanning many unrelated subjects — finance, health, math, physics, everyday life, sports, and more — free to use with no account required. Its physics section also covers common electronics formulas, such as Ohm's law and resistor color codes. Omni Calculator's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm the exact catalog it carries today, so this page does not claim which specific electronics calculators it does or does not currently publish, and no dollar figure for it appears anywhere on this page — only its well-documented identity and category, a free general-purpose calculator website, are stated.

These six tools do one narrower job. They are dependency-free static pages built for one electronics curriculum: resistor color codes and Ohm's law, LED series resistors, a loaded voltage divider, 555 timer astable/monostable design, RC/RL filters, and RC charge/discharge time. Where a calculator has a design mode — LED, voltage-divider, 555-timer, RC-filter, and RC-time — it does not stop at the formula: it snaps the computed value to the nearest standard E24 part and shows the exact pair to buy, instead of leaving an odd value like 8,342 Ω to source by hand. The 555-timer calculator also flags a design a real chip cannot run — a duty cycle at or below 50%, an R1 that over-currents the discharge pin, drift-prone resistor values. None of that requires an account, and nothing typed is uploaded anywhere.
