This calculator vs. a spreadsheet

Most tutors who compare a prepaid package against pay-per-session pricing do it with one spreadsheet line: package price against sessions times single-session price. That line answers a revenue question. It does not answer whether the package pays better per hour actually worked — and when a package promises a makeup for a missed session, those two questions can point in opposite directions.

What each one is for

A spreadsheet is fine for the revenue comparison, and most tutors already have one: package price in one cell, sessions times single price in another. What it leaves out, unless someone builds it in on purpose, is hours — specifically, that a package with a makeup guarantee still owes every session eventually, so a no-show does not shrink the hours worked the way it does under pay-per-session.

This calculator takes your single-session price, package size and price, no-show rate, and whether a miss earns a makeup, and reports revenue and effective hourly pay for both models side by side, plus the package price that would clear a target hourly rate.

Worked example: a $60 session, a 10-for-$450 package, 10% no-shows, makeup owed

A tutor charges $60 per single session, and sells a package of 10 sessions for $450 — a 25% per-session discount. From recent memory, about 10% of booked sessions are no-shows, and the tutor's package policy is to reschedule (not forfeit) a missed session. Each session runs 60 minutes with 15 minutes of prep, so 75 minutes of work per session delivered.

Pay-per-sessionPackage
Revenue for this block$540.00$450.00
Hours actually worked11.2512.50
Effective hourly pay$48.00/hr$36.00/hr

A revenue-only spreadsheet line sees $450 versus $540 and stops at "the package brings in less, but it's guaranteed up front." It never surfaces the second, sharper fact: because the missed session is made up rather than forfeited, the tutor ends up working more hours for less money — $36.00 an hour against $48.00 an hour charging full price per session and letting a no-show simply free that slot. The 25% discount is deeper than the 10% no-show rate it was meant to cover, and a makeup guarantee turns that gap into an hourly-pay loss rather than a wash.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet compare package pricing against pay-per-session?

It can compare revenue in one line: package price against sessions times single-session price. What it usually does not do on its own is account for the hours a makeup guarantee still owes on a missed session, which is the part that decides whether the package actually pays better per hour worked.

Why does a revenue-only comparison miss the real answer?

Because a package guarantees revenue but, with a makeup owed, does not reduce hours the way a pay-per-session no-show does. A no-show under pay-per-session simply frees that slot, unpaid and unworked. A no-show under a package with makeup owed is rescheduled, not forfeited, so every hour is eventually worked at the discounted rate. A spreadsheet cell comparing two revenue totals never sees that difference.

What would I have to build into a spreadsheet to catch this?

A row for whether a missed session is made up or forfeited, a formula that converts that choice into hours actually worked (not just sessions attended), and effective hourly pay for both pricing models from those hours — then keep that formula correct every time the no-show rate, discount, or makeup policy changes. This calculator runs that model directly and shows revenue and effective hourly pay side by side.

Does either one tell me what to charge?

No. Both a spreadsheet and this calculator only compute the consequence of numbers you supply — your single-session price, package size and price, no-show rate, and makeup policy. Neither one knows your market or your students; you decide what to charge.
