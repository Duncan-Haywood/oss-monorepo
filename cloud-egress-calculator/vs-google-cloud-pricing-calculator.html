This calculator vs. the Google Cloud Pricing Calculator

Google publishes its own free cost-estimation tool, the Google Cloud Pricing Calculator, at cloud.google.com/products/calculator. Its own instruction to a new visitor is to “Add and configure products to get a cost estimate to share with your team,” and Google's Cloud Storage price list at cloud.google.com/storage/pricing points readers to it directly: “For the Google Cloud pricing calculator, see the Calculator page.” Signing in is offered there to “view billing account pricing.”

That makes it the right tool for one job: what a configuration of Google Cloud products would cost on Google's own schedule. It prices Google's products against Google's numbers, which is something no third-party page, this one included, can claim to do. This site does not publish, cache, or endorse any provider's prices — it asks you to paste the rates from the price sheet or quote in front of you. Google also states the limits of its estimate on the calculator page itself:

“This tool creates cost estimates based on assumptions that you provide. These estimates may not accurately reflect the final costs on your monthly Google Cloud bill.”

So if the question is a Google Cloud number, open Google's calculator; this page says so plainly. What it does not do is the thing this tool exists for — putting a second provider's sheet next to the first one on the same workload, which is the shape of an actual migration decision.

Getting the question right also matters more here than the arithmetic does. Google's Cloud Storage price list separates network usage charges into “Data transfer within Google Cloud,” “Specialty network services,” and “General data transfer,” the last covering transfer out of Google Cloud or between continents — and it defines outbound data transfer as “data sent from Cloud Storage in HTTP responses.” Providers differ in how they cut those lines up, which is exactly why this tool asks for one outbound rate per option and shows its working, rather than pretending a single number is comparable across two sheets you have not read.

Beyond the comparison itself, this tool needs no account, no cloud project, and no sign-in. It is a static page, the arithmetic runs in your browser, and once it has loaded it keeps working with the connection off. Nothing you type is uploaded anywhere, and the copyable decision summary deliberately carries the totals and the cheaper option without the rate sheet behind them, so pasting it into a migration ticket cannot leak a quote you are under NDA about.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or the Google Cloud Pricing Calculator?

Most teams get more use out of both, because they answer different questions. The Google Cloud Pricing Calculator estimates the cost of Google Cloud products you add and configure, priced against Google's own schedule, so it is the right place to answer “what would this bucket and this outbound volume cost on Google Cloud?” This page does not try to replace that, and it never publishes or endorses any provider's prices. What it adds is the comparison Google's calculator is not built around: two providers' price sheets on one workload at the same time, the outbound-gigabyte volume where a cheaper storage rate stops being cheaper, and the CDN, replica, and storage-class calls that sit on top of that decision.

Why does this calculator make me type the rates in myself?

Because a rate this page had cached would be wrong the day a provider changed it, and you would have no way to tell. Every provider publishes its own current price sheet and its own estimator; those are the authoritative sources and this tool deliberately defers to them. Typing the rates also means the arithmetic works for a negotiated or committed-use rate that no public sheet lists, and it means nothing you enter has to leave your browser.
