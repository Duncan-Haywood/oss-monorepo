    I can sell more detailing services than I have bay hours for. Which do I keep?

    Enter the bay hours a week you have for paid work and each service's jobs, price, product cost, booking and card fees, consumables, bay time and own monthly cost. The tool finds the set of services that earns the most inside those hours, shows each one's profit per job, per bay hour and break-even jobs, compares the exact answer with the rules of thumb, says what more bay hours would be worth, and tests the answer against jobs 30% lower and higher.

    Arithmetic on figures you type. Services do not take bookings from each other here, and labour is charged at the hourly rate you enter. The example figures are placeholders, not any real shop's numbers.

    Questions auto detailers ask

    Can I sell every service I can do?

    You can, but a day-long ceramic coating and a 45-minute wash and wax do not earn the same per bay hour. Past the hours you can actually work, every extra service pushes out a better one. This tool finds the set of services that earns the most inside the bay hours you really have.

    How is a service's profit worked out here?

    Per job: price less product cost, less booking-app and card fees, less consumables, less the labour to do it. Multiply by jobs a month, then subtract the service's own monthly cost. Services that lose money are never picked.

    Why not just rank services by profit?

    Profit per bay hour, biggest profit and best margin each miss some combinations. When the hours are tight, a service that looks best can crowd out two others that earn more together. The tool tries every set of up to 14 services and shows how far each rule falls short.

    What are break-even jobs?

    The weekly jobs at which a service covers its own monthly cost after products, fees, consumables and labour. Below it, the service loses money no matter how busy the bay feels.

    Is this business or financial advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on the figures you type. It does not model services taking bookings from each other, reviews, add-on upsells, weather, cure times that block a bay or ramp-up time.

    Not decided

    Whether services cannibalise each other, add-on upsells, reviews and repeat customers, cure or dwell time that blocks a bay, travel time for mobile work, certification or insurance requirements, equipment bottlenecks beyond total hours, tax, and seasonality. Every service is treated as independent and every hour as interchangeable.
