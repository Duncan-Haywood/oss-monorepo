How much does a land survey cost?

Far less than acreage decides, and far more than corner count decides. A 5-acre parcel with lost corners in heavy woods can price higher than a 40-acre parcel with easy corners and a clean title — the opposite of what a per-acre rate assumes.

The same 5 acres, three different corner stories

Using this calculator's own verified defaults for everything except corner count, condition, and terrain:

Every row is the same 5 acres. A flat per-acre rate quotes all three at $600.00; the actual price ranges from $1,084.82 to $1,970.97 — corner count and terrain, not acreage, decide where in that range a job falls.

A small, hard parcel can cost more than a large, easy one

A 40-acre parcel with 4 easy corners, open terrain, and only 1 hour of title research prices at this calculator's $850.00 minimum job fee — about 82% less than the $4,800.00 a flat $120/acre rate would quote for 40 acres. The 5-acre parcel with 4 corners to set in heavy terrain above prices at $1,970.97, more than double the large parcel's price, on an eighth of the acreage. Acreage alone predicts neither number.

Common questions

How much does a land survey cost?

It depends far more on corner count and condition than on acreage. On this calculator's own verified defaults (5 acres, 3 corners to locate, 1 to set, moderate terrain), a survey prices around $1,274.05 — more than double a flat $120/acre quote for the same parcel ($600.00). A small, difficult parcel can price higher still than a large, easy one.

Why can a small parcel cost more than a large one?

Because field time is driven by corners, not acres. A 5-acre parcel with all 4 corners needing to be set from scratch in heavy terrain prices around $1,970.97 on this calculator's defaults — more than double a 40-acre parcel with 4 easy-to-locate corners in open terrain and a light research load, which prices at the $850.00 minimum job fee.

How much more does setting a corner cost versus locating one?

On the same 5-acre parcel, switching all corners from “to locate” in open terrain to “to set” in heavy terrain raises the field-hours figure from 1.67 to 8.07 — nearly 5x — because setting a corner with no recoverable monument means resolving its position from the deed and running a traverse to it, field time a flat per-acre rate has no line for.

Does a bigger parcel always cost more to survey?

No. On this calculator's defaults, a 40-acre parcel with 4 easy corners, open terrain, and 1 hour of research prices at the $850.00 minimum job fee — about 82% less than the $4,800.00 a flat $120/acre rate would have quoted for that acreage, because the corners and research drove almost no field or office time regardless of how many acres they sit on.
