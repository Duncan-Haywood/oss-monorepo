This calculator vs. QuickBooks Job Costing

Two different shapes: QuickBooks Online's job costing tracks every booked transaction against a project, inside a full ongoing accounting subscription. This page is a free, private six-field worksheet for one question — where did this one already-finished job move from estimate to actual, and what should that change about the next quote.

What each tool is for

QuickBooks Online Job Costing lives behind the Projects feature on the Plus and Advanced plans. Every invoice, bill, expense, and timesheet entry gets tagged to a project as the job runs, QuickBooks Time supplies the labor hours, and the Project Profitability report totals whatever was actually booked in the books over the job's life — estimates, transactions, and outstanding invoices in one dashboard. It is accurate exactly to the extent every transaction was tagged correctly, and it requires the accounting system underneath it: a chart of accounts, bank feeds or manual entry, and an ongoing subscription. QuickBooks' own pricing and plan details are not repeated here, since this session cannot verify today's exact figures against Intuit's current pricing page; only its well-documented feature set and plan structure are described.

This calculator does none of what QuickBooks' job costing does. It has no ledger, no chart of accounts, and no memory between visits. It takes six numbers you already have once a job is finished — estimated and actual labor hours, materials, and other costs, read off a spreadsheet, invoice, or job folder — and returns the cost variance, the labor-hour variance, whichever line moved the most (in matching units if you add a labor rate), and, if you add the job's bid price too, the estimated margin, actual margin, and the margin swing between them. It runs free in any browser, needs no account and no bookkeeping setup, and uploads nothing you type.

Most contractors get different value from each: QuickBooks (or a comparable accounting platform) for a running, transaction-accurate profitability record across every job, and a private calculator like this one for an instant, no-login read on where one already-finished job moved and which assumption to revisit before the next quote. This page's own figures are a completed-job review, not a ledger, an invoice record, or a substitute for bookkeeping.

This tool needs no account and no sign-in: it is a static page, the arithmetic runs in your browser, and once it has loaded it keeps working with the connection off.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or QuickBooks Job Costing?

They do different jobs. QuickBooks Online's job costing lives behind the Projects feature on the Plus and Advanced plans: every invoice, bill, expense, and timesheet entry gets tagged to a project as it happens, and the Project Profitability report totals whatever was actually booked over the job's life, integrated with QuickBooks Time for labor hours. It requires an ongoing accounting subscription, a chart of accounts, and consistent tagging discipline to be accurate. This calculator does none of that. It takes six numbers you already have after a job is done — estimated and actual labor hours, materials, and other costs — and returns the cost variance, labor-hour variance, the single largest movement, and optionally the margin swing, instantly, in the browser, with no account, no bookkeeping setup, and nothing uploaded. A contractor running their whole business's books through QuickBooks is better served finishing that setup; a contractor who wants a fast, private read on one already-finished job, without opening the accounting system, is in the right place here.
