      Common questions

      Why does the calculator ask which windows, not just the vehicle size?

      Because a “front two only” privacy job and a full seven-window kit both roll up to a shop calling it “one sedan,” and a flat per-vehicle price cannot see the difference. Film cost and install time both scale with how many panes are actually being tinted, not with the vehicle's size class.

      Why is removing old tint priced separately, and why does it cost so much more on the rear windshield?

      Stripping existing film is slower, separate work from applying new film, and it is only needed on the panes that already have tint. A rear windshield's defroster grid means the old film has to come off with a heat gun and patience instead of a fast peel, so this calculator prices its removal time well above a flat door window's.

      Why compare against a flat per-vehicle price?

      Because that is how the trade actually prices this work day to day — a posted “$180 sedan / $220 SUV” list. It is a reasonable starting point for an average job, and a poor one for a scope-light two-window job or a full ceramic kit with removal on every pane. The comparison panel below shows the gap on the job you just priced.

      Does this check my state's window tint darkness (VLT%) law?

      No. This is a pricing worksheet only. It does not check your state's tint darkness limits, medical exemptions, reflectivity rules, or any other film-legality requirement — verify those against your own state's statute before scheduling the job.

      Does anything about my job or customer get uploaded?

      No. Every number on this page runs in your browser only. Nothing is sent to a server, there is no account, and no vehicle, customer, or price is ever saved or transmitted. Usage counting only tallies anonymous feature events, and you can turn it off below.
