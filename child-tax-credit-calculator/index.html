    What's your Child Tax Credit worth?

    The federal Child Tax Credit is $2,200 per qualifying child under 17 with a Social Security number, plus $500 per other dependent (an ITIN-only child, a dependent who turned 17, or a qualifying relative) — made permanent by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act starting tax year 2025. Both shrink together once modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) clears $200,000 (single/HoH/QSS/MFS) or $400,000 (joint), and up to $1,700 per child can come back as a refund even if you owe no tax. This applies the phase-out, the filer-SSN requirement, and the refundable-ceiling formula.

    Common questions

    How much is the Child Tax Credit?

    $2,200 per qualifying child under age 17 (by December 31) with a Social Security number valid for employment. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act made this amount permanent starting tax year 2025, with inflation indexing going forward -- it is still $2,200 for 2026.

    What if my dependent doesn't have a Social Security number?

    A dependent with only an ITIN instead of an SSN, or a dependent who turned 17 during the year, does not qualify for the Child Tax Credit -- but may qualify for the separate $500 Credit for Other Dependents (ODC) instead. The ODC is entirely nonrefundable, unlike the Child Tax Credit's refundable Additional Child Tax Credit portion.

    Do I need a Social Security number myself to claim these credits?

    Yes -- an OBBBA change. The taxpayer (and, on a joint return, at least one spouse) must have a Social Security number valid for employment to claim either the Child Tax Credit or the Credit for Other Dependents, even if the child has a valid SSN. A couple where both spouses hold only an ITIN cannot claim either credit.

    What is the income phase-out?

    The combined Child Tax Credit and Credit for Other Dependents is reduced $50 for every $1,000, or part of $1,000, that modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) exceeds $200,000 (single, head of household, qualifying surviving spouse, or married filing separately) or $400,000 (married filing jointly). Even $1 over the threshold costs a full $50 -- the reduction rounds up to the next $1,000.

    What is the refundable Additional Child Tax Credit?

    Up to $1,700 per qualifying child of an unused Child Tax Credit can be refunded even if you owe no tax, called the Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC). It is capped at the smallest of $1,700 per qualifying child, 15% of your earned income over $2,500, and the Child Tax Credit remaining after the phase-out. This calculator reports that ceiling, not a guaranteed refund -- the actual amount also depends on your total tax liability, which this tool does not model. The Credit for Other Dependents is never refundable.

    Can I claim this if I file married filing separately?

    Yes. Unlike this portfolio's own senior-deduction-calculator, which excludes married filing separately entirely, MFS filers can claim the Child Tax Credit and Credit for Other Dependents for the dependents they support, using the same $200,000 phase-out threshold a single filer uses.
