UK Statutory Residence Test ties table: how many days with how many ties

If no automatic overseas test and no automatic UK test settles your residence for the tax year, the sufficient ties test does. It compares the days you spent in the UK with the number of ties you keep here. Which table applies depends on whether you were UK resident in any of the previous three tax years.

Leavers: UK resident in one or more of the last 3 tax years

Five ties can count: family, accommodation, work, 90-day and country.

Arrivers: not UK resident in any of the last 3 tax years

Four ties can count: the country tie does not apply.

Days in the UKResult
0 to 45Automatically not resident
46 to 90Resident with 4 or more ties

The ties

  • Family: a spouse, civil partner or partner you live with who is UK resident, or a child under 18 who is UK resident and whom you see in the UK on at least 61 days.
  • Accommodation: a place to live available for a continuous 91 days or more in the tax year, where you spend at least one night (16 nights if it belongs to a close relative).
  • Work: three or more hours of work in the UK on at least 40 days in the tax year.
  • 90-day: 90 or more days in the UK in either of the previous two tax years.
  • Country (leavers only): you spent more days in the UK than in any other single country.

Worked example

A recent leaver spends 101 days in the UK in 2026/27. They keep a family tie and an accommodation tie, so 2 ties. At 101 days a leaver needs 2, so they are resident under this test. At 90 days the requirement is 3, so they would not be: 11 fewer days changes the answer. Try your own numbers.

Common questions

How many days can I spend in the UK without becoming resident?

It depends on your ties. With no ties, up to 182 days; a recent leaver with four ties becomes resident at 16 days; an arriver with all four ties becomes resident at 46. The tables on this page give the threshold for every number of days.

What are the five ties?

Family, accommodation, work, 90-day and (for leavers only) country. Each is defined in HMRC's RDR3 guidance; the checker asks you a question for each.
