    Is booth rent or commission better for a nail tech?

    It depends on how many clients you book and how much of your chair time sits idle. Booth rent is a fixed weekly cost, so it loses on slow weeks and wins once you are busy; a commission split costs you a share of every service but nothing when the chair is empty. The calculator shows take-home per hour worked for booth rent, commission and an hourly job, and the clients per week where booth rent overtakes the others.

    Why take-home per hour worked instead of per hour booked?

    Because the hours you are at the salon waiting for a client, and the hours you spend booking, cleaning and ordering, are hours you gave up either way. Dividing by booked hours flatters every arrangement; dividing by hours worked shows what the week actually paid.

    Does it upload my numbers?

    No. The browser page works on the numbers and text you enter, in your own browser, and never sends them anywhere. The CLI reads a local file or stdin and makes no network call.

    How is take-home per hour worked calculated?

    Weekly net = what you keep (services, tips, retail) less rent, card fees, supplies, insurance and fixed costs. Self-employment tax is the rate you enter times 92.35 percent of net; the income reserve is a percent of net less half of that tax. Take-home = net less both, divided by hours worked: the larger of your hours at the salon and your booked hours, plus unpaid admin hours.

    What is the break-even client count?

    The fewest clients a week at which an arrangement reaches your target take-home per hour worked. The crossover is the client count where booth rent first pays more per hour worked than the commission split or the hourly job.
