This checker vs. eyeballing Settings → Branches

The ordinary way anyone checks branch protection is opening a repository's Settings → Branches page and reading the toggles, or running gh api repos/:owner/:repo/branches/:branch/protection and skimming the JSON. That confirms a rule is present -- required reviews are on, force pushes are off.

It does not confirm a rule still says what your team agreed it should say. A required review count quietly dropped from 2 to 1 during an incident still reads as "required reviews: on". A required status check left over from a CI job that was renamed six months ago still reads as "status checks: required" -- it just can never be satisfied again, because nothing produces a check with that exact name anymore. Both look identical to a glance and identical in the raw JSON unless you compare every field against a separate, written record of what it should be.

What it replaces is the part eyeballing the settings page cannot do at all: comparing every field against a separate written policy and naming, specifically, which ones no longer match.

Common questions

Isn't it faster to just look at Settings → Branches myself?

For confirming a rule exists at all, yes — open the page, see the toggle, done. This tool answers a different question: does every field still match what your team actually agreed the policy should be? A required review count that was quietly dropped from 2 to 1, or a required status check name left over from a renamed CI job, still shows as "on" in the UI. Reading the page confirms the rule is present; it does not confirm the rule still says what you think it says.

What does eyeballing the settings page actually miss?

Anything where the field is set but wrong rather than unset. The count under required approving reviews, whether Require approval of the most recent push is on, which named status checks are required (a renamed or deleted CI job leaves a required check nothing can ever satisfy, invisible unless you compare the exact string), and whether admin enforcement is on all look identical to a glance — a settings screen shows their current value, not whether that value still matches a decision your team wrote down somewhere else.

Do I need to write down a policy before I can use this?

Only informally — a few key: value lines are enough (see the field reference on the tool itself), and you can paste the actual settings first and treat that as your policy draft going forward. The comparison is only as useful as the policy is accurate, the same way reading the settings page is only as useful as your memory of what they should say.

Is this a replacement for reading the branch protection settings?

No — you still fetch the actual settings yourself, with gh api repos/:owner/:repo/branches/:branch/protection or the Settings → Branches page, and this tool never reads them for you. It replaces the field-by-field comparison against a written policy, not the act of looking at your repository's settings.
