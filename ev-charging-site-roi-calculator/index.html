    Questions site hosts and operators ask

    How much does an EV charging station make?

    It depends almost entirely on utilization: how many sessions each port serves per day. Revenue is kWh sold times price; against it sit energy, demand charges, card fees, network fees and maintenance. A busy DC fast site can earn a healthy margin per kWh, while the same site at two sessions a day usually loses money to fixed costs. Enter your own figures to see where yours falls and what utilization breaks even.

    What are demand charges and why do they matter for EV charging?

    Many commercial tariffs bill a monthly fee per kW on the highest short-interval power draw, whatever the energy used. Four 150 kW chargers can set a peak of several hundred kW in one quarter hour and be billed for it all month. At low utilization this fixed bill dominates; the tool shows demand charges as a share of revenue and how NPV changes if the rate were lower or higher.

    What is a good payback period for a charging station?

    There is no universal answer. Hosts that count the chargers as an amenity may accept a longer payback than an operator selling energy for profit. The tool reports simple payback, net present value at your discount rate and internal rate of return, so you can compare with your own hurdle.

    Level 2 or DC fast charging?

    Level 2 costs far less per port and has little or no demand charge, so it can pay back at low utilization where cars park for hours. DC fast charging costs far more but sells more energy per port per hour, and depends on high utilization and on demand-charge tariffs. Use the two presets as a starting point and replace every figure with your own quotes.

    Are the incentives included?

    Only the grant and rebate dollars you type in. Federal, state and utility programs change, have deadlines and conditions, and some have ended. Check current rules before counting on one; the tool does not know them.

    Is this financial or engineering advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on figures you type. It does not model taxes, depreciation, financing, utility tariff details, grid capacity or site engineering. Get quotes from installers and your utility and speak to a qualified advisor before committing capital.
