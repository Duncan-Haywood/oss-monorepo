    Questions people ask

    What is a demand charge?

    Many commercial electricity tariffs bill not just for energy used (kWh) but for the highest average power drawn in any 15- or 30-minute window of the billing month (kW). That one interval is multiplied by a rate, often $10 to $25 per kW, so a single short spike can set a large part of the bill.

    What is a demand ratchet?

    Some tariffs bill at least a set percentage, often 50% to 90%, of the highest demand reached in the previous 11 months, even if this month was quiet. A ratchet makes one bad afternoon cost money for a year, which is why the tool shows billed demand next to measured demand.

    Where do I get interval data?

    Most utilities let commercial customers download 15-minute data from their online account, often as a Green Button CSV. Paste the file contents here, or use the file picker. The tool reads the date and time column and the usage column on its own and tells you which it chose.

    Is a battery worth it for peak shaving?

    It depends on how tall and how long your peaks are. The cap table shows, for each level you could hold demand to, the yearly saving, the battery energy needed to ride through your longest peak above that level, and a rough payback from the costs you enter. Short, tall peaks pay back far faster than long, shallow ones. Load shifting or staggering equipment starts can capture some of the same saving for far less.
