    Questions founders ask

    How do I price Good-Better-Best SaaS plans?

    Ask a sample of customers or prospects the most they would pay per month for each plan, paste one row per person, and the tool tests the price ladders that matter. Each respondent is assumed to buy the plan that leaves them the most surplus (their maximum price minus the price), or nothing if no plan has any. It returns the prices that earn the most profit, who ends up on each plan, and what that is worth against your current prices.

    Why not price each plan at what the average customer would pay?

    Because customers choose between plans. Raising the middle plan can push people to the cheap one or up to the top one. The tool prices the whole ladder together, which is what moves the numbers.

    How many survey responses do I need?

    Enough that the answer does not swing when a few change. Try deleting a handful of rows and re-running: if the prices jump, collect more. With under 30 rows treat the result as a direction, not a number.

    Is a willingness-to-pay survey reliable?

    Stated prices usually run above what people pay. Use the result to rank ladders and see how fragile your prices are, then test the top candidate on real signups. This is arithmetic on your survey, not pricing or financial advice.
