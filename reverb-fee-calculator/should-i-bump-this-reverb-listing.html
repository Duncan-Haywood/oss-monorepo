Should I bump this Reverb listing?

A used guitar is listed at $800. A $720 offer is in the inbox. You paid $450 for the guitar and $10 to set it up. Shipping is $75 charged / $65 you pay. Reverb marketplace is entered at 5% of the item, payment at 3.19% + $0.49. Bump fee on the listing is $0, so the table uses a $3 illustration — a typical Reverb bump, not a live price. Overwrite it on the calculator.

A bump is a fixed cash out of this sale. It never pays on the same price. Paying $3 and still taking $720 always leaves $3 less. It only pays if it gets you from that offer to list — or to an offer high enough to cover the bump after marketplace and payment take their cut.

On this example, a $3.00 bump still pays if it gets you from the $720 offer to the $800 list — you keep $70.45 more than taking the offer with no bump. To keep no-bump offer cash after paying the bump, you need $723.27 ($3.27 more after percentage fees).

Waiting for list with no bump still leaves the most cash. None of those sentences is advice to buy a bump or refuse an offer — they are the arithmetic on the inputs above.

Common questions

Should I bump a Reverb listing or take the offer?

On the worked example — $800 list, $720 offer, $450 gear cost, 5% marketplace, 3.19%+$0.49 payment — taking the offer with no bump leaves $208.15. Paying a $3 bump and still taking $720 leaves $205.15. Selling at list with no bump leaves $281.60. Selling at list after the $3 bump leaves $278.60, which is $70.45 more than taking the offer. The bump only pays if it changes the price you take.

How much higher does a Reverb offer need to be to cover a bump?

Marketplace, payment, and Reverb Gives still take a cut of any higher offer. On the worked example a $3 bump needs $3.27 more item price, so $723.27 keeps the same cash as the $720 offer with no bump. Type your own bump on the calculator; this page does not claim Reverb's live bump prices.

Does a Reverb bump pay if I still take the same offer?

No. A bump is a fixed cash out of this sale. Paying one and still taking the same offer always leaves less by exactly the bump. It only pays if it gets you to list or to a higher offer that covers the bump after percentage fees.
