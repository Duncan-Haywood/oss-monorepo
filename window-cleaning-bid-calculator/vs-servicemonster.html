This calculator vs. ServiceMonster

Two different shapes: ServiceMonster is account-based field-service management software for running a window cleaning company's whole operation. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this one visit cost, given its panes, glass type, and travel, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

ServiceMonster is field-service management software that markets specifically to cleaning-industry businesses — window cleaning, pressure washing, carpet cleaning, and related exterior and interior cleaning trades. Its roots go back to 2002 as software built for carpet cleaners, and it is headquartered in Bellingham, Washington. It combines customer and account management, job scheduling and technician routing, estimates and invoicing, and automated marketing campaigns into one subscription system, with a mobile app for crews in the field. ServiceMonster's own marketing says it is trusted by thousands of service pros, and industry coverage reports it was acquired in 2023 by Teamfront. It is sold as an ongoing subscription account. ServiceMonster's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact plan names or dollar figures, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — cleaning-industry field-service management software, sold as a subscription account — are stated, and no dollar amount for ServiceMonster appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what ServiceMonster does. It does not manage customer accounts, route technicians across a schedule, or run marketing campaigns, and it stores no customer or job record on a server. It takes one visit's panes, grouped by glass type — ground-floor, French or divided-light, second- and third-story access, storefront plate, skylights, screens, and hard-water or restoration glass — with minutes per pane and sides, plus travel and setup time, cleaner wage, payroll burden, supplies, fuel, overhead, target margin, and a minimum job charge, and turns that into the per-visit price, the price per pane, the effective hourly rate, and the walk-away price below which the visit loses money — plus, if you already have a price on the table, a check of whether it clears that walk-away floor, and a single printable customer proposal for that one stop. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.
