## The finding this rescore deliberately does not score on

PR #240 measured which candidates can fetch their own runtime input data from
*this environment* and found five of eight cannot. It is a correct measurement
and it would be a category error to move any score for it, so this is written
down before a later pass does it by reflex.

The egress policy constrains **what an agent session in this sandbox can build
and measure**. It says nothing about what a deployed product could fetch from
ordinary hosting. An accessibility scanner that cannot reach a customer's
storefront from here is not thereby a worse business; it is a business this
portfolio cannot prototype or demo without one grant.

So reachability belongs in `time_to_revenue` reasoning at most — as *"blocked on
the owner before the first line can be written"* — and not in `defensibility`,
`distribution`, `gross_margin`, or `ai_leverage`. It is recorded as a build
constraint per candidate, not as a score. The two candidates whose scores did
move here moved for reasons that survive outside this sandbox entirely: public
data is public data on any host, and registry listings carry no price whoever
publishes them.

## What the pattern of measurements says

Six dimensions across two candidates have now been measured rather than
estimated. **All five score-bearing measurements moved the score down**, by one
point each; none moved up. Meanwhile the two corrections to *environmental*
assumptions both moved up: cold-listing odds went from an assumed ~0% to a
measured 4.9% (#218), and feed reachability went from "no reachable feed" to
full canonical scope (#250, #253).

The consistent direction is worth stating plainly, because it is the most
decision-relevant thing this pass produced and it cost nothing to observe:

> This pipeline's screening scores are optimistic about the businesses and
> pessimistic about the environment. Product assumptions — moats, AI leverage,
> what a listing earns — have lost a point every single time anyone checked.
> Environmental assumptions — what can be reached, what odds a cold publisher
> faces — have improved every time anyone checked properly.

Two consequences:

1. **The six unmeasured candidates should be read as upper bounds.** Nothing in
   the table above is evidence that the 7.0s are better than the 6.75s; it is
   evidence that they have not been measured yet. The two candidates that fell
   to the bottom half are the two that got checked.
2. **Cheap environmental checks have positive expected value; further scoring
   does not.** Every environmental re-check so far has *unblocked* something,
   and every product-assumption check has cost a point off a candidate nobody
   has sold anything to. Screening remains closed
   ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) intake gate); measuring what this environment
   can actually do remains cheap and keeps paying.
