# Quote margin check

## Target and pain

Freelancers and small agencies often price from delivery hours while forgetting
admin, revisions, coordination, and direct costs. A quote can look busy but pay
below the owner's intended hourly rate. The first product is a local calculator
that makes the hidden assumptions visible before the quote is accepted.

## Value and return use

The result is immediate: total hours, effective hourly rate, and the price needed
to hit the user's own target. Users return for each proposal and can keep their
inputs private. It is not a pricing recommendation or financial advice.

## Alternatives and differentiation

Spreadsheets, calculators, and project-management tools exist. The narrow wedge
is a fast, portable quote review that explicitly includes admin and revision
buffers, produces a shareable evidence report, and does not require an account
or upload. A future optional user-supplied AI key could summarize scope risks,
but the deterministic calculator is the complete free core.

## Acquisition and revenue

Search terms include "freelance effective hourly rate calculator", "agency quote
profit calculator", and "how to price revision hours". Templates and examples
can earn organic referrals. A later model could offer team templates, saved
quote history, or paid exports, but payment is intentionally not implemented.

## Delivery, cost, and operations

The vertical slice is a Python CLI with no server, API, inference, account, or
variable hosting cost. It can run in a user's CI or a small static/browser UI.
Usage is aggregate and local-only. Agent operation is straightforward because
the arithmetic is deterministic and tests cover invalid inputs and hidden time.

## Risks and portfolio fit

Liability is contained by showing inputs, formulas, and a disclaimer; the tool
does not certify profitability or advise on tax, legal, or financial decisions.
Users supply their own data, so there is no scraping or personal-data broker.
This is new relative to the portfolio: existing retail analytics measures store
performance, Print Atelier checks print assets, and evidence ventures preserve
technical or vendor state. None evaluates a user's proposed service quote.
