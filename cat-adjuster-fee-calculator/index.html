    Common questions

    Why does the per-claim fee alone not tell me whether a deployment is worth it?

    Because the per-claim fee only pays for claims you actually close, and closing them depends on a pace you cannot fully control. Meanwhile estimating-software, lodging, per diem, vehicle, and mileage cost accrue every day you are away from home, including the unpaid travel days spent driving to and from the disaster zone before your first claim closes. A fee schedule can look generous per claim and still pay a low effective hourly rate once the full trip's costs and a realistic pace are netted out.

    Does this tool tell me whether I'm licensed or appointed to work a deployment?

    No. This calculator only prices your own deployment economics from numbers you supply. It does not determine your licensing, appointment, or authority to handle a claim in any state, and it does not evaluate an IA firm's reputation, timeliness of payment, or claim-volume promises -- verify those independently before accepting a deployment.

    Why do unpaid travel days count toward the cost but not the claims?

    Because that is what actually happens on a deployment: you are paying for lodging, food, your estimating-software subscription, and your vehicle on the days you drive to and from the disaster zone, but you are not closing claims on those days. Leaving travel days out of the cost side is the most common way a deployment's real profit gets overstated.
