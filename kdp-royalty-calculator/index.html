    Common questions

    How many Kindle Unlimited page reads equal one paid sale?

    On the starting example — a $4.99 ebook on the 70% plan with a 2 MB file and $0.15/MB delivery — one paid sale nets $3.193. At a $0.0045 KENP rate that is 709.6 Kindle Edition Normalized Pages. Your own list price, file size, plan, and this month's KENP rate change that number; the readout updates as you type.

    Does this tell me to enroll in KDP Select?

    No. It reports two 90-day totals on the numbers you typed. Enrolling, leaving, or staying in KDP Select is a contract decision you make against Amazon's current terms, not a recommendation this page can make.

    Why can the paperback line go negative?

    KDP paperback royalty is a share of list minus printing cost. A short book on a low list can have a print cost larger than that share. The page shows the negative rather than hiding it, because that is the arithmetic Amazon's own print preview is warning you about.

    Is the print-cost formula the same as KDP's live preview?

    No. The blank-override starting formula is a published-shape approximation (a small fixed amount plus a per-page rate, B&W or color) so the page has a number before you paste one. KDP's own print preview, for your trim, paper, ink, and marketplace, is the figure to paste into the override box.

    What is left out?

    Expanded distribution, hardcover, audiobook, VAT, currency conversion, ads, returns, and any change in paid-sale volume from being in or out of KU. Those are real, and they are not in this arithmetic. A later line can add them; inventing them here would be a forecast wearing a calculator's clothes.
