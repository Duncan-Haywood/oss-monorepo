This calculator vs. BeProfit

Two different shapes, on opposite sides of the sale. BeProfit is a profit-analytics app installed on your store, reporting what your orders already earned. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what will this order, or this price, actually leave, before you set it.

What each tool is for

BeProfit is a profit-analytics and expense-tracking app for e-commerce merchants, distributed through the Shopify App Store and installed onto a store. It connects to the store and to advertising accounts and pulls order revenue, cost of goods, shipping, transaction fees, and ad spend into one dashboard, so profit and margin can be read across orders over time and broken down by product, channel, and campaign. It is sold as an ongoing subscription account tied to a store. BeProfit's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's plan names or dollar figures, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented category — an installed, connected, subscription profit-analytics app for e-commerce stores — is stated, and no dollar amount for BeProfit appears anywhere on this page.

This calculator does none of what BeProfit does. It does not connect to your store, read your orders, track ad spend, attribute revenue to a campaign, or keep any history at all. It takes one order's figures — item price, shipping charged, cost of goods, shipping you pay, packaging and other variable costs, and the monthly Shopify plan allocated across your expected monthly orders — plus the processing rates from your own statements, and turns them into what the order nets on Shopify Payments and on a third-party processor side by side, including the extra Shopify third-party transaction fee that generic fee pages skip. From the same inputs it reports the break-even listing price, the price that hits a target margin or a target dollar profit, return on the cash the order ties up, and the monthly volume at which Grow, Advanced, or Plus covers its own extra monthly fee. It runs free in any browser, installs nothing, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

The distinction that matters is tense. A connected analytics app can only report orders that exist — that is the source of its accuracy. This page has no orders and needs none, which is exactly why it can price one that has not happened yet, or test a plan change, or compare two payment paths on a hypothetical basket. Neither can do the other's job.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or BeProfit?

They answer questions on opposite sides of the sale. This calculator does one narrower thing, and does it before the order exists: from the item price, shipping charged, cost of goods, shipping you pay, packaging, the monthly plan allocated across expected monthly orders, and the processing rates you enter, it reports what a single order nets on Shopify Payments and on a third-party processor side by side — including the extra Shopify third-party transaction fee that generic fee pages skip — plus the break-even listing price, the price that hits a target margin or a target dollar profit, and the monthly volume at which Grow, Advanced, or Plus covers its own extra monthly fee. It runs free in any browser, installs nothing on the store, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A merchant who wants to know what the store actually earned last month across every order and ad campaign is better served by BeProfit or a comparable profit-analytics app; a merchant deciding what to charge, whether to switch payment paths, or whether a plan upgrade pays for itself is in the right place here.

Does this calculator connect to my Shopify store?

No. It is a static page and installs nothing. There is no app to add to your store, no OAuth screen, no permissions to grant, and no read access to your orders, products, or payouts. You type the rates from your own Shopify billing and processor statements, and the arithmetic runs in your browser — which is also why it can answer questions about an order that has not happened yet, and why a profit-analytics app that reads real orders cannot.
