    What this checks

    Merchants search “is Shopify Capital worth it” because the offer looks like $1,400 on $10,000 — 14%. The missing piece is time. Remitting 10% of $30,000 / month takes 114 days to repay $11,400. That 14% fee over 114 days is about 44.8% simple annualized. Waiting to save leftover profit costs the fee only if you miss extra sales you could not fund yourself.

    On the defaults — $10,000 advance, $11,400 repayment, 10% holdback, $30,000 monthly sales, $0 extra monthly profit, leftover from the $48 SKU at 100 orders — waiting keeps $0.00, a 12% bank line keeps −$374.79, and Capital keeps −$1,400.00. You need about $368.42 extra profit / month during the holdback for Capital to match waiting. Change the offer from your email.

    Common questions

    Is Shopify Capital a loan?

    Shopify Capital is usually a merchant cash advance: you receive a lump sum and repay a larger amount by remitting a holdback percent of daily sales. The gap between repayment and the advance is a fee, not a stated APR. This page turns that fee into a simple annualized cost over the days the holdback lasts. That figure is arithmetic on your inputs, not a Truth-in-Lending APR and not financial advice.

    When does Shopify Capital still pay?

    It pays when the extra profit you can only earn by having the cash now covers the fee before the holdback finishes. This calculator shows the fee, days of holdback, a wait-and-save path from leftover profit, a bank line at a typed APR, and the extra monthly profit that would make Capital match waiting. Leave extra monthly profit at zero if you are not buying inventory or running a campaign you would otherwise skip.
