What this does not cover

  • Sole proprietor / single-member LLC only. This applies the net-self-employment-earnings formula for a Schedule C filer. An S-corp or partnership uses a different formula (W-2 wages or a K-1 allocation) and this tool does not compute it.
  • Employees change the math. If you have eligible employees, a SEP-IRA must give each of them the same percentage of pay that you give yourself. This tool does not model that.
  • Not a Schedule SE substitute. This ignores the additional 0.9% Medicare surtax above $200,000/$250,000 and does not account for multiple businesses or controlled-group rules.
  • Point-in-time 2026 figures. These limits are re-indexed annually; confirm the current year's figures before relying on this in a later year.

Common questions

What is the 2026 SEP-IRA contribution limit?

The lesser of 25% of eligible compensation or $72,000. For a self-employed owner, 25% of compensation works out to an effective 20% of net self-employment earnings, and net earnings are capped at the $360,000 compensation limit.

How is a SEP-IRA contribution calculated for a sole proprietor?

Net profit minus half of self-employment tax gives net earnings from self-employment. Multiply by 20%, the self-employed equivalent of the 25% plan rate (25% divided by 1.25), because the contribution is deducted before the earnings it is measured against.

When can I open and fund a SEP-IRA for 2026?

A SEP-IRA can be set up and funded up to your tax filing deadline for the year, including extensions. A Solo 401(k) employee deferral generally requires the plan to be opened by December 31.

Is a SEP-IRA or a Solo 401(k) better for a self-employed person?

On the same earnings a Solo 401(k) usually allows more, because it adds an employee deferral to the same 20% employer share. A SEP-IRA is simpler to set up and never needs an annual Form 5500 filing. The calculator shows both figures side by side.
