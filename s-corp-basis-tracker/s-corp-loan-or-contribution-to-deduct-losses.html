S corp loan or capital contribution: which one unlocks a suspended loss?

When your share of an S corporation loss is bigger than your basis, the rest is suspended. You can free it by adding basis before the company's year ends, either as a capital contribution or as a direct loan from you to the company. Both give the same deduction this year. They are not the same afterwards.

One invented example

You start the year with $5,000 of stock basis and no loans to the company. Your share of the loss is $30,000. With nothing added you deduct $5,000 and $25,000 is suspended.

Option A: contribute $25,000 before year end

Stock basis becomes $30,000, the loss fits, and you deduct $30,000. Stock basis and debt basis are both $0 at year end and nothing is suspended. The $25,000 is capital in the company, not a balance owed to you.

Option B: lend the company $25,000 before year end

The loan has $25,000 of basis from the day it is made, so the loss fits and you deduct $30,000. The company still owes you $25,000, but the loss used all of that loan's basis, so its basis is $0.

Where they differ: getting money back

Next year the company repays the $25,000. With a basis of $0 the whole repayment is $25,000 of gain (ordinary income if the loan is a note, capital gain if it is an open account). If the company earns $10,000 first, income restores loan basis before anything else, so the same repayment gives $15,000 of gain.

Deduction this yearOwed to you afterGain if repaid next year with no income
Contribute $25,000$30,000$0not repayable as debt
Lend $25,000$30,000$25,000$25,000

So a loan keeps a claim on the money, but taking it back is taxed to the extent losses used its basis. A contribution gives up the claim. Neither changes the deduction.

What to check

  • Only a direct loan from you counts. Guaranteeing a bank loan does not add basis.
  • Document a loan as one: a written note, a rate and a repayment plan. Whether it is debt is a question of facts; a preparer should decide.
  • The deduction can still be limited by at-risk and passive-activity rules after basis. The tracker does not model them.

Questions

Should I make a loan or a capital contribution to deduct an S corporation loss?

Either adds the basis the loss needs, and the deduction is the same. The difference comes later. A contribution is not repaid. A direct loan can be repaid, but a loan whose basis losses have used is returned with gain on the reduced part. Which fits depends on whether you expect to take the money back, and how the loan is documented; ask a preparer.

Does a bank loan I guarantee give me basis to deduct an S corporation loss?

Generally no. Only money you put in yourself counts: stock purchases, capital contributions, and a direct loan from you to the company. Guaranteeing a loan from a bank or lending through another entity generally does not.

Does the contribution have to be made before year end?

Basis for the loss limit is tested at the end of the company's tax year, so money added after year end counts toward the next year, not the year that was limited.
