    Questions vineyard owners ask

    Should I sell my grapes or make wine?

    Compare the two on the same acreage. Selling the fruit nets the price per ton times your tons, less growing and picking. Making wine nets the bottles you actually sell times what you keep per bottle, less winemaking, dry goods, labour, the money tied up while it ages, excise tax, marketing and fixed winery overhead, less the same growing cost. This tool puts the two side by side and shows which one is ahead at your figures.

    How many cases of wine does a ton of grapes make?

    A common rule of thumb is about 150 gallons, roughly 60 twelve-bottle cases, per ton of fruit before losses. Your figure depends on the variety, the press and how much you lose in processing; both are inputs here.

    What sell-through do I need for bottling to beat selling fruit?

    The tool finds the share of bottled wine you must sell for the two paths to tie, and shows the gap at sell-through from 40% to 100%. Unsold bottles are counted at zero, which is deliberately cautious.

    How many acres does it take to justify a winery?

    Fixed yearly costs such as licences, insurance and accounting are spread over every case. If each acre earns more as wine than as fruit, the tool shows the acreage above which the overhead is absorbed; if it never does, it says so.

    Is this financial, tax or legal advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on figures you type. Alcohol licensing, excise tax, labelling and shipping rules are set by federal, state and local law; check yours with a qualified professional.
