      FOR ELECTRICAL CONTRACTORS

      Bid the run and the panel, not the charger.

      A Level 2 EVSE is a continuous load. Size the breaker at 125%, pick copper that holds a 3% voltage drop for the actual run, then add trench hours and panel work. A 25-foot indoor hang on a spare slot is one job. The same 48 A charger on a 70-foot trench to a detached garage that also needs a service upgrade is several times that price — and a flat “EV charger install” quote cannot see the difference.

      Questions electricians actually type

      How much should I charge to install an EV charger?

      There is no flat hang fee. Use this page: a short indoor 48 A job on a spare slot often lands near $1,200–$1,400 at the default rates. Switch the location to a detached garage, add a 60-foot trench, and pick a service upgrade — the same charger becomes a several-thousand-dollar job. That gap is the whole product.

      What size breaker and wire does a 48 A charger need?

      48 A continuous × 1.25 = 60 A breaker. Copper #6 usually carries that ampacity; a long run may upsize to hold 3% voltage drop at 240 V. The result panel shows the gauge and the calculated drop for the feet you entered.

      Do I have to include a service upgrade?

      Only if the existing service does not have the headroom. Enter the service rating and a honest existing-load estimate. If the new breaker is larger than the unused capacity, the tool flags it — it does not decide the upgrade for you, and it is not a permit load calculation. A 200 A service already estimated at 190 A of existing load has only 10 A of headroom; a 48 A charger needs a 60 A breaker, so that job gets flagged even though the panel itself has a free slot.

      Why does a long run sometimes need thicker wire than the breaker alone requires?

      Ampacity and voltage drop are two different limits. A 48 A charger's 60 A breaker is carried by #6 copper on ampacity alone, but at 160 feet that #6 run would sit past the 3% voltage-drop budget at 240 V. The calculator checks both and upsizes to #4 (1.98% drop at 160 ft) whenever the shorter-run wire would fail the drop check — the wire size shown is whichever limit binds for the feet you entered, not just the breaker size.

      How much can a detached garage and a service upgrade add to the price?

      At the default rates, a 48 A indoor hang on a spare slot with a 25-foot run prices near $1,263. Move the same charger to a detached garage with a 70-foot run, a 60-foot trench, and a service upgrade, and it prices near $4,587 — largely trench labor and restoration, the extra outdoor labor rate, and the 8 hours a service upgrade adds to the panel line. Same charger, almost 3.6× the price, because the job is not the charger.

      What does the baseline comparison number mean?

      The result panel prices your entered job against a fixed reference — the same wage, burden, overhead, and margin, but a 48 A indoor hang, 20-foot run, spare panel slot, no trench, no hardware. At the defaults that baseline prices near $1,189, so a $1,263 quote reads as about 6% over it. That reference is the "easy garage" number a customer often has in mind from a flat-fee ad; the ratio shows how far your actual job's numbers move it, not whether either price is right.
