Annual plans, spread across the months they cover

When a customer pays $2,900 up front for a year, only a twelfth of it is revenue this month. Bookkeepers want that as a schedule: billings, revenue recognised and deferred revenue by month, plus journal entries. Stripe sells this as an add-on priced on your volume. Rev Rec builds it from your paid invoices and credit notes. Try it below on an invoice-line export (nothing is uploaded), then run the open-source server with a read-only key.

Try it on an invoice export

One row per invoice line. Needs columns for amount, billing date and period start; period end, id and currency are used when present. Put credit notes in as negative amounts, or in parentheses. Or load made-up sample data.

Rows marked ↗ are future months of revenue that is already invoiced. Closing deferred revenue is billed to date minus recognised to date.

Questions

How is this different from Stripe Revenue Recognition?

Stripe Revenue Recognition is a paid add-on billed as a share of your Billing volume, with accounting-grade reports, multi-entity features and support. Rev Rec produces the core schedule most small SaaS companies hand to a bookkeeper (billings, revenue recognised, deferred revenue by month and the journal entries) on a server you run with a read-only key. It does not replace an audit-ready system.

What does it recognise, exactly?

Each paid invoice line is spread evenly per day across its service period, net of discounts and of tax that is included in the price. One-off charges are recognised on their date. Credit notes reduce revenue in the month they are issued.

Can it change anything in my Stripe account?

No. It lists paid invoices and credit notes with a restricted key (Invoices: read, Credit notes: read) and the tests assert every request is a plain GET.

Where does my Stripe key live?

In the environment of the server you deploy in your own account. The author runs nothing in between and never sees your key or your numbers.

Is this accounting advice?

No. It is a working schedule for your bookkeeping. Your revenue policy, your jurisdiction and your accountant decide what is correct for your books.
