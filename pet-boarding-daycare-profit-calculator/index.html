    PET BOARDING AND DAYCARE PROFIT CALCULATOR · RUNS IN YOUR BROWSER · NOTHING UPLOADED

    What occupancy does your dog boarding and daycare business need to break even, and how much cash does the ramp-up eat?

    Enter your runs and daycare spots, prices, expected occupancy and the number of dogs one attendant looks after. You get monthly profit with staffing that steps up as dogs are added, break-even occupancy, the price change that hits your target margin, how wage changes move profit, a sixty-month ramp-up with the lowest cash point and payback, and loan coverage.

    It works from your own figures. It does not know what kennels near you charge, how full they are, or what your licence requires.

    Your facility

    What it does, and what it leaves to you
    
    
      
      
    • Sales: runs × occupancy × 30 nights, plus daycare spots × fill × days open, plus one add-on per stay (nights ÷ average stay), less card and software fees as a share of revenue.
      • 
      
    • Staffing: average dogs on site ÷ dogs per attendant, rounded up, never below your minimum, times staffed hours × 30 days × wage plus payroll costs. This is a step cost, so the page scans capacity in half-percent steps to find break-even instead of solving a straight line.
      • 
      
    • Other costs: supplies per dog-day, rent, utilities, manager, insurance and other fixed costs.
      • 
      
    • Startup: build-out per run, equipment, deposit, launch and contingency. The loan is a share of that on level payments. Ramp-up runs sixty months with demand climbing in a straight line from the month-1 figure; payback is the first month cumulative cash covers your equity.
      • 
      
    • Not decided: local demand and competing kennels; boarding licence, zoning and noise rules; vaccination and bite-liability policy; the staffing ratio your insurer requires; holiday and seasonal swings; income tax and depreciation; and what a lender will offer.

    Common questions
    
    
    How much profit does a dog boarding and daycare business make?
    
    
    It depends on how many runs and daycare spots you fill, what you charge, and what attendants, rent and insurance cost. Enter your own figures; the page works out revenue after fees, supplies, staffing, fixed costs, monthly profit and margin at your planned occupancy.
    
    
    How much occupancy does a dog kennel need to break even?
    
    
    Costs do not rise smoothly with dogs: you add an attendant each time the ratio is passed. The page scans every half percent of capacity, with boarding and daycare at the same percent full, and reports the first level that covers operating costs and the first that also covers the loan.
    
    
    How many attendants do I need for my dogs?
    
    
    The page divides the average number of dogs on site by the dogs-per-attendant figure you enter, rounds up, and never goes below your minimum staff. Use the ratio your insurer, your state or county, or your own standard requires; the page does not know it.
    
    
    How much does it cost to open a dog boarding facility?
    
    
    Build-out per run, equipment, lease deposit, launch cost and a contingency, all from your inputs. A ramp-up table shows the lowest cash point while demand builds, which is the reserve to hold on top of your equity.
    
    
    Does this tell me whether a kennel will succeed?
    
    
    No. It works only from your figures. It does not know local demand, competing kennels, seasonality, licensing, zoning and noise rules, or disease and liability risk. Check those first.
    
    
    Is my data uploaded anywhere?
    
    
    No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent; a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
