This calculator vs. working out the Section 174A catch-up by hand

The 174A catch-up looks like one multiplication — a balance times a tax rate. The problem is that both the "cap it at taxable income" rule and the time-value difference between 2025 and 2026 are easy to skip when you are doing the arithmetic once, in a spreadsheet cell, rather than running both election paths side by side.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Forgetting that a deduction can only produce an immediate cash tax saving up to that year's taxable income before the deduction. A hand estimate often multiplies the whole deduction by the tax rate and calls that the saving -- but if the deduction is bigger than the year's income, the excess does not vanish or convert to cash; it can create or increase a net operating loss instead, which is a different (and generally less valuable) outcome.

What's the second most common mistake?

Comparing the two elections on nominal dollars alone. If your 2025 and 2026 rates and income are similar, the 1-year and 2-year paths can add up to about the same total tax saving on paper -- but a dollar saved in 2025 is worth more than a dollar saved in 2026, so a nominal-only comparison misses the time-value difference between the two paths.

What election-scope mistake gets made most often?

Confusing the 1-year-vs-2-year choice on the 2025 return with the separate retroactive election to amend 2022-2024 returns directly. That retroactive path existed under the same Rev. Proc. 2025-28, but its election window closed July 6, 2026. The live choice today is only between taking the remaining balance entirely in 2025 or splitting it across 2025-2026.

What rate mistake gets made most often?

Assuming the same marginal or effective rate applies in both years. A business anticipating a bracket change -- higher income, a change in entity structure, or a different mix of income -- can get a meaningfully different answer than one assuming a flat rate, and a hand estimate often skips modeling the two years separately.

Does this calculator know my complete tax situation?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page knows your full return -- other deductions, credits, AMT, self-employment tax, or state conformity to Section 174A, which varies by state. Neither verifies your small-business eligibility (the $31 million average-gross-receipts test) or models net operating loss carryforward value. Both apply the same published Rev. Proc. 2025-28 election structures to the numbers you enter; verify against the IRS's own guidance or a qualified tax professional before relying on either.
