This tool vs. scoring every job out of ten

Scoring a job list 1-10 feels rigorous, and usually comes back with three or four jobs clustered at the same number -- so the ranking still is not finished once the scoring is, and the tie gets broken by the same gut feel the scores were supposed to replace. This tool never asks for a number: it asks which of two jobs you would rather take, one pair at a time, and returns a complete order with nothing left to weigh.

What each approach actually does

Scoring out of ten asks you to convert an intuition into an absolute number with no fixed anchor, for every job on the list. Different jobs pull that number from different directions at once — urgency, pay, how much you dislike the client, drive time — and a 1-10 scale has far fewer distinct values than a typical job list has entries. Score twelve jobs and several will land on the same number, which means the hard part — deciding the order among the tied jobs — is still ahead of you, done by feel, after the scoring exercise that was supposed to replace feel.

This tool never asks for a score. It asks only which of two specific jobs you would rather take first — a comparison most people answer instantly and consistently — and runs a merge sort over your list, asking roughly n·log2(n) of those head-to-head questions before stopping with a complete order and no ties left over. The tradeoff it makes in exchange is assuming transitivity: if you prefer A to B and B to C, it never asks about A and C, and simply assumes you would also prefer A to C.

Common questions

What is actually wrong with scoring each job out of ten?

Not the math -- the input. A score asks you to convert an intuition ("I'd rather do this one") into an absolute number with no anchor, and different jobs pull that number from different directions: urgency, pay, how much you dislike the client, drive time. Score twelve jobs and several will land on the same number, because a 1-10 scale has far fewer distinct values than a list of jobs usually has entries, so the ranking still isn't finished when the scoring is.

So what does this tool ask instead?

Only which of two specific jobs you would rather take first. That is a comparison most people can answer instantly and consistently, where a 1-10 score is a judgement call you can second-guess every time you revisit it. The tool runs a merge sort over your list, asking roughly n log2(n) of these head-to-head questions, and returns a complete order with no ties left over.

Is a pairwise comparison always better than a score?

No -- it assumes transitivity: if you prefer A to B and B to C, it never asks about A and C, and simply assumes you would also prefer A to C. A 1-10 score does not need that assumption, because every job is judged against the same fixed scale rather than against each other. For most short job lists transitivity holds well enough that the tradeoff favours fewer, easier questions; the tool's own guide says so rather than hiding the assumption.

Is my list uploaded anywhere?

No. The ranking runs entirely in your browser. Your job list, your answers, and the finished order are never uploaded, and the optional usage counter records only which features were used and the day -- never any job names or list contents.
