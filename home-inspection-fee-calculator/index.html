    Common questions

    Why does a crawlspace or basement cost more than a slab foundation?

    A slab has nothing underneath to inspect. A crawlspace means suiting up and physically moving through a tight space to check the underside of the structure, and a basement usually holds the mechanicals (furnace, water heater, panel) in one place that still has to be inspected on top of the rest of the house. This calculator adds a flat time premium for each, on top of the square-footage base.

    Why does the rush report premium apply to labor time and not to travel or add-ons?

    A same-day or rush turnaround costs you scheduling flexibility on the inspection itself, not a discount on the mileage you already drove or the radon canister you already paid for. This calculator applies the rush premium to on-site labor cost only, the same way an after-hours premium applies to billed time rather than to parts in this portfolio's other trade calculators.

    Why are radon, sewer scope, termite/WDO, and mold priced as flat add-on fees instead of more minutes?

    Each of those is usually a separate instrument, a separate specialist visit, or a lab sample with its own turnaround — not simply more time spent walking the same house. Pricing them as their own flat fee keeps that cost visible instead of folding it into a vaguer per-square-foot number.

    Does this calculator replace my own state licensing requirements or InterNACHI/ASHI Standards of Practice?

    No. It only does arithmetic on the numbers you type in — time, mileage, add-on fees, overhead, and margin — to suggest a price. It says nothing about what your state licence requires you to inspect, what your governing Standards of Practice cover, or what your errors-and-omissions policy actually insures. Verify all of that against your own licensing board and standards body.
