This calculator vs. Housecall Pro

Two different shapes: Housecall Pro runs a home-service business — scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, payments. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this service call cost, after-hours premium and minimum charge included — before you quote it on the phone.

What each tool is for

Housecall Pro is field service management software for home service businesses, used as a central hub for the work around a job rather than the pricing of one. It covers a drag-and-drop scheduling calendar, real-time dispatch, automated on-my-way texts with GPS tracking, online booking, recurring service plans, a mobile app that lets a technician pull up the work order and record notes without returning to the office, estimates and invoicing with automated payment reminders, card payments, and a QuickBooks integration. It is sold as an ongoing account for the business, and it publishes that it is used by over 200,000 field service professionals across HVAC, plumbing, electrical, cleaning, and other trades. Housecall Pro's current plan names and dollar figures are not verified from this page, so none are repeated here: only its well-documented identity and category — field service management software, sold as an ongoing account — are stated, and no price for Housecall Pro appears anywhere on this page.

This calculator does none of what Housecall Pro does. It does not schedule a job, dispatch a van, send an invoice, or take a payment, and it holds no customer record between visits. It takes one service call — labor and travel minutes at your hourly rate, an after-hours or emergency premium applied to that billed time only (never to parts, since a cylinder costs the same at 2am as at 2pm), a flat trip fee, parts at cost with your markup, a callback reserve for a warranty return visit, overhead, and target margin — and turns that into the quoted price, compared against your stated minimum service charge so a short job never prices below what the trip itself costs. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Housecall Pro?

They do different jobs, and most working locksmiths end up wanting both. Housecall Pro is field service management software for home service businesses: a drag-and-drop scheduling calendar, real-time dispatch, automated on-my-way texts with GPS tracking, online booking, recurring service plans, a mobile app that lets a technician pull up the work order and record notes in the field, estimates and invoicing with automated reminders, card payments, and a QuickBooks integration. It is sold as an ongoing account for running the business, and it publishes that it is used by over 200,000 field service professionals across HVAC, plumbing, electrical, cleaning, and other trades. This calculator does none of that. It takes one service call — labor and travel minutes at your hourly rate, an after-hours premium applied to that billed time only, a trip fee, parts at cost with markup, a callback reserve, overhead, and target margin — and turns it into the quoted price, compared against your stated minimum service charge floor. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A shop dispatching techs, booking jobs and chasing invoices across every customer is better served by Housecall Pro or a comparable platform; a locksmith who wants an instant, private answer to what one call should cost before quoting it on the phone is in the right place here.

Does Housecall Pro price a lockout or rekey for me?

Field service platforms are built to move a job through scheduling, dispatch, invoicing and payment, and they hold whatever price book or flat-rate pricing you put into them. What they do not do is work out that number from your own labor rate, after-hours premium, parts markup, and minimum charge floor for one specific call. This calculator is the step before the price book: it turns those inputs into a single quoted price, so the number you load into a platform — or say on the phone before you're already at the door — has arithmetic under it.
