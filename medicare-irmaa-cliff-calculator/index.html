      Common questions

      What is the Medicare IRMAA cliff?

      IRMAA is an income-related surcharge added to Medicare Part B and Part D premiums. The tiers are cliffs: one dollar of modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) above a threshold moves you to the next tier for the whole year. For 2026 the first threshold is $109,000 for a single filer and $218,000 for a married couple filing jointly.

      Which year's income sets my 2026 IRMAA?

      Social Security uses your tax return from two years earlier. Your 2026 premium is based on your 2024 MAGI, and income you report in 2026 sets your 2028 premium. That is why a Roth conversion or large capital gain shows up in your premium two years later.

      How much is the IRMAA surcharge in 2026?

      The standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month. The Part B surcharge is $81.20, $202.90, $324.60, $446.30 or $487.00 a month per person depending on the tier, plus a Part D surcharge of $14.50, $37.50, $60.40, $83.30 or $91.00 a month per person if you have a Part D plan.

      What if I am married filing separately?

      If you lived with your spouse at any time during the year, married filing separately has only three bands: no surcharge up to $109,000, the fourth-tier surcharge above $109,000 and below $391,000, and the top tier at $391,000 and above. If you lived apart all year, the single thresholds apply.

      Can I appeal an IRMAA surcharge?

      If your income dropped because of a life-changing event such as marriage, divorce, the death of a spouse, retirement or a work reduction, the loss of income-producing property, loss of pension income or an employer settlement payment, you can ask Social Security to use a more recent year with Form SSA-44. A checklist and draft text are on this page. This is information, not advice.
