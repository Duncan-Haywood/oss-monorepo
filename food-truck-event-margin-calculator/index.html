      Common questions

      Why does a per-head quote need to count prep and travel time at all?

      Because the truck and its staff are committed to the job well before service starts. Commissary prep, the drive to the venue, and breakdown afterward are real hours that a flat "$8/head" price does not show unless you add them in — and they happen whether the event runs 2 hours or 6.

      Why does the calculator treat turnout as a risk, not just a cost line?

      A flat per-event quote is built from an expected or guaranteed guest count, but actual turnout depends on the event — a well-attended wedding or a festival with a longer line can serve far more guests than a corporate lunch headcount. The calculator reports what share of your price the food line already represents, so you can see how much room is left before a bigger-than-expected crowd erases the margin you built the quote on.

      How is the recommended extra-guest rate different from just charging my per-head food cost for the extra seats?

      It marks the extra guest's food cost up the same way the rest of the quote is marked up — food cost plus your overhead rate, divided by your target margin — so seats added the week before an event still earn the same margin instead of just covering the added food at cost.
