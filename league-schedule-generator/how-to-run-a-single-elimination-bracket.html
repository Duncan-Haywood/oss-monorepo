How to seed and run a single-elimination bracket, with a worked example

Single elimination is the quickest way to find one winner: lose once and you are out. It suits play-offs at the end of a league, one-day pickleball, darts or esports events, and any tournament where courts or daylight are short. The two decisions that go wrong by hand are the seeding order and the byes when the number of teams is not 4, 8 or 16.

A worked example: 6 teams, 5 games

Six invented teams are seeded 1 to 6. The bracket is sized up to 8 slots, and the two empty slots become byes for the top two seeds, so the #1 and #2 seeds skip the first round. Seeds 1 and 2 are on opposite halves, so they can only meet in the final. "Winner of R1M2" is whoever wins that game.

Questions

How many games are in a single-elimination tournament?

Always one fewer than the number of teams, whatever the size, because every team except the champion loses exactly once. Six teams play 5 games; sixteen teams play 15.

How does seeding work in a single-elimination bracket?

The bracket is filled so that seed 1 meets the lowest seed, seed 2 the second lowest, and so on, and so that the top seeds can only meet each other late: seeds 1 and 2 are on opposite halves and cannot meet before the final. In an 8-slot bracket the first-round pairings are 1 v 8, 4 v 5, 2 v 7 and 3 v 6.

How do byes work when the number of teams is not a power of two?

The bracket is sized up to the next power of two (8 for 6 teams, 16 for 12) and the empty slots go to the lowest seeds, so the top seeds skip round one. With 6 teams the #1 and #2 seeds have a bye; with 12 teams the top four do.

How many rounds does a single-elimination bracket have?

The base-2 logarithm of the bracket size, rounded up: 3 rounds for 5 to 8 teams, 4 rounds for 9 to 16 teams. A bye does not remove a round; the team just plays its first game in round two.

When is single elimination the wrong choice?

When every team has travelled and expects more than one game, or when seeding is a guess. A round robin or a double-elimination bracket gives each team more games, at the cost of more courts and time.
