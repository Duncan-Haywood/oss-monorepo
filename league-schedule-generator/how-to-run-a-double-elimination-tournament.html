How to run a double-elimination tournament: winners bracket, losers bracket and the reset

In double elimination a team is out only after its second loss. That makes it fairer than single elimination for tournaments where teams travelled and one bad game should not end the weekend. The price is roughly twice the games.

A worked example: 6 teams, 10 games

Six invented teams are seeded 1 to 6. The bracket is sized to 8, so the #1 and #2 seeds get a first-round bye. Teams that lose in the winners bracket drop into the losers bracket; a second loss ends their run. Matches are named so you can follow who plays whom: "winner of W2M1" is whoever wins that game.

Questions

How many games is a double-elimination tournament?

With 6 teams it is 10 games, or 11 if the grand final reset is needed. In general a double-elimination field of n teams plays between 2n - 2 and 2n - 1 games, because every team except the champion loses exactly twice and the reset game is played only when the losers-bracket team wins the grand final.

What is a grand final reset?

The winners-bracket finalist has not lost yet, the losers-bracket finalist has lost once. If the losers-bracket team wins the grand final, both teams now have one loss, so a second game decides the title. If the winners-bracket team wins the first game, the tournament is over.

How do byes work when the field is not a power of two?

The bracket is sized up to the next power of two (8 for 6 teams) and the top seeds skip the first round. In the example, the #1 and #2 seeds have a first-round bye, and the losers bracket skips every match a bye leaves without an opponent.

When should I use double elimination instead of single?

Use it when one bad game should not end a team's weekend, such as a youth or rec tournament where every team travelled. It costs about twice the games of a single-elimination bracket, so check you have the courts and daylight for them.
