Merchant Feed Check vs. DataFeedWatch

Two different shapes: DataFeedWatch connects to your store, transforms your product feed with rules you build, and exports it to many advertising channels — an ongoing subscription account. This page is a free, no-account checker for one question — does a feed you already have conform to Google Merchant Center's and Meta Catalog's own published product-data specification — before it syncs.

What each tool is for

DataFeedWatch is product-feed management software, sold under an ongoing subscription account to e-commerce sellers and agencies. It connects to your store or feed source, lets you build transformation rules — title templates, category mapping, attribute enrichment, custom labels — and exports the optimized result to many advertising and marketplace channels. Setting it up means creating an account, connecting a data source, and configuring channel-specific export templates, not a one-off check. DataFeedWatch's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific plan names, product-count tiers, or pricing, so only its well-documented category and identity are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page.

This checker does none of that. It does not connect to a store, build transformation rules, or export a feed anywhere. It takes a feed you already have — CSV, TSV, or JSON, pasted or loaded from a file — and reports, item by item, exactly what would fail Google Merchant Center's or Meta Catalog's own published product-data specification: missing required fields, invalid GTIN/MPN checksums, malformed prices and availability values, and known promotional-text disapproval triggers. It runs free in any browser, and the same logic ships as a zero-dependency Node CLI you can run as a CI gate right before your feed generator syncs. No account, no upload, nothing stored.
