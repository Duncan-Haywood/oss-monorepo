What is vector index overhead?

Raw vector bytes are one number: vector count × dimension × 4 bytes for float32, plus any metadata you store alongside each vector. What a deployed, queryable index actually occupies is usually more — here is why, and how to pick a number instead of guessing.

Why a deployed index is bigger than the raw vectors

A vector database does not just store your embeddings — it builds a data structure on top of them so that a similarity search does not have to compare your query against every stored vector one by one. That structure costs space. Three common contributors, though the exact mix and magnitude is engine- and configuration-specific:

  • Graph or tree structure. HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World) is the most common approach in production vector databases today: it stores links between nearby vectors across multiple layers so a search can skip most of the dataset instead of scanning it. Those links are extra data on top of the vectors themselves, and denser graphs (more links per node, more layers) trade more storage for faster or more accurate search.
  • Quantization bookkeeping. Some engines compress vectors for storage and search (product quantization, scalar quantization, binary quantization) to save space, but may keep additional metadata or a higher-precision copy to preserve search accuracy — which can partly offset the savings depending on configuration.
  • Replication. A managed provider commonly keeps more than one copy of an index for availability. Two full replicas is roughly double the storage of one, independent of anything about the index structure itself.

None of these are fixed percentages — they depend on the specific engine, its index type, your chosen build parameters (graph connectivity, quantization mode, replica count), and your dimensionality. That is why the Vector Database Cost Calculator asks for an overhead multiplier you set, rather than asserting a single number that would be wrong for most configurations and stale the moment any vendor changed its defaults.

How to pick a multiplier instead of guessing

The reliable way is to measure it, not look it up: deploy a small or representative subset of your data on your chosen provider, read the storage figure the provider reports for that index, and divide it by the raw byte size for that same subset (vector count × dimension × 4, plus metadata). That ratio is your multiplier for that specific provider, index type, and configuration — more reliable than a number taken from documentation written for a different tuning, or from a different engine entirely. Start at 1.0 (no overhead modeled, raw vectors only) if you have no measurement yet, and revise it once you do.

This page states general, widely documented facts about how vector search indexes work. It is not a claim about any specific vendor's current implementation, defaults, or pricing.
