This calculator vs. a flat $999 mold special

“$999 mold removal” is not a price for a remediation job — it is a price for one specific 80 sq ft closet, quietly applied to every mold call that comes in.

What a flat $999 mold special is

A flat price is the fastest number a company can put on a postcard, a search ad, or a lead-gen page: one price, no inspection, easy for a homeowner to compare between bids. It works as a lead magnet, and it works for the homeowner as a round number to shop other companies against. Nothing about the number says which containment, which demo, or which extras it was built for.

The number was set for one job — commonly a small, contained area like a closet or bath, roughly 80 sq ft, one containment zone, no demolition, one HEPA and dehumidifier day, no clearance test, no HVAC cleaning, and no dumpster — and then quoted to every caller afterward, whether or not the next job looks anything like the one the special was priced on.

What this calculator does instead

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter affected square footage and containment zones, demolition square footage, HEPA and dehumidifier days, clearance testing, HVAC cleaning, a dumpster, and an occupied home as their own line items, plus your crew's loaded wage, travel and equipment, overhead, and target margin. It turns that into the price, the crew hours, and a walk-away price below which the job loses money — for this job, not the closet the special was built on.

Common questions

Why does a flat $999 mold special get a remediation job wrong?

Because the cost driver is containment zones, demo, dry-out days, and extras, not "a mold job" alone. An 80 sq ft closet with one containment zone and one equipment day needs a fraction of the labor and material that an 800 sq ft finished basement needs, with two containment zones, four dry-out days, 240 sq ft of demo, a clearance test, HVAC cleaning, a dumpster, and an occupied home -- none of which scale with a flat marketed price. A $999 special charges both the same.

What job is the $999 number actually built around?

One specific job: a small, contained area like a closet or bath, roughly 80 sq ft, one containment zone, no demolition, one HEPA and dehumidifier day, no clearance test, no HVAC cleaning, and no dumpster. It is a marketing price meant to get the phone to ring, not a number derived from any particular home's containment, demo, or extras.

How big is the pricing gap in practice?

On this calculator's own shipped presets, at its own shipped shop rates ($45/hr crew wage, 36% burden, $145 travel and equipment, 18% overhead, a 30% target margin): even the 80 sq ft closet the $999 number is named for has a walk-away cost of $1,206.55 and a bid price of $1,723.64 -- the flat special already sits $207.55 below that job's own break-even cost, before a dollar of profit, and $724.64 below the calculator's own bid price. Applied to an 800 sq ft finished basement with two containment zones, four dry-out days, 240 sq ft of demo, a clearance test, HVAC cleaning, a dumpster, and an occupied home, the same $999 special is $7,081.40 below that job's own $8,080.40 walk-away cost, and $10,544.43 below the calculator's own bid price.

Is a flat mold special ever the right way to quote?

For a narrow band of jobs close to the one the special was built around -- a small, contained area, one zone, no demo, no clearance test, no HVAC cleaning, no dumpster -- a flat number can land close, though on this calculator's own worked example even that job already runs under cost. The risk is applying it outside that band without noticing, because the postcard price does not know which band a given job is in and neither does the crew until affected area, zones, demo, and extras are actually spelled out.

Does this calculator know what to charge in my market?

No. It has no data on local labor or equipment rates. It takes the affected square footage, containment zones, demo, HEPA and dehumidifier days, clearance testing, HVAC cleaning, a dumpster, occupancy, and your own crew wage, burden, and overhead, and turns that into a price for this job -- the arithmetic a flat special skips, not a market survey.
