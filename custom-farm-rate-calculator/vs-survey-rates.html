This calculator vs. a published custom rate survey

Several state extension services and farm publications run a regional custom rate survey: they ask operators what they charged for a job last season and publish the average, often broken out by region and by task (combining, planting, spraying, baling, tillage). That average is a genuinely useful market check — it tells you roughly what a customer in your area expects to pay. It does not know your machine.

A survey figure is computed from many operators' costs, not yours. Field capacity depends on your own speed, implement width, and field efficiency. Fuel and repairs scale with the hours a job takes on your machine, at your fuel price and your own repair history. Ownership cost — depreciation, interest, insurance, housing — is fixed for the year, so a machine run hard across many customers spreads that fixed cost over more acres than the same machine used lightly, and the two operators need different rates even doing the identical job. A single regional average cannot carry that difference; this calculator is built entirely from it.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or a published custom rate survey?

Use both, for different questions. A survey (such as a state extension service's regional custom rate survey) tells you what other operators charged last season, averaged across many farms, machines, and conditions — a useful market check on whether your number is in the right neighborhood. It cannot know your own machine's field speed, width, field efficiency, fuel burn, repair history, or how many hours you actually spread its ownership cost over. This calculator answers that second question: what does this specific job cost your specific machine, this year.

Why not just charge the survey average?

A survey average can be right for you by coincidence, but it is computed from someone else's costs. A machine run hard across many customers spreads its fixed ownership cost over more acres than a machine used lightly, so the same survey figure undercharges one operator and overcharges the other. Fuel price, local repair costs, and your own field efficiency also move the real number away from a regional average in either direction.

Does this calculator replace a survey?

No. A survey is still a useful sanity check and a reference point when a customer asks how your number compares to the going rate. This calculator does not collect or aggregate anyone else's rates and is not a substitute for that market context — it only computes your own cost-based number from figures you supply.
