Common questions

Why not just read a law-firm client alert?

Client alerts are written by licensed attorneys and are usually accurate about the rule as of their publication date — that is their real strength, and this tool does not replace it. The friction is that an alert explains the rule in general while you still have to apply it to your own LLC, compute your own date, and build your own list of what to collect. The specific risk is date drift: NYDOS narrowed the reporting scope in guidance confirmed December 31, 2025, so an alert written before that change, or a search result surfacing one, can describe a broader set of LLCs as Reporting Companies than the current guidance does.

Does this tracker replace a lawyer?

No, and it is not trying to. This tool is a reference and an organizer: it applies a published rule to the facts you enter and arranges what you will need, but it produces a likely category rather than a determination of your actual filing status. It is not legal advice and does not replace an attorney licensed in New York. If your ownership structure is layered, contested, or you are unsure whether your LLC has a New York nexus at all, that is a question for counsel, not for a web page.

Is my beneficial-owner information safer here than in a shared document?

It is handled differently, and deliberately so. Beneficial-owner details are name, address, date of birth, and government ID number. This tool runs entirely in your browser, never uploads anything, ships no copy-a-link feature at all, and does not save the worksheet to your browser unless you explicitly switch that on — it is off by default, and reloading the page clears the worksheet. A document in a shared drive, by contrast, syncs to a cloud account and inherits whatever sharing that folder already has. Neither approach is a substitute for your own judgment about where this category of data belongs.
