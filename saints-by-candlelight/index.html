Saints by Candlelight: bedtime stories of saints, angels and patriarchs

A free podcast of lighthearted, faithful stories from the Catholic and Orthodox traditions, told the way you'd tell an old legend by the fire. Thirty minutes to an hour each, slowing down as they go, so you can drift off before the end and miss nothing important.

3. Nicholas and the Three Bags of Gold

A young man in fourth-century Lycia hears that his neighbour's three daughters cannot marry for want of a dowry, and on three dark nights a bag of gold comes through their window. The gentle, funny, true-hearted story of how Saint Nicholas of Myra learned to give in secret, and how the secret got out anyway.

A fifth-century abbot by the river Jordan pulls a thorn from a lion's paw, gives him a job guarding the monastery donkey, and learns something about blame when the donkey goes missing and the lion is left carrying the water.

About the show

Lighthearted, faithful bedtime stories of the saints, angels and patriarchs of the Catholic and Orthodox traditions: the Archangel Raphael on the road with Tobias, the lion of Saint Gerasimus, Saint Nicholas and the three bags of gold, and many more. Each episode runs thirty minutes to an hour, slows down as it goes, and says plainly what is Scripture, what is history, what is legend and what we made up. Written by an AI, read by a synthetic voice, free to share under CC BY-SA 4.0.

Questions

Is this a Catholic or an Orthodox podcast?

Both. It tells stories that Catholic and Orthodox Christians share: Scripture, the saints of the first thousand years, and the angels. When a saint belongs to only one tradition, the episode says so, and the stories stay off the questions the two Churches disagree about.

Are the stories true?

Each episode says plainly what comes from Scripture, what is history, what is legend handed down about the saint, and what we invented, such as small conversations or a dog with opinions. Nothing invented changes what happens in Scripture or adds a miracle to a real saint as if it were history.

Is it suitable for children?

Yes. It is gentle and lighthearted, with no gore and no frightening scenes, and it is written for grown-ups and older children to enjoy together at bedtime. Younger children may prefer the shorter stories in other saints books.

Who writes and reads it?

The scripts are written by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, following a series guide that sets out the doctrine and honesty rules. They are read by Kokoro, an open-source synthetic voice. Every episode says so at the start.

How do I listen in a podcast app?

Copy the feed address https://duncan-haywood.github.io/monorepo-projects/saints-by-candlelight/feed.xml into any podcast app that lets you add a show by RSS link, such as Apple Podcasts, Pocket Casts, Overcast or AntennaPod. You can also play every episode on this page.
