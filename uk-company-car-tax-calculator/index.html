    Common questions
    
    
    What is the company car tax (benefit in kind) rate for an electric car in 2026/27?
    
    
    Fully electric company cars are taxed on 4% of their list price in 2026/27. The rate is scheduled to rise to 5% in 2027/28, 7% in 2028/29 and 9% in 2029/30. On a £45,000 car and a 40% taxpayer that is £720 a year, or £60 a month, in 2026/27.
    

    How is company car tax worked out?
    
    
    The taxable benefit is the car's P11D list price (plus optional accessories, less up to £5,000 of capital contribution) multiplied by a percentage set by its CO2 emissions or, for plug-in hybrids, its electric range. You pay income tax on that benefit at your own rate, usually collected through your tax code.
    

    Do I pay National Insurance on a company car?
    
    
    No. Employees pay no National Insurance on a company car. Your employer pays Class 1A National Insurance, at 15%, on the benefit.
    

    Is free fuel worth it?
    
    
    Rarely. If your employer pays for private fuel, a fuel benefit of £29,200 multiplied by the same percentage is added. On a car taxed at 28% that is £8,176 of extra taxable income, so unless your private mileage is very high you will pay less by buying your own fuel. Electricity for an electric company car is not a taxable fuel benefit.
    

    What percentage applies to a petrol or diesel car?
    
    
    It depends on the car's CO2 figure and rises through a table to a 37% maximum, with a 4 point supplement for diesels that do not meet the RDE2 standard. Because published tables differ between guides, this calculator asks you to enter the percentage printed on the car's quote or P11D rather than guessing it.
