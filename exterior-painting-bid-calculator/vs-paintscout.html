This calculator vs. PaintScout

Two different shapes: PaintScout is an account-based platform for estimating, proposing, and following up on every job a painting company's sales team sells. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this exterior job cost, given its elevation, stories, and access, before you open a sales tool.

What each tool is for

PaintScout is estimating, proposal, and sales-follow-up software built specifically for painting contractors — it describes itself as dedicated solely to the needs of painting businesses, unlike general-trade estimating tools. Companies use it to standardize how every estimator on a sales team prices and presents a job from shared finish-system templates, send that estimate on as a branded proposal, automate the follow-up that turns a sent quote into a signed job, and track the pipeline across every job the company runs. It is sold on an ongoing, per-seat subscription, with a separate add-on commonly used for job and production tracking once work is sold. PaintScout's own pricing page is not reachable from this session to confirm today's exact plan names or dollar figures, so no price for it is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — painting-specific estimating and proposal software, sold as a per-seat account — are stated, and no dollar amount for PaintScout appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what PaintScout does. It does not store a customer record, brand a proposal with a company's finish-system library, or automate a follow-up sequence across a sales team. It takes one house's elevation square footage, story count, trim length, shutters, and flags for scrape, primer, an occupied home, and hard second-story access, plus crew wage, payroll burden, and target margin, and turns that into the crew hours the job actually consumes, the price per elevation square foot, the bid price, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money — plus, if you already have a number on the table, a check of whether it clears that walk-away floor, and a single printable customer proposal for that one job. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or PaintScout?

They do different jobs, and a painting company of any size will likely end up touching both eventually. PaintScout is estimating, proposal, and sales-follow-up software built specifically for painting contractors, sold on an ongoing per-seat subscription with a separate add-on for job and production tracking. It is built to standardize how a whole sales team prices and presents jobs, track those proposals through follow-up, and report on the pipeline across every estimator and every job the company runs. This calculator does one narrower thing: starting from a house's elevation square footage, story count, trim length, shutters, and flags for scrape, primer, an occupied home, and hard access, it turns that into the price per elevation square foot, the crew hours the job actually consumes, and the walk-away price below which the job loses money, before you have entered anything into a sales system. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A company that already runs its whole sales pipeline through one system and needs every estimator quoting from the same templates is better served by PaintScout or a comparable painting-specific platform; a painter who wants an instant, private gut-check on what a job should cost from its elevation and access before opening a sales tool is in the right place here.
