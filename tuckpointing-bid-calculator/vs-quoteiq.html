This calculator vs. QuoteIQ

Two different shapes: QuoteIQ is a paid, ongoing CRM for running a masonry business's quoting, scheduling, invoicing, and photo-documented inspections across every job. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this one wall cost, given its method, access, and mortar, before you quote it.

What each tool is for

QuoteIQ is a paid subscription CRM marketed to masonry and tuckpointing contractors alongside several other trades. Its publicly described feature set covers customer quoting, scheduling, invoicing, automated marketing and review requests, inspection forms that attach checklist photos to an estimate or invoice, and an AI estimator that drafts a starting line-item structure from a typed job description or a photo. It is sold as an ongoing account for the whole business, not a single-job tool. QuoteIQ's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's plan names or dollar figures, so none is invented here: only its well-documented identity and category — a paid CRM marketed in part to masonry contractors — are stated, and no dollar amount for QuoteIQ appears on this page.

This calculator does none of what QuoteIQ does. It does not market your services, hold a customer list, or keep an inspection record on a server. It takes one wall's length and height, a pointing method (smear, grind-and-point, or rebuild), an access method (ground, scaffold, or swing stage), a mortar (Type N, historic lime, or color-match), replacement brick, lintels, power wash, and control-joint caulk, and turns that into the per-job price and the walk-away price below which the job loses money — plus, if you already have a number on the table, a check of whether it clears that floor, a three-method comparison on the same wall, and a printable customer proposal for that one job. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or QuoteIQ?

They fit different points in the job, and a masonry contractor of any size may end up using both. QuoteIQ is a paid subscription CRM marketed to masonry and tuckpointing contractors among other trades: customer quoting, scheduling, invoicing, automated marketing, inspection forms with photo evidence attached to an estimate, and an AI estimator that drafts a starting line-item structure from a job description or photo, run as an ongoing account across every job the business takes. This calculator does one narrower thing: starting from wall length and height, method (smear, grind-and-point, or rebuild), access (ground, scaffold, or swing stage), mortar (Type N, historic lime, or color-match), replacement brick, lintels, power wash, and control-joint caulk, it turns that into the per-job price and the walk-away price below which the job loses money. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A masonry business already running quoting, scheduling, invoicing, and photo-documented inspections across a full crew is better served by QuoteIQ or a comparable platform; a mason who wants an instant, private gut-check on what one wall should cost before opening a business system is in the right place here.

Does this calculator tell the difference between a smear and a grind-and-point job?

Yes, and that split is usually where a flat per-square-foot special goes wrong. A point-only smear over existing Type N bags is a surface coat with no grind; grind-and-point removes the old joint first, which is a different crew day; cut-out-and-relay is a third one again. This calculator prices each method's hours separately on the same wall, along with a three-method comparison, rather than quoting all three off one blended rate.
