How much to charge to finish a basement

Basement remodelers get shopped on a published $30 a square foot finished basement Facebook special. Those numbers cannot see the bath or the egress. Eight hundred square feet of rec-room paint, drop ceiling, and carpet is a different job from 1,000 SF of legal living with a full bath, two bedrooms, two egress windows, HVAC, and a permit.

Price the scope and the extras, not the $30 dump

A basement finish is finished square feet, then how much of the shell you are actually selling. Rec-room is paint, a drop ceiling, and carpet on walls that already stand. Framed-finish is studs, insulation, and drywall. Legal-living adds habitable-space extras. Drop ceiling is a grid. Drywall ceiling is hang and finish. Carpet is the cheap special. LVP is more hours and a higher invoice.

A rec-room is not legal living

The cheap special is almost always 800 SF of paint, drop ceiling, and carpet, no bath, no egress, no HVAC. A bedroom suite is a different crew month. Matching a per-SF number on a legal-living job is how a sold basement becomes a loss when the wet work and the egress eat the margin.

This is not waterproofing, a crawl, or a shower

Basement waterproofing is an interior drain, window wells, and a sump pit. Crawl-space encapsulation is a liner and access. A walk-in shower is a wet area, a slope, and glass. Drywall as a job is hang and finish gypsum. Flooring as a job is floor waste and tear-out. Interior painting is a coat. This prices a basement finish: finished SF, scope, ceiling, floor, and extras.

Walk-away price

Load the wage with payroll burden. Add overhead as a share of direct cost. Then take margin as a share of the customer’s price, not a markup on cost. The walk-away number is total cost. A proposed price under that number loses money before you take anything out of it.

Common questions

How much should I charge to finish a basement?

Charge from finished square feet, scope, ceiling, floor, bath, and extras — not a published $30 / SF special. Eight hundred square feet of rec-room paint, drop ceiling, and carpet is a different job from 1,000 SF of legal living with a full bath, two bedrooms, two egress windows, HVAC, and a permit.

Is a rec-room finish the same bid as legal living?

No. Rec-room is paint, a drop ceiling, and carpet on walls that already stand. Framed-finish is studs, insulation, and drywall. Legal-living adds habitable-space extras. Matching a per-SF special on a bedroom-suite finish is how a sold basement becomes a loss when the wet work and the egress eat the margin.

How do a bath, egress windows, and HVAC change a basement-finish price?

A half bath is a toilet and a sink. A full bath is a shower too. Egress windows, bedroom closets, a stair rebuild, a wet bar, HVAC extend, and a permit do not live in a published per-SF dump.

No. Basement waterproofing is an interior drain, window wells, and a sump pit. Crawl-space encapsulation is a liner and access. A walk-in shower is a wet area, a slope, and glass.
