    Should I run Faire Promoted Listings?

    Brands search this when Faire offers an extra percent on billed first orders in exchange for more retailers finding the line. The listing’s Promoted listings (%) field only taxes one already-won invoice. It cannot show last-click organic or extra first orders. This page is not Open House (a seasonal 0% window) and not Faire Direct (you brought the account).

    On the default $20 × 10 first order, four organic opening orders keep $303.60 with ads off. A 15% campaign that last-clicks 40% of those organic orders and adds one extra first order keeps $301.50 (−$2.10). Ads match if the rate falls to about 14.6%, extra first orders rise to about 1.0, or last-click organic falls to about 38.3%. Two extra first orders flip the campaign to $347.40 (+$43.80). A 10% rate on the same extra order keeps $327.50. A 22% campaign that last-clicks 55% and adds two extra orders keeps $270.60.

    Common questions

    Should I run Faire Promoted Listings?

    On the calculator defaults — four organic $20 × 10 first orders, one extra first order, a 15% promoted rate, and 40% last-click attribution on organic — skipping the campaign keeps $303.60 and running it keeps $301.50 (−$2.10). Ads match if the rate falls to about 14.6%, extra first orders rise to about 1.0, or last-click attribution on organic falls to about 38.3%. Two extra first orders flip the campaign to $347.40 (+$43.80). A 10% rate on the same extra order keeps $327.50. This page does the arithmetic from the rates you enter; it does not recommend turning a campaign on.

    Is Faire Promoted Listings the same as Faire Open House?

    No. Open House is a seasonal window when Faire typically waives marketplace commission and retailers expect a deeper opening discount. Promoted Listings is an extra percent on last-click billed first orders — including organic orders the campaign claims — in exchange for extra first orders you type. Faire Direct is a third path: 0% commission when you brought the retailer. This page holds those off the table so the comparison stays skip-versus-campaign.

    When does a Faire Promoted Listings campaign still beat skipping ads?

    When extra first orders cover the last-click tax on organic. On these defaults one extra order at 15% and 40% attribution loses $2.10 against skip. The campaign matches if the rate falls to about 14.6% or last-click organic falls to about 38.3%. Two extra first orders flip ahead by $43.80. Paste the promoted rate and attributed orders from the campaign preview on your account — this page does not publish Faire's live Promoted Listings schedule.
