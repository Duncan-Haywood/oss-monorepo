This calculator vs. a spreadsheet

A lot of hosts already track payouts in a spreadsheet: one column for trip price, one for the host-share percentage, a formula that multiplies them. That arithmetic is trivial and a spreadsheet does it fine once you already know which host-share percentage to type in. What it does not do on its own is hold Turo's whole plan-by-booking-window matrix -- three earnings plans times four booking windows is twelve possible host-share cells for the same trip, and a plain spreadsheet has no built-in way to check that the cell you used actually matches the plan and window you have.

Common questions

Isn't a Turo payout just trip price times host share?

The multiplication itself is trivial and a spreadsheet does it fine once you already know the right host-share percentage to type in. What a plain spreadsheet does not do on its own is hold Turo's whole plan-by-booking-window matrix — three earnings plans times four booking windows, twelve possible host-share cells for one trip — so you have to already know which cell applies before you can even start the formula.

Why is comparing plans and booking windows harder in a spreadsheet than it looks?

Seeing what the same trip would have paid under a different plan, or if the guest had booked earlier or later, means building and maintaining eleven more formula cells alongside the one you actually need — and updating all twelve every time the trip price or extra fees change. This calculator recomputes the full plan comparison and the full booking-window comparison automatically, from the same inputs, every time you calculate.

What happens if I type a percentage into the wrong cell in my own sheet?

Nothing — a spreadsheet returns whatever the formula says, whether the host-share percentage you typed matches the plan and booking window you actually have or not, and there is no built-in check that the two agree. This calculator derives the host share from the plan and booking window you select, so the two can never disagree, and it flags findings like a missing trip price rather than silently returning zero or a stale number.

Does this replace a host's own income-tracking spreadsheet?

No. A spreadsheet or ledger that tracks every trip, every payout, and every month's total over a year is still the right tool for that ongoing record. This page answers one question at a time — what does this trip pay under my plan and booking window, and what would it have paid under the other eleven combinations — and nothing it computes is saved anywhere once you leave the page.
