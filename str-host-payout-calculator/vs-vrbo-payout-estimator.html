This calculator vs. Vrbo's own earnings estimator

Vrbo offers a free earnings estimate for people considering listing a property: type an address and a few details, and it predicts a nightly price and seasonal income on Vrbo. It is forward-looking and single-channel — a forecast for a listing that may not exist yet, with no way to add an Airbnb or direct-booking leg and no field for a stay you have already booked or priced.

That makes Vrbo's tool the right choice for "what might this property earn on Vrbo" before you list. This calculator answers a different question: for a stay you already have real numbers for, which channel actually pays the most — Airbnb, Vrbo, or direct — and what it would take to do better.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Vrbo's own earnings estimator?

Both, for different questions. Vrbo's own estimator, offered when you start listing a property on vrbo.com, predicts a nightly price and seasonal income for a property on Vrbo alone. This calculator instead takes a stay you already have numbers for — nightly rate, nights, cleaning fee, occupancy tax, deposit — and runs it through Airbnb's 15.5% host-only fee, the retired 3% split, Vrbo's pay-per-booking and legacy annual plans, and direct booking, side by side, plus the listed-price raise needed to restore an old 3% payout and the occupancy you can afford to lose going direct. Vrbo's tool cannot do any of that: it has no Airbnb or direct-booking leg and no way to enter an actual booked stay.

Does Vrbo's estimator account for Airbnb or direct booking?

No. It is single-channel by design — it forecasts what Vrbo itself would pay for a listing on Vrbo. It has no field for Airbnb's host-only fee, Airbnb's retired 3% split, or a direct-booking site's processing fee, so it cannot answer which channel actually pays best on a given stay.
