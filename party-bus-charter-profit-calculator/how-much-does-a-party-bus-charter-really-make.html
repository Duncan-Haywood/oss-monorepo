How much does a party bus charter really make?

An $850 five-hour charter sounds like $170 an hour. After the driver, the empty miles and the bus's own wear it keeps about an eighth of that.

The worked example

A customer books 4 hours and the minimum is 5, so the charter bills 5 hours at $850.00. The driver also spends 1.5 hours positioning the bus from the garage and back, so driver and vehicle are tied up for 6.5 hours. At $28 an hour plus 12% payroll cost, driver pay is $203.84. The route is 90 miles including the empty ones at 8 mpg and $3.90 a gallon, so fuel is $43.88, plus $49.50 of maintenance and tires. Detailing is $45, the gratuity share paid to the driver is 5% of the price and card fees are 3% of the price, $25.50.

The bus cost $85,000 and should run 900 charters, which is $94.44 a charter. Yearly insurance, storage and permits are $38,000 over 160 charters, $237.50 on each charter.

What the quote has to be

For a 25% margin after 5% gratuity share and 3% card fees, the costs that do not move with price ($674.16) are divided by 0.67, which gives $1006.21. At $850.00 this charter is $156.21 short.

Does the bus pay for itself?

One charter earns $202.28 above its direct costs, before the bus's own wear. The bus costs $85,000, so it needs 420.2 charters, about 4.7 years at 90 charters a year, well inside its 900-charter life.

Comparing two charters

A shorter local charter brings in $680 for 3 hours with half an hour of positioning and 45 miles. It makes $92.21 against $107.84 for the first, but $26.35 per vehicle hour against $16.59. On a busy Saturday where the vehicle is the limit, the short charter fills the calendar better.

Common questions

How do I price a party bus charter so it makes money?

Start from the hours the vehicle is really tied up, not the hours the customer books: the billed hours (at least your minimum) plus the deadhead and positioning time to and from the garage. Pay the driver for all of them, add fuel and maintenance for every mile including empty ones, cleaning, the gratuity share you pay out, card fees, the vehicle's wear per charter and a share of your yearly insurance, storage and permits. Divide the costs that do not scale with price by one minus gratuity share, card fees and your target margin to get the lowest quote.

Why does a charter that looks busy lose money?

Because the hours that cost the most are the ones nobody is billed for. A five-hour minimum with ninety miles and an hour and a half of positioning keeps the driver and the vehicle busy for six and a half hours, and a share of insurance and the vehicle payment lands on every charter whether or not the calendar is full.

How many charters does a party bus have to run to pay for itself?

Take what one charter earns above its direct costs, which is everything except the vehicle's own depreciation, and divide the purchase price by that. The calculator shows the charters, and the years at the rate you expect to run the vehicle, and warns if the answer is more than the charters you expect in its life.

Which charter should I take when I have one vehicle free?

Compare profit per charter and profit per vehicle hour. When the vehicle is the limit, the charter with the higher profit per vehicle hour fills the calendar better, even if another one makes more by itself.
