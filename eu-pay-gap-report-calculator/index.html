How this works, and what it leaves out

  • Gap = (men's average minus women's average) / men's average. Positive means women are paid less. Pay is gross hourly pay: (base + bonus) / hours, or annual pay when hours are missing.
  • Variable pay gaps compare the average bonus among those who received one; the share receiving one is shown separately.
  • 5% rule (Art. 10): a mean gap of 5% or more in any category, either direction, that is not justified by objective gender-neutral criteria or remedied within six months requires a joint pay assessment.
  • Not modelled: deciding what is work of equal value, benefits in kind, workers who are neither F nor M in the data, national thresholds and report formats, reporting deadlines in your member state, and the filing itself. Common questions

What does the EU Pay Transparency Directive require employers to report?

Directive (EU) 2023/970 requires employers with 100 or more workers to report seven indicators: the gender pay gap in mean and median hourly pay, the gap in mean and median variable pay, the share of women and men receiving variable pay, the share of women and men in each pay quartile, and the pay gap by category of workers. Employers with 150 or more report annually from 7 June 2027, covering 2026; employers with 100 to 149 report every three years, first on 7 June 2031. Your member state's transposing law sets the final details.

How is the gender pay gap calculated?

The gap is the difference between the average pay of men and women, expressed as a percentage of men's average pay: (men minus women) divided by men. A positive figure means women are paid less. The Directive compares gross hourly pay, including variable components such as bonuses.

What is the 5% pay gap trigger?

If the average pay gap between women and men in any category of workers is 5% or more, and the employer cannot justify it with objective, gender-neutral criteria or remedy it within six months, the employer must carry out a joint pay assessment with workers' representatives (Art. 10).

What counts as a category of workers?

Workers doing the same work or work of equal value, grouped using objective gender-neutral criteria such as skills, effort, responsibility and working conditions. Use your own job grades or bands as the category column; this tool does not decide equal value.

How are the pay quartiles worked out?

All workers are ranked by hourly pay and split into four equal parts, from lowest to highest paid. The tool counts the women and men in each part.
