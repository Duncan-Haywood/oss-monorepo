How much to charge to install a home elevator

Accessibility and elevator contractors get shopped on a published $18,995 pneumatic home elevator installed Facebook special. Those numbers cannot see the shaft or the pit. Two stops of through-floor vacuum tube is a different job from a three-stop hydraulic in a new wood hoistway with a pit, a 240 V feeder, auto doors, battery lowering, and a state inspection.

Price the stops and the shaft, not the $18,995 dump

A home elevator is stops and travel, then a drive, then a hoistway. Pneumatic vacuum is a through-floor tube with no shaft. Winding drum is compact cable. Hydraulic is a ram, a pit, and a machine closet. Traction MRL is taller travel without a machine room. Through-floor is two deck cuts. A new wood shaft is framed. Masonry is CMU. Glass is a showpiece.

Pneumatic versus hydraulic on the same house

Homeowners usually start from the pneumatic special, then ask whether “a real elevator” is “a little more.” On the same stops it is not a little more. Hydraulic buys a unit, a pit, and a closet. A new shaft is a carpentry or masonry ticket of its own. The calculator prices all four drives on the house you already measured so you can quote the shaft job instead of matching the pneumatic number on a hydraulic install.

Through-floor is not a new wood shaft

The cheap special is almost always two stops of pneumatic through-floor, acrylic tube, no pit, no feeder, no permit. A three-stop hydraulic in new wood is a different crew week. Matching a published pneumatic number on a shaft job is how a sold install becomes a loss when the hoistway and the extras eat the margin.

This is not a stair lift, a ramp, or a walk-in tub

A stair lift is a chair on a rail — no shaft, no pit, no cab. A wheelchair ramp is carpentry: rise-to-run and landings. A walk-in tub is alcove plumbing. A walk-in shower is tile, slope, and glass. This prices a home elevator: stops, drive, hoistway, and extras.

Walk-away price

Load the wage with payroll burden. Add overhead as a share of direct cost. Then take margin as a share of the customer’s price, not a markup on cost. The walk-away number is total cost. A proposed price under that number loses money before you take anything out of it.

Hospital discharge is not the weekday bid

Families also shop a third special: “Mom comes home Friday — same $18,995 on Saturday?” A home elevator unit ships from the factory. Next-day air is a ticket on top of the rail-sized crate a stair-lift uses. Working around someone who just came home is a slower set. Saturday is overtime plus a weekend inspector. Common questions

How much should I charge to install a home elevator?

Charge from stops, travel feet, drive, hoistway, and extras — not a published $18,995 pneumatic special. A two-stop through-floor vacuum tube is a different job from a three-stop hydraulic in a new wood shaft with a pit, a 240 V feeder, auto doors, battery lowering, and a state inspection.

Is a pneumatic vacuum elevator the same bid as a hydraulic shaft elevator?

No. Pneumatic vacuum is a through-floor tube with no shaft and no pit. Hydraulic needs a pit and a machine closet. Traction MRL is taller travel without a machine room. Matching a pneumatic special on a hydraulic-plus-shaft job is how a sold install becomes a loss when the hoistway and the extras eat the margin.

How do a new shaft, a pit, and a 240 V feeder change a home elevator price?

Through-floor is a pair of deck cuts. A new wood shaft is framed. Masonry is CMU. Glass is a showpiece. Price all five hoistways on the same house to see the upgrade. A pit, a machine closet, auto doors, battery lowering, a dedicated feeder, and a permit do not live in a published pneumatic dump.

Is this the same as a stair lift, a wheelchair ramp, or a walk-in tub?

No. A stair lift is a chair on a rail — no shaft, no pit, no cab. A wheelchair ramp is carpentry: rise-to-run and landings. A walk-in tub is alcove plumbing. This prices a home elevator: stops, drive, hoistway, and extras.
