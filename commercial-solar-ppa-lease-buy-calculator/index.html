    Questions people ask

    Should a business buy solar, sign a PPA or lease?

    It depends on tax appetite, cash and how fast your utility rate rises. Buying earns the tax credit and depreciation if you can use them and has the highest long-run return. A PPA or lease needs no cash up front and is often simpler, but the provider keeps the credit and prices it into your rate. This tool puts all three on one set of annual cash flows so you can compare net present value, payback and year-1 cash.

    What is the PPA rate at which buying stops being better?

    The break-even PPA rate is the starting $/kWh, with your escalator, at which a PPA has the same net present value as buying. If a quote is below it, the PPA is the better deal on these numbers. If it is above, buying is. It moves with the utility escalation you assume, so check the sensitivity table.

    Why does the utility escalation matter so much?

    Every kWh the system produces is a kWh you do not buy at next year's rate. A fixed-price PPA or buy locks in the cost while the avoided utility price rises, so higher escalation favors the options with less annual outflow relative to savings. The table re-runs the comparison at 0, 2, 4 and 6%.

    What happens after the PPA or lease term?

    Contracts differ: renew, buy out at fair market value, or remove the system. The tool assumes you buy it out at a percentage of the installed cost that you choose and then run it yourself. Set the buyout to match your contract.
