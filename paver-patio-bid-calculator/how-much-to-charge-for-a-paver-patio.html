How much to charge for a paver patio

The number a homeowner wants is a price per square foot of patio. That number is not a takeoff. A 12 × 16 running-bond sitting area on six inches of compacted base is not the same job as 12 × 16 of 45° herringbone on a ten-inch driveway pad. The face is identical. The hole, the waste, the cuts, and the dump tickets are not.

Price the hole, then the face

Excavation depth is base + bedding + paver thickness. On the sample rates shipped with the calculator, a 12 × 16 patio at 6 inches of base excavates about 5.6 cubic yards. The same face at 10 inches of base excavates about 7.9. If spoils cannot stay on site, that extra yardage is also a dump ticket. Quoting both jobs at the same per-foot number is how the driveway pad eats the patio margin.

Pattern waste is material; extra cuts are labor

Running bond often starts around 7% extra pavers. 90° herringbone around 10%. 45° herringbone around 15%. Circular or fan layouts around 18%. Those extra pavers do not, by themselves, add hours — the crew still walks the finished face. The extra hours come from the cut list, which is why the calculator keeps a separate pattern labor factor. Mixing the two hides which part of a high bid is stone and which part is time.

Edge restraint does not care how big the patio is

A 12 × 16 rectangle has 56 feet of edge. A 4 × 40 walkway has 88 feet of edge on the same 160 square feet. Soldier courses, interior radii, and seat-wall returns add lineal feet that a per-square-foot special never sees. Measure the restraint, or you are giving away the plastic, the spikes, and the hours that hold the field together.

Load the wage, then keep a walk-away number

A $32/h crew loaded at 30% payroll burden is $41.60. Margin is a share of the price you send, not a markup on cost. The walk-away price is total cost — below that the job makes nothing before you take a dollar home. On the sample 12 × 16 running-bond patio the calculator prices about $4,381 ($22.82 per finished square foot, 20.9 crew-hours, walk-away $3,417). The same face as 45° herringbone on a 10-inch base prices about $5,151 ($26.83 per foot). Replace every rate with your invoices before you send either number.

What this is not

This is not an engineered pavement design, a soils report, a permit, or a recommended market price. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type. Compaction spec, drainage, polymeric-sand joint width, and local code are yours to confirm.

Common questions

How much to charge for a paver patio?

Charge from finished area plus pattern waste, compacted-base cubic yards, edge restraint, haul-away, loaded labor, overhead, and a margin that is a share of price. A default 12 × 16 running-bond patio on a 6-inch base prices around $22–$23 per finished square foot on the sample rates in this calculator — the same face as 45° herringbone on a 10-inch driveway pad is several dollars more per foot.

Why is a per-square-foot paver quote dangerous?

Homeowners measure the face they walk on. Pattern waste, excavation volume, dump tickets, and edge restraint do not scale with that face. Matching a cheaper per-foot quote written for a simple running-bond patio is how a herringbone driveway pad becomes a loss.

How much extra paver should I buy for herringbone?

Start around 7% for running bond, 10% for 90° herringbone, 15% for 45° herringbone, and 18% for circular or fan layouts. Those are starting points, not a manufacturer warranty — a border course and a lot of radii move the number.
