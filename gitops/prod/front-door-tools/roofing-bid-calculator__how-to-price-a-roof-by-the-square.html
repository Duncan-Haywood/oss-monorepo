How to price a roof by the square — not the house square footage

Homeowners and new crews search for “roofing cost calculator” and get a number times the house square footage. That is the floor plan. Roofers bid squares: 100 square feet of roof surface. The two numbers are not the same job.

A 1,800 sq ft ranch on a 6/12 pitch is about 20.1 squares of surface (1,800 × 1.118 ÷ 100), not 18. Add 10% waste for cuts, starter, and ridge and you are buying closer to 22.1 squares of covering. A 12/12 on the same footprint is about 25.5 squares before waste — forty percent more material and a slower, steeper crew day.

Tear-off is a second multiplier. One layer coming off is one pass of dump and labor on the existing surface. Two layers is twice the dump and twice the tear-off hours, on the same house. A flat “price per house square foot” cannot see any of that, which is how a bid that looked fine on a low-slope one-layer ranch loses money on a cut-up two-layer 10/12.

The roofing bid calculator does the arithmetic the trade already uses: footprint × pitch factor → squares, plus waste, layers, dump, loaded wage, overhead, and the margin you mean to keep. It also reports price per square and the walk-away number so you can score a cheaper quote against the same cost base.

Pitch factor is √(1 + (rise/12)²). Labor on 7/12–9/12 starts 25% slower, 10/12–12/12 50% slower, steeper than 12/12 twice as slow. Those steps are starting points, not a safety rating or a code opinion. Replace every default with your invoice and a rate timed from your crew.

Common questions

What is a roofing square?

A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface. It is not 100 square feet of the house floor plan.

Why is house square footage the wrong unit for a reroof bid?

Pitch turns footprint into more surface. A 6/12 roof is about 12% larger than its footprint; a 12/12 roof is about 41% larger. Waste and tear-off layers add more.
