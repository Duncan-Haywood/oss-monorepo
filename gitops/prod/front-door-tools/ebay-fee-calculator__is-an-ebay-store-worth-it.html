What this checks

Sellers search “is an eBay store worth it” because $7.95 looks cheap next to a 13.25% final-value fee. Forty $40 sales do not cover it. Fifty-eight of the same sales do.

On the defaults — $40 item, $6 shipping, 13.25% + $0.30, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 40 sales, $0 current subscription — staying off a store keeps $864.20 a month. Example Starter at $7.95 / 12.95% keeps $861.77 ($2.43 less). Basic keeps $849.13. Premium keeps $815.01. Starter still ties around 58 of these sales. Raise the month to 80 and Starter keeps $3.09 more.

It does not tell you to subscribe, and it is not tax or financial advice. A percent of a completed sale is Promoted Listings Standard versus skip. A fee discount for free returns is Top Rated Plus versus staying standard. The main eBay fee calculator still shows completed-sale profit and auction vs Buy It Now.

Common questions

Is an eBay store worth it?

A store trades a monthly subscription for a lower final-value rate. On the calculator defaults ($40 item, $6 shipping, 13.25%+$0.30, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 40 sales, $0 current subscription) staying off a store keeps $864.20 a month. Example Starter at $7.95 / 12.95% keeps $861.77 — $2.43 less. Starter still ties around 58 of these sales. That is arithmetic on your inputs, not advice to subscribe.

How many sales recover a $7.95 Starter store?

On these defaults the 0.30-point rate cut saves $0.138 per $46 order. $7.95 / $0.138 is about 58 sales. At 80 of the same orders Starter keeps $3.09 more than staying off a store.

Is this the same as Promoted Listings or Top Rated Plus?

No. Promoted Listings Standard is a percent of a completed sale. Top Rated Plus trades a fee discount for free returns. This page compares a monthly subscription against the final-value rate it unlocks.
