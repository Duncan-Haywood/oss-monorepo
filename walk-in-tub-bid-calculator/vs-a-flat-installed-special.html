This calculator vs. a flat "$3,499 installed" walk-in tub special

"$3,499 installed" is not a price for a walk-in tub — it is a price for one specific alcove, one tub, and one door, quietly applied to every bathroom the postcard goes to.

What a flat "$3,499 installed" special is

A flat installed rate is the fastest way to quote: one headline number on a postcard, a Facebook ad, or a website, next to a photo of a tidy bathroom. Homeowners shop it because it is easy to compare between bids, and it is the number an owner holding a competitor's flyer — or a family arranging an install after a fall — will ask you to match on their own bathroom.

The number behind it was set for one job — usually the cheapest configuration (an alcove swap on the existing drain, an acrylic soaker, an inward-swing door, no demo, no tile, no floor work) — and then reused for every tub afterward, whether or not the next bathroom looks anything like the one the rate was set on.

What this calculator is

This page keeps no account and sends nothing to a server. You enter the tub count, alcove scope (swap, relocate, new-construction, or custom-opening), tub (acrylic soaker, cast, whirlpool, air-jet, or extra-wide), and door (inward, outward, or walk-through), along with extras such as a cast-iron demo, a tile surround, grab bars, a handheld, sistering the floor, and a permit. It prices the scope, the tub, the door, and the extras separately, and returns a walk-away price and extra-work share below which the job loses money — for this bathroom, not a generic one.

A flat installed special is not wrong because it is simple — it is wrong when it is applied outside the one job it was built for, and nothing about a postcard rate tells you when that has happened. Common questions

Why does a flat "$3,499 installed" walk-in tub special get the price wrong?

Because the labor and material scale with the alcove scope, the tub, the door, and the extras, not with "a walk-in tub" alone. An acrylic soaker into an existing alcove with an inward door is a different job from a whirlpool with a drain relocate, a tile surround, a cast-iron demo, and a sistered floor. A flat installed rate quotes both the same and is wrong on one of them every time.

What does a flat installed rate usually leave out entirely?

The relocate, the pump, the tile, and the floor. A published installed price is set on the cheapest configuration it was ever quoted against — an alcove swap on the existing drain, an acrylic soaker, an inward door, no demo, no tile, no floor work — and has nowhere to put the hours for a drain or supply relocate, a whirlpool or air-jet unit's pump and GFCI time, an outward or walk-through door, a cast-iron demo, a tile surround, sistering the floor, or a permit. A contractor pricing off the flat rate alone is short before the alcove is opened up.

How much do the alcove, tub, door, and extras actually add over the flat rate?

On this calculator's own worked examples, at a $42 crew wage, 30% burden, 15% overhead, and a 20% target margin: the cheap special itself — an acrylic soaker, an alcove swap, an inward door — walks away at $3,005.86, comfortably under its own $3,499.00 flat rate. The typical aging-in-place job — a whirlpool, the same alcove swap, an outward door, grab bars, and a handheld — walks away at $6,912.34, $3,413.34 short of that same $3,499.00. An extra-wide tub with a drain relocate, a tile surround, a cast-iron demo, and a sistered floor walks away at $11,522.08 — $8,023.08 short, more than three times the flat rate on the same one-tub job. The scope, the tub, the door, and the extras move the number far more than "one walk-in tub" alone.

Is a flat installed rate ever the right way to quote a walk-in tub?

For something close to the one job it was actually priced on — an acrylic soaker into an existing alcove on the existing drain, an inward door, no demo, no tile, no floor work — a flat rate can land close, and on this calculator's own numbers that exact job comes in under it. The risk is applying the same number to a whirlpool or air-jet unit, a drain relocate, an outward or walk-through door, a cast-iron demo, a tile surround, or a floor sister without noticing, because the flat rate does not know which job it is looking at and neither does the installer until the alcove is opened up.

Does this calculator know what to charge in my market?

No. It has no data on local rates or what competitors charge. It takes the alcove scope, the tub, the door, and the extras, plus your loaded labor and overhead, and turns them into a walk-away price and extra-work share for the job you describe — the arithmetic a flat rate skips, not a market survey.

Nothing here says or implies that quoting a flat "$3,499 installed" special is a bad-faith practice — it is a common, honest shortcut that works until the job stops matching the one the rate was set on. This page describes where that shortcut breaks down, not a fault in anyone who uses it.
