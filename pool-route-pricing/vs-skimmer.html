This calculator vs. Skimmer

Different jobs: one runs the route business day to day, the other decides what the accounts on it should be charged.

Skimmer is a well-known pool service business platform, described on getskimmer.com — optimized weekly routing and scheduling, a technician mobile app for logging chemical readings and service visits per body of water, invoicing and payment collection, and back-office reporting. It is the kind of software a pool service company runs its whole operation through, day after day, account after account.

That is a different job from the one this page does. This calculator has no account, no route map, no service log and no invoicing. It exists for the step that comes before any of that software is useful: turning your service minutes, drive minutes, labor rate, chemical cost and route overhead into a minimum monthly account price, and checking whether the price you currently charge actually clears your target margin once the drive is counted.

What this page deliberately does not tell you: what Skimmer or any other platform costs, which plan includes which feature, or how their routing and billing tools are configured. Those change, and a stale figure repeated here would mislead you. Check the platform's own site for that, and check this calculator for the price you are going to load into it.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Skimmer?

Both, for different jobs. Skimmer is pool service business software: routing and scheduling, service and chemical logs per body of water, invoicing and payments, and a technician mobile app, run through a paid account. This page is none of that. It has no account, no route map, no service log and no invoicing. It answers the one question that software asks you to already know rather than answers: what should the minimum monthly account price actually be, given your labor, drive time, chemicals, and overhead. Put those numbers in and it returns a specific price floor, the route hours behind it, and what your current price is leaving on the table if it falls short.

Where does the account price that goes into route software come from?

From you. Route and billing software stores whatever monthly price you set per account and then invoices it reliably every cycle, which is genuinely the hard part of running a route business day to day. What it cannot do is tell you whether that price actually covers your true route cost, including the drive time between stops that no customer is billed for directly. That is the gap this calculator fills, and it is why the two sit next to each other rather than competing.
