This calculator vs. working out the QSBS exclusion by hand

The arithmetic looks simple — a percentage of a gain. The part that goes wrong by hand is which rule applies to which stock, and what the cap is actually a percentage of.

Common questions

What's the single most common mistake working this out by hand?

Applying the new tiered 50/75/100% schedule to stock acquired before July 4, 2025. The tiered schedule only applies to stock acquired AFTER that date — earlier stock keeps the old all-or-nothing 5-year cliff for its entire life, no matter when it is eventually sold.

What's the second most common mistake?

Using only the fixed dollar cap ($10 million or $15 million) and forgetting the 10-times-basis alternative. The cap is the GREATER of the two — for a large gain on a small basis (common when basis is low, like an early employee's exercised options), the fixed dollar figure usually governs, but for a large basis it can be the 10x-basis figure that is actually higher.

What arithmetic mistake gets made most often?

Applying the holding-period percentage to the total gain instead of to the CAPPED eligible gain. The cap is applied first to determine eligible gain; the percentage is then applied to that capped figure, not to the full, uncapped sale gain.

What planning mistake gets made most often?

Treating the per-issuer cap as a fresh allowance for every sale. It is cumulative, per issuer, across every sale of that company's stock a taxpayer ever claims the exclusion against — a second sale from the same issuer does not get a second full cap.

Does this calculator know my complete tax situation?

No. Neither this calculator nor a by-hand worksheet built from this page verifies whether the stock actually qualifies as QSBS, tracks your cumulative prior exclusions against this issuer, or accounts for day-precision holding-period timing. Both apply the same published federal rules to the numbers you enter; verify against IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional before relying on either.
