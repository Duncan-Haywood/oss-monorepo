    Common questions
    
    
    What is the 60% tax trap?
    
    
    Between £100,000 and £125,140 of adjusted net income you lose £1 of personal allowance for every £2 earned. That extra income is taxed at 40% and also pulls £0.50 of allowance into the 40% band, so the effective marginal income tax rate is 60%, or about 62% with employee National Insurance.
    
    
    How much do I need to pay into a pension to avoid it?
    
    
    Your adjusted net income minus £100,000. A gross pension contribution of that size brings you back to exactly £100,000, restores the full £12,570 allowance and, with salary sacrifice, costs far less than its face value. Enter your pay to see the real out-of-pocket cost.
    
    
    Does it also affect childcare?
    
    
    Yes. Tax-Free Childcare and the 30 hours of free childcare are lost entirely if either parent's adjusted net income is over £100,000, so the cliff can be worth thousands. Enter the yearly value to see it included.
    
    
    Is salary sacrifice better than a personal pension contribution?
    
    
    For tax they are equivalent here, but salary sacrifice also saves 2% employee National Insurance above £50,270 (8% below it) and often employer NI too. Personal contributions need a claim through Self Assessment for the higher-rate part.
    
    
    Does this cover Scotland?
    
    
    No. It uses the England, Wales and Northern Ireland bands. Scottish rates differ and the trap sits in a different place.
