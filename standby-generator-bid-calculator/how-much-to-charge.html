How much should I charge to install a standby generator?

Homeowners search that question and come back with one number. It hides everything that actually sets the price: the size of the machine, the length of the feeder, the transfer switch, and whether the fuel line is a short natural-gas tap or a far-yard propane run. A typical 22 kW natural-gas hang beside the house is not a far-yard propane set with a 400 A automatic transfer switch — and neither is what a flat "generator install" quote is pricing.

What a flat installed price cannot see

The feeder. Conductor size and run length between the generator and the transfer switch change with distance and amperage, not with a nameplate kW figure alone.

The transfer switch. An automatic transfer switch and a manual one are different equipment costs and different labor. Switch amperage — 100 A versus 400 A — changes both again.

The pad and setback. A precast pad and a poured pad are different costs, and clearance/setback requirements can add labor a "generator hang" line item never carries.

The fuel line. A short natural-gas tap and a far-yard propane run with its own tank placement are not the same trenching and piping job.

A bid you can defend

  1. Price the feeder and voltage-drop copper for the actual distance to the transfer switch.
  2. Price the transfer switch by type (automatic or manual) and amperage.
  3. Price the pad (precast or poured) and any setback labor.
  4. Price the fuel line for the actual run — natural gas or propane.
  5. Load the wage, then add overhead, and price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin) for a walk-away number.

The calculator does that arithmetic in your browser. The recommended-kW hint is a starting point, not a NEC 220 or manufacturer load calculation you can stamp or submit — confirm sizing and code compliance with a licensed electrician.

Worked example

A 22 kW natural-gas unit set on a precast pad beside the house, with a short feeder run to a 100 A automatic transfer switch and an existing gas line nearby, is a common, comparatively quick install. A far-yard placement for the same-size unit — driven by setback or noise requirements — with a longer feeder run, a 400 A ATS for a larger service, and a new propane tank and line, is a different job entirely, often several times the labor and material cost. The calculator prices both from the same inputs and reports dollars per kW so a homeowner's single "how much" number never has to guess which job theirs is.
