This calculator vs. Jobber

Two different shapes: Jobber runs a home-service business — quoting, scheduling, client and appliance history, invoicing, payments. This page is a free, private worksheet for one question — what should this repair cost, and does finishing it still beat telling the customer to replace.

What each tool is for

Jobber is field service management software for home service businesses, and it publishes a landing page built specifically for appliance repair. It covers quoting with optional line items and automated follow-ups, a drag-and-drop scheduling calendar with route optimization, a CRM that stores customer info, appliance make and model, serial numbers, job notes, checklists and service history, automated on-my-way texts and post-job feedback surveys, invoicing with payment reminders, online card processing with same-day payouts, and a client-facing “Client Hub” where a customer can approve a quote or pay an invoice on their own. It is sold as an ongoing account for the business, and it publishes that over 400,000 home service pros trust it. Jobber's current plan names and dollar figures are not verified from this page, so none are repeated here: only its well-documented identity, feature set, and category — field service management software, sold as an ongoing account — are stated, and no price for Jobber appears anywhere on this page.

This calculator does none of what Jobber does. It does not build a quote, schedule a job, send an invoice, or keep an appliance's service history between visits. It takes one service call — diagnostic and labor hours at your loaded rate, parts at cost with your markup applied to the parts themselves before anything else, trip cost, a callback and warranty reserve charged as a percentage of that combined direct cost, overhead, and target margin — and turns that into the walk-away floor below which the job loses money, the target-margin quote, the effective hourly rate you actually earn, and a repair-versus-replace verdict measured from both sides at once: the customer's price per remaining year of service, and what you keep if they decline and you sell the replacement instead. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Jobber?

They do different jobs, and most working technicians end up wanting both. Jobber is field service management software for home service businesses, with its own appliance-repair landing page: quoting with optional line items and automated follow-ups, a drag-and-drop scheduling calendar with route optimization, a CRM that stores customer info, appliance details, serial numbers, job notes, checklists and service history, automated on-my-way texts and post-job feedback surveys, invoicing with payment reminders, online card processing, and a client-facing “Client Hub” for 24/7 quote approval and payment. It is sold as an ongoing account for running the business, and it publishes that over 400,000 home service pros trust it. This calculator does none of that. It takes one service call — diagnostic and labor hours at your loaded rate, parts at your cost with your markup applied before anything else, trip cost, a callback and warranty reserve as a percentage of that direct cost, overhead, and target margin — and turns it into the walk-away floor below which the job loses money, the target-margin quote, the effective hourly rate you actually earn, and a repair-versus-replace verdict measured two ways at once: the customer's cost per remaining year, and what you keep if they decline. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type. A shop that needs quotes sent, jobs scheduled, a customer and appliance history tracked, and invoices chased is better served by Jobber or a comparable platform; a technician who wants an instant, private check on whether one quote clears cost and margin before saying a number out loud is in the right place here.

Does Jobber price an appliance repair for me?

Jobber's own quoting tool lets you build and send an estimate with line items and a markup, and its CRM keeps the appliance's serial number and service history on file for the next visit — but the number you type into a Jobber quote still has to come from somewhere. It does not derive a price from your loaded labor rate, parts markup, trip cost, callback reserve, overhead and target margin for one specific repair; it records and sends whatever number you already decided on. This calculator is the step before that quote: it works out what a repair actually costs you once parts markup, trip cost, callback reserve and overhead compound, so the figure you type into Jobber — or say at the door — has a floor under it.
