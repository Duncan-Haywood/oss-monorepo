Greece's myDATA e-invoicing mandate: what applies now

Greek B2B invoices are cleared through the tax authority's myDATA platform, phased in from early 2026 for larger businesses and from late 2026 for the rest. Below: the dates, the model, and what a free local checker covers.

The timeline (domestic B2B)

DateWhat changes
Early 2026Businesses with turnover above EUR 1 million must issue B2B invoices through a certified provider into myDATA. Sources give 2 February or 2 March 2026, and a grace period to early May is reported.
2 Nov 2026All other businesses follow. Earlier sources say 1 October (or 12 October) 2026; secondary reports say AADE decision A.1197/2026 moved it to 2 November 2026, with a gradual period to 31 January 2027, after which invoices go through a licensed provider or AADE's applications only. The decision text itself was not checked.

The dates above conflict between published sources, so this page does not pick one. Thresholds, grace periods, exemptions and fines have been amended; none are given here. This is a summary, not tax advice; confirm with the Greek tax authority (AADE) or your accountant.

How the invoice travels

This is a clearance model. The invoice is sent to AADE's myDATA platform, normally by a certified service provider, which returns a unique MARK number that goes on the invoice. The national format is an AADE XML schema, not the EN 16931 UBL or CII used in most other EU mandates. Invoices to public bodies use Peppol.

Common questions

When does Greece's B2B e-invoicing mandate apply?

In two phases: larger businesses (turnover above EUR 1 million) from early 2026, and all other businesses from 2 November 2026 (earlier sources say 1 October, and a postponement was reported on 30 September 2026). Sources disagree on the exact days (2 February or 2 March 2026 for the first phase), so confirm with AADE or your accountant; this page is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice format does Greece require?

Greek B2B invoices are submitted to AADE's myDATA platform, which returns a unique MARK number, usually through a certified service provider. The national format is an AADE XML schema, not the EN 16931 UBL or CII used elsewhere in the EU. Peppol is used for invoices to public bodies.

Can this checker validate a Greek invoice?

Partly. It applies the EN 16931 arithmetic, tax breakdown, mandatory-field and identifier checks, plus VAT, IBAN and BIC typo catches, each with a rule ID and a fix, to UBL, CII, Factur-X and JSON files. It does not read AADE's myDATA XML or apply its rules, so a clean result is an offline observation, not approval.

How do I check an invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker. Nothing is uploaded: every check runs in your browser. Fix what it flags, then submit through your certified provider or AADE's myDATA tools.
