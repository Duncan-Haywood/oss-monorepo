Germany's e-invoice mandate: what applies when

Receiving structured e-invoices has been mandatory in Germany since 2025. Issuing them becomes mandatory in two steps, 2027 and 2028. Below: the dates, the formats that count, and how to test a file before it goes to a customer.

The timeline (domestic B2B)

DateWhat changes
1 Jan 2025Every business must be able to receive an EN 16931 e-invoice. Issuing: paper and PDF still allowed.
1 Jan 2027Businesses with prior-year turnover above 800,000 euro must issue structured e-invoices. Smaller businesses may still use other formats.
1 Jan 2028Every business must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B sales.

Exceptions exist (small-amount invoices, tickets and others) and the rules are set by the Umsatzsteuergesetz and Ministry of Finance guidance. This is a summary, not tax advice; confirm with your adviser.

Which files count

XRechnung is plain XML in UBL or CII syntax. ZUGFeRD (called Factur-X in France) is a PDF that carries the same kind of XML inside it. Either must follow the European standard EN 16931. A PDF without embedded XML does not count, and neither do the ZUGFeRD Minimum and Basic WL profiles.

Public bodies add the XRechnung rules (BR-DE): a buyer reference or Leitweg-ID (BT-10), seller contact, payment instructions and more. A portal that rejects the file gives you a code, but only after you have sent it.

Test a file before you send it

Open the checker, drop your XML or ZUGFeRD PDF, and tick Also apply the German XRechnung rules. It flags arithmetic that does not add up, missing buyer reference, bad Leitweg-ID check digits, VAT, IBAN and BIC typos, and credit notes sent as invoices, each with a rule ID and a fix (the rule reference). Common questions

Since when must German businesses accept e-invoices?

Since 1 January 2025, every German business that receives an invoice from another domestic business must be able to receive a structured e-invoice in the EN 16931 standard. Issuing is phased in: until 31 December 2026 a paper or PDF invoice is still allowed, until 31 December 2027 a business whose prior-year turnover was at most 800,000 euro (and any business, with the buyer's agreement, for other electronic formats) may still use other formats, and from 1 January 2028 every domestic B2B invoice must be a structured e-invoice. Confirm your own situation with a tax adviser; this page is a summary, not advice.

Is a PDF invoice still enough in Germany?

For issuing, only during the transition periods above. An ordinary PDF is an image of an invoice, not a structured e-invoice. A ZUGFeRD or Factur-X PDF is different: it carries a machine-readable XML invoice inside the PDF, and that XML is what counts. Only the profiles that meet EN 16931 (EN 16931, Extended) qualify, not the Minimum or Basic WL profiles.

Which formats are accepted?

XRechnung (XML, in UBL or UN/CEFACT CII syntax) and ZUGFeRD or Factur-X (a PDF/A-3 with embedded CII XML) in an EN 16931 profile. Other formats are acceptable if they can be converted to a conforming one and both sides agree. This checker reads JSON, UBL, CII and Factur-X or ZUGFeRD PDFs.

How do I check an XRechnung or ZUGFeRD invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker and tick the XRechnung option. It reports missing buyer reference (BT-10), a Leitweg-ID with wrong check digits, absent payment instructions or seller contact, arithmetic that does not add up, and bad VAT, IBAN or BIC values, each with a rule ID and a fix. For the official rule set, the optional Schematron mode runs the EN 16931 and KoSIT XRechnung rules. Nothing is uploaded when you use the page.
