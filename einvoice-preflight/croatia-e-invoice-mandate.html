Croatia's e-invoice mandate: what applies now

Since 1 January 2026, Croatian VAT-registered businesses issue and receive domestic B2B and B2G invoices as structured e-invoices, with the data reported to the Tax Administration. Below: the date, the format, and what a free local checker covers.

The timeline (domestic B2B and B2G)

DateWhat changes
1 Jan 2026Fiskalizacija 2.0 starts for VAT-registered businesses: you must issue and receive structured e-invoices, and invoice data is reported to the Tax Administration.
LaterEntities outside the VAT system follow on a later date set in the rules.

Exceptions and transition rules exist. This is a summary, not tax advice; confirm with your accountant or the Croatian Tax Administration.

Which file counts, and how it travels

The invoice is a structured document in UBL 2.1 or CII syntax that follows EN 16931 and the Croatian national profile, exchanged through a certified access point, with the data reported to the Tax Administration. A PDF emailed to the customer is no longer the invoice for domestic B2B sales.

Common questions

When did Croatia's B2B e-invoicing mandate start?

On 1 January 2026 Croatian VAT-registered businesses had to issue and receive structured e-invoices for domestic B2B and B2G transactions, with the invoice data reported to the Tax Administration under the Fiskalizacija 2.0 rules. Entities outside the VAT system have a later start. Confirm your own situation with your accountant or the Croatian Tax Administration; this page is a summary, not advice.

Which invoice format does Croatia require?

A structured invoice that conforms to the European standard EN 16931, in UBL 2.1 or CII syntax, following the Croatian national profile (CIUS-HR), exchanged through a certified access point. A PDF or paper invoice is no longer the invoice for domestic B2B sales.

Can this checker validate a Croatian invoice?

Partly. It reads UBL, CII, Factur-X and JSON and applies the EN 16931 arithmetic, tax breakdown, mandatory-field and identifier checks, plus VAT, IBAN and BIC typo catches, each with a rule ID and a fix. It does not apply the Croatian national profile rules, the Tax Administration reporting or an access point's acceptance, so a clean result is an offline observation, not approval.

How do I check an invoice before sending it?

Drop the file into the checker. Nothing is uploaded: every check runs in your browser. Fix what it flags, then send through your access point or invoicing software.
