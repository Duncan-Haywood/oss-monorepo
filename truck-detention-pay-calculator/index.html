    Common questions

    How much detention can an owner-operator charge?

    Whatever the rate confirmation says. The usual terms are two hours of free time, then $40 to $100 an hour billed in 15 or 30 minute increments, often capped at 8 to 12 hours. Check the rate confirmation: if it is silent, you are negotiating, not claiming, and the broker is not obliged to pay.

    When does the detention clock start?

    Usually at the later of your arrival time and the appointment time. Arriving early does not start it. If you arrive after the appointment, it starts when you arrive. This calculator reads each stop that way.

    What proof do brokers want for a detention claim?

    Signed or stamped in and out times from the bill of lading or a gate receipt, the rate confirmation showing the detention terms, and your request sent inside the broker's window, often 24 to 72 hours after delivery or with the proof of delivery. Photograph the times before you leave.

    Is detention pay taxable?

    Yes. It is ordinary business income, reported with the rest of your freight revenue. This tool only totals the claim; it does not compute tax.

    Is this legal or financial advice?

    No. It does arithmetic on the times and terms you enter. Whether a claim is owed depends on your contract, which this tool has not read.
