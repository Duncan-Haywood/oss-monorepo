      The rules, and what they leave out

      • YouTube's chapter rules: the list starts at 0:00, has at least three timestamps in ascending order, and every chapter lasts at least 10 seconds.
      • Times: M:SS, H:MM:SS, fractions, and HH:MM:SS:FF frame timecodes; minutes and seconds stop at 59.
      • Titles: one per chapter; very long titles are warned about.
      • Fixed automatically: times put in order, one time format throughout, exact repeats dropped, an optional 0:00 chapter added, an optional shift for a cut intro.
      • Never guessed: which title to keep when two chapters share a time, how to merge a chapter that is too short, what to call the opening seconds unless you type a title.
      • Not checked: that the video actually matches the times, that YouTube will display the chapters, automatic chapters, or title quality. Read from memory of YouTube's help page on chapters; confirm against the current page.

      Common questions

      Why won't my YouTube chapters show up?

      YouTube builds chapters only when the description's list starts at 0:00, has at least three timestamps in ascending order, and every chapter is at least 10 seconds long. This tool checks each of those and names the line that breaks the rule. It cannot see your video, so after saving, look for the chapter markers on the progress bar.

      How do I turn Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve markers into YouTube chapters?

      Export the markers as a CSV or text list, paste it here, and set the frame rate to match your sequence. HH:MM:SS:FF timecodes are converted to seconds; Audacity label tracks (seconds, tab, label) are read as well. The result is a description-ready list.

      What if I cut the start of the video after writing the chapters?

      Enter the number of seconds you removed in the shift box. Every time is moved earlier by that amount before the checks, and a chapter that would fall before 0:00 is reported and left out of the cleaned list.

      Does it write the chapters for me from the video?

      No. It does not watch or transcribe the video, so it cannot invent chapter titles. It checks and converts a list you already have.

      What are the other formats for?

      chapters.json is the Podcasting 2.0 chapters file podcast apps read, FFmetadata lets ffmpeg write chapters into an MP4, and WebVTT is a chapters track for an HTML video. The last two need the video length.

      Is my text uploaded anywhere?

      No. The page works in your browser and a test in the code asserts there are no network calls.
