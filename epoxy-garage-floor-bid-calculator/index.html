        Common questions

        Why does skipping the grind make a $3/sq ft special come back as a peel?

        Because epoxy and polyaspartic coatings bond to a mechanically profiled surface, not a smooth slab. Degrease-or-acid-etch-only prep is the fastest and cheapest prep option here, but it does not open the concrete’s surface the way a diamond grind or shot blast does. A coating applied over an un-profiled slab can look fine on install day and start peeling within a season — the callback costs far more than the grind would have.

        Why does full-broadcast flake cost more than a solid-color coat?

        It is three coats instead of two, higher waste (12% versus 8%), and slower coverage per hour (220 sq ft versus 300), on top of the flake itself. The flake has to be broadcast to refusal and then topcoated, which is a separate pass. A metallic epoxy trades some of that flake material cost for even slower coverage — 180 sq ft per hour — because the metallic pigment has to be worked while wet to get the marbled look.

        Why do cracks and pits get their own line instead of being part of the square footage?

        Because patching a crack or a pit is prep work done before the coating goes down, priced by the linear foot of crack and the square foot of pit rather than by the floor area. A slab with 40 linear feet of cracking is a patch-and-fill job with a coating on top of it, not a clean floor with a coating — the patch labor and material do not show up if the price is only per square foot of floor.

        What counts as a “starved” flake floor?

        Full-broadcast flake typically lands around 0.15–0.25 lb per square foot. Below roughly 0.12 lb per square foot the floor looks thin and patchy rather than fully covered — usually the sign of underbuying flake for the area rather than a deliberate look. The calculator flags this so the material line matches what the finished floor is supposed to look like.

        No account and no network requests — the calculator runs entirely in your browser.         What’s the difference between the bid price and the walk-away price?

        The bid price already includes your target margin on top of labor, materials, and overhead. The walk-away price is the total cost underneath it — prep, coating system, flake, crack and pit repair — the point below which the job stops being profitable at all. Typing a price you are considering shows you the margin or the loss before you send it.
