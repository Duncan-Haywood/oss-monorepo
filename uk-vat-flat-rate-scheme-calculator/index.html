    Common questions

    How does the VAT Flat Rate Scheme work?

    You still charge your customers 20% VAT, but you pay HMRC a fixed percentage of your VAT-inclusive turnover, set by your trade sector, instead of output VAT minus input VAT. You generally cannot reclaim VAT on purchases, except single capital assets costing £2,000 or more including VAT.

    What is a limited cost trader?

    A business whose VAT-inclusive spend on relevant goods is under 2% of its VAT-inclusive turnover, or under £1,000 a year. It must use a flat rate of 16.5% instead of its sector rate, which usually makes the scheme worse than standard accounting for service businesses.

    Who can join the Flat Rate Scheme?

    Businesses expecting VAT-taxable turnover of £150,000 or less excluding VAT in the next 12 months. You must leave when total income including VAT passes £230,000.

    What is the 1% first-year discount?

    In your first year of VAT registration you can take 1 percentage point off your sector flat rate. This tool does not apply it to the 16.5% limited cost trader rate; verify on GOV.UK.

    Is the Flat Rate Scheme always cheaper?

    No. If you buy a lot of standard-rated goods and services you can reclaim VAT on, standard accounting often leaves you better off, and it also gives refunds when your input VAT exceeds your output VAT.
