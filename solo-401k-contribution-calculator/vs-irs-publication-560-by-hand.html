Common questions

Why not just work through IRS Publication 560 myself?

Publication 560 is the primary source and this tool does not replace it. The friction is the arithmetic: you take net profit, work out self-employment tax on 92.35% of it, halve that tax, subtract it, and then apply 20% (the effective rate for a stated 25% plan rate) to what is left. Skipping the self-employment-tax step and applying 20% or 25% to net profit overstates the employer contribution, and an overstated contribution is the mistake that can lead to an excess-contribution excise tax.

Does this replace a tax professional?

No. It applies published 2026 IRS figures to the numbers you enter, for a sole proprietor or single-member LLC filing Schedule C with no W-2 wages from the business. It is not tax advice. An S-corp or partnership uses a different formula, and an unusual situation is a question for a qualified tax professional, not a web page.

What if I also have a 401(k) at a W-2 job?

The employee elective-deferral limit is shared across every plan you participate in, not doubled. Enter what you have already deferred at the other job and the calculator reduces your Solo 401(k) deferral room accordingly.
