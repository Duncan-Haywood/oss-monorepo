      What's recognised

      InputHandling

      The answer is exact for the numbers you type (a dynamic programme over every set and order of loads, and walk-away rates solved against the best plan without each load). It does not know market rates, real routes, traffic, weather, fuel-stop prices, detention, broker credit or the driving-time rules that apply to you.

      Common questions

      Is this a load board or a market rate tool?

      No. Every rate, cost, speed and time window in the answer is a number you typed. It does not read a load board, look up market rates or predict what a lane will pay. It does the arithmetic on your own shortlist and shows which loads to chain, which to leave and the lowest rate each is worth.

      Does it upload my loads?

      No. The browser page works on the text you paste, in your own browser, and never sends it anywhere. The CLI reads a local file or stdin and makes no network call.

      Why not just take the highest rate per mile?

      Because the empty run to the pickup, the wait for its window to open and the rest you are forced to take are paid by the same week. On the worked list the best chain beats the highest-rate-first habit by $454.36 over the window, because it takes a lower-rate Memphis to Chicago load that sits on the way home.

      What is the walk-away rate?

      For each load in the plan, the lowest rate at which it still belongs in the best plan, found exactly against the best plan without that load. Counter at or above it; below it, you are better off with the alternative.

      Does it follow hours-of-service rules?

      No. It uses a simplified drive-then-rest rule you can change or turn off (11 hours driving then a 10-hour rest by default) so the clock cost of long drives shows up in the plan. It is not a logbook and not compliance advice; the rules that apply to you are yours to follow.

      How are distances worked out?

      Straight-line miles between approximate city centres times a road factor (1.2 by default), unless you give a load's own miles. That is good to a few percent on long hauls and rougher on short ones, which is why a load's own miles can be supplied.
