Should I install a customer-supplied egress window?

Owners call with a hopper in the driveway: “I already bought the window at Menards. Can you just cut and set it for $1,800?” That sounds like free margin because the sash and well invoices left the bid. They did not leave the masonry cut, the drain, the set hours, or the chance that the hopper will not pass a legal-bedroom inspection.

Zeroing the kit is not a free cut

A contractor-supplied slider still carries the sash and well invoices. An owner-supplied kit that actually fits zeros those lines and keeps the same cut and set hours. On a typical masonry legal bedroom — vinyl slider, new-cut masonry, steel well, cover, drain, ladder, $42 wage, 30% burden, 15% overhead, 20% target margin, 14 miles — the contractor-supplied bid is $3,976.62. Zeroing a $520 sash and a $480 well drops it to $2,539.12, but the owner-supplied walk-away stays at $2,031.30. Taking the Facebook special to “just cut and set what they bought” for $1,800 still loses $231.30.

Wrong size is a second trip

Store shelves default to a vinyl hopper and a 36-inch plastic well. Plenty of legal bedrooms need a slider or casement for the 5.7 sq ft clear opening, and a well that actually projects. Setting the wrong kit means an exchange or a legal replacement sash. Price that callback on the same opening before you agree. The cheap retrofit special is a different job

A hopper into an existing hole with a plastic well can still hold a margin at $1,800, even when you supply the kit. That is the Facebook special. Matching that number on a masonry new-cut — the job that actually makes a basement a legal bedroom — is how a sold opening becomes a loss when the saw and the well eat the margin. The calculator prices both openings on the same wage so you can show the owner the difference.

Common questions

Should I install an egress window the customer bought at a store?

Only after you price the cut without the sash and well invoices. Pulling their Menards kit is still a trip, a saw, and crew hours. A $1,800 install-only special often sits under that walk-away number even when the kit is free to you.

Does a customer-supplied egress window make the $1,800 installed special profitable?

Usually not on a legal-bedroom new cut. Zeroing a $520 slider and a $480 steel well drops the bid, but loaded labor, the masonry cut, the drain, travel, and overhead stay. On a typical masonry new-cut the owner-supplied walk-away is still above $1,800. A hopper that will not pass adds a second trip and a legal replacement sash.

What if the store-bought hopper or well will not pass inspection?

That is a second dispatch plus a legal replacement sash or a deeper well. Store hoppers often miss the 5.7 sq ft clear opening, and a 36-inch plastic well often misses the projection. Price that callback on the same opening before you agree to set whatever they rolled into the driveway.
