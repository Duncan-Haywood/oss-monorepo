    Per diem or receipts: which meal deduction is bigger for an owner-operator?

    Enter the days you were away from home, and the page works out the per diem deduction (full days plus travel days at 75%), applies the 80% DOT hours-of-service limit, compares it with the actual meal receipts you enter, shows the larger figure, and estimates the self-employment tax (15.3% × 92.35%) and income tax it saves.

    What this does and does not say

    • Per diem is days away times the M&IE rate, with travel days at 75%, then the 80% limit. Keep a log of dates and places; a per diem still needs records of when you were away.
    • Receipts are the actual meal costs, also limited to 80%, and you must be able to document them. The page shows the larger figure; whether you may use either method for your situation is a question for a tax professional.
    • Not checked: your eligibility, tax home, state rules, whether the rates here are current, and any 2025 or later legislation. W-2 company drivers: verify; rules changed under 2025 legislation.

    Common questions

    Can an owner-operator deduct per diem?

    A self-employed truck driver who is away from home overnight for work can generally deduct meals and incidental expenses, either with actual receipts or a per diem rate, subject to the 80% DOT hours-of-service limit. Verify the current rules and rates with the IRS and a tax professional.

    Can a W-2 company driver deduct per diem?

    Generally not as an unreimbursed employee expense, but rules changed under 2025 legislation: verify with the IRS and a tax professional before relying on any answer.
