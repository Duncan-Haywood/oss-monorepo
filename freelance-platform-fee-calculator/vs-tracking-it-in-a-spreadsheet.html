Upwork vs. Fiverr Fee Calculator vs. tracking it in a spreadsheet

A freelancer weighing the same job on both platforms usually ends up with a spreadsheet: a cell for Upwork's quoted fee, one for Fiverr's flat 20%, a withdrawal-fee line, and a tax-reserve percentage. Each formula is ordinary arithmetic. Keeping them right — especially remembering that Upwork retired its old 20%/10%/5% lifetime-billing tiers on 1 May 2025 in favor of a variable 0–15% fee quoted per contract — is the part that gets skipped when an older sheet or a generic fee-comparison page is reused for a new job.

What each approach actually does

A spreadsheet can reproduce every formula this calculator runs — each platform's cut, the flat withdrawal fee, the tax reserve, and the backward solve for a job price that hits a take-home goal. Building it means taking Upwork's fee as a manual per-contract input rather than a retired tiered formula, applying the withdrawal fee before the tax reserve in the same order on both sides, and getting the backward-solve algebra right rather than guessing a price and checking it.

This calculator keeps the job price, both platforms' net payouts, and the target take-home solve on one screen, already wired to the comparison tool. A shareable link carries the full scenario in the URL, and the Upwork fee explainer covers what changed in the May 2025 pricing update.

Worked example

A $1,200 job, Upwork's quoted fee at 8% for this client relationship, Fiverr's flat 20%, no withdrawal fee on Upwork, a $3 Fiverr withdrawal, and a 25% tax reserve on both:

UpworkFiverr
Platform fee$96.00$240.00
After withdrawal fee$1,104.00$957.00
Tax reserve (25%)$276.00$239.25
Take-home$828.00 (69.0%)$717.75 (59.8%)

Upwork nets $110.25 more on this job — a gap that only shows up because both cuts are run through the same withdrawal-fee-then-tax-reserve order, the step a rebuilt spreadsheet formula most often gets backwards or drops.

Same freelancer, a smaller $500 job at Upwork's 15% quoted fee, wanting $400 take-home after the same 25% tax reserve: the calculator's backward solve says $627.45 is the price to charge on Upwork to hit that goal, a figure a guess-and-check spreadsheet cell usually takes several tries to land on.

Common questions

Can a spreadsheet do this math instead?

Yes. The platform fee, the flat withdrawal fee, the tax reserve, and the backward solve for a target take-home are all ordinary arithmetic you can put in a spreadsheet. Getting there means encoding Upwork's fee as a manual input rather than a stale tiered formula, applying the withdrawal fee before the tax reserve on both platforms in the same order, and rebuilding the backward-solve formula correctly rather than just guessing a price and checking it.

What does a spreadsheet not do that this calculator does?

Keep the job price, both platforms' cuts, and the target take-home solve on one screen without re-deriving the formulas each time a rate changes. The easiest mistake to make by hand is reusing Upwork's old 20%/10%/5% lifetime-billing tiers, retired 1 May 2025 — a formula copied from an older sheet or a generic fee-comparison page will still use them. This calculator takes Upwork's fee as your own quoted figure every time, applies the withdrawal fee and tax reserve in the same order on both platforms, and produces a shareable link that carries the whole scenario instead of a file to keep in sync between two people.

Is a spreadsheet ever the better choice?

Yes, when your situation has a wrinkle this calculator does not model — a multi-milestone contract with a different quoted Upwork fee per milestone, a currency conversion on top of the platform cut, or a fee structure from a plan or region this tool's inputs cannot represent. A spreadsheet you build yourself can represent any rule you can describe. This calculator trades that flexibility for a form that already has the right fields for pricing or evaluating one job on Upwork or Fiverr.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. A shareable link carries your numbers in the URL itself rather than sending them to a server, and the optional usage counter records only which features were used and the day, never your job price, fees, or tax reserve.
