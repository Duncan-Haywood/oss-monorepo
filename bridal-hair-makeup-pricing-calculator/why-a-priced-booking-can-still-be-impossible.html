Why a booking that prices fine can still be impossible to finish

A rate card answers one question: does this booking clear your revenue minimum? It never answers the other one: can one artist actually finish everyone before the ceremony? Those are independent checks, and a group can pass the first easily while failing the second completely.

A worked example

A bride plus six bridesmaids, all taking the full hair-and-makeup service, at this calculator's own default rates and timings. One artist is booked, with a 4-hour (240-minute) window from arrival to the must-be-ready deadline.

CheckResult
Headcount7 (1 bride + 6 bridesmaids)
Service revenue (before minimums, travel, early start)$1,125.00
Service minimum$500.00 at 3+ people -- cleared with room to spare
Total minutes needed (services + buffers)520 minutes
Minutes available (1 artist × 240-minute window)240 minutes
Minutes short280 minutes
Additional artists needed2 -- three artists total, not one

The revenue check passes by a wide margin -- $1,125 against a $500 minimum, more than double. The schedule check fails just as clearly: 520 minutes of work does not fit in one artist's 240-minute window, no matter what the booking is priced at. This calculator prices the two extra artists into the booking automatically once it detects the shortfall -- $300.00 charged to the client for two assistants, $250.00 paid out to them, a $50.00 markup on the arrangement -- rather than leaving the artist to discover mid-morning that the group cannot be finished on time.

Every figure above comes from this calculator's own default rates and timings, run through the shipped scheduling arithmetic -- nothing here is rounded by hand.

Common questions

How can a booking be priced fine and still be impossible to finish?

Because price and schedule are two separate checks, and a rate card only ever runs the first one. Revenue comes from headcount times each person's price; feasibility comes from the total minutes every service takes, plus a buffer per person, against the minutes actually available before the must-be-ready deadline. A group large enough to clear your revenue minimum easily can still need more total minutes than one artist has hours in the morning.

What actually determines whether one artist can finish a group?

Three numbers: how many minutes each service takes (a full hair-and-makeup look is slower than hair alone), a buffer added after every person for setup and product changes, and the prep window -- the minutes from arrival to the deadline. Multiply minutes-per-service by headcount, add the buffers, and compare the total to the prep window. If the total is higher, one artist cannot finish everyone in time, regardless of how well the booking is priced.

What is the fix when the schedule does not fit?

A second (or third) artist working in parallel, which adds prep-window minutes to the booking's total capacity. That artist is a real cost -- a payout to whoever is booked -- and normally a real line item charged to the client, not something absorbed into the original quote. The gap between what the client is charged for the extra artist and what that artist is paid is the only part of this that is actually a pricing decision; everything upstream of it is a scheduling constraint, not a price.

Does a bigger rate card price prevent this?

No. Raising a service's price changes revenue, not the minutes that service takes to perform. A booking can be priced at any level and still take more total minutes than the prep window allows -- pricing and scheduling are independent, and a rate card that only tracks price has nothing that would catch the second one.
