This calculator vs. a flat day rate

A flat day rate — one lump sum for the whole bridal party's morning — is the fastest way to quote a wedding, and for a party that is nearly always the same size it can work fine. It stops working the moment the group's size, mix of services, start time, or trial-fee policy varies from booking to booking, because none of that is tracked separately in a single number.

What each one is for

A flat day rate is quick to quote and simple for a client to hear. What it does not do is separate the price into anything an artist can check against a specific booking — it does not scale automatically with headcount, it has no early-start or travel logic built in, and it cannot tell you whether the group fits inside the time you have before the ceremony.

This calculator prices the bride, the bridal party, hair-only, makeup-only, and kids' services separately, applies an early-start fee and travel, tops a small booking up to your service minimum, and runs the one check a lump sum cannot: whether the artists booked can actually finish everyone on time, and if not, exactly how many more artists the schedule needs.

Common questions

What is a flat day rate, and why do artists use it?

One lump sum for the whole bridal party's morning, regardless of exactly who gets what service. It is fast to quote and easy for a client to understand, which is why many artists start with it — especially for a bridal party that is usually about the same size.

What does a flat rate hide as the group gets bigger or smaller?

The price stays the same while the work does not. A flat rate sized for four bridesmaids under-prices an eight-person party and over-prices a two-person one, and it says nothing about whether one artist can actually finish either group before the ceremony — that is a scheduling question a lump sum cannot answer.

Can a flat rate tell me if I need a second artist?

No. A flat rate is a price, not a schedule. Whether one artist can finish a given group depends on the minutes each service takes, the buffer between clients, and the window from arrival to the must-be-ready deadline — none of which a lump sum quote tracks. A booking can be priced generously and still be physically impossible for one person to finish on time.

Does a flat rate handle a credited trial fee correctly?

Only if the artist works out the interaction by hand every time. Crediting a trial fee toward a flat day-rate booking is still a real discount on the combined total, and a flat quote has no built-in way to show that — it is one more thing tracked on the side rather than computed.
