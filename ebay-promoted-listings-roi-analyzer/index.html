    The model, and what it leaves out

    • Profit before ads = sales × your margin. Profit after fees = that minus ad fees, crediting the ad with every attributed sale.
    • Must be caused = ad fees ÷ profit before ads. It is the smallest share of the credited sales the ad must have really brought in. Above 100% the listing loses money even if the ad caused every sale.
    • Verdicts. loses: above 100%. marginal: above 60%. fails your assumption: positive at 0% sold-anyway but negative at the share you entered. pays: otherwise.
    • One margin for every listing. Real margins differ by item, so a verdict near a threshold is a prompt to check that item's own cost, not a finding.
    • Not modelled: returns and refunds after the report period, ad fees on cancelled orders, eBay's listing-level suggested rates, and any effect an ad has on a listing's organic ranking.

    Common questions

    Where do I get the Promoted Listings report?

    In Seller Hub go to Marketing, then Reports, and download the Promoted Listings report at listing level for a date range. Drop the CSV here or paste its text. Column names vary a little between campaign types, so the tool matches common spellings and tells you if it cannot find a Sales or Ad fees column.

    Why does it ask for my profit margin?

    eBay's report shows sales and ad fees but not what the item cost you. Profit before ads is your margin times sales, so every verdict depends on that one number. Use profit as a share of the sale price after item cost, eBay's fees and shipping you pay. If you do not know it, try 15, 25 and 35 and see which listings change verdict.

    What does 'must have been caused by the ad' mean?

    eBay credits an ad with a sale when the buyer clicked the promoted listing before buying. Some of those buyers would have bought anyway. The tool shows, per listing, the share of attributed sales that must really come from the ad for the fee to pay for itself. Above 100% means it loses money even if the ad caused every sale.

    Does it tell me what ad rate to set?

    It shows the largest ad rate your margin can carry and which listings are above it. It cannot predict how a lower rate changes impressions, because that depends on competitors, so treat it as where to look and test one change at a time.
