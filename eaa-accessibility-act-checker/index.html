    What it does, and what it leaves to you
    
    
      
      
    • Scope: products in Article 2(1) (consumer computers and operating systems, payment terminals, ATMs and ticketing and check-in machines, interactive information terminals, smartphones and similar terminals, smart TV terminals, e-readers) and services in Article 2(2) (electronic communications, audiovisual media access, passenger transport, consumer banking, e-books, e-commerce).
      • 
      
    • Microenterprise: fewer than 10 people and turnover or balance sheet of EUR 2 million or less. Article 4(5) exempts microenterprises providing services. It does not name makers, importers or distributors of products.
      • 
      
    • Not decided: how your Member State transposed the Directive, the exact reach of each Article 2 entry for your product, disproportionate-burden assessments, partner-enterprise proportional size rules, which harmonised standard is currently cited, and penalties.

    Common questions
    
    
    Does the European Accessibility Act apply to my business?
    
    
    It applies to certain products (such as e-readers, smartphones, payment terminals and ATMs) and services (such as e-commerce, consumer banking, e-books, passenger transport and audiovisual media access) that are placed on the EU market or offered to consumers there, since 28 June 2025. Microenterprises providing services are exempt. The page applies those rules to what you sell and your size.
    
    
    Is a small online shop covered by the Accessibility Act?
    
    
    Yes if it sells to consumers in the EU and has 10 or more people, or turnover and balance sheet both above EUR 2 million. An online shop with fewer than 10 people and turnover or balance sheet of EUR 2 million or less is a microenterprise service provider and is exempt under Article 4(5).
    
    
    Does the European Accessibility Act apply to businesses outside the EU?
    
    
    Yes where they place products on the EU market or offer covered services to consumers in the EU. A non-EU maker of a covered product needs its importer to carry the manufacturer's checks.
    
    
    What do I have to do if I am covered?
    
    
    Service providers design the service to the Annex I requirements and publish accessibility information. Manufacturers also issue a declaration of conformity and the CE marking. Web content is usually tested against WCAG 2.1 level AA through the EN 301 549 standard.
