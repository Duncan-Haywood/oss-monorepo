    Common questions

    What is the substantial presence test?

    You are a US resident for tax purposes if you were in the US at least 31 days this year and the weighted total is at least 183: all your days this year, plus one third of your days last year, plus one sixth of your days the year before. Any part of a day counts as a day. On the sample dates 91 days this year, 120 last year and 120 the year before weigh 151.0, so 31 more days stay under the test.

    Why can 120 days a year still make me a US resident?

    Because earlier years count. 120 days each year weighs 120 + 40 + 20 = 180, just under 183; 123 days in the third year reaches 183. The calculator shows the most days you can spend this year and still stay under, given the two years before.

    Which days do not count?

    Days as an exempt individual (for example F-1 or J-1 students, teachers and trainees within the allowed years), days you could not leave because of a medical condition that began here, crew members, Canada or Mexico commuters, and transit stops under 24 hours. Mark such a stay exempt and it is left out of the count.
