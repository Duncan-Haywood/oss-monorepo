    Common questions
    
    
    What are payments on account?
    
    
    Advance instalments towards next year's Self Assessment bill. Each is half of your last bill (income tax plus Class 4 NIC, after tax deducted at source), due on 31 January and 31 July.
    
    
    Why is my first Self Assessment bill so big?
    
    
    The 31 January bill carries two things: the balance for the year just ended and the first advance instalment for the next year. In the first year you have no instalments to set against the balance, so the total is about 150% of a normal year's tax. It is not extra tax; it is next year's tax paid early.
    
    
    Who does not have to make payments on account?
    
    
    Nobody pays them if last year's bill after tax deducted at source was under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your income tax and Class 4 NIC was collected at source, for example through PAYE.
    
    
    Can I reduce my payments on account?
    
    
    Yes, if you expect a lower bill for the new year you can claim to reduce them through your HMRC account or form SA303. It only changes timing: if you reduce too far, interest runs from the original due dates on the shortfall.
    
    
    What happens if I miss a payment?
    
    
    Interest runs from the due date and further penalties apply if the tax stays unpaid. Check the current rates on GOV.UK.
