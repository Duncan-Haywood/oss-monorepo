Plumber Flat-Rate Price Calculator · comparison

This calculator vs. time and materials

Short answer: time and materials bills the hours that happen; a flat rate bills the hours you promised. The calculator prices the flat rate from your true cost and shows the clock bill next to it, so you can see how far a job can run before the flat price stops paying.

What time and materials is

You bill an hourly rate for the time worked, usually with travel, plus parts at cost and a markup. The customer does not know the total until the work is done, and you are paid for a slow job and a fast one in proportion to the clock. It is the simplest thing to explain on a call and the hardest for a customer to compare with another quote.

What a flat rate is

A price agreed before the work, from a price book: the same job, the same number. It moves the risk of a long job from the customer to you, and the reward of a fast job from the customer to you. Customers can compare it; you must know your cost to set it.

The same job both ways

The default job is 2 hours on site, 0.75 hours of travel and $120.00 of parts, at a true cost of $100.00 an hour. The flat price is $595.00. On the clock at $135.00 an hour the bill is 2.75 x $135.00 + $198.00 of parts = $569.25. The two bills match at 2.94 hours.

Which to use

  • Flat rate suits repeatable jobs you have timed: toilets, faucets, water heater swaps, drain clears.
  • Time and materials suits discovery work where nobody knows what is behind the wall; many shops quote a diagnostic flat and the repair after.
  • Whichever you use, the clock rate should clear your true cost: at $90.00 an hour against a $100.00 cost every hour loses money, and the calculator says so.

Check your local licensing and written-estimate rules for how a price must be quoted.

Common questions

Is flat-rate pricing better than time and materials for a plumber?

Neither is better on its own; the difference is who carries the hours. On the default numbers the flat price is $595.00 and time and materials at $135.00 an hour bills $569.25 at the planned 2.75 hours, so the two bills match at 2.94 hours.

What does a flat-rate price cost me when a job runs long?

At 4.125 hours, 50 percent over plan, the flat price of $595.00 leaves $62.50 against $222.38 on the clock. At 5.5 hours it loses $75.00 while time and materials still earns $270.50.

When does flat rate pay better?

When you finish faster than the book time. At 60 percent of the planned hours the flat price leaves $310.00 against $135.75 on the clock, and the customer pays more than the clock would have charged.
