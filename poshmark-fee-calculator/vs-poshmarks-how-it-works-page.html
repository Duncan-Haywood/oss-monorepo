This calculator vs. Poshmark's own How It Works page

Poshmark states its current seller rate schedule on its own How It Works page and Help Center: a flat $2.95 fee on sales under $15, and 20% of the sale price on sales of $15 or more, with a prepaid USPS shipping label, payment processing, and seller protection already folded into that one number — no separate listing fee, no subscription, no line-item processing charge. Poshmark's own worked example is a $50 sale paying a $10.00 fee, leaving the seller $40.00. That schedule is free to check any time and is the right place to confirm what the current rate actually is.

It is worth checking, because it has moved once already. On October 3, 2024, Poshmark replaced this schedule with a 5.99%-of-order-total fee plus a flat $1–$3 tier, and added a matching Buyer Protection Fee charged to buyers. Sellers reported lower sales volume as buyers adjusted to the higher checkout total, and Poshmark reversed the change on October 24, 2024, restoring the original 20%/$2.95 split this calculator uses. Older seller guides and blog posts written during or just after that three-week window can still cite the reverted numbers — a real source of the exact kind of stale-fee confusion a page like this exists to clear up. Poshmark's own How It Works page reflects whichever schedule is current; this calculator is only as current as that page, so re-check it if a sale's numbers ever look off.

What Poshmark's own resources do not do is run the arithmetic on a decision still in front of a seller: whether $2.95 flat is actually cheaper than 20% would have been at this price, what a sale nets after the seller's own item cost and packaging, what price to set for a target profit, and whether accepting a lower offer or bundle price costs more of Poshmark's cut or more of the seller's own payout. This calculator does not try to store Poshmark's rate schedule as a lookup table that could drift out of date — it applies the two published tiers directly to whatever numbers are typed in, so it stays correct as long as the published schedule itself does.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Poshmark's own How It Works page?

Both, for different jobs. Poshmark's own How It Works page and Help Center state the current rate schedule: a flat $2.95 fee under $15, 20% of the sale price at $15 or more, with a prepaid shipping label and payment processing already included. That is the right place to confirm what the rate currently is. It does not compute the $14.75 break-even price where the flat fee equals 20%, does not show your profit after your own item and prep cost, does not solve for the price that hits a target profit, and does not check whether accepting a lower offer or bundle price actually costs more of Poshmark's cut or more of your own payout. This calculator does not try to reproduce Poshmark's rate schedule as a stored table — it applies the published tiers directly to the numbers you type, so it stays correct as long as the schedule itself does.

Does Poshmark have its own interactive fee calculator?

No. Poshmark's own materials state the schedule in prose and worked examples (a $50 item pays $10.00 and the seller keeps $40.00), but publish no what-if tool of their own for a different sale price, a different cost basis, or a lower offer.

Why does the fee schedule need double-checking at all?

Because it changed once already, recently. On October 3, 2024, Poshmark replaced the 20%/$2.95 split with a 5.99%-of-order-total-plus-$1/$2/$3 tiered fee and a matching buyer-side Buyer Protection Fee, then reversed course three weeks later on October 24, 2024 after seller backlash over reduced sales volume, restoring the original 20%/$2.95 schedule this calculator uses. Search results and older seller guides published during or shortly after that window can still cite the reverted numbers, which is exactly the kind of drift worth checking against Poshmark's own current page rather than a cached blog post.
