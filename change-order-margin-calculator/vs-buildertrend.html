This calculator vs. Buildertrend

Two different shapes: Buildertrend is an account-based platform for running a whole construction project. This page is a free, private, no-account worksheet for one number — whether a single change order's proposed price actually clears your cost floor and target margin.

What each tool is for

Buildertrend is a construction project-management platform founded in 2006 by Dan Houghton and Jeff and Steve Dugger, headquartered in Omaha, Nebraska. It is built around running a job end to end — scheduling, budgets, purchase orders, client communication, daily logs, and change orders, all tied to one project record that a builder, remodeler, or specialty contractor and their client both log into over the life of the job. It is an account-based platform: you sign up and use it as an ongoing service across projects, not a one-off calculator. Buildertrend's own site is not reachable from this session to confirm today's specific feature list, plan names, or pricing, so only its well-documented identity and category are stated here: nothing about it is invented, and no dollar figure for it appears on this page.

This calculator does none of that. It does not schedule a job, track a budget across trades, or manage a client portal. It takes one change order's added labor hours, loaded wage, materials, subcontractor cost, schedule delay, overhead, target margin, and contract markup cap, and turns that into a walk-away cost floor, a target price, and — if you have one — a check of whether your proposed price clears both, plus a plain-text approval summary. It runs free in any browser, keeps no account, and uploads nothing you type.

Common questions

Should I use this calculator or Buildertrend?

They do different jobs. Buildertrend is a construction project-management platform, founded in 2006 and headquartered in Omaha, Nebraska, used by home builders, remodelers, and specialty contractors to run a job end to end: scheduling, budgets, purchase orders, client communication, daily logs, and change orders, all tied to one project record across the life of the job. It is an account-based platform you sign up for and use on an ongoing basis. This calculator does none of that. It takes one change order's added labor, materials, subcontractor cost, schedule delay, overhead, and target margin, and turns that into a walk-away cost floor, a target price, and a check against your contract's markup cap, instantly, in the browser, with no account and nothing uploaded. A contractor who needs to run an entire job's schedule, budget, and client portal in one place is better served by Buildertrend or a comparable platform; a contractor who wants an instant, private read on whether one change order's proposed price actually clears cost and margin, plus an approval summary to send, is in the right place here.
