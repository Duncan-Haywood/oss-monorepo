    Questions people ask

    Who qualifies as a real estate professional for tax purposes?

    You must spend more than 750 hours a year in real property trades or businesses in which you materially participate, and those hours must be more than half of all the hours you work in any business or job. A full-time W-2 job almost always rules this out for you. On a joint return only one spouse has to meet both tests, but the 750 hours cannot be added across spouses.

    Does passing the 750-hour test make my rental loss deductible?

    Not by itself. Each rental activity also needs material participation, for example 500 or more hours on it, unless you elected to group all your rentals as one activity. If you pass the hours test but not material participation, the loss is still passive. The calculator shows which gate you are failing.

    What happens to the loss if I do not qualify?

    It is passive. Active participants can use up to $25,000 a year against other income, phased out between $100,000 and $150,000 of income. The rest is suspended and carried forward, then released against other income when you sell the property in a fully taxable sale. Status therefore changes when you get the deduction, and the multi-year table prices that timing.

    Is the short-term rental route different?

    Yes. A rental with an average stay of seven days or less is not treated as a rental activity, so you only need to materially participate in it. That is a different test and is not modelled here.

    Is this tax advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type in. Hours logs, grouping elections, the material participation tests, at-risk and basis limits and state rules decide your actual result, so confirm with a tax professional. Defaults are placeholders.
