          Common questions

          Why is labor almost three times the material here?

          Because stucco repair is skilled hand work on cheap materials. The default job is $381.22 of labor over 7.6 hours against $143 of material — mostly $44 of mix and $24 of caulk. It is the reverse of most trades on this site, and it means your bid is decided by how accurately you estimated hours, not by what you paid at the supply house. Under-estimating access or cure time hurts here in a way an equipment-heavy job would absorb.

          Why is “price per repair square foot” blank?

          Because the default job is priced by crack length, not area: 40 linear feet of cracking and 20 of caulk, with no delamination or tear-off square footage entered. The per-square-foot figure only appears once you enter repair area. The crack figure it does show, $24.94 per linear foot, is a division of the finished price and falls as the run lengthens — the site time and travel are the same whether you chase 40 feet or 100.

          Why is there a $75 permit on a crack repair?

          Because it is an editable default, not a claim that your job needs one. Cosmetic patching rarely requires a permit; re-lathing a wall or anything touching the weather-resistive barrier often does, and in some jurisdictions any exterior work over a threshold value does. Set it to zero when it does not apply — it is a straight pass-through with no hours attached.

          Should I quote before investigating moisture?

          Usually not, and the option is there for that reason. Cracking and delamination are frequently symptoms of water getting behind the stucco through failed flashing or window details. Patching over that hides the problem for one season and puts your name on it. The moisture-investigation option prices a look first; if you skip it, say in the proposal that the price covers the visible defect only and that concealed damage is a change order.
