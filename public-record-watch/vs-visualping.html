This tool vs. Visualping

Visualping visits a URL for you on a schedule and reports what changed on the page. This tool never visits anything — you supply two dated, authorized register exports yourself, and it reports what changed between the records in them, source-linked back to the register.

What each tool is for

Visualping is a general-purpose website change monitor. Point it at a URL and a schedule, and it fetches the page for you, keeps a screenshot history, and produces a visual diff and plain-language summary of what moved, with alerts when it does. That is exactly the right tool for a public page a scheduled visitor can reach and render on its own — a pricing page, a job listing, a status page.

Many licence, permit, and registration registers are not that page. They sit behind a search form, a session, a CAPTCHA, or an "export" button rather than a single stable URL a crawler can revisit, and a scheduled visitor cannot fill those in for you. This tool assumes exactly that case: you export the register however your own access actually works, keep each export as a dated, authorized snapshot, and this tool compares two of them — reporting which record ids were added, which were removed, and which fields changed on records that stayed, each one still linked to the source URL so a reviewer can open the original entry. It runs free in your browser or as a local command-line tool, keeps no account, and makes no network request of its own; there is nothing here that visits a register on your behalf, so there is nothing to configure, pause, or explain to whoever administers that register's access.

Common questions

Should I use this tool or Visualping to track a public register?

It depends on how the register can be reached. Visualping is a general-purpose website change monitor: point it at a URL and a schedule, and it visits the page for you, takes a screenshot, and produces an AI summary and visual diff of what moved, with alerts. That is the right tool for a public page a scheduled visitor can reach and render cleanly on its own. Many licence, permit, and registration registers are not that page: they sit behind a search form, a session, a CAPTCHA, or an export button, and a scheduled visitor cannot fill those in for you. This tool assumes exactly that case. You export the register yourself, however your access actually works, keep each export as a dated snapshot, and this tool compares two of them and reports what was added, removed, or changed, with the source URL carried through to each record. Nothing here visits a register on your behalf, so there is nothing that can be blocked, rate-limited, or logged in as you without your knowledge.

Does this tool fetch or scrape the register for me, like Visualping does?

No, and that is the whole design. Visualping's job is to visit a URL automatically on a schedule so you do not have to. This tool never makes a network request to a register at all — you supply both snapshots, captured however your own export process works, and it only compares the two you give it. That makes it usable for registers with no stable public URL, a login wall, a CAPTCHA, or a rate limit that an automated visitor could not respect anyway; it also means there is no scheduled crawler running against a register on your behalf that you would need to configure, pause, or explain.

Do I lose the screenshot and AI summary Visualping gives me?

Yes, this tool does not attempt that, and for a register export it is the wrong shape of comparison. A visual screenshot diff is built for pages where layout and rendered content are what changed. A register export is structured data — rows with an id, a set of fields, and a source URL — and the useful comparison is per-record: which ids appeared, which disappeared, and which fields changed on records that stayed, each one still linked back to its source so a reviewer can open the original entry. That is a different report than a picture of two page renders, and it is the one this tool produces.
