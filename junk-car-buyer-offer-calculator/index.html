    Questions junk car buyers ask

    What should I pay for a junk car?

    Work backward from what the car brings you. Add the scrap value of the stripped hulk (weight times the crusher's price per ton), the catalytic converter, and the parts that really sell after fees, then subtract the tow, paperwork, disposal, yard storage, your labour and the haul to the crusher. Subtract the profit you need and what is left is the most you can offer. This tool does that for the car in front of you.

    How is a junk car priced by weight?

    Crushers and shredders pay per ton of the stripped hulk. Weigh or look up the curb weight, subtract the parts you pull and sell separately, and multiply by the price per 2,000 lb ton. The price moves with the scrap market, so it is an input here, not a constant.

    How far can I tow a junk car and still make money?

    The tool finds the farthest one-way tow at which the seller's asking price still leaves your target profit, and shows the offer you could make at 5 to 80 miles.

    What if the parts do not sell?

    Parts are counted at the share you say actually sells, net of marketplace and payment fees, and the tool shows the sell-through the ask needs and what the offer looks like from none to all of the parts selling.

    Is this financial, tax or legal advice?

    No. It is arithmetic on figures you type. Title rules, licensing for salvage buyers, converter laws and environmental disposal rules are set by state and local law; check yours with a qualified professional.

    Not decided

    Whether the car's title is clean, whether you hold the licence a salvage buyer needs, whether the converter is legal to buy or sell where you are, which parts really are in good shape, the scrap price on the day the crusher weighs it, taxes, and your time beyond the labour figure. The hulk is priced at today's quote, and parts that do not sell count as zero.
