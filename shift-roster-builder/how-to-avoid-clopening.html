How to avoid clopening: what a rest gap between shifts costs

A clopening is a late close followed by an early open: someone locks up at 11pm and unlocks at 6am. The standard fix is a minimum rest gap between any two of a person's shifts. Owners worry that a rule like that leaves shifts empty or raises payroll. We measured it.

What it shows

For this cafe, a rest gap anywhere from 8 to 12 hours cost about $100 a week more than no rule and did not leave a single extra shift open. The search simply gave the awkward close-then-open pairs to different people. At 14 hours the rule starts to bite: the same staff can no longer cover three more shifts. So the question to ask of your own week is where your cliff is, not whether a rule is affordable in general. This is one invented example, not a benchmark, and your availability decides your numbers.

How to do it by hand

1. Mark every close and every open

List who works each closing shift, then check the next morning's opens for the same names.

2. Swap one side of each pair

Move either the close or the open to someone who is free and not already near their weekly limit. Do the people with the narrowest availability first.

3. Watch the week boundary

Sunday night's close and Monday morning's open are the pair most often missed. The roster builder can read last week's roster CSV so the rule holds across Monday.

Questions

What does clopening mean in scheduling?

It means working a closing shift and then the opening shift the next morning, leaving little time to sleep. A minimum rest gap between shifts is the usual way to prevent it.

How many hours of rest should be between shifts?

There is no single answer. Some employers use 8 to 12 hours as policy and some places regulate it for certain workers. Check the rules where you operate. The roster builder lets you set the gap and will not break it.
