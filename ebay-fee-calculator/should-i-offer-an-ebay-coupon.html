    What this checks

    Clothing, card, and small-goods sellers search “should I offer an eBay coupon” because Seller Hub surfaces a public code next to Send offer. A coupon looks like free conversion. It also writes a cheaper ticket on every redeemed sale, and dropping the Buy It Now so search shows the cheaper price can leave less than staying listed.

    On the defaults — $40 item, $6 shipping charged, 13.6% final value fee, $0.40 fixed, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, $5 return label, 10% returns, 15% coupon, 40% redeem, 40% listed sell-through, 55% coupon sell-through, 50% dropped-list sell-through, forty listings — staying listed expects $7.24, the coupon expects $8.93, and dropping the list expects $6.72. The coupon keeps $1.69 more on this decision and +$67.60 across forty listings. The coupon has to convert about 44.6% of the time, or the dropped list about 66.5%, before those paths match. If they would have bought anyway, leftover shrinks $1.87 ($18.11 vs $16.24). Dropping the list shrinks leftover $4.67 ($13.44).

    Matching conversion already loses. Keep the same listing and hold coupon sell-through at 40%. Listed still expects $7.24; the 15% coupon expects $6.50 — $0.75 less. A 25% code that everyone redeems expects $5.68 even at a 55% badge lift. An 80% dropped-list sell-through flips the cheaper BIN ahead ($10.76 vs $8.93).     Common questions

    Should I offer an eBay coupon?

    A seller coupon is a public code on a listing that still shows Buy It Now. Only the redeem share pays the cut; everyone else still pays the listed price. Dropping the list writes the cheaper price into search and sold comps. On the calculator defaults ($40 item, $6 shipping charged, 13.6% final value fee, $12 cost, $5 outbound, $1 pack, 10% returns, 15% coupon, 40% redeem, 40% listed sell-through, 55% coupon sell-through, 50% dropped-list sell-through) staying listed expects $7.24, the coupon expects $8.93, and dropping the list expects $6.72. The coupon keeps $1.69 more after sell-through. On leftover — if they would have bought anyway — listed leaves $18.11 and the coupon mix leaves $16.24, $1.87 less. The coupon has to convert about 44.6% of the time, or the dropped list about 66.5%, before those paths match. That is arithmetic on your inputs, not advice to publish a coupon.

    When does staying listed beat an eBay coupon?

    When the redeem mix eats more leftover than the badge lift returns. On the same $40 listing, matching the 40% listed sell-through already loses on a 15% coupon ($6.50 vs $7.24). A 25% code that everyone redeems expects $5.68 against $7.24 listed even at a 55% badge lift.

    Is an eBay coupon the same as an offer to watchers or a quantity discount?

    No. A seller-initiated offer is a private sent price to watchers, one accept rate. A quantity discount cuts N units into one box. Promoted Offsite is a campaign billed even if they bounce. A coupon keeps the listing at Buy It Now and only the redeem share pays less.
