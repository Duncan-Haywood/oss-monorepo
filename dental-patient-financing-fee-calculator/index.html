    Questions people ask

    Is third-party patient financing worth the merchant fee?

    Compare what you keep per accepted case after the fee with what an in-house plan or cash-only keeps, then weight each by how many presented cases patients accept under that option. Financing costs more per case but often raises acceptance. This tool shows the acceptance rate financing must reach to match each alternative, so you can judge whether your own numbers clear it.

    What does an in-house payment plan really cost a practice?

    Beyond waiting for the money, there are card fees on every instalment, the admin time to run each account, and patients who stop paying. The tool charges each of these, values instalments at today's money using what waiting costs you, and shows the unpaid balance when some share of patients default.

    How should I read the break-even acceptance numbers?

    Each one is the acceptance rate an option needs to earn the same expected contribution per presented case as the comparison option. If you believe financing lifts acceptance above that figure, it pays for its fee; if not, it does not. The acceptance rates you type are your assumptions, not benchmarks.

    At what merchant fee does financing stop beating my own plan?

    The result shows the merchant fee at which financing and the in-house plan earn the same per accepted case, holding your other inputs fixed. Above it, each accepted case is worth more on your own plan; financing then has to win on acceptance to be worth it.
