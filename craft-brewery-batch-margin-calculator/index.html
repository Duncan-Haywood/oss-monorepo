    How it decides, and what it leaves out

    • Batch cost: ingredients, labour hours at your rate and utilities and cleaning, divided over the barrels that reach a package after loss.
    • Excise: federal excise at the reduced craft rates ($3.50 a barrel on the first 60,000 barrels, $16 above) averaged over the barrels you sell a year, plus the state rate you enter.
    • Overhead: monthly overhead spread evenly per barrel. It lowers every channel by the same amount per barrel, so it does not change which channel wins, only whether it pays.
    • Per barrel-day: profit divided by batch barrels and fermenter days, so a 60-day beer is judged by the tank time it uses.
    • Not decided: state and local licensing, tied-house and franchise rules, sales and alcohol taxes on retail sales, tips, the real cost of keg loss and returns, working capital, and your TTB filing. Distributor prices and taproom costs here are your inputs, not market quotes.

    Common questions

    What is the real margin on a batch of craft beer?

    It depends far more on where the beer is sold than on the recipe. A barrel poured as pints in your own taproom can earn several times what the same barrel earns as kegs to a distributor. This page costs each batch after yield loss, labour, excise and a volume share of overhead, then shows profit per barrel in every channel.

    How much federal excise tax does a small brewery pay per barrel?

    Under the Craft Beverage Modernization Act a brewer making 2 million barrels or fewer a year pays $3.50 per barrel on the first 60,000 barrels and $16 per barrel above that. The page uses that rate and lets you add your state's excise. Confirm both with TTB and your state.

    Which beer should I brew more of?

    Rank by profit per barrel of tank per day, not profit per batch. A 60-day barrel-aged stout can show a bigger batch profit than an IPA and still earn a third as much for each day it occupies a fermenter.

    How many barrels is a keg?

    A half-barrel keg is 15.5 US gallons, so a barrel (31 gallons) is two kegs, about 248 pints of 16 ounces or 248 cans of 16 ounces.
